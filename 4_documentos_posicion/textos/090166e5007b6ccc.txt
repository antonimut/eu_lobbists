Reference: Contribution of the European Consumer Electronics Retail Council to the 
public consultation on the Sustainable Consumption of goods (Right to Repair) 
 
 
General Statement 
 
The European Consumer Electronics Retail Council (EuCER)  welcomes the European 
Commission’s proposal on the Right to Repair.  
 
Indeed, EuCER has been supporting this initiative since its early stages and receives positively 
the establishment of a framework to encourage repair.  
 
EuCER strongly promotes the implementation of circular economy within the European Union, 
which necessarily passes through the creation of a culture of repair and reuse.  
 
To be successful, this proposal should create a favourable en vironment for all economic 
operators and ensure a fair competition among them. To  that end, EuCER reminds that it is 
fundamental to control the cost and availability of spa re parts, and to raise consumers’ 
awareness on the benefits of repair. 
 
Facilitating consumers’ access to key information to help th em make informed decisions will 
greatly support the objective of this legislation. 
 
The European institutions must work together to guarante e incentives for both retailers an d 
consumers to effectively make repair feasible and attra ctive, for the benefit of consumers, 
enterprises, and of the environment.   
 
Specific recommendations 
 
Controlling the cost of repair 
 
This is the key element for the success of this proposal. Ind eed, the decision whether to 
replace or repair products is highly influenced by the co st. The proposal should therefore 
guarantee that affordable spare parts can be obtained.  Labour cost is also a major obstacle 
to make repair the preferred option. Financial incentivises should be foreseen to counter these 
issues.  
 
Enabling repair outside the control of manufacturers 
 
To that end, it is essential that EU legislations ensure the access to spare parts, to repair 
information and to software updates. Guidelines on rep air should also be provided by 
manufacturers. In practical terms, manufacturers should provide downloadable repair manuals 
and make available technical instructions to enable the  exploitation of 3D-printers to 
manufacture the components needed for repair.

Clarifying the role attributed to repairers, manufactu rers, sellers would also help the proper 
implementation of this legislation.  
  
In addition, EuCER supports the creation of a Europea n Repair Information Form. However, 
it is important to clarify how to assess the cost of repai r (e.g. manpower, shipping, value of 
repaired product only). 
 
Clarifying the scope of products covered by repairability requirements  
 
The proposal foresees that the obligation to repair b eyond the legal guarantee is limited to 
goods for which repairability requirements exist under E U Law. With the proposal for a 
Regulation on Ecodesign for Sustainable Products (ESPR), the number of products concerned 
are expected to increase exponentially. Existing and upco ming delegated acts should be 
sufficiently clear and proportionate to allow repair wh en relevant. To ensure certainty, time 
limits for repairability should be included in all de legated acts. Some existing delegated acts 
do not include such time limit for repairability. It i s important to mention that the decision to 
repair a product should be taken on a case- by-case basis as sometimes replacing a good is 
more environmentally friendly then repairing it.   
 
Foresee a role for fulfilment service providers and for online marketplaces 
  
Fulfilment service providers and online marketplaces often  escape obligations that fall on 
retailers. Provisions should be included in the legislation to obl ige them to set up structures 
allowing repair.   
 
Incentivising repair will be essential to the success of this legislation 
Financial incentives should be favoured considering that repair is not always economically 
feasible. A reduced VAT rate, of at least 50 %, for refurbished and repaired products and other 
tax incentives / state subsidies would create a favourable environment for repair.  
Encouraging consumers to choose repair over replacement  
 
Public communication campaigns would help this objective.  The role of retailers in advising 
consumers is crucial and the retail sector gladly contribute to help consumers make informed 
choices.  
 
 
EuCER will be happy to engage in future discussions with EU policymakers.

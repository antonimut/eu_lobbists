IACS Feedback on the revision of the EU Maritime Safety Package  
________________________________________________________________________ 
 
Note: comments refer to the reviews of both, Directive 2009/21/EC on compliance with flag State 
requirements and Directive 2009/16/EC on port State control. 
 
a.           Implementation of digital solutions 
The role of EMSA seems to be considerably strengthened regarding the development of relevant 
databases and tools that support the implementation of Directives on FS requirements, PSC, accident 
investigation, passenger ship safety. It is considered important that IACS is consulted when it comes 
to the format of electronic information exchange as referred to in Art 6 of the FS Directive. IACS needs 
to ensure that the format of existing solutions (already applied by ROs) are  compatible and 
interoperable with the Union Maritime safety database. Consideration needs to be given on how the 
collected data is intended to be used.1 
 
b.           High level group on flag State matters  
The Commission shall establish a H igh level group on flag State matters for discussing the 
implementation of the envisaged measures (ref Art. 9a FS Directive) including reporting formats, RO 
monitoring, RO performance criteria, and RO oversight. It is considered crucial that IACS adequately 
monitors the activities in that High level group. IACS needs to be considered an important party in the 
maritime safety regime and as such be included in the relevant discussions by representing the ‘private 
sector’, as foreseen.

Annex : IACS position on digitalization in PSC  (COWI Impact Assessment support study concerning 
possible revision of Directive 2009/16/EC on port State control -July 2021) 
 
Should the PSC Directive incentivize the use of electronic certificates (e.g. introduce require ments, 
set up a rewarding framework, introduce voluntary/mandatory validation pre -boarding the ship, 
…)? 
 – and what is the current use of electronic certificates among shipping companies / Member States? 
  
IACS Members consider that the use of electronic certificates, which is already in use, could have 
positive effects in terms of greater efficiency in the certificate management system by all parties 
involved, ROs, Flags and Companies. However, the authorization to the use of electronic certificates 
belongs to the Flag Administration. 
 
Should the EU define technical standards for electronic certificates or await a global standard? – or 
should it be left to the industry or others to define?  
 
IACS members considers that EU technical standards are not needed since the existing guidance in 
IMO Circular FAL.5/Circ.39/Rev.2 is sufficient as shown by the fact that, in accordance to the IMO 
guidance, each IACS Member has already developed and implemented its own processes and 
procedures for the issuance/endorsement/withdrawal of elec tronic certificates, their validation 
and traceability and has had these processes and procedures already accepted by a large number 
of individual Flag States authorizing IACS Members as their ROs (refer to 3.6). IACS Members should 
individually retain the  flexibility in developing/maintaining/updating their own e -certification 
system, in accordance with the IMO Circular. The IMO guidance has not presented any 
implementation problem so far and, when IMO will update it, IACS intends to contribute to the 
development of any practical measures to improve it, including the Procedures for port State 
control, and we believe that EU should cooperate within that global framework. 
 
Does your organisation issue or accept electronic certificates? – if not, what hinders you? if yes, what 
are the main benefits? When do you expect to be able to issue/accept e-certificates?  
 
All IACS Members have implemented procedure for the issuance of electronic certificates, their 
validation and traceability and implements their use ac cording to pertinent Flag Administrations 
authorizations and at the request of the owner. The main benefits are linked to traceability, proof 
against tampering, the reliability of stamping and signing the certificate, the electronic sending 
without a physical delivery and their continued availability in the website.

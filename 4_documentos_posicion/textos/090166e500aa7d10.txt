 Extending the scope of ESG ratings regulation to 
ensure real sustainability ratings  
 
July 2023  
 
Building on the Commission’s proposal: from transparency to tackling 
greenwashing  
 
The increasing popularity of sustainable finance has given rise to a new industry : the one of ESG 
(Environmental, Social, Governance) data providers and raters. While this new sector could play a crucial role 
in enabling sustainable investing, the way it currently functions rather leads to extensive “greenwashing” and 
“social - washing” 1 . Current high ESG scores are believed to be indicators of sustainability, while it has been 
well documented that ratings are heavily focused on the assessment of the financi al impact of sustainability 
risks for the rated corporation (inward materiality), without taking into account the impact the corporation has 
on nature and people (outward materiality). In the transport sector, large polluting carmakers, aircraft 
manufactur ers or shipping companies with too slow decarbonisation pathways, obtain remarkably and 
disturbingly high ratings 2 .  
 
The draft regulation proposed by the Commission on June 13th 2023 puts forward substantial improvements in 
the fields of minimal governance  requirements (including on conflicts of interests) and the transparency of the 
ratings. It nonetheless fails to set minimum quality thresholds for ESG ratings , thus fostering true 
harmonisation. This leaves the main problem of inward materiality and green washing unaddressed .  
 
Seven policy recommendations  
 
Transport & Environment makes seven basic policy recommendations  to the European Parliament and the 
Council of the EU that could considerably improve the Commission’s proposal :  
 
1.  Enforce double materiality: ensure ratings give priority to companies’ impact  on the outside world 
rather than ESG risks for companies;  
2.  Set minimum quality thresholds  for ESG ratings;  
3.  Attribute more importance to absolute performance  of companies in relati on to their impact 
materiality, in addition to assessing companies compared with their peers;  
4.  Differentiate “E”, “S”, “G”  ratings and link “E” to alignment with a 1.5°C pathway (=GHG 
emission reduction) and the phase - out of fossil fuels ;  
5.  Ensure minimum coherence with existing legislation  such as the Corporate Sustainability 
Reporting Directive, the Sustainable Finance Disclosure Regulation and the EU Taxonomy to enable

1. Extending the scope (Art. 1 - 3)  
 
Despite the fact that “ preventing greenwashing or other types of misinformation, including social - washing ” is 
among the objectives the Commission sets for this regulation, the option of  establishing minimum requirements 
on the content of ESG ratings has been discarded. “ Transpa rency requirements ” and “ rules on the organisation 
and conduct of ESG rating providers ” are the only tools in the proposed regulatory regime. This is a major 
mismatch and the basic flaw of the proposed approach: transparency and governance requirements alo ne 
are unable to stop greenwashing .  
 
They cannot for instance deal with the fact that rating agencies such as MSCI choose that CO2 emissions from 
the automobile sector only weigh 0.6% in the ESG rating of carmakers -  while these emissions are certainly the  
most material impact these companies have on the planet and climate. This results in the latter scoring 
unsettlingly high on environmental grounds. Transparency and governance requirements alone cannot change 
the fact that shipping giant Maersk currently gets similar ratings as cleantech companies such as Vestas Wind 
systems or SSE Renewables. This is all the more problematic that the latter focus on developing and providing 
environmentally friendly technologies reducing carbon emissions, while Maersk focu ses on transporting goods 
across the globe, which unavoidably increases emissions 3 . Similar ESG ratings attributed to companies 
operating in so different sectors generates misleading information for investors.  
 
Mandatory inclusion of specific Key Performan ce Indicators (KPIs)  such as GHG, SOx and NOx  
emissions, circularity or age of the fleet at certain minimum levels and in specific transport sectors will not 
hinder innovation as the Commission is afraid of. On the contrary, it will raise a quality bar an d thus accelerate 
the application of more efficient methods of data collection and elaboration.  
  
Therefore, Article 1 should be modified in order to include minimum quality requirements for ESG 
ratings .  
 
2. Provision of ESG Ratings in the Union (Art. 4 - 1 3)   
 
In order to implement T&E’s recommendations 1 - 5, modifications are needed in Articles 5 - 8. ESG ratings 
providers will need to demonstrate their adherence to quality criteria in their application for authorisation and 
renew their commitment on an year ly or biannual basis upon the possibility to see their authorisation suspended 
or withdrawn (Art. 8). This could be done by adding the following information in the current Annex I of the 
regulation that refers to Article 5:

1.  Weighting of the companies’  impacts on the outside world in the ratings with absolute minimum level 
and sectoral differentiation. Placing the double materiality principle at the heart of future ESG ratings 
is crucial to avoid greenwashing and ensure better consistency with other pieces of the EU sustainable 
finance architecture;  
2.  Demonstration of the u se of at least 50% of  KPI s provid ed both under the Sustainable Finance 
Disclosure Regulation (SFDR, principal adverse impacts -  PAIs -  under the SFDR Delegated 
Regulation 2022/1288) and the Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD) as minimum 
cross - cutting quality thresholds ;  
3.  In clusion of key cross - cutting indicators under SFDR and CSRD in the Annex I referring to Article 5;  
4.  Explanation of how these indicators weigh in ratings ;  
5.  Disclosure of the allocation between “E”, “S” and “G” factors in ratings  with minimum 30% for 
the “E” p illar and with an explanation of its alignment with a 1.5°C pathway ;  
 
Finally, in order to avoid weakening the enforcement of EU legislation, the activity of rating providers with no 
EU - based entity should only be allowed in the EU on the basis of an equiv alence agreement with other regulated 
jurisdictions and not on the basis of “endorsement” provided by individual companies or “recognition” provided 
by the European Securities and Markets Authority (ESMA). Articles 10 and 11 should therefore be deleted.  
 
3 . Integrity and Reliability of ESG Rating Activities (Art. 14 -  44)  
 
Conflict of interest regulation  (Recommendation 6) will be reinforced by applying Article 15 on the separation 
of activities (consulting, credit ratings, benchmarks, investment, audit and banking, insurance or reinsurance) 
at group rather than at entity level . This will on the one hand increase the reliability of ESG ratings for 
sustainability - driven investors and on the other hand boost the development of the EU - based ESG ratings 
industry.  The regulatory approach against other types of conflicts of interest that may arise can be strengthened 
by providing for the immediate ceasing of the problematic business relation or the rating activity in Article 23.3.  
 
Transparency and comparability of ratings (Recommendation 6) can be improved by moving the provision of 
some pieces of critical information from Article 22 (disclosure to rated entities) to Article 21 (public disclosure) 
and from point 2 to point 1 of Annex III: metrics, KPIs, data sources , data quality control. This amendment 
should also include the obligation for ESG ratings providers to list their sources of information generated by 
reputable Civil Society Organisations (Recommendation 7). Public disclosure of this information will also 
enable independent reviews and factual verification of ESG ratings. Allowing external scrutiny of ESG 
ratings  is a tool to ultimately improve the quality of ESG rating.  
 
Finally, transparency requirements should be explicitly linked with Articles 33 - 35 on administrative sanctions 
and also be subject to article 18 on the complaints mechanism.

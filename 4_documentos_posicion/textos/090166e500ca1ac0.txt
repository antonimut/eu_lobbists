 
 Feedback on the European Commission adoption of its ESG ratings proposal: The role of the ISO 17442 Legal Entity Identifier (LEI)  The Global Legal Entity Identifier Foundation (GLEIF) welcomes the opportunity to provide feedback to the European Commission’s proposal for a Regulation on the transparency and integrity of Environmental, Social and Governance (ESG) rating activities.   GLEIF welcomes that Article 13 of the proposal requires ESG ratings providers to include their legal entity identifier as part of the information to be submitted to ESMA for publication in the European Single Access Point (ESAP), whose search function would allow for a search based also on such identifier.  In this context, GLEIF argues that the mentioned ‘legal entity identifier’ should be specified in the proposed Regulation as the ISO 17442 Legal Entity Identifier (LEI). This would be consistent with the existing EU Credit Rating Agencies Regulation (CRAR), which already requires credit rating providers to obtain an ISO 17442 LEI for their identification.  From a supervisory point of view, it is critical to know who the rating provider is, so that supervisors can analyse any potential conflicts of interest and mitigate any associated risks. In addition, GLEIF argues that – if available – ESG ratings providers should be required to retrieve and submit the LEI of the entities which they rate, for the purposes of ESAP publication. This would improve the availability and reliability of ESG ratings information, as well as investors’ ability to find, compare and consume such information globally.  As a unique and universally available identifier, the ISO 17442 LEI is best placed to ensure the secure and effective identification of legal entities on a global basis, whether these are ESG rating providers or rated companies – as opposed to national identifiers, which are only applicable within countries’ borders.  There are innumerable national and regional standards for entity identification across the world and while different identifiers serve national needs, they can create significant conflicts and inefficiencies when reconciling data across geographical borders. And these geographical borders could apply to the movement of goods and services or company structures. The ISO 17442 LEI represents the clear, standardized and global entity identification system which can effectively address these issues – with a view to improving the timeliness, accuracy and reliability of ESG ratings.  About the LEI The ISO 17442 LEI was developed under the auspices of the G20 leaders and the Financial Stability Board during the 2008 financial crisis to create transparency amongst counterparties to financial transactions. GLEIF’s day-to-day work is overseen by more than 65 public authorities – including 25 from the EU.The LEI is an interoperable, open, and machine-readable identifier. It is available to all legal entities worldwide and is already in use in over 75 pieces of EU legislations for reporting purposes (e.g., the European Single Electronic Format (ESEF), the Markets in Financial Instruments Directive (MiFID), the European Market Infrastructure Regulation (EMIR), the Alternative Investment Fund Managers Directive AIFMD, the Transparency Directive and many others).

The political drivers behind the LEI initiative, including the G20, the FSB, and the European Systemic Risk Board (ESRB) have emphasised the need to make the LEI a broad public good. The Global LEI Index provides complete LEI data at the disposal of any interested party, conveniently and free of charge. This includes real-time snapshots of key company data and provides a history of any changes to it. In addition, data access is conveniently provided via website search functions, full file downloads and an Application Programming Interface (API).

FoodDrinkEurope views on EU food waste reduction 
targets set for food manufacturers  under the EC 
revision of the Waste Framework Directive 
 
 
According to the latest estimates, in the EU, nearly 59 million tonnes of food waste (131 kg per 
inhabitant) are generated annually with an associated market value estimated at €132 billion 
euros1. These staggering numbers reflect the necessity to continue to tackle this problem which is 
affecting businesses, the environment and society alike. 
 
The European food and drink industry is committed to lead by example by adopting the highest 
standards for sustainable value chains including  preventing and reducing food waste in all its 
business activities.    
 
Wasting food represents a major loss of natural resources and a needless source of greenhouse 
gas emissions that drive climate change. It also makes it harder to feed a growing global 
population. 
 
Food waste prevention remains a key priority for FoodDrinkEurope members, making it an integral 
part of their internal environmental management system and sustainability strategy aligning their 
approach with the food waste hierarchy. 
 
In this context, we support the European Commission’s ambition to further address the reduction 
of food losses and food waste across supply chains, thus contributing to the implementation of the 
United Nations (UN) Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) 12.3 to halve per capita food waste at 
the retail and consumer level by 2030 and reduce food losses along the food production and supply 
chains. 
 
Beyond developing EU targets at Member State (MS) level to reduce food waste for the processing 
and manufacturing  sector (10%) alongside retail and consumption level ( 30 % jointly for 
restaurants, food services and households) by 2030, from 2020 levels, the proposed revision must 
promote impactful action on the ground and collaborative multi-stakeholder initiatives at National 
and EU levels in order to yield positive outcomes.  
 
In moving forward with the design of the proposed revised rules to reduce food waste, we call on 
policymakers to consider the following priorities for the European food and drink industry:  
 
• Ensure that the legislative framework promotes impactful action to address food waste in 
line with internationally recognised frameworks (as mentioned above). 
• Ensure that the legislative framework is consistent and aligned with other EU policies, in 
particular the future EU Framework for Sustainable Food Systems and the EU Directive

on Corporate Sustainability Reporting and the foreseen development of sector specific

• Develop specific guidance on food waste measurement to help actors along the supply 
chain, as well as  MS authorities, to consistently interpret food waste data and reporting 
requirements (without causing  too much of an  administrative burden for food 
manufactures) set in the delegated act in food waste measurement2. As MS collected and 
reported data on food waste for the first time in 2022, it is important that future data is  of 
robust quality and comparable. In this context, we welcome the Commission’s decision to 
adopt delegated acts to supplement this Directive in order to potentially lay down a 
common methodology and minimum quality requirements for the uniform measurement of 
food waste levels.  We believe that such a metho dology should be based on the ‘target 
measure act’ approach outlined in the World Resources Institute’s “Food Loss and Waste 
Accounting and Reporting Standard”3. 
• Clarify how an earlier reference year for action taken by food manufacturers to reduce 
food waste can be considered and reported on for MS relating to action taken before 2020. 
Many food and drink manufacturers have taken steps towards food waste reduction using 
the baseline year 2015, in line with the UN SDGs. These efforts must be recognised by the 
measures to be implemented by Member States to achieve the EU -wide food waste 
reduction targets. 
• Clarify how the target will apply  to manufacturers that produce limited amounts of food 
waste. 
• Take into account the adjustment costs for economic operators in the agri -food sector, 
especially SME’s, for the implementation of future  food waste management systems. In 
this context, European-funded research on food losses and food waste should be 
conducted for every actor in the supply chain to help identify the specific challenges and 
develop solutions so that also no one is left behind. 
• Minimize the negative impact on employment and income in the food supply chain. 
Companies will need support in finding alternative ways to compensate income losses 
and support workforce in finding alternative employment options.   
• Take into account that an increase in overall European food production will likely lead 
to an increase in absolute food waste numbers even when strict food waste r eduction 
measures are being applied to reduce relative food waste per kg of produced food. The 
targets should allow for a certain measure of flexibility to account for such absolute 
increases as a solution cannot be downscaling European production, reducin g our food 
security and competitiveness vs imports. 
• Support opportunities to further facilitate  and raise awareness about the possibilities to  
redistribute surplus food in the EU in line with the food waste hierarchy. Local or regional 
food hubs must be created to reinforce the latter. Special consideration must also be given 
to the use of food going in the form of animal feed. 
• Foresee additional measures (i.e capacity building, funding) for tools at any stage of the 
chain that support  a balance between production and demand  as well as strengthen 
collaboration between actors across the whole food value chain and other relevant players

About FoodDrinkEurope 
FoodDrinkEurope is the organisation of Europe’s food and drink industry, one of Europe’s 
largest manufacturing sectors, a leading employer in the EU, and a key contributor to the 
economy (289,000 companies, 99% SMEs, 4.5 million employees).

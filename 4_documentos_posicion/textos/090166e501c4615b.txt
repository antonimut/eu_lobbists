The National Association of German Cooperative Bank s (Bundesverband der Deutschen Volksbanken und 
Raiffeisenbanken e.V. – BVR) is grateful for having  the chance to comment on the European Commission's  
legislative package to revise the requirements for crisis management and deposit insurance ("CMDI 
Review"), published on 18 April 2023. 
 
As a preliminary remark, the BVR is in favour of im proving crisis management for credit institutions. 
However, we are afraid that this welcomed goal will  not be achieved by the amendments proposed with 
the CMDI Review. Instead, the legislative proposal would considerably impair the performance of the we ll-
established national deposit-based guarantee scheme s and in particular such that are acknowledged as 
institutional protection schemes. Ultimately, the B VR fears that the proposal is a threat to the struc ture of 
the German banking market and to financial stability as a whole. 
 
The legislative proposal includes amendments to the  Bank Recovery and Resolution Directive (BRRD), the  
Single Resolution Mechanism Regulation (SRMR) and t he Deposit Guarantee Schemes Directive (DGSD). The 
BVR rejects the entire legislative package. Neverth eless, the following comments focus on the proposed  
changes to the BRRD. Suggested amendments to the SR MR and the DGSD will be dealt with in the separate 
comments in the respective Have your say-process. 
 
In more detail we would like to raise the following concerns: 
 
1.  Medium-sized and smaller institutions must remain subject to the insolvency regime.  
 
If a credit institution encounters financial challe nges, the authorized resolution authority must, in 
accordance with existing laws, determine whether th e institution should undergo liquidation through 
insolvency procedures or if it should adhere to the  specialized resolution framework. For credit insti tutions 
of moderate and small sizes, insolvency-based liqui dation presents a suitable, dependable, and balance d 
array of tools. In such cases, safeguard programs f or institutions and schemes guaranteeing deposits o ffer a 
favourable and economically viable means for small and medium-sized credit institutions to exit the 
market. The resolution mechanism in contrast is des igned and only suitable for particularly large cred it 
institutions of systemic significance. 
 
Nevertheless, deviating from this established appro ach, the European Commission is currently advocatin g 
for subjecting virtually all medium-sized and small  institutions to the resolution regime. This is env isaged 
by major changes to the Public Interest Assessment (PIA) whose outcome governs the decision on whether  
to apply the resolution mechanism or the insolvency proceeding to a credit institution. 
 
The BVR rejects this novel strategy for the ensuing reasons: 
 
 Resolution tools are primarily tailored for institu tions of systemic importance. Their 
complex design is not appropriate for small and med ium-sized credit institutions whose 
failure has minimal repercussions on overall financ ial stability. In this context we also refer 
to the annual risk assessment undertaken by BaFin a nd Deutsche Bundesbank for the small 
and medium-sized credit institutions directly super vised by BaFin (referred to as Less 
Significant Institutions - LSIs). Pursuant to the o utcome of this assessment, the potential 
consequences of solvency or liquidity crises are su bstantially pertinent for less than 1.4% 
of LSIs. 
 
 Local critical functions can be readily substituted . We therefore object to an inclusion of 
the local level into the PIA as intended by the ame ndments in Art. 2 (1) No. 35 draft BRRD. 
Also, the incorporation of the indefinite legal con cept of "regionality" within the context of 
the Public Interest Assessment (PIA) fails to limit  the discretion of the resolution authority 
in this assessment – contrary to the intentions of the Commission's proposal. 
 
 We consider the proposed changes to the PIA pursuan t to Art. 32 (5) draft BRRD as 
inadequate. First, there is no reason why a normal insolvency proceeding shall only be in 
the public interest if it meets the resolution obje ctives more effectively  than the resolution 
mechanism. Public interest should remain considered  as satisfied when resolution 
objectives are met to the same extent , as per the current version of the PIA. Second, th e 
inclusion of costs with a limited view to extraordi nary public financial support disregards 
extensive costs associated with resolution planning  and casts a distorted light on 
insolvency proceedings.

 The extension of the resolution mechanism will caus e extensive planning and onerous 
reporting obligations, this way imposing significan t administrative and financial burdens. 
This is disproportionate for small and medium-sized  entities. 
 
 Moreover, compliance with the institution-specific Minimum Requirement for Own Funds 
and Eligible Liabilities (MREL), set by the resolut ion authority, is likely to be particularly 
challenging for medium-sized and especially smaller  institutions that often lack a capital 
market orientation, making it difficult for them to issue the requisite eligible liabilities. 
 
Against this background, we advocate for a removal of the proposed changes to Art.  2 (1) No. 35 and Art. 32 
BRRD.  
 
2.  Co-financing of resolutions by Deposit Guarantee Schemes must remain limited.  
 
The current legal framework includes provisions for  the involvement of Deposit Guarantee Schemes in 
funding resolution actions. The extent of such fund ing is very limited – for a good reason: to safegua rd the 
financial soundness of the Deposit Guarantee Scheme s themselves. 
 
In contrast to this, the legislative proposal envis ages that Deposit Guarantee Schemes will assume 
unlimited liability going forward. Additionally, fu nds from the Deposit Guarantee Scheme can also be 
employed, for instance, to facilitate the transfer of even those deposits that lack protection. This i ntroduces 
a potential risk of depleting the financial resourc es of the Guarantee Schemes, thereby undermining 
depositor confidence in the effectiveness and functionality of the schemes: 
 
 In a worst-case scenario, all amassed funds within the Deposit Guarantee Scheme might 
need to be utilized for resolution financing. They would then have to be replenished within 
a maximum of six years. The augmented financial dem and due to a higher likelihood of 
seeking recourse during a crisis is compounded by t he planned expansion of depositor 
protection to previously unprotected deposits (see below), which contradicts the 
designated mandate. 
 
 If resolution measures co-funded by a Deposit Guara ntee Scheme prove unsuccessful, we 
understand that the same schemes must also fund com pensatory disbursements to 
depositors. Eventually there would be a dual burden for the schemes. 
 
 Unavoidably, subsequent increases in contributions undermine the competitiveness of the 
institutions. Unforeseen necessity for special cont ributions further erodes profits and 
impacts both competitiveness, financial stability, and customers' trust in the system. 
 
Against this background, we advocate for a removal of the proposed changes to Art.  109 and Art. 44 (5) 
point (a) BRRD.  
 
3.  Deposit Guarantee Schemes must keep their preferential position in insolvency proceedings.  
 
The Commission's legislative proposal is suggesting  the elimination of the priority status enjoyed by 
deposit guarantee schemes that have taken on the ri ghts and responsibilities of protected depositors 
during insolvency or have reimbursed depositors (kn own as "super preference"). Instead, the proposal 
institutes a general depositor preference during in solvency that would encompass all deposits 
indiscriminately. 
 
The BVR strongly rejects this due to the following unavoidable consequences: 
 
 The uniform treatment of all deposits essentially s ignifies that deposit guarantee schemes 
would receive notably diminished returns in insolve ncy proceedings, leading to relatively 
greater losses during compensation. Consequently, f inancial shortfalls would need to be 
rectified through increased regular contributions f rom credit institutions to the schemes. 
This is ultimately accompanied by the risk of subst antially undermining the credibility of 
the financing mechanism, coupled with the potential  erosion of depositor trust in the 
deposit guarantee schemes or the overall security of their deposits.

 There is no imperative for a uniform treatment of a ll depositors, especially considering that 
the claims of vulnerable depositors (individuals, m icro, small, and medium-sized 
enterprises) who possess deposits exceeding the cov ered amount already hold a higher 
position in the hierarchy compared to the claims of  typical non-covered and non-
preferential creditors. 
 
 The suggested general deposit preference would lowe r the standing of claims held by 
ordinary unsecured creditors, as all depositors, ra ther than specific ones, would now have 
precedence over them. We see a risk that this might  cause "bank-runs" by this creditor 
category in the event of an impending institution failure. 
 
Similarly, the claims of the resolution financing m echanism established in Art. 108 (8) draft BRRD and  
their preferred position in the insolvency proceedi ngs will probably regularly lead to losses for the 
deposit guarantee schemes and to their financial de pletion. This in turn will lead to a loss of confid ence 
on the part of depositors. 
 
Against this background, we advocate for a removal of the proposed changes to Art.  108 and Art. 55 (1) 
point (b) BRRD.  
 
4.  Alternative private and preventive measures may not be impaired.  
 
The Commission's legislative proposal associate add itional requisites with alternative measures by the  
Deposit Guarantee Schemes which have the purpose of  forestalling an impending default. The BVR objects  
to these propositions for the ensuing reasons: 
 
 Art. 30a draft BRRD introduces a supervisory evalua tion of alternative measures to avert an 
imminent default during an "early warning period". This evaluation relates to their effects 
and timing of implementation, see Art. 30a (2) poin t (b) draft BRRD. It is understood that the 
proposal does not imply mandatory approval of these  private measures. Nevertheless, the 
newly introduced engagement with the regulatory bod y introduces the potential risk of 
triggering a state-aid situation (especially agains t the proposed incorporation of Art. 32c 
draft BRRD, see below). This would impede swift and efficient action. 
 
 The introduction of the authority's option to marke t an institution in the phase before an 
FOLTF-status must be seen critically as well (Art. 30a (4) and (5) BRRD draft). This measure 
is a strong infringement of the institute's rights and would happen too early if it is not yet 
even clear that the institution will fail eventuall y. Additionally, the proposal is not 
compatible with measures aimed at averting a defaul t through an IPS (Institutional 
Protection Scheme). Consequently, as an alternative  to the proposed deletion of 
paragraphs 4 and 5, an addition is imperative to cl arify that the proposed marketing option 
is rescinded if there are alternative measures by a n IPS – with which it fulfils legally or 
officially recognized responsibilities. 
 
 The wording of Art. 32c (1) point (b) draft BRRD ca n be misunderstood to mean that in 
future all preventive measures, including those of private deposit guarantee schemes, are 
to constitute state aid. Since such a comprehensive  change in the legal situation is not 
intended, the wording should be made clearer to ens ure that it only applies if the 
conditions for state aid are met. 
 
 The implementation of preventive measures is couple d with the effective execution of the 
institution's resolution strategy (Art. 32 (1) poin t (b) draft BRRD). It is, however, unclear how 
privately executed measures to prevent an instituti on's failure should align with the 
effective implementation of the - potentially diver gent - resolution strategy. The 
objectives of resolution (gone concern) and institu tional protection (going concern) 
contradict each other so that the latter should not be subject to the first.

Against this background, we advocate for a removal of the proposed introduction of Art. 30a and Art. 3 2c 
(1) point (b) BRRD as well as of the change to Art.  32 (1) point (b) draft BRRD. If the marketing opti on in Art. 
30a (4) and (5) draft BRRD remains there must be an  exemption to cases where alternative measures by a n 
IPS would prevent the failure of the institution.  
 
 
5.  Contributions to the Single Resolution Fund (SRF) according to Art. 102 et seq. BRRD  
In terms of contributions to the resolution fund, w e consider it essential to incorporate some clarifi cations. 
Specifically, once the resolution funds reach their  target level by the end of 2023, regular contribut ions 
should no longer be imposed. However, it is necessa ry to explicitly set a non-dynamic target level of 1% of 
covered deposits, as the Commission and the SRB have so far assumed the opposite without a legal basis. 
The contributions already collected, particularly f or the Single Resolution Fund (SRF), are approximat ely 
40% higher than originally estimated. If these cont ributions persist, the fund will become even more 
overcapitalized than it already is at the end of 20 23. According to the current legal regulations, 
contributions cease when the target level is reache d. They only have to be reinstated through ex post 
contributions if the target level falls significantly short. 
If the bank levy continues to be imposed despite th is, it's crucial to explicitly state in the legal t ext 
(Art. 102(3) BRRD) that the collection process cost s encompass all expenses incurred by authorities an d 
institutions. Focusing solely on the costs related to collection by resolution authorities would make 
suspending the levy improbable. Additionally, suspe nding the collection process should not only cover the 
contributions themselves but also extend to the sub mission of the annual report used for contribution 
calculations.

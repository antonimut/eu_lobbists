 
Feedback to EC consultation on Proposal for a revised Victims’ Rights 
Directive 
 
The End FGM European Network strongly welcomes the Commission’s proposal to amend Directive 
2012/29/EU.  
 
The proposed Directive represents a strong instrument for survivors/victims1 of female genital mutilation 
living in Europe, and strengthening its provisions will ensure a more adequate approach to supporting 
victims of gender-based violence such as FGM. 
 
We welcome the clarification and recognise the stated clear complementarity and consistency between 
the provisions of this horizontal legislation and the sectorial legislation, which includes the integration in 
the text of the proposal for a Directive on combating violence against women and domestic violence. 
 
We also welcome the recognition of the shortcomings of the current Directive that emerged from the 
evaluation and are in line with those identified in relation to particular vulnerable groups, including of 
FGM and GBV victims and persons at risk.  
 
We also welcome the recognition that an update of the Directive is necessary to address the changes 
of minimum standards that have occurred in the past 10 years, especially referring to ensuring the 
application of a victim-centred and child-friendly justice system. 
 
In particular, we welcome the amendment of: 
 
▪ Article 3a, providing access to a helpline for better exercise of the right to information as well 
as the provision of emotional support and reference to specialised support services.  
 
➢ We recommend: it is essential that the harmonised EU helpline number 116 006 and 
the communication technologies associated to it, be effectively operating and fully 
accessible in all Member States; in each Member State, the helpline and relevant 
websites/communication channels should be cooperating closely with civil society 
organisations and support services in order to ensure the service is accessible and 
able to properly redirect the caller/user to the most adequate service where necessary. 
➢ A coordinated helpline must have an evaluation process which is robust to test 
effectiveness of the service, as well as measure impact for victims/survivors. Moreover, 
anyone working on such a helpline (coordinated at EU level or national) must be

▪ Article 5a, on the reporting of a crime being made easier, through different channels including 
through electronic communication technologies, more child-friendly and with stronger 
confidentiality rules, in particular regarding the residence status of the victim. 
 
➢ We recommend: the information about the consequences of reporting a crime should 
be accessible in a child-friendly manner and in the languages spoken by the affected 
community members, to ensure that the victim is not discouraged from reporting and 
has full knowledge regarding its rights pertaining, in particular, to the right to access 
(specialised) support services.   
➢ FGM victims/survivors often come from a migrant background or might be in the 
process of seeking asylum. Their documentation and residency status should not 
prevent them from being able to access the support services they need. 
 
▪ Article 9(1)(c), (3)(b) and (3)(c) introducing the State’s obligation to provide free-of -charge 
psychological support for as long as necessary in line with the individual needs assessment; 
targeted and integrated support in line with the Directive against VAW and DV and the 
provision of comprehensive protection and specialist support services to target the multiple 
needs and demands of victims. 
  
➢ We recommend: psychological support for victims of female genital mutilation should 
be accessible in the official languages and the language of origin of the individual 
where necessary to provide full accessibility and comfort for the survivor. It is important 
to consider the impact that gender-based violence can have on witnesses too - e.g., 
sibling impacted but not directly affected – and ensure that support is available to them 
as well. 
➢ All Member States should ensure the presence of multidisciplinary centres for FGM 
survivors that tackle the different needs of the victim with a holistic approach and the 
consultation of a team of varied professional expertise, in line with our the promising 
practices identified in Belgium, Sweden and France2 
 
▪ Article 9a, which includes the provision of targeted and integrated support services for child 
victims through a coordinated multi-agency mechanism. 
 
➢ We recommend: victims of female genital mutilation are often children, who do not 
realise what they are undergoing when this occurs. Medical examinations and their 
result can be traumatising for a survivor and should be dealt with highest consideration 
of the persons’ reality, without judgment and with sensitive person-centred 
communication. Member states should ensure that WHO guidelines on person-centred

▪ Article 24, which insists on the need to take into account the best interest of the child in cases 
where the offence involves the holder of parental responsibility.  
 
➢ We recommend: this is particularly relevant for FGM survivors, as in the case of FGM 
the parents or the family is often involved in the decision of carrying out the practice on 
their daughter, often based on beliefs which are not consciously aimed at doing harm. 
The best interest of the child should be carefully assessed case by case to ensure that 
the care and support the child needs are provided and, if possible and suitable, in 
cooperation with the relatives, avoiding the unnecessary disruption of family relations.  
 
We recommend that the provisions related to support services and helplines are accompanied by the 
obligation of dedicated national funding aimed at ensuring that these measures are sustained in the 
long term and harmonised across the EU. The management of such funding should be held 
accountable through an appropriate monitoring mechanism. 
 
While the Victims Rights Directive is set to provide for the minimum standards for victims of a crime, we 
insist that it should also ensure to strengthen and promote the adoption of higher minimum standards of 
crime prevention measures across the EU. 
 
We will continue to support the European Commission, European Parliament and Council towards the 
adoption of a strengthened Victims’ Rights Directive and to ensure its full transposition in Member 
States.

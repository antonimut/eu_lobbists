The Central Union of Agricultural Producers and Forest Owners  MTK welcomes to express views on the Soil  
Monitoring Directive proposed by the European Commission on 3 July 2023.  
 
The aim of th e directive is important: to support sustainable soil management  and long -term health, which is 
strongly supported by the Finnish farmers and forest owner s. A healthy soil is a prerequisite for  long-term food 
production as well as healthy forests that seques ter carbon. Nevertheless, MTK would like to bring up concern s 
related to  the future  legislative work and the most substantive  points that could hav e negative consequences 
both for agriculture and forest management. 
 
 
First, the scope of the legislation con cerns the whole EU territory. This brings a substantive chan ge compared to 
the current legislation where soil health monitoring especially in forests is part of national competence. Although 
soil monitoring would be a shared competence of t he E U, national soil-related science and knowledge  on the 
best-possible management practices should contribute to this directive. 
 
MTK supports the aim to improve the health of soils. Nevertheless, MTK stresses the need for flexibility when 
choosing the best possible indicators and criteria for defining healthy soils by 2050. 
 
As environmental conditions vary across the EU it can be difficult to find indicator s that are suitable for all soil 
types. Given that the health of forest and agricultural land can be defined by various ways depending on the local 
soil characteristics, the definition for a healthy soil should allow regionally adapted ranges. 
 
Also, factors that affect soil indirectl y and where the landowner has only little control over, e.g., climate change, 
transboundary air deposition, geology, and soil age should be well considered when determining indicators. 
 
 
MTK underlines that the main objective should be that the soil is heal thy. Therefore, the  directive’s approach 
should be result-based, and not focus on certain practices. If an indicator shows a weakened soil health, it needs 
to be improved, but the methods to reach this goal must be flexible. 
 
In the Commission’s proposal it is  stated that the soil is unhealthy if one of the presented indicators is not within 
the range. This “one out-all out” principle is not supported by MTK as the indicators are not comparable. Some of 
them are  measured on the EU level  in the same way  and in some cases (e.g., phosphorus) the levels are 
determined in various ways in different countries , which makes the comparability difficult . Also, the proposal 
would require data that is not measured in general. Applying this would mean a lot of new measurements, many 
of which can be very expensive and can thus bring substantive bureaucracy and costs. 
 
Therefore, MTK supports an approach where the countries were allowed to decide the ranges themselves, based 
on the most suitable  methods used . In this way, the data would be  considerably mor e extensive, and the  
uncertainties would be eliminated. Also, according to the directive, measurements are proposed to be carried out 
every five years. As changes in many soil factors are slow , this would be too often, and ev ery 10 years would be 
already enough.

In Nordic forest management, soil preparation is part of active, sustai nable forest management which boosts 
carbon accumulation in forests in the long -term, although, from a short -term perspective, t here is some 
decomposition of organic matter. Also, lowering the water level is a prerequisite for cultivating organic soils, even 
though the decomposition of organic matter takes place at the same tim e. Such farming and forest management 
practices which have been proven to be beneficial in Nordic conditions should not be restricted by this directive.  
 
MTK sees that delegated acts do not support the fact that decisions on environment-related legislations should be 
implemented as close to the landowner as possible. The number of delegated acts should be minimised in files 
like these to consider regional differences and maintain a diverse decision-making process in all EU institutions. 
 
 
Regarding the data collection the end use and the scope need to be clear. Also, the national already existing good 
practices in data collectio n, e.g. National Forest Inventories (NFI) or the soil monitoring network c overing the 
whole agricultural area of Finlan d should act as a n adequate basis for providing the necessary information. The 
data should not disclose any private information of landowners and the interpretation of the data must be based 
on the best available scientific expertise. No obligations for data collection should be left for landowners. 
 
Lastly, the implementation of the di rective must consider market-based measures. This is linked espec ially to the 
new “soil certification schem e” that is pr oposed part of the direc tive. The added valu e of having several  
certification schemes in place needs to be reviewed. In addition, having  numerous forest and agriculture-related 
EU files on the table,  the impact assessment of different files needs to be  analysed and well communica ted to 
landowners.

Summary of Trafikverket's consultation responses 
 
In general, it can be stated that when travel and transport are needed, the use of efficient, cross-
border rail transport have a great potential to contribute to desirable reductions in the climate 
impact of transports and at the same time strengthen Europe's and thereby also Sweden's economy 
and competitiveness in the world. This also applies to intermodal transport involving rail. A well-
functioning model for allocating rail capacity is essential for transport to meet real market demand 
and for transport to be carried out in a reliable manner, which is crucial for the attractiveness of rail.  
Trafikverket is positive to the proposed change in the capacity allocation process, which aims to 
achieve a long-term, stable and predictable capacity allocation that meets market and political 
demand of transports and travels and to better combine it with the need for construction works.  
The proposal is in line with the work of infrastructure managers at EU level, partly together with 
sector stakeholders.  
The railways in Sweden have major needs of investment and maintenance in the coming years, 
which is a challenge because more work in the infrastructure, while they are ongoing, severely limit 
the ability to transport goods and passengers. For this reason, a well-functioning planning process is 
important.  
The proposed regulation strengthens the role of infrastructure managers. Therefore, regulation 
must ensure that infrastructure managers meet stakeholders' needs (demand for capacity) as far as 
possible in their capacity offer. One change is that the market need for capacity will be obtained over 
several years, as opposed to 8 months before the start of the working timetable, which takes place 
today. Market needs should then be met through a high degree of pre-planned capacity and it needs 
to be ensured that capacity allocation is balanced from all stakeholders needs and carried out in a 
transparent, competitively neutral and non-discriminatory manner. 
Prioritizing the capacity needs of different stakeholders, at different time stages in the process and 
with what is the basis for higher priority (freight traffic and commercial or community-supported 
passenger transport as well as construction works), is not easy to understand and needs to be 
clarified. The lack of clarity means that it is not possible to get an overview or estimate of the impact 
of the regulation.  
The extended mandate of infrastructure managers needs to be balanced by sufficient involvement of 
stakeholders/applicants in the development of the rail market and by provisions allowing for 
effective supervision.  
Such a comprehensive and structural proposed change in capacity planning and traffic management 
in Europe should be introduced gradually and in a controlled manner, taking into account the 
capacities of all stakeholders and with joint plans, in order to minimize the risk of negative 
consequences for passengers and transport buyers.

Trafikverket sees the proposal for organization as ambitious, but complex and in parts unnecessary. 
The organizational structure and decision-making process should be simple, clear and effective for 
all stakeholders. Designing and establishing new organizations and collaborations takes time and 
focus from the pace of development regarding what is to be achieved Trafikverket believes that the

SERA Directive (Directive 2012/34/EU). By repealing the Freight Corridor Regulation, which has

support and focus than is the case in the proposed regulation.

responsibility for capacity planning and traffic management, with requirement of more and stronger 
coordination and consultation with all stakeholders, in order to enable and create conditions for

not apply the Regulation, the Commission can regulate in detail through implementing and 
delegated acts, which indirectly require infrastructure managers to comply with the Regulation and 
its purpose.

capacity planning and traffic management processes for the Swedish Transport Administration and 
all relevant stakeholders.

Trafikverket's overall views 
Creates opportunities for long-term, stable and more predictable planning 
The European Commission's impact assessment indicates that the current process does not meet 
the need for capacity in a sufficient way. The current capacity process is regulated in the SERA 
Directive and enshrined in national law, which means that there is limited scope for infrastructure 
managers to take the necessary steps to create a more long-term, stable and predictable planning. 
The problems we have in Sweden exist in a similar way in other countries in Europe.  
It is challenging, in the current process, to once a year receive all stakeholders' needs for capacity 
and in a short time lay the puzzle of creating timetables based on everyone's needs, including 
construction works. In the new process, planning starts several years before the trains are due to 
run and this should lead to a more reliable process and provide better quality.     
The change allowed by the proposed regulation is therefore welcomed. Much of the development 
regarding the capacity allocation process on which the regulation is based, has been actively been 
developed by Trafikverket and its colleagues in EU, in order to achieve a better process. 
Trafikverkets focus on starting the process and creating conditions at earlier stages is intended to 
lead to more stable and reliable planning and is supported by the new regulation, which creates 
conditions for taking further steps in that direction.  
The proposal is largely in line with the developments that are already underway and 
the plans that exist regarding the conditions for the use of infrastructure  
The European Commission's proposal largely reflects the development that has been carried out 
jointly for several years by the infrastructure managers in the EU, in the association RailNetEurope 
(RNE). Mainly through the projects TTR for Smart Capacity and European Traffic Management 
Network, Commercial Conditions and the international disruption management developed in the 
freight corridors established under Regulation (EU) No 913/2010 Regulation of the European 
Parliament and of the Council concerning a European rail network for competitive freight (Freight 
Corridor Regulation).   
Gradual and controlled deployment is recommended (Articles 75-77) 
The ongoing development of the capacity allocation and traffic management process developed 
within the RNE has been planned to be introduced gradually by infrastructure managers in the EU, 
given legal support. It is important that the Regulation allows for such a gradual and controlled 
introduction in order to avoid ending up in a situation where excessive changes in the development 
of capacity planning and traffic management risk becoming unmanageable and damaging traffic 
and transport. Trafikverket has experienced that it can have consequences for all parties involved if 
the development steps do not fully work, therefore a step-by-step, controlled introduction is 
preferable. The development of the capacity allocation and traffic management process should build 
on existing European plans, as partly described in the paragraph above, rather than starting over 
and developing something new.

before they began to have any effect, and to go from introverted organizational establishment to 
development of what the organizations are aiming for, so that it results in some change.   
The proposal reinforces the requirements for stable and long-term planning and 
budget for all infrastructure measures affecting capacity, including maintenance  
The current regulation on the financing of infrastructure managers (SERA, Article 8) provides, inter 
alia, that Member States shall publish an "indicative strategy for the development of railway 
infrastructure" which "shall cover a period of at least five years and shall be renewable". 
Infrastructure managers are then required to adopt, 'within the framework of the general policy 
defined by the Member State concerned' and taking into account the aforementioned strategy and 
the funding provided by the Member State, a program of operations, 'including investment and 
financing programs'.  
Trafikverket perceives the provisions of the draft regulation on Strategic capacity planning, Article 
12, as a tightening of the current rules, in such a way that infrastructure managers – in addition to 
the above-mentioned obligations – in their strategic capacity planning shall "take into account [...] 
the expected or planned development of infrastructure capacity".

With a funding period according to the proposed regulation, it seems that a situation may arise 
where a newly elected parliament does not have the opportunity to implement its policy regarding 
infrastructure financing during its entire term of office. 
The proposal (Article 10) emphasizes that planning and coordination of construction works 
(measures in the infrastructure) should take place earlier than at present in order to provide 
stakeholders with stable and predictable conditions for traffic, i.e., distribution of the capacity 
available for traffic when the necessary construction work has been planned. Therefore, an earlier 
planning and budget for infrastructure measures is required. To know what production will look like 
in 5 years, orders for construction works need to be made 7 years before production. Resources need 
to be reallocated and strengthened to make this possible. 
This also requires more concrete and detailed procurement of construction works at an earlier stage.   
Regulation and implementing acts are likely to create a more harmonized application 
in Europe  
Trafikverket notes that the implementation of current EU legislation to date does not seem to have 
achieved the intended harmonization of the railway network within the EU. 
Taking into account the principles of subsidiarity and proportionality as well as the development of 
the railways to date, Trafikverket notes that an EU regulation may be necessary to bring about the 
desired change. 
However, an EU regulation should be limited to clear provisions. The current proposal is in several 
respects unclear and therefore needs to be adjusted. The decision-making procedures of different 
groupings (such as the European Network of Infrastructure Managers, ENIM) and the legal effects 
of the decisions and guidelines adopted therein are unclear. Since the supervisory authorities have 
to rely on the legislation in force in their supervision, it is necessary that decisions taken have a clear 
legal effect.  
It should be clear which rules apply to which infrastructure 
It is unclear whether and how infrastructure managers can apply two different allocation regimes to 
their network (Article 18(7)).  
Clarify which infrastructure managers the proposed regulation covers  
According to the proposal, as Trafiverket understands it, it is mandatory for all infrastructure 
managers, responsible for lines that constitute the TEN-T core and extended core networks, to be 
included in the ENIM (Article 55(2)). In this regard, Trafikverket finds that ambiguities may arise 
regarding which infrastructure managers should be included in ENIM depending on how the word 
'lines' is interpreted and how the TEN-T Regulation is to be interpreted. One interpretation gives 
that in addition to Trafikverket, other infrastructure managers such as those responsible for 
connections to ports in TEN-T are also included. If so, is it reasonable for these infrastructure 
managers (e.g., municipalities) to participate in ENIM?

The regulation strengthens the role of infrastructure managers. This needs to be balanced by 
strengthening tools/measures for regulators and ensuring that the process is managed in a

companies to be able to use railway, invest in locomotives, wagons and other resources. Framework

needs to be clarified what role framework agreements will have in the new capacity process in 
relation to other forms of allocation of capacity rights. While public authorities and other

an obstacle to cross-border services or to the entry of new railway undertakings, and to new needs

other, which are listed below.  
It needs to be clarified: 
• If traffic, signed through framework agreements, has a higher priority than others it is also

efficient prioritization. 
• if the Member State's decision to prioritize certain services with the support of the regulatory 
body at national and national law takes precedence over the capacity and track works needs 
of other stakeholders.  
• what it means to consider the capacity needs of a stakeholder and/or the state. The principle 
should be that the capacity offered by infrastructure managers should meet the needs of 
stakeholders as far as possible.   
• if the stakeholder who indicates the need for capacity early in the process has a higher 
priority than the one who indicates their need at a later time.   
• what priority framework agreements have in relation to construction works, commercial 
needs and government needs for capacity. 
• whether it is possible to apply for and obtain framework agreements for cross-border 
services and, if so, who decides and assigns.

A transparent valuation model as a socio-economically efficient prioritization should apply 
throughout the capacity allocation process.    
Trafikverket has positive experience of a transparent model for socio-economic prioritization 
calculations. Trafikverket has identified that the current model needs to be developed in parts. A 
corresponding European model that also includes environmental aspects will now be jointly 
developed. This also includes assessment of emissions in comparison with other alternatives. The 
criteria in the socio-economic prioritization evaluation model in Article 8 of the proposal are good 
but incomplete in relation to the detailed criteria described in the rest of the Regulation. They need 
to be supplemented with freight traffic perspectives and further concretized by, for example, taking 
into account goods and passenger volumes carried in the trains in the proposed valuation model.  
The proposal divides the needs of passenger transport into different categories. Freight transport 
needs to be categorized in a similar way on the basis of long-term stable and short-term flexible 
needs. 
Trafikverket  notes that the Member States, nationally, will have an increased opportunity to guide

Commercial Conditions that govern the behavior of the contracting parties to the 
desired effect  
Trafikverket believes that the commercial condititons should be designed in a way and at a level that 
steer the parties' behavior in the desired direction in the form of incentives in agreements between 
the parties, but that there should also be room for the parties to compensate each other in order to 
regulate damage.  
The commercial condition model is proposed to steer the behavior of the contracting parties and 
should lead to efficient use of infrastructure capacity. The model is based on the model that is jointly 
developed within RailNetEurope. This model is planned to be introduced in 2025 on a small scale 
and further developed based on the steering effect it provides. What the European Commission is 
proposing is a compensation model, while infrastructure managers have developed an incentive 
model. Trafikverket doubts whether a model where the parties only compensate each other will have 
the desired effect.  
Commercial Conditions need to be managed as a whole, together with track access charges, fees,  
rules and incentives that steer behavior between the contracts parties to ensure that the parties 
delivers and act as intended and with good quality.  
Maintain room for negotiation in the business relationship and ensure balanced 
contracts  
The proposal requires the regulatory body to decide on the level of charges to be paid by the 
infrastructure manager (Article 40 (4). Trafikverket considers that this makes it more difficult by 
limiting the scope for negotiation in the business relationship between the parties. Furthermore, the 
proposal provides for the regulatory body, in accordance with its other tasks, to monitor and 
supervise, not to decide on its application, and should also apply to ensure that there is a balanced 
contractual relationship between the parties. If the regulatory body decides on the level, is it 
possible to appeal?

Specific characteristics of the goods - relationship with Regulation 913/2010 - "Freight 
corridor Regulation" 
According to the proposal, the freight corridors created under Regulation 913/2010 will cease in 
their current form on 9 December 2029. However, it is unclear what will replace the freight 
corridors. The EU has not abandoned the corridor approach and, among other things, in the revised 
TEN-T regulations is establishing so-called "European transport corridors", ETC. It is unclear to the 
Trafikverket how prioritization and coordination of freight traffic will work in the future. 
Trafikverket would particularly like to emphasize the importance of the marketing and quality work 
that is carried out directly towards transport buyers in the freight corridor cooperation together 
with all stakeholders. Trafikverket sees great growth potential for goods with, for example, ongoing 
industrial establishments and has experienced that market, continuity planning, quality work and 
prioritized attractive capacity in the corridor have been important in enabling growth. The 
established cooperation between the actors that currently exists with the aim of securing transport 
buyers who need to transport goods takes place through, among other things, advisory groups for 
freight companies and terminals (RAG and TAG). This interaction has not been taken into account 
in the proposal. The priority that freight has been attributed by the Freight Corridor Regulation has 
forced cross-border collaborations as in ScanMed, which has, for example, resulted in better 
transport time/commercial speed for freight trains and safeguarded the freight.  
The new Rolling Planning procedure is still under development, has not been tested in practice and 
may not be sufficient to meet freight transport demand in strategic capacity planning. There is a 
risk, when goods are not highlighted, that freight transport will have worse conditions in relation to 
today. 
Overall responsibility for quality work with all stakeholders 
According to the proposal, infrastructure managers should take overall responsibility for quality 
work together with relevant stakeholders, both in terms of objectives, analysis, identification and 
follow-up of quality-enhancing measures aimed at fulfilling the purpose of the regulation and the 
common objectives. The proposal is similar to the way already applied in Sweden through the 
initiative Together for Trains in Time and in the freight corridor ScanMed, but the proposal 
includes more stakeholders.  
Company-related privacy is only possible for a limited time (2 years) in Sweden 
The proposal states that infrastructure managers shall respect the commercial confidentiality of 
information provided to them. However, according to the Swedish constitution – the Freedom of 
the Press Act (1949:105) – a document is public if it is held by an authority and is to be regarded as 
having been received by or prepared by an authority. The right to access official documents is thus 
constitutionally protected, but the Public Access to Information and Secrecy Act (2009:400) 
contains provisions that impose restrictions on this right. For example, it is possible to classify 
public documents with regard to business secrets, but only for a limited period of two years. In cases 
where the infrastructure manager is a public body such as Trafikverket, the proposal in this part 
becomes problematic, as it would not be compatible with Swedish legislation to access public 
documents after any confidentiality period of two years. It will be particularly difficult if applicants

Need for adjustment in the description of the capacity strategy 
In the description of the timeline for the development of the capability strategy under the proposal, 
a first version will be published X-60 and a first consultation will be held X-58. This will be changed 
to publication of the X-36 capability strategy and publication of the draft X-38 (in line with the TTR 
project). Most likely, this is a typo. 
Article 16 states that the capability strategy shall be continuously updated. This should be changed 
so that no updates should occur until the next process step. 
Railway legislation needs to be synchronized with public transport legislation 
The regulation can be interpreted as requiring the infrastructure manager to describe the overall 
capacity needs and to take into account the needs of all stakeholders, including in the segment of 
'commuter and regional trains'. According to public transport legislation, it is the regional public 
transport authorities that has to describe the need for regional public transport. Therefore, it may be 
reasonable that the need for the segment "commuter and regional trains", i.e., regional public 
transport, should be described by the regional public transport authorities. It should thus be 
clarified in the Regulation that the responsibility of the infrastructure manager should be to collect 
and take into account the capacity needs of all stakeholders, not to define and describe the need.  
A journey or transport is carried out from door to door, where the railway is an 
important part. 
The railway is part of a door-to-door journey and transport solution. To make this possible, a 
multimodal perspective is required based on the needs of the user (i.e., the person who has a need 
for a journey or to transport goods). From that perspective, it is important to reflect on the role of 
railways in the transport system and how the different modes of transport best can be linked to 
entire transport solutions. Coordination of the development of legislation and processes for other 
transport modes is needed for coherent intermodal transport planning.

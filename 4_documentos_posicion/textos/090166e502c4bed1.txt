CountEmissionsEU 
SNCF Group position 
9 October 2023 
 
The European Commission has put forward a proposal for a common methodology 
for greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions in the transport sector that goes in the right 
direction, but that is not strong enough to generate a real change in the behavior of 
travelers and shippers. To reach its full potential in providing comprehensive and 
objective information to customers as widely as possible, CountEmissionsEU must 
not just be a voluntary tool, but must become mandatory for all modes of transport, 
including private car s. Furthermore, the "well -to-wheel" approach is insufficient to 
properly represent the impact of transport and  needs to be extended to vehicle 
manufacturing and maintenance. Co-legislators must seize this opportunity to make 
CountEmissionsEU an effective lever for modal shift, and thus reduce GHG emissions 
from one of the most polluting sectors. 
 
In its scientific opinion on setting an EU-wide climate target for 2040, the European Scientific Advisory 
Board on Climate Change stresses that decarbonizing the t ransport sector "requires a change in 
behavior". While transport is the only sector where GHG emissions have increased over the last 
three decades, with a 33.5% rise between 1990 and 2019, it is indeed urgent to change behavior 
by making available to trans port travelers and shippers all the information relating to the 
environmental impact of their journeys and goods transports . This is all the more true given that 
emissions come mainly from road transport (71.7%), for which there is enormous potential for modal 
shift to low-carbon modes such as rail. Rail transport's share of emissions is 0.4%  only, even though 
it accounts for 8% of passenger transport and 19% of freight transport in Europe.  
The SNCF Group supports the CountEmissionsEU initiative, which aims to encourage people and 
companies to use sustainable modes of transport. This initiative is a first step towards achieving a 
level playing field across the European Union, as it will demonstrate the comparative advantage of 
rail over other modes of transport in terms of GHGs. In force since October 2013 in France, the 
obligation to provide customers with information on GHG emissions applies to all transport service 
providers – a case which should fuel the current reflections. 
The SNCF Group's main recommendations are: 
• Make GHG emissions reporting mandatory for all entities covered by CountEmissionsEU; 
• Ensure that all modes of transport are covered, including private cars; 
• Extend the scope of ISO to the vehicle manufacturing and maintenance phases; 
• Provide a harmonized format for output data , to ensure that it can be communicated and 
compiled. 
 
Recommendations of the SNCF Group 
 
Make GHG emissions reporting mandatory for all entities organizing or providing 
transport services 
Applying CountEmissionsEU only to large transport service providers who choose to disclose their 
emissions is largely insufficient. The modal shift induced by CountEmissionsEU will be greater if 
GHG emissions accounting and reporting becomes manda tory for all transport service providers .

Online platforms offering transport services should also be obliged to disclose the GHG emissions 
of all modes of transport proposed for the journeys they offer. 
 
Ensuring that all modes of transport are taken into account 
While CountEmissionsEU aims to create significant behavioral changes for passengers and 
companies, the scheme falls short of the desired ambition by only applying to companies organizing 
freight and passenger transport services, thus excluding private cars and private jets. In so doing, the 
EU will miss the opportunity to encourage many road transport customers who will not be in a 
position to know the impact of their journey and comprehensively compare all transport options . It 
is therefore essential that passenger cars - which in the EU account for 60.6% of emissions from the 
road transport sector, which in turn accounts for 71.7% of tran sport sector emissions - are brought 
within the scope of the regulation in order to be even more effective in promoting modal shift. This 
will highlight low -carbon transport options such as rail and, ultimately, encourage consumers to 
choose their transport and delivery options with greater consideration for environmental issues.  
To make the inclusion of the private car a reality, digital multimodal mobility services (distributors, 
itinerary planners, comparators, etc.) could be required to display the emis sions associated with 
each mode available for a given journey. This point could also be taken into account in the future 
regulation on MDMS. 
Not covering all modes of transport will also pose an equity issue since the burden of calculating 
GHG emissions will be imposed on some modes but not all. Above all, the SNCF Group insists on the 
need for all modes to calculate their GHG emissions on the basis of the same methodology.  
It will be important for small carriers – especially urban and intercity carriers – to be supported to meet 
these obligations. 
 
Include GHG emissions from vehicle manufacturing and maintenance   
CountEmissionsEU is based on a "well-to-wheel" approach. We believe that the regulation must go 
beyond this scope, which does not refl ect the true extent of GHG emissions from different modes 
of transport: GHG emissions from vehicle manufactur ing and maintenance must also be covered . 
This is the only way to ensure a fair comparison between modes of transport, given that a relatively 
large proportion of GHG emissions can take place at these stages (in the case of electric cars, for 
example, most GHG emissions are generated at the manufacturing stage, even though they are 
considered to generate no GHG emissions during use). 
Ultimately, this scope extension will benefit vehicles with a long lifespan and high usage and promote 
material savings and the use of recycled materials, in line with the EU's circular economy action plan. 
One way of extending the scope of CountEmissionsEU without modifying ISO 14083:2023 is to 
include GHG emissions data relating to vehicle manufactur ing and maintenance directly in the 
databases to be established under CountEmissionsEU. Another possible approach is to include, 
directly in the regulation, th e methodology applicable to the calculation of GHG emissions from 
vehicle manufacturing and maintenance (French public green transition agency ADEME has already 
developed it) - or to provide for it to be established later by delegated act. 
This more ambiti ous and objective approach should only apply if passenger cars are covered by 
CountEmissionsEU. Otherwise, it will put the railways at a disadvantage, as they will have to take into 
account their actual life-cycle emissions, while passenger cars will not be subject to any requirements 
and their manufacturers will communicate about zero-emission vehicles. Finally, non-CO2 effects – a 
subject not covered by the ISO standard  – such as condensation trails forming in the sky as aircraft 
pass overhead, have a sig nificant impact on global warming and should therefore be taken into

Shortening the time frame for the entry into application of the regulation 
As proposed by the European Commission, the CountEmissionsEU regulation will apply after 42 
months after its entry into force. Although transport providers should be given time to organise 
themselves to comply with the methodology proposed by CountEmissionsEU, this is far too long 
compared to the climate emergency that requires the rapid decarbonisation of the transport sector. 
A lot of data related to emission factors and emission intensity is already available, which allows the 
CountEmissionsEU databases to be established in a much shorter timeframe. 
 
Provide a harmonized format for output data to ensure that it can be reported and 
compiled 
The format of the output data is not regulated by the ISO standard. However, it is of the utmost 
importance that the information provided by transport providers is provided in a harmonised, 
simple form that can be compiled even if it comes from different carriers. This is to allow users of 
transport services to easily measure the environmental impact of their journeys.  
A harmonised format could also allow transport operators to provide their customers with a periodic 
carbon footprint in order to better account for the impact of cumulative journeys.  
Finally, the CountEmissionsEU Regulation should provide for information o n GHG emissions to be 
communicated both before the purchase, in order to inform the customer's choice, and after the 
purchase of the transport service. 
 
Verification of output data 
With regard to the verification of output data, we suggest that bodies accr edited for CSRD auditing 
should also be able to verify the compliance of the data calculated under CountEmissionsEU.

Exclude all forms of carbon offsetting from CountEmissionsEU 
GHG emissions calculated under CountEmissionsEU should not be eligible for carbon offsetting . 
This would distort the comparison between modes of transport and call into question the credibility 
of CountEmissionsEU. 
As part of the "market -based" approach for electricity, it is possible to assert Power Purchase 
Agreements (PPAs) as well as Guarante es of Origin (GO). A great deal of vigilance is required with 
regard to the possibility of claiming GOs in a manner dissociated from PPAs . While PPAs allow for 
the additional production of energy from renewable sources, GOs only recognise an already existing 
supply of renewable energy. Thus, if transmission service providers can claim to use renewable 
electricity by sourcing only in GO and not PPAs, then this discourages them from engaging in PPAs 
and de facto does not promote the development of renewable e nergy. Similarly, users are not 
properly informed whether these two products can be used equivalently.  
 
Include electricity emission factors in the European database to facilitate the 
location-based approach 
For electricity, the ISO standard provides in the location -based approach that GHG emissions from 
the electricity consumed can be quantified at the local, regional or national level. It is essential that 
the database provided for by the CountEmissionsEU Regulation includes emission factors based on 
the average energy mix at national level – according to a common methodology at the EU level. This 
will ensure uniformity in GHG emissions reporting by transport operators across the EU.  
This scenario could be a pplied to other energy carriers using an infrastructure network (hydrogen, 
gas/biogas).

About SNCF GROUP 
SNCF group is a world leader in passenger and freight transport services. Its corporate purpose is 
“working for a dynamic, caring, sustainable societ y”. The group has 270,000 employees in 120 
countries and a turnover of 41.4 billion euros in 2022, of which a third abroad. The SNCF group is 
organised around a single parent company and includes SNCF Réseau (management and operation 
of the French rail network), SNCF Gares & Connexions (management and development of stations), 
Rail Logistics Europe (rail freight transport and logistics), SNCF Voyageurs (regional and intercity 
public transport, high -speed rail in France and Europe), Geodis (logistics in Fran ce and 120 
countries) and Keolis (public transport in Europe and worldwide).

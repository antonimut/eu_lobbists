Kimberly-Clark’s position on the revision of the Waste Framework 
Directive  
 
Kimberly-Clark produces Personal Protection Equipment (PPE) for professional use, which 
encompasses protective apparel, gloves of different compositions and protective 
eyewear. Many of these products are single use and used, for example, in clean room 
environments and/or to protect workers from harmful and hazardous substances used in an 
industrial environment.   
 
We welcome the European Commission’s stated goals in its proposal to reduce environmental 
and climate impacts, increase environment quality and improve public health associated with 
textiles waste management through better post-consumer collection and recycling across the 
EU, curbing the negative impact of fast fashion. 
 
However, we consider that the use and circularity challenges of single-use PPE used in labs, 
clean rooms and industrial environments are very different from fast-fashion. The recyclability 
and reuse potential of PPE varies greatly dependi ng on their properties, uses, and risk of 
contamination. If PPE are contaminated during use, they must subsequently be incinerated.  
 
To address the waste from  our PPE, Kimberly-Clark established the RightCycle™ program 
through which we collect non -contaminated PPE and other products from our customers’ 
facilities and ensure they are recycled.   We are currently implementing RightCycle TM for 
scientific apparel and nitrile gloves in several countries (UK, Ireland, Spain, France, Belgium, 
Netherlands, Austria, Switzerland and Germany), and are working hard to scale it up for all 
products and markets.   
 
The current European Commission proposal exempts some but not all PPE products from the 
scope of the new EPR provisions for textiles, without a ny rationale given for this approach. 
For example, Kimberly -Clark’s nitrile gloves  (CN code 4015…)  are out of scope whereas 
nitrile-coated gloves (CN 6116…) are included (please see below).

Kimberly-Clark calls for all PPE products to be exempt from the new EPR requirements on 
textiles in the revised EU Waste Framework Directive  as such products are used in an 
industrial or commercial environment and  will not function well in the EPR schemes that will 
emerge from this legislation as the volumes, materials used and distribution channels are very 
different; they often need to be single use; and they often become contaminated and must be 
incinerated.    
 
Kimberly-Clark thus recommends an amendment to Article 22a (“EPR provisions for textiles”) 
clarifying that:  
 
Personal Protection Equipment (PPE) including protective apparel, gloves of different 
compositions, and protective eyewear, which are designed for professional use in 
medical and/or industrial settings and provide protection against exposure to heat, 
chemicals, biological and cutting hazards, shall be exempt from these EPR provisions.  
 
Furthermore, we recommend amendments to the Annex IVc “Household textile products, and 
textile articles of apparel and clothing accessories that fall within the scope of Article 22a”.  
 
The Annex includes all of Chapter 61 (“Articles of apparel and clothing accessories, knitted or 
crocheted”) and Chapter 62 (“Articles of apparel and clothing accessories, not knitted or 
crocheted”). As such it covers not only consumer textiles / fast fashion but also PPE used in 
commercial and industrial environments.  Consequently, we believe Kimberly -Clark PPE

Introduction 
 
Fleet Cards Europe (“FCE”) welcomes the introduction of the EU Greening Transport 
Package, which was published by the European Commission on 11 July 2023. FCE members 
fully support the aim underlying these measures - to increase sustainability within the transport 
sector and help it cut CO2 emissions by 90% by 2050 compared with 1990 - as set forth in the 
European Green Deal. As an industry, transport was responsible for 26% of all EU greenhouse 
gas (GHG) emissions in 2020, with road transport alone accounting for 20% of that total1. It is 
thus incumbent on our industry to support efforts enabling the sector to decarbonise further. 
 
Of the three initiatives put forward, “CountEmissions EU” is of particular interest to FCE 
members as it will have a direct impact on our customers, i.e., European freight and passenger 
service providers, many of whom are family-owned SMEs. In the EU, approx. 80% of the over 
one million commercial road transport companies fall into this category 2; it is on behalf of 
these, our core customers, that we wish to emphasise the following arguments.  
 
A common framework 
 
If you can’t measure it, you can’t manage it. To this end, it makes sense to propose a common 
methodology which will standardise the calculation of GHG emissions in the EU  transport 
sector and ideally also beyond. This must be our starting point if we are to successfully meet 
the ambitious reduction targets rightly set by the EU in the Green Deal and Fit-for-55 Package. 
 
At present, there is no universally accepted framework for the calculation of GHG emissions 
in transport.  This often leads to a signif icant discrepancy in results that compromises the 
comparability of GHG emission figures on the market and provides inaccurate and misleading 
information on a transport service’s performance 3. FCE members agree  with the need to 
devise a harmonised methodol ogical framework for accounting emissions of transport 
services via tools certified by a conformity assessment body. 
 
Scope  
 
As mentioned, it should be noted that the European commercial road transport sector is 
primarily composed of family-owned SMEs with small fleets that enable trade flows within the 
single market 4. Often operat ing on meagre margins due to high fuel prices and driver 
shortages, they must also remain competitive with their non-EU peers. It is th us essential to 
strike a balance to ensure the transport sector moves towards climate neutrality, whilst also

FCE members therefore support the fact that reporting under the proposed regulation will be 
voluntary, with only those entities that choose or are mandated  by other means to calculate 
and disclose GHG emissions required to the adhere to the common EU methodology. The 
entire transport sector is currently undergoing a major transformation in shifting from fossil 
fuels to cleaner energies; whilst the industry is committed to embracing change  and is 
investing in solutions to measure and cut emissions, this should be on the understanding that 
it will take time to implement. Commercial road transporters should be given sufficient time to 
make the adjustments required of them in the medium and long-term.  
 
As an association, we believe it will ultimately become the norm for commercial transport 
companies to report their emissions because the shippers, partners, and customers they work 
with will increasingly demand it of them. In the fuel card sector, customers are increasingly 
requiring fuel card companies (their suppliers) to provide detailed information on well-to-wheel 
(WtW) emissions as part of procurement processes, which naturally necessitates a degree of 
flexibility in the use of  both primary and secondary data when calculating and reporting on 
GHG transport-related emissions. We are of the view this trend will continue unabated and 
that, sooner or later, it will become necessary for all suppliers to disclose this information. In 
short, we believe this change - with the development of a common framework - will occur 
“organically” rather than through a hard imposition. As sustainability inevitably becomes a key 
criterion for profitability and growth in the transport sector, so too it will form an integral part of 
corporate strategies. To accelerate this process, it is vital that transport operators can be sure 
all commercial data they disclose will be treated confidentially and not, for instance, shared 
with competent authorities and/or third parties upon request. Transport operators 
understandably want to ensure that any data transmitted will be appropriately safeguarded.  
 
Methodology  
 
FCE members welcome the fact that the common methodology being proposed will be based 
on ISO 14083:20235 which is based on the GHG protocol  and could further be enhanced by 
a framework such as the GLEC. If we are to calculate and report on emissions, then we must 
do so from WtW, considering energy consumption and GHG emissions throughout an entire 
lifecycle, from production, distribution, and supply through to consumption. Only then will the 
emissions reported by EU transport companies provide a transparent and accurate overview. 
A WtW approach does, however, create additional complexity compared with a tank-to-wheel 
approach since a greater number of parameters must be considered, meaning there must be 
reliable default GHG emission factors on the planned central EU database allow ing for 
accurate calculations. Ultimately, such an approach will only work  provided all stakeholders 
involved - from a fuel’s extraction to  its consumption - collaborate fully and disclose their 
emissions. This obligation should not just rest with transport companies, but also those further 
“upstream” in the supply chain.

The fact that ISO 14083:2023 is recognised worldwide and can thus be used to generate data 
on GHG emissions for international transport chains creates an opportunity for global 
alignment, recognising that many European hauliers operate beyond EU borders. Ultimately, 
if Europe is to pioneer the setting of GHG emissions reporting standards, it should do so with 
the aim of shaping a universal standard given the interconnected nature of supply chains and 
the global threat posed by climate change.  
 
 
 
Closing remarks 
 
In summary, FCE members would like to underscore the following: 
 
▪ a harmonised methodological framework for the accounting of GHG emissions, based 
on both primary and secondary data, is needed if the transport sector is to meet ambitious 
GHG reduction targets; 
 
▪ reporting requirements should remain voluntary, as currently proposed, so as not to 
burden the many small and medium-sized European transport companies and allow 
for a gradual uptake of the reporting of GHG emissions;  
 
▪ if the transport sector is to truly decarbonise, calculations should be based on a well -
to-wheel methodology  looking at the entire lifecycle of a fuel . To allow for accurate 
calculations, a central EU database must provide reliable default GHG emission factors;   
 
▪ the proposed framework should be based on globally recognised standards , 
providing a potential blueprint for other jurisdictions beyond the EU to follow suit. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
About FCE 
Fleet Cards Europe (FCE)  represents the independent fuel card sector in Europe, which 
facilitates the access and supply of fuel and other related on-the-road goods and services for 
millions of commercial users across Europe. FCE members represen t a major share of the 
B2B fuel cards market in Europe and our membership base includes key independent players 
in this market which are headquartered and operate across the continent. Most European 
logistics, transportation and other fleet businesses rely  on the services (issuing, billing, fuel 
discounts, etc.) provided by FCE members, helping them to move goods and people efficiently 
and seamlessly across Europe on a daily basis. For further information, please contact the 
FCE Secretariat at: fleetcardseurope@pentagroup.co 
FCE stands ready to engage with policymakers and regulators to further explain the business 
models of its members, focusing also on the mechanics of t he functioning of the card or any 
alternative device used for the identification of the user of the services.

Cefic Fertilisers Efficiency Enhancers comments to the Directive proposal on Soil Monitoring and 
Resilience 
 
 
Fertilisers Efficiency Enhancers, a sector group of Cefic representing  the value chain of nitrogen 
stabilisers and other fertiliser enhancers in Europe, welcomes the opportunity to provide input to the 
proposal for a Directive on Soil Monitoring and Resilience (Soil Monitoring Law) (2023/0232 (COD)), 
referred to as ‘legislative proposal' in this document). 
Efficiency enhancers are a key element of agronomic sustainability: they enable an improved 
efficiency of fertilising products by ensuring better climate protection and increasing yield potential1.    
As a sector group of Cefic, we would like to provide our opinion from an agricultural soil perspective, 
whilst endorsing the input shared by Cefic.  
Definitions (Art 3) 
We are concerned with the definition of ‘healthy soil’ in the Commission’s proposal as it lacks 
distinction between different soil and land uses (e.g. soil use for industrial activities, road 
construction, food production). We consider that the definition of healthy soil should incorporate the 
land use purpose of the soil. The risk-assessment and the ‘health’ designation should also take those 
different end-uses of soil into account accordingly.  
In the present proposal, contaminated sites with a demonstrably unacceptable risk to humans and 
the environment are conceptually equated to “potentially contaminated sites”, meaning those sites 
for which such a risk is possible but not proven. Hence, both are defined as "contaminated sites". A 
corresponding distinction is urgently needed to differentiate the two and the definition of the soil 
contamination in the proposal needs to take into account whether there is evidence that the 
substance poses an unacceptable risk to human health and environment. 
Assessment of soil health (Art 9)  
It is of outmost importance to acknowledge nutrient content as a fundamental parameter for 
assessing and maintaining soils' health, as it directly influences the overall fertility and productivity of 
agricultural lands. 
The proposed definition of ‘unhealthy soil’ implies that if one of the criteria is not met, the soil will 
automatically be considered as unhealthy. Depending on the number of indicators applicable, 
complying with all those descriptors at the same time can be challenging. Moreover, it will make it 
difficult to show progress over time on the status of soil health in a specific district2.

From an agricultural perspective, soil fertility should be considered as a key indicator of soil health 
and should be defined in a clear and realistic manner.  A sufficient amount of nutrients must be 
returned to the soil via fertilisation to ensure sufficient nutrition for the plants while maintaining 
fertile conditions and preventing soil degradation.   
Moreover, to avoid that the Soil Monitoring Law unintentionally overlaps or is inconsistent with other 
existing pieces of legislation, we suggest that the reference in Annex 1 (“Concentration of a selection 
of organic pollutants”), does not apply to substances approved in the EU under the Fertilising 
Products Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2019/1009).   
Last but not least, in our view Nutrient Use efficiency (NUE) should be included in the list of 
Sustainable Soil management principles since it ensures the reduction of nutrient losses from mineral 
and organic fertilisers, contributing to reducing their use and consequently bringing benefits to 
climate and the environment.  The use of Urease and Nitrification Inhibitors improves NUE increasing 
soil health and securing yield.  
Risk-based approach (Art 12) 
We support the principle of a risk-based approach and sustainable risk management. A risk-based 
approach consistent with how the land is used, or is planned to be used according to its purpose 
should apply to soil investigation and remediation measures.  
Annex I part B 
Concerning soil contamination, we consider that in Annex I part B the criteria for healthy soil 
conditions should be clarified in order to connect them to a risk assessment methodology.  
A risk assessment taking into account the purpose/land use of the soil (based on the methodology 
defined according to the Member State) should be the basis for the definition of soil contamination.  
This way a site-specific evaluation can be performed, and focus can be put on the relevant hotspots 
where risks cannot be excluded. 
We thank you for your attention, and remain available for further information.

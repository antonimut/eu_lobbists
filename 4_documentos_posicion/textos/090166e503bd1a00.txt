Directive on Soil Monitoring and Resiliance –  
comments from the Swedish Forest Industries’ (SFIF)
Background
The Swedish forest industry is based on the sustai-
nable utilisation of a renewable, accruing and main-
ly European natural resource – wood. In accordance 
with the principles of the circular economy, the 
forest industry uses wood to manufacture products 
that serve as carbon storage, are recyclable and can 
be utilised in energy production at the end of their 
lifecycle.
The Swedish forest industry is committed to 
contributing to making Europe the world’s first 
climate-neutral continent by 2050. This can best 
be achieved by transitioning from a linear economy 
that is based on non-renewable natural resources 
to a circular bioeconomy that is based on renewa-
ble raw materials. These measures must also be 
possible within the framework of the Directive on 
Soil Monitoring and Resilience. In addition signifi-
cant climate benefits will be achieved by switching 
from the use of non-renewable materials to the use 
of renewable materials.
Furthermore, the forest industry is strongly com-
mitted to preventing biodiversity loss by promoting 
nature management in commercial forests and the 
exclusion of the most valuable sites when conduc-
ting harvesting operations.
Healthy soils are the basis for all land use, in-
cluding sustainable forest management, in terms 
of climate, biodiversity and soil productivity alike. 
In sustainable forest management, taking soil into 
account means planning and implementing forest 
management in such a way that the soil’s capacity 
to produce various ecosystem services is maintai-
ned in the long term.

Position by SFIF:
Main arguments
The Swedish Forest Industries Federation share the 
following position with the Finnish Forest  
Industries Federation. The Directive proposed 
by the European Commission pertains to all land 
areas within the EU. The directive is new legislati -
on that brings soil firmly within the scope of com -
mon EU legislation. 
The Swedish Forest Industries Federation 
welcomes the fact that soil resilience is promoted 
through EU legislation. The Swedish forest  
industry and the manufacture of wood-based  
products are based on wood raw materials obtain -
ed from sustainably managed forests. Healthy and 
resilient soil contributes to securing the vitality 
and growth of forests and, consequently, the suffi -
ciency of wood raw material in the long term. 
In the further preparation of the directive, the 
aim must be to achieve an outcome that secures the 
resilience of soil, the self-sufficiency of wood raw 
material for European industry, and global compe -
titiveness. In order to achieve appropriate and pre -
dictable legislation, the Swedish Forest Industries 
Federation emphasises that due consideration 
should be given to the following aspects in the  
further work on the Commission’s proposal:
1. Ensure predictability of legislation and 
compliance with the subsidiarity principle;
2. Rationalise the monitoring framwork and 
assess the suitability of the criteria; and
3. Re-examine the proposed sustainable soil 
management principles

compliance with the subsidiarity principle

the EU, for which reason a directive is an appropri -
ate instrument for legislation on soil monitoring 
and resilience. Leeway between Member States is 
very important. However, the proposed directive 
states that the Commission has the power of

The Swedish Forest Industries Federation

legislation. The implementing and delegated powers

an ordinary legislative procedure. In addition, the

will enable the implementation of best practices 
enhancing the long-term resilience of soils at a local

The legislation will have a direct impact on the 
use of forests and on the wood-processing industry, 
which is why it must already be predictable when 
it enters into force. Unlike on agricultural soils, for 
example, measures affecting the soil are only carried 
out a few times in a hundred years in an actively 
managed forest. In forest soils, changes occur very 
slowly over a long term, while on the other hand, in 
the short term, the change can be significant. This 
should be given due consideration in the implemen-
tation of legislation. Legislation should encourage 
long-term work to promote good soil condition, 
with due consideration given to national special 
characteristics and the history of land use.
In order to avoid unclear and even misguided 
interpretations, the content of the directive must be 
clear. In the draft directive, Article 23 on penalties 
is unclear as far as its grounds and targeting are 
concerned, and it will have to be amended in further 
preparation to ensure clarity and specificity.  
1.2  Rationalise the monitoring framework 
and assess the suitability of the criteria 
The directive proposal sets out a detailed monito -
ring framework of soil health, including an obli -
gation to report detailed data to the Commission 
and the European Environment Agency (EEA). The 
Swedish Forest Industries Federation considers the 
development and collection of soil data important, 
but holds that from the perspective of forests, the 
obligations seem excessive in terms of, for example, 
the reporting frequency. Hardly any changes will 
take place in forest soils in five years.
With regard to the criteria for healthy soil  
condition, the Swedish Forest Industries Federa -
tion notes that some of the criteria involve sig -
nificant uncertainties. An example of this is soil 
organic carbon (SOC) in organic soils, the amount 
of which is difficult to measure. The uncertainty is 
reflected in the reliability of results and reporting. 
An extensive Soil Health Benchmarks research pro -
ject has been commenced in Europe with the aim of  
developing indicators and limit values for different 
soil ecosystems in cooperation with stakeholders. 
For this reason, the criterion indicating change 
in the amount of organic carbon in organic soils 
should only be outlined when the investigation has 
been brought to completion. 
Both existing and future regulations attaching 
the land use in the EU require increased monito -
ring and reporting by Member States. The Swedish 
Forest Industries Federation emphasises the 
cost-effectiveness of monitoring and confidence in 
the ability of the Member States to produce har -
monised data. Monitoring should primarily make 
use of the expertise of national research institutes 
and existing national systems. The Swedish Forest 
Industries Federation emphasises that the monito -
ring of forest soil condition and resilience should be 
incorporated as part of the National Forest  
Inventory. This would ensure both cost-  
effectiveness and a better assessment of the 
cause-and-effect relationships between different 
measurement results obtained from forests and 
forest soils.
In addition, the directive proposal requires that 
the data collected under it must be public. In order 
to guarantee the property rights, it is questionable 
to require that geospatial data for monitoring the 
condition of soil be published openly on, for  
example, the Internet. The degree of publicity of 
raw data should also be re-considered.
1.3  Re-examine the proposed sustainable 
soil management principles
The directive proposal lists the principles of sustai-
nable land use, using which Member States define 
good and bad practices in terms of soil. Sustainable 
soil management practices must be adopted and 
practices that negatively affect soil health must be 
abandoned.

hasises the development of measures that promote 
the resilience of soil in the long term. In Finland,

Climatic and growth conditions may change sig-

be noted that reaching the targets requires several 
methods of land use, which must be possible within 
the framework of different EU legislations.
THE SWEDISH FOREST INDUSTRY is an essential contributor  
in the green transition to a more circular and biobased 
economy. The industry refines wood resources to bio-based 
products, such as pulp, paper, board, packaging material, 
sawn timber, refined wood products, biobased electricity 
and heat and advanced biofuels. The core business is  
industrial activities based on wood sourced from sustainably 
managed forests, but among the industry are also some of 
the largest private forest holdings in Europe. Any forest,  
climate, environmental, energy and product related  
European Union policy is of high importance.

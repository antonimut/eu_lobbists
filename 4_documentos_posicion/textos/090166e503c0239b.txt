FNADE’s position paper on Soil Monitoring Law 
 
October 2023 
 
FNADE, the French association for waste management and environmental services , supports the 
European Commission proposal for a Directive on Soil Monitoring and Resilience which is the first 
piece of legislation on soil health. 
Indeed, having healthy soils in Europe  is a great challenge , both to protect human health  and to 
provide agriculture with natural and organic nutrients and fertilisers. 
Waste sector is already a European key player in achieving healthy soils by 2050 and contributes to 
both objectives:  
• Soil remediation: 
Waste management companies are fully involved in the soil remediation process , developing 
constantly new technologies to remove pollution and hazardous substances contained in former 
industrial areas and contaminated soils with the aim of recovering and reusing the soils when 
possible. This activity has a very beneficial effect on health, the protection of groundwater and the 
environment in general. 
• Production of natural organic nutrients:  
By p roducing from biowaste, biomass and sewage sludges organic nutrients and fertilisers , the 
waste management sector provides natural elements needed to foster agricultural productivity and 
to avoid the use of synthetic mineral fertilisers. 
Furthermore, compost has a very positive effect on climate change  by helping restore forestry and 
agricultural lands, and storing a high quantity of carbon.  
 
Comments on the Commission’s proposal: 
Nevertheless, given the high stakes involved , FNADE regrets the draft lacks ambition by not 
proposing intermediate targets before 2050 and no methodology to reach 100% of healthy soils. 
The definition of sustainable soil management practices is to be undertaken by Member States. 
However, the proposed timeline (from four years after entry into force of the Directive) is undefined 
and lacks ambition. Hence, we suggest that within two years after the Directive’s entry into force, 
sustainable soil management practices should be defined by the Member States in a way that 
promotes and emphasises the benefits of the use of organic fertilisers. The role of composting as a 
risk reduction measure, as indicated in Annex V, should be strongly promoted.

Furthermore, FNADE would like the Directive to recall the importance of biowaste, biomass and 
sewage sludge treatment through composting or methanization to produce organic materials and 
fertilizers that will have a great benefit in restoring soils and developing of micro-organisms.  
In addition,  FNADE would like to point out that the Directive should specify the framework for 
implementing the methodologies listed in Annex II for determining or estimating the values of soil 
descriptors, in order to take into account  the parameters for analysis . For instance, regarding 
extractable phosphorus, three different methodologies can be used, depending on the pH of the 
soil analysed. In France, we use the Olsen method for a pH between 4,5 and 8,5, the Dyer method 
for a pH under 7 and the Joret-Hebert method for a pH above 7. In this case, the values obtained 
change according to the methodology used. But the values also vary depending on the type of soil 
(clay, silt , sand…) and the type of crop to be grown . Therefore,  the maximum value  set for 
extractable phosphorus should be adjusted accordingly.  
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
FNADE is the French association representing the entire waste industry value chain. As a key player in the circular

fossil energies. It provides solutions to major environmental and climate challenges.

For the consideration of the European Commission: 
The Association of Finnish Local and Regional Authorities supports 
the objectives of the European Commission’s proposal for the Di-
rective on Soil Monitoring and Resilience 
The Soil Health Directive has a multidimensional relevance for Finnish mu-
nicipalities and their land use. Finnish municipalities have a high degree of 
autonomy by European standards and are responsible for land use planning 
and the location of functions as well as construction and maintenance of 
basic infrastructure in their areas. In addition, municipal officials have li-
censing and monitoring tasks related to the scope of the Directive. 
- The Association of Finnish Local and Regional Authorities (AFLRA) sup-
ports the objective of Article 11 to monitor land take activities and to mini-
mize their harmful effects on the environment but wants to ensure that the 
Directive will allow the use natural or semi-natural land for construction or 
other infrastructure when necessary for building sustainable communities. 
It is also important that compensation of the loss of soil capacity to pro-
vide ecosystem services can take place on an aggregated level. 
Reason: The rest of Europe is further ahead of Finland and the other 
Nordic countries in terms of urbanisation and urban density. In 
sparsely populated countries, the use of undeveloped land for urban 
development may be in the environmental interest when the popula-
tion is concentrating in centres. 
- AFLRA wants to emphasise the need to take current and planned land use 
into account when conducting the risk assessment and management of 
contaminated sites (Article 15). The decision on appropriate risk reduction 
measures and their timing should be based on the site-specific risks to hu-
man health and environment, as well as the needs of current and planned 
land use.  
- Instead of the delegated acts proposed in Article 10.4 (to adapt the sus-
tainable soil management principles) and Article 15.6 (to adapt the list of 
risk reduction measures and the requirements for site-specific risk assess-
ment to scientific and technical progress), AFLRA prefers updating guid-
ance. The decisions concerning most suitable activities should be taken on 
national or local level. Local specificities are very central to soil issues, and 
participatory processes are needed to take them into account in legislation.

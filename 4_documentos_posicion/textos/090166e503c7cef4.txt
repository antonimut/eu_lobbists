CountEmissions EU: European Commission consultation response 
November 2023 
 
Eurogas welcome the possibility to provide feedback on the CountEmissions EU act adopted by the 
European Commission on the 11th of July 2023. 
 
Gaseous solutions including renewable and low carbon fuels play an increasingly important role  in 
decarbonising the transport sector, which is currently responsible for about a quarter of GHG 
emissions in the Union 1. In particular, gaseous fuels have an important role to play in the heavy-
duty transport sector. Beyond significant benefits in term s of GHG emission  reduction, gaseous 
fuels also contribute to improved air quality, especially in  congested urban areas.  Accurate and 
comparable information on environmental performance of transport services should be the 
backbone of good policy making supporting  the uptake of more sustainable transport alternatives. 
Adopting an approach not solely revolving around the engine technology is the only way to 
correctly recognise the benefits of renewable and low carbon fuels . Eurogas welcomes the 
CountEmissions EU initiative as a crucial step towards reducing greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions of 
transport services and ensuring the transparency and comparability of GHG emissions data  
reported within the transport sector.  
 
Respect for technology neutrality 
Eurogas welcomes the choice of an approach based on energy lifecycle quantification of GHG 
emissions. The benefits of such an approach are twofold:  
• The approach is consistent with the existing policy framework on renewable energy 
(Renewable Energy Directive), on emissions trading (ETS) , on maritime decarbonisation and 
increased renewable energy incorporation (FuelEU maritime). 
• The approach is appropriate to  correctly account  and report  the emissions of renewable 
fuels and to distinguish them from fossil fuels, notably in the case of biomethane.  
 
Consistency with existing standards 
Eurogas appreciates the EC’s decision to ensure a level playing field between decarbonisation 
technologies by relying on the EN ISO standard 14083:2023 as a reference methodology. This 
choice ensures consistency with internationally recognised norms.  
 
Data reliability 
Eurogas favours the prioriti sation of primary data sources, as it rewards the better performing 
transport modes and decarbonisation efforts. We also welcome the establishment of an EU -wide 
database for default values and emission factors  by the E uropean Commission and the European 
Energy Agency  when relying on secondary data . We recommend  prioritising the consistency of 
these values  with existing EU regulations, and the active engagement of the relevant 
stakeholders in the establishment of this database , notably by means of an official stakeholder 
forum. This approach has been undertaken in the context of the Renewable Energy Directive

Scope of application  
Eurogas recogni ses the importance of the uptake of GHG emission accounting in the transport 
sector while sharing the European Commission’s concern on the burden imposed  on the industry.   
To meet the objectives of the proposal and at the same time  strike the right balance  in terms of 
scope of application , Eurogas suggests exploring the mandatory application of the proposal with 
exemptions for SMEs or only for small and micro -enterprises2. Additional support measures, 
facilitation and mentoring will be required to ensure successful adoption and implementation of 
the CountEmissions EU act.

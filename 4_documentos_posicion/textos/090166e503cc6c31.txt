The European Potash Producers Association (APEP) wishes to express our support for 
the European Commission's proposed Directive on Soil Monitoring and Resilience. We 
believe that this initiative is a significant step towards improving the conditions of 
European soils, thus achieving alignment with the EU Zero Pollution ambition. 
 
We recogni se that there is significant soil variability between each Member State, 
characterised by varying soil types, conditions, and agricultural practices. The rich 
diversity in soil characteristics and local conditions necessitates a flexible approach that 
accommodates these differences. Setting uniform soil health indicator values for such a 
wide range of soil types and conditions would be counterproductive, as it may lead to 
unrealistic standards in some regions while falling short in others. It is c rucial to 
acknowledge and respect the unique challenges and opportunities presented by the 
varying landscapes within the EU.  
 
• Soil Health Indicators 
 
We believe that a more nuanced and region-specific or even site-specific approach to soil 
health indicators would better serve the goal of achieving healthy soils across the Union 
while acknowledging the inherent differences in the Member States' soil profiles and 
needs. It should be considered , inter alia,  that some soil areas have been affected by 
industrial activities in the past or contain elevated contents of certain substances due to 
geogeny or historical land use . These historical factors must adequately be taken into 
account when determining the state of soils.  We believe that the inclusion of  a 
corresponding regulation  would ensure that those factors are considered adequately.  
There might be cases where it is not possible not proportionate to bring all soils to a 
healthy status.

We understand the importance of establishing clear and effective standards for soil 
health, but it is equally essential that these standards accurately reflect the conditions 
under which crops can thrive. We propose that further scientific research and consultation 
with experts in the field be considered to ensure that these thresholds are set at levels 
that promote both soil health and agricultural sustainability. 
 
• Relationship with other EU legislation 
 
The relationship between the proposed Directive on Soil Monitoring and Resilience  and 
other EU directives remains unclear. We appreciate the existence of specific legislation 
for environmental protection such as the currently revised  Emissions Directive, the 
Directive on Waste, Landfill Directive, Mining Waste Directive. As specific legislation s, 
they should take precedence over the regulations of the Directive on Soil Monitoring and 
Resilience, and we hope that this is reflected in the current proposal. 
 
• Access to data 
 
Regarding Chapter IV, Article 16, we are concerned about the planned full publication of 
the register of contaminated and potentially contaminated sites. Since the information is 
geographically explicit and includes information on private land, we oppose unrestricted 
access to all data. 
 
• Replace the One-Out-All-Out criterion with a Soil Health Index 
 
Article 9 of the proposed Directive states that all soils which do not respect at least one 
of the criteria set out by the proposal itself must be considered unhealthy, thus introducing 
a one out -all out criterion to assess soil health. APEP considers that r elying only on a 
single criterion to assess soil health overlooks the complexity of soil itself and fails to 
provide a complete analysis and a reliable assessment of the different aspects at play.  
 
To provide a more accurate assessment of soil health, we advise replacing such criterion 
with a Soil Health Index, which would represent a more context-specific approach. Such 
an index would encompass multiple indicators and reflect the overall health and quality 
of the soil, taking into account its physical, chemical, and biological properties.

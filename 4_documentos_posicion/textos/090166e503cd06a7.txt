ECOFI comments on the Proposal for a Soil Monitoring Directive 
and its Annexes 
 
ECOFI welcomes the European Commission’s legislative proposal for a Soil Monitoring 
Directive and its stated aim to enhance the health of one of our most valuable assets: our 
soil. ECOFI also welcomes the opportunity to provide feedback on the Proposal and its 
Annexes. 
 
By definition, organic-based fertilisers (OBFs) and soil improvers add organic matter to soils, 
improving their health and fertility. As the European organisation representing the organic-
based fertiliser industry in Europe, ECOFI would like to offer the following comments relating 
to the Soil Monitoring Directive. 
 
ECOFI welcomes a harmonised definition of soil health and a comprehensive monitoring and 
assessment framework but is concerned about the proposed one-size-fits-all approach 
 
ECOFI would like to raise its concerns regarding the importance of recognising and 
respective soil diversity across the Union. In this respect, ECOFI laments the ‘one-out-all-out’ 
approach, which it does not deem appropriate for the varied soil types and land uses which 
exist across Europe. This approach, which labels a soil as unhealthy if it fails in just one 
category, is overly simplistic, and fails to capture the complex nature of soil health. Accurate 
data collection and measurement is the foundation of sustainable soil management. 
 
ECOFI therefore calls on the EU institutions to incorporate flexibility in choosing soil health 
indicators to be included in this Directive, allowing for appropriate regional and use 
adaptations. 
 
ECOFI calls for financial support for farmers and land managers to implement these practices 
and conduct soil monitoring activities, as well as invest in sustainable soil management 
practices as listed in Annex III. 
 
ECOFI calls on the European Commission to take proactive measures to provide support, 
technical assistance, and resources to ensure a cohesive implementation of the Soil 
Monitoring Law across Member States.  
 
The current proposal introduces provisions that also cover industrial soils which are already 
subject to regulation under various pieces of existing legislation. To avoid unnecessary 
burden and ensure effectiveness, ECOFI underlines the importance of avoiding double 
legislation and ensuring clarity and coherence of requirements in the Soil Monitoring 
Directive with existing industrial regulations.

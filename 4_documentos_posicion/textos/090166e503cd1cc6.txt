European Biostimulants Industry Council 
on the Proposal for a Soil Monitoring Directive and its Annexes 
See: https://ec.europa.eu/info/law/better-regulation/have-your-say/initiatives/13350-Soil-
health-protecting-sustainably-managing-and-restoring-EU-soils_en  
EBIC welcomes the European Commission’s legislative proposal for a Soil Monitoring Directive and its 
stated aim to enhance the health of one of our most valuable assets: our soil. EBIC also welcomes the 
opportunity to provide feedback on the Proposal and its Annexes. 
Plant biostimulants applied to the soil can help optimise nutrient inputs while maintaining or improving 
soil health and fertility. As the European organisation representing the plant biostimulants industry  in 
Europe, EBIC would like to offer the following comments relating to the Soil Monitoring Directive. 
EBIC welcomes a harmonised definition of soil health and a comprehensive monitoring and 
assessment framework but is concerned about the proposed one-size-fits-all approach 
Getting the data right 
While EBIC supports the objective and general approach of the Proposal for a Directive, EBIC would like 
to raise its concerns regarding the importance of recognising and respecting  soil diversity across the 
European Union. EBIC therefore  laments the ‘one -out-all-out’ approach, which it does not deem 
appropriate for the varied soil types and land uses which exist across Europe. This approach, which 
labels a soil as unhealthy if it fails in just one category, is overly simplistic, and fails to capture the 
complex nature of soil health. Accurate data collection and measurement is the foundation of 
sustainable soil management, so we need to get this right. 
EBIC therefore calls on the EU institutions to incorporate flexibility in choosing soil health indicators 
to be included in this Directive, allowing for appropriate regional and use adaptations. 
Level playing field 
To prevent disparities, EBIC also calls on the European Commission to take proactive measures to 
provide support, technical assistance, and appropriate resources to ensure a cohesive 
implementation of the Soil Monitoring Law across Member States . EBIC is therefore in favour 
of the idea of a Working Group involving Member States and relevant stakeholders  to facilitate a 
coherent implementation of measures in similar soil districts across the EU. 
Regulatory consistency 
The current proposal introduces provisions that  also cover industrial soils which are already subject to 
regulation under various pieces of  existing legislation. To avoid unnecessary burden  and ensure 
effectiveness, EBIC underscores the importance of avoiding double legislation and ensur ing 
clarity and coherence of requirements in the Soil Monitoring Directive with existing 
industrial regulations. 
Support for farmers 
EBIC also calls for financial support for farmers and land managers to implement these practices 
and conduct soil monitoring activities, as well as invest in sustainable soil management practices as 
listed in Annex III.

   
 
Union Customs Code Reform 
Feedback on Proposal by VNO-NCW | MKB-Nederland 
 
The Dutch business community embraces the simplifications introduced by the European Commission 
in the proposal for the New UCC. Based on a data-led approach the reform can reduce cumbersome 
customs procedures, reducing 27 systems to just one harmonized model. Moreover: the reform 
addresses the present challenges faced by EU Customs deriving from a significant surge in trade 
volumes, particularly in e-commerce and a rapidly expanding number of EU standards to be verified 
at the border. However we have concerns about whether these simplifications can be implemented 
broadly, based on the current proposal. We will explain this in more detail in this feedback and 
present some ideas for improvement. It goes without saying that we would be happy to contribute 
ideas to the further development of this in the follow-up process. 
 
Our main request: Make simplification accessible by introducing the ‘associate representative’ 
 
Obtaining Trust and Check Trader status will be a barrier, especially for SMEs 
We understand the need to appoint a single responsible person, not only for the collection of 
customs duties but also regarding adjacent legislation (e.g. CBAM), and we agree that, in most cases, 
the importer should be that person. We welcome the introduction of the Trust and Check (T&C) 
concept and the associated simplifications. However, the requirements associated with the T&C 
trader status can be a barrier for SME importers. The investments needed to obtain a T&C trader 
status require significant financial resources and customs expertise. In this regard, we fear that the 
European Commission's expectations expressed during the Trade Contact Group meeting on June 7 
2023, that 85% of all importers will become T&C traders, are unrealistic at this moment. 
 
Large group of importers will not benefit from the simplifications 
The scheme presented by the European Commission shows that there is no T&C facilitation 
applicable to the representative unless the importer is indirectly represented. We believe that this is a 
missed opportunity, especially for the group of SME importers who cannot qualify for T&C. In many 
cases, they will want to use T&C customs representatives to benefit from the advantages of T&C. 
However, the indirect representation provided for in the scheme does not offer sufficient relief. The 
question is whether customs representatives are willing to take on the additional risks associated 
with indirect representation. After all, a customs representative has almost no control over the 
accuracy of the information required by other regulations. He therefore will not want to take that 
risk. As a result, it is likely that a large group of importers will not be able to benefit from the 
simplifications of the New UCC, which is the intended purpose of this fundamental revision.  
 
We introduce the ‘associate representative(s)’ , a specialized form of direct representation 
Therefore, we advocate not using indirect but direct representation as the starting point, and we 
propose introducing a specialized form of direct representation, which we tentatively call the 
'associated representative(s).' The associated representative(s) will act in the name and on behalf of 
the importer but will be much more mature and involved in decision-making and will be similar to an

internal customs department. The lack of customs knowledge and procedures at the importer level 
will be filled by one or more associated representatives with T&C status. In line with the principles of 
the New UCC, the importer who decided to outsource their customs activities will remain the sole 
person responsible for the outsourced customs activities. It is up to the associated representative(s) 
and the importer to define the roles and responsibilities in this regard. In case of non-compliance 
caused by the direct customs representative(s), the importer will have to hold the customs 
representative accountable in other (civil) ways for their actions. However, the importer remains the 
sole responsible party for customs activities. At this point we fully support the proposal. 
 
Customs debt should arise at place of establishment of the representative 
According to Article 169, paragraph 1 of the New UCC, a customs debt arises at the place where the 
importer is established. Given the increased role of the customs representative, we believe that this 
limitation on the place where a customs debt arises should be reconsidered. Assuming that the 
associate representative takes on all or most of the responsibilities related to customs activities, the 
place where the representative is established should also be the place where a customs debt arises. 
In this context, we also note that we disagree with the preamble of the New UCC. We do not believe 
that the incurrence of a customs debt at the place where the importer is established, as opposed to 
the place where the goods are placed under a customs procedure, will be a significant simplification 
for market participants and, especially, for SMEs. Limiting simplifications such as deferment of 
payment and the use of comprehensive guarantee (reduced or waiver) to T&C importers and only 
indirect representatives will have negative consequences. 
In short: make simplification accessible by introducing a special form of direct representation, 
which also determines the place of service. 
In summary, to simplify the interaction, particularly for SMEs, we advocate the introduction of a 
specialized form of direct representation that we have called ‘associate representative’. The place 
where the associated representative(s) is/are established must also be deemed the place where the 
customs debt was incurred. Depending on the yet-to-be-determined conditions for the T&C status of 
representatives, at least deferment of payment and the use of comprehensive guarantee (reduced or 
waiver) should be granted as minimum simplifications. 
 
Other points to take into account 
In addition to the point discussed above, we would also like to draw your attention to the matters 
stated below. We have not elaborated much on these here, but we would be happy to engage in a 
discussion for further clarification. Most importantly we ask you to ensure close consultation with 
impacted businesses to refine the proposed customs simplifications and make them practical. We 
believe that this way, we can collectively contribute to the actual simplification of the system as 
intended by the proposal, allowing both businesses and Member States to benefit from it. 
Our additional suggestions are: 
• Maintain the current 90-day period for temporary storage to prevent congestion in seaports and 
airports. 
• Simplify special procedures by implementing a single indefinite suspension regime for customs 
operations. Under such a single regime the importer/authorization holder would be allowed to 
perform all forms of operations under customs control, provided this can be tracked from his 
administration. 
   
• Harmonize data-sharing requirements between marketplaces and customs authorities to improve 
effectiveness and reduce complexity. 
• The rules concerning the deemed importer need to ensure a level playing field between all 
marketplaces regardless of their place of establishment, business model, size or shipping method. 
• Address concerns about the proposed removal of the customs duty de minimis, considering 
potential negative impacts on businesses, EU customers, and global trade relationships.  
• Address the current issues with the IOSS system before expanding it to customs duty.

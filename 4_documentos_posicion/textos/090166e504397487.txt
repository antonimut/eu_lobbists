Count your transport emissions – “CountEmissions EU” 
 
EIM, the European Association representing Rail Infrastructure Managers  (IMs), welcomes 
the initiative related to provide more transparent information regarding transport -related 
greenhouse gas  (GHGs) emission to monitor and reduce emissions and to improve the 
efficiency of transport services.  
 
As stated in the Polluter Pays Principle evaluation, it is important to highlight that the use of 
rail for both passengers and freight could contribute to the reduction of GHGs emissions . 
Passengers (as final users) and freight transport clients must have proper information about 
GHGs emissions to be able to consciously choose more sustainable modes of transport. EIM 
believes that the current proposal and policy option do not adequately address this possibility.  
 
Moreover, it is essential to ensure that the information given to the consumers is in an easily 
readable format that allows an easy comparison between the different transport options for 
the same. For this reason EIM agrees with the Whell-to-Wheel 1 approach but wants to stress 
the need for precise data collection and data processing rules given the vast differences in the 
“data supply chains” of various transport modes.  
 
In order to have the most reliable results, Railway Undertakings (RUs) should be considered 
as a source of “primary data” 2 together with IMs. Currently the proposal does not have any 
incentive for the use of primary data. Additionally, the proposal does not take into account the 
economic value of those data  (i.e. on the short-term it foresees only “common rules on the 
communication and transparency”). 
 
Representing IMs, EIM believes that the proposal should also address a definition of “hub” 
which is not provided. 
Furthermore, the scope of infrastructure related emissions should also be clearly delineated  
in order to distinguish between operational emissions and constructions emissions.  
 
Given the close link with the “European Mobility Data Space” and “Multimodal Digital Mobility 
Services” proposals, EIM would like to suggest to reference them more in depth in the current 
proposal. Both already have provisions related to disclosing pollution data to the final 
consumer. 
 
A clear exclusion for personal soft mobility (i.e. electric scooters) should also be considered. 
Not doing so would allow for a distortion of the information provided also given the complexity 
of calculating GHGs emissions for this kind of mobility.

About EIM 
EIM, the association of European Rail Infrastructure Managers, was established in 2002 to promote the interests 
and views of independent infrastructure managers in Europe, following the liberalisation of the EU railway market 
It also provides technical expertise to the appropriate European bodies such as the European Railway Agency. 
EIM’s primary goal is promoting growth of rail traffic and the development of an open sustainable, efficient, 
customer orientated rail network in Europe. For more information, visit www.eimrail.org.

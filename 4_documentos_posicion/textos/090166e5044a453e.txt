ACEA REPLY TO THE COMMISSION’S EX-POST 
PUBLIC CONSULTATION ON THE 
COUNTEMISSIONS EU INITIATIVE  
NOVEMBER 2023 
The European Automobile Manufacturers' Association (ACEA)  remains fully committed to 
bringing CO2 emissions down to zero, supporting Europe’s goal of reaching climate neutrality 
by 2050. We believe that a collaborative approach, involving all stakeholders, will be key to 
achieving these ambitious goals.  
 
For this reason, ACEA welcomes the Commission’s proposal for a harmonized framework for 
the calculation of greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions  of transport services  (so called 
CountEmissions EU) . We believe that harmonizing emissions calculation methodologies 
across the European Union has the potential of enhancing the reliability, accuracy and 
comparability of GHG emissions data.  
 
Today, companies are proactively assuming greater responsibility for the environmental 
sustainability of their supply chains. They are utilizing existing tools to measure their emissions 
and contribute to the global effort to mitigate climate change. A harmonized framework will 
further support these initiatives by providing a consistent and standardized approach to 
emissions calculation.  
 
However, while we are positive about the proposal, we also have some concerns that need to 
be addressed.  
 
Consistency with other regulations  and with already existing 
methodologies 
It is crucial that the new European rules do not duplicate existing ones  and that they are 
coherent with calculation methodologies already in place. The regulatory landscape is already 
complex, and adding more layers of regulation could lead to confusion and inefficiency.  
 
Furthermore, it is important that the new rules fully align with other relevant regulations, such 
as the CO2 standards for Heavy -Duty Vehicles (currently under review) and the Renewable 
Energy Directive. These regulations have been carefully designed to address specific aspects 
of emissions and energy use, and any new rules should complement and reinforce the agreed 
regulatory framework, rather than contradict or undermine it. 
 
Similarly, it is unclear for ACEA how the proposed harmonized methodological framework is 
coherent with other existing reporting regimes and methodologies and will enable the reduction 
of GHG emissions of the transport sector, i.e.:

Regarding accounting methodologies already in place,  the Commission’s technical quality 
checks of already existing default values should be simple and straightforward. 
 
Implementation of the Regulation (delegated and implementing 
acts): involvement of Member States and industry experts 
The proposal grants significant implementation powers to the Commission via delegated and 
implementing acts. These powers pertain to the technical rules and specifications regarding 
third-party datasets, certifiers, verifiers, accreditation, etc.  
 
We understand that the proposal is for a framework, with the details to be included in 
secondary legislation and defined at a later stage. For these technical details, such as default 
values, emission factors, and intensity factors, we believe it is crucial that the Commission 
involves Member States and industry experts. Their insights and expertise will be invaluable 
in ensuring that the technical rules and specifications are accurate, relevant, and effective. 
 
Furthermore, we believe that proper  means for  monitoring and technical support must be 
provided to the Commission to perform its duties. This will enable them to effectively oversee 
the implementation of the technical rules and specifications and ensure that they are applied 
consistently and correctly across all Member States.  
 
Comparing GHG emissions from different modes: ensuring fairness 
The European Automobile Manufacturers' Association (ACEA) recognizes the importance of 
Article 4 in the CountEmissions EU proposal. This article provides the opportunity to examine 
how the CountEmissions EU operates in the market, how the method is utilized, and whether 
there is a need for adjustment.  
 
We believe that a thorough and ongoing evaluation of the CountEmissions EU's effectiveness 
in the market is crucial. This will ensure that the method remains relevant and effective, and 
that it continues to provide accurate and reliable emissions data. 
 
Furthermore, we believe that it is important to monitor for any potential distortions in the market 
that could lead to unintended effects on transport choices. The goal of CountEmissions EU is 
to promote environmental sustainability, and it is essential th at it does not inadvertently 
discourage sustainable road transport options.

Distortions can be originated due to the fact there are still major gaps and inconsistencies in 
the way data and statistics from different modes are compiled, like for instance the utilisation 
of vehicle (all modes) capacity/occupancy rate, or in freight transport, where modal CO2 
intensity is generally measured with respect to weight rather than volume. The standard carbon 
intensity metric is gCO2/tonne-Km. This weight-based definition of CO2 intensity tends to 
favour modes and carriers carrying more dense products. Finally, availability of reliable data 
varies depending on the modes. As explained in the box, the current limitations of official data 
and the shortcomings of available statistics have an impact on transport efficiency 
measurement of the different modes of transport. 
 
 
To ensure that Article 4 functions correctly, we propose regular reviews and adjustments based 
on market performance and feedback from industry experts. This will allow for continuous 
improvement and refinement of the method, ensuring that it remains effec tive and beneficial 
for all stakeholders.  
 
Conclusions 
In conclusion, ACEA supports the move towards a harmonized emissions calculation 
framework in the EU. We believe that this will enhance the accuracy and comparability of 
emissions data and support companies in their sustainability efforts. However, it is essential 
that these new rules are carefully designed to align with existing regulations and avoid 
unnecessary duplication. ACEA also emphasizes the importance of involving Member States 
and industry experts in defining the technical details of the proposal  and providing adequate 
monitoring and technical support to the Commission.  Finally, a careful implementation to avoid 
any potential market distortions or unintended effects is of outmost importance. 
MEASURING TRANSPORT EFFICIENCY: LIMITATIONS OF OFFICIAL DATA 
AND SHORTCOMINGS OF AVAILABLE STATISTICS 
At company or individual level  the efficiency of transport can be measured in many ways, using a 
range of utilization and productivity measures. Attempts to measure the efficiency of transport at a 
macro (or national) level are frustrated, however, by a lack of official data and concern about the 
consistency of some of the statistics that are available. Using government data, it is only possible to 
assess the efficiency of road transport. No general statistics are compiled on the efficiency of rail 
transport (both passenger and freight) or water-borne transport. This is a serious anomaly given the 
current modal shift approach that EU institutions have adopted. 
 
In the absence of data on load factors/occupancy rates and energy efficiency on the rail and 
waterway systems, it is not possible to compare the environmental performance of the different 
modes on a consistent basis. For freight, some of the available data  on the utilization of intermodal 
road-rail freight services, however, suggest that rail capacity could be much more effectively used. 
The greater statistical transparency of the road sector, in particular on freight, places it at a relative 
disadvantage in public political debates over vehicle loading/occupancy rates as capacity 
utilization/occupancy rates on competing modes is not subject to the same degree of scrutiny.

Envien Group feedback to - CountEmissions EU: Framework for calculation of GHG 
emissions from transport operations 
 
Envien Group, as one of the lead producers of biofuels, used in motor fuels - diesel and gasoline 
in Central and Eastern Europe , welcomes and supports the initiative of European Commission in creating 
common methodology  for calculating greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions  from transport services. This 
harmonized method can ensure another step towards a greener future. Especially we would like to highlight 
application of Well-to-wheel methodology which allows rather precise calculation and comparison of lifecycle 
emissions between different technologies used in transport.  
However, it is necessary to put forward  some key elements which should be discussed and put 
into consideration by the European Commission: 
The positive aspect of the regulation is its voluntariness of application of the verification system 
for SMEs , which is how the European Commission tries to prevent excessive administrative burden . 
However, since around 99.7% of companies operating in the transport services sector  are SMEs, only 
remaining 0,3% of companies would be obliged to verify their calculations. On the other hand, we recognize 
that costs of verification for SMEs could be a barrier to wider implementation.  
Nevertheless, it is essential to define this voluntariness more clearly and thus prevent possible 
inconsistencies. This can be done by a slight change in article 2 of the proposal by e.g.  adding “which 
decides to calculate and make available the CO2 emissions from the transport service”.  
Another key argument may lie in the fact that R enewable energy directive has already 
established a certain methodology for calculating life-cycle emissions as well as certain default values  for 
the calculation. The proposed regulation acknowledges the importance of aligning with existing legislation, 
specifically the Renewable Energy Directive (RED II), in order to maintain consistency. However, there is 
a discrepancy in the calculation of greenhouse gas emissions from biofuels between ISO 14083 
and RED II. Notably, the ISO standard does not account for emission savings from carbon capture and 
replacement (eCCR), carbon capture and storage (eCCS), and soil carbon accumulation via improved 
agricultural management (eSCA), which puts certain biofuels  at a disadvantage compared to the 
methodology outlined in RED II. Emission factors of liquid biofuels presented in Table K.1 (Annex K) of ISO 
14083 do not align with the regulation set by RED II nor they represent accurate emission footprint of liquid 
biofuels which are available at the EU market. We would like to highlight that it is crucial to differentiate 
greenhouse gas emission footprint of biofuels based on the type of feedstock, which they are produced 
from. This is in line with RED II methodology, which under Annex V of the RED II differentiates between

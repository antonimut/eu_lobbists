The Bothnian Corridors response on the Commission’s proposal for 
a regulation on the use of railway infrastructure capacity in the 
Single European Railway Area 
 
To: The European Commission  
From: The Bothnian Corridor 
Date: 27 of September 2023 
 
 
The Bothnian Corridor welcomes the ongoing efforts by the European Commission to 
develop a proposal for a regulation on the use of railway infrastructure capacity in the 
Common European Railway Area - a part of the Commission’s plan to promote green 
freight transport and address the multifaceted challenges faced by the railway transport 
sector. 
The proposed measures also align with the Commission's ongoing work with revising the 
Trans-European Transport Network (TEN-T), imposing higher standards on infrastructure, 
and integrating various modes of transportation in the transport system. Furthermore, the 
Commission's strategy for sustainable and smart mobility outlines ambitious goals, 
including a 50% increase in freight traffic by 2030, doubling it by 2050, and a commitment 
to doubling high-speed train traffic (defined in Sweden as speeds exceeding 250 km/h) 
by 2030, followed by a tripling of the same by 2050. 
In our response, we have chosen to primarily focus on the sections of the regulation 
related to capacity allocation and cross-border traffic. This decision is partly influenced 
by the extensive railway traffic in our regions, projected to grow significantly. This traffic 
comprises a diverse mix of freight transport, regional commuting, and long-distance train 
journeys. Additionally, our shared geography encompasses 72% of the country's area and 
includes three of Sweden's four railway connections to Norway and the only land 
connection to Finland.  
Our response also raises questions about who bears the burden of responsibility outlined 
in the proposal for infrastructure managers and the scope for Swedish regions to steer 
towards a transport-efficient society. 
Key recommendations and feedback 
• The Bothnian Corridor wholeheartedly endorses the aspects of the regulation that 
seek to integrate maintenance and inventory planning into the process of capacity 
allocation. 
 
• The Bothnian Corridor views positively the elements in the proposal that seek to 
facilitate and enhance dialogue between infrastructure managers and users, with 
the goal of addressing capacity issues and challenges. 
 
• The Bothnian Corridor advocates for the Commission to exercise moderation in 
utilizing delegated acts in the future, ensuring that capacity planning aligns with 
the genuine desires and needs of travelers and shippers.

• The Bothnian Corridor asserts that all main routes identified by the Swedish 
Transport Administration should be incorporated into Sweden's capacity planning, 
adhering to the requirements outlined in the regulation, to ensure the efficiency 
and seamless operation of the entire transportation system in Sweden. 
• The Bothnian Corridor emphasizes the importance of including intermodal nodes 
and ports in the transport planning process. Furthermore, it calls for future 
developments in legislation to recognize the significance of urban nodes in 
achieving effective capacity utilization within the transportation system. 
• The Bothnian Corridor contends that a national (Swedish) review of existing goals 
for the transport system is necessary to avoid unnecessary conflicts in the 
utilization of the railway network. 
• The Bothnian Corridor urges the Swedish Transport Administration to thoroughly 
consider the possibilities outlined in the proposed regulation, that allows for 
intentions behind investments in infrastructure, as decided in the (Swedish) 
regional transport plans, to be considered when later allocating capacity. 
• The Bothnian Corridor believes that there is a need for additional clarification in 
the proposal regarding which infrastructure managers are affected by the stated 
requirements. Furthermore, these actors should be identified at the Swedish level 
to ensure effective communication with the relevant stakeholders. 
• The Bothnian Corridor deems that the Swedish Transport Administration should 
collaborate with other Nordic infrastructure managers to ensure seamless railway 
border crossings. 
• The Bothnian Corridor believes that the Swedish Transport Administration, in 
collaboration with the Swedish government, should early on consider the railway 
infrastructure needs of the Swedish Armed Forces in the initial stages of 
infrastructure planning. 
Limited Infrastructure and Insufficient Investments 
The shortage of capacity in the railway transport sector is a well-known problem that 
requires immediate action. Unfortunately, these capacity challenges have led to 
prolonged issues with congestion, delays, and reduced service quality. The requirements 
set out in the proposal to ensure available capacity in the railway network would exert 
significant pressure on large parts of the Swedish railway. A Railway system which has 
historically been underfunded both in terms of maintenance and in the form of new 
investments. This has led to the large-scale presence of single-track lines, narrow 
tunnels or bridges unfit for modern trains, insufficient passing tracks, and stations with 
limited capacity and which does not meet TEN-T standards. Upon the implementation of 
the regulation, it is therefore necessary to enact a gradual implementation, which allows 
for the Swedish Transport Administration to address the significant maintenance backlog. 
Before subjecting the railway network to more pressure, hinders the ability to take 
necessary maintenance measures for a qualitative and efficient capacity utilization. 
The Bothnian Corridor looks favorably on the openings that the proposal provides for 
aligning the process of capacity allocation with long-term investments and maintenance 
of the railway network. The proposal achieves this by requiring infrastructure managers 
to conduct capacity analyses and develop action plans for overloaded tracks in

National Goals for Railway Traffic 
There are various needs and potential goals for railway infrastructure usage. The 
regulation provides in several instances room for infrastructure managers and member 
states to establish priorities for what the railway traffic should achieve. At the Swedish 
national level, consideration should be given to the regulation's impact on the entire 
transport network to achieve functional capacity utilization. This is especially relevant for 
the rail freight traffic which in Sweden, often occurs on regional tracks which operates 
outside the TEN-T network, which then continues with cross-border transport via the 
TEN-T core network's railways and/or ports. 
An already established holistic system perspective that the Swedish Transport 
Administration could use, is the eight main routes identified in the Swedish Transport 
Administration’s implementation plan for 2023–2028. Failure to include an analysis on 
how co-ordination should look like for the entire transport network beyond what is 
addressed by the proposed regulation. May result in inefficient use of existing capacity 
during the implementation. Potential outcomes of such failure could be, suboptimal 
timetables, poorly coordinated maintenance work, and difficulties in allocating capacity 
among different actors.  
Increasing capacity in the rail system requires substantial investments, including the 
expansion of existing tracks but also reinforcement of critical infrastructure outside the 
railway network, such as urban nodes, ports, and terminals. Finding appropriate funding 
as well as establishing a strong long-term political will, is crucial for implementing such 
investments. 
The regulation imposes no requirements for national processes, such as the 
development of national infrastructure plans, to consider the different processes 
described in the regulation. The proposal also lacks insight into how the railway network 
connects with the wider comprehensive transport system. Nationally, efforts should be 
made to integrate the development of a strategic long-term plan for infrastructure with 
the processes for investment and maintenance planning, capacity allocation planning, 
and contingency planning outlined in the proposal. This approach would provide an 
overview of the overarching goals for the sector and prevent inconsistencies when 
working and planning for a holistic and intermodal transport system. 
Regional and Municipal Needs for Railway Infrastructure 
In Sweden regions have no responsibility or mandate to coordinate in a broader 
interregional context. However, the issues addressed in the Swedish regional 
development strategies (RUS) often depend on development outside their own 
county/region and sometimes even beyond the national borders. Infrastructure, 
particularly railways are interconnected in a regional, national, and international systems, 
whose relevance and efficiency depend on the harmonization and standardization for 
both freight and passenger traffic.  
Despite this, according to (Swedish) law, regions have clear obligations to ensure the 
availability of public transportation and often serve as financiers for crucial railway 
infrastructure. One should therefore, during the Swedish implementation, utilize the

provisions in the regulation, such as Article 31.8, which allows for longer framework 
agreement allocations. For exceptional cases, such as extensive long-term investments 
in railway infrastructure made by municipalities and/or regions can exceed a 15-year 
period. 
Additionally, the proposal consistently mentions the role and responsibility of the 
infrastructure manager in constructing, managing, and maintaining railway infrastructure. 
In Sweden, the Swedish Transport Administration is the national infrastructure manager, 
However, there are also numerous other infrastructure managers, such as companies or 
municipalities that own their railway facilities, combi terminals, and/or ports. 
Consequently, we raise critical questions regarding the proposal's failure to specify 
which infrastructure managers are obligated to follow the burdensome requirements set 
by the regulation. 
Cross-Border Traffic and International Coordination 
Cross-border railway traffic is complex due to regional differences in rule 
implementation, organizational structures, geographical challenges, and the need to 
coordinate between countries with different preconditions. Easing the facilitation of 
cross-border traffic requires an open dialogue among various stakeholders, including 
shippers, suppliers, and infrastructure owners, within and between member states. 
Harmonization of rules and cooperation with third countries, such as Norway, is also 
crucial for promoting smooth cross-border traffic. Hence, during the ongoing process of 
developing the regulation, simultaneous efforts should be made to harmonize rules and 
standards between the EU and important third countries. Furthermore, during the 
implementation of the regulation, particular attention should also be given to the 
challenging border crossings to and from Finland. As travel by rail is negatively impacted 
by Finland’s geographical and technical isolation from the wider European railway 
network and standards. 
Strengthening international accessibility is crucial for the continued development of the 
Bothnian Corridor and Sweden. Ensuring good accessibility is essential for attracting 
foreign investments, maintaining the tourism industry, attracting foreign labour, and 
ensuring the business sector's access to international export markets. Ensuring functional 
border crossings to Norway and Finland is particularly important within the geography of 
the Bothnian Corridor. 
The Swedish Armed Forces' Need for Railway Infrastructure 
The EU and notably Sweden, faces a new security landscape where military 
requirements and needs of the railway transport system play a critical role in ensuring 
that European armed forces can respond to crisis both within and outside EU’s external 
borders. Therefore, the inclusion of the Swedish Armed Forces in the dialogue and 
planning of cross-border traffic is imperative. This ensures, that considerations related to 
military defence, civil- and military preparedness and mobility issues are considered in 
the implementation of the proposal. The regulation should also further consider clearer 
alignment with the goals outlined in the European Commission's action plan for military 
mobility 2.0. Which can serve as a guiding framework for handling military aspects of 
railway transport sector beyond what is already described in Article 47 regarding 
capacity allocation during crises.

The cooperation of the Bothnian Corridor is dedicated to strengthening transport 
infrastructure and improve the connections between Sweden and the rest of Europe. The 
Bothnian Corridor is an extension of the EU's core network corridors Scandinavia-
Mediterranean and North Sea-Baltic northwards and links together Norway, Sweden, and 
Finland, along with the connecting routes. The cooperation focuses on the railway 
corridors but also includes important connections to other modes of transportation. 
Our main goals are to have the core network corridor Scandinavia-Mediterranean 
completed by 2030 and the comprehensive network ready by 2050.  
The Bothnian Corridor cooperation is based on the regions' mission, boundaries, and 
priorities. The work's starting point is formulated in each region's Regional Development 
Strategy (RUS), following each region's priorities in designated areas. 
The Bothnian Corridor collaboration includes the regions of Region Dalarna, Region 
Gävleborg, Region Jämtland Härjedalen, Region Norrbotten, Region Västerbotten, 
Region Västernorrland, and Region Örebro County.

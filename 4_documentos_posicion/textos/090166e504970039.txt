Introduction 
Deutsche Umwelthilfe e.V. (DUH) welcomes that the Commission proposes to amend the Waste Frame-
work Directive (WFD) and appreciates the opportunity to provide feedback on the proposal. Since DUH 
has been working intensively on food waste for years, we will focus on aspects related to food waste. 
Food waste: General Assessment 
DUH generally appreciates that the Commission recognizes the massive environmental impacts of food 
waste and that the Commission formulates the aim to reduce environmental and climate impacts. DUH 
also welcomes that the Commission proposes binding food waste reduction targets.   
However, DUH encourages the European Commission to formulate more ambitious targets that live up 
to the SDG 12.3 targets. Targets that are more ambitious are needed not least because the accompany-
ing assessments and reports unequivocally state that member states have made too little progress in 
the past years. 
DUH recommends increasing the reduction targets to 50% from farm to fork (including primary produc-
tion). With respect to food waste measurement, it needs to be ensured that the methodologies applied 
in the member states are reliable, comparable and subject to scientific evaluation. To prevent the gener-
ation of food waste in the first place, further legislation needs to be aligned (e.g. on date labelling and 
marketing standards). 
Our recommendations are elaborated further in what follows. 
Reduction targets: need for more ambitious targets from farm to fork 
The reduction targets (proposal for new Art. 9a(4)) lack ambition and do not meet the targets formu-
lated in SDG 12.3, which the European Union and its member states have signed (and reiterated in the 
Farm-to-Fork-Strategy). SDG 12.3 sets the goal to halve food waste by 2030, whereas the proposal for 
the revision of the WFD formulates a reduction target of 10% for processing and manufacturing and a

joint reduction target of 30% for retail, other distribution of food, restaurants, food services and house-
holds only. This is not only below the SDG 12.3 commitments but endangers climate and environmental 
targets given that food waste is responsible for at least 6% of GHG-emissions in the European Union1. 
Additionally, a reduction target for agricultural production is missing. Such a target is indispensable and 
needs to include food waste generated before and during harvest and slaughter. Recent data indicate 
that production is the largest contributor to food waste2 in the EU, making food waste targets for the 
agricultural production sector indispensable to achieve a significant reduction of food waste. This re-
quires not only an inclusion of production into the reduction targets but also a revision of the definition 
of “food waste” (see below). It is very disappointing that the Commission did not include agricultural 
production into its proposal despite the acknowledgement that it was the demand most frequently 
raised during the consultation process
3. 
Furthermore, setting a joint target for retail, other distribution of food, restaurants, food services and 
households diffuses responsibility between different sectors and carries the risk of burden shifting to 
private households and consumers. It ignores the important gatekeeping role that the retail sector in 
particular plays. 
In sum, the reduction targets proposed by the Commission should be reformulated and the level of am-
bition needs to be altered significantly. DUH encourages the Commission to raise the level of ambition 
and to formulate a 50% target from farm to fork (with individual targets of 50% for each sector). This 
implies 
- Increasing the reduction target to 50% for each sector and revise the proposal for a new Art. 
9a(4) accordingly 
- Include primary production and formulate a specific target of 50% for production in Art. 9a(4) 
including pre-harvest and harvest as well as pre-slaughter and slaughter. 
- Disentangling the reduction targets for retail, other distribution of food, restaurants, food ser-
vices and households and prescribe a 50% reduction target for retail, food services and house-
holds individually and adjust Art. 9a(4(b)) accordingly.  
- Additionally, it could be considered expressing the targets as maximum caps of food waste in kg 
per capita and year to account for past efforts of the member states.

2020 compared to 88 million tonnes in 2012 while highlighting at the same time that there has not been 
significant reduction at member states level. It is plausible that the official data reported by some of the

Whereas DUH welcomes that member states have to monitor food waste and that food waste will be 
included in the early warning reports, we reiterate that a valid, reliable, transparent and comparable 
methodology needs to be established and an evaluation of the data reported and methodologies used 
by the member states is needed. 
Food waste hierarchy and policy instruments 
Concerning the food waste hierarchy it should be clarified without ambiguity in Art. 9a(1(c)) that food 
used for animal feed should be preferred over non-food use. It is vital that the food waste hierarchy is 
made compulsory and the following hierarchy should be clarified in the WFD: Prevention – Use for hu-
man consumption (donation, redistribution, etc.) – Use for animal feed – recycling and recovery (com-
post, energy use, etc.) – disposal. 
DUH fears that – also given the low ambition concerning the reduction levels and the joint target for re-
tail and consumption – the measures proposed will not lead to a significant reduction of food waste as 
the measures and instruments rely heavily on awareness-raising and voluntariness. In this respect com-
petences and mandate of the national authorities according to the proposed Art. 29a need to be clari-
fied, particularly with respect to monitoring and measures to be taken in case of non-compliance. 
Additional revisions needed 
To prevent food waste from farm to fork, the definition of food waste needs to be aligned to include pre-
harvest and pre-slaughter waste. This implies that either food waste needs to be defined without refer-
ence to the definition of food in the General Food Law or it requires an alteration of the definition of food 
in regulation EC 178/2002 and the EU delegated decision on food waste measurement ((EU) 2019/1597). 
To address power imbalances in the food supply chain and to make sure that food waste is not shifted to 
the agricultural sector, the regulation on unfair trading practices ((EU) 2019/633) needs to be revised to 
avoid food waste resulting from unfair trading practices and to address power imbalance in the agricul-
tural value chain. Practices such as increasing cosmetic specifications, which are an important driver of 
food waste in primary production, need to be eliminated
5. In this regard, it is also vital that marketing 
standards be examined and dropped where unnecessary to allow for marketing also fruit and vegetables 
that do not conform to cosmetic specifications but are otherwise nonhazardous (so-called “ugly fruit”). 
Additionally, the European Commission should timely initiate the announced legislation on date labelling 
and consider further exemptions from the best before labelling for products such as pasta or rice (Regu-
lation 1169/2011, Annex X) and make sure that nonprofit organizations that save and redistribute food 
(e.g. food banks) can enjoy legal certainty.

Conclusion 
DUH encourages European policymakers to live up to the SDG 12.3 commitments and the goals formu-
lated in the Farm to Fork Strategy. We therefore recommend to: 
- Setting ambitious food waste reduction targets 
o Include primary production 
o Increase food waste reduction targets to 50% from farm to fork with separate targets for 
each stage of the supply chain 
- Increase the reliability, transparency and comparability of data on food waste 
- Align further legislation (i.e. on marketing standards, date labelling, unfair trading practices) that 
is vital for preventing food waste

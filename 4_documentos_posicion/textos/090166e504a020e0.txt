 
 
 
 
 
Inditex’s contribution: 
 “Public Consultation:  Revision of the Waste Framework Directive” 
Inditex welcomes the targeted revision of the EU’s Waste Framework Directive (WFD). The WFD requires updating, 
particularly to align EU legislation with the Union’s policy objectives to promote circularity in the textiles industry 
and leverage the role that Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) schemes can play to that end.  
The European Commission’s (EC) proposal to revise the WFD, published in July 2023, should be more ambitious 
in harmonising EPR schemes across EU M ember States and provide clearly unique modulated fee structures. 
The EC’s proposal should also provide stronger market signals for the development of new infrastructure and 
technological innovation to scale-up the separate collection, sorting, reuse, and recycling of textile products. This 
infrastructure and innovation are key to meet the 2025 EU target for mandatory separate textile waste collection 
and to enable the transition towards a circular textile industry, which will reduce dependency on third-country raw 
materials and foster demand for recycled ﬁbres and reuse. 
We look forward to working with EU co-legislators to share our views and expertise to inform decision-making on 
the WFD revision. We invite EU co-legislators to review the report by environmental consultancy Eunomia entitled 
Further Considerations to Textiles EPR and Complementary Measures
 (May 2023), which was commissioned by 
a group of industry stakeholders, and which provides clarity and consensus deﬁnitions on critical themes covered 
under the WFD.  
Our recommendations on the EC’s WFD proposal are to: 
1. Regulation for textiles EPR to guarantee harmonisation 
2. Ensure a common fee structure and ecomodulation criteria across Member States 
3. Guarantee that deﬁnition of obligated producers avoids free riding 
4. Ensure EPR fees will drive RDI capex funding for ﬁbre-to-ﬁbre recycling 
5. A single EPR scheme per Member State for apparel, home textiles and footwear 
6. Establish a centralised EU producer registry 
7. Clarify aspects of PRO activities and responsibilities   
8. Fully align the EU’s customs code with the scope of products covered in Annex IVc 
 
1. Regulation for textiles EPR to guarantee harmonisation 
We strongly recommend the EC to propose a Regulation for textiles EPR to ensure maximum harmonisation 
of EPR schemes in the EU. Obligations and incentives should be fully aligned across Member States to drive 
economies of scale for reuse, textile recycling and preparation for recycling to meet environmental goals.    
Only harmonisation at EU level will leverage the effectiveness of PRO  schemes (Producer Responsibility 
Organisations) in delivering environmental beneﬁts. We call on EU co-legislators to amend the EC’s proposal 
to set the maximum feasible harmonised requirements for national EPR schemes across the EU internal 
market, including on scope, deﬁnitions, harmonised fee structures, allocation of fees for ﬁnancing reuse and 
preparation for recycling industrial base, common ecomodulation, reporting rules, and KPIs.  
2. Ensure a common fee structure and ecomodulation criteria across Member States 
We call on EU co-legislators to create a common EU fee structure for EPR schemes. Modulated fees should 
be applied uniformly and consistently across EU Member States. This would strengthen the effectiveness of 
PRO organisations in driving ecodesign and sustainability in the textiles sector while signiﬁcantly reducing 
the administrative burden for producers.

The EC’s proposal states that the EC may deﬁne speciﬁc ecomodulation criteria in future implementing acts 
to align with the ecodesign criteria that will be established under the Ecodesign for Sustainable Products 
Regulation (ESPR). ESPR ecodesign criteria will not be deﬁned and implemented for some time given that 
work has not yet begun on the ESPR Delegated Act in the Ecodesign Forum. It is important for economic 
operators to gain a better understanding now of how future ecomodulation criteria will be deﬁned and 
implemented to guide current business planning for sustainability.    
3. Guarantee that deﬁnition of obligated producers avoids free riding 
It is key that the deﬁnition of obligated producers in Article 3, paragraph 4 (b) has an appropriate scope that 
captures all producers of textile, textile- related and footwear products that sell relevant new and ﬁnished 
products in the EU internal market for the ﬁrst time. This will avoid loopholes and e nsure the polluter pays 
principle in proportion on their contribution.     
 
The deﬁnition of consumer in Article 3, paragraph 4 (f) should include businesses or public authorities. This 
would mean that EPR obligations would extend to those economic operators that sell business-to-business 
and who engage in public procurement with public authorities.   
4. Ensure EPR fees will drive RDI capex funding for ﬁbre-to-ﬁbre recycling 
The EPR fees should not exceed those necessary to ensure the circularity of textiles products in a cost -
efficiency way. EPR schemes should cover the cost of the collection, transport, sorting, preparation for reuse 
and preparation for recycling.  So that, we support the EC’s proposal to use funding from producers to invest 
in RDI for scaling-up ﬁbre-to-ﬁbre recycling. However, this funding should be capex focused and not extend 
to opex for recycling facilities, as recyclers will become the customers of the PROs schemes in the circular 
economy.  
We also support cost -efficient consumer information campaigns on sustainable consumption and waste 
prevention, as delivered by Producer Responsibility Organisations (PROs) for textiles that our industry will set 
up. 
5. A single EPR scheme per Member State for apparel, home textiles and footwear 
We recommend the establishment of a single EPR scheme per Member State to  ensure a more efficient 
EPR and build the infrastructure capacity needed for the textiles secondary raw materials market.  A single 
EPR scheme will have the following advantages: 
• A unique PRO open to all companies in the sector may contribute to gain efficiency in waste 
management. In particular, a single PRO can coordinate and centralize the management of waste, 
leading to greater efficiency in collection, transportation , sorting, preparation for reuse and 
preparation for recycling. These efficiency gains can contribute to a resilient internal market. 
• Consolidating resources (and avoiding duplicities derived from multiple PROs) can also lead to 
cost reductions in waste management. This beneﬁts businesses, governments, and consumers. 
• A single PRO may have greater bargaining power to negotiate agreements and collaborations, 
which can lead to the implementation of more sustainable policies and practices in the supply 
chain and ultimately beneﬁt the customers. Besides, it can also reduce supply chain disruptions in 
the secondary materials market, which will provide further conﬁdence to investors in the sector.  
• A centralized waste management in a single PRO also allows for better tracking and traceability of 
products throughout its life cycle, essential for ensuring responsible and transparent management. 
Besides, a single PRO would allow a more effective supervi sion by the authorities, facilitating 
compliance with environmental regulations and the implementation of sustainable practices.

6. Establish a centralised EU producer registry 
We believe that a centralised EU registry should be established and maintained by the European 
Commission to streamline registrations of economic operators and the provision of necessary registration 
details to national competent authorities. This reduces administrative burdens to a minimum for companies 
that operate across many EU member states.  
7. Clarify aspects of PRO activities and responsibilities   
It is important to clarify the following aspects of the EC’s proposal relating to the activities of Producer 
Responsibility Organisations: 
• The interplay between the information to be provided to consumers on sustainable consumption 
of textiles and the ongoing determination of the types of claims and information that can be 
provided to consumers to empower responsible purchasing behaviours.  
• The deﬁnition to be used in Article 22c, paragraph 13 (d).  
8. Fully align the EU’s customs code with the scope of products covered in Annex IVc 
We support the EC’s proposal to introduce Delegated Acts to align the scope of products covered under 
EPR schemes, as outlined in the revised WFD’s Annex IVc, with the EU’s Combined Nomenclature (CN). This 
is important to ensure that textile, textile -related and footwear products that are placed on the EU internal 
market by economic operators outside the EU and that fall under the scope of the Directive are subject to 
compliance obligations in line with EU law. It will also facilitate registrations of econo mic operators in the 
producer registry (see point 8 below).

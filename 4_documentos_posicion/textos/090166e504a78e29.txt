Proposal on food waste reduction targets as part of the revision of the Waste 
Framework Directive 
Confcommercio’s feedback 
21 November 2023 
 
Confcommercio welcomes the opportunity to provide feedback on the proposed 
amendment to the Waste Framework Directive, specifically concerning aspects 
related to food and food waste. 
 
The Commission's proposal sets ambitious targets for waste reduction, 
although some considerations are necessary.  
 
With Article 9a.4, Member States are expected to ach ieve the following food 
waste reduction targets (at the national level) by December 31, 2030: 
1. Reduction by 10% of the generation of food waste in processing and 
manufacturing compared to the amount generated in 2020; 
2. Reduction by 30% of the generation of food waste per capita in retail, 
restaurants and households compared to the amount generated in 2020. 
According to the results of the first EU-wide monitoring of food waste , primary 
production accounted for 11% of food waste in 2020. However, the 
Commission has not set targets for primary production, and the objectives do 
not cover the entire food supply chain. Therefore, we believe that the word 
'production' should be inserted before 'processing' in letter a), paragraph 4 of 
Article 9a. 
 
We consider it essential to foresee and implement support  measures for 
the catering sector due to the low quantity of food waste produced by this 
category compared to others, especially households. 
Furthermore, for the same sector, greater clarity is needed on the methodology 
used to measure waste production. 
According to Recital No.13, the joint food waste target applying to retail and 
other distribution of food, restaurants and food services and households, is to 
be expressed as a percentage change in food waste levels per capita in order to 
take into account population changes.  In particular, according to Article 9a.1, 
Member States can choose the most suitable measures to achieve these 
targets. It is important to emphasize that any measures adopted should be 
proportional to the capacity and role of stakeholders in the food supply 
chain and focus on preventing a disproportionate impact on SMEs.

Article 9a.2 suggests that Member States shall monitor and assess the 
implementation of their food waste prevention measures by measuring the 
levels of food waste on the basis of a common methodology. In this regard, it is 
unclear whether flexibility is contemplated in how these targets should be 
measured or whether the common methodology adopted by the Commission 
should be considered mandatory. The Commission's proposed amendment 
refers to the common methodology that explicitly allows Member States to 
measure food waste using different methods, in compliance with the minimum 
quality requirements specified in Article 4 Minimum Quality Requirements  
(representative sample and sensitive methodology in the cas e of methods in 
Annex III, or, alternatively, the use of the best available information in the 
case of methods in Annex IV) to ensure the reliability and accuracy of 
measurements. Therefore, we believe that the proposal should be 
interpreted in the sense of the greater flexibility already provided by 
current provisions. 
 
According to Article 9a.5, the year 2020 will serve as the baseline for assessing 
progress. However, Member States may use a reference year pr ior to 2020 if 
they can provide data collected using methods comparable to the methodology 
and minimum quality requirements for uniform measurement of food waste 
levels as established by Commission Delegated Decision (EU) 2019/1597. 
This provision allows flexibility and ensures that proactive efforts previously 
made are not overlooked. However, at the same time, it may be challenging 
for a Member State to provide valid data collected using methods 
comparable to the methodology adopted by the Commission. 
 
Delegated Decision 2019/1597 already provides th e possibility for Member 
States to measure food waste using methods other than the common 
methodology in Annex III, provided that such alternative methods are 
equivalent in terms of relevance, representativeness, and reliability. In 
particular, Annex IV identifies these alternative methods: 
1. Calculation of the amount of food waste based on the most recent data 
reported under Regulation 2150/2002. 
2. Calculation of the amount of food waste based on data related to 
socioeconomic indicators set out in point b) of the same Annex IV. 
The common methodology in Italy was also discussed at the last meeting of the 
Table for the Fight Against Waste and Food Assistance of the Italian Ministry of 
Agriculture. In particular, the flow of data on food waste from Italy to Eurostat 
was discussed in view of recent and upcoming developments. The topic was 
also addressed in the first meeting of the subgroups recently established within 
the same Table, in which we will participate st arting from the second 
convening. 
 
Lastly, we believe that flexibility regarding the common reference period 
to be used is important . This is even more crucial in light of the policies and 
results achieved by individual Member States , such as Italy, which have 
actively supported the fight against food waste. In fact, 2020 is not a 
representative year for waste production in the catering sector due to the long 
closures imposed on businesses to counter the COVID epidemic. There is

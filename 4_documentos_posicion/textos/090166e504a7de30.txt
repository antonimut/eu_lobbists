FEFCO position on the European Commission’s review of the Waste Framework Directive –  
food waste reduction targets 
 
The European Federation of Corrugated Board Manufacturers (FEFCO) supports the intention of the 
European Commission’s revision of the Waste Framework Directive (WFD) to reduce waste production. 
This legislative update is especially critical given the environmental, economic and societal impacts of 
food waste. 
 
 
FEFCO’s key recommendations for the revision are:  
1. The Waste Framework Directive should recognise the vital role of packaging in reducing food 
waste.  
2. The waste hierarchy should be re-evaluated considering the added value of recycling alongside 
reuse for the prevention of waste.  
 
 
These recommendations are further detailed below.  
 
1. The Waste Framework Directive should recognise the vital role of packaging in reducing 
food waste.  
 
To underline the importance of packaging,  the WFD  should include an  additional provision under 
paragraph 1 of article 9a; “supporting innovation in packaging, taking into account the important 
role of food packaging in the food value chain to prevent the generation of food waste and ensure 
food safety”.  
 
Packaging plays a key role in the food supply chain,  protecting products from damage during transport 
and storage, ensuring food safety and providing legally required information to consumers. In the context 
of this legislation, packaging is especially crucial to prevent food waste; in fact, research has shown that 
corrugated packaging can extend the shelf life of fresh food products by up to three days1. 
 
A reduction of packaging is therefore counterproductive to the circular economy as a whole given that 
damaged food products result in higher negative environmental impacts e.g. food waste, loss of damaged 
goods, use of primary resources or emissions to the environment.  By recognising the integral nature of 
packaging in the food sector, the legislation will more completely support the prevention of food waste in 
Europe. 
 
2. The waste hierarchy should be re -evaluated considering the added value of recycling 
alongside reuse for the prevention of waste. 
 
Regarding future revisions of the Waste Framework Directive, we would like to stress the importance of 
revisiting the structure of the waste hierarchy. As it stands, the hierarchy prioritises waste prevention 
methods based on the supposed environmental benefits they bring, placing reuse above recycling. 
However, reuse and recycling should be considered complementary measures for achieving 
waste prevention.

A range of recently published studies have resulted in acknowledgment that reuse is not always the most 
environmentally beneficial option; sometimes, recycling is. Therefore, automatically favouring one waste 
prevention method over another creates a rigid structure. By allowing waste prevention measures to be 
considered equally, the highest environmental benefit can be achieved . Evidence-based studies and 
evaluations can facilitate effective assessment of which measure is best-suited in specific cases. 
 
 
Corrugated cardboard packaging is renewable and recyclable, with a recycled content of 89% 2 on 
average and recycling rate of over 90%, making it the most recycled paper & board packaging in the EU. 
Such a circular form of packaging should be considered an ally in the fight against food waste.   
 
FEFCO is committed to supporting the European Commission’s efforts to prevent waste in the EU. The 
recognition of the role of packaging in this revision of the Waste Framework Directive will help to further 
reinforce the legislation. Future revisions should continue to strengthen waste prevention endeavours 
through the reframing of the existing waste hierarchy.

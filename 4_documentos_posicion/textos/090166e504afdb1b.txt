ACE Position Paper: Proposal on Waste Framework Directive 
(Food Waste & Textiles) 
 
Introduction  
ACE – The Alliance for Beverage Cartons and the Environment – provides a European platform 
for beverage carton manufacturers and their paperboard suppliers to benchmark and profile 
beverage cartons as a safe, circular and sustainable packaging solution with low carbon benefits. 
Thanks to their composition, beverage cartons allow to safely pack, transport, shelve and use 
sensitive products such as milk and juice. 
ACE is committed to continue its sustainability journey as demonstrated by the sector’s 2030 
Roadmap (www.beveragecarton.eu). 
ACE welcomes the European Commission’s (EC) revision of food waste and textile aspects of the 
Waste Framework Directive (WFD) :  
 
We support the introduction of legally binding food waste reduction targets, in 
line with Sustainable Development Goals and SDG target 12.3 to have per capita 
food waste at the retail and consumer level and reduce food losses along 
production and supply chains by 2030.  
Setting ambitious mandatory targets is key to effectively reducing food waste, along with data 
and measurements to provide necessary insight into where food loss and waste is occurring .  
 
However, we call for additional measures to: 
 
Recognize the contribution of packaging innovation in reducing food waste. 
Beverage cartons are a sustainable and essential packaging solution allowing the safe transport, 
storage and use of microbiologically sensitive products such as milk/dairy, plant-based products, 
juice and food (respectively beverage cartons pack ca. 75% of milk1 and 59%2 of juice in Europe), 
that provide consumers with essential vitamins and nutrients and are consumed daily by millions 
of EU citizens as part of a healthy lifestyle.  The microbiological nature of these products makes 
them more sensitive than e.g., soda and water.  
Aseptic packaging, such as beverage cartons, support the reduction of food loss by eliminating 
harmful bacteria, curbing the natural decay of highly perishable food and helping to extend it’s 
shelf-life. They protect food from physical damage, heat, light, and contamination, for up to 12 
months without the need for refrigeration or added preservatives. They can also contribute to 
food security in times of disrupted supply chains . Furthermore, processing innovation helps 
optimise food production and keep food from being spilled or spoilt. Efficient technologies can 
help get more out of raw materials thus reducing losses and allowing potential waste to be 
valorised. Consequently, our packaging does not only contribute to the resilience of food systems 
but also contributes to reducing food waste.  
Legislation should recognise the key role that food processing and packaging play in 
reducing food waste by providing higher efficiency, longer food shelf -life, traceability, 
and more circular supply chains.

Innovation in packaging and use of packaging when needed has been identified as one of the 
recommendations of the European Citizens’ Panel on Food Waste to speed up food waste reduction 
in the EU. This clearly recognizes the importance of packaging in preventing food waste , as 
packaging can ensure food safety and transportation of food in goods. Therefore we firmly believe 
that an additional provision should be added under paragraph 1 of Article 9a; “supporting 
innovation in packaging, taking into account the important role of food packaging in 
the food value chain to prevent the generation of food waste and ensure food safety ”.   
 
Foster separate collection.  
In line with our ambition to deliver the most sustainable , circular and safe packaging, t he 
beverage carton industry invests in increased capacity to  recycle the fibre and non -fibre 
components of our beverage cartons. Without collection and sorting, packaging designed for  
recycling will not have access to the appropriate recycling process and hence cannot be recycled.   
ACE calls for  mandatory 90% collection target for all packaging formats by 2030. EU -wide 
minimum requirements and a target for separate waste collection at 90% are necessary to further 
increase recycling, support investment in new recycling infrastructure by securing the supply of 
inputs and demand for recycling capacity, and enhance the quality of the secondary raw materials. 
To achieve this, separate collection must be easy for consumers. From an operational point of 
view, we believe that waste collection should rely on locally relevant and efficient methodologies 
(e.g., kerbside collection rounds from households and businesses, acceptance in waste centres, 
etc.). In light of the above, beverage cartons could be collected in a separate lightweight stream 
or as part of a deposit refund system (where this would help to reach high collection targets). 
 
Ensure secondary raw materials find their way into new products with the 
least environmental impact. 
To ensure circularity, it is crucial that secondary raw materials find their way into new products 
with the least environmental impact.   Paper recycling is already high -quality recycling, as it is 
recycled back into paper products  (often paper packaging products) , for which otherwise  virgin 
fibres would be needed. We support the Commission’s intention to optimise recycling i n order to 
achieve higher recycling rates and ensure high-quality secondary raw materials . However, we 
believe that a definition of high -quality recycling should not penalize already well -functioning 
recycling processes. Recyclability and high-quality recycling should not be linked with closed 
product loop requirements but with the quality of recycled materials and their potential 
to substitute primary raw materials.   
 
Ensure legislative coherence and alignment across waste policy to achieve 
Green Deal objectives 
To achieve high ambitions and streamline policies, the WFD needs to be the dedicated instrument 
setting waste-related overarching principles and definitions. The objectives set in the WFD must 
also be reflected and aligned across other policies.

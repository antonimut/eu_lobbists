ILA position paper -  
Proposal for a Regulation on circularity requirements for vehicle 
design and on management of end-of-life vehicles 
November 2023 
 
 
On 13 July 2023 the Commission proposed a new Regulation on end-of-life vehicles 
 
This proposed Regulation replaces the Directive on end-of-life vehicles (ELV Directive) that sets 
clear targets for ELVs and their components. It also prohibits the use of hazardous substances 
when manufacturing new vehicles (especially lead, mercury, cadmium and hexavalent 
chromium) except in defined exemptions when there are no adequate alternatives. 
 
We appreciate the possibility to comment on the European Commission’s proposal for a 
Regulation that for the first time seeks to cover design and production of vehicles placed on the 
EU market in addition to end-of-life treatment.  
 
We welcome the increased ambition and the desire to level the playing across Member States 
that is signalled by the proposed Regulation but in one important aspect we believe the 
Commission needs to reconsider its current position. 
 
Article 5 of the proposal seeks to restricts the use of lead, cadmium, mercury and hexavalent 
chromium in vehicles and Annex III provide exemptions from this restriction. This is taken over 
from the current ELV Directive.  
 
However, after the Commission impact assessment supporting the proposed Regulation on 
end-of-life vehicles was undertaken, Regulation (EU) No 2023/ 1542 [Batteries] of the European 
Parliament and of the Council was published. This new Regulation ensures that the entire 
lifecycle of ALL batteries is comprehensively regulated. Moreover, Article 6 of the Batteries 
Regulation already includes specific provisions for the assessment of risks to human health or 
the environment, arising from the use of a substance in the manufacture of batteries or from 
the presence of a substance in the batteries when they are placed on the market, or arising 
during their subsequent life cycle stages, including during repurposing or the treatment of 
waste batteries. This article provides Commission with the opportunity to propose a delegated 
act for restrictions on substances of concern if there is an EU wide risk.

In this regard we believe that the Battery Regulation already adequately addresses risks of 
hazardous substances, including lead, cadmium, mercury and hexavalent chromium, in vehicle 
batteries.  
 
Rectal 16 of the ELV Regulation proposal states that Commission intends to eventually transfer 
restrictions and related exemptions for battery substances, as appropriate, to the Batteries 
Regulation such that they should not be regulated in future by the ELV Regulation. However, to 
leave this transfer to some future date is not only an inefficient use of regulation, but it would 
also create unnecessary additional uncertainty for battery producers at a time when the EU is 
trying to encourage the development of world leading battery value chains to address climate 
change objectives. Removing chemical restrictions of battery substances entirely from the 
scope of ELV legislation would reinforce the consistency and coherence of the regulatory 
framework by ensuring that chemical management of hazardous substances in all batteries is 
overseen under a single piece of EU legislation, the EU Batteries Regulation. 
 
We request that: 
 
• Restrictions of battery substances be removed entirely from Article 5 and the scope of 
the proposed Regulation on circularity requirements for vehicle design and on 
management of end-of-life vehicles.  
• The next lead battery exemption review scheduled for 2025 under the ELV Directive 
annex II.5b(i) should be paused and instead incorporated into the comprehensive 
report required under Article 6(5) of the Batteries Regulation (EU 2023/1542) being 
undertaken by the Commission and European Chemicals Agency (ECHA) on 
substances of concern present in batteries or used in their manufacture.

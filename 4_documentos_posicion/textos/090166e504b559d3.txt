VF Corporation – Feedback on the revision of the Waste Framework Directive 
 
VF outfits consumers around the world through its diverse portfolio of iconic lifestyle brands, including 
the Timberland ®, Vans ® and The North Face ®. At VF, we power movements of sustainable and active 
lifestyles for the betterment of people and the pla net. Through our environmental sustainability efforts, 
we are elevating our commitment to take bold action on climate change to protect our planet for 
generations to come.  
Our enterprise -wide environmental sustainability strategy prioritizes sustainable m aterials, circularity, 
and sustainable packaging to drive scalable change by reducing the environmental impact of VF and our 
family of brands. VF and our portfolio of brands are advancing initiatives that align with the guiding 
principles of circularity: m inimizing virgin, non -renewable, fossil-based inputs; supporting regenerative 
processes; (re-)using materials and products for as long as possible; and reducing the waste to landfill 
stream. 
 
VF welcomes the Commission's efforts to revise the Waste Framework Directive (WFD) and would like to 
highlight few concerns on the design of the future extended producer responsibility (EPR) schemes that 
could inhibit the apparel and footwear industry’s ability to successfully design products with recycled 
materials and make compliance more difficult. Our recommendations echo the Commission’s intention 
to revive the textile sector, as explained in the EU Textiles Ecosystem Transition Pathway. The revision of 
the WFD must be an opportunity for apparel companies to grow in line with the ambitions of the EU Green 
Deal.  
 
1. Harmonization of EPR requirements and one, EU-wide register of producers. 
VF welcomes the Directive ’s ambition to harmonize the EPR scope, definitions, waste hierarchy 
operationalization, and cost coverage across the EU. We emphasize the urgent need for harmonization in 
reporting standards, frequency and unit of measure across the EU as well as across different national EPR 
organizations. Harmonization of fee structure and of fee eco-modulation criteria would reduce complexity 
and incentivize more circular design. One single EU-wide database for manufacturers registration rather 
than having separate national databases, will increase transparency and reduce the administrative  
burden.  
2. Harmonized end-of-waste criteria at EU level. 
VF advocates for harmonized end-of-waste criteria for textiles across EU member states. Despite the data 
limitations and emerging technology availability, it is vital to develop end -of-waste criteria for all the 
biggest fiber streams, such as cellulosic fibers (in particular cotton), synthetics (polyester, polyamide), 
blended materials (cotton -polyester mixes), a mong others. In addition to e nd-of-waste criteria , 
incentivizing the use of secondary raw materials will promote the recycling and reuse of textiles.

3. Obligated producers to be defined by selling the product to the final consumer. 
To ensure EPR fees are paid in the jurisdiction where textile product will be managed as waste, VF urges 
authorities to consider designating obligated producer at the point of sale to the final consumer.  
When selling a product through various channels such as retail, directly online or other channels, the 
correct designation of obligated producer will increase efficiency, avoid double costings and ensure that 
the right entity is covered according to the polluter pays principle.  
 
4. Recognition and incentivization of manufacturers’ take-back schemes. 
The WFD should recognize the existence and success of take-back schemes already operating.  Complexity 
of the EU legislation and the lack of harmonization between  different Member States  complicate the 
implementation of take back programs, repair, and recommence. Brand take-back schemes complement 
EPR schemes, support Member States in achieving waste collection targets and encourag e responsible 
consumer behavior.  Brands that design for durability to extend product life, design out waste so fewer 
raw materials are used or discarded and design for recyclability so that products can eventually be 
deconstructed, should see a decrease in EPR fees to incentivize this behavior.  
We recommend reviewing the report by environmental consultancy Eunomia entitled Further 
Considerations to Textiles EPR and Complementary Measures (May 2023), which was commissioned by 
a group of industry stakeholders that provides clarity and recommendations over key elements 
regulated under the WFD.

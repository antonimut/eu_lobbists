  
 
 
 
 
 
 
EURATEX Feedback  
on the Waste Framework Directive revision proposal 
 
Executive summary 
 
EURATEX welcomes the proposal for a targeted revision of the Waste Framework Directive 
(WFD) and the EU regulatory efforts to solve the textile waste problem, both to reduce waste 
and to enable textile waste recycling. The 2025 mandatory separate textile w aste collection 
in the EU requires coordination and investments to build a European textile recycling value 
chain which could recycle 2.2 mln tons by 20301. The proposal seeks to achieve harmonisation 
on key features of extended producer responsibility (EPR), which is welcomed , as EURATEX 
has been advocating for a harmonised framework, being essential for companies to operate 
in the EU. EURATEX also stresses the need to use EPR  as a tool  to support the circular 
transition for the sector’s companies, especially the SMEs.   
 
EURATEX outlines its main considerations for improvement of the proposed legislation and 
ensuring proper and effective implementation, whilst achieving textile circularity goals and 
preserving the competitiveness of EU businesses in a level playing field: 
 
1. EPR harmonisation is essential for achieving circular economy goals, a level playing 
field and reducing unnecessary burdens for companies 
2. Micro enterprises should be included in the scope with simplifications  
3. Make online platforms jointly liable to ensure proper compliance 
4. Effective market surveillance is needed to eliminate free riders 
5. Exclude PPE from the EPR scope, and include leather goods  
6. Common EPR fee structure is needed, including harmonised eco-modulation criteria 
based on the ESPR, and make the fee visible to the consumer 
7. Refrain from setting targets for recycling of textiles , market data is currently 
insufficient to inform better regulation 
8. Boost the development of the recycling value chain and support SMEs  
9. Provide sufficient time for implementation  to develop EPR schemes tailored to 
national specificities and a smooth transition  
10. Ensure equal treatment of all EPR stakeholders and shared responsibilities 
11. Prioritise the development of EU-wide end-of-waste criteria for textile waste  
12. Provide a holistic approach to EPR to ease compliance, and uniform registration 
13. Ensure a consistent legal framework and effective consultation with stakeholders

1. Drive harmonisation in EPR to advance circular textiles in a level playing field 
 
EURATEX considers that EU -wide harmonisation in EPR schemes is essential to achieve the 
environmental benefits they seek, by ensuring that the obligations and incentives are aligned 
across EU Member States. Implementation of national schemes with diverging  coverage or 
objectives risks serious inefficiencies in delivering a well -functioning system for textile reuse 
and recycling in Europe. Harmonised EPR will increase the effectiveness of the fee modulation 
and research and development for new and existing r ecycling and preparation for recycling 
technologies.  
 
A common framework for EPR will avoid variations in compliance requirements which will 
result in increased administrative burdens through lack of consistency and inefficiency, 
leading to additional costs for companies. This concerns particularly SMEs that do not have 
the necessary capacities to comply with different requirements when operating in several EU 
Member States. At the same time, it should be recognised that specificities among national 
waste management systems exist, and flexibility should be mai ntained to accommodate 
country-specific operational solutions (e.g. logistics). 
2. Include micro enterprises in the EPR scope with simplifications 
 
EURATEX recommends including micro enterprises in the scope of EPR. Acknowledging that 
there is not yet complete data for all EU Member States, these enterprises may cumulatively 
introduce significant quantities of products into the EU market, thus creating important 
competitive asymmetries and risks for losing relevant market information. They could also be 
used to circumvent the p rovisions of the Directive, especially regarding third country 
producers categorised as ‘micro enterprises’. Micro enterprises shall be included in the scope, 
based on volumes of products placed on the EU market, rather than on the size of the 
company. The industry considers that the proposed volume -based approach is an effective 
way to support, among other things, the management of administrative burden and 
assessment of the impacts of the legislation. 
 
In this regard, it is important to ensure that Member States introduce effective simplifications 
and facilitations regarding at least administrative requirements and payment of contributions 
at national level for micro, small and medium -sized enterprises. This will help to avoid 
unjustified bureaucratic burdens and streamline compliance.   
 
3. Hold online platforms jointly and severally liable for EPR compliance 
 
The inclusion of online sales from third countries is necessary to ensure a level playing field 
for responsible EU companies and contribute to the overall objectives of EPR and is strongly 
welcomed by EURATEX. In fact, the control by the national competent  authorities on online 
sales is challenging, given the high number of operators active on online marketplaces and 
  
 
 
 
 
 
 
product flows. It is extremely complex to obtain ex -post fulfilment of obligations by foreign 
companies that have operated illegally (as proposed). 
 
Therefore, to prevent large quantities of non -compliant products from entering the EU 
market, EURATEX considers that it is necessary to require online marketplaces to pre -verify 
operators selling products online to EU consumers, ensuring their continuous registration in 
producers registers and payment of EPR fe es. Additionally, EURATEX considers that it is 
necessary to attribute joint and several liability for online sales - either via own dedicated e-
commerce channel and via multi -brand marketplaces (platforms) - together with the third 
country economic entity that sells products to EU territory through these intermediaries. This 
should be done by means of uniform EU-wide rules to ensure effective compliance. 
 
4. Ensure effective market surveillance for elimination of free riders 
 
EURATEX highlights the importance of ensuring that each company that places products 
concerned on the EU market, including through online sales from third countries, complies 
with the legislation and pays its EPR fees. National market surveillance authorities should be 
equipped with the necessary capacities and infrastructure to ensure that there are no free 
riders undermining the objectives of EPR and disrupting the fair competition in the EU Single 
Market. A level playing field for EU companies is essential for competitiveness. 
 
5. Exclude all PPE and include leather goods 
 
EURATEX welcomes that the proposal covers household textiles, articles of apparel, clothing 
accessories and footwear, and no technical textiles. However, the products and their CN 
codes listed in the proposed Annex IVc may still cover personal protective e quipment (PPE). 
For instance, code 62101092 stands for surgical gowns and 62101098 for surgical drapes. PPE 
must be excluded from the scope of EPR, as they may present difficulties for collection and 
recycling and may also obstruct EPR by contaminating oth er apparel and home textiles if 
collected and treated together. Reusable hygiene products (CN 610822) should also be 
excluded due to specific technical functionalities and risks of contamination . Used products 
shall be excluded to support reuse and textile circularity (referring to CN 6309). EURATEX also 
suggests including leather goods falling under codes CN 4202 and 4205, alongside 4203.  
EURATEX recognizes that the implementation of the EPR and setting up producer 
responsibility organisations (PROs) requires, among other things, considerable resources and 
therefore, the scope of EPR should be focused, well-defined and clear. This will help avoiding 
overcomplicating the system and contribut ing to directing efforts more effectively towards 
the development of the circular economy goals and value added results. In addition, it will 
help the sorting at source which is also an important step towards efficient collection systems 
and processes.  
 
  
 
 
 
 
 
 
6. Seek a common fee structure with uniform eco-modulation criteria 
 
A common EPR fee structure is needed, including harmonised eco-modulation criteria based 
on the Eco -design for Sustainable Products Regulation (ESPR). The proposal refers to ‘ other 
Union law establishing harmonized sustainability criteria and measurement methods for 
textile products’ which is unclear and leads to confusion on the future objectives. EURATEX 
recommends removing any confusion from the legislation.   
 
EURATEX considers that the proposal lacks ambition on the issue of eco -modulation, as it 
mentions that the Commission ‘may’ set specific criteria for eco-modulation in the future to 
avoid differences in the application of the ESPR criteria. EURATEX supports further specifying 
of the criteria for eco -modulation that aligns with and advances ESPR objectives. Therefore, 
stronger commitments should be made on eco -modulation. Modulated fee criteria must be 
applied uniformly and consistently across all EU Member States, towards greater impact and 
creating real incentives for producers to adopt more circular design. 
 
Additionally, the EPR fee paid on each product should be made visible to the final consumer. 
This is to provide better transparency on the efforts and investments of the European industry 
to achieve the goals of and obligations arising from the green trans ition. This will also 
contribute to awareness raising in consumers and their ability to make better informed and 
responsible purchasing decisions. Consumers will be made aware of the sustainability 
performance of products due to eco -modulation, and become more conscious when 
discarding products after knowing the end-of-life management costs paid for their products. 
To that end, however, it is important to increase the knowledge of consumers about EPR and 
its relevance for better understanding the fees paid and avoiding confusion. 
 
7. Refrain from setting targets for textile recycling at this early stage 
 
EURATEX welcomes that the proposed legislation does not set specific targets for recycling of 
textiles, as there is currently no sufficient reliable data on the EU market capacities and 
recycling infrastructure to inform regulatory developments mandating r ealistic targets. The 
current focus of policy making should be on promoting and advancing circular goals including 
development and implementation of functional, cost-effective business models and recycling 
technologies, as well as increasing consumer aware ness, rather than setting binding targets 
prematurely.  
 
8. Support the development of the recycling value chain 
 
EURATEX welcomes that the proposed legislation enables financing operations to build the 
reuse and recycling infrastructure. It also supports funding for research and development of 
recycling technologies, particularly fibre-to-fibre recycling. These are necessary to help build 
  
 
 
 
 
 
 
the recycling value chain in the EU to recycle up to 2.2 mln tons of waste that will be available 
for recycling in 2030, and advance textiles circularity.  
EURATEX believes that EPR should finance the transition to circular textiles, where waste 
should be transformed into new raw material. EPR should not only be limited to research and 
development in recycling, but it should finance operations including the creation of the whole 
recycling value chain, meaning collection, soring, preparation for reuse and recycling, and 
recycling. In this transition, it is important to support SMEs, which may lack necessary 
capacities and resources, in pursuing innovation and technological development. To be 
effective, the legislation must be comprehensive and solution-oriented, and it should support 
the circular economy’s systemic change throughout the entire value chain.  
EURATEX notes that the proposed Article 22a (4)(a) point 3 seems to omit one element in the 
value chain, namely ‘preparation for recycling’, and should be amended to include both 
preparation for re-use and recycling. 
It is worth noting that, based on lessons learned, the most advanced EPR scheme in the EU, 
namely Refashion, has been supporting research and development in recycling for many 
years. However, at the current stage, the French scheme has widely enlarged the type of 
actions financed by EPR to include capex and opex. 
9. Provide sufficient time for implementation and a smooth transition 
 
The setting up of a national EPR scheme is a complex process that involves various 
stakeholders. It therefore needs to be ensured that the legislation establishes a feasible 
timeframe to properly design and implement the rules, tailored to the needs of the  national 
waste management system, based on proper consultation with all relevant parties. 
 
As the mandatory separate collection of textile waste comes into force on 1 January 2025, 
EURATEX expresses concerns about the time gap until fully fledged EPR schemes will be in 
place to support the proper functioning of textile waste management, as the proposal would 
require producers to finance the waste management costs of products after entry into force 
and implementation deadline. It must be clarified how the system will be organised in 
practice, and who will take care of the products that are now already on the market. EURATEX 
calls for utmost efforts to ensure a smooth transition and effective management of waste in 
the implementation period. 
 
10. Ensure equal treatment of all EPR stakeholders and shared responsibilities  
  
EURATEX believes that the legislation should set equal treatment for all EPR stakeholders to 
participate and benefit from the opportunities offered by the transition to circular economy. 
  
 
 
 
 
 
 
This is essential for building cost -effective national EPR schemes that drive business 
competitiveness. 
 
EURATEX considers that producers should not bear the costs of textile waste management 
for ‘social enterprises’ and ‘other non -waste operators’, which may operate and compete in 
the same market. Currently, social enterprises are responsible for sorting the  waste they 
generate and for bearing the costs of their own waste management. 
 
It is also important that social enterprises report on the waste they handle, as that is 
important to have all the data necessary to assess the infrastructure needed for recycling. 
These entities shall make publicly available data about the weight collected and the rate of 
reuse, preparation for reuse, recycling, specifying separately the rate of fibre -to-fibre 
recycling, and on the rates of other recovery, disposal and exports. 
 
EURATEX stresses that the legislation should clearly define terms used and the actors 
concerned. The proposal provides no definition for ‘social enterprises’ and ‘other non -waste 
operators’ that appear on numerous occasions in the provisions. To be able to  assess the 
impact of the legislation, a clear understanding is needed of who these actors are and how 
they influence the development of circular economy business models. 
 
Additionally, regarding the proposed obligation for producers to cover the costs of carrying 
out compositional surveys of collected waste, EURATEX stipulates that household mixed 
waste remains - by law - the responsibility of municipalities, and that such surveys should be 
within their responsibilities. 
 
11. Prioritise development of EU-wide end-of-waste criteria 
 
EURATEX welcomes the ongoing work by the Commission and the Joint Research Centre to 
define the end -of-waste criteria for textile waste and reusable textiles and highlights the 
urgency for its development and implementation. The setting of harmonized end -of-waste 
criteria is essential in the transition to circular textiles. Without clear end-of-waste criteria, it 
will be very difficult to recycle textiles into fibres and use those fibres again as raw materials. 
Moreover, if these criteria are not harmonized at EU level, there will always be problems and 
additional burdens to use recycled fibres in another Member State. 
 
12. Create a holistic approach to EPR schemes and uniform registration 
 
A system of separate national producers’ registries will allow authorities to better control 
registrations and the application of EPR, which ultimately remains a task for Member States. 
However, EURATEX stresses the need to avoid diverging registration procedures per Member 
State. It is essential to provide concrete and uniform rules for a level playing field preferably 
on the possibility to register through one uniform procedure for all Member States where a 
  
 
 
 
 
 
 
producer/importer is active, also with regards to the rules on public access for better 
transparency. 
 
EURATEX stresses that a link should be established between the different EPR schemes that 
countries develop, e.g. for plastics, paper, textiles, as it would be extremely difficult for SMEs 
to create and maintain records in so many different schemes at the same time. A tool should 
be created, especially for SMEs, that would enable the reduction of the administrative burden 
and make it easier for companies to manage information and their obligations. 
 
13. Ensure a consistent legal framework and effective stakeholder consultation  
  
EURATEX notes that EU companies already struggle to navigate through all the policy and 
legislative initiatives currently under development. It is therefore imperative to establish a 
clear and coherent legislative framework that provides predictability and minimises 
administrative burdens and costs for businesses. 
 
In the preparation of ongoing and future legislation, it is essential to enable a constructive 
dialogue with all relevant stakeholders and to consider whether the obligations and 
requirements are such that companies, and especially SMEs, will be able to co pe with. 
Effective stakeholder consultation will make it possible to identify the needed support and 
potential solutions to manage impacts on EU businesses and their continued competitiveness.

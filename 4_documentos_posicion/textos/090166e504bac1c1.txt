H&M Group view on the proposed revised EU Waste 
Framework Directive 
 
November 2023 
 
H&M Group welcomes the proposed revision of the Waste Framework Directive (WFD) which aims to establish a 
harmonised Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) for textiles and footwear across EU Member States. We 
advocate for unified EPR rules to drive a  shift towards a more circular operating model in the fashion and textile 
industry.  
 
While the textile sector traditionally follows a linear approach, H&M Group is committed to shifting to responsible 
resource use and transitioning to a circular system. There is still a long way to go – for the entire industry – and we 
recognise the need for change at a system level. If the proposal is implemented effectively, it presents a substantial 
opportunity to drive efficient textile waste management and promote a circular economy. 
 
H&M Group recommends to the EU policymakers to consider the below key policy recommendations for better 
incentivising sustainable design, establishing textile waste management infrastructures in all Member States and 
providing a harmonised and cost-effective implementation framework for textile producers. 
 
H&M Group is drawing its recommendations from the recently published report  by Eunomia consultancy 1  
exploring the textile industry's opportunities and challenges of end-of-life management of garments and providing 
policy recommendations for EU harmonised EPR rules. 
 
Executive summary  
 
1. Adopt a definition of ‘obligated producer’ setting the obligation at the final point of sale to the consumer.   
2. Support voluntary in-store collection of textiles.  
3. Establish harmonised and standardised reporting requirements for companies. 
4. Implement one single register for producers.  
5. Guarantee cost coverage of necessary activities under the EPR scheme.  
6. Ensure harmonisation and consistency of ecomodulation criteria among Member States.  
7. Set up clear governance for PROs and EU consortium of PROs for continues improvement.  
 
 
In light of the increasing fragmentation of EPR rules across the EU, the fashion and textile industry faces challenges 
with complying with divergent EPR requirements. Therefore, to advance the adoption of common circular economy 
principles and deliver on mandatory separate collection of textiles as of January 2025 across the EU, H&M Group 
strongly supports harmonised EPR for textiles and footwear at the EU level.   
Obligated Producer (Article 1, paragraph 4b)  
 
The current proposal lacks clarity regarding the obligated producer responsible for paying the EPR fees . This will 
likely create interpretation discrepancies and practices that could avoid responsibility. We strongly advocate for a 
clear definition of the ‘Obligated producer’ and recommend setting the obligation with the final point of sale to 
the consumer, as recommended in the Eunomia report.  This would ensure that rules apply to all market 
operators selling a product to a European consumer. It would also ensure that the  EPR fee is paid in the country

Voluntary in-store collection points (Article 22c) 
 
We emphasise the importance of safeguarding existing take -back systems in stores during the 
implementation of the proposed EPR rules in Member States.  These systems, managed by diverse stakeholders 
such as brands with in -store and online take-back initiatives, like H&M, as well as charitable organisations, play a 
pivotal role in advancing the transition to a circular economy, notably as the collection of used clothes is the first 
step in closing the loop. 
 
Furthermore, the provision of in -store collection points enhances convenience for consumers and, consequently, 
contributes to achieving high capture rates of textile waste. In our advocacy for inclusive take -back schemes, we 
propose a framework that accomm odates garments in any condition, thereby promoting both reuse and recycling 
practices. 
 
Looking ahead, as new Producer Responsibility Organizations (PROs) are established in Member States, careful con-
sideration should be given to incentivising or rewarding, for instance through eco-modulation of fees, brands 
that actively engage in implementing effective take -back systems. This approach not only acknowledges and 
supports the role of brands but also fosters a collaborative and incentivi sed approach toward achieving the 
objectives of the EPR scheme in the context of textile waste management. Brands should also be given fair access to 
set up collection systems under the future PROs.  
 
Harmonising reporting requirements for companies to PROs (Article 22c)   
 
Haromonising EPR reporting obligations for companies to Producer Responsibility Organi sations (PROs) is 
essential for streamlining implementation and reducing compliance and reporting complexities and costs. The WFD 
should play a pivotal role in ensuring the alignment of reporting requirements for EPR across all Member States. 
 
There is a need for the development of a standardised template for reporting obligations applicable across 
the EU. This template should encompass various reporting aspects, including timing, frequency, operational 
procedures and the data required to fully streamline the reporting process for companies . It is crucial that 
these reporting requirements also align with the specifications outlined in Article 22c, point 17a, referring to PROs' 
public reporting obligations, ensuring consistency in the data provided.  
 
The proposed template should therefore be accompanied by comprehensive and clear guidelines  aimed at 
assisting textile producers in collecting and accurately filling in the required data. Additionally, sufficient time 
should be allowed for companies to set up their data collection systems through reasonable implementation 
timelines. Ultimately, the details of the template should be clearly defined in secondary legislation in order to 
guarantee a consistent and predictable reporting system for market operators. 
 
One single registration for producers (Article 22b)  
 
Given that many textile producers operate in multiple EU countries, it is imperative that companies can efficiently 
handle registration of the products across the EU by registering once in a unified system for the whole of the 
EU.  This approach would allow producers to register in one Member State and manage their registration obligations 
in all Member States. This streamlined approach not only reduces administrative burdens but also ensures time 
efficiency for textile producers. 
 
Cost coverage of activities under the EPR scheme (Article 22a, paragraph 4)  
 
As recommended by Eunomia, H&M Group advocates for the EPR fee that considers the costs of waste disposal, in 
line with the polluter pays principle  to alleviate the financial burden on municipalities and citizens . We suggest

that EPR fee should focus on expenses associated with textile waste management, such as collection, sorting, 
transport preparation for reuse or preparation for recycling 2, emphasi sing funding for the capital 
expenditures (CAPEX) of recycling operations.  
Supporting the  advancement of recycling, particularly through robust research and development (R&D) for 
innovative waste management technologies, such as automated sorting and further enhancing textile -to-textile 
recycling, holds the key to making recycling cost -competitive with virgin fibers. This, in turn, will strengthen the 
development of a robust secondary raw materials market. 
Acknowledging that the current allocation of EPR fees  for R&D in new waste management technologies may be 
insufficient, it is important to underscore the significance of directing investments towards Horizon projects. These 
initiatives are essential for advancing technological advancements, ensuring sustainable development and fostering 
innovation crucial to effectively tackle the challenges posed by textile waste. 
Furthermore, we propose that the EPR fee and its allocation should be standardised at the EU level, specifically the 
budget allocated by PROs, providing Member States with a clear breakdown of how funds will be allocated for 
consumer information, campaigns research, R&D and educational materials.  
 
Harmonisation of eco-modulation of fees (Article 22c, paragraph 4)  
 
The Commission’s objective to align the eco -modulation of EPR fees with ESPR is highly welcome. However, the 
proposal lacks clarity on  how this alignment will be ex ecuted to truly create  harmonisation and consistency. To 
drive meaningful change in product design, we strongly urge the Commission to specify clear criteria for fee 
modulation across the EU in secondary legislation. Modulation of fees should be uniformly, consistently and 
easily applied across all EU Member States.  The WFD should prevent a situation where, eg. a product with 
recycled content is eligible for a discount in one Member State and penalised in another. 
 
Governance of Producer Responsibility Orgnisations (PROs) (Article 22c)  
 
H&M Group supports the suggested approach of the European Commission to have multiple PROs within a Member 
State, emphasizing the importance of maintaining an open and competitive market for a well -functioning scheme. 
We would like to see clear governance rules from the EU to ensure that all the PROs meet similarly high 
standards in terms of quality and operations but also costs and deadlines.  
 
Additionally, we propose an EU independent PRO consortium which should serve for information sharing and 
continuous improvemen t. This body should ensure a yearly exchange of best practices between the Member 
States. It could also outline EU KPIs and targets to accomplish in the next year. This body, equipped with deep 
knowledge of the textile industry and waste management, would facilitate a yearly exchange of best practices among 
Member States, outline EU KPIs, and set targets for the upcoming year, ultimately driving operational 
improvements.

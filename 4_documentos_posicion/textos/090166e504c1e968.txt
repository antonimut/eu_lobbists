MAIF feedback on the European Commission’s proposal for a regulation on circularity 
requirements for vehicle design and on management of end-of-life vehicles (ELVs)   
23 November 2023 
MAIF 
MAIF is a mutual insurance company open to everyone. MAIF is the sixth largest motor vehicle insurer, the fifth 
largest home insurer, the leading insurer in the associative sector and in the local authorities one in France. It 
covers the needs of about fou r million members (property insurance, personal protection, health, assistance 
savings, credits, etc.). Together with its 9000 employees, the MAIF group achieved a 4,5 billion euros turnover in 
2022. 
In 2020, MAIF became a mission-driven company – a status regulated in French law – by including in its bylaws 5 
social and environmental commitments. These principles guide MAIF in its day-to-day activity. 
MAIF is registered under the European Union’s transparency register under the number 62975755109 -62. 
Contact: Simon DESMARES – Public Affairs Officer – simon.desmares@maif.fr 
As an insurance company  committed to promoting the circular economy , MAIF is a n important actor in the 
management of end -of-life vehicles (ELVs) in France. As such, we have on several occasions shared with the 
European Commission the importance of:   
- Better traceability of vehicles and parts: this is essential to enable the market to grow  and become 
more fluid 
- Coordination of the actors involved in the vehicle life cycle: MAIF has developed an industry along 
these lines with the creation of a platform to put its network in touch with each other 
This response is a continuation of our previous contributions, in particular on: 
- The presentation of the European Commission’s roadmap for the revision of the end -of-life vehicles 
directive (November 2020) 
- The public consultation on the revision of the end-of-life vehicles (October 2021) 
To illustrate what kind of company MAIF is, we would like to start by describing our re-use of car parts industry, 
which has been structured since 2010 to supply the repairs for which we are responsible. 
1. MAIF, a pioneer in the re-use of car parts industry   
For each irreparable vehicle, MAIF offers its members: 
- Free removal of any vehicle that is roadworthy and complete (except under special conditions) 
- Destruction of the vehicle in accordance with regulations: before being crushed, the cars are 
depolluted and dismantled to recover anything that can still be used (e.g. doors, wings, indicators ) 
- The administrative formalities are taken in charge by our partner recycling company  
Regarding the use of reused parts: 
- When the accident vehicle is examined, the MAIF insured is offered the possibility of repairing the 
vehicle using used parts by the repairer and/or expert networks  
- Each part is selected, disassembled, checked and referenced using a bar code to trace its origin back to 
the source vehicle  
And so:  
- Every year, MAIF entrusts 30, 000 non-repairable vehicles to its network of approved recyclers

2. MAIF welcomes the measures concerning the recyclability of vehicles , but would like to see greater 
consideration given to the reparability of parts  
As MAIF is in favour of increasing the use of recycled materials in the design of new vehicles, we welcome:  
- The recycling targets imposed by the Article 4 
- The measures in Article 6 concerning recycled plastic 
The «  life cycle  » approach to car parts , proposed by the regulation , seems to us to be  the best way of 
encouraging the recycling of parts right from the design stage:  
- MAIF is therefore in line with the information requirements set out in Article 11 , as these will 
encourage repair and maintenance operators to replace parts  
- During the 2021 consultation, MAIF stressed the importance of better sharing of information from the 
manufacturer to the deconstructo r, to encourage optimal dismantling of the vehicle and the reuse of 
these parts  
However, it is important not to limit ourselves to recyclability requirements. The reparability of parts is just as 
essential:  
- Legislation must force manufacturers to design parts that can be easily or partially repaired , so that 
the whole part is not systematically changed, but only the defective part is replaced  
- For example:   
o Headlight lenses are often replaced because of damage to the casing, even though the 
electronics are intact 
o Similarly, the use of 3D printers to produce headlamp lugs means that the headlamp does not 
have to be completely replaced  
We stress the importance of reparability because it is a virtuous circle: 
- Environmentally: the use of eco-design ultimately leads to :  
o Reducing the use of (petroleum-based) raw materials  
o Produce less waste 
o Avoiding waste  
- Economically:  
o The reparability of parts contributes to better control of repair costs, which can make 
repairable a vehicle that is economically irreparable 
o As transport is one the main areas of expenditure for the French, car repairs are a lever for 
containing the surge in car-related costs  
3. MAIF stresses the importance of making repair data accessible and supports measures to promote vehicle 
traceability  
The re-use industry is not limited to the dismantling of parts, it also includes their repair. As a result, MAIF has 
made access to so-called « repair data » a crucial issue for re-use: 
- Under Article 11, while manufacturers must make dismantling data available free of charge, repair data 
is not mentioned 
3 
 
- This data is essential for checking the working order of parts and informing repairers about their 
characteristics (installation, settings and programming) 
- Furthermore, insofar as Article 32 of the text requires all re-used to be covered by a warranty, recyclers 
must have access to this data to ensure that they are in good condition. However, prices that are too 
high discourage the industry  
- This is why MAIF is calling to repair data to be added to the information  supplied to waste 
management, repair and maintenance operators by manufacturers, referred to in Article 11 and listed 
in Annex V 
- We therefore warmly welcome the establishment by Article 13 of a circularity passport for all vehicles 
on the market  
4. MAIF welcomes the measures on ELV management, but warns of the need for consistency between EPR 
requirements at European and French levels  
MAIF supports the requirements of Section 2 of Chapter IV on Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR), in 
particular :  
- Article 16 establishing a general EPR obligation 
- Article 17 requiring Member States to set up a register to monitor compliance with these requirements  
European harmonisation is essential to encourage cooperation between treatment operators and 
manufacturers. However, MAIF is vigilant to ensure that the new obligations introduced by the regulation are 
consistent with the existing French framework: 
- The law of 10 February 2010 on the introduction of an EPR system for vehicles  
- The decree of 24 November 2022, in application of this law, specifying the management rules for ELV 
and the conditions for implementing the EPR obligation  
Finally, we welcome the measures designed to reduce the number of ELVs escaping from approved treatment 
facilities and thus combat illegal channels more effectively. In particular:  
- Articles 23, 24, 25 and 26  (Section 3, Chapter IV)  relating to the collection of ELVs, their delivery to 
authorised treatment facilities and their destruction certificates 
- The provisions concerning the export of vehicles (Chapter V), the efficient implementation of which 
will be based on effective cooperation between customs authorities (via the MOVE -HUB electronic 
system referred to in Article 45)

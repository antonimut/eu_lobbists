FNADE’s position paper on End-of-Life Vehicles Regulation 
 
November 2023 
 
FNADE, the French association for waste management and environmental services, always supports 
any initiative aimed at both promoting circular business models and reducing environmental 
footprint of products and hazardous substances. 
Then FNADE fully supports the revision of the End -of-Life Vehicles Directive that makes a clear link 
between design issues and end -of-life treatment aiming to improve recycling efficiency and that 
requires mandatory recycled content. Shifting from a directive to a regulation also seems to be a 
good initiativ e and would help to provide a better harmonization of end-of-life vehicles 
management within the different Member States. 
Furthermore, the Commission’s draft is consistent with other EU legislation (Waste Framework 
Directive, the Batteries Regulation, the WEEE Directive, the POPs Regulation) but also with the 
European Critical Raw Materials Act that could help to increase EU’ s strategic autonomy through 
recycling especially in automotive sector that needs sustainable supply of strategic and critical raw 
materials.  
The proposal aims to tackle the lack of integration of circularity in vehicle design and production 
leading to high dependencies on primary raw materials. It would also set up measures to reduce the 
great number of vehicles that are currently not collected neither treated under proper 
environmental conditions and the large volume of non-roadworthy and polluting used vehicles that 
are exported from the EU every year. 
 
More circularity 
Establishing eco-design requirements (Article 4), reuse and recycling targets ( Article 34) up to 85% 
of the total weight of a vehicle, then setting up requirements aiming first to remove easily parts and 
components to replace them ( Article 7) and then at end -of-life stage to allow reuse or recycling 
before shredding (Article 30), are a real first step that will obviously improve circularity by increasing 
the lifespan of products. 
Proposed recycling targets up to 30% for plastics and mandatory recycled contents in new vehicles 
should clearly enhance eco -design, recyclability and recycling in automotive sector. FNADE fully 
supports the objective of 25% of recycled plastics in vehicle s and  the possibility given to the 
Commission to extend the scope to other materials in the future. Nevertheless, FNADE would stress 
that the 25% required in closed loop must not hinder free access to the recyclates market and should 
not set a priority acc ess to producers. Furthermore, FNADE is wondering which resins would be 
concerned by this closed loop.

Then FNADE would prefer to set up a 4 -year delay  regarding the implementation of minimum 
recycled content. 
However, FNADE does not support the provision foreseen in Article 28(3), according to which the 
mixing between ELVs and WEEE and packaging waste when shredded, is not allowed. This measure 
is not pragmatic and would hinder the current processing methods. Input to a shredder is optimized 
by metal type, size etc. and such a prohibition would only cause extra costs with no environmental 
benefits.  
 
Restriction of hazardous substances 
The proposal also requires restriction on hazardous substances at production stage but also at 
dismantling, recycling and disposal stage which is in line with the European zero pollution ambition. 
FNADE always supports provisions aiming to phase out hazard ous substances ( Article 5), to 
decontaminate waste ( Article 29) in order to promote high quality recycling without any legacy 
substances.  
The ban on landfilling for non-inert fraction of shredded parts will also have a positive effect on 
reducing hazardous substances and also on improving recycling or recovery rates. 
 
Improve collection and export control 
High collection targets that are set in the proposal is the first step to tackle the great number of ELVs 
that are not currently collected within Europe and to also harmonize the collection within the 
different member States. 
Proposed measures on digital passport (Article 13) and registration of producers should have a very 
positive effect on the monitoring of the life cycle and end-of-life of vehicles sold in Europe. 
Finally, better control on exports is an absolute necessity to reduce illegal exports on the guise of 
reuse in third countries , and the provision contained in Article 38 is fully supported by FNADE. 
Indeed, a clear difference between end -of-life and roadworthy vehicles will make it difficult to 
export vehicles that have become waste in third countries and will help to ensure an environmental 
sound manner dismantling, recycling, recovery and disposal within European countries supplying 
European industry with secondary raw and strategic materials.  
 
FNADE is the French association representing the entire waste industry value chain. As a key player in the circular 
economy, the waste industry produces recycled materials, fertilisers, and green energy to replace natural resources and 
fossil energies. It provides solutions to major environmental and climate challenges.  
FNADE in figures: 257 private member companies, 44,536 employees in France, 11.4 billion euros in turnover and 800 
million euros in investments. It is a member of the European Waste Management Association (FEAD).

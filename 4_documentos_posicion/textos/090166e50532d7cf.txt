Comments from SIGRAUTO on the proposal for a 
Regulation on circularity requirements for vehicle 
design and on management of end-of-life vehicles 
(The Spanish version is included at the end of the English version) 
From the Spanish Association for the Environmental Treatment of End of life Vehicles  
(SIGRAUTO) constituted in 2002 and formed by the Spanish Association of Automobile 
Dismantlers and Recycling (AEDRA), the Spanish Association of Automobile and Truck 
Manufacturers and Importers (ANFAC) and the Spanish Federation of Recovery and Recycling 
(FER), we would like to convey the position of the sector and some considerations that we 
believe should be taken into account in relation to the proposal for a European Re gulation on 
circularity requirements for vehicle design and on management of end of life vehicles (ELVs). 
Before going into the specific points of the Regulation on which SIGRAUTO and its members 
have comments, we believe that it is important to make a brief introduction of the current 
situation in Spain and what has been achieved thanks to the close collaboration between the 
sectors represented in SIGRAUTO and both central and regional administrations in Spain. 
The regulation that governs the management of end of life vehicles  (ELVs) in Spain is Royal 
Decree 265/2021 and thanks again to the previously mentioned collaboration, it already 
establishes measures that provide solutions to practically all the problems that the Commission 
wants to solve with the future Regulation. 
In Spain there are more than 1,400 Authorized Treatment Facilities (ATFs), 25 shredding facilities 
and 10 post-shredding facilities. All of them have obtained their authorization, proving that they 
comply with all the necessary technical requirements to guarantee that they can carry out the 
established environmental treatment and they also comply with all the requirements related to 
environmental insurance and staff training established in the regulations. 
The owner of a vehicle (any type of vehicle, not just passenger cars and light commercial 
vehicles) who wants to get rid of it because he considers that it has reached the end of its useful 
life, must deliver it to an ATF along with its documentation, since Spanish regulations establish 
that only ATFs can deregister vehicles from the General Vehicle Registry of the General 
Directorate of Traffic (the authority in charge of vehicle registration in Spain) and this procedure 
must be carried out electronically from the ATF facility itself . After processing the online 
deregistration, the ATF gives the owner both the certificate of destruction (CoD) and the proof 
of the  official deregistration of his vehicle from the Registry. This entire process has all the 
guarantees and has proven to be a huge success in which there has been no incidents. 
In Spain, the owners of any vehicle that is registered in the General Vehicle Registry have several 
obligations such as the vehicle having a valid Technical Vehicle Inspection (ITV) - roadworthiness 
test - and insurance, but, more specifically, it must pay the Mechanical Traction Vehicle Tax 
(IVTM) annually. 
With this situation, in Spain we can say that the problem of vehicles with unknown whereabouts 
does not exist since all citizens that want to stop being responsible of their obligations for having 
a vehicle registered have to deliver it to an ATF in order to obtain its deregistration. Proof that 
the IVTM is very effective is that in the month of December there are twice as many

We must point out that until 2021 there was a small "escape route" which were the temporary

a time limit. This has been corrected in Royal Decree 265/2021 by establishing that the owner

Regarding the possible export of used vehicles that should be considered  as ELVs and not 
exported, although the problem no longer exists for more than 15 years due to strong control 
by the customs authorities, the Royal Decree 265/2021 has established the obligation for the 
vehicle to have a valid ITV (roadworthiness test) in order to be exported. 
The ATFs carry out the depollution and the operations to improve recycling on the ELVs they 
receive, then they carry out the disassembly and preparation for reuse of all those components 
that may be used in the repair of other vehicles and subsequently the Spanish standard 
establishes that they must send the rest of the vehicle to a shredding facility where their 
treatment will continue by separating the different recyclable or recoverable fractions. 
Royal Decree 265/2021 establishes that only ATFs can sell used vehicle parts and we consider 
that this should be included in the future Regulation. Furthermore, in order to avoid the 
cannibalization of vehicles anywhere and without any type of control, the obligation must be 
clearly established that, for any used component that is sold, especially on the Internet, it must 
be possible to prove that it comes from an ATF. 
Both the ATFs and the shredding and post-shredding facilities have an authorization as waste 
management companies and one of their obligations is to report at least annually on all the 
operations they have carried out including all the waste entries and waste outlets. In addition, 
they are inspected both by the inspectors of the regional governments and by the state security 
forces and bodies that ensure the protection of the environment. 
During all these years, technological improvements have been introduced in the ATFs but 
especially in the shredding and post-shredding facilities that have allowed the recovery levels to 
increase from 76% in 2002 to 84% in 2006 and approximately 94% in 2023 which must 
undoubtedly be considered a huge success. 
Summarizing, in Spain, all ELVs are collected and treated by the authorized treatment chain that 
complies with the regulations and this chain already recovers 94% of all the materials that make 
up the ELVs. All of this is done in a competitive market, where ELVs have always had a positive 
value and therefore are economically self-sustaining. 
Therefore, we believe that much progress has been made and for this reason, we consider that 
the Regulation should aim to improve the existing situation and especially the problem of 
vehicles with unknown whereabouts in other Member States, but it should not put the systems 
that are already working at risk and especially the sectors that currently carry out the treatment 
of ELVs. 
For all this, we have some concerns on some of the aspects of the proposed Regulation that, in 
our opinion, will not lead to any environmental improvement and will simply increase the cost 
of current processes but which may also have other negative consequences. More specifically, 
we consider that the mandatory removal of certain parts and components at the ATF does not

Explanatory memorandum: Costs obtained in the impact analysis. 
Firstly, in relation to the Explanatory Memorandum, we understand that when statements are 
made regarding the costs that the implementation of the Regulation may entail for both 
administrations and users and especially in the case of “one in – one out” , c an lead to major 
errors, as already happened when Directive 2000/53/EC was under discussion and significant 
negative market values were anticipated when the reality is that ELVs have had a positive value 
in at all times since the entry into force of the Di rective despite having gone through very 
different economic situations, including some of the biggest crises in recent decades. 
Furthermore, setting a specific cost figure is an attempt at theoretical simplification and at 
fictitiously unifying situations within the European Union, when experience has shown that, 
among the different EU countries, there are significant differences b etween the costs and, 
especially, in the way of covering them and this is recognized both in the explanatory 
memorandum and in the “impact analysis” itself. 
The "impact analysis", although it is a necessary exercise, is still indicative and we consider that 
it throws very questionable figures and, in some way, induces certain formulas to cover certain 
costs (when, as we have already pointed out, said costs could not exist in many countries) that 
can be much more complex than what is reflected in the document. 
Whereas 38) Monitoring of costs and setting of contribution

assumes that this situation is going to change. Furthermore, it is indicated that producer must 
cover the costs derived from the obligations aimed at improving the quality of the materials, but 
how are they supposed to cover them? Who are they going to pay those amounts to? Can the 
producer partially finance an investment in a certain facility and not do so in others? 
Subsequently, it establishes that the competent authorities together with producers and waste 
management operators should monitor the average costs of collection, recycling and treatment

Article 16) Extended producer responsibility 
Both this article and much of the Regulation indicate that vehicle producers have the obligation 
to ensure that the vehicles they have put on the market in a given Member State are collected 
and treated in accordance with the Regulation and that waste management operators treating 
ELVs meet the established recovery targets. The companies that currently manage ELVs are, on 
the one hand, the Authorized Treatment Facilities (CATs), which number around 15,000 in the 
EU and, on the other, the shredding and post-shredding facilities, which are more than 300. In 
both cases, they are private companies that have obtained authorization from the competent 
authorities and that carry out their activity in a highly competitive market in such a way that: 
- The volume of vehicles that an ATF receives will depend, mainly, on its ability to offer a 
greater economic compensation to the last owner and this will be determined by its 
efficiency in processing when it comes to obtaining greater profitability from the 
vehicles it processes. 
- The volume of vehicles received by a shredding facility will depend, mainly, on the 
economic compensation that it can offer to the ATFs and, again, this will be determined 
by its efficiency in the treatment. 
- The volume of shredding waste received by a post-shredding facility will depend, mainly, 
on the economic compensation that it can offer to the fragmentation facilities and, 
again, will be determined by its efficiency in treatment.  
Vehicle producers have no way to guarantee that the vehicles they have put on the market, and 
that are owned by a citizen, are collected in certain ATFs since the citizen is free to deliver them 
to any ATF and the ATF has the obligation, according to the Regulation itself, to receive any 
vehicle since it has the authorization to do so. Interfering with this flow of ELVs would 
undoubtedly constitute a violation of competition laws. 
Likewise, the producer also has no way of guaranteeing that its vehicles will be treated in 
accordance with the Regulation since it does not have any authority to control the actions of the 
ATFs or the shredding and post- shredding facilities since it would interfere in the running of an 
activity that operates under a regime of authorization and control by the competent authorities 
in environmental matters. 
Finally, in relation to the obligation to guarantee that the waste management operators treating 
ELVs meet the recovery targets, the producer cannot interfere in a competitive market and, 
therefore, cannot determine which shredding facility should a given ATF send its vehicles or to 
which post-shredding facility a shredding facility should send its resulting fractions. Therefore, 
the ATFs will send the vehicles to the shredding facilities authorized and controlled by the 
competent environmental authorities. But, in addition, producers do not have the right to obtain 
information on the actions carried out by the ATFs and the shredding and post -shredding 
facilities and, even less, to force them to change anything in their processes because they are

independent companies that operate in a competitive market. If a n ATF decides to send its 
vehicles to an authorized shredding facility where the recovery levels achieved are lower than 
in others, the producer cannot force anything to change and cannot finance a technological 
improvement in that specific facility even if required by the competent authority  because it 
would be giving a competitive advantage to said facility over the others. 
In recent decades, we have seen how the competent authorities are transferring a large part of 
their responsibilities to other economic operators (without simultaneously providing the 
necessary legal security) and, in this case, to the producers. The Regulation establishes a large 
number of obligations for producers, in relation to the design and manufacturing of the vehicles 
they put on the market, and for the ATFs that are going to treat the ELVs and for the shredding 
and post -shredding facilities. Now, w hat does not seem logical is that the right to grant the 
authorization to an installation belongs to the competent authorities but it is intended that the 
producers ensure compliance with the provisions of the regulations. 
The proposed Regulation, like Directive 2000/53/EC, introduces a system that we could describe 
as “dual”: on the one hand, it recognizes the role of waste management operators (mainly 
dismantlers and shredders) who must obtain the proper administrative authorization (and that 
to a certain extent act as delegates of the authority since they are empowered to issue the 
Certificate of Destruction) and on the other hand an “extended producer responsibility” is 
superimposed. And all this on a complex product, but with a positive average net value, in which 
the decision to deliver an ELV is attributed to its last owner, which generally happens many years 
after having been registered for the first time in a Member state. In practice, this "duality" has 
been resolved since the entry into force of the Directive, reserving the producers a role of 
"guarantee in reserve" in the event that the network of ATFs was not capable of providing 
correct treatment to the ELVs on an economically self-sustaining basis. 
The competent authorities must be the ones  establishing what treatment the ELVs must 
undergo and must also be the ones who carry out the control that the provisions of the 
regulations are complied with and, in the event that they are not being complied with, they have 
the right to take the appropriate measures, leading, where appropriate, to withdraw the 
authorization. For example, if a facility is not carrying out any of the treatment operations 
established in the regulation or is not reaching the recovery targets that the rest are reaching or 
if the whole set of facilities is not reaching the targets, only the competent authorities can 
require improvements in their performance or withdraw authorization. The producer should 
only be responsible – as established in the Regulation – for covering possible negative market 
values when the costs of treating ELVs exceed the income obtained from the recovery of parts 
and materials. 
In the specific case of the obligation on producers to guarantee that waste management 
operators treating ELVs meet the targets, there is a contradiction with the article 34 itself, which 
establishes that the Member States must guarantee that the targets are achieved by waste 
management operators. This contradiction is also reflected in numerous points of the Regulation 
where producers are required to provide information that, as we have previously pointed out, 
they do not have and cannot request and obtain from the operators. 
The experience accumulated in decades of operation leads us to suggest that, in the text of the 
Regulation, the obligations that the producers cannot fulfil in practice should be eliminated and

Article 18) Formulas to comply with the EPR

through an producer responsibility organisation  but, in all the sessions in which the European 
Commission is participating in relation to this Regulation the Commission has repeatedly spoken 
of the possibility of producers adopting “hybrid” compliance systems, indicating that, for 
example, they could co mply with some obligations such as setting a collection system  
individually but others such as the control of t he recovery levels achieved collectively. In our 
opinion, this possibility is really interesting but greater specificity would be necessary in the text 
of the Regulation. We consider that the text should clarify how this “hybrid” model could be 
articulated because, at the moment , the Regulation does not include this option in any point 
and, therefore, it does not seem that this possibility really exists despite the fact that the 
Commission considers it as an option and that is repeatedly raising it as an option.

Article 20) Financial responsibility of producers 
Although this issue has already been discussed in considerable detail when analysing article 16, 
it is important to highlight some additional issues related to this specific article. First of all, we 
find that, while article 26 establishes that the owner of the vehicle must deliver it to an ATF, the 
costs to be covered by the financial contribution paid by the producers include the costs of 
collection. If it is the owner's obligation to deliver it to an ATF, it seems that the collection costs 
should not be included among the costs that the producer must assume, understanding more 
specifically the costs of possible transport of the vehicle to the ATF. The inclusion of transport 
costs would imply an unaddressable casuistry open to all types of mischief since the owner could 
decide to take his vehicle to a very distant facility to obtain a higher value, or the transport cost 
could be artificially increased or the owner might even say that it was necessary to transport it 
to collect it when he had taken it by his own means to the ATF. 
It is important to point out  that the position of the Spanish Association of Automobile 
Dismantlers and Recyclers (AEDRA) is that, as long as ELVs have a positive market value, the 
ATFs, represented by AEDRA, have no interest in receiving any economic from the producers, 
since if they received it the only thing that would happen is that the economic compensation 
received by the last owner when delivering his vehicle would be unnecessarily increased. 
Other costs that producers must cover are those of data collection and reporting to the 
competent authorities when, as explained above, producers do not have any right to request 
that independent companies - authorized by the competent authorities - provide them with data 
on their management, which is also highly sensitive information in relation to competition. 
The specific modification proposal is as follows:

Financial responsibility of producers

of-life vehicles; 
c) the costs of establishing notification system referred to in Article 25; 
d) the costs of data gathering and reporting to the competent authorities.

Articles 23.4) and 23.5) Collection points 
This article establishes that Member States may authorize waste management operators other 
than authorized treatment facilities to set up collection points. We consider that the figure of 
collection points should be either directly eliminated or restrict its possible authorization to 
exceptional cases for highly isolated territories such as small islands or with an extremely low 
population density and where it is found that there is no ATF that provides the service. 
Taking into account that ELVs are considered hazardous waste as long as they are not 
depolluted, it seems absolutely incoherent that, while an ATF is required to depollute a vehicle 
as soon as possible, these collection points are allow ed to keep the vehicles there for a year 
without doing anything. If this figure is not limited to exceptional cases in which no ATF covers 
the collection and treatment of ELVs, this figure could radically change the current operation of 
the treatment chain and represent a gre at decrease in the protection of the environment and 
human health since, by greatly reducing the obligations of these collection points – they only 
have to collect and store the vehicles without having any equipment – and being able to store 
the vehicles there for a year, they could create this type of points with a business model based 
on collecting vehicles and subsequently auctioning them to the highest bidder among authorized 
treatment facilities. 
In our opinion, this figure should be eliminated as should the new document to confirm receipt 
that is required even from ATFs (which is an absurd administrative burden since they issue the 
destruction certificate at the time of receiving the vehicle). In Spain, where there has never been 
even the slightest incident and where all vehicles have been delivered in the very extensive 
existing ATF network, it would undoubtedly be a tremendous step backwards that would only 
bring distortion to the system.  
In any case, if the Commission considers it necessary to maintain this figure, we consider that 
the possibility of establishing a collection point should be restricted to exceptional cases where 
it is found that there is no ATF providing the service and that the deadline for sending the 
vehicles to an ATF is reduced to a maximum of three months, and it may even be established 
that it must be sent as soon as possible, as stated for other aspects of the Regulation. In any 
case, the requirement to issue the document confirming receipt should be eliminated in the case 
of authorized treatment facilities.

Article 27. Obligations for authorised treatment facilities 
We consider that, with the current wording, after going through an authorized treatment 
facility, ELVs (which still include plastics and other materials) could arrive at steel facilities for 
melting without having previously go through a shredding facility. This represents serious 
damage to the environment in terms of emissions and non-compliance with recovery objectives. 
Furthermore, traceability through all links in the recycling chain is not met. 
Although the Regulation indicates shredding as a preliminary step, we believe that it is necessary 
to prevent the ELVs from not being shredded. To do this, we propose to include a wording similar 
to the one we have in our transposition of Directive 2000/53 into our domestic law.

Article 29) Depollution

En las últimas décadas , se está viendo cómo las autoridades competentes están trasladando 
gran parte de sus responsabilidades a otros agentes económicos (sin proporcionar en paralelo 
la seguridad jurídica necesaria) y, en este caso , a los productores. En el Reglamento se 
establecen gran cantidad de obligaciones tanto para los productores , en relación al diseño y 
fabricación de los vehículos que ponen en el mercado, como para los CATs que van a tratar los 
vehículos que llegan al final de su vida útil y para las instalaciones de fragmentación y post -

instalación sea de las autoridades competentes pero se pretenda que sean los productores los

están facultados para emitir el Certificado de Destrucción) y por otro lado se superpone un a

Artículo 29) Descontaminación 
El término “lo antes posible después de la entrega” no parece el más adecuado. En nuestra 
opinión, la Comisión debería fijar un plazo máximo para que las instalaciones autorizadas de 
tratamiento lleven a cabo la descontaminación. En nuestra opinión, un plazo de 30 días sería lo 
más adecuado ya que es el plazo actualmente establecido en muchos Estados Miembro. 
La propuesta concreta de modificación es la siguiente: 
Artículo 29.1 
Descontaminación de vehículos al final de su vida útil 
1. Tan pronto como sea posible Antes de que transcurran 30 días tras la entrega de un vehículo 
al final de su vida útil a la instalación de tratamiento autorizada, dicha instalación 
descontaminará estos vehículos antes de someterlos de nuevo a tratamiento, de conformidad 
con los requisitos mínimos establecidos en la parte B del anexo VII. 
Artículo 30) Retirada obligatoria de piezas y componentes para su reutilización 
y reciclado antes de la fragmentación  
Apoyamos a la Comisión en el establecimiento de medidas para tratar de los niveles de reciclado, 
pero consideramos que no debe existir total libertad en cuanto a las tecnologías para alcanzarlos 
y por tanto no deben establecerse  exigencias de retirada manual o semiautomática de 
determinadas piezas, ya que se han desarrollado durante más de 20 años tecnologías post -
fragmentacion (separadores magnéticos, inductores, sensores ópticos, rayos x, flotación, etc.) 
que permiten alcanzar los objetivos de una manera más eficiente y tanto desde el punto de vista 
técnico, económico y ambiental. 
Además, muchos de los componentes incluidos en las entradas 1 -12, aunque sean retirados 
manualmente irán destinados a su reciclado ya que las instalaciones autorizadas de tratamiento 
ya buscan todas las opciones de reutilización y reacondicionamiento o remanufacturado. Esto 
implicará que dichas piezas y materiales tengan que ser sometidos en cualquier caso a un 
proceso de fragmentación y postfragmentación para la retirada de impropios o sustancias. No 
entendemos el establecimiento de esta jerarquía de tecno logías sin ningún mejoramiento 
ambiental ni de calidad de las fracciones resultantes. 
La propuesta concreta de modificación es la siguiente: 
Artículo 30

Artículos 32) y 33) Comercio de piezas y componentes usados, 
remanufacturados o reacondicionados y Reutilización de piezas 
La propuesta de Reglamento debe mantener lo que establece en el Real Decreto 265/2021 
donde se deja perfectamente claro que “La extracción de componentes, partes o piezas para su 
preparación para la reutilización y comercialización únicamente podrá realizarse en un CAT y 
siempre de vehículos que previamente hayan sido descontaminados y se haya emitido el 
correspondiente certific ado de destrucción o de tratamiento medioambiental ” porque es la 
forma adecuada de garantizar los objetivos medioambientales, la se guridad vial y los derechos 
de los consumidores y usuarios.  
Que el CAT sea el que extraiga las piezas y las prepare para la reutilización es una garantía para 
la seguridad vial puesto que quien realiza la operación es un profesional que sabe lo que extrae 
y que es responsable de ello. Desde el punto de vista medioambiental si la pieza se extrae en el 
CAT, se garantiza que las operaciones se hacen en unas instalaciones adecuadas, por un personal 
cualificado y que está sometido a estrictos controles por parte de la administración competente. 
Desde el punto de vista de  la garantía de los consumidores, quien adquiere la pieza a un CAT 
tiene asegurados sus derechos como consumidor en los casos en los que exista algún defecto 
mientras que si se hace a particulares, a través de plataformas de venta online, tales garantías 
no existen. 
El futuro Reglamento debe poner coto a la competencia desleal que supone que haya personas 
que ejerzan la actividad del desguace de forma ilegal en cualquier sitio y sin ningún control y 
para ello debe establecer medidas que garanticen que quien quiera llevar a cabo la actividad de 
recuperar piezas deba hacerlo estableciendo un CAT. Para ello debe eliminarse cualquier 
posibilidad de que se comercialicen piezas usadas que no provengan de un CAT y esto es 
especialmente importante en el caso de las ventas por internet. 
Por otra parte lo que deben promover los Estados Miembros es la utilización de los recambios 
preparados para la reutilización por las instalaciones de tratamiento autorizadas así como de las 
remanufacturadas y reacondicionadas por otros operadores económicos. 
La propuesta concreta de modificación es la siguiente: 
Artículo 32 
Comercio de piezas y componentes usados, remanufacturados o reacondicionados 
A partir del [OP: insértese la fecha correspondiente al primer día del mes siguiente a los treinta

usados, así como cualquier operador económico que comercialice piezas de recambio y 
componentes remanufacturados y reacondicionados deberá, en el punto de venta: 
a) garantizar que las piezas y componentes estén etiquetados de conformidad con el

b) proporcionar una garantía para las piezas y componentes usados, remanufacturados 
o reacondicionados.

Artículo 34.1) Reutilización, reciclado y valorización 
Teniendo en cuenta que los objetivos establecidos hasta la fecha del 85% de reutilización y 
reciclado y del 95% de reutilización y valorización se debían calcular en relación al peso de los 
vehículos completos, no pueden mantenerse estos niveles excluyendo las baterías. Si se quieren 
mantener esos niveles de recuperación, deben incluirse las baterías en el cálculo. Es 
especialmente importante en el caso de los vehículos eléctricos en los que la batería de tracción 
supone una parte muy significativa del peso y si no se incluye en el cálculo será mucho más difícil 
alcanzar dichos objetivos. El establecimiento de los niveles de recuperación que deben 
alcanzarse de un determinado flujo de residuos debe hacerse tras analizar las opciones reales 
de recuperación y por tanto no se puede simplemente mantener los fijados en la anterior 
normativa si se cambia completamente el peso de referencia.  
La propuesta concreta de modificación es la siguiente: 
Artículo 34.1 
Reutilización, reciclado y valorización 
1) A partir del [OP: insértese la fecha correspondiente al primer día del mes siguiente a los treinta 
y seis meses después de la fecha de entrada en vigor del presente Reglamento] los Estados 
miembros velarán por que los operadores de gestión de residuos cumplan los siguientes 
objetivos: 
a) la reutilización y la valorización, calculadas de forma conjunta, serán como mínimo 
del 95 % por peso medio por vehículo, excluidas las baterías, y año; 
b) la reutilización y el reciclado, calculados de forma conjunta, serán como mínimo del 
85 % por peso medio por vehículo, excluidas las baterías, y año.

Artículo 49) Reporte a la Comisión 
En este artículo se indica que la Comisión deberá adoptar un acto de ejecución que establezca 
la metodología y las normas para calcular , por un lado , la cantidad y el peso de las piezas y 
materiales retirados en los CATs, el peso de los residuos reciclados, incluyendo los puntos de 
medición y la forma de calcular y verificar los niveles de reutilización, reciclado y recuperación 
alcanzados. En nuestra opinión , es sumamente importante que este acto de ejecución que 
sustituirá a la actual Decisión 293/2005/CE se lleve a cabo buscando la eficiencia con un enfoque 
pragmático.  
Es necesario, como reconoce la propia propuesta de Reglamento, alcanzar un equilibrio entre la 
obvia necesidad de recopilación de datos y la carga de trabajo administrativo que ello puede 
suponer para la eficiencia del trabajo diario de los operadores auto rizados. Ni tiene sentido ni 
es realizable en la práctica medir y pesar pieza a pieza y vehículo a vehículo, cuando se puede 
alcanzar resultados de validez semejante mediante la aplicación de medios base estadística.  
Teniendo en cuenta que las instalaciones de tratamiento autorizadas no suelen contar con una 
báscula, la propia Decisión 293/2005 establece que el peso de cada vehículo se debe obtener a 
partir del peso que se refleja en la documentación sin incluir en ningún caso el peso del 
conductor, fijado en 75 kg, ni el del combustible, fijado en 40 kg. En pruebas realizadas por 
SIGRAUTO comparando el peso obtenido mediante esta fórmula y el peso real (obtenido 
pesando vehículo a vehículo a la entrada de la instalación de tratamiento) se pudo constatar que 
las diferencias eran de aproximadamente el 0,5%. 
La Decisión de la Comisión 293/2005 , aun teniendo aspectos mejorables, incluía ciertos 
mecanismos que convendría mantener en el futuro acto de ejecución si , realmente, se quiere 
establecer una metodología apropiada para la realización de estos cálculos. En relación a la 
cantidad y el peso de las piezas y los materiales retirados en los CATs, la Decisión establecía lo 
siguiente: “calcularán la reutilización (A) siguiendo el método de substracción siguiente: peso 
individual del vehículo (Wi) menos peso del vehículo al final de su vida útil descontaminado y 
desmontado (carrocería) (Wb) menos el peso de los materiales descontaminados y desmontados 
enviados para la valorización, el reciclado o la eliminación .” En nuestra opinión , esta 
metodología sigue siendo la mejor forma de llevar a cabo este cálculo máxime cuando el 
Reglamento ha introducido unas nuevas obligaciones de trazabilidad en los envíos de vehículos 
a las instalaciones de fragmentación de forma que se va a saber , exactamente, qué vehículos 
van en un determinado envío desde una instalación de tratamiento a la instalación de 
fragmentación. Siendo la anterior la mejor de las opciones y posiblemente la que proporcione 
la información más fiable, en ningún caso se puede exigir el pesaje de las piezas preparadas para 
la reutilización en un CAT y de considerarse necesaria una evaluación del peso, ésta debería 
hacerse en base a unos pesos de referencia medios para cada tipo de pieza pero excluyendo 
aquellas que tengan un peso tan pequeño que no vayan a tener influencia en la cifra total.

Además de esto , para los cálculos de las cantidades que se recuperan en las instalaciones de 
fragmentación y de post -fragmentación, la Decisión 293/2005 establece que debe hacerse 
aplicando los resultados de las “campañas de fragmentación” al peso de los vehículos que entran 
en la instalación de fragmentación. En nuestra opinión , el futuro acto de ejecución debe 
mantener las “campañas de fragmentación” como metodología para las fases posteriores al CAT 
teniendo en cuenta que  las instalaciones de fragmentac ión necesitan procesar, 
simultáneamente, distintos flujos de materiales con diferente “resistencia mecánica” para que 
el rendimiento de la maquinaria sea el adecuado (dependiendo de los casos el rendimiento 
puede llegar a reducirse en más de un 50% lo que supone doblar el consumo energético) y eso 
hace que sea imposible asignar, de forma individualizada para cada uno de esos flujos de 
materiales, unos resultados concretos.  La Comisión ya cuenta incluso con unas “ Directrices 
relativas a cómo realizar campañas de fragmentación” y, actualmente, varios países emplean 
esta metodología obteniendo unos resultados fiables y verificables.  
No hay una propuesta de modificación concreta pero consideramos muy necesario que se tome 
en consideración lo anteriormente señalado en relación al punto 5 del artículo y al futuro 
desarrollo de los actos de ejecución que desarrolle la Comisión.  
ANEXO VII.B.2.c) REQUISITOS MÍNIMOS PARA LA DESCONTAMINACIÓN 
En los requisitos mínimos para la descontaminación, (ANEXO VII. Parte B), tal y como está 
redactado, podría entenderse que debe llevarse a cabo la retirada del sistema de aire 
acondicionado completo (que incluye, compresor, valvulería, tuberías, conexiones, 
intercambiado de calor, etc.) y no solo la extracción del fluido.  
 
Creemos que el objetivo que propone el Reglamento es la retirada del fluido y la gestión del 
mismo y no la retirada del sistema de aire acondicionado completo que supondría un trabajo 
complejo en el que se emplearía mucho tiempo y coste, pero sin beneficio ambiental. Al igual 
que para el resto de fluidos del vehículo, existen equipos de extracción de los fluidos de los 
sistemas de aire acondicionado que permiten su almacenamiento y posterior gestión según lo 
establecido en el Reglamento 517/2014. Tampoco está incluido como a componente a retirar 
en el anexo VII C.

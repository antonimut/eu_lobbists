2. Introduction
On 13 July, a proposal regarding regulations from the European Parliament and the Council
of the European Union on circularity requirements for vehicle design and the management
of ELVs was announced by the European Commission, after which a public consultation
period of approximately 5 months was conducted.
Based on this proposal, JAMA member companies will continue their efforts to improve the
circularity and sustainability of automobiles, while actively contributing to the establishment
of a sustainable recycling-oriented and decarbonized society in the EU.
This proposal is expected to have a profound impact not only on the manufacture and sale
of automobiles in the EU, but also on the industrial competitiveness of the entire automotive
supply chain, including small- and medium-sized parts manufacturers. In addition to
circularity and sustainability, we also believe that there should be no reversal to current
decarbonization efforts.
Accordingly, we feel it is extremely important to carefully examine what impact this forward-
looking proposal will have on circularity, sustainability, industrial competitiveness, and
decarbonization in order to ensure that it is sufficiently balanced.
With the above in mind, JAMA would like to share its comments as follows.

3. General Comments
• This proposal specifies management requirements are both detailed and cover a wide
range of areas, such as the management of end-of-life vehicles and the classification of
recyclates, including post-industrial recyclates (PIR), post-consumer recyclates (PCR), and
recyclates derived from end-of-life vehicles (ELVs). JAMA considers initiatives that enhance
circularity and sustainability to be extremely important, but at the same time, also feels it is
important to pursue economic rationality while maintaining and strengthening the industrial
competitiveness of the automobile and parts manufacturing, as well as the recycling industry.
For this reason, we believe minimizing management requirements for recycling to only those
that are necessary will promote both cost-competitiveness and sustainable recycling.
• Compensating waste management operators for their costs under extended producer
responsibility may lead to reduced awareness among operators regarding the need to reduce
recycling costs, which we fear may decrease the cost competitiveness of vehicles produced
by European manufacturers outside of Europe. In strengthening extended producer
responsibility, we feel it important to focus on both promoting recycling and strengthening the
competitiveness of European manufacturers, which will, in turn, lead to further development
of the European automotive industry as a whole.
• We feel it is crucial to carefully consider factors such as commercially available recycling
technology and economic rationality when regulating the practical operations of waste
management operators, such as requiring the removal of an extremely large number of
specific parts by operators. We also believe that appropriate regulations should be
established at the appropriate time, taking into account market demand, levels of
infrastructure development, and CO2 emissions. Furthermore, we believe that thorough
consideration of these issues will result in the elimination of unnecessary tasks, enabling
increases in recycling costs and unnecessary CO2 emissions to be minimized.
• Automobiles are products for which other important aspects besides recycling must be
taken into account, such as durability, safety, and reductions in CO2 emissions.
Furthermore, the automobile industry comprises a global supply chain of tens of thousands
of parts from countless parts manufacturers, the majority of which are small and medium-
sized enterprises (SMEs). Therefore, with regard to requirements related to various
designs, we believe it is necessary to secure sufficient lead time by interviewing interested
parties on the actual state of affairs. To comply with these regulations, we anticipate that a
vehicle development period of at least five years or more will necessary and kindly ask that
lead times be considered to ensure an adequate development period after various
7
requirements related to development specifications have been defined.
• Furthermore, in considering future measures, we believe that a thorough impact analysis
must be conducted of automobile markets and recyclers not only within the EU but also those
located overseas. It is our feeling that engaging in dialogue with Japanese automobile
manufacturers, suppliers, and others stakeholders who have effectively operated under
Japan’s Automobile Recycling Law for 20 years will achieve significant results and contribute
to the development of a better system.
4. Main Comments
Article 3
• In this proposal, plastics are defined as polymers. However, in consideration of recycling
technology, we believe it is necessary to start by targeting recyclable plastics, such as
thermoplastics, before adding other materials, and then only after taking into account market
technological trends and the level of infrastructure development, all while securing a sufficient
lead time.
Article 4
• Although methods of calculating recyclability rates will be reexamined in the future, from
the viewpoint of improving operational efficiency and minimizing related costs for many
stakeholders such as parts manufacturers, we believe it is necessary to hold discussions on
the premise of continuing management within the already established IMDS1.

If model codes are also added to existing vehicles, it will be impossible for suppliers to
evaluate and adopt sudden material changes.
• PCR materials must be used for 25% of the recycled resin usage rate for vehicles, of which
25% are required to be ELV-derived materials. Because waste management operators mix
PIR, PCR, and ELV-derived materials together as compounds, managing their traceability
would require significant effort and can lead to sharp rises in material costs. There are also
concerns that this may decrease the competitiveness of vehicles produced by European
manufacturers outside of Europe. We therefore believe that recycled materials should be
managed in their entirety and by setting targets without categorizing them as PIR- or PCR-
derived materials, etc.
• In addition, if high targets are set for areas inside the EU where infrastructure is still
underdeveloped, there are concerns that materials will instead be procured from outside the
EU, possibly hampering the development and construction of recycling infrastructure in the
EU. Furthermore, there are concerns that vehicle prices may rise due to increased expenses
incurred through costly management and adoption, possibly leading to a decline in the
competitiveness of vehicles produced in the EU that use the same parts outside the EU. We
therefore believe that targets should be set in a flexible manner, such as by raising them in
phases while carefully monitoring the level of infrastructure development in the market.
• Promoting chemical recycling is crucial from the perspective of resource recycling and we
believe that the automotive industry contributing to the use of recycled chemical products will,
in turn, provide significant contribution to the establishment of a mass-production system for
recycled chemical products for use by EU industries as a whole. We therefore believe that
recycled chemicals should be included in the count of recycled materials.
Article 7
• Although this article mandates that parts be designed to enable removal and replacement
in a readily and non-destructive manner, because the structure of vehicles varies by
manufacturer and because the tools and equipment used for dismantling differ by waste
management operators in each country, we feel it would be challenging to establish universal
standards that apply to all vehicles. Furthermore, manufacturers in Japan have already
announced their various recycling-focused design initiatives, which have been highly praised
by dismantlers. In order to promote recycling, we feel that it is more important to be actually
utilized by dismantlers than by using a type-approval requirement. We therefore feel that it is
appropriate to exclude it from the type-approval requirement or to require that dismantlers be
9
informed via manuals, etc.
Article 9
• The creation of a circularity strategy involves many requirements common to all vehicle
types, such as compliance with laws and regulations, data collection, and assurance
methods. Therefore, a circularity strategy should be created at the corporate level rather than
for individual vehicle types.
• Furthermore, although the circularity strategy will be shared publicly, disclosing confidential
information related to intellectual property rights and business strategies should be avoided,
and the scope of disclosure should be decided by each producer.

• The proposal mandates the declaration of the usage rate of recycled materials, such as

that recycled materials should be managed in their entirety without categorizing them as PIR-
or PCR-derived materials, etc.

Article 11
• The proposal requires increasing the range of vehicle types and requirements. However,
we feel that providing information on older vehicles (current production models and especially
models that have been discontinued) is not feasible as it would require significant man-hours
and is practically impossible. Therefore, the new obligations should be limited to new vehicle
types in WVTA2. (i.e., we believe that vehicles which undergo a change in vehicle type as
defined by the WVTA should be the subject of the new obligations)
• Furthermore, dismantling information is already disclosed via the IDIS3, and from the
perspective of streamlining operations and minimizing associated costs, we believe it is
necessary to utilize this system while enabling information to be provided for up to six months
after type approval in the same manner as the existing IDIS.
Article 12
• We believe that the labeling of materials, etc., is practically impossible in the case of older
vehicles (current production models and especially models that have been discontinued).
Therefore, the labeling e-drive motors should be limited to new vehicle types in WVTA. (i.e.,
we believe that vehicles which undergo a change in vehicle type as defined by the WVTA
should be the subject of the new obligations)
• Furthermore, smaller units of parts are impractical to label, and the effects of such labeling
is questionable. We therefore believe that regulations regarding labeling need to be
established, taking into account both physical aspects and effectiveness. Generally speaking,
we believe that labeling parts at the assembly level is most practical.
• Furthermore, because parts manufacturers need a preparation period, we feel that the
requirement should be applied from 36 months after the regulation is enforced.
Article 13
• Although the Circularity Vehicle Passport must be linked with information on removing and
replacing parts under Article 11, from the perspective of streamlining operations and
minimizing associated costs, we believe that the existing IDIS platform should be used.

Article 16
• According to the requirements, producers are responsible for collecting and treating ELVs,
and for ensuring that waste management operators achieve their reuse, recycling, and
recovery targets. Although we understand that the proper treatment of ELVs is extremely
important, because the actual collection and treatment of ELVs are performed by
government-licensed waste management operators, unless individual contracts with
producers are set in place, producers have no authority to enforce or manage the
achievement of targets. We therefore believe that the achievement of targets should not be
included in the extended producer responsibility, and that it should instead be the
responsibility of the government entity that grants licenses. Or, in the event that it is included
in the extended producer responsibility, we believe that only operators that have entered into
contracts with producers should be able to handle the disposal of ELVs.
Article 18
• Although the proposal requires producers or organizations for which the producer is
responsible to publish the status of recycling targets that they have achieved, because they
do not own the ELVs and have no contracts with waste management operators, they cannot
manage the data directly. In the case of Japan, the government entity that grants licenses is
responsible for collecting various types of information from waste management operators,
and producers who do not have contracts with waste management companies are incapable
of managing such figures correctly. We therefore believe that the publication of target
achievement statuses should be performed by the government entity that grants licenses,
and that the handling of ELVs should be limited to operators who have contracts with
producers. Furthermore, producers should be responsible for publishing the performance of
these contracted operators.
Article 20
• The proposal mandates that the producer's financial contributions shall cover waste
management operators' costs. However, this requirement is unrealistic since producers
cannot receive detailed income and expenditure reports from waste management operators
without contracts. Moreover, even if such contracts do exist, there are concerns that cost
compensation will hinder operators' awareness of cost reductions and significantly increase
recycling costs. Furthermore, this could result in a significant cost burden for producers
whose main sales market is the EU, and there are concerns that it could decrease
competitiveness outside the EU. We therefore believe that this provision should be removed.
12
• It is mentioned that while financial contributions from producers should cover the costs
related to data gathering and reporting to the competent authorities, we believe that it is
necessary for the government entity that grants licenses and each licensed operator to
perform such data gathering and reporting. Furthermore, we believe that respective
government entities and operators should be responsible for bearing the associated costs.
Article 21
• The proposal requires determining the financial contribution ratio between producers based
on factors like the vehicle’s weight and the proportion of non-recyclable materials. However,
because this is a contractual agreement among private-sector producers or organizations for
which the producer is responsible, we believe that it is necessary for producers to have
defined rights.
Article 22
• In other EU member states, producers are required to guarantee the net cost incurred by
waste management operators for treatment. However, for reasons similar to Article 20, we
believe this article should be deleted because it is unrealistic.
Article 23
• The proposed regulation requires collecting waste parts from vehicle repairs. However,
because existing operators already collect and recycle waste parts in the most rational way,
it would seem that a reuse/recycling market has already been formed. There are concerns
that this provision could hinder the reuse of used parts in the current market, and may
significantly impact existing operators from a business management perspective.
Furthermore, CO2 emissions may be increased due to the additional transport it would
require, etc. We therefore believe that this requirement should be removed.
Article 27, Article 30
• The proposed regulation requires the removal of specific parts. However, it is uncertain
whether these removed parts will be utilized effectively, and it will depend on various
conditions such as market demands, advancements in recycling technologies, and
infrastructure. There are concerns that the removal of unused parts will not only increase
unnecessary recycling costs, but also cause unnecessary CO2 emissions. We therefore
believe that the removal of specific parts should be left as a recommendation, or that parts
13
should be removed to the degree that is economically feasible.
Article 31
• The proposal requires that any parts that are removed undergo conformity assessments
and document management. However, such a detailed regulation could result in a significant
workload for waste management operators, resulting in higher management costs.
Conversely, it may also worsen profitability, possibly hindering progress in reuse and
recycling, and impeding the promotion of reuse and recycling initiatives. We therefore believe
that any regulations be limited to the extent that management workload is not increased, and
sufficiently consider the actual conditions faced by operators.
Article 33
• The regulation requires member states to offer incentives for reusing parts. However, we
believe that the content and level of such incentives should be modified in a way that does
not impede free competition in the market.
Article 35
• Although the regulation prohibits the use of ASR 4 landfills, due to differences in
infrastructure conditions between EU member states, we believe that the application timing
of regulations should be determined by considering the status of infrastructure in each
country.
Article 36
• According to the regulation, documentary evidence approved by the competent authority of
the destination country shall count towards the achievement of reuse, recycling, and recovery
targets set out in Article 34. However, considering that vehicles exported from the EU will be
recycled appropriately in accordance with the laws of the destination country, this is
something that cannot be controlled by producers. We therefore believe that the portion for
vehicles exported from the EU should not be included in the calculation of recycling rates.

5. Other Comments
Article 4
• We believe that a new calculation method should be presented early during the draft phase
and an opportunity provided for stakeholders to share their opinions on whether that
calculation method is indeed feasible. Because the compilation of data for calculation will
also require the cooperation of numerous global component manufacturers, any changes in
the calculation method may have a significant impact for stakeholders.
• Although data necessary for the calculation process is supposed to be retained, it is difficult
to permanently store such large amounts of data. We therefore believe it is necessary to set
retention periods that also takes into account the model cycle.
• In this proposal, the term "reusability" is used for the first time. However, clarification is
necessary as to whether it has the same meaning as "reusable and/or" used in the current
legislation.
• 2.(e) states that reusability rates, recyclability rates, and recoverability rates shall be
calculated. However, because calculating only the reusability rate would depend on the
market needs within each EU country at the point when a new vehicle is scrapped, it would
be impossible to confirm actual utilization. For this reason, we believe that only recyclability
rates and recoverability rates should be used, as has been the case.
• We believe that the notation order of the requirements in states in Article 4, Paragraph 1
and Article 34, Paragraph 1 be made consistent with each other.
Article 5
• We believe that the content of Paragraph 2 should be clarified so that it is clearly
distinguished from certification requirements.
• We believe that type approval for new vehicles should be limited to the four heavy metals
specified in the ELV regulations.
• We believe that exclusion criteria for the prohibition of the four heavy metals in paragraph
3(b) should include an assessment of socio-economic viability and should be applied upon
the enforcement of the regulation.
• We would like all L-category vehicles (motorcycles) to be included in the scope of this article.
15
Furthermore, we believe that the details of exemptions, such as their content and duration,
should be thoroughly discussed with relevant industries.
Article 6
• It is necessary to clarify the rationale behind the numerical targets set for the implementation
of these requirements across all EU member countries.
• We believe it would be best to clarify the fact that any materials derived from
repair/replacement parts can also be counted as recycled materials.
• We believe that calculation method used for recycled resin usage rate, which will be
determined in the future, should be globally applicable, and should be presented as soon as
possible during the planning phase. Because the cooperation of global suppliers is essential
for complying with requirements and collecting data, a certain amount of lead time is
necessary for preparation.
Article 9
• This article stipulates that a circular strategy be submitted within 30 days of type approval
being granted. However, because (EU) 2018/858 stipulates that OBD5 and repair information
be provided within six months of approval, we believe that these should be standardized.
• Although it is assumed that updates will be carried out using content similar to the current
preliminary assessment of recyclability rates, we believe that integrating the current biennial
assessment of recyclability rates into a five-year scheme would be more efficient.
Article 10
• We believe that calculation method used for recycled vehicle material usage rates, which
will be determined in the future, should be globally applicable, and should be presented as
soon as possible during the planning phase in order to provide stakeholders with an
opportunity to share their opinions. This is because the cooperation of global suppliers is
essential for complying with requirements and collecting data.

• If materials can be efficiently separated using post-shredder technology (PST), we believe

• It must be clarified as to whether parts subject to removal can be removed at the assembly

printed circuit boards are subject to removal from the vehicle.

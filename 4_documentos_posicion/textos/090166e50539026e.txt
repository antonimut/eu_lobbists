Make vehicles truly circular: 
FIGIEFA Position and recommendations to improve repairability 
and competitive independent services on end-of-life vehicles 
 
Introduction 
 
• On 13 July 2023, the European Commission published a proposal on circularity 
requirements for vehicle design and management of end -of-life vehicles, amending 
Regulation (EU) 2018/858 and 2019/1020 and repealing Directives 2000/53/EC and 
2005/64/EC (2023/0284 COD). 
 
• The proposal aims at improving the circular design and end-of-life treatment of vehicles 
in view of increasing reusability, recyclability and recoverability while reducing waste, thus 
“closing the loop” of the circular economy in the automotive sector.  
 
• FIGIEFA represents the European independent automotive aftermarket distributors. The 
automotive aftermarket consists of a wide variety of stakeholders, which forms a major 
ecosystem by itself, from production to decommissioning, ensuring that vehicles sustain 
green, safe, smart and affordable mobility over their entire lifetime. This e cosystem 
employs over 4.5 European citizens, in 500.000 companies, who are predominantly SMEs 
(cf. Eurostat figures).  
 
• FIGIEFA highly welcomes the European Commission’s proposal and wish to see it swiftly 
adopted by the European Parliament and the Council of the EU, taking into consideration 
the recommendations below. 
 
• FIGIEFA would like  to highlight hereunder some points that should be improved and 
addressed during the legislative process. Indeed, the current proposal does not cover 
critical aspects related to vehicle’s repairability (1) and it needs improvements to ensure 
fair competition between vehicle manufacturers and independent operators for end-of-
life services (2).

1. Include “repairability by design” as a mandatory circularity requirement for vehicles 
 
Issues • The European Commission’s proposal aims at improving reusability, 
recyclability and recoverability of vehicles, while reducing waste at their end-
of-life (Art. 1, Art. 4). However, it does not address repa irability as a “by 
design” requirement, thereby ensuring that parts and components from end 
of life vehicles can be repaired.  
• Sector-specific legislation (such as the Type Approval Regulation) sets the 
rights and obligations of vehicle manufacturers and independent operators 
regarding access to repair and technical information for the repairability of 
vehicles, but there is no “repairability by design” requirement for vehicles in 
the law at this date. 
• New vehicle production techniques may reduce the repairability of vehicles. 
For example, vehicle manufacturers are developing new single-piece casting 
by which large vehicle body parts are moulded in one single piece.1 Concerns 
are raised on the negative environmental impacts of such techniques when 
it comes to repairing localised damages on vehicles.2 
Solution • FIGIEFA would like to recommend the introduction of a general “repairability 
by design” requirement for vehicles into the law. 
• The current Proposal should also aim at ensuring the repairability of vehicles’ 
components and parts at end of life state wherever such repair is possible, 
which requires that vehicles are designed in such a way that repairability is 
possible. 
• FIGIEFA would like to recommend the introduction of a specific “repairability 
by design” requirement that applies on mono -material metal or plastic 
components heavier than 10 kg (as listed in Annex VII, Part C). 
• The European Commission should assess the environmental impacts of new 
vehicle production techniques  to ensure that they are in line with the 
circularity principles set out in this proposed legislation.

2. Fair competition between vehicle manufacturers and independent operators  
 
Issue • Vehicle manufacturers and independent automotive operators both provide 
maintenance, repair and end -of-life services to vehicles and car owners via 
their own channels. Fair competition is indispensable to maintain accessible 
and affordable services and to stimulate innovation and circularity in the 
automotive sector. 
• The European Commission’s proposal brings very positive measures to level 
the playing field between vehicle manufacturers and independent operators  
in end-of-life services, which should be preserved by the co -legislators (e.g., 
Art. 11, Art. 13, Art. 33). However, some points could be improved.  
Solution • Definition of “repair and maintenance operators” should be aligned to the 
current definition used in Regulation 2018/858 in order to ensure that all 
actors of the automotive aftermarket are covered by this Regulation. 
• More collection points for end -of-life vehicles : require Member States to 
allow waste management operators other than authorised treatment facilities 
to set up collection points. 
• Information requirements on removal and replacement : precision shall be 
introduced in Annex V to make sure that the wording “number” refers to the 
“OE number” , without which independent automotive operators cannot 
identify the part in question. 
• Removal and replacement of EV batteries: repair and maintenance operators 
should be able to repair EV batteries. 
• Removal and replacement of digitally coded components and parts in a 
vehicle: repair and maintenance operators should be able to use multi brand 
diagnostic tools to activate the parts and components. 
• Access to the circularity vehicle passport: access should be facilitated for 
third parties (including repair and maintenance operators who will be using 
reused replacement parts as part of their repair offer). 
• No unclear and unjustified charges imposed by vehicle manufacturers on 
independent operators to access information on the removal and 
replacement of vehicle parts:  there is already a binding requirement in the 
proposal on vehicle manufacturers to provide access to information in an 
“unrestricted, standardised and non-discriminatory” manner (Art. 11(1)). Such

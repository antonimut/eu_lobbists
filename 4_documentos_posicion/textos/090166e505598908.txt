 
 
Plastics play a crucial role in in the automotive sector, and contribute to the enhancement of vehicles’ 
safety, enable electrification, emissions reduction, and improve the overall environmental impact of 
the industry by making the components more durable, and lighter.  
Covestro welcomes the European Commission proposal for vehicle design and on management of 
end-of-life vehicles Regulation (ELVR, COM(2023)451)1 which outlines measures to enhance vehicle 
circularity, encompassing aspects of vehicle design, performance and information requirements, end 
of life treatment and extended producer responsibility (EPR) systems.  
Our company wishes to highlight the following points: 
Need for further clarity on definitions: 
In order to allow an effective implementation, further work on the proposed definitions is needed in 
our view. e.g. plastics definition to clarify which materials count to the target, recycled plastics to be 
in line with the recycling definition of Waste Framework Directive, post-consumer (plastics) waste as 
new requirement, circular plastics to clarify the proposed additional target etc. 
To illustrate the relevance of definitions a practical example : depending on the details of the 
definitions, recycled plastics from end-of-life tyres wouldn´t count at all to the recycled content target, 
because: 
- end-of-life tyres from used cars can be chemically recycled into raw materials for the 
production of plastics; 
- but since the target requires “ plastic recycled by weight from post -consumer plastic waste” 
they only count if end -of-life tyres are covered by the definition for post -consumer plastic 
waste, respectively if tyres are defined as plastics;  
- furthermore, even though they are post -consumer waste originating f rom cars, they would 
not count to the closed -loop target as end -of-life parts probably do not fall under the 
definition of “from end-of-life vehicles”.  
 
Support for a minimum of 25% target for recycled and circular plastics: 
Covestro supports mandatory recycled content targets in the automotive sector, as we believe this 
could create the right conditions for a market pull, thus improving the environmental profile of the 
industry. This support should be considered  by leveraging a ll options for alternative raw materials  
(waste, bio-based and carbon capture (CCU)).  
Instead of a minimum of 25% recycled plastic target, w e therefore support the recommendation by 
PlasticsEurope2 to consider a minimum of 20% of plastics recycled by weight  from post-consumer  
waste, to be complemented by a an additional 5% target for circular plastics, derived from recycled, 
bio-based, or CCU feedstocks. We consider that this approach, to be achieved through mechanical,

The definition of “post-consumer (plastics) waste” in particular is missing from the Regul ation. 
However, Covestro is concerned that an interpretation such as in the packaging and packaging waste

is highly restrictive, difficult to verify and implement, and a barrier to trade, as it would not allow for 
imports of recycled polycarbonates from other regions to the EU.  
 
At least 25% of plastics recycled from the same type of vehicle (closed-loop recycling): 
In our view , “end-of-life treatment rules ” are more efficient compared to  a closed-loop sub-target 
which needs a clear definition and a further verification process to be implementable. In addition, the 
dismantling list5 reads like a closed list . Covestro believes  that this would not account for future 
developments and innovation. It could therefore be valuable to consider including  a review clause. 
It is also worth considering whether – as part of the dismantling list - a generic part description such 
as "any other mono-material plastic components, heavier than 10 kg"6 is enough, or whether this can 
be further clarified e.g. plastic roof modules, plastic roof spoiler . This clarification would  avoid that 
future plastic part design is curbed to 9.9 kg  in order to  be excluded from the list, thus saving 
dismantling costs. 
Meeting these circularity requirements for plastics will require significant investments in waste 
management, collection, sorting, and increased recycling capacity. The targets should  therefore be 
accompanied by a set of  “enabling conditions ” addressing the  rules for calculating and verifying 
recycled and circular plastics, including mass balance.  
 
Acceptance of chemical recycling using the mass balance credit model: 
Immediate acceptance of chemical recycling, including a mass balance credit allocation model 
exempting fuel use, is essential for achieving the recycled content target and triggering investments 
for Europe's transition. Chemical recycling is a key enabler for the plastics industry to meet EU climate 
and circularity objectives. This technology diverts plastic waste from incineration and landfill, reducing 
fossil resource usage and overall emissions. Rapid adoption of the measurement methodology 
implementing act in Article 6(2) is crucial.

Addressing substances of concern and chemical safety: 
Covestro believes that the ELVR is not suitable for regulating substances of concern or chemical safety, 
as these are already addressed in the comprehensive REACH regula tion. Any ELV R updates should 
align with current and future revisions of th is legislation to avoid creating loop holes and legal 
uncertainty. « Substances hindering recycling  » should be addressed in the design for recycling 
provisions within the circulari ty strategy of each vehicle and the circularity vehicle passport. The 
proposal should consider innovative recycling technologies potential to address substances of concern. 
 
Extended producer responsibility (EPR) schemes for the automotive sector: 
EPR sche mes in the automotive industry are valuable for managing producers' responsibility in 
financing end -of-life vehicle recycling. EPR incentivizes the easy supply of high -quality recycled 
material for producers, promoting circularity. Collected EPR funds shou ld adhere to a transparent 
framework stimulating innovation and required infrastructure development for Circular Economy 
goals. Clear, predictable, and harmonized criteria are necessary for defining EPR categories and fee 
modulation. Eco-fee modulation sho uld not lead to material substitution unless proven to reduce 
environmental impact without compromising resource efficiency. These conditions should be 
minimum requirements in secondary legislation defining the automotive sector's future EPR systems. 
 
Adhesives in the dismantling and recycling of end-of-life vehicles: 
Addressing the circularity strategy for vehicle design (Article 9 and Annex IV) and the EPR requirements 
(fee modulation, Article 21) , the European Commission explicitly identifies adhesives as potentially 
hindering end-of-life vehicle circularity. Consequently, adhesives are considered as potential obstacles 
to circularity. The language in these articles , and the annex implies that adhesives pose significant 
challenges to the dismantling and recycling of end-of-life vehicles. 
Covestro believes  that such a targeted  yet generalized mention of adhesives as impediments to 
dismantling and recycling in the ELVR proposal is inaccurate. Adhesives can, in fact, facilitate successful 
dismantling and recycling. We consider that the current wording creates an uneven playing field, with 
other materials and bonding technologies that may impede dismantling. If mentioned, these materials 
or technologies should be adequately specific and reflect the existing pra ctices, successes, and 
limitations of end-of-life vehicle dismantling and recycling.  
 
Further development and public funding : 
 
Covestro emphasizes the importance of public funding, esp. European Framework Program mes and 
investment funds (like Innovation Fund) to support collaborations aiming at demonstrating chemical 
recycling technologies or new material development. Funding helps  to collaboratively develop 
solutions and build large-scale value chains from the collection and sorting of end-of-life materials to 
the production of new goods with increased recycled content. There are still many technological 
challenges to overcome, especially for upscaling technologies to industrial demonstrators and world-
scale plants, which also need to become environmentally and financially viable. Funding needs to be 
more focused on specific goals and more flexible concerning formats and timelines of projects.

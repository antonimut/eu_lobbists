The European Precious Metals 
Federation (EPMF) comments to the 
revision of the End-of-Life Vehicles 
 
On July 13th 2023, the European Commission proposed a new Regulation on end-of-life vehicles (ELVs). 
The proposal is based on an evaluation of current legislation, which consists of two directives (Directive 
2000/53/EC on end-of-life vehicles (ELV Directive)) and Directive 2005/64/EC on the type -approval of 
motor vehicles regarding their reusability, recyclability, and recoverability (3R type-approval Directive). Its 
overall objective is to modernise the EU existing legislation and to improve the functioning of the EU 
single market while reducing the negative environmental impacts linked to the design, production, service 
life and end -of-life treatment of vehicles and contributing to the sustainability of the automotive and 
recycling sectors.  
In auto manufacturing, precious metals have long been important for guaranteeing the environmental 
performance and achieving goals towards clean and sustainable mobility. Precious metals are used in 
various automobile parts, e.g. exhaust emission control catalyst, engine units, switches,  relays, 
connectors, breakers and fuses to name a few. As vehicles grow more high -tech, precious metals will 
become more important too. The automotive sector shifts to zero -emission mobility, and vehicles 
increasingly integrate electronics, as a result there will be an increase in demand for critical raw materials, 
incl. the precious metals. Moreover, the end -of-life vehicles become a valuable resource through 
recycling. Recycling of the precious metals, in its turn, will highly contribute to main goals of the Critical 
Raw Materials Act (CRMA), EU Circular Economy Action Plan and the EU's strategic autonomy. 
The EPMF welcomes the possibility to comment on the European Commission’s proposal for a 
Regulation that aims to facilitate the transition of the automo tive sector to the circular economy, at all 
stages of the vehicle - from design to final treatment at end-of-life. Therefore, the EPMF recommends: 
• the consistency with other EU policies . The EPMF suggests paying special attention to this 
issue and, to the coherence with the REACH. Article 5 of the proposal requires to minimise as 
far as possible the presence of substances of concern in vehicles and in their parts and 
components. It also restricts the use of hazardous substances in vehicles (especially lead,  
mercury, cadmium and hexavalent chromium) except in defined exemptions when there are no 
adequate alternatives. Vehicles and their parts, all contain metals with a defined hazard. As an 
example, the ongoing classification decisions and/or risk management procedures covering 
several strategic metals: e.g. lead prioritisation process under Authorisation (needed for precious

Article 36 of the proposal specifies that treatment of end -of-life vehicles may be undertaken 
outside the EU, provided that the shipment of end -of-life vehicles is compliant with Waste 
Shipment Regulation. Shipments of end-of-life vehicles to a third country shall only count towards 
the fulfilment of obligations and targets set out in Article 34: the treatment took place in conditions 
that are broadly equivalent to requirements in the EU legislation, including human health and 
environmental protection requirements. The EPMF is eager to see more coherence between ELV 
proposal and Waste Shipment Regulation and stricter rules on illicit exports of end-of-life vehicles.  
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
ABOUT THE EPMF 
Since 2007, the European Precious Metals Federation has supported European companies working with 
gold, silver, rhenium, and the six metals referred to as the Platinum Group including platinum, palladium, 
osmium, rhodium, ruthenium, and iridium. 
Our 35 Member Companies and 3 national associations include world leaders in extraction, refining, and 
recycling of precious metals. They also include a highly diverse range of companies involved in consumer 
and industrial applications that touch the lives of European citizens from jewelry to financial investments 
to the mobile phones in their pockets to the catalytic converters in their vehicles to the solar panels and 
rapid chargers at their homes.

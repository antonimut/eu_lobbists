POSITION PAPER 
The Proposed End-of-Life Vehicles Regulation 
Brussels, 4 December 2023 
 
Introduction 
ESIA represents the European semiconductor manufacturing sector which operates and 
competes in Europe and globally. The automotive sector is an important downstream user of 
semiconductor products. A conventional combustion engine car contains well over 1,0 00 
semiconductor products which control essential safety features such as airbags and 
automated breaking, calibration of fuel injection, and running infotainment systems. A typical 
electric car contains 2-3 times as many semiconductor products as a conventional combustion 
model. Most prominently, semiconductor products are used within the power train of electric 
vehicles, the charger, the inverter, the DC/DC converter, the battery, the central processor, 
and the motor. 
In this context, ESIA welcomes the Commission proposal for a new ELV Regulation that will 
replace the current ELV Directive to address the EU’s external environmental footprint linked 
to the export of ELVs and used vehicles and to facilitate the transition of the automotive sector 
to more circularity at all stages of the vehicle life cycle. Nevertheless, there are several points 
in the Commission proposal which should be reconsidered. 
 
I. Substances of concern 
Article 5(1) "Requirements for substances in vehicles", of the proposal sets out that vehicles 
shall be designed and manufactured in a way that minimizes the content of Substances of 
Concern (SoC).  The term "Substances of Concern" is a new term introduced by the proposal 
and which can also be found in the proposal for the ESPR . ESIA encourages to adopt a 
coherent definition of "Substances of Concern" throughout the entirety of EU legislation. 
 
II. Conditions for exemptions (deviation from RoHS Directive) 
Article 5, paragraph 4 delegates to the Commission the authority to adopt delegated acts in 
accordance with Article 50 to amend Annex III in order to adapt it to scientific and technical 
progress by exempting certain parts, components and homogeneous materials of vehicles 
from the obligation to be free of lead, mercury, cadmium or hexavalent chromium as set out

in paragraph 2, if certain conditions are met. In the present draft of the ELV Directive, all listed 
three conditions (according to Article 5, paragraph 4 (b)) must be met. However, in the RoHS 
Directive, it is sufficient if only one of the listed conditio ns is met. We recognize that vehicles 
are very complex products and exemptions listed in Annex III are very different and apply to 
completely different technologies or sub-systems in a vehicle. Accordingly, ESIA proposes to 
provide the 3 criteria with the additional parameter of an "any of the conditions" or an "or" link. 
In addition, the individual conditions differ a bit between the ELV and RoHS Directive. ESIA 
do not see a risk of lowering the level of protection provided by this directive due to this change 
as the three conditions are to some extend interdependent and thus the EU Commission 
remains free in not granting an exemption if the conditions are not fulfilled. On the other hand, 
this change would give more flexibility to the EU Commission, especi ally in case when one 
condition would be met with very high impact. ESIA considers it positive that in the present 
draft the socio-economic consideration and the availability of substitutes are already listed in 
the conditions for granting an exemption. 
 
III. Possibility for new exemptions (Art.5(4), last paragraph) 
Current and future developments in the automotive industry, such as climate neutral mobility 
and autonomous driving, will cause major changes in components and sub -systems used in 
vehicles, which will rely among others on lead, mercury, cadmium, and hexavalent chromium. 
To allow for continued innovation , it is essential that it continues to be possible to apply for 
exemptions for these and additional substances under the new ELV Regulation and that these 
substances will not be regulated under the REACH Regu lation. Such exemptions would 
usually apply to very specific niche applications that are often enablers for sub -systems and 
the whole vehicle. ESIA recommends defining a clear process and timeline for new 
exemptions, not to hinder innovations. 
 
IV. Wrong references and misleading wording “without delay” 
In article 5, paragraph 7, reference is made to the corresponding paragraph 4 of the article. 
ESIA proposes to review the current draft of the ELV Regulation to determine whether it would 
not be necessary to reference Article 5, paragraph 5 here. In additi on, this paragraph 
describes that the Committee for Socio -economic Analysis of the Agency (SEAC) will issue 
an opinion on the report and the specific amendments proposed. The European Chemicals 
Agency (ECHA) is then to submit this opinion to the Commission  without delay. ESIA notes 
that the SEAC opinion was previously associated with a corresponding public stakeholder 
consultation, and we recommend also supporting the development of the SEAC opinion with 
a possibility to submit comments as in paragraph 6 for the Agency´s opinion. ESIA would like 
to stress that with the transferral of new tasks to ECHA it must be ensure that ECHA has the 
proper expertise to deal with the assessments and that stakeholders are adequately involved. 
 
V. Recycled Content 
In article 6, paragraph 1, specific targets are set for plastics contained in each vehicle that is 
type-approved, which shall contain a minimum of 25% of plastic recycled by weight from post-

consumer plastic waste . The definition of plastics, as defined in  Article 3, point (5), of 
Regulation (EC) No 1907/2006 goes well beyond the definition of plastics used in the Type 
Approval Regulation which refers to ISO 1043 as to include materials which cannot be 
recycled and are well below the 100-gram threshold set out in Annex VI of the Type Approval 
Regulation. 
Moreover, plastics used in semiconductor components are unique within our industry to fulfil 
the high reliability, durability, and safety requirements for type -approval of vehicles and 
therefore not suitable for using recycled post-consumer plastic waste. 
Therefore, ESIA strongly recommends using the definition of plastics set out in ISO 1043 also 
in the proposal for an ELV Regulation. 
 thus going beyond those plastics as classified in ISO1043 and target therefore also amounts 
below the 100 grams . Plastics used in semiconductor components, do not fit within the 
definition of ISO1043 due to its heavily cross -linked matrix and its high concentration of filler 
material, and their amounts used are far under the 100 grams. These plastics are unique 
within our industry to fulfil the high reliability, durability, and safety requirements for type -
approval of vehicles and therefore not suitable for using recycled post-consumer plastic waste. 
It is therefore important to limit the specific scope of plastics to only those plastics defined in 
ISO1043 and above the 100 grams, or where:  
i. the use of virgin plastic is unavoidable; 
ii. it is demonstrated that socio-economic benefits outweigh the risk to human health or 
the environment arising from the use of plastics with post-consumer plastic waste;  
iii. there are no suitable alternative substances or technologies. 
 
VI. Tracking Information 
In article 10 the tracking information such as the volume of recycled materials used in new 
vehicles is required. Current information sharing in the automotive market is based on IMDS 
system https://www.mdsystem.com/imdsnt/startpage/index.jsp. This is the automobile 
industry's material data system, it is a voluntary used tool, not an official required data bank. 
The system allows the tacking of substances content of the material used in the automobile 
and the relevant recycled content. Rules for reporting are defined according to the tool. Clear 
definition of recycling and on content reporting should be defined to comply with the Directive 
requirements to gain clarity and ensure applicability. 
 
 
Conclusion 
ESIA would like to reiterate that it welcomes the proposal to replace the current ELV Directive 
with a Regulation as this will ensure more coherence across the Union. ESIA would like to ask 
the Commission to take into account the suggestions made in this position paper and amend 
its proposal for an ELV Regulation accordingly.

ABOUT ESIA 
The European Semiconductor Industry Association (ESIA) is the voice of the semiconductor industry in 
Europe. Its mission is to represent and promote the common interests of the Europe -based 
semiconductor industry towards the European institutions and stake holders in order to ensure a 
sustainable business environment and foster its global competitiveness. As a provider of key enabling 
technologies, the industry creates innovative solutions for industrial development, contributing to 
economic growth and responding to major societal challenges. Being ranked as the most R&D-intensive 
sector by the European Commission, the European semiconductor ecosystem supports approx. 
200.000 jobs directly and up to 1.000.000 induced jobs in systems, applications and services in Europe. 
Overall, micro- and nano-electronics enable the generation of at least 10% of GDP in Europe and the 
world.

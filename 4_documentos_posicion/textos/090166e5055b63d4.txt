About the ICCT 
The International Council on Clean Transportation (ICCT) is an independent 
nonprofit organization founded in 2001 to provide first-rate, unbiased research and 
technical and scientific analysis to environmental regulators. In the last five years 
alone, we have worked successfully with regulators and lawmakers around the world 
and have played a significant role in 48 distinct regulations and policies. Together, 
these efforts are projected to result in billions of tons of carbon dioxide reductions 
and prevent thousands of premature deaths over the next decade and beyond. 
 
General Feedback 
The ICCT welcomes the opportunity to comment on the Regulation of the European 
Parliament and of the Council on circularity requirements for vehicle design and on 
management of end-of-life vehicles, amending Regulations (EU) 2018/858 and 
2019/1020 and repealing Directives 2000/53/EC and 2005/64/EC. 
 
We appreciate the concept of linking the design of vehicles to their end-of-life 
handling, to enhance their circularity and reduce the environmental impact of the 
materials. We welcome in particular the focus on the selected materials. For 
conventional internal combustion engines, depending on the vehicle model, around 
80% of manufacturing greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions come from the materials 
(mining and material production). For electric vehicles, non-battery materials are 
responsible for around 50% of material-related GHG emissions. Out of the materials 
present in an average vehicle, steel plays the most important role when it comes to 
emissions, as the processes for producing primary (i.e. virgin) steel are still very 
much relying on coal and natural gas. Increasing the share of recycled steel in 
vehicle production could significantly abate emissions. At the same time, increasing 
the quantity and quality of steel recovered from the recycling of end-of-life vehicles is 
important to ensure that more recycled steel is available.  
 
We believe that setting a minimum recycled content for steel for vehicle production 
would send the right signal to the industry, and help prepare industrial processes to 
increased circularity of vehicles. More specifically, a minimum share of recycled steel 
content in new vehicles will reduce the GHG emissions related to vehicle production; 
promote the market for secondary materials; push companies to design their 
products with recyclability in mind and reduce the problem of polluting elements 
found in recycled steel (e.g. copper). Although efforts are sparse, some auto 
manufacturers have already started disclosing the share of recycled steel they use in 
vehicle production (e.g., BMW, Renault, and Volvo with average recycled steel 
shares of 8%, 11%, and 15%, respectively), which is a sign that the industry is 
preparing for the shift from virgin materials to circular ones. Setting a clear minimum 
share of recycled steel would encourage these efforts and provide the right market 
signal for the use of more sustainable materials. In addition to environmental 
benefits, a more circular usage of steel reduces the European Union’s reliance on 
the international supply chains for iron ore. 
 
On this note, we welcome the introduction of an extended producer responsibility, 
which can positively motivate advanced design for recycling. This will also help avoid 
the need for more advanced scrap separation and refining technologies to increase

Specific Feedback 
 
Article 2: Scope 
Vehicle types M2, M3, N2, N3, and O are excluded from the provisions in articles 4-
13, 21-22, 28, and 30-36 of the Regulation. Vehicle types L3e, L4e, L5e, L6e and 
L7e, in contrast, are excluded from the provisions in articles 38-45. We do not see 
the need for such an exclusion, especially for what concerns reusability, recyclability, 
recoverability; recycled materials content; substances present in vehicles; design 
requirements; extended producer responsibility; general requirements for shredding; 
and information provisions. We encourage to include all types of road vehicles in all 
of the provisions of the Regulation. Considering also that the provisions for vehicle 
types M2, M3, N2, N3, O, and vehicle types L3e, L4e, L5e, L6e and L7e are set to 
become valid only after 60 months from the entry into force of the Regulation, we 
believe there is plenty of time for vehicle manufacturers to prepare to comply with the 
Regulation. 
 
Article 6: Minimum recycled content in vehicles 
Paragraph 3 
We strongly believe this Regulation should set a minimum requirement of recycled 
steel content in vehicles. Using recycled steel will reduce the GHG emissions related 
to vehicle production; promote the market for secondary materials; push companies 
to design their products with recyclability in mind and reduce the problem of polluting 
elements found in recycled steel (e.g. copper). Although efforts are sparse, some 
auto manufacturers have already started disclosing the share of recycled steel they 
use in vehicle production (e.g., BMW, Renault, and Volvo with average recycled steel 
shares of 8%, 11%, and 15%, respectively), which is a sign that the industry is 
preparing for the shift from virgin materials to circular ones. Setting a clear minimum 
share of recycled steel would encourage these efforts and provide the right market 
signal for the use of more sustainable materials.

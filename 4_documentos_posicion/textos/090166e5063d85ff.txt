COM M ENT  
Comment 
of the German Insurance Association (GDV)  
ID-number 6437280268 -55 
 
on the  proposals amending inter alia  Directive 2013/11/EU 
on alternative dispute resolution for consumer disputes  and 
repealing Regulation (EU) No 524/2013  on online dispute 
resolution for consumer disputes  
Summary 
 
The German insurance industry welcomes the opportunity to comment on the pro-
posal of the EU Commission. The German insurance ombudsman, founded in 
2001, is a good example of a successful and well-received alternative dispute res-
olution body. 
 
We support the EU Commission’s objective to eliminate redundant bureaucracy, 
inter alia by abrogating the ODR Regulation. However, regarding the extension of 
the scope of the ADR Directive, we are concerned that the proposal does not con-
sider all implications that this approach would entail. Whereas including disputes 
arising from pre-contractual obligations could be beneficial for consumers, we have 
strong reservations against including disputes on non-contractual consumer rights 
in the scope of the Directive. 
 
In any case, it is essential to clarify that the ADR procedure is only open to claims 
accorded to individual consumer by other sources of law and does not allow for an 
actio popularis.

Necessary changes to the proposal 
 
• If an extension of the scope is considered, it should only include disputes aris-
ing from pre -contractual obligations. ADR is not a suitable forum to resolve 
disputes arising from non-contractual situations. 
• If, however, the scope is extended to non-contractual consumer rights, the Di-
rective should clarify that no claims can be pursued in an ADR procedure which 
the claimant could not pursue in a regular court. It is important to establish that 
ADR does not create new causes of action but provides an alternative way to 
pursue claims which are based on other sources of law. 
 
Justification 
 
The ombudsman responsible for insurance matters in Germany has established 
himself as an out-of-court dispute resolution body and has proved his worth. He is 
well received by consumers and companies alike. Thanks to his high level of ex-
pertise in insurance contract law, he can resolve quickly, effectively, and cost -ef-
fectively the proceedings he receives. Statistics show the ombudsman has con-
sistently received between 1 1,000 and 14,000 admissible complaints per year in 
recent years. ADR should stay an affordable, simple and fast way for the consum-
ers to resolve disputes. 
 
Against this background an extension of the scope to disputes arising from pre-
contractual obligations could be considered. Considering the close link between 
pre-contractual obligations and the relevant contract law, such an extension could 
enable more consumers to benefit from the expertise of the ADR entities in their 
respective areas of responsibility. 
 
However, we are concerned that an extension of the scope beyond pre-contractual 
obligations to encompass non-contractual rights would lead to a weakening of 
the ADR entities. 
 
The effectiveness and acceptance of the ADR entities and their decisions are 
based on their high level of knowledge and expertise in their areas of competence. 
Their success is largely due to this focus – as an example the specialisation of the 
German insurance ombudsman on insurance contract law. Removing this focus by 
extending the scope of ADR to non-contractual rights would overburden these en-
tities, create longer durations of proceedings and higher costs. 
 
Furthermore, an important factor contributing to the speed and efficiency of ADR 
entities is the restriction of admissible type of evidence in the procedure. The

German insurance ombudsman , f or instance,  only considers documentary evi-
dence. In contractual disputes, this is in most cases sufficient since these disputes 
generally arise from  legal questions, such as the interpretation of contractual 
terms. However, in non -contractual disputes, where the underlying facts are al-
ready in dispute, the ADR entities could only provide limited assistance due to their 
lack of investigative powers. 
 
If the proposed coverage of non -contractual claims is maintained despite these 
reservations, it is essential to clarify that the ADR Directive does not create new 
causes of action. In other words, the ADR mechanism should only be available for 
claims that can also be pursued by the claimant through ordinary courts. 
 
For example, the reference to Union law on unfair commercial practices ( para-
graph, point i of Article 2 ADR Directive as proposed by the EU Commission) could 
lead to legal uncertainty if no such clarification is given. The Unfair commercial 
practices directive provides for different causes of action, not all of which are open 
to individual consumers. Any consumer can seek compensation, price reduction, 
or the termination of the contract for specific damages suffered from a violation of 
the Directive (Article 11a UCPD). On the other hand, claims for injunctive relief are 
restricted to certain persons or organizations (Art icle 11 UCPD). The legislative 
objective behind this restriction is to prevent a flood of individual proceedings for 
injunctive relief. 
 
Therefore, it is important to clarify that the ADR Directive does not create additional 
individual causes of action but provides an alternative resolution mechanism for 
claims arising from other sou rces of law (e. g. Art icle 11a UCPD). Opening the 
ADR procedure to claims brought forward by individual consumers who are not 
themselves affected by the alleged infringement (“action popularis”) would contra-
dict the objectives of the ADR Directive and significantly weaken its acceptance.

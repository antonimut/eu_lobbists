COM(2023) 649 final 
 
Proposal for a directive amending Directive 2013/11/EU on Alternative 
Dispute Resolution (ADR) 
Suomen Yrittäjät – the Federation of Finnish Enterprises (FFE) is an inter-
est and service organisation for small and medium-sized enterprises 
(SMEs) for all fields around entire Finland and their owners. As Finland’s 
largest organisation, representing over 100.000 enterprises, we work every 
day to support free entrepreneurship and an entrepreneur-friendly society. 
We thank for the opportunity to express our opinion and views on the given 
proposal for amending Alternative Dispute Resolution framework. 
1. General remarks 
The alternative dispute resolution system in Finland is working quite fine 
and we do not consider the revision of the ADR-directive necessary. We 
would be happy to continue with the old directive as it is. We have our con-
cerns about the proposal and how the proposed changes would affect the 
Finnish well working ADR-system. The recommendations given by the 
Finnish ADR-entities are accepted and followed by companies extremely 
well (in 4 out of 5 cases the resolution proposal is accepted and followed by 
the trader).  
2. Extension of geographical scope  
In principle, we have a positive attitude towards bringing operators from 
outside the Union into the scope of the directive and thereby strengthening 
the "level playing field" and competitive neutrality in the Union area (art 
1(2)). We are concerned though what the expansion of geographical scope 
would in practice mean for Finnish ADR entities and whether the expansion 
would benefit consumers. 
We are particularly concerned about the resources of the Finnish ADR enti-
ties as they are currently overworked and the processing times for disputes 
are long from the point of view of both consumers and traders. We are con-
cerned that the proposed geographical expansion would “explode” the

amount of resolution requests from consumers overloading the ADR enti-
ties. The resources of ADR entities should be guaranteed. 
There is also the question of the participation of the businesses from out-
side EU in the procedure. We have our concerns if these companies from 
outside EU would really be interested in taking part in Finnish ADR-process 
or even be interested in the outcome which is legally not binding. We are 
concerned that the expectations of consumers are not met, and the credibil-
ity of the well working system would suffer. 
The interest of companies taking part in the process is also related to the 
question of the procedural languages. In Finland the procedural languages 
are only Finnish and Swedish. What is the probability that a company from 
outside EU would participate in an ADR-process in Finnish or Swedish 
causing very expensive translation costs for those companies? Changing 
the processing languages to include, for example, English would require a 
wider reformation of Finnish administrative laws.    
We find it very important though that the participation in the ADR process 
remains voluntary for the companies. 
3. Widening the scope to non-contractual matters  
We do not support the extension of the scope to purely non-contractual 
matters (art 1(1)). At this moment, for example, the information given in 
marketing can be evaluated as part of the contract. In general, in Finland, 
marketing and procedure in customer relations is so called collective con-
sumer protection, which is supervised by Consumer Ombudsman. The su-
pervision by the Consumer Ombudsman has been effective and appropri-
ate. ADR entities should not be tasked with the general tasks of law en-
forcement. The proposed extension to non-contractual issues would require 
reforming the structure of Finnish consumer protection legislation, which we 
do not support. 
4. The right to bundle the cases brought against the same trader together  
The proposal would give ADR entities a right to bundle similar cases 
against the same trader into one process if the consumers accept the bun-
dling (art 1(3)). According to the Finnish legislation a group complaint filed 
by a Consumer Ombudsman is possible. We find this very sufficient and 
well working. In practice the ADR entities also work in a way, that they do 
handle same type of cases against same trader at the same time and then 
only write the resolution proposals as individual resolutions.

5. Mandatory response 
We do not support imposing an obligation to answer on business operators 
(art 1(3)). The obligation to answer is unnecessary in the Finnish system. 
The provision in question would unnecessarily increase the administrative 
burden on traders and on ADR entities. In Finland the ADR entities will give 
their resolution on the matter regardless of whether the company partici-
pates in the process or not. The proposed regulation is also not in line with 
the Commission's goal of reducing the administrative burden on companies 
by 25%. 
In addition, we consider the response time of 20 days is unreasonably 
short. It also remains unclear whether the trader would receive the content 
of the consumer's complaint for his response. 
6. Deletion of the information obligation of traders  
We support the removal of traders' notification obligation regarding ADR 
entities in a situation where the consumer and the trader cannot come to an 
agreement in a disagreement situation (art. 13(3)). We do consider though, 
that the commissions estimations regarding the reduction of the administra-
tive burden of companies is somewhat over-optimistic. 
7. Assistance for consumers 
We support the establishment of ADR contact points for cross-border dis-
putes (art 14). We consider it particularly important that entrepreneurs are 
also entitled to help. This is especially important for micro and SME compa-
nies. It would be worth considering the extension of contact points' assis-
tance to domestic disputes as well (paragraph 4). 
8. End note 
In Finland the ADR-system and monitoring of compliance with the con-
sumer protection legislation works well and is efficient. Should there be 
needs in other member states to strengthen their system it would be ex-
tremely important that it would be done in a way that the change would not 
weaken the situation in those member countries where the system works 
well.

Position Paper 
 
Legislative Proposal on Preventing Plastic Pellet 
Losses to the Environment to Reduce 
Microplastics Pollution  
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Plastic pollution poses a significant environmental challenge and is unacceptable.  Loss of plastic 
pellets is one of the primary sources of microplastic pollution  in the environment 1 however is 
preventable.  Plastics Europe welcomes the European Commission's proposal to address this issue 
but suggests  that refinements are required  to enhance its effectiveness considering the EU's 
ambitious microplastic reduction targets2.  
We acknowledge that the European Commission’s legislative proposal  seeks to mitigate plastic 
pellet loss through a whole supply chain approach.  Specifically, obliging pellet loss prevention for 
all handling activities and ensuring compliance by setting mandatory auditable requirements that are 
subject to third party certification.  It is welcome that the Commission’s proposal builds upon

Plastics Europe welcomes the European Commission's proposal to address plastic pellet loss .   
We suggest that further refinement is required to achieve the European Union’s 2030 microplastic 
reduction targets.  Plastics Europe proposes: 
• Expanding the definition of plastic pellets to include additional physical forms of plastic resin 
(i.e., flakes and powders ) used as feedstock in the production of plastic product 
manufacturing.  
• Lowering the certification threshold for medium and large operators to encourage wider 
industry participation. 
• Certification requirements with amended validity periods for low tonnage operators to further 
enhance the effectiveness of pellet loss prevention measures for these operators.  
These proposals aim to  support the EU's microplastic reduction ambitions through a more 
comprehensive approach which ensures that unintentional microplastic releases are minimised 
as far as possible.

Definitions of Plastics Pellets: 
Plastics Europe recommends expanding the definition of plastic pellets to include flakes and 
powders.  Presently, these forms of pre-production resin are not explicitly within the scope of the 
proposal, nevertheless they also contribute 6  to unintentional releases of microplastics .  
Consequently, flakes and powders should also be explicitly included in the Article 2(a) of “plastic 
pellet”.  A more comprehensive definition will help to combat preventable microplastics releases to 
the environment by removing uncertainty for economic operators handling pellets.  
 
Threshold for Certification Obligations for Medium and Large Operators: 
Plastics Europe proposes lowering the threshold for medium and large operators to be subject to 
certification obligations from 1 ,000 tonnes per year to a lower value .  The current threshold 
corresponds to a significant volume of pellets handled both by mass and by operations .  This 
adjustment aims to widen industry participation in the prevention of plastic pellet loss at all levels of 
the supply chain, and further promote concrete reduction in pellet loss beyond large installations. 
In conjunction with a lower threshold for certification, Plastics Europe would also propose certification 
with extended certificate validity for medium and large operators that are below the (current) 1,000 
tonnes per year threshold.  Considering the size and lower tonnage of these operators, such an 
approach would be a useful addition to the proposed framework.  It would help facilitate compliance 
by expanding the applicability of certification, whilst lowering the additional cost of certification for 
low volume operators.

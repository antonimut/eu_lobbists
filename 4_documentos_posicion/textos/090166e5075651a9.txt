Draft regulation preventing pellet loss: EuPC 
feedback: proposed amendments  
EuPC is the leading EU -level Trade Association, based in Brussels, representing European Plastics Converters. 
EuPC now totals about 51 European Plastics Converting national and European industry associations, it 
represents close to 50,000 companies, producing o ver 50 million tonnes of plastic products every year. The 
European plastics industry makes a significant contribution to the welfare in Europe by enabling innovation, 
creating quality of life to citizens and facilitating resource efficiency and climate protection. More than 1.6 million 
people are working in about 50,000 companies (mainly small and medium sized companies in the converting 
sector) to create a turnover in excess of 280 billion € per year 
Brussels, 17 January 2024 
The EC has released a package of measures on the prevention of unintentional microplastics among 
which a “Proposal for a Regulation on preventing pellet loss to reduce microplastics pollution”. 
We see the current European Commission’s proposal as an additional important step towards the 
objective of near to zero pellet loss by creating a harmoni zed level playing -field across the whole 
supply chain. As responsible companies, we see it as important that pellet losses are prevented so as 
to prevent this source of microplastics to end up in the environment. 
As a more general remark, we would like to remind that this measure in itself should be considered in 
conjunction with other targeted at litter such as closing down of poorly managed landfills and 
dumpsites. 
Generally, plastics should not end up in landfill but preferably be recycled. Waste management and 
existing EPR systems should also be transferred into a circularity approach instead of in a linear 
approach. Material neutrality objectives and similar material circularity targets will create the level 
playing field that Europe really needs. 
We would like to emphasize the importance for this regulatory proposal to be rapidly adopted while 
keeping in mind the specific constraints of micro, small and medium size companies. 
We have as well taken note of the draft ENVI report and the ITRE proposed  amendments. We would 
like to share our position on those and also suggest specific amendments related to technical 
aspects or the regulation or aiming at reducing its socio-economic burden on industry whilst 
ensuring a smoother and faster adoption and implementation taking into account the needs of 
SMES.

Comment: We understand as handler of pellet covers all the actors in the supply chain: resin

III. Definition of pellets, dust and related obligations 
 
Explanation of proposal and discussion:  
In our understanding plastics flakes and powders are included in the scope of the regulation as their 
losses to environment should be avoided.  
Amendment 7 of the ENVI report proposes to modify the definition as follows: “(a) ‘plastic pellet’ means 
a small mass of preformed polymer -containing moulding material, regardless of its shape and form, 
and including powders, flakes and dust , that is used as feedstock in plastic product manufacturing 
operations;” 
This is however problematic as dusts are not used as a feedstock in manufacturing operation. Also, due 
to their nature, once lost to the environment they cannot be retrieved as they are too fine and therefore 
not recoverable. We therefore propose to make a distinction between pellets and plastics dust. 
Proposed amendment :  
Art. 2 .a “‘plastic pellet’ means a small mass of preformed polymer-containing moulding material, 
having relatively uniform dimensions in a given lot such as cylinder, bead, flake or powder that is 
used as feedstock in plastic product manufacturing operations;” 
Art2.a.bis “plastic dust” are small particles resulting from the machining/grinding of plastics 
materials and articles. 
 
Insert new article 3.1.a.: Economic operators, EU carriers and non-EU carriers shall ensure that losses 
of plastics dusts are avoided.

IV. Companies in scope of certification, frequency of self-declaration, 
support to SMEs 
 
III.1 Preamble 
The ENVI committee proposes in amendment 14 to make companies treating more than 250 T per site 
as well as small companies subject certification foreseeing self-declaration for micro-companies only. 
We understand the approach as a willingness to extend coverage of control through certification. There 
is no clear reason provided for this in the rapporteur report.  The regulation foresees obligation 
applicable to all companies to prevent and cleanup pellet losses and enforcement by Authorities. The 
Commission proposal by exempting small companies from certification  aims at reducing the 
administrative burden on companies. It does not decrease in any way the obligation and responsibility 
put on SMEs.  
 
Beyond the potential exemption from certification, the Commission proposal does not take into 
account sufficiently challenges faced by SMEs nor supports the actual adoption and implementation of 
the regulation. For the time being the regulation basically avoids certification costs for smaller 
companies. This is however not the main cost.  
We estimated that the implementation of pellet loss prevention measure would range from around 3 
€/T/year for facilities 10 kT and up whilst it could reach up to 16 €/T for facilities handling lower 
quantities (1 kT). For smaller companies the cost taking into account amortization were divided 
percentage wise as follows: personnel (60%), equipment (30%), auditing 10%, whilst for large facilities 
the distribution of the costs was as follows: personnel (35%), Equipment (64%), auditing 1%. 
 
The obligation of the Commission to develop training material is not precisely defined. It might be as 
light as providing some documents online. The actual need is to support companies in their 
development of the risk assessment plant. To reach out to smal ler companies training should be face 
to face and grounded in practice. Adequate resources should be dedicated to this.  
Access to existing EU funds at regional or EU level should be foreseen also to help the acquisition of 
necessary prevention and mitigation equipment and the organization of trainings/ pre-audits. 
The obligation of Member States is even less specific. At the minimum existing funds for vocational 
training should be allocated at National level to develop such training and advice companies esp. SMEs.  
 
For smaller companies, the cost of prevention and mitigating equipment may be a barrier to the fast 
implementation of the regulation which is immediately applicable (i.e. within 1 year from now most 
likely). We would therefore ask a financing mechanism to be foreseen in cooperation with Member 
States for instance the possibility to faster amortize equipment, therefore reducing the tax level short 
term. It is important that even simple equipment such as vacuum cleaner, retention trays or expenses 
for adequate signage within plant may be acquired shortly.

V. Equipment and procedures 
 
The Commission has foreseen that equipment and procedures should be in place for preventing, 
containing and cleaning up pellet spills and losses. The annex I lists equipment and procedures to 
be considered taking into account the nature and size of the companies. Certain amendments of 
the ENVI committee intend to make those measure mandatory. This is not applicable, since not all 
risk management measures are applicable to all companies. Also, when considering the size of the 
companies the extent of releases may be significantly reduced calling for different measures to be 
implemented. For the regulation to be pragmatic, simpler measures must be considered for smaller 
companies for which due to their size certain equipment such as silos are not accessible or 
convenient. Procedures on the other hands should be generalized. 
 
We therefore call for the rejection of amendments: amendment 38 and 41. 
 
Amendment 39 tries to be more specific on catchment devices and drain covers. However, it is too 
specific as the choice to place trace or the dimensioning of mesh actually depends on the site setup 
and the risk of spill/loss related to the specific location where it has to be installed. 
We therefore call for the rejection of this amendment. 
 
Amendment 40 asks containers where pellets are recovered to be sealed. This is not making sense 
since most containers will be used regularly e.g. in view of emptying for internal recycling. Seals 
are not necessary the most appropriate risk management measure and are definitely not practical 
for handling on site. 
We therefore call for the rejection of this amendment. 
 
We do not oppose to amendment 42. We would however suggest to add the words “when 
applicable”.

VI. Exemption from certification for companies having in place an 
environmental or quality management system 
 
Recital (27) proposed amendment EuPC 
 
(27) In order to be EMAS registered or are certified according to ISO 9001 or ISO 14001, economic 
operators are required to comply with environmental legislation, including this Regulation. 
Consequently, economic operators which are registered to EMAS should be considered compliant 
with the requirements laid down in this Regulation provided that an environmental verifier has 
verified that requirements laid down in this Regulation have been included in their environmental 
management system and implemented. These economic operators  should therefore be 
exempted from the obligations of certification and notification to competent authorities whe n 
renewing self-declarations and risk assessments. 
 
Justification: Compliance with regulation and verification of this compliance is integral part of the 
ISO 9001 and  14001 certification for environmental management systems as well. We will proceed in 
a thematic manner. ISO 14001 concerns more particularly certification of an environmental 
management system, whilst ISO 9001 relates to a management system for quality including 
minimization of waste. 
 
Article 6: exemption for Environmental Management Systems in place proposed EuPC amendment 
 
Environmental management systems  
 
Economic operators which are registered to the Community eco-management and audit scheme in 
accordance with Regulation (EC) No 1221/2009  or are certified according to ISO 9001 or ISO 14001 
are exempt from compliance with the notification obligation laid down in Article 4(2) and the 
obligations laid down in Article 5(1) and (2) of this Regulation provided that the environmental 
verifier as defined in Article 2(20) of Regulation (EC) No 1221/2009 has checked that requirements 
laid down in Annex I have been included in the environmental management system of the economic 
operator and have been implemented. 
 
Justification: 
Companies are more using ISO 14001 than EMAS for the environmental management system. Within 
ISO 14001, regulatory requirement are a priority and a must to obtain the ISO certification. This would 
therefore guarantee compliance with the regulation. Therefore, we would like to ask to qualify a valid 
ISO14001 certification to be deemed compliant and result in exemption of additional certification or at 
least have the audit included in the ISO 14001 audit to save costs.  Even more companies have an ISO 
9001 certification in place for quality management, which includes the minimization of waste 
generation as a target and a section of compliance with regulation.

VII. Pellet loss evaluation method 
 
Article 13: Standardized methodology to estimate pellet losses proposed EuPC amendment 
 
“1. For the purposes of compliance with the obligation referred to in Article 4(7), first subparagraph, 
point (c), a methodology to estimate quantities of losses shall be developed in harmonised standards 
in accordance with the procedures established by Regulation (EU) No1025/2012. as a Commission 
guidance that will be elaborated with the involvement of impacted stakeholders.  
 
2. Where no European standardisation organisation accepts the request to draft a harmonised 
standard or where the Commission considers that the proposed standard does not satisfy the 
requirements which it aims to cover, the Commission shall establish the methodology referred to in 
paragraph 1 by means of an Implementing act. 
Justification:  
Whilst the estimation of pellet loss may provide an indicator to estimate the impact/efficiency of public 
policy and also help companies to benchmark their efforts to the rest of the industry progress , one 
should watch out when developing a standard/implementing act to keep the methodology both 
flexible and simple. 
The focus of the regulation is in adopting concrete prevention and mitigation measures. 
EuPC has developed and is testing pellet loss estimate methodologies and tools. Whilst practicable, we 
came to the conclusion that both risk assessment and pellet loss estimation methodology should be 
simplified as much as possible to enable proper assessment, adhesion and spread of best practices. We 
would therefore recommend in a first stage to develop a guidance document in a group involving key

VIII. Penalties and sanctions 
In amendment 5 to recital 39 and amendment 37 to article 15.2 , the ENVI rapporteur calls for 
penalties to be up to 4% of the turnover of a legal person in the European Union instead than at 
Member State level as currently in the EC draft regulation. Prevention of pellet losses is a local matter 
and Member States do not have jurisdiction over other Member States.  We therefore call to reject 
these amendments 5 and 37 of ENVI.

suspend the operation of a plant when the infringement of the rules poses and immediate danger to

IX. Ratio legis 
 
Recital 1 provides the main ratio legis for the measure. Some of the statement therein constitute 
generalization and opinions. Whilst we obviously agree and are committed for the need to prevent 
pellet losses in the environment, the ratio legis should however be precisely stated. 
 
Recital 1, sentence 1 
Microplastics are ubiquitous , persistent, and therefore  transboundary. If persistent, they may be  
detrimental to the environment. As they may concentrate pollutants already present in the 
environment, they may be potentially harmful to human health. 
 
Justification: 
This rephrasing helps to acknowledge the presence of microplastics in the environment.  
It also reminds that, from a  scientific point of view,  not all microplastics are equal . Either from a 
scientific or a regulatory point of view, some biodegrade while other are persistent. The latter means, 
scientifically, that the interactions with microorganisms are difficult, suggesting that persistent 
microplastics should be considered as chemically inert.   
It is a known scientific fact that microplastics may swell in contact with some pollutants (resulting from 
human activities) present in the sea. By doing so, they are concentrating pollutants which should not 
be there in the first place.

The impact on marine wildlife refers to any pollutant and is not a property of microplastics.   
However, the early draft suggests that physical harm or death is likely. This is a shortcut to 
the widespread images of seaside wildlife in countries where waste management is at least 
ineffective, with all the waste floating into the sea, decomposing and becoming part of the food chains 
of birds and turtles, living by the sea.  The size of the pieces largely exceeded the definition of 
microplastics and pellets.  While this should be recognised, it should also be accurate.   
 
Recital 1, sentence 6 
 
Microplastics also contribute to climate change as an additional source both of greenhouse gas 
emissions and of pressure on ecosystems 
Justification:  
It is quite difficult to understand  in which way a source of carbon for life or a persistent (and not 
decomposing object) could be the source of greenhouse gas emissions.   Any releases would be 
insignificant compared to releases from decomposition of biomass.

FEPORT Feedback Microplastics Regulation proposal 
 
 
1. Introduction  
FEPORT represents the interests of 1225 private port companies and terminals performing cargo 
handling and logistics related activities in the seaports of the EU. FEPORT Members employ over 
390.000 workers. 
FEPORT welcomes t he Commission proposal for a regulation on preventing plasti c pellet losses to 
reduce microplastic pollution  as an important step towards the reduction of the amount of 
microplastics unintentionally released into the environment.  
FEPORT members are already engaged in initiatives aiming at tackling microplastics pollution and 
believe that this Regulation is a good means to reduce the adverse impacts  of plastic pellet loss  on 
the environment, climate, the economy and human health.  
Our feedback includes some proposals regarding the re duction of the administrative bu rden for 
companies, while providing some recom mendations on how seaport terminal o perators should be 
categorised under this Regulation. 
1. Administrative burden 
FEPORT understands the rationale behind the possibility for companies that are already certified 
under the Community eco-management and audit scheme (EM AS) to be excluded  from this 
Regulation’s notification and certification requirements. This exclusion will allow those companies 
to reduce administrative costs. 
However, many terminal o perators rather com ply with  ISO 14.001 for the certification of thei r 
environmental management sys tems. Under other EU legis lative initiatives such as 
CountEmissionsEU, the validity of ISO audit schemes is already recogni sed. F EPORT therefore 
recommends extending the exemptions granted to EMAS-certified companies to companies that are 
certified via ISO 14.001, provided that this Regulation’s requirements are incorporated in the ISO 
14.001 audit scheme they use. 
In a similar vein, FEPORT would like to stress that it is important to prevent duplication regarding

2. Categorization of terminal operators 
There is a need to clarify how seaport terminal operators performing cargo handling operations are 
classified in the framework of this Regulation 
“EU carrier” is defined in article 2(f) as “any natural or legal person established in a Member State, 
engaged in the transport of plastic pellets as part of its economic activity by using road vehicles, rail 
wagons or inland waterway vessels.” 
This definition is confusing since - even though terminal operators do no t transport plastic pellets 
via road vehicles, rail wagons or inland wat erway vessels – the cargo handling activities performed 
by terminal operators are connecting different modes of transport and therefore crucial to enable 
any type of transport of plastic pellets via road, rail or the inland waterways.  
Besides, Annex III, which spells out o bligations for EU and non -EU carriers, lays down obligations 
for carriers during loading and unloading activities but  these activities are usually performed by 
terminal operators or stevedoring companies and not by road, rail or barge operators. 
To improve clarity, FEPORT therefore proposes to clarify the definition of EU and non -EU carrier 
and amend it as follows: 
Article 2(f-g) 
‘(non)-EU carrier’  (non)-EU transport operator means any natural or legal person established in a 
Member State, engaged in the transport  of plastic pellets  by u sing road vehicles, rail wagons or 
inland waterway vessels , or t he transhipment of cargoes co ntaining plastic pellets in multimodal 
terminals, rail terminals, inland and seaport terminals as part of its economic activity by using road 
vehicles, rail wagons or inland waterway vessels; 
3. Conclusion 
FEPORT and its members welcome the Microplastics Regulation proposal and look  forward to 
having a constructive dialogue with the co -legislators to ensure that th is text includes clear 
definitions, is easy to implement for companies and does not lead to an unnecessary increase of 
administrative burden.

EUROPEAN COMMISSION CONSULTATION ON AN EU DISABILITY CARD & 
EU DISABILITY PARKING CARD 
 
 
In consultation with its member societies, Patient Advisory Committee, and  the ECO 
Survivorship and Quality of Life Network, the European Cancer Organisation (ECO) is pleased to 
respond to the European Commission consultation on an EU Disability Card and an EU Disability 
Parking Card. We would like to take the opportunity of the consultation to raise policy attention 
to:  
a) the needs of cancer patients and survivors encountering forms of disabilities as a result 
of their disease and treatment; and, 
b) the needs of individuals with disabilities in respect to access to cancer services and 
cancer control more generally.  
 
What follows below are our 3 key points of response.  
 
1. The support of Europe’s cancer community for European Disability Card and European 
Parking Card 
 
The European Cancer Organisation supports the European Commission’s efforts to agree with 
Member States the creation of a European Disability Card and to achieve an improved European 
Parking Card. 
 
Cancer can often be considered a disability because of the changes caused by cancer and cancer 
treatment. For example, some types of cancer may create forms of physical and cognitive 
disabilities. Case studies in this instance could include a scenario of a patient impacted by lung 
cancer having arising reduction in physical mobility, or a breast cancer survivor with upper body 
impairment as a result of treatment complications 1.  Vision problems may also arise from some 
forms of eye cancer 2 or as a result o f neurological radiation and surgery 3. Cancer and its 
treatment can also be associated to patients experiencing ‘brain fog’ or ‘chemo brain’ 4, i.e. a

The potential value of the European Disability Card in supporting the social prescribing agenda 
 
The European Disability Card, and its proposed use to help support improv ed access, and 
discounts for, cultural and other services, such as sports and leisure activities, is also welcomed 
in the context of a growing recognition of the value of cultural activity and outdoor physical 
activity and recreation, in supporting improve ments in mood, psychology and outlook for an 
individual. The European Disability Card, in the context of cancer, may therefore be a helpful 
further support in advancing the agenda of ‘social prescribing’ for cancer patients and survivors. 
 
When the Europea n Commission, EU Member States and others evaluate the benefit to be 
achieved by such initiatives as the European Disability Card and European Parking Card, the 
above considerations and benefits should be strongly taken into account. 
 
Social prescribing: supporting quality of life for people living with cancer 
 
Social prescribing is a means of connecting patients to a range of non -clinical services in the 
community to improve their health and well-being. It builds on the evidence that addressing 
social dete rminants of health such as socioeconomic status, social inclusion, housing, and 
education is key to improving health outcomes. 
 
The World Health Organisation is promoting the more systematised use of such approaches 
in health and cancer care, including through providing a Toolkit on How to Implement Social 
Prescribing.

2. Defining disabilities: the need to get it right 
 
Defining disability can be challenging, as it may encompass a spectrum of conditions, each with 
unique characteristics, making it difficult to establish clear -cut boundaries and determine 
which categories should be included .  It may be conceptualised as a dynamic interaction 
between contextual and personal factors, to whom many cancer patients and survivors may 
belong.

Article 3 of the Commission’s Proposal for a Directive lays out different definitions, including of 
‘persons with disabilities’. The definition currently proposed is the following: ‘persons who have 
long-term physical, mental, intellectual or sensory impairments which in interaction with various 
barriers may hinder their full and effective participation in society on an equal basis with others’.  
 
ECO considers of utmost importance that, in the future application of any definition of ‘persons 
with disabilities’, all cases of disab ility associated to cancer diagnosis - illustrated by some 
examples provided above - gain coverage. For this reason, if future guidance around the 
application of the definition were to be developed , all stakeholders - including from major 
disease areas suc h as cancer – should be consulted . Furthermore, future review of the 
application of the legislation should include close consideration of any individuals who 
experienced that their disability or impairment fell outside of the legislation’s definition , for

In this respect, there could be a case for creating separate scopes of inclusion for the European

Finally, ECO wishes to call upon a clarification and definition of ‘short stays’ in Article 3 of the 
Proposal relating to the EU Parking Card for Persons with Disabilities . It has been noted by 
many stakeholder s that there is a limitation within the currently proposed legal text as the 
affording of rights under the scheme to a nationa l visiting another EU country is assured for 
“short stays”. While the concept of ‘short stay’ is not fully defined in the current EU proposal, 
the position of the Council of Ministers, published in November 2023, is that “short stay” should 
be defined as 3  months. ECO identifies potential problems with creating such a definition, for 
example, for cancer patients receiving their treatment in another country  over periods longer 
than 3 months. This can sometimes be the case for rare, childhood and other cancers.  
 
3. The pathway to strong implementation 
 
Scope of inclusion 
ECO recommends that the European Disability Card, granting discounts to services, sports and 
leisure facilities, free visits to museums, be accessible for all cancer patients, regardless of their 
cancer type, and requiring no further assessment of disability.  This would provide important 
assistance towards the quality of life and well-being of all who experience a cancer diagnosis 
and impacts of treatment , and support the growing recognition of social prescribing  within 
healthcare approaches.  
 
Card formats – physical and electronic 
The Commission’s Proposal calls for both Disability and Disability Parking cards to be issued 
physically and to be complemented by a digital format. The European C ancer Organisation 
welcomes this initiative, and advocates for its extension to braille and easy to read versions . 
Brain and eye cancer can indeed induce changes in one’s sight and capacity to read5, which must 
be addressed adequately for an inclusive proposal.

Reducing administrative burdens  
It is important, as far as possible, that steps be taken to reduce bureaucratic and administrative 
burdens associated to applying for, and being assessed, for a Disability card, and that timescales 
for assessment are  reasonable. Therefore, ECO recommends strong attention to appropriate 
harmonisation and simplification of both card format and processes associated to gaining use 
of the card.  
 
Bringing together EU Apps with common purposes. 
ECO encourages those implementing EU supported mobile applications, such as any that are 
developed to support the European Disability Card and European Parking Card, to be mindful 
and proactive in considering the mutual support that might be achieved in connection to other 
EU supported Apps aiming towards common purposes. An example in this respect could be 
achieving strong linkage and cross -referral to the EU Cancer Survivor Smartcard App 
(smartCARE).  
 
Promotion of the scheme 
To achieve strong implementation, ECO recommends EU funding be ringfenced to support 
awareness of the Disability Cards among the target user populations  – including cancer 
patients.   
 
Implementation policies will need to be reviewed after 5 years of implementation, with strong 
levels of stakeholder engagement. 
 
4. Going further on disability rights  
 
In reviewing the proposal, and while welcoming its broad intent and key features, ECO sees 
opportunity for the proposal , and EU policy more generally,  to have more ambition . This 
includes further actions that may envisaged to achieve greater harmonisation of approaches 
towards upholding the rights of persons with disabilities. 
 
Financial Toxicity

European Strategy for the rights of persons with disabilities 2021-2030, which notably seeks to 
address the financial difficulties (high travelling expenses, inability to work..) and exposure to 
poverty faced by persons with disabilities, otherwise known as ‘financial toxicities’.  The latter

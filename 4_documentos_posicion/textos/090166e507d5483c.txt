German Chamber of Commerce and Industry 
DIHK position on the European Commission's proposal for a Regulation of the European Par-
liament and of the Council establishing an EU Talent Pool 
On 15 November 2023, the European Commission presented a legislative proposal for a Regu-
lation of the European Parliament and of the Council establishing an EU Talent Pool. We thank 
you for the opportunity to comment on the above-mentioned draft. 
This opinion is based on the statements of the Chambers of Commerce and Industry received 
by DIHK up to the submission of the opinion as well as the economic policy/European policy 
positions of DIHK. Should DIHK receive further relevant statements not yet considered in this 
opinion, DIHK will supplement this opinion accordingly. 
A. The most important aspects in brief 
Measures that speed up and simplify skilled immigration for companies are generally to be 
supported. If the EU Talent Pool can contribute to this with little bureaucracy and low costs, 
the DIHK believes that Germany should consider participating.  
A practical implementation of the EU Talent Pool is crucial for companies. The interoperability 
of the EU Talent Pool platform with existing national and EU-wide systems must therefore be 
ensured. A list of shortage occupations should be discarded, as companies should be able to 
decide for themselves for which occupations they wish to recruit labour and skilled workers 
from third countries. The establishment of an EU Talent Pool must be accompanied by effi-
cient processing of immigration applications, rapid recognition of third-country nationals' 
qualifications and support measures for third-country workers to integrate into the labour 
market and society. 
B. Relevance for the German Economy 
Despite the tense economic situation, the shortage of skilled workers in German companies 
continues to grow. According to the latest DIHK Skilled Labour Report, more than half of Ger-
man companies are unable to fill vacancies in the long term because they cannot find suitable 
employees. This is particularly true of skilled workers. It is estimated that a total of 1.8 million 
jobs cannot be filled as desired. The shortage of skilled workers is a major challenge not only

C. General introduction – general part/general reflections 
On 15 November 2023, as part of the European Year of Skills, the European Commission pre-
sented a comprehensive package on the mobility of skilled workers and learners. The aim of 
the package is to make the EU a more attractive place for third-country workers and profes-
sionals and to address skills shortages across Europe. 
Part of the package is a legislative proposal from the European Commission for an EU Talent 
Pool in the form of a Regulation. The EU Talent Pool will be the first EU-wide online platform 
to match third-country workers and professionals with companies in the EU. Participation in 
the EU Talent Pool will be voluntary for Member States. The EU Talent Pool will target specific 
occupations at all skill levels, based on the most common shortage occupations in the Union. 
Companies from Member States participating in the EU Talent Pool will be able to submit their 
vacancies to the Talent Pool database through a national contact point. 
In the context of Europe-wide skills shortages, it is necessary and right to promote immigra-
tion from third countries and to make the EU a more attractive destination for labour and 
skills from third countries. Measures such as the EU Talent Pool, if properly organised, can 
contribute to this. Practical design and easy access for businesses are crucial. The IHK organi-
sation's position on specific aspects of the EU Talent Pool is set out below. 
D. In detail – other aspects/special part 
Interoperability with national initiatives  
Commission proposal: According to the Commission's proposal, the technical interoperability 
of the EU Talent Pool platform with IT infrastructures of national systems should be ensured. 
DIHK assessment: The participation of employers, including SMEs, in the EU Talent Pool must 
be as easy and unbureaucratic as possible. Ideally, employers should have access to all profiles 
of EU and non-EU skilled workers through a single website ("one-stop shop"). It is also im-
portant to ensure that only candidates who meet the legal immigration requirements are pre-
sented to companies in Germany. Interoperability of the EU Talent Pool with existing systems 
at national and EU level, in particular with the EURES and Europass portal, needs to be en-
sured. Job offers posted on "Make it in Germany" should also automatically appear on the EU

List of shortage occupations  
Commission proposal: The EU Talent Pool will target specific occupations at all qualification 
levels, based on the most common shortage occupations in the Union. To this end, an EU-wide 
list of shortage occupations will be drawn up and regularly updated. The proposed list of 
shortage occupations is available here. The criteria for the EU-wide list are shortage occupa-
tions that exist in a significant number of Member States, as well as occupations that directly 
contribute to the digital and green transformation. Member States may decide once a year to 
adjust the list of shortage occupations at national level in order to respond to the needs of the 
national labour market.  
DIHK assessment: The creation of an EU-wide list of shortage occupations seems difficult and 
bureaucratic. Experience in Germany with the former so-called "positive list" has already 
shown these difficulties in practice. The criteria for filling the EU shortage occupation list are 
currently not sufficiently presented (for example, it is not clear what is meant by "shortage oc-
cupations that exist in a significant number of Member States", nor what is the definition of 
"occupations that contribute directly to the digital and green transformation", see below). The 
definition of a shortage occupation also depends on the data situation. Occupations for which 
there is too little data risk not appearing on the shortage list. EU-wide and national lists also 
do not adequately reflect regional characteristics. For this reason, a shortage occupation list as 
a prerequisite for participation in the EU Talent Pool should be abandoned and instead the EU 
Talent Pool should be opened to all workers and professionals as well as occupations (for 
which immigration is in principle possible). Companies must be able to decide for themselves 
for which vacancies they wish to recruit workers and professionals from abroad. This requires 
an intuitive and standardised way of selecting workers from different sectors on the platform 
in order to quickly find suitable profiles.  
Non-binding national lists of shortage occupations for each EU Member State could be pub-
lished on the EU Talent Pool platform to provide job seekers with information on which occu-
pations are most in demand in EU Member States. 
Digital and green jobs:  
Commission proposal: In addition to shortage occupations that exist in a significant number of 
Member States, the EU-wide list of shortage occupations should also include occupations that 
contribute directly to the digital and green transformation. 
DIHK assessment: More clarity is needed on how to define the occupations that contribute to 
the digital and green transformation. Favouring certain occupations and sectors must be 
avoided. The whole value chain must always be considered. This is because a large number of 
occupations contribute to the digital and green transformation without being classified as

Skills First approach  
Commission proposal: The Commission's proposal follows the "Skills First" approach, which 
has already been presented and supported by the European Commission in other documents. 
The approach is based on the idea that companies should not only consider formally recog-
nised qualifications when recruiting but should also recruit based on skills and competences 
acquired through informal learning or work experience.  
DIHK assessment: The strength of Germany as a business location is based primarily on its 
well-developed system of dual vocational training and high-quality continuing education at 
bachelor and master level. This results in a more versatile workforce that can move quickly in 
the labour market and has a solid basis for its own educational biography. For this reason, vo-
cational qualifications must not be devalued by a 'skills first' approach in the future.  
Nevertheless, in many countries there are competent practitioners - i.e. people without formal 
qualifications but with extensive professional experience - whom German companies could 
very well employ in skilled jobs and who should therefore also be given opportunities on the 
German labour market. For this reason, for a residence permit for employment in non-regu-
lated occupations, professional experience should be considered in addition to the existence 
of vocational or university qualifications. After entry and employment in Germany, a regulated 
professional validation procedure could be used to reliably assess and formally certify profes-
sional competence. The ValiKom pilot project funded by the Germany Federal Ministry of Edu-
cation and Research and the experience gained by the Chambers of Industry and Commerce 
and the Chambers of Skilled Crafts in validating informally acquired vocational skills provide a 
good basis for this. A legal basis for individual vocational assessment procedures - as currently 
proposed by the German Federal Ministry of Education and Research in a draft law - is im-
portant to give companies and skilled workers a clear signal and a secure perspective. 
Supporting measures to speed up immigration and recognition procedures and the integra-
tion of skilled workers  
Commission proposal: The 15 November package also includes a Commission recommenda-
tion to simplify the recognition of skills and qualifications of third-country nationals. The pro-
posed measures would modernise the current EU recognition system and bring it more in line 
with the system for EU citizens moving to another Member State. The aim is to increase the 
capacity of national recognition authorities to simplify and speed up procedures by improving 
the comparability of third-country nationals' qualifications and by improving methods for as-
sessing the skills of job seekers. The aim is to ensure that recognition decisions are taken 
quickly and reliably.

Participating Member States may decide to introduce fast-track immigration procedures for 
jobseekers registered in the EU Talent Pool to ensure faster recruitment. Such fast-track pro-
cedures may include obtaining visas and residence permits for work purposes, as well as ex-
emption from the principle of preference for EU citizens for vacancies posted on the EU Talent 
Pool IT platform. 
The EU Talent Pool platform should include information on recruitment and immigration pro-
cedures, recognition of qualifications, rights of third-country nationals and living and working 
conditions in the Member States. The National Contact Point should also provide additional 
support, such as specific information on visa and residence permit regulations, family reunifi-
cation procedures or the integration of third-country nationals in the Member State. 
DIHK assessment: In order to ensure the implementation of the EU Talent Pool, it is important 
that the national authorities work more efficiently and are provided with sufficient human and 
financial resources. The rapid recognition of qualifications and skills of third-country workers 
must be ensured in order to speed up recruitment processes for companies. The European 
Commission's proposals for faster recognition therefore make sense in principle.  
It is currently unclear what measures Member States can take to introduce fast-track immigra-
tion procedures for jobseekers registered in the EU Talent Pool. More information is needed 
to assess this. In principle, this is a matter for the Member States to decide; the companies’ 
view is that there is a need to speed up the processes in Germany overall and that there 
should be no special provisions for recruitment under the EU Talent Pool. 
To ensure that immigrants remain in Germany and the EU in the long term, long-term integra-
tion must be considered from the outset. An adequate supply of integration courses should be 
ensured. In addition, regional welcome centres should be the first point of contact for incom-
ing foreign workers and companies and actively support them in all matters relating to inte-
gration. More support is also needed for companies and third-country workers in finding ac-
commodation, applying for visas, dealing with authorities, opening bank accounts, health in-
surance and the like.  
One of the most important levers for the successful and long-term integration of skilled work-
ers is a good knowledge of the language of the destination country. Jobseekers' profiles on the 
EU Talent Pool platform should include information on what language level they already have 
and in which language. Having attended a language course in the country of origin is also an 
advantage.

F. Description of DIHK 
Who we are: 
The 79 Chambers of Commerce and Industry (IHKs) are members of the German Chamber of 
Commerce and Industry (DIHK). Our common target: the best conditions for successful eco-
nomic activity. 
At the federal and European level, DIHK represents the interests of the entire commercial sec-
tor vis-à-vis policy makers, the administration, and the public. 
Several million companies from the trade, manufacturing industry and service sectors are legal 
members of a CCI – from kiosk owners to Dax corporations. All IHKs are legal members of the 
DIHK. Thus, DIHK and the IHKs are a platform for the many and varied concerns of companies. 
We bundle these through a defined procedure based by a legal basis into common positions of 
the business community and thereby contribute to the participatory debate on economic pol-
icy.

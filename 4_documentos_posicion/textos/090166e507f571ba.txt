 
  
 
   
 
SOLIDAR submission to the Call for Evidence on the proposal for a 
regulation - COM(2023)71 (EU Talent Pool) 
January 2024 
 
SOLIDAR is a European network of Civil Society Organisations  (CSOs) with a global reach working to advance social 
justice through a just transition in Europe and worldwide. For more information about SOLIDAR or this submission, 
feel free to contact Julie Martinaud at julie.martinaud@solidar.org . 
  
SOLIDAR is a fervent supporter of the expansion of regular pathways for all types of migration to 
the EU. It also endorses, in principle, initiatives aimed at facilitating procedures or opening 
conditions of admission in support of regular pathways. As is clearly  expressed in the proposal, 
the EU Talent Pool has the objective to ease international recruitment in view of filling the gaps 
in the European labour markets, short of one million workers annually. 
  
While appreciating that the proposal aims to be constructive, SOLIDAR would like to see a 
platform that is not designed solely around the short-to medium term needs of EU employers 
but takes into account the needs of the workers in a decent work perspective . There are high 
risk that such a tool would reinforce the significant power imbalance that exists between 
companies who are given the opportunity to fish for employees on terms driven by them, and 
individual applicants from third countries aspiring to work in the EU. SOLIDAR welcomes the fact 
the platform is open to applicants from all skills levels but regrets the chosen focus on key sectors 
with shortages, rather than something open for all sectors, revealing a certain short-sightedness 
of the plan. Labour shortages precisely usually occur in sectors and occupations with unattractive 
working conditions, which applicants in the Talent Pool may not be aware of. This calls for 
corrective measures to mitigate the information asymmetry and the power imbalance, to avoid 
turning those sectors and occupations into precariousness traps for migrant workers.  
  
SOLIDAR calls for all regular migration initiatives to be embedded in a comprehensive, long-term 
approach to migration to the EU for all purposes. Regular migration pathways s hould be 
enshrined in general legislation and opened to all third- country nationals, in reverse to the 
trend of using bilateral labour  migration partnerships as means of migration governance, 
illustrated by the EU Talent Partnerships for instance. Under this trend nationals of third-country 
that, for instance, are willing to cooperate with the EU on migrants’ readmission and returns 
objectives, selectively face increased opportunities to migrate to the EU while nationals of less 
cooperative countries see Europe close its door to them. SOLIDAR welcomes bilateral agreement 
and fast -tracking approaches when they have the purpose to reach equity such as by giving 
increased support or lowering procedural conditions of admission for vulnerable groups or 
individuals. In this regard, while it is very welcome that the proposal mentions the Talent Pool 
could support the operationalisation of complementary labour pathways  for people in need of 
international protection, it is not clear how this will materialise . Given the present scarcity of

In addition, EU labour  migration policies should promote decent work and social inclusion. This 
means the EU Talent Pool should lead to quality immigration procedures with quality work and 
residence permits, of reasonable initial duration and renewable. Workers should not be 
considered as stopgaps for EU economies but should be given the same treatment as EU nationals 
and same opportunities in terms of developing their career, further their education and training 
if they wish to, and possibility to pursue their life in the EU. 
Furthermore, a long-term global approach to migration should take into account the impact of 
migration to the EU of the third countries the workers come from, with the risk to create skills 
shortages in those countries. 
  
The following recommendations emerge from our analysis of the proposal and SOLIDAR 
network’s long-standing experience advocating for social rights: 
  
1. Mitigate the risk of leading to poor working conditions and situations of exploitation 
SOLIDAR notes with satisfaction that the proposal has taken into account several of the 
recommendations made by CSOs, including SOLIDAR, in previous consultations, regarding the 
need to better protect workers against the risk of unfair recruitment and  exploitative working 
conditions. However, simply stating the need to mitigate this risk is not enough to actually curb  
it. Here are concrete avenues to effectively protect beneficiaries of this scheme: 
a. Setting up mandatory common standards for Member States’ provision of 
complete and transparent information, as well as tailored support, to applicants 
about the procedures and their rights during and after the selection process, in a 
language they understand. 
b. Ensure fair recruitment by mitigating potential bias. For instance, employers 
should not be allowed to filter by gender, by nationality, or age of the applicant 
on grounds of non- discrimination; profiles should be kept as anonymous as 
possible until after screening. The 
International Labour Organisation's Fair recruitment 
guidelines can be a good benchmark for establishing common standards. 
c. Closely link the deployment of the Pool and the immigration procedures to the 
implementation of other improved instruments, e.g. the Revision of the Single -
Permit directive enabling workers to change employer without losing their permit, 
but also the Commission’s Recommendation on recognition of qualifications of 
third-country nationals, ensuring that the Pool's features do not prevent accurate 
demonstration of the skills and qualifications of applicants  The pre- screening 
phase of the recruitment process, in particular,  should not lead to weeding out 
 
  
 
   
 
applicants due to erroneous assessment of their skills and qualifications caused by 
misunderstanding of learning done in third countries. 
d. Ensuring workers and third parties’ access to complaint mechanisms. Suspension 
or exclusion of the Pool should be considered for employers found to have abusive 
practices, directly or through their subcontractors. 
 
2. Open up the governance 
The Talent Pool must involve all stakeholders in its development, monitoring and evaluation.  
a. Trade Unions and social partners in general  are key stakeholders to advance 
workers’ rights, both sectorally and across sectors. Relevant Trade Unions from 
the national and international levels, from the EU receiving countries and the 
(major) sending countries or regions, should have a voice in the  EU Talent Pool 
governance. It is appreciated that Social partners’ role is recognized but an 
observer status in the Talent Pool Steering Group is not enough, they should be 
full members. Trade Unions should be involved in the implementation of the pool, 
especially regarding informing, supporting and counselling applicants. 
b. Civil Society organisations  such as a number of SOLIDAR members, represent 
people who are not employed as well as vulnerable groups with lesser access to 
unionization. They can have a particularly key role in what concerns reaching out 
to vulnerable communities, especially if the Pool is to be open to people in need 
of protection, to help them navigate job- searching and migration procedures. 
They also play a role in fostering social inclusion, as well as advising and 
monitoring compliance with fundamental rights. They also must be involved in the 
decision-making around the Talent Pool, with an observer status in the Steering 
Group. Practically, this could be materialised with two representatives from EU -
level civil society organisations and two representatives from national- level civil 
society organisations to be selected by the Secretariat through an open call for 
applications. 
 
3. End the focus on shortage occupations as it may: 
a. Aggravate the skills shortages or ‘brain drain’ in third countries, 
especially in the 
health care sector. SOLIDAR's publication the Social Rights Monitor highlights that 
emigration of qualified workers to the EU is an issue in Serbia, for instance.  In 
essential public sectors such as healthcare , workers’ migration from African 
countries to Europe (e. g. from Nigeria, Ghana and Zimbabwe) is putting at serious 
risk the achievement of Universal Health Care objective by 2030.  
b. Have the adverse impact to box third -country nationals into specific categories 
affecting their opportunities and professional mobility; in addition, migration 
corridors for specific jobs or sectors are often very gendered, reinforcing bias 
around both gender and nationalities in relation to these sectors/occupations; 
 
  
 
   
 
c. Drive salaries down in a specific sector or occupation  especially as sectors with 
shortages may often be the ones where salaries and other employment and 
working conditions are degraded; 
d. In addition, it is short -sighted and fails to address the long -term employment 
needs of the EU in the process of the twin transitions. 
 
4. Introduce targeted measure to disseminate information and offer tailored support to 
access the Pool for people in need of international protection, both outside and already 
residing in the Union. 
 
a. People in need of protection already present on EU territory – typically asylum 
applicants - and willing to work in the EU constitute an underused pool of potential 
workers that can contribute to answering labour market needs. As evidenced by the 
European Council on Refugees and Exiles (ECRE)
, they face hindered access to the 
right to work and even when they have the right to work, structural challenges 
dissuade employers to hire asylum-applicants. The EU Talent Pool can contribute to 
solving EU labour market shortages and the access to employ ment issues by 
facilitating the matching. For instance, ''Changing lanes'' programmes for asylum -
seekers who are already in the asylum claim procedure, to apply for a work permit 
without having to return to their country, have been 
put in place in Germany and can 
be channeled by the Pool, also potentially inspiring other countries to deploy similar 
programmes.

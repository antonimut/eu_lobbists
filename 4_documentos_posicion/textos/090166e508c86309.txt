Cefic feedback on the One substance, One Assessment 
legislative package 
Cefic welcomes the Commission's efforts to improve and enhance the safety assessment of 
chemicals across EU legislation. The common data platform is a positive step forward, 
consolidating all information and activities in one place. With this, the chemical industry  
particularly look s forward to seeing improved communication and coordination flows around 
regulatory processes, while offering much-needed predictability.  
Cefic would like to highlight some key issues identified in the proposal as laid out in the following 
considerations: 
Proposal for a Regulation establishing a common data platform on chemicals 
The consolidation of all information and activities on a substance in one place, as proposed by the 
Regulation establishing a common data platform, is as a step in the right direction. Cefic expects 
this would lead to important improvement of communication on planned and ongoing regulatory 
processes as well as the coordination of such activities, to allow – among other things – for better 
predictability for the industry. 
Cefic underlines key challenges arising from the new proposal and recommends the following: 
1. Ensure a high level of security of the  confidential information contained in the platform : 
Safeguarding the confidentiality of industry’s sensitive or strategic information is crucial to 
preserve competition on the market and incentivize innovation.  While providing public access to 
information on the properties and effects of chemicals on human health and the environment, the 
legitimate rights of businesses to the protection of commercially valuable information and 
intellectual property rights must be protected.  
2. Avoid unnecessary burden on companies for the notification of commissioned studies:  
The proposed obligation for notification of studies commissioned by the industry must be 
proportionately scoped and focused on studies which would have an added value to the risk or 
regulatory management of chemicals. The administrative burden on companies (and subsequently 
the Agency) should not be underestimated, taking into account the large number and diversity of 
legislative acts included in Annex  I to the proposed Regulation, as well as the vast amount of 
studies, measurements and analyses performed on a daily basis by the industry. 
Similarly to the EFSA Transparency Regulation ( Article 32b), the possibility to submit late 
notifications must be included in Article 22 of the proposal , where valid justification is provided.

Lastly, we note that the proposed obligation on laboratories and testing facilities to co-notify may

3. Do not duplicate the established processes in data generation:  
We note that the proposed Data Generation Mechanism will not overlap with existing 
mechanisms or duplicate existing processes already established in European legislation, such as 
the substance evaluation under REACH . The Data Generation mechanism should be used only 
when there is a clear and specific need for data that cannot be generated under existing 
processes.  
Before undertaking new scientific studies, it is important that all relevant information is taken 
into consideration, including  on the methodologies of the study and to avoid potential 
duplication of work (incl. at global level ). Stakeholders could share their knowledge and 
experiences and should therefore be consulted on scientific studies to be commissioned under 
the data generation mechanism.  
4. Engage with stakeholders in the development of data formats and controlled vocabularies  
Standard d ata formats and controlled vocabularies must  be developed in consultation with 
stakeholders and especially the industry, as the expectation is that in the long term the formats 
and vocabularies set by the Agencies  will be used also for the submission of data by the duty 
holders.  
While the industry supports the principle of using the same data formats and tools across 
different pieces of legislation as much as possible, this should not increase the burden on industry 
or delay regulatory processes. 
5. Consider the study context when re-using data  
Prior to the re -use of data, special attention must be paid to the design of the study that 
generated the data (incl. substance identity, limitations, protocols, scope and units ). The study 
context and the applicability domain of the generated data needs to be  valid in the context of 
the re -use. This could be particularly significant especially with regard to substance identity , 
which should be well defined and assessed relevant for the re-use scope. (For example, not to 
consider non -food grade info for food applications .) This a pproach should be followed with

Proposals on the re-attribution of scientific and technical tasks to the Agencies 
Cefic recognizes that the reattribution of scientific and technical tasks will have a significant impact 
on ECHA; and not only in terms of budgetary implications and human /administrative resources 
required. A reorganization of the work of the Agency will be necessary to ensure that its 
Committees will cope with the increased workload without compromising the quality and the 
timeliness of their work. 
New structures – committees or working groups  – are likely to be necessary and definitely the 
reinforcement of the existing ones  (the Risk Assessment Committee is already facing substantial 
workload; the Committee for Socio-Economic Analysis will also be impacted with additional tasks). 
Further to additional budget and staffing , the setup as well as the organisation of the work need 
to be considered. With no clarity on the future structure of the Agency’s scientific committees it 
is impossible to conclude on whether  the proposed resources will be sufficient  for the Agency to 
manage the increased workload. 
Without a proposal for an ECHA Basic Regulation it is unclear whether and how the Agency (incl. 
its committees and working groups) will be adapted to allow for the smooth and efficient 
integration of the new tasks.

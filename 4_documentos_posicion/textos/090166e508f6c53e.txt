FEEDBACKSUBMITTEDBYEUROGROUPFORANIMALSONTHEPROPOSALFORAREGULATIONONTHEWELFAREOFDOGSANDCATSANDTHEIRTRACEABILITY
For more than four decades, Eurogroup for Animals has brought together animal advocatesthroughout Europetosignificantlyenhancethewelfareandprotectionof animals.
This proposal for a Regulation on the welfare of dogs and cats and their traceability hasbeen extremely well-received, introducing much-needed measuresregardingthetraceabilityof cats and dogs, breeding practices, and imported animals. Likewise, we are pleased tonote that the recommendations put forth by both the Voluntary Initiative on the health andwelfare of pets (specifically dogs and cats) and EFSA's opinion on housing and health ofcatsanddogsincommercial breedingestablishmentshavebeensubstantiallyrecognized.
Nevertheless, we feel that additional efforts are necessary to address issues such asextreme breeding and online sales, among others. To guide the European Commissionthrough our suggested amendments andtheir underlyingreasoning, wewill adhereprimarilyto the structure of the Chapters and, to a lesser extent, the Articles. It isnot our aimfor thisdocument tobeexhaustiveinnature.
SUBJECTMATTER&SCOPE
We believe in the need of the subject matter and scope to go beyond cats and dogs,including all animals that are permitted to be traded and kept as pets. As per Art 13 TFEUnot onlycatsanddogsbut other companionanimalsshouldbefullyregardedbytheUnion.
DEFINITIONS
Following the above rationale we advocate for the inclusion of a new definition of“companion animal” meaning any dog or cat, or any other animal kept for the purposes ofcompanionship or leisure. Likewise throughout the proposal and whenever relevant, “dogsandcats” maybereplacedby“companionanimals”.
We believe that “supplying” should include anyone while transferring of ownership occursand natural persons should not be excluded regardless of the means by which the supplytakesplace, onlineor offline.

CHAPTERIII IDENTIFICATIONANDREGISTRATIONOFDOGSANDCATS
Identification and Registrationshouldbemandatoryfor ALLdogsandcats. EUlawoperateswith the objective of eliminating existing and foreseenbarrierstotrade, asthelatter arejustas damaging to the integration of the EU’s single market as those that already exist. Withinthis context it is very important to stress that ANY dog or cat can become part of aneconomicactivityat anymoment intheir life.
Whenalitter isborn, it isdifficult toestablishwhether all, someor noneof theanimalswill betraded. If ALL animals are microchipped and their details are verifiably recorded andregistered shortly after birth it complies with Article 114 TFEU. However, a systemwhereI&R becomes mandatory only when animals are traded leaves enormous inaccuracies interms of the animals’ age, origin, health status etc. Not only that but for example not beingable to establish the age of an animal may constitute an impediment to trade as differentMember States may have different age restrictions on the trade of dogs and cats fromoneMember Statetoanother.
With this in mind Art 17.2 should aim at a verifiably identification and registration by aveterinarian; without verification, a database risks holding false information. In addition,registration for ANYother dog and cat (strays) in the Union, is to be made inthenameoftheresponsiblecompetent authority.
Online Platforms must provide not only for the animal I&R to be verified but for theassociation of that animal to the current owner/supplier, only when both are verified, the adshall be published. Placing theonusexclusivelyontheacquirer toverifyI&Rof theanimal isnot suitable for traceability accuracy. Such a double verification system: Veripet alreadyexistsandhasbeensuccessfullyimplementedincountriessuchasIreland.

Popular brachycephalic breeds have a high prevalence of obstructed labour, an incidencevery evident in the English bulldog[1] and, toalesser extent, theFrenchbulldog. Caesareansections are required in approximately 60–80% of cases to solve obstructed labour inbitches[2], perpetuating unnecessary suffering, impeding natural breeding and abusingartificial insemination.
In light of the above the following paragraph should be added: Breedingwithanimalsthathave undergone a caesarean section should be prohibited. In the case of animals inwhich a caesarean section was necessary due to breeding-related anatomical andphysiological changes, bitches should be excluded from breeding from the firstcaesarean section. Bitches that have had a caesarean section for other reasonsshouldbebannedfrombreedingfromthesecondcaesareansectiononwards.

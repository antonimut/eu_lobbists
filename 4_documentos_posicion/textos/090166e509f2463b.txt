 
Expedia Group feedback on the review of the Package Travel Directive (PTD) 
 
 
Expedia Group welcomes the Commission's initiative to review Directive 2015/2302 on package travel and linked 
travel arrangements (PTD) nearly four years on from the outset of the COVID-19 pandemic. This global crisis brought 
about a significant decline in travel, resulting in an unprecedented number of cancellation requests from consumers 
within a short timeframe. Th e crisis revealed fundamental flaws in the regulatory framework on package travel, 
uncertainty for consumers while also unfairly and disproportionately burdening package travel organisers. Through 
its proposed revision of the PTD, the Commission has not succeeded in effectively addressing many of these crisis-
related challenges. This feedback paper outlines these shortcomings and proposes recommendations for an 
improved legal framework that safeguards consumer interests while ensuring the continued availability of packages 
in the market.  
 
Prepayment Limitation: The European Commission's revised PTD, notably through Article 5(a), sets a cap of 25% 
on consumer prepayments to the organisers of a package, aiming to shield funds and streamline refund processes. 
However, this provision places considerable financial strain on organi sers while overlooking the primary risk to 
consumer funds – the air component of the package. The tourism sector relies heavily on prepayments to manage 
operational costs and secure bookings, making this restriction financially burdensome and estimated to drive up 
package prices by 3-4%. The Commission has proposed a degree of flexibility in the prepayment limitation by allowing 
for higher prepayments where a need to pre-pay suppliers exists. However, this will still result in financial pressure 
on organisers, and may also lead to confusion for consumers who will, in most cases, pay a prepayment well in excess 
of 25%. For example, most packages include a flight, and the flight component of a package will always be a significant 
proportion of the overall value of a package (i.e., the flight component could make up approximately 50% of the 
overall cost of a package on average) and airlines will generally always require full prepayment in order to secure the 
flight ticket.  
As an alternative, Expedia Group proposes that the flight component of a package be ringfenced through an escrow 
arrangement and for this obligation to be imposed directly on the airlines through the existing IATA framework or 
through a bespoke escrow arrangement for flights. Under this scenario, IATA would release funds to the airline at 
the travel date and, where necessary, pay out refunds to consumers. A solution with a similar logic already exists as 
a ‘settle as you fly’ arrangement in commercial air travel and could be further developed towards an arrangement 
which ensures that the funds are released when the consumer disembarks on the flight.  
Insolvency Protection: While package organisers have a regime of insolvency protection that they are bound by a 
notable gap exists for airlines. Airline insolvencies pose a real risk to consumers and adequate legal protections need 
to be introduced to protect passengers from the losses and risks they may face during an airline insolvency. Data 
shows that the number of passengers impacted by airline insolvencies is substantial. According to the European 
Commission’s study on the current level of protection of air passenger rights in the EU, even in pre-pandemic times 
- between 2011 and October 2019 - 5.6 million passengers were affected by airlines insolvencies. The significant 
impact of airline insolvencies on passengers underscores the urgency of including such protections in the review of 
the Air Passenger Rights Regulation. Expedia Group calls for comprehensive measures to ensure consumer security, 
irrespective of the type of air service the consumer purchases (e.g. standalone travel products or package).

Package Definition:  The Commission's revisions to Article 3(2) broaden the package definition to include 'type A' 
LTAs, formed when two different travel services are booked within three hours, inadvertently burdening traders who 
don’t intentionally sell packages, with package organiser responsibilities and introducing legal uncertainties. This 
expansion also encompasses situations where additional travel services are booked within 24 hours, further 
complicating matters and imposing tracking burdens on package organi sers. Additionally, the Commission's 
extension of 'click -through' package complexity now includes the transfer of 'other personal data' alongside 
travellers' name, payment details, and email address, now without a 24-hour limit. This change creates challenges 
for package organisers sharing customer details with third parties, as any data sharing may constitute a package, 
irrespective of timeframe or data type. Expedia Group recommends reinstating the 24-hour limit for 'click-through' 
packages and refining the concept to ensure that package definition only includes the transfer of travellers' 'other 
personal data' when accompanied by their name, payment details, and email address.  Furthermore, while the 
Commission aims to simplify the Linked Travel Arrangement (type ‘B’ LTAs) definition under Article 3(5), ambiguity 
persists. Expedia Group proposes removing linked travel arrangements from the text entirely to streamline the 
regulatory framework for the benefit of consumers and package organisers. 
Cancellation Rights: In contrast to past crises such as , volcanic ash or terrorist attacks , which led to airlines 
cancelling flights, the COVID-19 crisis involved consumers cancelling their travel plans due to movement restrictions 
and other restrictions at the package holiday destination.  
The PTD under Article 12(2) allows for cancellations by consumers and a full refund in cases of “unavoidable and 
extraordinary circumstances”. In such cases, package organisers are required to refund consumers in full for their 
package bookings. The Air Passenger Rights Regulation, by contrast, requires airlines to refund passengers where 
the airline cancels the flight . Despite the restrictions imposed by governments, many airlines declined to cancel 
flights and continued or resumed flight operations during COVID-19 and would therefore not always refund flight 
bookings that formed part of a package. Package organisers were often left to bear the financial burden of the flight 
component of a package  when the consumer cancelled a package under Article 12(a), but the underlying flight 
component of the package had not been cancelled by the airline. This has proven to be unsustainable for package 
organisers and consumers alike. Unfortunately, in its review of the Air Passenger Rights Regulation, the European 
Commission decided not to amend the pillar relating to cancellations of flights by consumers in case of major crises. 
The lack of consistency between PTD and the Air Passenger Rights Regulation regarding termination and refund 
rights for “unavoid able and extraordinary circumstances” presents a major hurdle in protecting the rights of 
consumers and refund timeframes and places a disproportionate burden on package organisers.  
The European Commission attempted to address the issue of consumer refunds by proposing to add a limited B2B 
refund right in Article 22(2) of the PTD review and in the revision of the Air Passenger Rights Regulation. Despite this 
being a step in the right direction, it is not enough to address the liability gap  as the proposed B2B refund right 
would only apply if the service provider cancels or fails to provide the service .  Airlines can easily sidestep this 
obligation if the flight goes ahead or by only allowing organisers to bundle fully refundable flights in packages. Since 
the cost difference between refundable and non -refundable flights is significant, this w ould make packages 
unattractive for consumers. As such, both the PTD and Air Passenger Rights Regulation must be amended further 
and aligned with regard to cancellation rights for “unavoidable and extraordinary circumstances” to ensure packages 
remain an attractive option for consumers and protect the viability of the organiser. 
 
Travel warnings : The review of the PTD should reconsider what constitutes unavoidable and extraordinary 
circumstances and the circumstances under which organisers are responsible. Broadening the scope of Article 12(2), 
but not providing a B2B refund under the PTD and Air Passenger Rights Regulation when the airline hasn’t cancelled 
the flight component of the package, will result in an even bigger strain on package organisers.  In addition, in its 
proposal for Article 12(3)(a) the Commission adds new wording with a view to clarifying the extent to which official 
travel warnings issued by authorities should be used to qualify an unavoidable and extraordinary circumstance and 
justify the cancellation of a package  with a full refund . However, the wording is vague and would benefit from 
clarifications on which government travel advice is definitive. Furthermore, clarification is needed that organisers 
should not bear 100% of the cancellation costs where the event is foreseen or ongoing, and the consumer has been 
warned about the risks involved. If consumers are provided with clear information before completing their booking, 
then they should be given the option to book packages (which may be lower in cost) but come without recourse if 
informed about the risk involved and the foreseeable event occurs. Therefore, Expedia Group calls for a clear 
understanding of the foreseeability of an event and its impact on a consumer’s right to terminate and get a full 
refund.   
 
Summary: It is in everyone’s interest to promote transport and tourism in Europe and encourage consumer 
confidence in future bookings. However, the current structure places all the financial burden on one party in the 
operating chain, the organiser , and this approach is not sustainable.  A clear, simplified, and coordinated approach 
to the revision of the PTD and related guidance is urgently needed to provide a system where burdens are shared 
equally and fairly among participants. Expedia Group calls on policymakers to ensure a fair outcome in the revision 
of the PTD and secure the healthy continuation of the package travel ecosystem. Without urgent action, we could 
see a negative impact on the package offering and on consumer choice in turn. Package travel in the EU has 
traditionally been a success story, contributing significantly to the EU economy and enhancing consumer protection 
and satisfaction. However, it is now necessary to revise the Commission’s proposals, as they have the potential to 
undermine package travel and ultimately lead to its decline.

Position regarding the regulation proposal on the welfare of 
dogs and cats and their traceability 
 
EUROPETNET is an NGO founded in 2000 which gathers 47 databases in 26 countries among which 20 
Member States of the European Union  to work on identification and registration o f cats, dogs and 
ferrets. EUROPETNET’s activities are acknowledged in the European Commission’s regulation proposal 
in the part dedicated to the feedbacks from the impact assessment.  
 
 
 
Identification and registration must be mandatory for all cats and dogs 
EUROPETNET and its members welcome the regulation proposal on the welfare of dogs and cats and 
their traceability.  
For years now, EUROPETNET has been advocating for mandatory identification and registration  of 
domestic carnivores by certified professionals into interoperable databases recognized by the Member 
States (or regions pending of the internal organization of each country).  
However, EUROPETNET calls on the European institutions to go further and make identification and 
registration mandatory for all cats and dogs. Anything less would be an oversight maintaining the 
existing divergent framework on identification and registration of pets at European level. Mandatory 
identification and registration of cats and dogs is the only way to efficiently fight against illegal trade 
of companion animals.  
Moreover, EUROPETNET’s members would like to emphasize one concrete point: no one can know, at 
the birth of a litter, which animals are going to be traded (transfer of responsibility) and which will not. 
If identification and registration is only mandatory at the point of the transfer, the information stored 
into the database (e.g. age, keepership…) will be less reliable and more prone to manipulation.

As an example, in France identification and registration of dogs are mandatory. 90% of dogs are 
registered in the I -CAD database. The provisions regarding identification and registration in the 
proposed regulation would only concern 50% of the dogs registered in the French database.  
 EUROPETNET and its members therefore call for a modification of Article 17 paragraph 1 
making identification and registration mandatory for all cats and dogs.  
 
Data stored in the databases and interoperability  within a coordinated and 
coherent framework  
As the European Commission foreseen the adoption of implementing acts regarding the content of the 
databases (Article 19 paragraph 3(a)), EUROPETNET’s members would like to suggest the following 
categories :  
 Species 
 Sex 
 Date and country of birth 
 Breed 
 Neutering or not 
 If relevant, function of the dog (e.g. guide dog). 
The European Commission also foresees the adoption of an implementing act regarding the 
interoperability of the databases  between the Member States (Article 19 paragraph 3(b)). The 
Commission also considers that while the databases must be implemented with in 3 years, the 
requirements relating to their interoperability will only have to be met within 5 years.  
However, it would seem more efficient for interoperability to be provided at the beginning of the 
implementation of a database.  Therefore, EUROPETNET recommends the alignment of the delay 
offered to establish a database with the one foreseen for ensuring the interoperability of the 
databases.  
 EUROPETNET calls for a modification of the Article 19 paragraph 2 and the introduction of a 
3 years delay for ensuring interoperability between the databases.  
In addition, the adoption of an already existing solution as the basis of the foreseen interoperability 
based system will lower the cost for the implementation of the system. The EUROPETNET tool, 
following in-depth work,  working as an add -on on existing databases, already responds to the 
requirement  to guarantee interoperability between 47 databases in 26 countries, and could be used 
as a reference. 
EUROPETNET’s solution at a glance… 
EUROPETNET solution is an add-on on existing databases which permits : 
 Live API (Application programming Interface)-transfer of non-sensitive data 
 The identification of the database where the details are stored in compliance with 
the GDPR via only one request on the EUROPETNET portal. 
 Safe and secure implementation for a new database.

EUROPETNET’s members would also like to emphasize the fact that if the European institutions decide 
to design a system with direct interoperability between the databases (not via an add-on program as 
EUROPETNET’s solution ) there is a high risk of failure and slow services associated with high 
maintenance cost because the system will need to rely on each database’s abilities.  
 Based on more than 20 years of experience on identification and registration of cats and 
dogs, EUROPETNET truly believes that its solution should be the starting points of the 
discussion regarding interoperability of databases foreseen through an implementing act by 
Article 19 paragraph 3(b)).  
 This implementing act should foresee an add -on based system and not direct 
interoperability between all the European databases making the implementation of the new 
system more efficient, more reliable and less expensive. 
 
 
Tackling illicit trafficking in e -commerce: towards effective information 
verification 
EUROPETNET supports the provisions concerning verification of animal registration  (Article 17 
paragraph 6).  
While the introduction of an information verification system is to be welcomed, it is nevertheless to 
be feared that the burden of verification and control will fall exclusively on the buyer. This could lead 
to a shift in the burden of responsibility. 
Alternatively, a solution involving a system integrated in online platforms (such as software) would be 
an effective and non-burdensome measure that would guarantee to platforms and consumers that the 
animal about to be advertised is registered at a traceable seller while being in line with the aims and 
provisions of the Digital Services Act (Regulation (EU) 2022/2065, DSA). In this way, animal registration 
with the advertiser will be provided, and not simply offered as an option for consumers to verify.  
With this in mind, EUROPETE NET has developed the "VeriPet" API-solution, which enables the cross -
border registration status of marketed animals to be verified.  VeriPet uses chip -data from the 
Europetnet central database that facilitates for live ID-registrations from all members. As a service on 
the Europetnet platform, VeriPet then performs queries verifying ownership and registration of a pet 
stored on national level 
 EUROPETNET and its members are therefore calling for an amendment to article 17, 
paragraph 6, modifying the verification to be carried out by purchasers and encouraging the 
introduction of integrated software solutions for verifying the cross -border registration 
status of traded animals. 
 
VeriPet at a glance…  
VeriPet connects platforms to databases and helps verify if an animal is registered to the person 
offering it: When creating an advert, the seller must enter the animal’s chip number in a field, and 
provide the phone number listed in the pet registry. If the data  matches with the data in the pet 
registry, VeriPet automatically sends to the phone number listed in the registry under this chip a one-
time code that is necessary for the ad to be posted. Only the registered owner will get this code.

Thus, before the ad is live, it is guaranteed that the animal is duly registered onto the advertiser. This 
validation will ensure traceability of the advertiser  and ensure consumer protection in the animal 
trade.

As a conclusion, EUROPETNET would like to emphasize its support for the adoption of this legislation 
- hopefully with the modifications highlighted in this document. As the only experienced actor on 
the subject of identification and registration of pets at European level, EUROPETNET and it s 
members need to be part of the debates on this text between the European institutions. Moreover, 
with 47 member databases, EUROPETNET is available to participate in the definition of the criteria 
that will allow the European institutions to select the representati ve databases in each Member 
States that should be made interoperable. Finally, EUROPETNET solution for interoperability should 
be known by all European decision makers as it represents the easiest and cheapest way to achieve 
interoperability.

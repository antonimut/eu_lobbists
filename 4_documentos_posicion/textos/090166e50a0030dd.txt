 
 
 
 
AnimalhealthEurope welcomes the opportunity to provide feedback to the public consultations on the 
Welfare and traceability of dogs and cats (new rules) (europa.eu)  and on Animal welfare – protection of 
animals during transport (europa.eu). AnimalhealthEurope is the association representing manufacturers of 
animal medicines, vaccines and suppliers of other animal health products in Europe . The health and the 
welfare of animals are intrinsically linked and for this reason animal welfare is a priority for 
AnimalhealthEurope’s members. We would therefore like to further develop our feedback below. 
 
 
General 
 
AnimalhealthEurope: 
- welcomes the recognition of the ‘One Health approach’, of the five domains, and the integration 
of a full article on animal health in the Proposal on dogs and cats 
- suggests to use a more positive language in the recitals of the Proposal on dogs and cats  
 
 
AnimalhealthEurope welcomes the clear recognition of the importance of the health of an animal for its 
general level of welfare. Elements concerning the health of cats and dogs can be found in different 
provisions of the Proposal. We applaud in particular the integration of an entire article 13  and paragraph 3 
in Annex I on the health of cats and dogs bred or kept in establishments or placed on the Union market. The 
health-welfare interlinkage however could have been further emphasi sed in the different parts of the 
Proposal, in particular in the recitals.  
 
The One Health approach recognises the link between human, animal health and the environment. As animal 
health and welfare are interdependent, it is very logical that the Proposal on dogs and cats recognises the 
One Health approach in its recital (8). While w e welcome this inclusion, we also regret that the recital 
seems to only recognise One Health in the context of the identification and registration of cats and dogs for 
the purpose of controlling the transmission of animal diseases to people, and does not recognise any positive 
contribution that such measures would bring in the form of healthy cats and dogs providing companionship 
or assistance through the human -animal bond as well as avoiding spreading pathogens agents  common to 
wildlife in the environment. 
 
Animal welfare science developed over the last years and the so -called five domains are now more widely 
accepted than the former five freedoms, as they not only aim to avoid negative experiences for the animal, 
but they recogni se that animals should also experience positive feelings and that negative experiences 
should be kept as mild and as few as possible. AnimalhealthEurope welcomes the addition of the five 
domains as a modernisation of the general setting for the Proposal. 
 
Whilst we understand the intention in the recitals of the Proposal on dogs and cats  focuses on addressing 
the many challenges for animal welfare, stray populations, and public health, etc., we would have 
appreciated a mention of the positive contributions of dogs and cats on human health and society . 
Influenced by behaviours linked to health and well -being, the human - animal bond creates favourable 
emotional, psychological, and physical interactions between people and animals, positively shaping societies 
and communities. There is even some evidence that it can have positive economic impacts on healthcare 
systems. This deserves a mention supporting why this Proposal is important.

Animals used for scientific purposes 
 
 
 
The Directive on the protection of animals used for scientific or educational purposes (Directive 2010/63) 
was adopted in 2010. This Directive could therefore not be mentioned in relevant legislation for the welfare 
of cats and dogs (such as Directive 98/5 8) or on the protection of animals during transport (Regulation 
1/2005). AnimalhealthEurope therefore welcomes the clarification offered thanks to the reference made to 
Directive 2010/63, in both Proposals on cats and dogs (Article 2.2 in connection with r ecital 13) and on 
transport (Article 2.4 in connection with recital 17). 
 
AnimalhealthEurope would however like to bring to the attention of the Commission that Article 1.5 of 
Directive 2010/63 excludes “veterinary clinical trials required for the marketing authorisation of a 
veterinary medicinal product” from the scope of the Directive. 
 
Veterinary clinical trials come very late in the process of authorisation of a veterinary medicinal product 
and are required under Regulation 2019/6 on veterinary medicines. During this phase, a medicine which was 
developed to prevent or cure the disease for a given animal species must be tested on animals of this same 
species. As they come late in the approval process, veterinary clinical tests are generally safe for the tested 
animals. They are nonetheless a necessary part of the approval process and aim at avoiding species-specific 
risks that cannot be detected by other methods such as Non-Animal Methods (NAMs). 
 
The definition of the veterinary clinical trials written in the Regulation 2019/6 may be subject to different 
interpretations due to the huge diversity of cases (e.g. research trials in the field or in an experimental 
station, premarketing authorisation and post marketing trials, etc).  
 
In order to avoid any unpredictable and unintended conflicting requirements that c ould result from dual 
legislation and could impact the future availability of veterinary medicines, AnimalhealthEurope calls on DG 
SANTE, in collaboration with the EMA to review and confirm that the provisions in both proposals will not 
affect the mandatory veterinary clinical trials required to put veterinary medicines on the market.  
 
Similarly, Article 14(3)(a) of the Proposal on the protection of animals during transport foresees that “ the 
animals to be transported are accommodated at the place of departure for at least 1 week prior to 
departure”. This Article is not listed in Article 2(4) and should therefore apply to animals involved in 
scientific purposes or in veterinary clinical trials. Given the variety of animals transported for clinical trials 
and for scientific purposes, AnimalhealthEurope believes that it can be detrimental t o assume that all 
animals from all species involved in clinical trials and for scientific purposes can be stay at least 1 week to 
the place of departure. We would welcome clarification from the Commission and the European Medicines 
Agency on the above and possibly suggest to add Article 14(3)(a) to the list of Articles listed in Article 2(4). 
 
 
AnimalhealthEurope: 
- demands that Article 14(3)(a) is added to the list of Articles listed in Article 2(4) of the Proposal 
on transport and, 
- that DG SANTE and the EMA review and confirm that the provision on the Proposal on transport 
prescribing that “the animals to be transported are accommodated at the place of departure for 
at least 1 week prior to departure” will not affect the availability of animals involved in marketing 
authorisation under Reg. 2019/6 and for scientific purposes 
- demands that DG SANTE and the EMA review and confirm that the provisions in both proposals, 
including the above mentioned “1 week rule”, will not affect the mandatory veterinary clinical 
trials required to put veterinary medicines on the market under Reg. 2019/6 
 
 
Definitions 
 
AnimalhealthEurope: 
- suggests to lower the maximum threshold for shelters and breeding establishments to derogate 
to the core of the Proposal on cats and dogs (or alternatively to reduce the number requirements 
smaller establishments can derogate to) . Pet shops shall not be allowed to derogate to such 
requirements 
- suggests that shelters should not be allowed to perform breeding activities 
- calls on the Commission to ensure coherence between the wording of the 2 proposals and to 
replace the term “cage” with “container” in the Proposal on dogs and cats  
 
 
A maximum threshold for breeding establishments to derogate to the core of the Proposal on cats and dogs 
is defined in Article 4 , in link with recital 15 . It allows the “establishments keeping up to three bitches or 
queens and producing in total two litters or less per establishment and calendar year ” to not comply with 
the provisions relating to the five domains of animal welfare of the Proposal. Similarly, “ pet shops keeping 
at any given time three dogs or less or six cats or less ” and “shelters keeping at any given time ten dogs or 
less or twenty cats or less” are also excluded from most of the obligations concerning the welfare of cats 
and dogs. As a consequence, they are not obliged to comply with minimum welfare requirements under this 
legislation, such as on feeding and watering, housing, health, behavioural needs or on painful practices.  
 
Focusing on the health aspect, proportionality might not apply in the field of cats and dogs as “small” 
breeding establishments represent an important share of the commercial activities in some Member States 
and could therefore water down the provisions of  the Regulation. AnimalhealthEurope therefore 
recommends lowering the threshold. Some Member States indeed already have lower thresholds in their 
national legislation, for example keeping one bitch or queen and selling one litter a year (French ordinance 
2015-1243). A similar rule should apply to pet shops. Alternatively, it could be possible to re strict the 
exemption to some articles of Chapter II, for example to Article 16 on the approval of breeding 
establishments only, rather than a blanket exemption. 
 
AnimalhealthEurope welcomes the inclusion of shelters in the scope of the Proposal. Given their intrinsic 
essential activity of collecting  and housing abandoned, stray or confiscated cats and dogs, shelters are 
particularly vulnerable to disease occurrence and spread. Hence, and although we fully understand the need 
and the intention to limit red tape and support these actors fulfilling a “ public interest function ”, we 
believe that keeping up to ten dogs or up to twenty cats at the same place creates risks for the health of 
these animals and of their keeper. For this reason, we would recommend to also lower the threshold of 
Article 4 for the shelters and use funds collected under this legislation (for example through the activities 
linked to animal registration) to help small shelters to comply with the provisions of Chapter 2, in particular 
with the One Health related aspects. 
 
Shelters also represent a significant amount of animals living in European households and are defined in 
Recital 18 and Article 3.14.  This definition does not mention the absence of breeding /reproduction 
activities, which is one of the important elements which shall distinguish shelters from breeding 
establishments. We therefore believe that shelters should not breed animals or allow reproduction and that 
this should be reflected in the core of the legislation. 
 
The Proposal seems to interchangeably use the words “cage” or “container” in different places, referring 
to the same objects (Article 12.3, Recitals 30 and 32). In the absence of a definition and harmonisation of 
this wording, the Proposal is somewhat unclear. We suggest to replace the term “cage” with “container” in 
order to align with the wording of the Proposal on transport. 
 
 
Animal health and animal welfare 
 
AnimalhealthEurope: 
- welcomes the provisions on preventive animal health care 
- calls on the Commission to extend these provisions beyond parasite control, where available, and 
to more species, where possible 
 
 
AnimalhealthEurope welcomes the Commission decision to include an entire Article 13 on the health and 
welfare of cats and dogs. In particular, we applaud the provisions on biosecurity, aiming at separating a 
possibly sick or vulnerable animal from the others and ensuring that it receives treatment from a 
veterinarian as well as the provisions on preventive animal care. We however would like to highlight that 
parasites are only one of the sources of diseases and that other diseases (including some zoonoses) are fully 
preventable. Cats and dogs bred and kept in establishments or placed on the European market should be 
protected against these diseases. Together with the traceability provisions of this Proposal, a ll these 
preventive measures support the One Health approach in the European Union (Recital 8) and where needed, 
could be further detailed in Annex I, Paragraph 3. 
 
The Proposal on the protection of animals during transport also includes elements on the health of animals 
during transport, mainly linked with the provisions on the fitness for transport (Annex I, Chapter I(10)). We 
suggest extending these provisions on “necessary preventive veterinary treatments” to all animals covered 
by this Proposal and not only to cats and dogs  and to include the prevention and control of internal and 
external parasites, unless there is a contraindication (e.g. respecting the withdrawal period before slaughter 
for food production animals). 
 
 
Identification and registration (traceability) 
 
AnimalhealthEurope: 
- calls on the Commission to ensure better accuracy of the data in the databases, while respecting 
some other requirements, such as compliance to GDPR, etc. 
- believes that the databases should allow enough flexibility to access information such as 
vaccination and other health records, etc. 
 
 
AnimalhealthEurope welcomes the Provisions on identification and registration (I&R) of cats and dogs 
(traceability) of the Proposal on the welfare of cats and dogs. Together with veterinary care and education, 
I&R constitute the core of responsible animal ownership. Over the last years, an increasing number of 
Member States have adopted mandatory I&R as part of their national legislation  (Knowing the Law - The 
CARO Project - A database for companion animal responsible ownership (caro-project.org)). Although an EU-
wide database would have been the most effective solution, it is time that the Commission commits to make 
identification and registration mandatory and that databases are interoperable and to fight against the lack 
of or improper I&R (Recitals 39 & 40). Non-professional owners who were supplied with a registered dog or 
cat and who would like to cede the ownership of the animal should be responsible for ensuring that the 
change of ownership is reflected  in the database (Article 17). Competent Authorities should operate the 
changes in the databases within an acceptable time. 
 
Starting with microchips, identification technologies have developed in an unprecedented way over the past 
years. The I&R databases can support the objectives of this Regulation and beyond that  the One Health 
approach in many ways: it could provide genealogical information to fight against inbreeding (recital 27) or 
allow for the keeping of medical records to help improve the health of the animals and protect their keepers 
 
 
from possible zoonoses (Article 13) as it is already the case in some countries. For this reason, it is important 
to allow for the necessary flexibility in the databases, hence allowing for future improvements. The change 
of ownership as well as the end of the life of a dog or a cat  should be reflected in the database in the 
animal’s file . On the other hand, more harmonisation is needed between Member States concerning 
information made available to the public and in particular concerning the alignment with some other 
requirements such as GDPR . In particular, only certified civil servants should be allowed to access some 
information in the database and changes to the database should only be made under veterinary supervision, 
by certified veterinarians. 
 
 
Trainings  
 
AnimalhealthEurope: 
- welcomes the inclusion of provisions on education and training in both Proposals and recommends 
to include trainings on first aid, animal health and indicators to the Proposal on transport  
 
 
Education and trainings are essential to prevent animal suffering. AnimalhealthEurope welcomes the 
inclusion of provisions on education and training in both Proposals and would like to suggest extending these 
provisions. The Proposal on transport should include trainings for monitoring the indicators listed in Article 
26.1, in particular the signs of health and physical problems other than injuries (Article 37.2). It should also 
include training on first aid to make sure animals will never be left without immediate assistance when 
needed.  
 
 
Information for future owners & online sales (cats and dogs Proposal)  
 
AnimalhealthEurope: 
- welcomes the provisions on responsible ownership of the Proposal on dogs and cats and call on 
the Commission to ensure s that the sellers should be responsible for the authenticity of the 
information provided to the future owner 
 
 
AnimalhealthEurope welcomes the Provisions on responsible ownership of cats and dogs and on the 
obligation for operators to provide information about responsible ownership, including the obligation to 
display a warning statement for animals advertised online (Article 8 and Recital 38). Experience however 
showed that buying or adopting an animal can be an impulsive decision, which can lead to abandonment or 
animal suffering. For this reason, many actors such as responsible shelters implemented the rule that cats 
and dogs cannot be purchased or adopted from the first visit, but that future owners should come back after 
few days to get their companion animal. Such good practices should also be part of the legislation , in 
particular when the animal is advertised online.  
 
Articles 8.2 and 17.4 and .5 and Recital 41 describe the rules for the online advertising of cats and dogs. 
These draft provisions put on the future owner the burden to verify the identification and registration of 
the animal, hence allowing for possible fraud. AnimalhealthEurope believes that the sellers as well as online 
platforms should take responsibility for the animals that are placed on the European market. Such systems 
are already available, like VeriPet for example (VeriPet – the Technical Solution to Stop Illegal Puppy Traders 
- FOUR PAWS International - Animal Welfare Organisation (four-paws.org)). 
 
 
 
 
 
Veterinary supervision  
 
AnimalhealthEurope: 
- believes that the presence of a veterinarian at loading and unloading is not realistic and would 
recommend to extend the scope of this provision of the Proposal on transport  
- recommend that transport vehicles are equipped with first aid kits 
- suggest to make better use of the animal welfare visit of the Proposal on dogs and cats  
 
 
There is currently a shortage o f veterinarians and in particular in official veterinarians in Europe. 
AnimalhealthEurope understands and welcomes the intention  of the Proposal on transport  to ensure that 
the loading of animals in vehicles goes well, as loading and unloading are critical for the safety of an animal 
transported. We however doubt that Article 17.2 is implementable and suggest to extend the list of people 
who can supervise the loading operations. Some other actors could for example ensure the safety of the 
animals, provided that they receive appropriate training from veterinarians. It’s important to note that it’s 
not only during the loading and unloading that accidents can happen. This said, transport should only take 
place when the vehicles are equipped with proper first aid kits.  
 
We welcome the animal welfare visits of Article 10 of the Proposals on Dogs and Cats. We believe that they 
should also be used as the opportunity to control documentation certifying that the caretakers duly acquired 
the competences required in Article 9. The animal welfare visits should also be used by the veterinarian to 
check the health of the animal. Article 13 of the Proposal on cats and dogs should therefore also include 
individual health plans and regular health checks for the animals.

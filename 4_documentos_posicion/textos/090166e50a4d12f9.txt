Merck feedback to the Commission Proposal for a 
Regulation establishing a common data platform on 
chemicals 
 
 
Merck welcomes the opportunity to comment on the proposals by the Commission to 
streamline regulatory processes and to improve cooperation between the agencies responsible 
for chemical management in the EU. 
 
Merck develops, manufactures, and markets high -quality medicines, innovative products for 
the biotech and pharmaceutical industries and for academic research as well as specialty 
chemicals for high -tech applications.  We enable innovation in Life Science, Healthcare and 
Electronics by providing a vast portfolio of tens of thousands of substances and hundreds of 
thousands of products that are e.g. required by laboratories, the bio(chemical) industries and 
the semiconductor industry. We are a manufacturer, distributor, downstream user as well as a 
test laboratory that enables our own innovation and that of others. 
 
This places us in a unique position to provide feedback regarding the handling of study data 
and would like to add to the comments of CEFIC and the VCI on this topic. We believe the 
current proposal could result in a very significant challenge for both industry and authorities in 
terms of data management. 
 
In order to ensure the objectives of the proposal are met, we ask: 
 
• for a description of what studies qualify for notification and which do not need to be 
added; and 
• that these studies should be limited to those with clear regulatory relevance; 
• for careful consideration of substance identity is made when using data from different 
regulatory schemes for an assessment; and 
• that care is taken that confidentiality provisions such as those provided under REACH 
Article 10(a)(xi) are not accidentally breached. 
 
The list of regulations in scope of study notification (Annex I) is extremely extensive and there 
is no description in the proposal about what constitutes a study. This could result in a wide 
range of scientific activity which may be considered a “study”. For example, physicochemical, 
product efficacy or quality testing, and even transportation and packaging testing, as well as 
basic research in pharmaceutical development could be considered within the scope of 
notification, or be included later. There is a need for an exhaustive description of what studies 
qualify for notification and which do not. 
 
As a company carrying out tens of thousands of tests for third parties in addition to our own, 
we are required to consider the impact of the proposal on operations where we test across a

product portfolio of tens of thousands of  substances. This would mean we would potentially 
be required to submit hundreds of thousands of study notifications. Many notifications which 
would have a negligible utility for assessments made by authorities as they may be performed 
as part of product d evelopment and do not form part of the information requirements (e.g. 
REACH Annexes VII to X). Unless addressed, this proposal would inadvertently put them within 
scope of study notification.  
 
Assessing which of these studies need notification and the not ification itself would require 
significant resources and IT systems, at an estimated  higher cost than the 30 euro/per hour 
cost used as a reference in the staff working document. Instead, a clear definition of applicable 
study types that require notification is critical. These should be limited to those with a clear 
regulatory relevance, such as studies that form part of the information requirements under the 
legislation listed in Annex I of the proposal. 
 
As a supplier of high specification fine chemicals (e.g. GMP materials for pharmaceutical 
production and reference standards), we would also like to remind that the purity profile of a 
substance has important effects on the outcome of an evaluation. If data and assessment 
outcomes are to be re- used by authorities, important context regarding the test substance 
identity and purity may be lost and could result in incorrect conclusions about safety or 
required risk management measures. Data owners will likely need to be notified by authorities 
to be able to provide contextual information and authorise the intended data re -use. 
Maintaining confidentiality of the information will also be a challenge, beyond the provisions 
made in the proposal for protection of PCN and Cosmetics notifications in Art 3(3). 
 
This common data platform has the potential to achieve the goal of streamlining processes for 
both authorities and industry and to provide additional certainty for industry to operate. 
However careful consideration about the scope of data requests needs to be made in order to 
avoid working counter to the stated objectives by introducing an additional administrative 
burden for both ECHA and industry. 
 
We would welcome any additional industry engagement in finding an efficient way to exchange 
study notification data.

EC public consultation 
Chemical safety – better access to chemicals data for 
safety assessments 
FEFANA position 
 
Feedback on the proposal for a regulation - COM(2023)779 
Building on its previous feedback (as of A ugust 2022) on the EC call for evidence for the initiative on better access to 
chemicals data for safety assessments1, FEFANA would like to flag the following concerns and proposals:  
1. Expected impact on the lifecycle of applications for authorisation under the General Food Law 
The proposed provisions on the notification of studies commissioned or carried out by business operators are consistent 
with a similar transparency -related notification obligation stipulated under the General Food Law ( GFL, Article 32b ) 
however, how those provisions should coexist and what effects would derive from the introduction of mandatory use of 
the IUCLID software in order to synchronise chemicals data management  was not adequately assessed . EU companies 
falling under the scope of the GFL, already penalised by the administrative pressure generated by the transparency rules 
introduced in 2021, will have to get acquainted (via additional regular trainings) with the required additional IT notions.  
From FEFANA members experience of almost three years with the  EFSA enforcement of procedures  related to the 
Transparency Regulation2, a significant impact on the lifecycle of applications for authorisation is clearly noticeable. The 
procedures in place to comply with the transparency rules, particularly  those related to the pre -submission phase 
(including the notification of studies and confidentiality management ), have shown to be significantly more time - and 
resource- demanding than what originally e xpected3. The resulting increasing delay in processing applications 4 has 
significantly worsened the pre-existing backlog which in turn heavily affects the operations of the EU feed businesses (e.g., 
affecting EU companies’ ROI - Return On Investment, hindering the introduction of innovative and sustainable products in 
the EU market…).

Thus, we would like you to consider more in depth the experience gained by DG SANTE and the EFSA with the objective 
to avoid the introduction of complex and non-fit-for-purpose transparency-related procedures that could: 
• generate additional backlog on EFSA processing of applications submitted in the EU (EFSA-ECHA interaction in the 
management of a new shared data platform might engender some additional delay5); 
• require EU companies notifying chemicals studies in ECHA/EFSA also in case of internal research purposes, quality 
controls and/or for chemicals developed not to be sold in EU (Article 22(1)); 
• compromise the rationalization effort made by the EFSA (in terms of reduction of the  administrative hassle) 
clarifying which analytical measurements are exempted from notification of study obligations 6 (under the EU 
Transparency Rules related to the General Food Law - GFL). The formulation of Article 22 (1) comp romises the 
harmonization effort by diverging from EFSA established practices and standards e.g . physicochemical data are 
not mandated under EFSA practical arrangements but might be required under ECHA guidelines, leading to 
potential discrepancies in data submission and evaluation processes for chemicals having multiple uses; 
• further increase the administrative burdens for EU business operators and subsequently on the EU authorities. 
We would therefore welcome a harmonised approach following the existing practice under the GFL, with the aim to 
minimise the administrative hassle. For this we would suggest aligning the scope of the studies to be notified according to 
art 22 of the proposed regulation with the Transparency regulation (EU) 2019/1381 and EFSA’s practical arrangements on 
the matter.  
REMINDER - Quotation (ref. to FEFANA feedback submitted on 12/08/2022)1 
We therefore call on the Commission to take more time to evaluate the consequences of this significant horizontal 
legislative initiative on data together with stakeholders that are involved in the production of data, and in placing on the 
market and using of regulated products and substances. FEFANA is convinced that a thorough impact assessment (on top 
of targeted consultations with selected stakeholders) coupled with close participatory processes with food- feed area 
stakeholders will be the key to making the better access to chemicals data approach a success which can effectively reduce 
the duplication of work. 
2. Unclear estimations used to substantiate policy decisions  
In the “Explanatory Memorandum” of the proposal, in paragraph “2. Legal Basis at the point on proportionality (pp.7-8), 
it is quoted that “the economic operators and laboratories have been estimated to experience some administrative burden 
linked to the submission of study notifications that will only take 30 minutes for each”;  as the source of this estimation is 
not provided, we would like to flag that this figure is not understandable. Moreover, since a number of business operators 
do not have experience using the proposed IUCLID  software, additional e stimations should be integrated and  should 
distinguish between familiarised users of the IUCLID tool and newcomers. Also, the hourly salary considered in the cost 
calculation (29.1) seems quite low in view of expertise required. H arnessing industry expertise before setting standard 
formats and controlled vocabularies is essential for ensuring effective and efficient adoption of the proposed measures.

Food and feed business operators are, in most of the cases, not familiar with the specific software tool needed to reach 
the interoperability of the standard data formats. Therefore, it should have been assessed that additional time will be 
necessary as to align existing and proposed functionalit ies, data handling, and the specific extended use of IUCLID  
requirements.  
3. Law-making not based on the views of those impacted 
 
The pre -market approval existing for feed additives under the General Food Law requires applicants to provide an 
extensive set of data (similarly to what happens in case of other EU-regulated products) to assess different types of risks 
related to the safety of the feed additive for the animal, for users, for consumers, and for the environment. FEFANA would 
like to stress that, even if the proposal does not aim at creating any new data requirements, it may result in more complex, 
time- and cost-intensive administrative processes for business -operators as well as for other actors invol ved in EU 
competent agencies. When authorities intend to re -use data contained in the common data platform, this should be 
conducted in transparent and open manner to ensure validity of the data used with regards to the substance identity and 
specific requirements of the concerned legi slation. We would therefore request you to consider applying open public 
consultations for the use of chemicals data contained in the common data platform generated under the different 
regulations, prior to assessments.  
 
For these time-related aspects as well, we regret that no ex-ante Impact Assessment has been considered. It appears that 
only certain aspects were assessed before sending the EC proposals to the European Parliament and the Council for 
examination. It emerges that f ocus was given to the impacts on EU agencies’ resources  while consequences on EU 
businesses have largely been overlooked  by not taking into due account the diversity and large number of Union acts 
included in the Annexes of the proposal. The proposed approach cannot therefore ensure the necessary evidence-based, 
transparent EU law-making based on the views of those impacted. 
 
4. Call for an Impact assessment 
 
Contrary to what is alleged in the documents under public consultation, t he proposed measures will imply costs to 
businesses and have significant economic impact at EU scale.  We suggest that possible data- and cost-sharing processes 
which already exist in different legislations need to be analyzed  and streamlined in this context and insights should be 
drawn for this new law -making exercise to prevent unintended negative impacts hitting the EU business operators.  We 
therefore call on the Commission to  carry out a full-fledge impact assessment for its proposals and take more time to 
evaluate the consequences of its very significant legislative initiatives together with stakeholders that are involved in the 
safety assessment of regulated products.

About FEFANA  
FEFANA (EU Association of Specialty Feed Ingredients and their Mixtures) is the united voice of the specialty feed 
ingredients business in Europe. Our membership comprises manufacturers and traders of feed additives, functional 
feed ingredients, premixes and other mixtures of specialty ingredients that enter the food chain via feed. FEFANA 
facilitates the dialogue between EU institutions and feed business operators while promoting feed and food safety 
and a fair and competitive market. For any additional information please contact the FEFANA Secretariat at 
info@fefana.org or visit our website at www.fefana.org.

TDMA response to public consultation on  
“One Substance One Assessment” (OSOA) proposal 
 
The Titanium Dioxide Manufacturers Association  (TDMA), welcomes the Commission's efforts 
to improve and enhance the safety assessment of chemicals across EU legislation. With this, we 
look forward to seeing improved communication and coordination flows around regulatory 
processes, while offering much-needed predictability. 
The TDMA also expresses its support to the feedback submitted by Cefic, the European Chemical 
Industry Council, on the OSOA legislative proposal package, highlighting some further points for 
consideration of particular importance and relevance for titanium dioxide (TiO₂).  
What is one substance? 
The identity and toxicological profile of some chemicals may be clear across a range of 
applications. However, in other cases such as for TiO₂, the situation is more complex and needs 
to be taken into consideration for OSOA, as it exists in different forms. 
The form that is used as a food additive is a very specific form of untreated/non-surface treated 
pigmentary non -nano grade with a specific specification and strict purity requirements; 
representing less than 1% of the European market. Which is different than the pigment forms 
used in the main applications of plastics, paints and food contact materials . This is imperative 
since in their recent evaluationi, EFSA has considered forms that were not used as a food additive 
in their assessment, an error that distorted the final results, and highlights the need to be specific 
about what is meant with “one substance”. 
Other food additives or food contact materials may be considered mixtures of substances under 
REACH and the implications of this for OSOA needs to be considered. In addition, it is unclear 
how a grouping such as microplastics, as defined in the recent ECHA restriction, could fit into the 
OSOA system, given it does not refer to specific registered substances. 
Nanomaterials and one substance 
A lot of the scrutiny of TiO₂ is related to concerns about nanomaterials, though only 1% of the 
volume of TiO₂  on the EEA market meets the definition of a nanomaterial under REACH . The 
update of the REACH regulation brought several new requirements for substances meeting the 
definition of a nanomaterial. This includes legally defining the concept of sets of nanoforms

It is, thus, important to consider how these subcategories will be addressed in the OSOA 
process. Furthermore, the initial focus of the observatory will be on nanomaterials, which bears 
the risk of spotlighting individual substances  unnecessarily. As the observatory will not be a 
substitute for required risk management action where a hazard or risk has been identified, it can 
only cover such situations and substances where such concrete action has not been identified. 
Therefore, there is a risk that the observatory will highlight requirements  for further scrutiny 
(and thus put a substance into the spotlight) based on uncertainty. 
Considerations to One Assessment 
The TDMA welcomes the OSOA proposal to include a consideration of risk and hazard. Likewise, 
the current approach to include industry expertise into the process, for example as observers in 
the ECHA committee processes. As well as  the proposed packages and conflict resolution 
mechanisms put in place.  
As the uses of a substance and therefore the risk to the user will vary, the current approach of 
differentiation and consideration of such variation is a positive feature that should be 
preserved and continued . Evidence shows that conclusions related to chemicals can be 
borderline and therefore it is not surprising that independent scientific bodies report different 
outcomes. Hence, independent reviews and opinions should be considered as a strength in an 
open and transparent society. 
How about data quality? Accessing relevant academic data 
The TDMA recognizes that academic data has a role to play in science-based policy making and 
that a process needs to be in place to ensure that data is reliable and robust. Prior to the re -use 
of data, special attention must be paid to the design of the study that generated the data 
including substance identity, limitations, protocols, scope and units. Both the study context and 
the applicability domain of the generated data needs to be valid in the context of the re-use. 
It is also noteworthy that the Titanium Dioxide Industry Consortium (TDIC) routinely screens 
and includes relevant academic articles into the TiO₂ REACH lead registration dossier. The other 
concern is the inherent bias as non-adverse outcome being less likely to be published. The OECD 
Guidelines for the Testing of Chemicals were developed for this reason, and it is important that 
they remain the key benchmark.  
Data requirements and assessment methodologies - Important 
concerns raised in the RPA / FoBiG report (Dec 2023) 
RPAii and FoBigiii, two well-known advisors, were commissioned by EFSAiv to carry out a study to 
collect and analyse data requirements (DRs) and risk assessment methodologies (RAMs) applied 
within the regulatory frameworks of EFSA, the European Chemicals Agency (ECHA), the European 
Medicines Agency (EMA), SCCS and SCHEER.  
 
Page 3 
The study summarises the main findings as follows: “ The study identified inconsistencies within 
regulatory areas in some cases, e.g. between legal acts and guidance documents. Furthermore, 
it found differences between regulatory frameworks in the DRs and RAMs  with respect to 
terminology, general and detailed DRs and RAMs, the quality standards for experimental studies 
and other issues. Based on the identified differences and the potential for harmonisation across 
the regulatory frameworks, several recommendations were outlined in the study that involve 
addressing specific as well as more principal and structural differences.” [emphasis added] 
TDMA considers these findings as important for the implementation and execution of OSOA.

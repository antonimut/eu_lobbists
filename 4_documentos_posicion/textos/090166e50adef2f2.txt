Public consultation: Chemical safety – better access to chemicals datafor safetyassessments
PositionfromEurogroupfor Animals, CrueltyFreeEuropeandtheEuropeanCoalitiontoEndAnimal Experiments04/04/2024
Eurogroup for Animals, Cruelty Free Europe and the European Coalition to End AnimalExperiments welcome the publication of the proposal to establish a common data platformon chemicals data (“the proposal”), and very much support the objectives, as initiallyexpressed by the Commission, to: “improve accesstochemicalsdatabyremovingtechnicaland administrative obstacles. This is based ontheprinciplethat datashouldbeeasytofind,share and reuse, aswell asbeinteroperableandsecure.” (Seethesectiontitled“[a]bout thisinitiative”, introducing both consultations on better access to chemicals data for safetyassessments.)
While we acknowledge that meeting all of the above proposed objectives presents manylogistical and legal challenges, and that the current proposal is a welcomed first step, wealso wish to express our concern that the means to ensure data can be reused is not yetsufficiently elaborated. We believe that reuse of data – which would necessarily entail newdata sharing opportunities and obligations – has always been central to the Commission’sobjectives concerning the “OneSubstanceOneRegistration” (OSOA) package, andthat thiselement is essential in terms of ensuring that newanimal testing only takes place when allother meansof obtainingtheresultssought havebeenexhausted.
Wewouldliketocomment onanumber of specificelementsof theproposal, toalignwiththepriorities set by the EUin other policy areas, includingaddressingthephasingout of animaltesting in chemical safety assessments as a priority, thus making the legislation consistentandfuture-proof.

Westronglywelcometheproposal’semphasisonincreasingtheuseof humanbiomonitoringdata (as well as other positive impacts). This promotes the application of human-relevantresearch concerning the effects of chemicals on individuals and at population level, andwillinformthedevelopment anduptakeof non-animal test methods.
Datasharing
We wish to draw specific attention to the wording of Article 17 of the proposal whichacknowledges that “Authorities shall not use chemicals data contained in the common dataplatform to fulfil any legal obligations of duty holders”. While this is consistent with thecurrent limitations of the proposal, it raises the question of what could or should happen ifand when authorities know that relevant data exists but cannot use it to the fullest extentpossible.

Proposalsfor additional measures
Laboratoryinspectionsandmonitoring
Good Laboratory Practice (GLP) represents the most commonly used compliancemechanism by which authorities can ensure testing laboratories are operating according tocommonly agreed standards. While GLP compliance does not apply to all data generatedaccording to the Union acts listed in Annex I, it does represent a useful tool for ensuringapplication of scientific rigour. Also, GLP compliance is a criterion for the OECD’s MutualAcceptance of Data provision, an important means of ensuring that data from a testconducted in a member or adhering country will be accepted by regulators in any othermember or adheringcountry; thisprovisionsignificantlycontributestheavoidanceof testsonanimals.
We very much hope that for the purpose of increasing transparency and placing greateremphasis on the need for scientific rigour within test houses, wherever possible, thenamesof relevant national GLP compliance monitoring authorities shall be made public via thecommon data platform. It is in the interest of both the public and authorities to knowwherestudies are conducted because testing to meet EU requirements may be conductedanywhereintheworldaslongasrequiredstandards, for exampleGLP, aremet.
The issue of falsification of study results is well known, for example as illustrated by thepublicly available report on the fraud committed by the Hamburg LPT laboratory, as wasrevealed in 2019. This highlights the need for rigorous EUinspections and accreditation oftesting laboratories. The OSOA package may be one means of increasing transparencyconcerninglaboratorystandards.

We hope to see the introduction of a review clause, so that provisions relating to thecommon data platform can be reassessed in the near-future and at regular intervals.Additional obligations, e.g. to ensure moreeffectivedatasharingprovisionsandcoordinatedassessments of substances regulatedunder different Unionactswouldbewelcome. If theseobjectives are not achieved in the near term, the original OSOA objectives (which arebroader than provisions set out within the proposal) should still besustainedastheultimatemilestones and be implementedmoreeffectivelylater. It mayalsobecomeapparent that notall of the Unionactslistedcurrentlyapplythemost up-to-datescientificknowledgeregardingapplication of non-animal test methods and the transition to species-relevant NGRAapproaches, andthisshouldbeaddressedassoonaspossible.

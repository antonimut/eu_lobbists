Statement of the Austrian Federal Economic Chamber (WKÖ) 
Proposal for a new regulation on the screening of foreign 
investments 
 
The intention of the European Commission (EC) to strengthen the economic security of the 
European economy is to be welcomed. However, this should be done with a sense of 
proportion and must not get out of hand in the direction of state-controlled foreign trade. 
 
Foreign direct investment makes a fundamentally important contribution to economic 
growth and employment in Austria. The free movement of goods, services and capital is a 
key driver of prosperity and growth for all trade and economic partners involved. The 
Austrian and European economy is dependent on foreign investment in many areas, 
including research. The intention of avoiding risks to the sovereignty of the EU is 
understandable, but this must not lead to us preventing necessary foreign investment.  
 
In addition, it must be ensured that there will be no countermeasures by third countries 
that restrict or make it impossible for European and Austrian companies to access 
markets/invest in these countries. Especially in the current situation, it is essential to 
generate growth impulses through all channels. As a small country with just under nine 
million inhabitants, Austria in particular is dependent on trade with other countries. 
Therefore, global opening and equal access to markets and investment locations must have 
top priority. 
 
The following points should be emphasized on the basis of an initial assessment: 
 
 Revision of the FDI Regulation 2019 
Irrespective of the content, the question arises as to why the European Commission 
is resorting to a new regulation to "solve" the existing problems of the current FDI 
regulation. There would certainly be better solutions to encourage member states 
that have not installed a mechanism in the last five years to implement FDI 
regulation 2019. There is also a need for a comprehensive analysis of the impact of 
the control provisions to date on the investment activities of foreign companies in 
the EU and on the economic sectors affected. In addition, an analysis of the 
differently implemented systems of the EU member states is required, particularly 
regarding the bureaucratic burden and the creation of additional costs for 
companies that are already under increasing competitive pressure.  
 Comprehensive analysis of the effects to date required 
 Analysis of the differently implemented systems 
 
 Mandatory review mechanism for all member states 
In order to create a level playing field and prevent distortions of competition in the 
internal market, a mandatory introduction of FDI screening measures for all 
member states is to be welcomed in principle but it remains to be seen whether 
better cooperation between the Member States will lead to greater legal certainty 
for companies and reduce distortions of competition. However, care must be taken

to ensure that the review of individual transactions that are planned across national 
borders is completed as quickly as possible and is not associated with excessive 
bureaucracy for companies.  
Despite improved cooperation, the implementation of FDI regulation is still the 
responsibility of the member states, which means that 27 different solutions can 
still be expected, which are unlikely to improve legal certainty for companies. 
 Potential additional bureaucratic burden for companies due to 27 different 
systems 
 Questionable whether mandatory review mechanism leads to reduction of 
distortion of competition 
 International research cooperation and thus innovation are hindered 
 
 Minimum requirement for investment areas 
The draft regulation still does not clarify what exactly is covered by the economic 
sectors mentioned and how broadly the economic sectors mentioned are defined. 
Specifically, the question arises as to whether or not a company that only falls 
under the aforementioned technology sector in one area is also affected by the 
measures. A precise definition is required here. 
With the Investitionskontrollgesetz – InvKG, Austria already has an instrument in 
which the critical areas are defined - as the areas here have also been defined very 
broadly and in part unclear, the question arises as to whether the areas now 
desired to be controlled by the EC are already covered by the InvKG. Under no 
circumstances should there be an expansion of the controlled areas that go beyond 
the existing restrictions of the Austrian Investment Control Act. 
 Overly broad definition of investment areas 
 Legal uncertainty for companies operating in sub-sectors 
 Potential additional bureaucratic burden for companies 
 
 Extension of EU screening to investments by EU investors 
We are very critical of the extension of the legal basis to the EU internal market 
and the extension of FDI screening to investments by EU investors that are 
ultimately controlled by persons or companies from a non-EU country. We doubt the 
feasibility of such an extension and see the potential for more bureaucratic burdens 
for European and Austrian companies. Extending FDI screening to investments by EU 
investors does not minimize the risk of European and Austrian companies moving 
abroad in response to excessive EU measures, but rather increases the likelihood of 
this happening. It could also prevent important investments by interfering with the 
internal market. Therefore, a comprehensive study of the possible effects on 
European companies and their investment activities is required in advance. 
 Potential emigration of Austrian companies 
 Additional bureaucratic effort 
 Interference with entrepreneurial freedom 
 Potential risk of preventing important investments

General remarks 
UNIFE welcomes the European Commission’s Proposal on the Screening of Foreign Investments in the Union  (the 
Proposal hereafter) published on 24 January 2024.  
 
Ahead of the legislative debate between the European institutions and in the context of the current feedback consultation 
process, UNIFE would like to share its first reactions to the Proposal, highlighting positive developments compared to the 
current FDI Regulation, as well as to elucidate on a number of areas that deserve further consideration. 
 
 
Mandatory screening mechanisms and better harmonization 
Mandatory screening:  
In order to properly assess foreign direct investments that pose a threat to security or public order and to address loopholes 
and fragmentation across Member States as regards the current legal framework , it is important to establish an unique 
legal framework across EU countries. Today, several years after the entry into force of the current FDI Regulation, only 22 
Member States have implemented a screening mechanism.  
 
Article 3 of the Proposal introduces the mandatory requirement for Member States to establish screening mechanisms with 
a number of minimum requirements . According to Article 4(4), there is an obligation to impose authorisation requirement 
for foreign investments where the Union target established in their territory is part of or participates in projects or 
programmes of Union interest (Annex I) or is economically active in one of the areas listed in Annex II. Furthermore, Article 
4(2) point (g) also ensures that foreign investments subject to an authorization requirement shall be screened before the 
foreign investment is completed. UNIFE welcomes such pro posals, although the scope of Annex I and of Annex II 
still remains too narrow and requires further extension (see next section).   
 
The nature of the mandatory screening mechanisms with minimum requirements are of particular importance for 
UNIFE for twofold reasons: firstly, it allows a better coverage of foreign direct investments across Member States and thus 
diminishes the risk of loopholes within the different EU jurisdictions and secondly, it introduces minimum requirements , 
such as the authorization requirement for foreign investments in key-areas.  
 
Better harmonization in terms of procedures through the cooperation mechanism: 
The Proposal establishes a cooperation mechanism to enable Member States and the Commission to exchange  
information on foreign investments  likely to negatively affect security or public order , assess their potential impact and 
identify potential concerns . It also introduces specific common procedures and requirements to be applicable against 
foreign investments.  
 
UNIFE wishes to underline its support  for provisions in light of achieving a better harmonization in terms of 
procedures, in particular regarding Chapter 3 of the Proposal which introduces a number of procedures for Member States 
to notify the Commission and other Member States through the cooperation mechanism of any foreign investment in a 
Union target established in their territory.  
 
It is particularly positive having Article 5(3) that makes possible for Member States to notify  a foreign investment in 
situations beyond the remits of Article 5 (e.g. when is part of or participates in projects or programmes of Union interest, 
is economically active in identified areas or the foreign investor or the foreign investor’s subsidiary in the Union is direc tly 
or indirectly controlled by the government) that could be of interest to the other Member States and the Commission from 
a security or public order perspective.  
 
Furthermore, Article 10 introduces harmonized requirements in terms of information that shall be provided in the 
notification. UNIFE welcomes such requirements, in particular when it comes to the need to provide specific 
information about the investor (name, global ultimate owner, ownership structure, description of the investment, among 
others) which contributes to a better assessment of the investor and supports a deeper screening exercise from Member 
States.

Further increasing of the scope of screening mechanisms 
EU programmes and projects (Annex I of the Proposal):   
UNIFE welcomes the Proposal’s improvements in terms of expanding the scope of EU programmes and projects 
(Annex I of the Proposal)  to be screened against foreign investment which is aligned with UNIFE’s position. Among 
others, the Proposal now includes Horizon 2020, Horizon Europe, Trans -European Networks for Transport (TEN -T), 
Connecting Europe Facility (CEF) and Important Projects of Common European Interest (IPCEI).  
 
However, the Proposal still lacks additional important EU programmes and projects and UNIFE calls for the 
inclusion of the European Structural and Investment Funds (ESIF) where, thanks to the Cohesion Fund, the European 
Regional and Development Fund and the Just Transition Fund, Member States also allocate EU funding to boost 
investments in rail infrastructure, including at the urban level, rolling stock, signalling system s and alternative fuels 
solutions.  
 
Moreover, UNIFE believes that the Proposal should also include off -the-Multiannual Financial Framework (MFF) , 
such as NextGenerationEU, in particular its Recovery and Resilience Facility (RRF) through which many of the investments 
in the rail sector are planned for the coming years and whose projects have been the target of unfair competition practices 
from non-EU bidders1, as well as the European Investment Bank where the rail supply industry also catches public financial 
support to increase investments.  
 
Sectors and items (Annex II of the Proposal):  
Compared to the current FDI Regulation, the Proposal introduces a novelty in terms of its Annex II by listing a number 
of items of particular importance for the security or public order interests of the Union and therefore to be 
screened against foreign investments . Moreover, Article 4(4) point (a) of the Proposal introduces an imposition of an 
authorization requirement for foreign investments related related to Annex I and Annex II. However, the items listed in 
Annex II are in most cases transversal in ter ms of their applicability in specific sectors and industries, which may create 
different legal interpretations regarding the obligation to be screened against foreign investments , or sometimes even too 
wide: in the category of “Net-Zero Technologies”, according to the Net -Zero Industry Act, only a few rail goods may be 
included in its scope, for instance rail electric traction technologies. 
 
UNIFE believes that Annex II falls short in addressing specific needs of the European Rail Supply Industry. In fact, 
Article 13 looks at foreign investments that are likely to negatively affect security or public order, in particular when it comes 
to the security, integrity and functioning of physical or virtual critical infrastructure  (Article 13(3) point (a)) and to the 
availability of critical technologies (Article 13(3) point (b)) of which infrastructure managers, railway undertakings and 
operators of services facilities are included as per Directive (EU) 2022/2557 and Directive (EU) 2022/2555.  
 
The inclusion of these Directives is a positive development since rail goods (e.g. rolling stock) and technologies (e.g. 
ERTMS) will be deployed by such entities  and rail supply companies may be also considered as “essential or important 
entities” (Article 3 of Directive (EU) 2022/2555). Nevertheless, such consideration depends on the definition of Member 
States and provisions will be only related to mainlines and not to the complete rail segments (mainlines, urban 
transport, metro lines, etc.). 
 
Given the fact that nor the aforementioned Directives nor the Annex II of the Proposal explicitly include rail goods and 
technologies, UNIFE calls on having Annex II including a dedicated category as follows: “rail-related sector and its 
technologies”2, in order to be screened against foreign investments since the rail sector is an important part of 
the critical infrastructure and technologies that should be screened against foreign investments on grounds of 
security and public order.  National accreditation bodies shall be also included in the scope of the Proposal ’s Annex II 
since foreign investors will have access to critical homologation and certification information, as well as information 
regarding the design of critical rail technologies.

Although the Commission is empowered to adopt delegated acts as per Article 19 in order to amend the list of projects and 
programmes of Union interest, as well as to amend the list of technologies, assets, economic activities, etc., UNIFE 
believes that Annex I and Annex II shall be enlarged since the entry into force of the Proposal.  This is particularly 
relevant for the Rail Supply Industry given the considerations related to the “resilience of infrastructures of particular 
importance for the security or p ublic order interests of the Union” and the “advancement of technologies of particular 
importance for security or public order of the Union” as per points (a) and (c) of Article 19(2). 
 
 
 
Ultimate origin of the investor and intra-EU investments 
Ultimate origin of the investor: 
UNIFE welcomes the Proposal’s article 5(1) point (b) which mandates Member States to notify the Commission 
and the other Member States through the cooperation mechanism of situations, among others, that the foreign investor 
or the foreign investor’s subsidiary in the Union in directly or indirectly controlled by the government , including 
state bodies, regional or local authorities or armed forces of a third country.  
 
UNIFE also welcomes Article 10(1) which lists the information to be provided in the notification, as follows: name 
of the investor, the global ultimate owner of the investor and the Union target, the ownership structure of the investor and, 
where applicable, of the corporate group to which the investor is part. 
 
The acquisition of European rail supply companies by foreign investors that are owned or controlled by a State can 
create two classes of competitors on the European market and put European companies at a disadvantage as they are 
bound by market and EU competition policy rules (especially on State Aid), while their foreign competitors are not.  
 
Moreover, foreign investors that are owned or controlled by the State of a third country may seek to acquire control of or 
influence in European undertakings and use these assets to the detriment not only of the EU’s technological edge but also 
its security and public order. This is particularly important for the rail sector as CRRC (China Railway Rolling Stock 
Corporation) is the only large rolling-stock manufacturer that is a state-owned and -controlled. 
 
Intra-EU investments:  
In strengthening national FDI screening mechanisms, it is important to ensure that intra-EU investments are not made 
more cumbersome. Many national FDI screening mechanisms also encompass FDI from within the EU. Such practices 
lead to additional administrative burden for European investors.  
 
However, UNIFE believes that Article 5(1) point (b) introduces a balanced position when it comes to mitigating the 
administrative burden and the risk of having EU foreign investors or its subsidiary in the Union that are directly 
or indirectly controlled by a government of a third country  which may lead to circumventing national FDI 
mechanisms. This can happen if a foreign investor establishes a subsidiary in a Member State in which a screening 
mechanism is not enforced, organizes its future investments through that subsidiary, or if the target company’s assets are 
transferred before the actual FDI is made to an entity in another Member State without a screening mechanism or with less 
stringent screening procedures.  
 
Furthermore, this is also aligned with the European Parliament INTA Committee exchange in January 2024, in which some 
Members of the Parliament were of the opinion that intra-European investments should be also scrutinized when the owner 
of the entity in question making the investment is located in a third country. 
 
 
 
Enforced role of the European Commission 
Although the decisions on investment policy remains first and foremost in the hands of Member States, the FDI legal 
framework should establish a platform to increase cooperation and collaboration between the European Commission and 
Member States on foreign  investments. Compared to the current Regulation, the Proposal establishes  positively an 
increased role of the European Commission in certain cases , especially in the framework of the cooperation

mechanism. For instance, according to Article s 8 and 11(4), Member States have to react to the Commission’s opinion 
prior to any decision of their screening exercise on whether or not accepting foreign investments.  
 
Nonetheless, UNIFE believes that article 8(8) creates a certain level of loophole by mentioning exceptional 
circumstances to allow the Member State concerned in situations that that its security or public order requires issuing a 
screening decision before the deadlines established by the Proposal.  Moreover, Article 7(5) of the Proposal relates to 
situations where Member States submit comments and the Commission issues opinion on notified foreign investments, 
and the Member State concerned shall give utmost consideration to such comment or opinion. As previously stated, 
although the ultimate responsibility of investment policy remains in the hands of Member States, UNIFE considers that 
the wording “utmost consideration to such comment or opinion” remains to o vague and may lead to discarding 
such comment or opinion from Member States and the Commission. 
 
Overall, UNIFE is of the opinion  that the role of the European Commission must be further strengthened, in 
particular with regard to “critical infrastructures” and “critical technologies” related to rail . The Commission, as 
well as the Member States, must have a leading role in preparing their opinions/assessments prior to any decision on 
whether or not accepting foreign investments that pose a risk to security or public order. 
 
Last but not least, given the particular geopolitical context in which the European Rail Supply Industry operates, UNIFE 
believes that the Proposal should be aligned with the other legal instruments available to the European 
Commission, such as the Foreign Subsidies Regulation. We consider it essential that there is reciprocity check to assess 
investment openness with the country from which the investment is originated in the EU.  
 
As a matter of example to consubstantiate such need, in March 2024, the European Commission opened its first in -
depth investigation in the context of the Foreign Subsidies Regulation against CRRC  (Qingdao Sifang Locomotive 
Co., Ltd., a subsidiary of CRRC Corporation, a Chinese state -owned train manufacturer, worldwide leader) in the context 
of a public procurement procedure in Bulgaria for the provision of several electric push -pull trains, maintenance and staff 
training services worthing €610 million contr act as CRRC bid with a price difference of about 50% compared to the 
European competitor. Based on the information publicly available, the Commission refers in the notice 3 released early 
March 2024 that there are sufficient indications that CRRC has received foreign subsidies that distort the internal 
market, quantifying foreign financial contributions of approximately EUR 1.745 billion.  
 
Against this background, UNIFE strongly believes that the Proposal's requirements must have a holistic approach 
and complement other legal instruments available to the European Union , in order to maintain the Union as a 
destination par excellence for foreign investment but that this occurs in markets outside the European Union for European 
economic operators and not in their disadvantage, creating even more strategic economic dependencies.

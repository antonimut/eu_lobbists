BusinessEurope generally welcomes the European Commission’s proposal for a 
Regulation on the screening of foreign investments in the Union, published on 24 
January 2024.  
 
Our guiding principle is that the EU needs to remain an open and attractive destination 
for trade and investments, while risks related to security and public order stemming from 
certain types of investments need to be addressed effectively, in a targeted a nd 
proportionate manner, and in full respect of Member States’ exclusive competence in 
matters related to national security. It is our view that the Commission’s proposal overall 
respects this balance. Nevertheless, it remains vital that trade and investment must not 
be restricted by additional protectionist measures and disproportionate barriers. 
 
We particularly welcome the following elements: 
 
• The legal basis for the screening of foreign investments at national level remains 
security and public order. This is critical to ensure that the national instruments 
are targeted and well-suited for their set objective. 
 
• It becomes mandatory for all Member States to establish a screening mechanism. 
This is expected to contribute to mitigating loopholes in the current system from 
a security and public order perspective, while also addressing fragmentation in 
the Single Market, ensuring a level-playing field for investments in the EU.  
 
• In this respect, we also agree with the Commission’s proposal to set a minimum 
scope for the screening of certain types of investments into the Union in 
accordance with Art. 4.4, provided that these present a risk and are clearly and 
narrowly defined . and a minimum set of requirements to ensure a smooth, 
effective and – to the extent possible – coordinated implementation of screening 
mechanisms in accordance with Art. 4.1, 4.2 and 4.3.

that they have adequate means and resources available to comply with these 
requirements. Does the Commission envisage the publication of guidance to the

We would like more clarity on the following aspects:     
 
• We see the value in the proposals to reinforce the cooperation mechanism and 
increase the accountability of investment screening, for instance by making sure 
that the feedback process in the form of Comments issued by Member States or 
Opinions issued by th e Commission is more formalised. However, we are 
concerned that the system of mandatory notifications for certain types of 
investments, including intra-EU investments, as per the conditions set in Art. 5.1 
and in particular Art. 5.1 (b), has the potential to increase the number of cases 
treated, and contributing to delays and uncertainty. The review of the Regulation 
should aim to reduce national notification cases that are non -essential, reduce 
the number of unnecessary reviews by the European Commission and other 
Member States under the cooperation mechanism, and limit increased reporting 
for EU companies and foreign investors, while protecting confidential information. 
 
We would like to understand better the definition of “control” that will be used in 
this context. We understand that guidance provided to help in the implementation 
of sanctions can provide useful insights. However, given that investment 
screening is a dif ferent policy field, would additional guidance be provided to 
screening authorities?  
 
• The proposal gives the possibility to Member States or the Commission to launch 
own initiative procedures in cases where there are considerations that a foreign 
investment that is not notified through the cooperation mechanism is likely to 
negatively affect  the security or public order of other Member States.  The 
cooperation mechanism would cover a broader range of transactions than those 
subject to the notification obligation or that have already been through a review 
process at national level. Such a prov ision would create added uncertainty for 
investors up to at least 15 months after a deal has been concluded and should 
only be used with caution. This can trigger sensitive considerations, while there 
is also a risk for politicisation of cases. How own initiative procedures will work in 
practice? What does it entail beyond the stage of the issuing of comments by 
other Member States and / or a Commission Opinion? 
 
• Regarding the factors/criteria that should be taken into consideration when 
determining the likelihood of an investment posing security or public risks, the 
Regulation proposes the availability of "critical technologies". Facing the dynamic 
geopolitical en vironment, any Regulation aimed at those technologies and 
applications that are classified as "critical" must be continuously adapted to

• Regarding the scope of screening, mandatory screening and notifications of 
investments that target areas identified especially in Annex II of the Regulation 
may be considered too broad, leading to potential overloading of the system and 
increased costs, especially for start-ups and SMEs. This is important as Annex II 
consists of rapidly developing sectors where the duration of screening might have 
negative impacts on financing flows and competitiveness. We would welcome a 
more dedicated discussion on addres sing adverse potential impacts of this 
provision. 
 
• The extended scope of the draft Regulation, to include indirect foreign 
investments through EU subsidiaries, including those legitimately established in 
the EU, will result in widespread screening of intra-EU deals in strategic sectors. 
The criteria of est ablishing a long-lasting link between the foreign investor and 
the EU company could cover a great number of situations, leading to an increase 
of precautionary filings made by EU companies that have a significant foreign 
shareholder base. Whilst it is expe cted to help reduce gaps in national security 
and public order risks in the EU, national screening regimes must respect the free 
movement of capital and the EU fundamental freedom of establishment to avoid 
disguised protectionist measures within the single market.  
 
Access to cross-border capital is suboptimal within the EU despite its role as an 
indispensable driver of growth and competitiveness, none the least to set -up 
innovative ventures in critical technologies and enable them to reach a sufficient 
scale to compete on international markets.  
 
The notions of control or effective participation in the management by individuals 
or entities from a non -EU country are not clearly defined, including in the 
definition of “foreign investment”. Having only a limited view of the scale of the 
additional scr eening notifications such a scope extension would entail, it is 
strongly advised to conduct an impact assessment on intra -EU capital flows 
before adopting the revised Regulation. Additionally, it is not clear how the new 
Regulation would interact with the exclusive competence of the European 
Commission to assess mergers of a Union dimension under the EU Merger 
Regulation. 
 
• The requirement for investors to make filings in multi -jurisdictional deals on the 
same day could be burdensome in practice. Member States have divergent filing 
deadlines. The pre -notification phase is often an important informal step in the 
screening proc ess to allow the parties to the transaction and the relevant 
authorities to assess the need for a filing and whether all the information 
requirements are satisfied to launch the screening mechanism. How would the

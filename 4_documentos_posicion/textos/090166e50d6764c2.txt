Position of Rheinmetall on EDIP 
The European Defence Industry Programme (EDIP) as proposed by the EU Commission will 
be the first step and main instrument for the implementation of the European Defence Industrial 
Strategy (EDIS). EDIP is composed of three parts: (1) the “programme” following the EDIRPA 
and ASAP logic with a budget of EUR1,5 billion, (2) the Ukraine Instrument, including ASAP , 
EDIRPA and further activities inside or for Ukraine, and (3) a  European Security of Supply 
regime (SoS).  
As one of the major defence companies in Europe, Rheinmetall and its entities in various EU 
Member States – in the light of our successful single market – have an inherent interest in the 
implementation of EDIP .  
 
(1) The “programme” 
Building on a good experience with ASAP , Rheinmetall fully supports the “programme” and its 
measures to enhance the capacity and resilience of the European Defence In dustrial Base 
(EDTIB). However, facing existential security threats arising from Russia and the growing 
tensions between the China and the US, a more substantial budget will be inevitable in order 
to create a European defence industrial readiness. Since the current security crisis is as critical 
to our European welfare as the Covid -19 pandemic , we strongly advise to explore the 
possibility of reallocating unused budget from the R ecovery and Resilience Facility ( RRF). A 
more substantial budget is essential to ensure the efficacy of European measures, which are 
complementing rather than displa cing Member States efforts to enhance their security and 
resilience. For achieving this objective, the ASAP logic plays a significant role. The EDTIB must 
be capable of supplying its Member States sufficiently. After decades of poor investments and 
acute non-European off the shelf procurement, production capacities must be increased in a 
timely and consistent manner. In this regard, Rheinmetall also supports the voluntary 
establishment and maintenance of “ever-warm facilities” which are indispensable for upholding 
defence readiness. For enhancing common procurement activities, EDIRPA has great 
potential. In order to facilitate the process for Member States cross-certification of defence 
products leading to mutual recognition of certification  should be a precondition rather than a 
supportive action. Reducing unnecessary bureaucratic obstacles is the first  step to establish 
European interoperability, interchangeability and military standardisation. The possibility of 
forming a SEAP is a political decision, yet, we are open to contracts . We also welcome the 
opportunity of also widening the ASAP logic to joint and cross-border actions.

(2) The Ukraine Instrument 
The unprovoked Russian war of aggression against Ukraine requires the united support of the 
EU and its allies for Ukraine as the first line defender of our liberal and democratic values and 
norms. Military supply as well as the support of the Ukrainian DTIB are existential for Ukraine 
as a sovereign and democratic nation state. Direct defence-industrial cooperation strengthens 
Ukraine's defence capacities in the short term and provides another strong anchor for Ukraine's 
further rapprochement with the EU in the long term. Therefore, Rheinmetall sees a strong basis 
for furth er cooperation  within the consideration of Ukraine as a quasi -Member State  and 
welcomes all activities related to the Ukraine Instrument. Given the extraordinary situation, 
actions should be retroactively eligible since the start of the full-scale invasion of Ukraine on 
the 24 February 2022. However, we dissuade from opening the eligibility for the Ukraine 
Instrument to third -country entities  since this would undermine the overall objective of 
strengthening the resilience of the European and Ukrainian DTIB.  
(3) Security of Supply 
Given the current security situation, the wish for a Security of Supply regime is comprehensible 
yet incompatible with the free European single market enshrined in the treaties. Rheinmetall 
rejects any activity related to enforced information gathering  and priority -rated orders. 
Moreover, especially in a crisis the disclosure of any additional information on Rheinmetall ’s 
supply chains increases our vulnerability to  an extent that is not compatible with neither 
national security nor European resilience.  As the situation requires, t here should be agreed 
measures on a European level in a supply crisis. Nevertheless, the proposed definition is too 
vague. Here more clari fication will be needed. As part of these measures, we welcome the 
idea of advance purchasing agreements . The decision to request the commission to act as 
procurement agen t is a political decision. However, the insufficient budget will reduce the 
practicability of the pre -payment mechanism . The call on Member S tates to ease the 
bureaucratic efforts through implementing national fast-tracking of permit granting procedures 
will prove valuable in and before a supply crisis.  
Regarding the governance, we acknowledge the necessity of an advisory board. Yet we are 
concerned about setting up confusing and inefficient double structures . We have similar 
concerns regarding  the structured dialogue with industry . Here we recommend clear 
communication and avoidance of ad -hoc consultations to not interfere with the high-level 
representative’s internal duties.

European Defence Industry programme (EDIP) 
 
  
Business & Science Poland (BSP) would like to express its appreciation for the European Commission’s 
initiative on the support of competitiveness, responsiveness and innovation capacity of the EU defense 
industry, as is intended by the EDIP regulation – the subject of these public consultations.  
 
BSP highlights in the opinion areas of the EDIP regulation deemed to require further consideration and 
possible amendments. The issues described below are categorized into two aspects  of the EDIP 
regulation – the financial and the substantive.  
 
The financial aspect 
 
• Budget and targeting of EDTIB support  
BSP welcomes efforts aimed at increasing competitiveness and production capacity of the European 
Defense Technological and Industrial Base (EDTIB), especially regarding a new geopolitical situation, 
war in Ukraine, and targeted attacks on Polish cyber infrastructure.  
 
Notwithstanding, in BSP’s opinion the size of the EDIP budget (1.5 billion euros to be spent between 
2025-2027) is insufficient, given the needs of the European defense industry. It would therefore  be 
recommended that additional funds from the EU budget should be allocated to this goal. Furthermore, 
a proportionately larger part of the funds  available through the EDIP should be earmarked for 
Member States, which are closest to the Russian Federation. Poland has already provided Ukraine 
with support worth over 20 billion EUR, accounting for 3.1% of its GDP, a substantial proportion of 
which was military support. In this regard , the  defense industry potential of Poland shou ld be 
preferentially supported through the EDIP. 
 
• EDIP support for the production of military capabilities  
The EDIP regulation should continue to emphasize  the importance of supply support for the 
production of 155mm munitions. It should in our opinion include separate regulations in this area 
within the EU's A ct in Support of Ammunition Production (ASAP) and consider the creation of 
production capacity from scratch, as well as financing through a simplified procedure. 
Another issue is the level of EDIP support for eligible costs when investing in defense capabilities. BSP 
deems the level of 35% coverage of these costs, as indicated in the draft regulation, too moderate, 
especially considering the capital-intensive processes in the production of capabilities such as artillery 
ammunition or missiles for air defense systems. BSP considers a level of 50% of eligible costs coverage 
to be optimal, or even 80% in the case of production of the latter, critical capabilities. 
Regarding the financial coverage for capabilities production , it  should address countries with less 
developed arms industry base, so as not to create monopolies in countries with an already developed 
industrial base. This could be resolved through the introduction of geographically-targeted coverage.

The substantive aspect  
 
• European Defense Project of Common Interest 
An important aspect of the European Defense Projects of Common Interest are the operational 
requirements to qualify for their implementation.  
There are many concerns about the complicated nature of bureaucracy  associated with the 
application procedure, coming from stakeholders applying for hydrogen projects. They also report the 
burdensome necessity of dedicating abundant resources in order to be engaged in these undertakings 
in the first place. This could be addressed in an explicit statement by the European Commission that it 
shall make every effort to make the application procedure as accessible as possible to the potential 
applicants. This, in turn, could be achieved through the creation of a specialized and accessible 
organizational framework. 
Furthermore, the European Defense Projects of Common Interest should be carried out in coherence 
and relation to NATO and its infrastructure , otherwise this may result in loss of untapped synergies, 
both in the financial and military aspects. 
• Fund to Accelerate Defense Supply Chains Transformation (FAST) 
BSP highlights the problematic vagueness of the regulations regarding the possibility of establishing 
a Fund to Accelerate Defense Supply Chains Transformation. The regulation should specify concrete 
solutions pertaining to the structure of the Fund and its management principle s, which it currently 
does not specify. 
BSP supports the creation of  the Fund. We think that it  is imperative that such a Fund  is established 
and not introduced as an optional instrument within the EDIP regulation. 
• Structure for European Armament Programme (SEAP) 
The BSP constates that given SEAP is a domain of the member states and their respective government 
bodies, the decisional power within the member states should be vested in entities responsible for the 
member states’ armament agencies.  
• The Defense Industry Readiness Board 
BSP considers the dialogue with the defense industry coordinated through the Defense Industry 
Readiness Board, to be a valuable element of the EDIP initiative.  
We also positively assess the announcement of regular production capacity monitoring, to be carried 
out by the European Commission and the Defense Industry Readiness Board . Importantly, the 
protection of classified information in the whole process must be carefully considered and addressed, 
so as to mitigate the possibility of unwarranted access or leaks.

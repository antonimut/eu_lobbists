ChargeUp Europe’s input to the Call for Evidence on MID targeted technical amendment.   
 
I. Introduction 
ChargeUp Europe welcomes the targeted  technical amendment of the MID to update and expand its 
scope and the annexes setting technical requirements of measuring instruments to the EV charging 
infrastructure.  
As recognised by the European Commission  in the call for evidence , the current MID is  unfit to keep up 
with the technological progress that occurred in the last decades as it covers mainly smart metering and 
i) it does not explicitly allow DC metering ii) does not explicitly allow use of remote displays. When MID 
was passed in the year 2014 , there were only very few public charging stations erected in Europe. The 
situation is overwhelmingly different today, with 7.6 million charging points in Europe  in 2023  and 
accelerated deployment expected to achieve AFIR and transport decarbonisation targets.  
 
However, since the adoption of MID in 2014, Member States have adopted national metrology laws and 
measures applying to the meters inside the EV chargers  which result  in legal uncertainty and 
fragmentation in the EU Single Market. These technical requirements differ from each other and from the 
MID. The EV charging industry is already today  facing increased costs due to being  exposed to non-
harmonised requirements across Member States  and the consequent necessity to adapt products to 
different national requirements. Fragmentation of the single market due to non -harmonised metrology 
rules translate into increased costs for the consumer and varying levels of protection of the consumer 
across the EU. 
ChargeUp Europe thus welcomes the opportunity to provide evidence of the technical issues and of the 
fragmentation due to diverging national legislation and to provide key recommendations on the adoption 
of the targeted amendments. 
 
II. Evidence of fragmentation and technical issues 
 
• Spain’s draft amendments to order ICT/155/2020 of 7 February 2020, regulating the metrological 
control of the state of certain measuring instruments, recently subject to the notification 
procedure under Directive (EU) 2015/1535 1: With these proposed changes, Spain intents to

change the rules for electrical energy measuring instruments and systems when used for electric 
vehicle charging stations.  
 
The proposed rules are significantly different from those of the MID . The MID has been used for 
many years for electric vehicle charging applications, without any problem.  Concerning the 
technical content, the Spanish rules diverge extensively from the MID. The divergence favours 
unfair competition and reduces the consumer protection. For instance, for the electric current, 
the minimum operating range which is proposed is s ignificantly reduced with respect to MID. As 
a result, during normal use of charging stations, the current will be outside the rated operating 
range for extended periods.  
 
This divergence is of very high relevance to consumers and consumer protection. In the Spanish 
proposal, Annex XX item 2.5 (Table 2) defines MPEs without any relevance to consumer protection 
and the protection of the fairness of trade. Those lead to a situation where, in violation of Article 
7(1) MID, fully MID compliant active electrical energy meters are ba nned from the Spanish 
market. Furthermore, Table 4 defines MPEs for active electrical energy meters for DC applications 
only for part of their operatin g range. This allows manufacturers to misleadingly specify large 
operating ranges for the current, while the consumer protection is limited to 25 % Imax to Imax, 
regardless of the manufacturer's specifications. It is worth noting that, already today, charg ing 
stations often operate below 25 % Imax. This weakens not only consumer protection, but it also 
encourages unfair competition between manufacturers. Various other provisions of Annex XX 
violate the provisions and concepts of MID extensively. 
 
• Mess- and Eichrecht in Germany: With no doubt, Germany has the strictest rules from all EU 
member states with regards to putting EV charging stations onto the market. Just to name one 
example (out of many), replacing a broken charging cable is possible without problem s in every 
EU member state, but not in Germany. In Germany, this repair needs to be followed by an official 
federal calibration (“Eichung”). This is a procedure which typically takes about one day and costs 
several hundred Euros. Even worse, as long as this “Eichung” has not happened, the charging 
station must not be used in the meanwhile and has to be put out of operation. The waiting time 
to get an appointment for the “Eichung” is at minimum several weeks, typically 1-2 months. 
 
• LNE in France: France has recently put into effect another national deviation from MID, namely 
LNE compliance. It is not exactly clear what this encompasses, as documentation (in any other 
language than French) is hardly available – but an LNE approval seems to be necessary for DC 
meters and DC charging stations to be allowed to put such a product onto the French market. 
 
III. Evidence of economic impact 
In the view of ChargeUp Europe, all of the examples mentioned in the previous subsection somehow 
violate EU legislation and impede the free movement of goods on the Union market. The cost of Member 
State specific product modifications will increase the cost of the Green Deal, if this fragmentation cannot 
be avoided. In the end, the manufacturers will add those additional costs to the price tag of every EV 
charging station, meaning that eventually this will have to be paid for by the consumers. Specific regional 
rules sets will also lead to legal uncertainty, since any product complying with MID does not necessarily 
comply e.g. with the rules in Germany All of this will impede the roll -out of charging infrastructure on 
which the Green Deal depends and for which Regulation (EU) 2023/1804 was issued last year. 
 
IV. Recommendations 
 
 
• Without harm to the subsidiarity principle, the MID as amended by the targeted technical 
amendment should replace national regulations currently deviating from MID, for EVSE, as this is 
necessary effect to end  the Single Market fragmentation  due to non -harmonised technical 
requirements. 
• Perform an impact assessment to exclude any risk of negative impact on the industry due to the 
new technical requirements for EVSE electricity meters that would be introduced by the targeted 
technical amendment. In fact, the proposed amendment would expand the scope of the MID and 
introduce new technical requirements going beyond a mere targeted technical amendment. The 
industry will need to perform a proper risk analysis to confirm the industry can comply with the 
new requirements.   
• Ensure sta keholders’ involvement also after the adoption and before the transposition and 
application of the amended MID. 
• Application of the MID rules  as amended by the targeted technical amendment shall not be 
retroactive and shall not apply for charging infrastructure built before the application date. 
• We recommend the application date to be set after a 36-months period following the entry into 
force of the Directive.  
• Considering that the targeted technical amendment would introduce new specific technical 
requirements, the Directive should provide for a 10-years transitional period as was also specified 
in Article 23 of DIRECTIVE 2004/22/EC (MID). 
• The targeted technical amendment shall include a clarification of what class of EVSE the amended 
MID will be applicable to, making appropriate distinctions between AC and DC chargers, and the 
location of the installation (residential or commercial).

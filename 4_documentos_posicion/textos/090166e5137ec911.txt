 
 
 
Hager group statement to call for evidence on MID targeted amendment 
 
Hager group welcomes a targeted revision of the measuring instrument directive to adapt them to current 
markets and technologic needs and consequently to avoid national regulations which acts against a well 
functioning single market. 
Considering electricity energy metering we welcome the explicit definition of direct current meters as part 
of the directive and the definition of essential requirements for E-Mobility charging. 
As the MID revision shall help the energy transition by empowering consumers to make full use of 
decentralized production from small-scale renewables and of new energy services/schemes such as 
demand response and energy communities, an overlap of requirements should be avoided. This is 
particularly true for EVSE used in private location and seen as a major flexibility potential and according 
EU Network Code Demand Response to be measured with a dedicated measurement device. For this 
application neither the EVSE nor the dedicated measurement device should be in the scope of the new 
created annex Va but remain in annex V. To avoid any confusion we propose to limit the scope of annex 
Va to EVSE used in the context of the AFIR. 
 
In details: 
 
New annex to cover measuring systems for electric vehicle supply equipment (EVSE) 
We welcome the addition of a new annex to cover the new use cases of charging an electrical vehicle. The scope of 
this new annex should nevertheless be as clear as possible as: 
- there will be EVSE without any metering (charging@home behind the utility meter) 
- there will be EVSE using annex V meters for informative or energy management applications 
- there will be EVSE using annex V meters as a Dedicated Measurement Device as define in the EU Network 
Codes Demand Response to be able to provide flexibilities 
- … 
To avoid confusion with the above listed application we propose to limit the scope to EVSE used in the context of the 
charging infrastructure according to Alternative Fuels Infrastructure Regulation (AFIR, Regulation (EU) 2023/1804) 
Transposition and entry into force 
We are in favor of a transposition time for the member states of 12 month after publication in the OJEU and an entry 
into force 24 month after publication considering that the modification are limited and the market is moving rapidly. 
Nevertheless we are also asking for a transition period for national type approval certificates published before the 
entry into force until their expiry with a maximum of 10 years. 
Annex I: amendment of the definition of Direct Sales 
If the deletion of “the place of transaction” is done to reflect the technical evolution that the second party involved in 
the transaction is not at the place of the transaction e.g. CPO backend validating the direct payment it may make 
sense. 
Nevertheless is the modification is made to enable that the measurement is interruptible or the display enabled to 
be resetted to zero or to include the requirement of presentation of the measurement result to both parties in the 
transaction, it may not be the best approach. 
 
It should be considered that in the EVSE use case there will be direct sales and non direct sales using the same 
EVSE and the same measuring system. See also our comment on annex I point 10.4. 
Annex I: amendment of the definition of Utility 
We support the fact to consider that the index of meters used for other use case than Utility metering may be re-
settable. Nevertheless hybrid meters with one non-resettable index (e.g. total index) and one resettable index should 
be considered and allowed to be used as Utility meters. 
Annex I: adjustment of point 8.4. on measurement data and software protection 
To be in line with the aim of this revision and remained focused the proposed modification to ensure availability and 
authenticity should be integrated into annex Va. 
Annex I: adjustment of point 10.2. on the indication of results 
The inclusion of protection against accidental deletion should be limited to meters with a resettable index. 
Annex I: adjustment of point 10.4. on the presentation of results for direct sales trading transactions 
The adjustment of this point is directly linked with the modification of the definition of direct sales. It should be 
considered that for one and the same EVSE we may have the possibility to pay directly after the transaction and in 
this case to be considered as “direct sales” or to be charged by a service provider for example at the end of the 
month (e.g. toll collection in France may be made directly at the tollgate or by an electronic toll collection billed once 
a month). When using a service provider the data is presented on the bill but not immediately.

We welcome the possibility to adapt the way of displaying to the technical evolution. The more flexible rules should

1- The meter has no display at all: in this case the remote display shall be metrologically controlled 
2- The meter has a display (which can be read out in case of doubt) and this display is reported to a remote

Annex V: clarification of the requirements and the definitions to ensure that it is clear that both AC/DC, and 
bidirectional are included in the scope of the Annex 
We welcome the clarification or the explicit inclusion of DC energy meters in annex V 
Annex V: in Table 2 extension of operating temperature to all, below -25 and above +55 Celsius 
Our interpretation of the foreseen modification is that you want to limit the table to two columns one with “-25 °C to 
+55°C” and the second on with “-40 °C to +70 °C” 
Annex V: deletion of points 5.1, 5.2 and 5.3 as they are redundant requirements within the Annex or with 
other annexes 
We are thinking that this points shouldn’t be deleted as we didn’t find any redundancy 
Annex V: adjustment of point 4.3 on short duration disturbances: rewording of the title and correction of the 
critical change calculation for future-proofing 
This point is too generic so that we are missing details to be able to take position 
Annex Va: definition of EVSE Measuring systems 
In regard to the “specific transfer point” the proposed definition is confusing. We proposed to fine tune the definition 
considering the two existing possibilities:  
1- EVSE with a fixed socket outlet: in this case the transfer point is the socket outlet 
2- EVSE with an attached cable: in this case the transfer point is the plug at the end of the attached cable 
Annex Va: other definitions such as I, Ist, Imin, Itr, Imax, U, Un, f, fn, PF, ripple, harmonic, d, MMQ, transfer 
point 
There is, from a metrological point of view no need to change even adapt the already defined parameters. 
A modification may lead to a confusion in particular when the metrology for the measuring system is provided by a 
separately approved meter according annex V. 
The proposed reduced range between Ist and Imax may have been made on the assumption that the EV lowest  
charging current is 6 A as specified in the EVSE standard. Having analyzed a wide range of EV charging behavior 
the charging current is even often going down to 1 A which means for an 32 A Imax  a factor 32. 
In addition the use case of bidirectional charging will tend to have a bigger range than the proposed factor 10. 
It should also be considered that in some member states EVSE have to provide a possibility to charge electrically 
powered transport devices for use on public roads or in public spaces which de facto have a low charging current 
which would need a bigger factor between Ist and Imax.  
 
In conclusion our statement is that the parameters already defined and use in annex V should remain unchanged  
Annex Va: regarding operating requirements, alternatives are presented for measuring systems to 
compensate for energy loss from the cable and connector mounted between the energy measuring point 
and the transfer point: 
Considering the losses in the cable above a specific EVSE power makes sense. We propose to consider cable 
losses above 50 kW charging power as follow: 
1- Using a 4-wire measurement at the transfer point 
2- Compensating the power losses of the cables in the measurement system 
For the second case: 
- If the cables are not replaced they are sealed by a metrological seal during the calibration of the measuring 
system 
- If the cable are replaceable, the cable characteristics are clearly identified in the type approval and  the type 
is defined in the manufacturer documentation or labelled on the measuring system. In this case a installer 
seal is sufficient 
Annex Va: when exposed to disturbances, the measuring system shall 
This point is not clear enough to be able to take position but the essential requirements should be coherent with 
annex V 
Annex Va: regarding putting into use 
As the use case considered in annex Va is EV charging, there is no reason to differentiate according to the location 
of the EVSE between the residential and commercial. We propose to set the requirement to class A without 
considering the location as it is state of the art in the national requirements in Germany. 
Annex Va: the manufacturer can choose between B+F, B+D or H1 procedures to perform the conformity 
assessment 
We welcome the proposed modules for the conformity assessment which is in line with Annex V

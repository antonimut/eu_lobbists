TARGETED REVISION OF SINGLE CMO REGULATION 
 
CIBE has taken note of the Commission Proposal for a REGULATION OF THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT 
AND OF THE COUNCIL amending Regulations (EU) No 1308/2013  (CMO Regulation), (EU) 2021/2115 
and (EU) 2021/2116 as regards the strengthening of the position of farmers in the food supply chain. 
- This proposal follows the announcement in spring 2024 by the EU Commission to propose a set of 
measures intended to enhance the position of farmers in the food supply chain. 
 
- CIBE supports the general objectives to improve farmers’ position along the supply chain , notably 
through the contractual relationships between farmers and their buyers. 
 
- CIBE highlights that the sugar beet contractual framework remains of crucial importance and CIBE 
supports to include  the general provisions proposed by the Commission  in the sugar beet 
framework and to improve it . Excluding European sugar beet growers from such improvements 
would not be appropriate and would contradict the objectives of these proposals. 
 
- CIBE therefore welcomes the specific amendments to the Annex X of CMO Regulation. CIBE notes 
that these amendments would add some points in th is Annex which could facilitate and clarify 
contractualisation terms  between growers and processors , without changing the nature of the 
balance between parties. 
Taking into consideration the organisation of the sugar beet processing season, the revision clause 
proposed should be adapted accordingly (the 6 months suggested might not be appropriate). 
 
- CIBE would like to take the opportunity of a revision of the CMO Regulation to propose a revision of 
the sugar reference threshold (Article 7 (c))  so that this reference remains consistent with  the 
objectives of this targeted revision.  Indeed, the white sugar reference threshold does not reflect 
anymore the costs of production of beet and should be adjusted  accordingly, based on EU 27 
inflation. 
The value currently in force is the 2009 nominal value. Having a nominal value as a reference in a 
regulatory basic text essentially implies the impoverishment of the sector: value is thus lost every 
year because of inflation. If we correct this value with EU 27 inflation, since the introduction of this 
reference, the reference threshold for white sugar of €404.40/t corresponds to a reference, in 2025 
terms, of almost €585/t (ex-factory).  
Applying the rule of correspondence, this updated reference matches with the current average beet 
production cost in the EU which has significantly and structurally increased these past years  (i.e. 
around €38/t).

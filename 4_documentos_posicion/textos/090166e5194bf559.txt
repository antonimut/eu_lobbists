Enel responses to European Commission “Have your say” on the 
Proposal for a Directive amending Directive 2014/32/EU as regards 
electric vehicle supply equipment, compressed gas dispensers, 
and electricity, gas and thermal energy meters 
 
The Enel Group welcomes the proposal to amend the current Measuring Instrument Directive-
MID to cover the evolving needs of the energy sector in line with the objectives of the European 
Green Deal. Our company would like to share  some improvements to the current text  to 
increase regulatory certainty and avoid possible discrepancies in the interpretation of the text 
between Member States.  
For smart meters: 
• Article 2 of the proposed MID revision should be  amended to ensure that measuring 
instruments that comply with Directive 2014/32/EU will continue to be managed in the 
same way after the entry into of force of the new Directive without any time limit. For 
this reason, we proposed to delete some part of the Article, as indicated below:  
 
“By way of derogation…covering measuring instruments that fall in the scope of…and that have 
been placed on the [ADD market before the date of entry into force of this Directive ] [DELETE 
before [OP please insert the date = 24 months after the date of entry into force of this Directive], 
shall remain valid until the expiry of their validity…” 
 
• In Annex I, points 10.6, the following sentence must be removed:  
“The data may be made available, in addition, by means of metrologically controlled remote 
channel.” 
since it is considered not sufficiently detailed and structured, it may cause interpretative 
difficulties that are hard to resolve. Additionally, due to its specificity, it is included in a 
document expressly concerning measuring instruments, for which metrolog ical 
validation rules are already present. 
• In Annex III (1), the proposal to add “transferred between circuits” to the definition of 
electrical energy meter is unclear. The final amendment should better clarify the 
meaning and whether the new definition extends the scope of the MID. 
 
“An active electrical energy meter is an instrument which measures the active electrical energy 
consumed in a circuit or [CLARIFY transferred between circuits]” 
 
• It might be relevant for the MID revision to consider the provisions on smart meters of 
the IR (EU) 2023/1162 on interoperability and access to metering data  and the 
Data Act (EU) 2023/2854, together with the secondary laws and the guidance 
stemming from such acts. If possible, it would be relevant also to consider the 
provisions on data sharing from the upcoming implementing regulations on 
interoperability and data ac cess to promote demand response, customer

According to the IR (EU) 2023/1162 (from now on IR), smart meters placed in the 
EU market or put into service after July 2019, shall follow the minimum 
requirements for providing access to historical and near -real time data (the so-
called reference model, applicable since January 5th 2025), consisting of: 
• a process model, that describes the procedures (e.g., how to authenticate users),  
• a role model, with a set of roles/responsibilities and their interactions. For MID it 
is especially relevant to consider that: 
o metered data administrators, will store and make validated data available 
online and show complementary data, and shall keep updated a data 
access log 
o metering point administrator, provides and administrates the metering point 
characteristics, and parties registered to it 
o meter operator,  will install, maintain, test, certify and decommission 
physical meters.  They will offer access to near real-time data and send the 
information necessary to use and/or decrypt meter data. 
o Data access provider, responsible for facilitating access, including in 
cooperation with other parties, to validated historical metering and 
consumption data by the final customer or eligible parties 
• an information model that contains information objects, their attributes, and the 
relationships between these objects  
In addition, the IR states smart metering data should be available by means of European 
standards, where possible (recitals 8 and 9) 
The Commission proposal f or a Data Act  (EU) 2023/2854 , provides certain 
obligations for connected products and related services , to provide access for 
consumers to generated data. The provisions on data access are covered by the 
previous IR, however some provisions non-existent in the IR impact smart meters:  
• Gatekeepers (e.g. Apple) shall not be eligible third parties, receiving SM data  
(Article 5).  
• Any compensation for making data available shall be non -discriminatory, 
reasonable, and may include a margin.  
• Infringements for not sharing the data will be subject to penalties laid down by 
Member States under the Data Act.

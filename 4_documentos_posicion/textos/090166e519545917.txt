Feedback on the Targeted Technical Amendment adopted proposal – Measuring 
Instruments Directive 
Tesla | Input following adoption of the proposal by the Commission (Have your say) – March 2025 
 
• Ensuring correct national transposition 
- Amended MID should make national legislation redundant. Currently, the absence of harmonised 
requirements for metering solutions for charging points leads to diverging national legislations. It 
shall be defined what equivalency of MID still will be granted, and under what conditions MID 
amendment shall make national- or industry driven measurement methodologies redundant and 
obsolete and therefore be removed and not allowed as national deviation (e.g. MessEG/MessEV 
in Germany).   
- Amended MID should explicitly exclude any national deviations, in particular the retroactive MID 
compliance requirements in national legislation  (some of which enforce it today)  for 
existing/legacy recharging infrastructure built before 1 January 2028.  
 
• Transitional Period (Article 3) 
The transitional period should be extended from 24 to 36 months. R egulatory and design complexity 
(product development and certification timelines)  must be considered, in addition to the fact that the 
regulatory gap before the adoption of the final text and transposition leaves industry in an uncertain 
position.  
 
• Remote Displays (Annex I, Point 10.6) 
- Allowing the use of remote displays, e.g. a smartphone , triggers consumer preference, aligned 
with the charging behavior today and given increased future interactions with consumers on 
mobile devices and other web user interfaces in -vehicle or mobile devices. Those solutions also 
ensure new functions like Plug & Charge enabling automatic authentication (in line with the 
Alternative Fuels Infrastructure Regulation) and consumer participation in flexibility markets, local 
congestion and recharging services based on real -time electricity prices (passive imbalance 
markets).  
- Requiring a display screen be added to each charger introduces an unnecessary piece of 
technology that increases upfront costs, maintenance costs, and creates an additional potential 
point of failure thereby reducing reliability. Moreover, screens can fail  due to extreme weather 
exposure (either hot or cold) and are easy targets for vandalism whereby a screen can be smashed 
and render the charger unusable. For these reasons there should be flexibility to allow the use of 
remote displays for transaction data. 
- Moreover, the final text should specify the meaning of ‘metrologically controlled’.

• Local Data Storage (Annex I, Points 10.7 & 10.8): 
Amended MID should be a forward -looking piece of legislation.  Mandating local data storage 
requirements is an outdated approach, which increases costs for the manufacturers and consumers and 
hinders innovation. Digital solutions, such as a cloud-computing solution or a mobile device should be the 
only means of storing and presenting the data to the consumer. Any local storage requirement would lead 
to needing a complete redesign of charging stations, which would cause further delays in the rollout of 
the EV charging infrastructure, and consequently the achievement of the EU’s climate objectives.  
 
• Technical Standards (Annex V, Point 2): 
Amended MID should reference IEC 61851 -23 standards for AC/DC charging to maintain flexibility as 
standards evolve. Referring to the standard rather than technical values would ensure that the legislation 
remains more accurate long -term and follows the evolution of the standard in a dynamic way , limiting 
future MID updates to the minimum necessary level. 
 
• Cable Swapping (Annex Va, Point 4) 
Allowing the replacement of cable without recalibration of the MID meter inside the charging station is a 
welcome development and it should be retained in the final text. Cable swapping is a regular charging 
station maintenance practice. This addition reduces costs and processing time, allowing the charger to be 
operational and available to customers faster and simplifies this maintenance procedure. It is key to 
harmonize this principle across the Member States, as today some of them require a recalibration of the 
meter after a cable swap.

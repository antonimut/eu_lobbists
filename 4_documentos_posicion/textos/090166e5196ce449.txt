ChargeUp Europe’s response to public consultation on Measuring Instruments – technical 
update of EU rules 
 
 
Introduction 
ChargeUp Europe welcomes the European Commission’s proposal for targeted amendments to the 
Measuring Instrument Directive (Directive 2014/32/EU). If consistently and uniformly implemented 
by EU member states, the revised Directive would harmonize requirements for metering 
instruments of EV charging stations across the EU, ending fragmentation due to differing national 
rules and requirem ents, strengthening the Single Market, and avoiding unnecessary costs for 
manufacturers and consumers. 
 
By responding to the present public consultation, ChargeUp Europe would like to provide 
comments to certain provisions of the proposal that would require the consideration of co -
legislators, namely:  
 
1. Provisions on technical requirements that would need improvement  
1.1. Storage of measurement data (Points 10.7 and 10.8 of Annex I) 
1.2. Rated Operating Requirements (Point 2 of Annex Va) 
1.3. Minimum Measurable Quantity (Point 2 of Annex Va) 
1.4. Timeline for the application of the revised Directive (Articles 2 and 3) 
 
2. Important provisions to maintain in the proposal  
2.1. Replacement of manufacturer seal (Point 4 of Annex Va) 
 
3. Provisions that necessitate clarification 
3.1. Use of remote display (point 10.6 of Annex I) 
3.2. Retroactivity of requirements 
 
 
1. Provisions on technical requirements that would need improvement 
1.1. Storage of measurement data (Points 10.7 and 10.8 of Annex I) 
The approach prescribed for the storage of measurement data under points 10.7 does not 
reflect state of the art technology, it is not justified by a practical rationale, and should be 
changed. 
The sentence under point 10.7 stating “ the measurement data shall be fully established in a 
device or a system  so that it can be immediately presented to the consumer” seem to add a 
requirement to include measuring data in a local storage device in the charger so that they can 
be immediately available to the costumer. This means that, to be immediately presentable to the 
customer, data must be stored in the device, because if they were stored in the back end, it would 
not be possible to present them to the customer immediately.

The approach thereby prescribed is technically unfounded and not consistent with modern 
technological solutions based on digitalisation, where data can be stored in a cloud or using a 
mobile device as a display. Not only the approach proposed would project  industry to a 
technological past, but it would result in significant costs for industry: for EV chargers 
manufacturers this approach would mean fully redesigning the EV charger to add a very large 
storage function to each charger without any technological or practical rationale.   
It is worth noting that cloud -computing based solutions would not present security concerns as the 
data set is unchangeable and encrypted when stored in the cloud, while the prescription of storing 
measuring data in the device itself is not reported in any member state.   
1.2.  Rated Operating Requirements (Point 2 of Annex Va) 
Concerning the rated operating requirements under point 2 of Annex Va, it is proposed to replace 
the detailed listing of technical values for AC and DC charging now present in the text, with a 
simple reference to the standard IEC 61851-23. We discourage listing technical values in the 
text of the Directive because these are subject to changes over time. The best approach is 
to reference the standard so that the Directive can dynamically and automatically capture 
any changes introduced in the standards.  
In addition, the values reported under point 2 concerning DC chargers incorrectly copies only the 
left-hand side of table 118 of IEC 61851-23 and therefore only regard chargers with I<200 A. This 
error implies that the Directive, as the text stand now, would not set reasonable limits for typical 
High-Power Charging (HPC) chargers, which easily present I>200 A. 
1.3. Minimum Measurable Quantity (Point 2 of Annex Va) 
Concerning the MMQ (Minimum measurable quantity) the specification of a limit to the MMQ is 
very beneficial, however setting MMQ lower or equal to 1 kWh for DC chargers is not technically 
sound nor reasonable considering the high power involved. In fact, 1 kWh are achieved in the first 
second(s) of a typical EV charging session. It can be doubted that chargers can always maintain 
sufficient accuracy for that initial ramp -up phase, with which every charging session begins. 
Moreover, this makes it mandatory t o provide many decimal digits for DC charging (probably 4 
digits) to provide sufficient accuracy. For HPC this is not sound. It would be extremely difficult to 
guarantee sufficient accuracy in that frame. Third, the MMQ are also much larger in other 
metrology cases, e.g. most petrol stations. 
The proposal is to set MMQ between 2 kWh and 5 kWh for DC chargers and equal or lower 
than 2 kWh for AC chargers. 
1.4. Timeline for the application of the revised Directive (Articles 2 and 3) 
The suggestion is  extending the transitional period envisaged under article 2 for the 
application of the Directive’s provisions implemented by Member States from 24 months to 
36 months to allow easier adaptation to amended rules by industry.  
In fact, article 2.1 reads that a measuring instrument that are in conformity with the current MID 
(2014/32) can placed on the market for a period of 24 months after entry into force of the 
amendments to the MID.

these details.  
 
2. Important provisions to maintain in the proposal  
2.1. Replacement of manufacturer seal (Point 4 of Annex Va) 
The provision under point 4 of Annex Va concerning the possibility of replacing  the 
manufacturer seal at the charger cable with an installer seal is strongly welcomed and 
should be kept in the final text. With that, a broken or defective charging cable can be replaced 
by a qualified technician, without the subsequent need for a federal or official recalibration. The 
absolute influence of the charging cable on the accuracy of the measurement is already very 
small (typically around 0.3% -0.5%), and the relative difference between two cha rging cables is 
absolutely negligible. There is no technical justification at all to require a costly and time -
consuming official recalibration after swapping such a charging cable, as it is required by some 
Member States today.  
This specification on cable swapping introduces the important principle that the Charging 
Point Operators should be able to replace cables on their own stations without incurring 
unnecessary costly processing. We encourage swift harmonization across member  states 
once the amendments to the Directive enter into force. 
3. Provisions that necessitate clarification 
3.1. Use of remote display (point 10.6 of Annex I) 
The novelty that remote display use is allowed under point 10.6 of Annex I, via either an external 
screen or a consumer’s device, e.g. a smartphone, is welcomed. It remains unclear however 
what is the intended meaning of “metrologically controlled” conside ring that the “consumer’s 
device” referenced in the text in practice would be a normal personal smartphone. In this case, 
metrological control via those devices would simply be impossible.  
We would encourage a clarification on the correct understanding of point 10.6 of Annex I and the 
rationale behind that and we suggest the removal of the sentence  
3.2. Retroactivity of requirements 
One important element of the discussion that is missing in the proposal is providing a framework 
for how to address retroactivity for existing infrastructure that is not covered under the new 
framework. This is an important opportunity to create harmonization across member states and 
ensure that CPOs and manufacturers are not stuck with bespoke renovation or retrofit 
requirements for existing infrastructure across different member states. For example, if one 
country provides a 4-year enforcement phase in period while another takes the approach of a 10-

year timeline, there will be strong inconsistency. Additionally, it is important that well-functioning 
and reliable infrastructure that is already on the ground today is not prematurely forced to be 
replaced. The existing EVSE should be operated for the pe riod of its natural lifetime, without the 
need for upgrading and retrofitting it to meet the requirements introduced with the targeted 
amendments to the MID.  
The recommendation is that the proposal be amended to provide direction on retroactivity 
to members states and not impose any retroactive requirements for legacy infrastructure 
pre-existing the entry into force of the targeted amendments to the MID.

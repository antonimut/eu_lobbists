 
 
 
Hager Group feedback to proposal for a directive – COM(2024)561 
 
Hager Group welcomes the revision proposal of the measuring instrument directive to adapt them to 
current markets and technologic needs and consequently to avoid national regulations which acts 
against a well functioning single market. 
 
Considering electricity energy metering we welcome the explicit definition of direct current meters as part 
of the directive and the definition of essential requirements for measuring systems for electric vehicle 
supply equipment (EVSE). 
 
The key point is to avoid any confusion regarding the applicability of the requirements of Annex Va. 
In a number of cases, electric vehicle charging requires a measurement often carried out using an instrument that 
complies with the requirements of Annex V.  
Below is a non-exhaustive list of such uses: 
• Home charging measured separately (individual house or condominium with designated parking) on a 
specific delivery point or not 
• Measurement done for energy management 
• Measurement done in the context of providing flexibility 
Even if the directive on measuring instruments does not define the use case, it is essential not to create confusion 
and to clarify the framework for the use of the requirements of Annex Va. 
 
Below we have summarized our comments on the proposed amendment made through the text COM(2024)561 
and its annexes. 
Recitals 
To avoid any confusion, it is necessary to clarify in the recitals that the requirements for electric vehicle charging 
apply to the measuring instruments used in EVSE. We propose to add "the measuring instruments used in" in 
recital (2). 
 
(2) The scope and the associated essential requirements covered by Directive 2014/32/EU were already established 
by Directive 2004/22/EC3, of which Directive 2014/32/EU is a recast. Thus, they have remained unchanged for more 
than 20 years. In the meantime, new measuring instruments have appeared on the market that are not covered by 
Directive 2014/32/EU. That is notably the case for the measuring instruments used in electric vehicle supply 
equipment and compressed gas dispensers, which are important for the successful rollout of clean mobility. 
Moreover, Directive 2014/32/EU does not cover thermal energy meters for cooling applications. In addition, as far as 
electricity and gas meters are concerned, Directive 2014/32/EU neither covers the use of hydrogen and other gases 
that can be used as alternatives to more traditional gases, nor enables to take full advantage of smart metering 
which plays an important role in achieving the Union climate objectives. Therefore, it is appropriate to amend the 
scope of Directive 2014/32/EU and the essential requirements set out in the Annexes to that Directive in order to 
take into account technological progress. 
 
Similarly, the requirements of Annex Va should not apply in the context of the use of measuring instruments for 
energy management or charging power purposes. We propose to clarify the scope by adding "whose measurement 
result is used as the basis for a commercial transaction" in recital (7). 
 
(7) A new Annex Va to Directive 2014/32/EU should be inserted in order to address the need for harmonised 
essential requirements with regard to measuring systems for electric vehicle supply equipment whose 
measurement result is used as the basis for a commercial transaction. 
 
Article 2: Entry into force and transition period 
The transitional arrangements as described in article 2 is confusing, in particular for instruments where existing 
national certificates have already been published and will be covered by the amendments of the directive. 
We propose to use the text of the former version of the directive.

Annex I: adjustment of point 10.2. on the indication of results 
The inclusion of protection against accidental deletion should be limited to meters whose index can be reset. We 
propose to be explicit on this subject by adding "for meters with reset capability." 
 
10.2. The indication of any result shall be clear and unambiguous, for meters with reset capability protected 
against accidental deletion, and accompanied by such marks and inscriptions necessary to inform the user of the 
significance of the result. Easy reading of the presented result shall be permitted under normal conditions of use. 
Additional indications may be shown provided they cannot be confused with the metrologically controlled indications. 
 
Annex I: clause 10.6 display of the result 
Clause 10.6 derogates from the requirements of clauses 10.1 and 10.5; however, it lacks coherence and needs 
clarification. 
• Requirement 10.6 (a) is identical to the requirement of 10.1. 
• Should it be understood that to comply with 10.6, one must meet requirement (a) AND (b)? To clarify the 
situation, we propose adding an "or" between (a) and (b). 
• Requiring traceability makes sense for (b) but not for (a) since the display system is on the measuring 
instrument. 
• The requirement introduced by the last sentence is inconsistent with that required in (b). The traceability and 
lack of manipulation required in (b) allow confirming the origin of the measurement result by a metrologically 
controlled measuring instrument. This approach avoids metrological control of the communication chain. 
This requirement is therefore superfluous and counterproductive to achieving the desired objective. We 
propose deleting the last sentence of 10.6. 
 
 
‘10.6. By way of derogation from points 10.1. and 10.5., for gas and electricity meters, measuring systems for electric

Annex I: clause 10.7 display of the result 
Clause 10.7 should only apply to EVSE that allow direct sales (which is the case for publicly accessible EVSE, see 
AFIR). 
 
10.7. By way of derogation from point 10.4., for measuring systems for EVSE used for direct sales and 
measuring systems for compressed gas dispensers, the measurement data shall be fully established in a device 
or a system so that it can be immediately presented to the consumer. 
 
Annex I: clause 10.8 display of the result 
Clause 10.8 should only apply to EVSE that allow direct sales (which is the case for publicly accessible EVSE, 
see AFIR). During a direct sale of energy for electric vehicle charging, the measurement result is only presented 
to the end customer (buyer), as the seller is not physically present at the time of the transaction. The transaction 
information is transmitted electronically to the latter. We propose to modify clause 10.8 as follows: 
 
Proposed update: 
10.8. By way of derogation from point 10.4., measuring systems for EVSE used for direct sales shall be 
designed to present the measurement result to the end customer and transmit it to all other parties in the 
transaction when installed as intended. 
 
Annex Va: scope 
As explained for the recitals, it is important to clearly define the use case to which the requirements of this annex 
apply. To avoid any confusion, it is necessary to specify that the requirements for measuring instruments used in 
EVSE apply when the measurement result is used as the basis for the commercial transaction. We propose adding 
"as the basis for the commercial transaction of charging or discharging" in the scope of the annex. 
 
 
ANNEX Va 
MEASURING SYSTEMS FOR ELECTRIC VEHICLE SUPPLY EQUIPMENT (MI-003a)  
 
 
 
The relevant requirements of Annex I, the specific requirements of this annex, and the conformity assessment 
procedures listed in this annex apply to measuring systems for EVSE intended to be used as the basis for the 
commercial transaction of charging or discharging in residential, commercial, or light industrial environments. 
 
Annex Va: definitions 
As with the scope, it is necessary to clarify the definition to avoid any confusion by adding "intended to be used as 
the basis for the commercial transaction of charging or discharging" in the definition. 
 
DEFINITIONS 
A measuring system for EVSE intended to be used as the basis for the commercial transaction of charging or 
discharging refers to a system that includes all relevant metrological functions related to the transfer (in either 
direction) at a specified transfer point of electrical energy between EVSE (e.g., electric vehicle charging stations) and 
electric vehicles. 
 
Annex Va: specific requirements 
The operating conditions described in Table 1 of this annex are different from those specified in Annex V. Therefore, 
it is not appropriate to use the same designation for accuracy classes. We propose, for example, to name them 
"class Aa, class Ba, class Ca."  
Our preferred alternative to keep the same designation would be to use the same operating conditions as in Annex V 
and to refer to table 1 of Annex V.

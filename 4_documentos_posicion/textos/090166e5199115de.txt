Comments on the European Commission's 
proposals to improve the position of farmers 
in the food chain 
 
Spanish Agrifood Cooperatives 
 
In response to the mobilisations of the agricultural sector at the beginning of 2024, the 
European Commission has worked on various initiatives with the aim of addressing the sector's 
demands. One of these is the proposals to amend several EU regulations in order to strengthen 
the position of farmers in the food chain.  (“Proposal for a Regulation of the European 
Parliament and of the Council amending Regulations (EU) No. 1308/2013, (EU) 2021/2115 
and (EU) 2021/2116 as regards the strengthening of the position of farmers in the food supply 
chain”). 
Through this document, Cooperativas Agro -alimentarias de España submits its comments on 
the proposal under debate to the institutions. The document is structured in an initial 
comment of a general nature on the orientation of the measures for the protection 
of farmers in the value chain, followed by a summary of our main assessments of 
the Commission's proposed wording, and finally a series of specific amendments to 
the articles proposed by the European Commission: 
 
1. INTRODUCTION 
We welcome the initiative of the EU institutions and Member States to put producers at the 
centre of the debate on agriculture and livestock farming, the need to improve their position 
in the value chain and to recognise that the profitability of the primary sector is necessary for 
the market to function properly.  
We partially agree with the recognition that the situation of prices in the markets is caused by 
factors external to the sector. The analysis must recognise that a large part of the production 
sector at EU level is of a small economic dimension, is poorly structured in the market and 
starts from a position of weakness in relation to customers and suppliers of a greater economic 
dimension. On the other hand, the CAP lacks the economic instruments to address market 
management, crises and the large fluctuation s that occur in prices, both of products and of 
production costs, which in general terms continue to trend upwards. This would have been an 
opportunity for the Commission to propose some changes to the regulatory framework to 
provide for this type of crisis management tool.

Strengthen cooperatives and producer organisations 
The historical response to the lack of economic dimension and atomisation of agricultural and 
livestock farms in most Member States has been the integration of producers into agri -food 
cooperatives in order to move up the value chain, gain negotiating power with customers and 
suppliers through joint marketing and procurement, acquire production goods and professional 
services to be more efficient and take advantage of the economies of scale provided by the 
union, thus achieving improved profitability. Howev er, the cooperative enterprise does not 
have a uniform treatment or a common reality in the Member States, so the EU devised the 
PO as a form of recognition that could be granted either to cooperatives themselves or to non-
cooperative enterprises that purs ued similar objectives, operated in a similar way and 
performed the function of marketing. 
We should remember that these organisations (co-operative enterprises, POs) are a means to 
an end, and not an end in themselves, in order to achieve the objectives of improving and 
strengthening the position of producers in the chain. For this reason, the role of POs should 
be that of enterprises created by producers, governed by them and for their benefit. 
The EU regulations on POs are best represented in the regime of the fruit and vegetable sector, 
based on operational programmes; and in the so -called sectoral interventions, which allow a 
similar regime to be extended to other sectors within the CAP. The main aim of these structural 
instruments is to encourage producers to unite in the market through operational programmes 
co-financed with community funds and the PO itself, to finance multi-annual investment plans, 
which contain investments and expenses in favour of the PO and the farms of the members, 
and which are oriented towards certain objectives: improving competitiveness, improving 
marketing, innovation and environmental management, etc.  
In view of the above, we are against changing or relaxing the definition or the requirements 
for recognition of POs to make room for other entities that do not pursue the same objectives 
or submit to the same conditions. It is a mistake to consider that ma king recognition more 
flexible for other types of non -business entities will improve the position of producers in the 
market.   
We also reject the idea of recognising the concept of collective price negotiation. This concept 
leads to the understanding that non -business entities, which can be recognised as POs, can 
negotiate on behalf of their members without being companies, that i s, without being 
responsible for the production being negotiated or for the contracts that are signed, as well as 
their fulfilment. This formula would be introducing false expectations and weakening the figure 
of the business PO. The (unsuccessful) experience of the dairy sector should serve as a lesson 
in this regard. 
 
Modification of the Directive on Unfair Commercial Practices in the agri -food value 
chain 
Spain is a pioneer in this type of regulation to regulate Unfair Commercial Practices in the 
market and to ensure that it functions better. The aim of this regulation is to avoid the abuse 
of some companies over others, especially those of agricultural and  livestock producers, in 
commercial relations through its pioneering food chain law, amended in 2021.

We support both the Spanish law and the EU directive on this matter. However, from our 
experience in Spain and in the market, we must draw attention to its limited scope in the face 
of certain objectives, especially those that seek profitability and minimu m prices for farmers 
in the market due to the application of this law.  
For this reason, we warn that reinforcing a system of written contracts does not ensure 
balanced negotiation, but rather that what has been agreed by both parties is fulfilled, while 
limiting and controlling the use of certain commercial practices considered unfair. Furthermore, 
commercial relationships are between two parties, and their non -compliance, control and 
penalisation do not have a generalised effect, but rather on the specific commercial 
relationship, so the scope of the law is useful but limited to achieving a change of culture, but 
not an effective change in the short term. 
We recall that both the law and the directive aim to regulate Unfair Commercial Practices in 
commercial relations, so the relationship between cooperatives and their members should 
remain outside the scope of these regulations because they are not commerci al relations and 
have their own national legislation, which is statutory and non -commercial in nature.  
Finally, any definition or modification that regulates and defines the figure of the farmer and 
his position in the market through this regulation must be consistent with the figure of the 
cooperative enterprise model, and that this is not penalised. It would not make sense to 
encourage the union of producers to strengthen their position in the market, and then not to 
grant them adequate treatment in this regulation. 
 
2. THE ORGANISATION'S POSITION 
 
Brief summary of the position of Spanish Agrifood cooperatives  
 
2.1 Proposed amendments to Regulation 2021/2115 
Regarding the proposed amendments to 2021/2115, and beyond the proposals presented 
by the Commission in December 2024, which we find interesting although partly open to 
interpretation, we believe that this opportunity should be seized to amend other articles of 
the Regulation: mainly those provisions on operational programmes (OP) in the fruit and 
vegetable sector that should be better adapted to the needs of producers and their 
cooperatives, to improve legal certainty, avoid restrictive or misinterpreted provisions and, 
above all, harmonise, simplify and reduce bureaucracy in the management of this interesting 
tool, which is highly appreciated by the sector. At the end of this document we make our 
additional contributions to the Commission's own proposal, in relation to some othe r articles 
of the Regulation. 
 
2.2 Proposed amendments to Regulation 1308/2013 
Regarding the proposed changes to Regulation 1308/2013, we believe that the recognition of 
POs should be carried out while keeping the objectives and definitions of producer 
organisations unchanged, without distorting them.  Throughout the Commission's 
proposal, it seems that, instead of choosing to extend the existing model in the fruit and 
vegetable sector (a successful model consisting of financially sound POs, which has 
proven its effectiveness for decades) to other sectors, the Commission intends to str eamline 
this model for all sectors so that it can be assimilated more quickly. But we believe that, in

doing so, it will not contribute to balancing market forces and, what is worse, it will disrupt 
what was already working. The agricultural and livestock sectors need to be structured around 
real and effective organisations, and not paper POs. At the very l east, the consolidated and 
successful model achieved in the fruit and vegetable sector must be respected.  
The POs that should be extended and strengthened, in order to really promote the 
concentration of supply and improve the position of farmers in the f ood chain, are the 
economic POs that play the true role of MARKETING (bringing to market, signing 
contracts and effectively commercialising) the production of their members, as the POs 
in the fruit and vegetable sector are already doing. On the contrary, POs geared towards 
‘representation/collective bargaining’ (so that each producer can market their own product) 
play an ‘abstract role’, which does nothing to favour structuring, and in fact discourages 
structuring into truly economic structures. After the s uccess of the fruit and vegetable 
experience, we continue to support the sectoral intervention model based on economic POs, 
with means of commercialisation, well -dimensioned, based on democratic governance, 
controlled by producers, etc. and duly recognised. 
 
 ‘POs not recognised as POs’ 
We reject the Commission's proposals that seek to grant a role in the market to 
a diffuse, undefined and confusing figure, such as  that of a ‘PO that has not been 
recognised as a PO’, to grant it the same exceptions to the rules/competition as recognised 
POs. The introduction of these modifications would only introduce confusion and legal 
uncertainty and generate expectations that wi ll only lead to unequal treatment to the 
detriment of the members of the ‘recognised POs’ who will be destabilised. On the other 
hand, if an entity is to meet the requi rements of POs, someone will have to verify it, that 
is, someone (the Member State) will have to recognise that they meet those conditions 
that give them the possibility of not submitting to the rules of competition! By expecting 
unrecognised POs to perform the same functions as recognised POs, the regulation would 
set up a contrast between ‘genuine POs’ and ‘light POs’ and, therefore, would be 
detrimental to sectors (such as fruit and vegetables) in which there are already ‘genuine 
POs’ (economic POs) that  fulfil their function (mainly effective marketing) and operate 
efficiently. The solution to improving the concentration of supply and bargaining power is 
to promote strong, duly recognised, economic producer organisations that have bargaining 
power in the market and market the product effectively. On the contrary, the development 
of this strange figure of the ‘PO that has not been recognised as a PO’ does not 
contribute at all to solving the problems of market imbalance. Moreover, granting 
exceptions to competition law to unrecognised and non-economic POs is not only 
not the solution to balancing the market, but could also aggravate the imbalance 
by weakening currently recognised POs. 
In our opinion, the most appropriate way to strengthen the position of farmers and 
livestock breeders in the chain, and to consolidate the role of POs and extend it to all 
sectors, would be to guarantee that these other sectors have a PO regime and operational 
programmes similar to that of the fruit and vegetable sector.  
 
 
 Non-economic APOs 
We also do not agree with the novelty proposed by the Commission that recognised 
APOs (without economic activity) made up of recognised POs can plan 
production, optimise costs, market and negotiate contracts on behalf of

members, if their weight with respect to the product in question does not exceed 33% of 
the total. The conditions under which a PO can market and negotiate contracts for the

For those economic POs, it should be clear that the 33% ceiling would not apply to them. 
Nor should the 33% ceiling apply in the case of POs that carry out other activities, such as 
promotion, research, market monitoring, quality management, etc.  
 
 
 ‘Producer’ and ‘producer member’ vs ‘farmer’ 
We are concerned  about the changes proposed by the Commission in which it 
replaces the term ‘producers’ with ‘farmers’ in relation to the members of POs . 
This new terminology could be detrimental to second -degree cooperatives recognised as 
POs, whose direct members are cooperatives formed by producers. This issue was already 
taken into account and respected in the implementing regulations governing fruit and 
vegetable producer organisations (Regulation 891/2017 defines ‘producer’, ‘producer 
member’, and eve n refers to ‘non -producer members’). We believe that the same 
terminology should be maintained, at least for fruit and vegetables, in order to provide 
certainty regarding the eligibility of actions under operational programmes and compliance 
with the conditions for recognition).  
 
 Direct producer-customer contact 
We continue to oppose the authorisation of direct contact between buyers and PO 
members. If the PO is the one that carries out the commercialisation, it would not make 
sense for the members to be in contact with the buyer. We consider that this clause, which 
was included in 2017, should be eliminated, or at least not made even more flexible.  
 
 
 Obligation of written contracting and the registration of contracts 
With regard to the new obligation introduced on written contracts, we welcome the 
establishment of an exception for the relationship between the member and his or her 
cooperative. However, we would like to warn of the imprecision of the exception 
established for cooperatives. The text proposed by the Commission includes that the 
written contract will not be required when ‘a member of a producer organisation or 
cooperative supplies the agricultural products in question to the producer organisation or 
cooperative of which he or she is a member, provided that the statutes of that producer 
organisation or cooperative or the rules and decisions provided for in these statutes or 
derived from them contain provisions with similar effects’. 
We in the organisation are opposed to this wording which makes the non -
consideration of the cooperative member relationship as a commercial 
relationship conditional on the existence of a statutory agreement with similar

business model. 
 
2.3 Proposed Regulation on cooperation between enforcement authorities 
responsible for ensuring compliance with the Unfair Commercial Practices 
Directive  
 
Despite the fact that the proposal formulated by the Commission has not focused on modifying 
the Directive on Unfair Commercial Practices, we believe that it may be a favourable political 
moment to reflect on the convenience of including some unfair commercial practice.  
For all these reasons, we would like to request that the practices that the Commission 
considers unfair include: “The imposition of a particular container (in particular, the imposition 
of reusable plastic containers (RPCs) on suppliers delivering fresh fruit and vegetables”, which 
implies an additional cost for them.  
Retail chains have been, for many years, imposing/inducing its suppliers (FV cooperatives 
or any business that packages and sells FV to retail chain) to deliver its FV using a certain 
transport container from a certain distributor.  The containers are foldable reusable-
returnable plastic containers  and the most typical manufacturers are IFCO and EPS. 
These containers are not bought but rented by coops /other businesses. In exchange for 
using those containers, fruit suppliers (obliged to supply their product in those containers) 
must pay a rental and a a security deposit. 
The system to put this type of packaging into circulation works as follows:  
­ the company that manufactures and owns the containers (IFCO, EPS) delivers them to 
the point of packaging (e.g. the Coop);  
­ these are used by the producer/cooperative/packer to transport FV to their customer's 
premises (the retail, who can also use them to display FV in store)  
­ Once used, the distribution company picks them up, folds them, transports them to the 
storage site.  
­ At the end of the chain, the owner company is responsible for collecting the empty 
folded boxes at the distribution facilities

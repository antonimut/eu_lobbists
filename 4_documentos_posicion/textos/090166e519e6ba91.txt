 
 
 
Oxfam’s input to public consultation 
on the regulation of the European parliament and of the Council on cooperation 
among enforcement authorities responsible for the enforcement of Directive (EU) 
2019/633 on unfair trading practices in business-to-business relationships in the 
agricultural and food supply chain 
In December 2024, the European Commission proposed new rules to strengthen cross-
border cooperation and coordination between enforcement authorities in Member 
States and the Commission regarding Unfair Trading Practices (UTPs). Oxfam welcomes 
this initiative and the Commission’s commitment to improving UTPs enforcement and 
protecting farmers’ revenues, as recently emphasized in Commissioner Hansen’s Vision 
for Agriculture and Food.  
However, key weaknesses in the UTP Directive’s enforcement remain unaddressed, 
particularly in relation to access to complaint mechanisms and raising awareness 
among suppliers. Additionally, the proposal overlooks suppliers outside the EU, who 
should receive equal protection when selling directly into the EU market. As stated in 
Paragraph 12 of the preamble of the Directive 2019/633  : 
"Suppliers established outside the Union should also enjoy protection against unfair 
trading practices when they sell agricultural and food products into the Union" 
Furthermore, Oxfam calls for a broader review of the UTP Directive, including adding a 
ban on purchasing below production costs to the blacklist, as requested by multiple 
farmers' organizations. This measure should be adopted at the EU level by the end of 
2025 to close legislative gaps and ensure fair enforcement across all Member States. 
Spain, France, and Belgium have already implemented such bans through national 
legislation, proving their feasibility. 
While improving coordination among enforcement authorities is a step forward, it will 
not be enough to address the Directive’s shortcomings. Oxfam, along with a coalition of 
NGOs and agricultural organizations, has developed a position paper outlining the 
urgent need for a comprehensive revision of the UTP Directive. 
To enhance the current proposal COM(2024) 576 final, we recommend addressing at 
least the following key areas: 
1. Ensure third-country suppliers are included in the scope of the proposal 
"This Directive applies to sales where either the supplier or the buyer, or both, are 
established in the Union." Art 1 of UTPs Directive 2019/633. This ensures that suppliers 
outside the EU, who face similar unfair practices when selling directly to EU buyers, are

also protected from UTPs. Keeping this aspect within the scope of the new proposal is 
essential to ensure fair market conditions between EU and non-EU producers while 
promoting ethical and sustainable trade relationships. We recommend explicitly 
including a reference to cross-border collaboration with third countries in Article 2, 
paragraph 1 “Scope” of the proposal and in Article 3 “Definitions”. 
2. Improve access to complaint mechanisms and strengthen international 
cooperation 
UTP Directive enforcement remains weak, with low complaint rates, often due to lack of 
awareness or fear of retaliation. According to the 2024 Joint Research Centre (JRC) 
survey, 88% of respondents reported experiencing UTPs, yet only 271 complaints were 
officially filed. Given that the EU had 9.1 million agricultural holdings in 2024, this 
means only 0.003% of farmers filed a complaint. 
This issue is even more pronounced for third-country suppliers, who have little 
awareness of the Directive and lack information on complaint mechanisms and 
enforcement authorities in the EU. No complaints have been filed by third-country 
suppliers to EU enforcement authorities, despite the occurrence of UTPs. This has been 
documented in recent studies on unfair trading practices in Ecuador and Rwanda, 
commissioned by FTAO and co-financed by Oxfam.

3. Ensure national UTP legislation is not weakened by cross-border cooperation 
UTP Directive 2019/633 allows Member States to introduce stricter rules to combat 
UTPs (Article 9). Some countries, such as Belgium, France, and Spain, have already 
strengthened supplier protections, particularly by banning purchasing below 
production costs. 
However, Article 5 of the current proposal allows enforcement authorities to refuse 
information sharing if a Member State has stricter rules. This could undermine national 
UTP regulations, contradicting the Directive’s overall goal of combating unfair trading 
practices. We recommend removing this restriction.

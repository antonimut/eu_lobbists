FoodDrinkEurope feedback to the EC’s proposed regulation 
on cooperation among enforcement authorities responsible 
for the enforcement of the UTP Directive (COM(2024) 576 final)  
 
 
FoodDrinkEurope, representing the EU food and drink industries, welcomes and supports the 
European Commission’s proposed Regulation to enhance cross -border enforcement against 
unfair trading practices (UTPs) in the EU.   
 
The food and drink manufacturing sector comprises more than 300,000 businesses, with nine out 
of ten employing fewer than 20 people. Together, they purchase 70% of all EU farm produce and 
invest over €2 billion annually in innovative, sustainably-produced products. From SMEs to large 
multinationals, the food and drink industry shares a primary goal: to continue providing high -
quality, sustainable, and affordable products to European consumers. The European Commission 
announced the publication of this act in the context of strengthening farmers’ position in the supply 
chain and FoodDrinkEurope supports this principle. Achieving this objective requires fairness and 
cooperation across the supply chain.   
 
The explanatory memorandum to the proposed regulation highlights that gathering information, 
infringement and imposing and enforcing fines and other equally effective penalties can be difficult 
where the buyer is located in another Member State.  This initiative represents a significant step 
toward strengthening fairness and transparency in the food supply chain. 
 
Given the increasing influence of European retail alliances  (“ERAs”) and the fragmentation of 
national regulations, a coordinated approach  and a collaborative mechanism are  essential to 
ensure that all suppliers, regardless of their size or location, receive consistent protection against 
unfair practices. 
 
Closing enforcement gaps 
While the UTP Directive has already been helpful in addressing the unfair trading practices in the 
food supply chain, enforcement has been hampered by lack of cooperation between enforcement 
authorities, notably in the case of ERAs, locating themselves in jurisdictions that are less strict to 
avoid application of national UTP law . Stronger rules on cross ‐border cooperation will help fill 
these gaps.  
 
Addressing different national approaches  
In implementing the UTP Directive, some Member States have retained the €350 million threshold 
while others have removed or modified it. These discrepancies can only impede the cross‐border 
enforcement of the same UTP when the buyer is established in a Member State with a threshold 
regime and the supplier is established in a Member State with a stricter regime.  
 
Many national enforcement authorities have highlighted practical challenges in dealing with cross‐
border cases due to the lack of harmonisation of national UTP laws, revealing a clear need for

Moving forward: call for further revision of the UTP Directive to reduce fragmentation and 
ensure fairness for all in the food chain 
In addition to supporting this cross ‐border proposed Regulation, FoodDrinkEurope calls for the 
UTP Directive to be revised to further reduce fragmentation and protect all suppliers. In order to 
enhance its effectiveness, the priorities of the upcoming revision of the Directive should be to: 
 
• provide a harmonised framework across the EU Member States. A consistent approach would 
create a level playing field that would help boost the EU’s growth and competitiveness, which 
is important for all operators, including farmers. 
• remove the €350 million threshold to extend the scope of the UTP Directive to suppliers of all 
sizes and avoid double standards. What is “unfair” to small businesses does not become “fair” 
to large companies.   
• extend the list of black UTPs to include practices such as abusive logistical penalties, abrupt 
termination of commercial relation (unreasonable delisting of products).

Alias – “Wine Package”.  
 
Copa and Cogeca demands - In a nutshell 
 
➢ (Re)planting authorizations: Support for exempting valid authorisations (both 
new and re-planting) granted before January 2025 from administrative 
penalties in case of non-use and extending the validity of replanting 
authorisations to eight years. Demand to set a fixed date within the agricultural 
calendar on which all the authorisations of the year expire.  Demand to make 
the simplified procedure for replanting authorisation the norm. (Art. 1, par. 1). 
➢ Limitations on New Planting Authorisations: Support to introducing 
limitations on new planting authorisations in regions where crisis measures 
have been implemented, allowing Member States to adjust production 
potential to market demand. (Art. 1, par. 2). 
➢ Opposition to Yield Limitations: Opposition to setting conditions on 
replanting authorisations that limit yield increases, arguing such measures 
would hinder innovation and economic viability. (Art. 1, Par. 3). 
➢ Clarification of Low/No Alcohol Wine Definitions: Request for clearer 
definitions of "alcohol-free," "alcohol-free 0.0%," and "alcohol-light" wines, and 
concern over potential misinterpretation of terms in different languages.  
Demand to clearly introduce a transitional period for labelling requirement to 
include the products from the 2026 harvest until exhaustion of stocks (Art. 1, 
Par. 5).  
➢ Labelling: Demand to assure that mandatory particulars to be presented on 
the label are only mentioned once. Demand to exclude from mandatory 
obligations on labelling the products that are produced and intended for third -
market exports (modification of Art. 119 CMO).

Introduction  
Following the discussions held within the High -Level Group on Wine Policy (HLG), the 
European Commission published, on 28 March 2025, a draft legislative proposal —referred 
to as the “ wine package”—aimed at reforming several key regulations governing the EU 
wine sector. Specifically, the proposal introduces targeted amendments to Regulation (EU) 
No 1308/2013 on the Common Organisation of the Markets, Regulation (EU) No 2021/2115 on 
the Strategic Plans, and Regulation (EU) No 251/2014 on Aromatised Wines. 
In light of these proposed changes, Copa and Cogeca have adopted the present position 
paper to outline their views and recommendations on the draft legislative initiative.

Context  
The European wine sector is currently experiencing a deep structural crisis. Over the past 
decades, both domestic and global wine consumption trends have shifted significantly. The 
EU’s share of global wine consumption has declined from nearly 60% in 2000 to 48% in 2022. 
Younger gene rations are adopting new consumption habits, often favouring non -traditional 
beverages, while the cultural role of wine is also evolving —moving away from its longstanding 
place as an “ingredient” of the meal.  
These challenges have been further exacerbated by a series of disruptive events, including the 
Covid-19 pandemic, the closure of the Chinese market, and the far -reaching consequences of 
the war in Ukraine. 
In 2024, the EU Commission’s Wine Market Observatory —a dedicated expert group —
conducted an in-depth analysis of the sector, concluding that urgent action was required. Later 
that year, the High -Level Group on Wine Policy, composed of senior officials from the 
Commission and Member States, convened to discuss the path forward. In December 2024, the 
group reached a consensus on a comprehensive set of recommendations addressing both the 
immediate and long-term needs of the EU wine sector.  
Building on these recommendations, the European Commission published a draft legislative 
reform on March 28, 2025. This proposal introduces targeted amendments to key legislative 
frameworks, including the Common Organisation of the Markets (Regulation 1308 /2013), the 
Strategic Plans (Regulation 2021/2115), and the Aromatised Wines Regulation (Regulation 
251/2014). 
Copa and Cogeca welcome the Commission’s swift response in addressing the challenges 
facing the wine sector and hereby present their position on the proposed “wine package” 
legislative reform. 
 
 
Policy options  
 
1. Article 1, paragraph 1 - (Re)planting authorization – amending Article 62 
(3), Regulation (EU) No 1308/2013 
a. The Commission proposes to amend Article 62(3) to exempt valid 
authorisations granted before January 2025 from the administrative penalty 
in the event of non-use. 
b. Furthermore, it is proposed that the validity of replanting authorisations be 
extended to eight years from the date of issuance, with the condition that 
they are not subject to an administrative penalty in case of non-use.  
c. → Copa and Cogeca believe that both measures will contribute to the more 
efficient management of production potential in the Member States (MS) 
by providing winegrowers with the flexibility  to take the necessary time to 
assess market trends and determine the optimal timing for planting new 
vineyards. These adjustments to planting and replanting authorisations 
will support the stabilisation of the wine market and facilitate the 
alignment of wine production with market demand. 
d. → Moreover, Copa and Cogeca believes that the same principles  
concerning the exclusion form the administrative penalty  should be 
extended to replanting authorisations granted before January 1, 2025 (as 
defined in Article 66 of the CMO Regulation).

e. → Furthermore, Copa and Cogeca  believes that in order to ease the 
counting and  of valid plating and replanting authorisations in the EU, and 
the lives of winegrowers, it will be useful to fix a set calendar date for each 
year on which the authorization  are due to expire  for that year . This date 
should be set at July 31st of each year. 
f. → Lastly, Copa and Cogeca  would like to take this opportunity to simplify 
the administrative hurdles that winegrowers face in the process of 
replanting and of being granted the authorisation, by proposing to extend 
as the norm, the so -called “simplified procedure” for which after the 
grubbing-up of a vineyard, the replanting authorisation is automatically 
granted by the regional authority without the need for the farmer to file the 
administrative request. This procedure is already in place in different 
regions, and it is possible a ccording to article 9 of Delegated Regulation 
2018/274. Copa and Cogeca also points out that the maximum period for 
apply for replanting authorisation is 5 [five] years, to which it would be 
added 8 [eight] years of validity of the authorisation, according to the new 
proposal from the Commission.  
g. → Copa and Cogeca  fully support the exemption from administrative 
penalties for valid authorisations granted before January 2025, as well 
as the proposed extension of replanting authorisation validity to eight 
years, and asks to extend the exemption from the administrative penalty 
to replanting authorisations granted before January 2025  
h. → Copa and Cogeca asks for a set date within the calendar year on which 
the expiration of new and replanting authorisation take effect. This date 
should be set at July 31st of each year.  
i. → Copa and Cogeca requires that a simplified procedure for replanting 
authorisation (as defined in article 9 of Delegated Regulation 2018/274) 
to be fixed as the norm. 
 
2. Article 1, paragraph 2 - National vineyards increase – amending Article 63, 
Regulation (EU) No 1308/2013 
a. The Commission proposes to amend Article 63 to introduce the possibility 
of additional limitations on new planting authorisations in regions where 
national or European crisis measures—such as distillation, green harvesting, 
and grubbing up—have been implemented. 
b. The proposal provides Member States with the possibility to impose 
geographical restrictions on where new authorisations may be used, 
specifically within the same regions where crisis measures have been 
enacted.  
c. Furthermore, it is specified that production potential must be adjusted to 
align with market demand. 
d. → Copa and Cogeca  believe that the proposed modifications to Article 63 
are well -structured and beneficial for market stabilisation. However, it is 
important to note that regions not benefiting from crisis measures (such as 
distillation, grubbing up, or green harvesting) s hould also have the 
flexibility to limit, or even set to zero, the increase in vine area. 
e. → Copa and Cogeca support the amendments to Article 63 concerning 
the possible limitations on new planting authorisations in regions that 
have activated crisis measures in the wine sector.

3. Article 1, paragraph 3, point b - Limitation of yield at regional level for 
replanting - amending Article 66(3), Regulation (EU) No 1308/2013 
a. The Commission introduces the possibility for Member States to impose 
conditions on granting replanting authorisations under Article 66(3). These 
conditions would include using the authorisation within the same 
geographical area were grubbing up has occurred and restricting the use of 
only those varieties and production methods that do not increase average 
yields.  
b. → Copa and Cogeca  argue that imposing limitations on yields and 
production techniques aimed at restricting yields would be 
counterproductive and hinder innovation within the wine sector. Such 
limitations could stagnate the development of more efficient production 
techniques and the introduction of new, more competitive vine varieties. 
New vines, by their nature, are more productive than older ones. Moreover, 
from an economic perspective, restricting vineyard yields would reduce the 
profitability of wine production, as per-hectare costs would rise, potentially 
stifling entrepreneurship. Additionally, the proposal fails to specify a 
benchmark against which yields would be measured. 
c. →  Copa and Cogeca  oppose any conditions related to yield limitations 
for replanting authorisations and request the deletion of paragraph 3, 
point (b) of Article 1 in the legislative proposal, which amends Article 
66(3) of Regulation (EU) No 1308/2013. 
 
4. Article 1, paragraph 5 - Dealcoholized wines - amending Article 119(1), 
Regulation (EU) No 1308/2013 
a. The Commission introduces additional definitions for the categories of low - 
and no -alcohol wines: "alcohol -free" if the alcoholic strength does not 
exceed 0.5% by volume; "alcohol-free 0.0%" if the alcoholic strength does not 
exceed 0.05% by volume; and "a lcohol-light" if the alcoholic strength is 
above 0.5% by volume but at least 30% below the minimum alcoholic 
strength of the category prior to de-alcoholisation.  
b. → Copa and Cogeca have concerns regarding the translation of the terms 
"low" and "light" into national languages. These terms may convey different 
meanings, potentially extending beyond alcoholic strength to encompass 
nutritional or organoleptic properties of the product.  
c. → Moreover, there is ambiguity in the definitions for products with an 
alcoholic strength that fall between 30% below the minimum alcoholic 
strength of the category and the actual alcoholic strengths of the category. 
Further clarification is needed on how to define this gap. 
d. → To assure that mandatory particulars of the labelling on wines are only 
repeated once on the label, and by so doing prevent any risk of doubling the 
costs of labelling, it is proposed to insert the following sentence to Art. 119 of 
the CMO Regulation : “Products referred to in points 1 to 11, 13, 15 and 16 of 
Part II of the Annex VII marketed in the Union or for exports shall contain 
the following compulsory particulars at least once in the labelling and 
presentation:” 
e. → Furthermore, since new labelling rules are inserted with the reform, and 
considering previous experiences with unclear timelines, it is suggested to

insert a transitional period for the new rules. Thus, the following wording 
should be inserted: “ grapevine products up to and including the 2026 
harvest year may be labelled in accordance with [Article 119(1) of Regulation

until stocks are exhausted”. 
f. → Copa and Cogeca request additional clarification on how to define the

g. → Copa and Cogeca also requires specifying that mandatory particulars

5. Article 1, paragraph 6 - Labelling - amending Article 122(1), Regulation (EU) 
No 1308/2013 
a. The Commission introduces delegated powers to further define the use of 
electronic means on wine labels and to adapt to technological 
developments. Specifically, it is clarified that electronic means may be 
represented by a pictogram or symbol rather than by written words.  
b. → Copa and Cogeca welcome this proposal, as it enhances legal clarity in 
the interpretation of Article 119, improves consumer access to information, 
and removes potential barriers within the Single Market. We call for the 
swift adoption of the secondary legislation deriving from this amendment.  
c. → Copa and Cogeca asks that wines intended exclusively for exports to 
third countries should not be subject to mandatory labelling requirements. 
Since the wines in question are intended for marketing in third-country 
markets, only the legal provisions regarding the labeling of the destination 
country should apply . This provision would greatly ease administration 
procedures and considerably reduce costs.  
d. →  Copa and Cogeca support  the amendment to Article 122(1) of 
Regulation (EU)1308/2013. 
e. → Copa and Cogeca requires the exclusion form the mandatory labelling 
requirements for those products that are aimed at export to non -EU 
third countries.

include, in addition to Interbranch Organisations, recognised P roducers’ 
Organisations 
b. → While supporting the underlying approach, Copa and Cogeca note that 
the High -Level Group’s recommendations explicitly state that Producers’

managing Geographical Indications ” should all be granted tools to

7. Article 1, paragraph 8, point (a) and (b) - National payment for distillation, 
green harvest, and grubbing up - amending Article 216, Regulation (EU) 
No 1308/2013 
a. The Commission proposes a reform of the management and financing of 
crisis measures in the wine sector—specifically distillation, green harvesting, 
and the introduction of grubbing -up measures. The reform would allow 
national funds to be used to support these crisis interventions. 
b. The annual budget allocated to green harvesting and distillation would be 
capped at 20% of the national resources dedicated to the wine sector. For 
grubbing-up plans, payments would be limited to the actual cost of the 
operation, while including an incentive to encourage participation.  
c. → Copa and Cogeca strongly support this initiative, considering that crisis 
measures—particularly grubbing -up plans —are essential to address the 
short-term needs of the market . However, EU funds should also be used in 
addition to national ones to finance crisis measures.  
d. → Concerning the yearly sectorial budget limitations imposed to the 
financing of distillation and green harvest, Copa and Cogeca  ask to 
increase it to 30%. Considering that the current legal setting allows for a 
Member State to dedicate up to 15% of its national sectorial budget to gren 
harvest and distillation measures,  it is logical that each  one of the 
measures (distillation and green harvest) can benefit from a 15% budget 
each, thus a total of 30%. 
e. → Copa and Cogeca support  the possibility for MS to finance directly 
crisis measures and requires the possibility to use EU funding to co -
finance crisis measures. 
f. → On the budget limitations on the measures of distillation and green 
harvest, Copa and Cogeca ask to increase the yearly threshold to 30% of 
the national sectorial budget.  
 
8. Article 1, paragraph 8, point (c) - Crisis measures activation - amending 
Article 216, Regulation (EU) No 1308/2013 
a. The activation of crisis measures, as listed in Article 216, would be 
accelerated through the submission of a substantiated communication to 
the Commission, without the need to proceed via secondary legislation.  
b. → Copa and Cogeca believe that shortening the time required to activate 
crisis measures will significantly enhance the EU wine sector’s capacity to 
respond swiftly and effectively during justified, contingent crisis situations. 
Faster activation will increase both the effi ciency and responsiveness of 
such measures.

c. → Copa and Cogeca strongly support the possibility for Member States

9. Article 1, paragraph 9 – Dealcholised sparkling wine - amending Article 
216, Regulation (EU) No 1308/2013 
a. The Commission introduces several derogations to the oenological

10. Article 3, paragraph 1, point a - Wine tourism - amending Article 58 (1), 
Regulation (EU) No 2021/2115 
a. The Commission proposes to extend access to the financing of wine tourism 
measures not only to recognised Interbranch Organisations, but also to 
recognised Producer Groups managing Geographical Indications. 
b. → While Copa and Cogeca  strongly support the broadening of access to 
wine tourism support programmes under the National Sectorial Plans, we 
believe that the list of eligible actors should be expanded further. In line 
with the recommendations of the High -Level Group, recognised P roducer 
Organisations, together with  cooperatives and independent enterprises, 
should also be included. A wider range of actors involved in wine tourism 
would enhance the promotion of the sector, its terroir, and its products. 
Cooperatives and individual e nterprises, in particular, possess in -depth 
knowledge of the local territory, its history, and the wines, and are often the 
direct organisers of tourism activities. 
c. → Copa and Cogeca support the proposed amendment and call for the 
inclusion of recognised Producer Organisations, cooperatives, and 
independent enterprises among the actors eligible to access wine 
tourism support. 
 
11. Article 3, paragraph 1, point b - Restructuring & Conversion measures – 
amending Article 58(1), Regulation (EU) No 2021/2115 
a. The reform proposes to allow Member States to introduce conditionality for 
accessing vineyard restructuring and conversion support, aimed at 
ensuring that such operations —whether through varietal conversion, 
relocation, replanting, or vineyard management techniques—do not lead to 
an increase in yield. 
b. → Copa and Cogeca consider that imposing limitations on yield and 
production techniques would be counterproductive and detrimental to 
innovation in the wine sector. Such restrictions risk stagnating progress 
toward more efficient practices and the developmen t of new grape 
varieties. By definition, new vines are typically more productive than older

ones. From an economic perspective, yield limitations would reduce the 
profitability of winegrowing (by increasing per -hectare costs) and 
ultimately discourage entrepreneurship. Furthermore, the legislative text 
lacks a clear benchmark for measuring yield increases. 
c. →  Copa and Cogeca  therefore oppose any yield -related conditionality 
linked to access to restructuring and conversion measures. We request 
the deletion of point (b) of Article 3 of the legislative proposal, which 
concerns the amendment of Article 58(1) of Regulation (EU) No 2021/2115. 
 
12. Article 3, paragraph 1, letter c - Promotion – amending Article 58(1), 
Regulation (EU) No 2021/2115 
a. The Commission proposes to introduce a non-renewable five-year time limit 
for promotional activities in consolidated markets for wines with 
Geographical Indications (GIs). 
b. → While Copa and Cogeca support the legislative change in recognition of 
promotion as a vital tool to strengthen market presence, we regret the 
introduction of a strict time limitation. Many EU wine producers have been 
active in key export markets for well o ver five years, and continued 
promotion is still essential—particularly in the current context of structural 
crisis and the urgent need to both consolidate existing markets and 
develop new ones. 
c. →  Copa and Cogeca support the move toward a more flexible approach 
to promotional activities for EU wines in consolidated markets and call 
for the removal of any fixed time limitation. 
 
13. Article 3, paragraph 2, letter a and c - Investment - amending Article 59, 
Regulation (EU) No 2021/2115 
a. The Commission is proposing to facilitate the access to finance for 
investments to recognized Producers Organisations under Art. 59 
concerning Investments in several dimensions of the wine sector 
(processing facilities, infrastructure, marketing, enhance sustainability, 
innovative products).  
b. → Copa and Cogeca strongly support this initiative, as broader access to 
financing will enhance the competitiveness and sustainability of wine 
enterprises. However, we believe that cooperative enterprises should also 
be included in this amendment, as recommended by the High-Level Group. 
While cooperatives may represent larger entities, they are composed of 
small and micro enterprises that have chosen to join forces to remain 
competitive. Excluding cooperatives from investment support would not 
only undermine this business model but also discriminate against the very 
small-scale producers it represents. 
c. →  While supporting the proposed amendments to Article 59 of 
Regulation (EU) 2021/2115, Copa and Cogeca call for the explicit inclusion 
of “cooperative enterprises ” among the actors  mentioned in the new 
text of Article 59 (Article 3, paragraph 2, letter a, c  of the Commission 
proposal).

14.  – Flexibility in the management of projects  
Member States should be allowed to activate certain flexibilities in the implementation of 
sectoral interventions, in line with the provisions of Delegated Regulation (EU) 2023/1225 
(Article 5) and Implementing Regulation (EU) 2023/1619, which have provided for special 
flexibility measures in the wine sector in 2023. The Member State would activate such 
flexibilities in justified cases of need, under the close supervision of  the European 
Commission, but without having to go through the lengthy procedure of issuing secondary 
legislation. 
Such measures should draw inspiration from Article 2 of Commission Delegated 
Regulation (EU) 2021/374 and Article 5 of Delegated Regulation 2023/1225, for example:  
- Adjust planned operations according to a simplified procedure (such as changes 
without prior approval). 
- Allow partial implementation of plans in duly justified cases (sometimes the 
predefined implementation timetable cannot be followed) and payment of single 
actions.  
- Possibility to increase European public support (percentage of EU contribution) to 
the national budget for supporting planned measures in the viticultural sector 
(investments, restructuring and conversion, promotion, and other actions listed in 
the national plan). 
- Greater flexibility in the implementation of projects in terms of timing and 
achievement of objectives. 
o Postpone the deadline for planting authorisations for 12 months (in justified 
cases of need) 
 
 
 
Conclusion 
Copa and Cogeca  express strong support for the changes proposed by the Commission, 
acknowledging the importance of these measures for stabilizing and sustaining the EU 
wine sector in the short, middle, and long term.  
However, we defend the enlargement of the scope of certain proposals  of the 
Commission’s text to include actors such as small and micro enterprises  who join a 
cooperative to benefit from funding and promotional opportunities. Furthermore, Copa 
and Cogeca call for the removal of certain restrictions, such as time limits for promotional 
activities in consolidated markets and yield limitations  linked to restructuring and 
conversion measures. Finally, we urge greater clarity  regarding the definition of certain 
(dealcholised) wine categories and the removal of ambiguities in the proposed oenological 
practices, in order to support innovation and enhance the competitiveness of the European 
wine sector.

Proposals for increasing available resources of the InvestEU Programme and

Preamble 
As highlighted in the ELTI 2024 Strategic Outlook, “InvestEU has been an important milestone 
in the implementation of the EU budget, by extending the mechanism of budgetary guarantee 
and by replacing multiple existing instruments by one single mechanism with the InvestEU 
guarantee Fund and the InvestEU Advisory Hub complemented by the InvestEU Project Portal”. 
Additionally, the Interim evaluation of the InvestEU Programme, emphasizes the need to 
“ensure continuity between the current and forthcoming MFF by building on the existing legal, 
contractual and operational framework to avoid a  ‘stop and go’ approach (which can damage 
long-term planning and investor confidence)”. 
More generally, the Commission should promote a “one-stop-shop” model, based on a single set 
of rules, leveraging the EU open architecture and adhering to the principle of subsidiarity to ensure 
proximity to final beneficiaries by relying on National Promotional Banks and Institutions’ (NPBIs) 
deep understanding of local investment needs and stakeholder networks. This would apply to 
InvestEU and other EU programmes. 
Against this backdrop, any improvements to the InvestEU Programme within the current 
Multiannual Financial Framework (MFF) should: 
• Envisage a reasonable increase in EU financial resources to facilitate a smooth transition 
to the next  MFF (Section I)  for which ELTI advocates for budget continuity for the 
InvestEU programme in the next MFF; and  
• Introduce simplifications to a llow for faster implementation and more effective of all 
financial products backed by InvestEU, while maintaining relevant safeguards and reporting 
requirements (Section II).

efficiency of the InvestEU guarantee. However, further improvements are needed to (i) increase 
the size of the guarantee, (ii) ensure proportionate access to additional resources for implementing 
partners other than the EIB Group, (iii) maximise the impact of administrative burden reduction. 
In the light of these objectives, we propose the following a set of amendments.

amount of guarantee made available under the EU compartment  to all IPs . These actions would contribute to address the “cliff edge effect” (amounts of 
supported projects will sharply decrease after 2024) in line also with the Conclusions and findings of the Interim evaluation of the InvestEU Programme, Mario 
Draghi’s report on the future of European competitiveness, and the EU competitiveness Compass.

Justification 
If proposal #1 cannot be accepted, proposal #2 should at least be accepted to ensure adequate distribution of the guarantee among implementing 
partners.   
 
The proposed amendment to InvestEU’s guarantee allocation are essential to address the "cliff edge effect" expected between 2 024 and 2027, ensuring a 
fairer distribution of financial capacity among all Implementing Partners (IPs). While the European Commis sion has acknowledged the issue and identified 
three sources of enhancement of the guarantee capacity —an increase of €2.5 billion from surpluses and reflows, an “enhanced combination” with legacy 
instruments, and a strengthening of the Member State compartment—the total allocation does not respect the 75%-25% split between the EIB Group and all 
other IPs. With the Commission expecting the network of IPs to increase from 17 to 24, it is clear that €0.6 billion  of additional guarantee (corresponding to 
12.5% of the total increase of resources) made available to other IPs (than the EIB Group) is insufficient. To ensure equitable access and maximise InvestEU’s 
impact, we propose (i) reallocating 25% of revenues from all legacy instruments to other IPs and (ii) fully utilizing €2 billion of expected reflows to double the 
InvestEU guarantee to €5 billion. Additionally, as InvestEU implementation requires approximately 18 months (from the Call for expression of interest until the 
guarantee agreement is amended), swift adoption of the amending regulation is crucial to avoid delays , and possibly non-legislative measures from the 
Commission. These measures will provide continuous financial support to all IPs, ensuring a balanced and effective deployment of InvestEU resources.

Justification 
 
As also highlighted by section 6.5 of the Interim evaluation of the InvestEU Programme, the complexity of InvestEU still hinders its full potential. Operations 
in strategic sectors such as defence, space and cyber-security, face strict limitations for transactions over EUR 10 million, which need to comply with criteria 
regarding ownership and suppliers, including those in third countries. For such operations, final recipients must have their executive management in the EU 
and ensure that risk mitigation measures apply to all suppliers, such as telecom equipment vendors and service providers. These restrictions hinder InvestEU-
backed operations in strategic sectors, ultimately harming  the development and adoption of advanced/digital technologies in Europe, which lacks relevant 
players and financial resources compared to global competitors such as the U.S. and China. To foster financing and investment operations in strategic sectors, 
it is suggested to exempt such operations from current requirements in duly justified circumstances, thus enabling European companies to grow, ultimately 
reducing reliance on non-EU technologies.

Justification 
 
The proposed change to reduce the reporting obligations is welcome. However, the threshold of €100 000 is likely to benefit mainly microenterprises, which

also in line with the Commission’s objective to reduce reporting burdens by at least 35% for SMEs, as recently announced in the Communication on 
Implementation and Simplification.

7. Simplification of InvestEU management reporting requirements 
Legal reference 
Proposal for a Regulation (EU) 2025/0040 (COD) (Omnibus II Regulation)  
Article 1 –Paragraph 20 new letter (b) 
Current text  Proposed Amendment 
N/A 
 
Paragraph 20, a new subparagraph letter (b) is added as follows:   
 
‘(b) in paragraph 2, a second subparagraph is inserted as follows: 
 
‘In accordance with Article 160(2) of the Financial Regulation , 
implementing partners may be exempted from providing to the 
Commission a management declaration and a summary of the final audit 
reports and of controls referred to in paragraph 1, letter (c) and (d), 
Article 158 of the Financial Regulation’.’ 
Justification 
 
Article 160(2) of the Financial Regulation states that the Commission “shall rely” on Implementing Partners’ rules, systems, and procedures for implementing 
budgetary guarantees. However, as highlighted in sections 6.1, 6.5, and 6.6 of the Interim Evaluation of the InvestEU Programme, the European Commission 
still imposes burdensome reporting requirements on Implementing Partners (IPs), financial intermediaries, and final recipients. 
In addition to the existing risk, financial, operational, progress, and State aid reporting, Implementing Partners are still required to provide the European 
Commission with additional annual reporting documentation. These “management reporting requirements” consist of: 
- A Management Declaration of Assurance confirming the correct implementation of Union funds; 
- A summary of the final audit reports and controls carried out by the Implementing Partner on InvestEU operations; 
- Audit conclusions from an independent audit body resulting from an agreed upon procedure drawn up in accordance with internati onally accepted 
audit standards. 
This approach disregards the outcome of the Pillar Assessment process, which aims to assess and validate Implementing Partner s’ rules, systems, and 
procedures (known as “pillars”) concerning: 
- Internal control systems; 
- Accounting systems; 
- Independent external audits; 
- Management of financial instruments. 
Finally, it should be noted that certain InvestEU Advisory Partners are already exempt from the obligation to produce a summary of audit controls due to their 
status as “Member State Organisations”.

InvestEU simplification proposals 
List of non-legislative measures to be implemented by the European 
Commission (EC) 
1.  Governance and approval process of InvestEU operations: 
• Non-legislative action: i) change to the InvestEU Guarantee Agreement – General Conditions, PART 
B – Approval Process (Article 5 to 9), and ii) IT development of the InvestEU Management Information 
System (MIS) , and iii) shorten the timeframe required for sustainability proofing summary 
submission, in advance of an Investment Committee (IC) meeting. 
Proposed change:  
i) Implementing Partners shall have the possibility to complete their internal approvals of new 
operations prior to the conclusion of the Policy Check/Investment Committee approval, 
provided that the approval by the IP’s Governing Body is granted on conditional basis, i.e. 
subject to the EU Guarantee being approved by the Investment Committee. This would 
streamline and shorten the approval process as it would remove the need to coordinate the 
timelines of the IC meetings and IP’s Governing Bodies meeting , which in some case can 
significantly delay the approval process.  
ii) Submission dates related to the C onflict of Interest (CoI)  and Guarantee Request Form 
(GRF) should remain separate in the MIS system, allowing Implementing Partners (IPs) more 
time to complete the required documentation for the IC meeting.  
iii) The IC has recently started requesting that the sustainability proofing summary be 
submitted five weeks in advance of the IC meeting, instead of the previous two -week 
deadline. This presents a significant challenge for Implementing Partners, as it does not  
align with an efficient project approval timeline, particularly in relation to the availability of 
the Environmental and Social due diligence final results. It thus means that these extra 
weeks need to be factored into the approval plan, which slows down the process. 
Implementing Partnes would therefore welcome a return to the previous two -week 
submission timeframe, or if possible, a one -week deadline, which would better 
accommodate the operational constraints of the deal approval process. 
Simplifying the InvestEU approval process would directly benefit project promoters and final 
beneficiaries by allowing Implementing Partners (IPs) to act more quickly.  
2. InvestEU reporting: 
2.1 Management Reporting Requirements 
• Non-legislative action: change to the InvestEU Guarantee Agreemen t – Annex III, PART B – Annual 
Management Reporting Requirements. 
• Proposed change: the European Commission should allow Implementing Partners (IP) to sign one 
unique Management Declaration of Assurance (MDA) for all instruments implemented under 
indirect management for both the internal (e.g. InvestEU) and external (e.g. NDICI) action , and 
uphold the principle of Article 160(2) of the EU Financial Regulation stating that the Commission

shall fully rely on rules, processes, and procedures of the IPs for the implementation of budgetary 
guarantees. 
2.2 Operational and Financial Reporting Requirements 
• Non-legislative action : change to i) the InvestEU Guarantee Agreement, Annex II – Operational 
Reporting and the Methodology for InvestEU Key Performance and Monitoring Indicators , and ii) 
allow reporting in more established file formats. 
• Proposed change: The operational report has both an extremely detailed and complex content and 
a very high frequency (every two months, possibly every three months for the “progress report”). The 
burden is transferred on project promoters, since most data are provided by the latter . This 
complexity results in frequent queries to project promoters (data requests, meeting s, etc. ) and 
regular requests for corrections between the Commission and the IPs. 
Against this backdrop: 
o Content: the number of items collected in the report should be significantly reduced. i) The list 
of indicators must be limited to the most relevant ones — specifically, those shared by the 
Commission with external stakeholders, such as the InvestEU Advisory Board. ii) Parts B and C 
of the operational reporting are intended for the Commission’s internal use; therefore, they 
should be heavily streamlined, focusing only on items that justify their burden on project 
promoters. Additionally, the methodology for  calculating figures should be expanded and 
improved by adding real-life examples based on the first three years of implementation of the 
Programme. Finally, all reported items must be based exclusively on signed operations. 
o Frequency: frequency of operational report and cash -flow forecast report should be reduced 
from every six months to once a year  as proposed in the first draft Ominibus II Regulation . 
Progress reports should be eliminated as there seems to exist no clear legal basis for them. 
2.3 Structured formats  
• Non-legislative action : change to the InvestEU Guarantee Agreement, art. 16.1 (claims form), 
annexes II Operational Reporting (art. 1.1), III.A Financial Reporting (f), IV Risk Reporting) (art. 1.1), 
X State Aid Reporting (art. 1) 
• Proposed change: the EC has insisted that reports in a structured format be provided in a different 
file type (XML, not XLS). We understand the logic for wishing to collect data in XML format (it enables 
a more rapid absorption and analysis), but there is an insensitivity to the difficulties being generated 
for the reporting teams downstream. For example,  as part of the Commission’s migration to XML 
reporting, one of InvestEU’s Excel -based reports was converted into 11 XML -based reports, 
necessitating 11 submissions of what once was previously one submission. Due to the semi-annual 
frequency of this report (unless the proposed switch to annual reporting is approved), Implementing 
Partners will have to go through this process twice a year, resulting in a total of 22 submissions. In 
addition, transferring data in the XML format (which is not widely used), requires specific staff 
training, which in turn generates heightened “key person risk” for when such staff members become 
unavailable.  
Against this background: Given the particularity of the XML format, skills for which are scarce, we 
would recommend that the EC allows IPs to continue to report also in “traditional” formats, and a 
specialised team within the EC to be tasked with converting and centralising reports into the desired 
XML format.

4. Risk management and pricing of the InvestEU guarantee:  
• Non-legislative action: i) revision of the current risk management approach based on the role of the 
Technical Assessment Unit (TAU) to align it with that of external action programs (e.g. NDICI/EFSD+), 
ii) limit the expected data that IPs relay as part of the risk report to the minimum required. 
• Proposed change:  
i) The option to calibrate the exact level of First -Loss Cover (FLC) for a loan, as can be done 
currently under some IPs’ EFSD+/UIF facilities, would be helpful in the future, in particular 
for operations that may require a very low level of FLC to be financed. At present, for at least 
one IP the InvestEU Guarantee Agreement (GA) mandates a set of fixed FLC levels . One 
element that “fixes” the choice of FLC to one of these levels is the way in which the guarantee 
fee is presented - with a different table of fee s (differentiated by WAL and risk rating of the 
loan) per each of the mandated FLC levels. Instead, EFSD+ agreements offer one such fee 
table (differentiated by tenor and risk rating ) wherefrom the fee figure applicable to a loan 
can be applied pro -rata for any FLC level (up to a maximum specified level); f or these 
facilities, this single pricing grid is offered by the EC’s risk advisory unit based on an up-front 
best-guess estimation of the portfolio mix  (including the likely split by risk ratings and FLC 
levels). Such flexibility would be useful for instance in cases where an FLC level of less than 
the minimum option in the GA would be needed.   
ii) The EC has started requiring detailed loan repayment calendars as part of risk reports. At 
the time of including a new loan in a guarantee portfolio, such calendars are indicative and 
subject to change (depending on disbursement conditions, prepayments, va riations in the 
repayment timeline) and as such create the burden of having to update multiple loans with 
multiple data points in every iteration of the risk report. In the absence of customised 
systems /automatic reporting, this can grow into a significant task. Instead, the EC is invited 
to consider if it would be sufficient that the risk report presented data reflecting the changes 
in the risk rating of the loan and the changes in outstanding balances over the reporting 
period. 
5. Early Detection and Exclusion System (EDES):  
• Non-legislative action : IT development of the European Commission's Early Detection and 
Exclusion System  
• Proposed change : IPs are required to exclude final recipients/financial intermediaries that are 
subject to a decision on exclusion contained in the published early detection and exclusion system 
database (EDES). However, such database is currently only available “manually” (man-to-machine 
interface) and cannot be inserted easily in IPs’ compliance processes. The EDES database should 
be publicly available (like the list of Restricted Persons) or at least to be sent to IPs upon each 
update.

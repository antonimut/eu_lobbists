Public consultation on “Trade measure addressing the negative trade-related effects of global 
excess capacity on the EU steel sector”. 
 
This submission is filed by Federacciai, the Italia n Steel Association. With roughly 130 members, 
Federacciai represents 99% of Italian crude steel production. Its membership accounts both producers and 
transformers, among which steel tube, steel wire and forging producers.  
Italy is the first EU country for EAF steel product ion (90% of national crude steel production in 
2024) as well as for energy efficiency in steelmaki ng (+40% vs. EU average). Furthermore, Italy is the  
second largest EU market, in terms of crude steel p roduction, consumption and direct employment. 
Specifically, the full Italian steel sector current ly employs over 70.000 direct workers. In addition,  in 
2024 Italian crude steel production ranked 12 
th at global level.  
As shown in World Steel Figures, the EU steel marke t is the largest worldwide market in terms of 
imports. Concerning the impact by EU member states,  Italy is the first country in the Union for steel 
imports from third countries. Specifically, in 2024  Italy imported roughly 26% of EU third countries 
imports, followed by Belgium (17%) and Spain (13%).  
Two main issues have driven EU import surge in rece nt years: the increasing excess of capacity 
and trade flows distortion resulting from the application of the 232 US in the steel sector.  
Global excess capacity, a long-lasting issue of the  steel sector, has become massive and structural 
over the last decade. According to OECD, in 2024 gl obal overcapacity was around 600 million tonnes, 
more than 30% of crude steel production in the same  year. Moreover, it is projected to further improve  to 
more than 700 million tonnes by 2027. Global excess  capacity has twofold effects; it restricts EU 
producers’ activity on both the domestic and foreign markets.  
In addition to global excess capacity, EU imports s trongly increased after the introduction of 232 
US, in April 2018. In order to prevent trade deflec tion, since the 1 st  February 2019 the European 
Commission established a safeguard measure, accordi ng to tariff-quota rate, evaluated on traditional 
flows in the period 2015-2017, above which a 25% du ty is levied on imports. Since the introduction of 
steel safeguard tariff-quota have been increased by almost 25% due to the application of the liberalization 
principle as established by WTO, while European dem and entered in a downward trend. Consequently, 
the disconnection between quota levels and European  demand has made this tool ineffective to protect 
and promote EU steel capacities. Issue which has be come more severe with the recent imposition of a 
50% blanket import tariff by the US administration.   
As stated in the “Steel and Metals Action Plan”, th e new instrument should guarantee a highly 
effective level of protection to the EU steel marke t. As argued above, the current steel safeguard is not 
sufficient to prevent trade flows in a contest of g lobal steel capacity and distortion of trade flows to EU 
market. In order to address the negative trade-rela ted effects of global excess capacity on the Italia n and 
European steel sectors, Federacciai believes that t he following principle should be considered in 
designing the new post-safeguard mechanism:  
 Products concerned: all steel products subject to 232 US should be included. In addition, steel 
products should be classified according to their qu ality, differentiating non alloy and other 
alloy products, on the one hand, and stainless products, on the other hand.

Since the recent extension of the 232 US to steel p roducts derivatives, the new instruments 
should be applied immediately also to all derivativ e products classified in chapter 73 of 
Custom Code. In particular:  
 73 01 20: Angles, shapes and sections 
 73 02 30: Switch blades, crossing frogs, point rods and other crossing pieces 
 73 07: Tube or pipe fittings (for example, couplings, elbows, sleeves), of iron or steel  
 73 08: Structures (excluding prefabricated building s of heading 9406) and parts of 
structures (for example, bridges and bridge-section s, lock-gates, towers, lattice masts, 
roofs, roofing frameworks, doors and windows and th eir frames and thresholds for 
doors, shutters, balustrades, pillars and columns),  of iron or steel; plates, rods, angles, 
shapes, sections, tubes and the like, prepared for use in structures, of iron or steel 
 73 11: Containers for compressed or liquefied gas, of iron or steel  
 73 12: Stranded wire, ropes, cables, plaited bands, slings and the like, of iron or steel, not 
electrically insulated  
 73 16: Anchors, grapnels and parts thereof, of iron or steel  
 73 25: Other cast articles of iron or steel  
 73 26: Other articles of iron or steel 
Furthermore, the list should be revised according t o the inclusion of other steel derivatives in 
the US legislation.  
 Import market share: Import market shares need to b e identified from a period before global 
excess capacity spilled over through trade (before the first China steel export explosion of 
2015/2016) when capacity utilization was viable as a condition for profitability and 
sustainability. Hence, import market share should b e defined according to the level experienced 
in the period 2012-2013. In particular, 15% for fla t steel, 5% for long steel, and 15% for 
stainless steel of EU current demand.  
 Design: In order to account for the interest of all  operators in the steel value chain, the new 
framework should be designed according to tariff-ra te quota (TRQ) to avoid direct costs on 
steel-users (blanket percentage tariff) or risk of shortage (quantitative restriction in the form of 
absolute quota).  
 Application to third countries: In order to guarant ee the fully efficiency of the new mechanism, it 
should be applied to all third countries without ex ception, even in case of trade agreements, 
concluded and/or under negotiation.  
 Duty rate: The out-of-quota duty rate should be defined to an appropriate level to effectively limit 
the imports to the targeted market shares. In order  to prevent discrepancies between the 232 US 
duty rate, currently set at 50%, and the new trade mechanism, the EU out-of-quota duty level 
should be set in a way to mirror the US one, so as to avoid trade deflection. 
 Entry in force: Since the current steel safeguards is no longer sufficient to protect the Italian and 
European steel industry given the structural excess  of capacity and the destabilization of the 
multilateral trade system by recent US measures, th e new mechanism should enter in force as 
soon as possible, for example from 1st  January 2026.  
 Adjustment: In case of significant changes in EU ma rket demand, the new instrument should be 
flexible enough to allow quota volumes to be adjust ed in order to maintain a constant market 
share for imports.

ArcelorMittal contribution to the public consultation on “Trade measure addressing the 
negative trade-related effects of global excess capacity on the EU steel sector” 
 
ArcelorMittal is one of the world’s leading integrated steel and mining companies with over 125,000 
employees, a presence in 60 countries and primary steelmaking operations in 15 countries. We are 
the largest steel producer in Europe , among the largest in the Americas, and have a growing 
presence in Asia through our joint venture AM/NS India. In Europe, ArcelorMittal directly employs more 
than 60,000 people and has steel -making operations in 8 countries. We make a broad range of high -
quality finished and se mi-finished steel products ranging from flat products (including sheet and 
plate), long products (including sections, sheet piles, rails, quality wire rod, rebars, bars, billets and 
blooms), pipes, tubes and wires (including industrial wires, cold heading quality wires, wire ropes, wire 
fencing, nails, and steel fibers) serving a diverse wide range of key industries such as automotive, 
construction, energy, high-tech and defense.  
We appreciate the leadership shown by the European Commission in announcing in its Steel and 
Metals Action Plan the intention to replace the steel safeguards by a “long -term measure 
providing a highly effective level of protection” . The EU is indeed the only major steelmaking region 
seeing a constant and worrying decrease in capacity existentially threatening the future of the EU steel 
industry. In the last few years, EU steel production has already fallen sharply - from 160 million tons 
(MT) in 2018 to aro und 130 MT in 2024. Without effective policy action, this downward trend is 
expected to accelerate, leading to a growing gap between domestic production and EU demand - a 
trajectory that would clearly amount to the de -industrialization of the European stee l sector. This 
situation is threatening the backbone of the European manufacturing system, as we produce inputs 
vital for the strategic independence of the EU, provide many quality jobs across many EU member 
states and are key for the success of the EU’s green transition.  
Some of the options considered by the European Commission in its questionnaire are promising 
since they will enable to put in place the “long term measure providing a highly effective level of 
protection” as so urgently needed by the EU steel industry and as announced in the Steel and 
Metals Action Plan. Although it is evident that an upfront across-the-board import tariff of 50% would 
be a highly effective and easy to manage measure, we understand the need to balance the interests of 
several stakeholders involved and thus support a Tariff -Rate Quotas (TRQ) system. However, for it to 
be “highly effective” , in our view it needs to integrate at least the following five essential aspects:

• The quota must set import market share at 15% for carbon flat and 5% for carbon long with 
no possibility of carry-overs. 
• A persuasive and effective tariff of 50% that is applied from the first ton above quota.  
• Apply to the full range of steel products with a strict “melted and poured” regime.  
• No preferential treatment for third countries is allowed. 
• A long-term system with a review clause. 
 
The above-mentioned measures should absolutely not be diluted with compromises that will weaken 
the measures. This tool shall not allow for any more leakage and circumvention. This new trade 
measure must be adopted as soon as possible , well ahead of the 30 June 2026 expiry date of the 
current steel safeguards! Below we provide a short explanation for each of the above -mentioned 
elements.  
 
1. Import market share set at 15% for carbon flat and 5% for carbon long with no possibility 
of carry-overs. 
For the TRQ system to be “highly effective” , import levels should be limited to a market share that 
supports sustainable capacity utilization and the long-term viability of the EU industry. We believe 
quotas need to correspond to import market shares of a period when demand was equally weak as 
today (2012 -2013, just before China’ steel export surge) but where due to lower import shares the 
industry benefitted from healthy capacity utilization (at least 80%). Therefore, with reference to 2012-
2013, the TRQ a llowed import volumes shall be half the level of 2024 imports. This would concretely 
mean import quota to be set at 15% import market share for carbon flat (around 11 million tons of 
imports allowed) and 5% for carbon long (around 2 million tons of imports allowed). These quotas 
then need to be redistributed among the individual product categories within each segment pro rata 
the share of the imports of the product category in the total imports of the segment.  
It is important that no carryover of unused quota is allowed to prevent short -term import surges 
that distort the EU market . Under the current EU steel safeguards, some allowed carryovers 
generated significant import surges (e.g., HRF in Q4 2023 and 2024, HDMC Q2 2024 etc.). This must 
be avoided to ensure the effectiveness of the measure. 
 
2. A persuasive and effective tariff of 50% that is applied from the first ton above quota.  
To ensure the TRQ system acts as a credible deterrent against excessive imports, a 50% tariff applied 
from the very first ton above the quota is essential . This is not an arbitrary figure - it aligns with 
measures implemented by other major economies (United States, India, Indonesia, Canada and 
more). A 50% tariff above quota in the EU would be both effective and justified considering the cost 
asymmetries European producers face, sending a clear signal that the EU is serious about defending 
its industrial b ase. A weaker or staggered tariff would fail to deliver the impact needed, allowing 
continued erosion of market share and profitability for EU producers.

3. Apply to the full range of steel products with a strict “melted and poured” regime.  
The new measure should apply to  all products currently covered by the existing safeguard 
measures, with some adaptations for specific categories (e.g., 4B and 3A/3B) . In addition, an 
equivalent protection must be put in place as soon as possible  for all steel intensive finished and 
semifinished goods. A broad product scope is essential: if protection targets only a narrow set of 
finished products, unfair trade practices are likely to shift further down the value chain, eroding the 
measure’s effectiveness. Furthermore, to prevent circumvention and maintain the integrity of the new 
measure, the origin of steel products must be determined by where the steel is melted and poured, 
not merely where it is processed or traded. This requirement ensures that only genuinely domestic or 
qualifying foreign production counts towards TRQ eligibil ity. Without a strict melted and poured rule, 
there is a hi gh risk of transshipment or minimal processing being used to bypass quota restrictions, 
rendering the measure ineffective and undermining trust in its enforcement. To ensure the highest 
level of protection  and avoid circumvention  through minimal processing in lower -duty countries, 
producers shall declare where products were melted and poured . This information is needed to 
determine whether more stringent duties than those set by the new tool - such as anti -dumping or 
countervailing duties – should apply.  
 
4. No preferential treatment for third countries is allowed. 
Any form of preferential treatment for third countries could risk creating loopholes , ultimately 
putting at risk the overall measure. Until a comprehensive global trade agreement is in place that 
imposes comparable sustainability and fair market requirements like it was envisaged with the EU-US 
initiative around a Global Arrangement on Sustainable Steel and Aluminum (GASSA) announced in 
2021, all imports (including those from so -called “developing countries”) should be subject to the 
same conditions. 
 
5. A long-term system with a review clause. 
To provide long-term certainty for investors, workers, and industrial planners, the TRQ system must be 
designed as a long-term solution rather than a temporary fix  (such as the current safeguards ). The 
challenges facing the European steel industry, ranging from structural overcapacity in third countries 
and trade distortions to the low carbon transition, are long -term in nature. We believe that such a 
measure can be designed as WTO compliant thereby preserving the integrity of this important 
institution. While a long-term measure is essential for predictability, it should include a well-defined 
review clause  to ensure adaptability in response to changing market conditions, technological 
progress, or geopolitical developments. Temporary measures create instability and discourage the 
investments needed for decarbonization and modernization, whereas a long -term regulatory 
framework - with periodic, structured reviews - will underpin a sustainable, competitive, and resilient 
European steel industry. 
Once again, data from EUROFER is unambiguous: EU steel production continues contracting — from 
160 MT in 2018 to 130 MT in 2024 — and, without decisive policy intervention, this downward trend will

widen the gap between domestic production and EU demand. This de -industrialization trajectory 
requires an urgent policy response. 
Many steel companies, including ArcelorMittal, have already announced temporary and permanent 
idling or reduction of primary production as well as unfortunately delays in decarbonization 
investments. The lack of appropriate trade protection not only endang ers thousands of jobs but also 
stalls critical decarbonization projects needed for key climate targets. Furthermore, de -
industrialization in Europe would not reduce global emissions - high carbon steel would still be 
produced and consumed outside of the EU, often with a much higher carbon footprint than the current 
EU industry. This is why we strongly urge the European Commission and all EU institutions to 
continue this course of action and adopt a long-term measure providing a highly effective level 
of protection!

AEGIS EUROPE POSITION ON THE CALL FOR EVIDENCE  
FOR AN INITIATIVE FOR MEASURES  
TACKLING THE NEGATIVE TRADE-RELATED EFFECTS OF EXCESS CAPACITY 
  
On 18 July 2025 the Commission launched a call for evidence on an initiative for tackling 
excess capacities in the steel sector. Interested parties, across the EU steel supply chain, were 
given until 18 August to reply to the questionnaire on a trade measure.  
In the section of the call for evidence addressing the problem that the Commission initiative 
aims to tackle, reference is made to overcapacities in general. The call for evidence refers to 
energy intensive sectors such as steel, metals, and chemicals describing them as the backbone 
of the European manufacturing system as they produce inputs vital for whole value chains and 
provide many quality jobs across many EU member states. However, the problem of 
overcapacities is not limited to energy intensive sectors. Overcapacities is a phenomenon which 
is plaguing all manufacturing sectors.  
AEGIS Europe, an alliance of EU manufacturers, agrees that manufacturing is the backbone of 
the EU economy. Europe always has been, and must remain, an innovative manufacturing 
power-house based on market economy principles. Our values in the EU are built on this. Those 
values have underpinned our trade policy both multilaterally and bilaterally.  
Any Commission proposal on overcapacities must not only apply to the Steel sector but to all 
sectors suffering from overcapacities.

The root causes of overcapacities are many.  
In China, the problem is an economy that is planned. It is planned to stimulate exports, it is 
planned to impede imports, it is planned to dominate globally important sectors like, for 
example, steel and aluminium despite an absence of iron ore, bauxite or renewable power. This 
has consequences for all steel and aluminium downstream industries like shipbuilding, rail, 
bicycles, electric vehicles and the supply chains that supply these sectors. In addition, China 
gives priority to sectors including new generation information technology, biotechnology, new 
energy, new materials, high-end equipment, new energy vehicles, green and environmentally 
friendly products, aerospace and marine equipment. China subsidises production and rewards 
achieving the central plan over profitability. As stated in the 14th five year plan: ‘It is crucial to 
build more secure, resilient, and efficient modern supply chains not subject to disruptions and 
outside interference.’

In other countries, the problem is the subsidised desire to have national champions and shiny 
new production facilities focussed on export , but divorced from domestic needs. Sometimes 
these facilities are built with Chinese state backed subsidies.  
On 7 April 2025, the Commission initiated a system to monitor import trends so that surges in 
imports can be identified quickly and action taken. The CN Codes surveyed by the Commission 
are limited. Even so, the August survey shows significant increases in  chemicals and 
Machinery, which are suffering from massive over capacities. It appears that the Commission 
is identifying import surges but not proposing the tools needed to address the surges in imports.  
 
 
Finally, the Commission proposal is to be based on Article 207 TFEU. Article 207 defines the 
EU’s competence on the common commercial policy. This policy is common to all member 
states. In addition, it must be common to all sectors. The Commission cannot be seen to 
discriminate in relation to one sector when many sectors are suffering from the same issue.  
 
AEGIS Europe therefore calls for an instrument to tackle overcapacities. This instrument:  
i) must not be limited to the steel sector; and  
ii) available independent of the Safeguard instrument.  
  
The instrument must the imposition of punitive tariffs, or any equivalent measure, at the border, 
on goods sourced in third countries which have given support directly or indirectly to the rise 
of economically irrational overcapacities.

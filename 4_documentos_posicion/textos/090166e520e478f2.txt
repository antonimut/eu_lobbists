   Luxembourg, August 18th, 2025           EUROMETAL response to the public consultation on the new steel trade measure    EUROMETAL represents steel distributors, traders, and service centres across the EU, who connect domestic and international supply with over one million downstream users in manufacturing, construction, and related sectors. Our members play a systemic role in securing access to steel for SMEs and strategic industries alike.  A new trade measure, if well designed, can strengthen market stability, investment confidence, and supply chain resilience. However, the impact will depend heavily on the details of its design and implementation.  • Quota design: A combination of country-specific and residual TRQs offers predictability for established flows while maintaining flexibility for distributors sourcing across multiple origins. Quarterly administration with carry-over would smooth deliveries and avoid artificial shortages or spikes. Out-of-quota tariffs should be moderate and flexible. Excessively high levels could harm legitimate trade and limit access to specialised grades not produced in the EU.  • Melted & poured rule: Introducing such a rule would strengthen transparency, traceability, and alignment with wider EU sustainability policies such as CBAM, CSRD, and ESPR. It would reduce circumvention and reinforce fair competition, but its implementation must remain proportionate, avoiding unnecessary burdens for operators handling complex international supply chains.  • Predictability and lead time: Above all, rules must be communicated clearly and well in advance of entry into force. Distributors need time to adjust sourcing strategies, while downstream industries require predictability to absorb the transition without disruption. Poorly calibrated or rushed measures risk distorting supply chains, penalising reliable suppliers, and creating volatility — especially for SMEs.  • Steel derivatives and fabricated components: EUROMETAL stresses the growing risk of circumvention via imports of derivatives and fabricated products. These flows already displace demand for traditional steel products, distorting competition and undermining the role of EU-based distributors and processors in the value chain. Addressing this risk must be part of the new framework.  • Competitiveness and global context: For EU distributors, competitiveness depends on stable and diverse access to steel both inside and outside the EU. Protective measures must not become overly rigid or punitive, as this could provoke retaliation from third countries, damage long-standing trade partnerships, and restrict access to competitively priced material.

   To be effective, the future instrument must therefore be balanced, transparent, and easy to administer, particularly for SMEs. It should replace current measures with rules that are simple to apply, fair to all operators, and responsive to market developments — ensuring continuity, reliability, and competitiveness across the European steel value chain.  
 
 EUROMETAL is the European Federation of Steel, Tubes and Metals Distribution & Trade.  Founded in 1950 alongside the creation of the European Community for Coal and Steel, EUROMETAL has evolved into a member-oriented service provider. EU steel distribution & trade account in EU for 3.500 companies, mostly small and medium sized enterprises, providing jobs to 100.000 people in the EU.  As a systemic player in the supply chain, steel distribution accounts for 60% of all steel supplies to more than one million end users in EU manufacturing industries and EU construction related sectors. EUROMETAL actively advocates for its members, providing a platform for collaboration, insights, and representation at the European level to ensure the sustainable growth and competitiveness of the steel distribution and trading sectors.

ORGALIM FEEDBACK ON PLANNED TRADE MEASURE ADDRESSING THE NEGATIVE 
TRADE-RELATED EFFECTS OF GLOBAL EXCESS CAPACITY ON THE EU STEEL SECTOR 
 
 
Orgalim welcomes the opportunity to provide feedback on the planned new trade measure to address the 
negative trade-related effects of global excess capacity on the EU steel sector. 
 
Orgalim represents Europe’s technology industries, comprised of 770,000 innovative companies spanning 
the mechanical engineering, electrical engineering, electronics, ICT and metal technology branches. 
Together they represent the EU’s largest manufacturing sector, generating an annual turnover of over 
€2,755 billion, manufacturing one-third of all European exports and providing 11.6 million direct jobs. 
 
Our industries are entering their third consecutive year of economic downturn, having experienced a 5.6% 
contraction in annual turnover, a 1.2% decline in employment and a 3.2% decline in investments in 2024, 
according to 0ur Spring 2025 Economic Report1. These figures are a clear sign that the framework 
conditions for successfully continuing to manufacture technology in Europe are worsening at a worrying 
rate. The increasing cost pressures to which our industries are exposed, often resulting from ill-conceived 
and burdensome EU policies, are becoming progressively unbearable and will ultimately drive new 
investments in manufacturing outside the EU.  
 
As outlined in the Draghi report, Europe’s innovation and industrial competitiveness benefit from the 
presence of the full industrial value chain. Especially in the technology industries, innovation occurs in 
ecosystems combining expertise from both the upstream and downstream parts of our value chains. 
Hence, it is important for the competitiveness of Europe’s technology industries to keep European steel 
production viable and competitive. 
 
The EU steel safeguards, due to expire on 30 June 2026, constitute a clear example of ill-conceived EU 
policies that negatively affect the competitiveness of our industries. Orgalim, alongside other downstream 
EU industry associations2, has consistently opposed the EU safeguard measures on steel imports since 
their provisional imposition in 2018. The safeguard measures unduly restrict our access to steel at 
competitive prices and generate a huge administrative burden for our industries, pushing up the overall 
costs of manufacturing in the EU3. As of 1 January 2026, the negative impact of steel safeguards will

steel for our industries (irrespective of whether it is sourced from EU suppliers or from third countries), 
resulting in a massive loss of competitiveness both on the EU market as well as on other markets to which

challenging international scenario, with a huge spike in global uncertainty caused by the tariff policies of

For all of the above reasons, Orgalim wishes to express its concern and opposition in principle to the 
planned new trade measure to replace the steel safeguards. Such new measure could further restrict our 
access to steel from third countries at competitive prices, pushing up manufacturing costs for our 
industries and hurting our competitiveness. Downstream industries manufacturing finished products in 
Europe remain fully exposed to international competition both on the EU market (where finished products 
are rarely subject to trade defence measures and are not under the scope of CBAM) as well as on third 
country markets to which our industries export. Technology companies in Europe simply cannot afford to 
shoulder further restrictions on access to steel at competitive prices, both on the EU market (where prices 
are already expected to increase due to the phaseout of free ETS allowances) and on international markets 
(where prices are already expected to increase due to CBAM). Moreover, the current approach has enabled 
players from countries such as China to circumvent upstream restrictions by dumping downstream 
products instead. This not only undermines the intended effect of the trade measures but also allows these 
countries to capture more added value, further distorting the level playing field and exacerbating the 
competitive pressure on EU-based manufacturers 
 
We fully appreciate the current threats to which the EU steel industry is exposed due to global 
overcapacity and the resulting need to restore a level playing field. Europe's ability to innovate is partly 
supported by the presence of a complete industrial value chain within its borders. European technology 
companies often work closely with domestic steel producers, which means that Europe's technology 
industries benefit from having a strong steel production capacity within Europe. 
 
It should be noted that our industries are also faced with a clearly uneven level playing field when 
competing with highly subsidized competitors in third countries, whose products are placed onto the EU 
market without fully complying with EU standards and regulations. Competitors are using artificially cheap 
steel – driven by the state aid supported global excess capacity – to push out European producers using 
European steel, especially SMEs. We cannot support another unilateral EU trade defence measure that 
focuses exclusively on the upstream part of the steel value chain, without any solution for the resulting 
cost pressures and loss of competitiveness that will be experienced by downstream industries. Instead, we 
encourage the Commission to make stronger use of other existing trade defence instruments in response 
to illegal practices by trade partners to restore a level playing field for both steel producers and 
downstream industries. Also, Orgalim supports increased use of multilateral, plurilateral and bilateral 
measures to secure a level playing field for European companies on non-EU markets.

We therefore call on the European Commission to refrain from introducing such measure, while standing 
ready to work with the Commission to explore other possible solutions that would restore a level playing 
field for EU steel producers without unduly hurting the competitiveness of downstream industries.  
 
Should the Commission still wish to proceed with the introduction of the new measure, it is absolutely 
essential that it is designed in a way that improves the key flaws of the current system of steel safeguards 
and minimises negative impacts for Europe’s technology industries. In this respect, Orgalim recommends 
the following: 
 
• Steel products originating from close partner countries such as Norway, Switzerland and the UK 
must be fully excluded from the scope of the new measure. 
 
• The current “mixed” system of country-specific and global quotas of the steel safeguards should 
be replaced with a system of global quotas without maximum caps for specific countries of origin. 
The quotas should be large enough to ensure sourcing flexibility for steel users, avoiding the 
situation where certain quotas are exhausted within days from the beginning of the quota period. 
This is very common with the current system of steel safeguards and often forces downstream 
manufacturers to pay high out-of-quota tariffs.  
 
• Quotas should also be administered on an annual basis, instead of a quarterly basis, and out-of-
quota tariffs should be kept as low as possible. 
 
• Trade defence measures should be targeted specifically at products for which dumping practices 
are actually occurring. Products that are neither manufactured within the EU nor of interest to 
European industry to produce do not require protective measures 
 
• Following a careful impact assessment, the Commission should consider surgically extending the 
scope of the measure to a restricted set of downstream steel-intensive products to prevent 
circumvention. 
 
• In general, administrative requirements for companies to import under the quotas should be 
minimised, in line with the EU’s simplification agenda. 
 
Finally, we take note that the Commission does not intend to conduct a full impact assessment of the new 
measure. Given the impact that the new measure could have on our industries, we believe that this is not 
acceptable. We urge the European Commission to refrain from implementing such measure until a full 
impact assessment is conducted. The impact assessment should consider the cumulative impact of the 
future measure, CBAM, the phaseout of ETS free allowances and other existing trade defence measures 
already in place on steel products on the competitiveness of Europe’s technology industries.

EU should implement an inclusive trade policy to enhance the 
competitiveness of the entire Europe's steel value chain 
 
Steel represents the backbone of Europe with the European Coal and Steel Community 
being the ancestor of the European Union. Today, steel is in decline, from 160 million 
metric tonnes of crude steel production in 2018 to 136 million tonnes in 20221. The 
European Commission is stepping in to support the steel industry, aiming to strengthen 
the industrial base, support the steel workforce and decarbonise the sector for a 
climate-neutral future.  
 
The EU Safeguard on steel adopted in 2018 provided a much-needed protection for 
the steel industry, but it failed to embrace the entire value chain. As a result, the steel 
packaging industry has been squeezed between the increasing cost of raw materials, 
which generate higher costs of production, and the downstream competition exerted 
by imports of semi-finished metal packaging products and finished goods originating 
in third countries.  
 
Steel packaging is a circular champion and key part of the metal value chain with one 
of the highest recycling rates among materials, 82% in 20232. Packaging is one of the 
highly valued and most visible metal products on the market today. Steel cans enable 
continuous engagement with European consumers and provide high-value scrap for 
recycling and secondary raw materials. The sector is critical for the circularity of the 
EU economy and the decarbonisation efforts of the steel industry.  
 
In 2024, MPE members imported around 15% of steel outside the EU due to different 
reasons, among them a lack of available material from the European suppliers and 
significantly reduced service levels. MPE members already experience a reduced 
capacity and increased lead time from EU steelmakers, which has led to a shortage of 
certain specialised material critical to our industry.  
 
Today, steel packaging is facing severe competition from cheaper but less circular 
materials (like plastic or multilayer cartons) and increased imports of steel packaging 
and components from outside the EU, often coming from subsidised economies with 
large overcapacity in metal packaging production.  
 
With the tariffs imposed by the US, the risk of trade diversion of semi-finished steel 
products and finished goods is ever-increasing. Trade flows traditionally destined to 
the US from third countries will be partly diverted to Europe, circumventing the 
safeguard measures and increasing the pressure on the downstream steel industry,

The European Commission's ambition to save steel should encompass the entire steel 
value chain, including steel packaging and components. Introducing measures solely 
for the steel industry, without protecting downstream industries, would raise steel 
prices in Europe and allow steel packaging producers outside Europe to enter the 
market and benefit from significantly lower raw material costs – steel represents 
between 40% and 70% of production costs depending on the packaging format. This 
will threaten the survival of the European metal packaging industry, which would also 
negatively impact European steel producers through loss of volumes. 
 
A tailored and inclusive approach will offer a mutually beneficial solution, safeguarding 
the EU steel industry while minimising negative impacts on steel users and ensuring 
fairness among users. Any trade measure on steel should aim to support the 
competitiveness of the entire value chain by including all product groups within the 
following codes CN 7310 and CN 8309. 
 
Moreover, the new trade measure should set a clause giving priority access to the 
European downstream industry purchasing steel produced in the EU. Export of raw 
steel materials should only be allowed when the European demand for steel is satisfied. 
It should also set requirements for the steelmakers to maintain excellent service levels 
and reduce lead times for European customers.   
 
Furthermore, the EU should envisage a mechanism that would allow the steel industry 
to directly benefit from the income generated by any duties imposed to further support 
the circularity and decarbonisation of the entire steel value chain.  
 
MPE remains committed to working with the European Commission and all 
stakeholders to find the most suitable solution for an inclusive trade policy that can 
enhance the competitiveness of the entire Europe’s steel value chain, including 
downstream industries like packaging.  
 
 
 
Metal Packaging Europe is the united voice of Europe’s rigid metal packaging industry 
(steel and aluminium), bringing together manufacturers, suppliers, and national 
associations. Our members produce over 140 billion cans annually, supporting 66,000 
direct jobs in Europe across 350 production sites. Metal packaging actively contributes 
to the Circular Economy and supports numerous value chains and applications, like 
beverages, food, fish, chemicals, paints, aerosol and others.

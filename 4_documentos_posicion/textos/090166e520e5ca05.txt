SUPPORTING THE EUROPEAN STEEL  
INDUSTRY THROUGH SAFEGUARDS
The European Carbon and Graphite Advanced Materials Association (ECGA) welcomes the opportunity  
to respond to the Commission’s targeted consultation on future trade measures for the EU steel sector.  
As  a key  upstream  supplier  of  graphite  electrodes  and  recarburisers  (constituting  30%  of  Europe’s 
synthetic graphite demand), we underscore the strategic interdependence between our industry and  
the steel sector. We support robust, long-term safeguards to address the escalating crisis driven by  
global overcapacity, trade diversion, and unfair practices. 
Graphite  electrodes  are  strategic  for  decarbonisation,  yet  they  are  being  increasingly 
manufactured in China and India
According  to  a  report  from  the  Joint  Research  Centre  (JRC)  published  in  2023,  the  preferred 
technological route for the production of steel by 2030 will be recycling of steel scrap through electric  
arc furnaces (EAFs), which require graphite electrodes. Thus, the JRC expects the demand for graphite  
electrodes to go from 1 million tonnes a year in 2020 to 5.7 million tonnes by 2050. 
However, most of the supply growth is poised to happen in China, which has invested heavily in recent  
years in the manufacturing of both needle coke, the main feedstock for graphite electrodes, and the  
electrodes themselves. This evolution is apparent on the following graphs, based on Wood MacKenzie  
data.

These Asian investments in electrodes production have fragilised the EU electrodes industry, which was  
already  weakened  by the domestic drop in  demand caused by a weak EU  steel industry. EU steel  
consumption is now  forecast to decline  by 0.9% this year. This follows declines of 8% in 2022, 6% in  
2023 and 1.1% in 2024.  As a result, many decarbonisation projects announced during recent years by  
steel makers in the EU have been either postponed or shelved . Such investments would have increased  
the European demand for graphite electrodes, since these are used for steel recycling. 
ECGA and its members consider that the currently erratic US trade policy could be the final nail in the  
coffin for the EU steel industry. The series of haphazard increases in US tariffs on EU steel (25%, raised to 
50% in June 2025, now 15%) have intensified the deflection of global surplus steel toward the EU.  
EUROFER estimates that 27 million tonnes of steel previously destined for the US now threaten the EU  
market. This represents a quarter of European annual demand. Meanwhile, China continues to ramp up  
its steel exports. Last year, China exported over  100 million tonnes, an increase of 17% in comparison  
with 2023.
The current crisis of the EU steel industry, if not resolved, will inevitably result in factory closures and  
job losses on the suppliers side. Therefore, European manufacturers of graphite electrodes consider that 
it is in the interest of the EU to protect its steel industry and the suppliers of that industry, which are  
characterised by a low environmental footprint and proper health and safety rules at work.

Safeguarding the EU steel industry will stabilise the graphite electrodes market
Over the past decade, the graphite electrodes market has experienced extreme volatility, particularly  
within the Ultra-High Power (UHP) segment essential for EAFs used in steel recycling.
Before  the  COVID-19  pandemic,  global  supply  struggled  to  meet  demand.  But  pandemic-induced 
lockdowns triggered a sharp decline in steel demand, which cascaded into plummeting demand for  
graphite electrodes and created a significant market oversupply. Temporary instabilities were induced  
by the EU's cycle of economic reopening and renewed restrictions.
Since the end of the pandemic, graphite electrode prices have dropped sharply due to persistent global  
oversupply. This imbalance is mainly driven by a significant slowdown of the construction sector in  
China,  which  has  reduced  domestic  demand  for  both  steel  and  graphite  electrodes.  The  resulting 
overcapacity  has  led  to  increased  exports  of  both  dumped  steel  and  dumped  electrodes  into 
international  markets,  including  the  EU.  In  the  EU,  those  exports  have  met  an  already  struggling 
domestic production due to high energy prices. 
While  the  European  Commission  has  instated  temporary  anti-dumping  duties  to  protect  European 
producers of graphite electrodes, the underlying structural problem remains unsolved. As of this year,  
China’s demand for steel has not fully recovered yet, and its excess capacities keep on flooding the EU,  
even more so now that the US is heavily protectionist. Moreover, in the long term, the global market for  
graphite electrodes is predicted to remain oversupplied, mainly due to China. 
Since ECGA and its members do not see their competitive position improving any time soon, we are in  
favour of any measure that would stabilise the EU demand for recycled steel and our own products too.

Measures supported by ECGA and its members
We support the following measures for the EU steel industry:
A Tighter caps for quotas
• Set TRQ volumes at ~50% of 2024 import levels (approx. 16-18 Mt).
• Apply TRQs universally to all third countries without exemptions.
• Enforce a strict "melted and poured" origin requirement.
• Reduce residual quota caps to maximum 10% for high-pressure products (e.g., hot rolled coils).
B Dynamic adjustments
• Implement quarterly TRQ administration (no carry-over).
• Introduce automatic downward adjustment of TRQ volumes if imports exceed 25% market share 
for three consecutive months.
• Conduct quarterly reviews aligned with OECD steel capacity reports and shifts in US trade policy.
C Synergy with decarbonisation policies
• Full block imports of low-carbon steel in order to increase domestic electrodes capacities.
• Classify steel scrap as a strategic raw material and restrict its exports.

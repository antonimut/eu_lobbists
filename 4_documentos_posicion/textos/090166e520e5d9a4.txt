Consultation on Trade Measure Addressing the Negative Trade-Related Effects  
of Global Excess Capacity on the EU Steel Sector 
Arvedi Group Submission 
Introduction 
Finarvedi, representing the Arvedi Group - the leading Italian producer of carbon and stainless flat 
products and welded tubes - welcomes the opportunity to contribute to the European Commission’s 
consultation on the new trade measure for the steel sector. As a key player in Italy and Europe, Arvedi 
calls for a robust, highly effective long-term instrument to safeguard EU steel capacity, strategic 
autonomy, and the green transition, but highlights specific priorities rooted in the experience of producers 
of flat steel and tubes. 
1. Addressing the Steel Crisis: Underutilized Capacity and Structural Overcapacity 
EU steel production has reached critical lows, with capacity utilization plunging and production falling 
sharply in recent years. The underlying issue is structural, global overcapacity, projected to exceed 700 
million tonnes by 2027, which distorts trade flows and threatens the viability of European producers. 
Immediate, decisive action is essential to prevent further consolidation, closures, job losses and 
irreversible deindustrialisation.  
2. Trade Spill-overs: Market Distortion and Import Surges 
Existing safeguard measures have failed to deter surging imports, which now represent a persistent trade 
deficit for the EU. Import volumes have grown while domestic demand has shrunk, undermining the 
competitiveness of European steelmakers. Trade spill-over from global excess capacity, and recent U.S. 
blanket tariffs (50%), have intensified diversion into the EU market, necessitating a response that restores 
balance and prevents circumvention.  
3. Scope: Covering All Steel and Downstream Products 
The new measure must encompass the entire steel value chain. Coverage should include all basic steel 
products (carbon flat, long, stainless) and steel-intensive downstream goods such as tubes and fabricated 
products. This comprehensive approach is required to avoid trade diversion further down the value chain.

4. TRQ Structure: Restoring Viable Import Market Shares 
A well-designed tariff-rate quota (TRQ) regime should be introduced, per existing product category, 
aligned with market needs and capacity utilization targets. Key features should include: 
 Global quotas, with caps per exporting country, no country-specific subquotas.  
 Quarterly administration, with unused quota not transferable to subsequent periods. 
 Import market shares capped at levels supporting viable operations: 
a) Carbon flat products: maximum 15% 
b) Long products: maximum 5% 
c) Stainless products: maximum 15% 
d) Tubes: aligned with historic periods of stable EU demand and pre-excess capacity conditions 
5. Above-Quota Tariffs: Effective Deterrence 
Tariff levels applied above quota should be set at a minimum of 50%, mirroring the U.S. Section 232 
approach, and enforced from the first tonne above quota. Such a rate represents the necessary deterrent 
to trade deflection and ensures out-of-quota imports do not undermine the EU market. Above-quota 
tariffs must cumulate with any existing anti-dumping or countervailing duties.  
6. Origin Rule: Melted & Poured 
The new regime must be built on a strict “melted and poured” rule of origin for all steel products and 
relevant downstream goods. This effectively prevents circumvention strategies such as minimal 
downstream processing or transshipment and has proven robust in the international policy framework.   
7. Non-discrimination: Uniform Application 
All third countries should be included, without exemptions for WTO developing countries or FTA partners, 
until a global arrangement ensures comparable standards and requirements. Preferential treatment 
would undermine the effectiveness and integrity of the measure.  
8. Review and Adjustment Mechanism 
An annual review process must accompany the instrument, assessing its effectiveness, quota levels, 
market conditions, and industry health. Flexibility to adapt in response to shocks or demand fluctuations 
is critical, ensuring the instrument remains effective and responsive to evolving market realities.  
9. Supporting Sustainability and Decarbonisation 
Capacity restoration and stability are prerequisites for enabling major investments in decarbonisation. 
Only a predictable, long-term framework will provide sufficient planning certainty for EU producers to 
lead on climate-neutral steelmaking, innovation, and strategic value chains.  
Conclusion 
Arvedi Group strongly supports the European Commission’s commitment to implement a new, highly 
effective trade measure, learning from the shortcomings of earlier safeguards. The instrument must be 
implemented in the shortest terms, restoring viable market shares, applying robust above-quota tariffs, 
covering all steel and relevant downstream products, and maintaining strict rules of origin, following the 
guiding principles advanced by EUROFER. This is essential not only for the survival and competitiveness of 
the EU steel industry but also for Europe’s strategic autonomy and ability to deliver a sustainable industrial 
transformation.

SCHP CR position on climate targets for 2040 
On 2 July 2025, the European Commission proposed, in accordance with Article 4(3) of Regulation 
(EU) 2021/1119 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 30 June 2021 establishing a 
framework for achieving climate neutrality and amending Regulation ( EC) No 401/2009 and 
Regulation (EU) 2018/1999 ("European climate legal framework"), the Union's climate target for 
2040. The proposal sets a target of  a 90% reduction  of GHG to  1990. The Chemical Industry 
Association of the Czech Republic ("SCHP CR") rejec ts this proposal and recommends that other 
trade unions in the Czech Republic, including the Czech political representation, take a reserved, 
even negative, position on the target. This opinion is not an expression of reluctance to decarbonize, 
but rather a reflection of deeper concerns about the competitiveness of the key industrial sector in 
the Czech Republic and  also in  the EU . Sector,  which is fundamental for economic stability , 
innovation and also for the technological development. 
The reason for rejecti on of the Commission's proposal is mainly the failure to comply with the 
procedure resulting from the provisions of Article 4(5) of the above -mentioned Regulation, which 
refers to the factors to be taken into account when setting the target for 2040. In our opin ion, the 
Commission took into account only some factors when setting the proposal, while ignoring the short- 
and medium -term social and economic impacts [in particular letters c), d) and h)] 1, the current 
situation of the industry in the EU [letter e)]2 and also the issue of an economically acceptable solution 
and technological neutrality [letters d) and f) 3]. We are missing in the commission's proposal 
comprehensive assessment of the impacts on employment in the chemical industry and downstream 
sectors, also the impacts on regional development in industrial areas and an analysis of the availability 
of technologies necessary for such an ambitious emission reduction. The proposal does not reflect the 
financial costs of implementing decarbonization or the specificities of individual member states. 
The proposal describes the need for follow-up legislative steps. We believe that strengthening energy 
efficiency, developing renewable sources, energy savings and the circular economy are still legitimate 
goals. However, the problem lies in the way how the targets will be fulfilled in terms of other legislative 
proposals from the Commission. The proposal itself announces that the 90% target will be followed by 
a package of measures – similar to the “case of Fit for 55” – in the sense of “Fit for 90”. Such measures 
will necessarily lead to further tightening of the EU ETS system and an increase of the price of emission 
allowances, which, in combination with the termination of free allowances for energy -intensive 
industry, will be liquidating. “Fit for 90” will also lead to pressure on energy prices, pressure on energy 
efficiency limits and on permit and operational emission values, which are not in line with technological 
neutrality and will lead to further increases in costs, which are already degrading the competitiveness 
of European products on world markets – unfortunately CBAM system cannot help with, if we do not 
produce for the worlds markets, we have no profitable industry. 
We can also expect the setting of higher targets for renewables, which are currently not being met in 
many respects due to the hesitation of investors and member states.4

The Commission declares that the achievement of the targets must be fair and take into account the 
specificities of member states. However, EU legislation is effective throughout the EU. Modification 
options when transposing directives are limited and non-existent when adapting regulations. Industrial 
countries without sufficient RES capacities and in less favourable climate conditions , will always pay 
the price for meeting the targets in the form of loss of competitiveness, higher inflationary pressures 
and job losses. In this regard, it is necessary to introduce a differentiated approach to meeting climate 
targets, that takes into account the specific starting positions of individual Member States and their 
economic structure - including the introduction of fair solidarity mechanisms and compensation for 
those, who bear a disproportionate burden. 
Setting the 2040 target in 2025 is premature. It is necessary to consistently evaluate the fulfilment of 
the goals in 2030 (according to the Fit for 55  package) and set realistically achievable  goals. It is 
necessary take into account investment cycles, amortization, RES production in the EU and the 
pressure of foreign competition. High goals cannot be achieved in a relatively short time frame without 
hastily closing down production sites in the EU. 
We therefore recommend that the EC and EP only consider setting climate targets for Europe 2040 
after assessing the fulfilment of the Fit for 55 targets, i.e. after 2030.

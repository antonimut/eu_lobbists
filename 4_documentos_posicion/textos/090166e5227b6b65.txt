Proposal for a Regulation of the European Parliament and of

 Enagás feedback  
Executive summary 
Enagás fully supports the establishment of a robust and forward-looking legislative 
framework to deliver the 2040 climate target. The company is committed to playing 
an active role in the decarbonisation of the European economy through the 
development of renewable hydrogen infrastructure, the transformation of existing 
assets, and the deployment of innovative solutions across the energy value chain. 
To ensure the success of the 2040 target, it is essential that the upcoming legislative 
proposals provide a stable, predictable, and enabling environment for investment. 
This includes recognising the strategic role of hydrogen infrastructure, leveraging 
existing assets such as LNG terminals, and supporting the integration of new energy 
carriers and carbon management solutions. 
Enagás stands ready to collaborate with EU institutions, Member States, and 
stakeholders to contribute to a secure, competitive, and climate-neutral European 
energy system. 
Positive feedback on the proposed Regulation 
Enagás welcomes the European Commission’s proposal to set a binding target of a 
90% net reduction in greenhouse gas emissions by 2040. This target is a crucial 
milestone on the EU’s path to climate neutrality by 2050. 
As a company deeply committed to the energy transition, Enagás has set its own 
climate ambition: to achieve net zero emissions by 2040 for Scope 1 and 2, and by 
2050 for Scope 3. This commitment is supported by a science-based decarbonisation 
roadmap aligned with the 1.5°C scenario, and includes a 50.4% reduction in 
operational emissions by 2030. These targets reflect Enagás’ determination to lead 
by example and contribute meaningfully to the EU’s climate goals. 
The company believes that setting the 2040 target now provides much-needed 
predictability for long-term investment decisions and reinforces the EU’s credibility in 
international climate diplomacy. It also sends a strong signal to industry and society 
that the transition to a low-carbon economy is irreversible and must be accelerated. 
Enagás supports the Commission’s emphasis on a pragmatic and flexible approach, 
which recognises the need for enabling conditions, technological neutrality, and a just 
transition. The company also values the role of industrial competitiveness and 
innovation as key drivers of decarbonisation, and encourages the EU to continue 
fostering a stable regulatory and financial framework to support these efforts.

Hydrogen infrastructure as a key enabler of the 2040 climate target 
The legislative framework to implement the 2040 climate target must recognise the 
strategic importance of hydrogen infrastructure in achieving deep decarbonisation 
across the European economy. 
While electricity grids will continue to play a central role, many industrial and 
transport sectors cannot be electrified and will rely on renewable hydrogen as a key 
energy vector. The development of a dedicated hydrogen network — including 
transmission pipelines, cross-border interconnections, and underground storage — is 
essential to enable sector coupling, support the deployment of renewable energy, 
and ensure a resilient and integrated energy system. 
Hydrogen infrastructure will be critical to reaching the 2040 climate target in a cost-
effective and inclusive manner. Its deployment should be considered a core enabling 
element of the post-2030 legislative framework, alongside other strategic 
components of the energy transition. 
Leveraging existing infrastructure to support decarbonisation 
Enagás’ existing infrastructure — particularly its network of LNG terminals in 
Barcelona, Cartagena, Huelva, El Musel, Bilbao, and Sagunto — represents a strategic 
asset for the EU’s decarbonisation and energy resilience objectives. These facilities 
are already integrated into the European energy system and are well-positioned to 
evolve into multimolecule hubs capable of handling a range of low-carbon and 
renewable energy carriers. 
The transformation of LNG terminals into platforms for hydrogen derivatives (such 
as ammonia and methanol), bioLNG, and CO2 handling will enable the flexible and 
efficient deployment of new energy vectors. This approach maximises the value of 
existing infrastructure, reduces the need for greenfield investments, and accelerates 
the transition to a climate-neutral economy. 
In particular, the repurposing of LNG tanks for ammonia storage and the use of waste 
cold from LNG regasification to support ammonia or CO2 liquefaction are examples 
of how existing assets can be adapted to serve future needs. These terminals also 
offer maritime access and technical capabilities that are essential for the development 
of import, export, and bunkering infrastructure for renewable fuels and captured 
CO2. 
Enagás believes that the role of existing infrastructure should be fully recognised in 
the post-2030 legislative framework as a key enabler of the 2040 climate target. 
Their integration into a forward-looking energy system will support the 
decarbonisation of industry and transport, enhance supply security, and contribute 
to a cost-effective and inclusive energy transition. 
About Enagás 
Enagás is the Transmission System Operator (TSO) for natural gas in Spain, 
responsible for the development, operation, and maintenance of the country’s gas 
transmission network. The company owns and operates liquefied natural gas (LNG) 
terminals in Barcelona, Cartagena, and Huelva, and holds majority stakes in El Musel 
E-Hub (Gijón), Bahía de Bizkaia Gas (Bilbao), and Saggas (Sagunto).

Enagás has been provisionally designated as the Hydrogen Transmission Network 
Operator (HTNO) in Spain, reflecting its strategic role in the country’s energy 
transition. The company is leading the development of the Spanish hydrogen 
backbone, a nationwide infrastructure designed to connect all peninsular autonomous 
communities and enable the decarbonisation of hard-to-abate sectors. 
Several segments of this backbone have been selected as Projects of Common 
Interest (PCI) by the European Commission, including the internal hydrogen 
infrastructure in Spain, the H2Med interconnections with France and Portugal, and 
two underground hydrogen storage facilities. These projects are eligible for co-
financing under the Connecting Europe Facility (CEF) Energy programme.  
In addition, Enagás is actively developing carbon capture, utilisation and storage 
(CCUS) infrastructure, beginning with the submission of the Pycasso project as a PCI, 
which aims to establish a cross-border CO₂ transport and storage corridor between 
Spain and France.

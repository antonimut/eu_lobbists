Position of the Grupa Azoty Group on the draft indirect target for reducing greenhouse gas 
emissions in the European Union for 2040 presented by the European Commission  
 
The Grupa Azoty Group (Grupa Azoty) analysed the results of the variant scenarios for setting an 
intermediate target for 2040 presented by the European Commission (EC) in February 2024, on the basis 
of which the EC estimated that the level of emission redu ctions of 90% compared to 1990 would be the 
most favourable scenario. We would like to emphasize that the scenarios for Polish, presented during the 
work on the update of the National Energy and Climate Plan, as part of the scenario with additional 
measures (WAM), set a much lower level of ambition, i.e. a reduction in emissions by 66% (without taking 
into account LULUCF) in 2040 compared to 1990. The Grupa Azoty  noted with great concern the 
differences in the assessment of achievable targets at the EU, national, regional and industry levels , 
and informed accordingly about its postulates in this regard. 
 
The Grupa Azoty assumes that the very definition of the intermediate target has been provided for in the 
European Climate Law 1 in order to support the achievement of the "net -zero" target set for 2050 by 
adjusting the key decarbonisation instruments (in particular, the EU ETS greenhouse gas emission 
valuation), the current perspective of which is mainly the 2030 target . According to the EC, therefore, 
setting an intermediate target for 2040 will be the basis - and the framework at the same time - for 
adapting decarbonisation instruments for the period after 2030  and adaptation of decarbonisation 
instruments for the post-2030 period.  
 
In this context , the Grupa Azoty raises legitimate concerns about the intermediate target for 2040 and 
the effectiveness and impact of the adjustments to the instruments appropriate to the new target, 
basing its assessments on the effects of their current operation, incl uding those resulting from the 
adjustment based on the 2030 target under the Fit for 55 regulatory package.  
 
With such an ambitious decarbonisation target for 2040, the alignment of regulatory instruments will be 
subject to significant challenges, both for national governments and the industrial sector in their dealings 
with EU authorities. The main disputed areas that may occur in this regard are:

The draft amendment to the European Climate Law,  includes a total of eighteen guidelines for the 
European Commission to be included in legislative proposals to adapt regulatory mechanisms accordingly 
– it is worth highlighting a few that the Grupa Azoty supports in principle:  
− sectoral flexibility across sectors to support the achievement of the objectives in a cost -effective 
manner; 
− technological neutrality, cost-effectiveness, economic efficiency and economic security, 
− the need to strengthen the global competitiveness of the EU economy, in particular small and 
medium-sized enterprises and the sectors most vulnerable to carbon leakage, in order to ensure fair 
competition; 
− changing the international situation and the efforts taken to achieve the long-term goals of the Paris 
Agreement, as well as the EU's support to its partners in tackling climate change and its impacts. 
 
These are key indications, and the Grupa Azoty expects real support measures that do not pose a serious 
risk of deindustrialisation, and this will result in maintaining and imposing further ambitious targets 
and obligations on energy-intensive industries, which are already in a situatio n where they are losing 
competitiveness to other regions of the world.

In view of the above expectations, the Grupa Azoty Group is calling for the following solutions , which 
should be included in the 2040 target: 
➢ taking into account the capabilities of the Polish economy in the negotiations on the 2040 target, 
including lower reduction ambitions resulting from the ongoing work on updating the National 
Energy and Climate Plan; 
➢ a revision of the Fit for 55 package to change the most unfavourable solutions for industry, 
including m.in:  
• maintaining the allocation of free greenhouse gas emission allowances under the EU ETS 
for sectors vulnerable to carbon leakage, while maintaining the introduction of CBAM 
fees from 2026; 
• abolishing the mandatory target for the share of renewable fuels of non-biological origin 
(RFNBO) in industry; 
• easing the qualification requirements for RFNBO hydrogen and low-carbon hydrogen; 
➢ a change in the rules of operation of the EU ETS mechanism consisting in the adoption of solutions 
rewarding the achievement of decarbonization measures (reward method, e.g. under tax 
mechanisms) and not creating burdens for the failure to implement these measures for industrial 
production using current technologies (the penalty method); 
➢ creating a mechanism to compensate for increased decarbonization costs for industrial sectors, 
which will be implemented with EU and not national funds; 
➢ dedicating funds to support decarbonisation to each of the Member States, in an amount 
adequate to the needs resulting from current emissions and the need to reduce them, as well as 
the need to maintain industrial production in individual sectors;  
➢ application of more lenient regulatory requirements in relation to industrial sectors strategic 
from the point of view of ensuring security, e.g. the fertilizer sector strengthening food security; 
➢ introducing border protection mechanisms to price the difference in full regulatory costs in the 
field of climate action in third countries versus the EU and to establish compensation fees on this 
basis;  
➢ wider enabling of the use of international carbon credits to make it more flexible for entities 
operating in the EU to implement climate and energy policy activities.

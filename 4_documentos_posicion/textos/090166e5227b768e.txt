 
 September 2025 
 
SSAB feedback on the European Climate Law 
amendment public consultation  
 
SSAB is committed to our transformation journey and is currently investing more than €5 billion to transform our 
Swedish steel production system. We will decommission the blast furnaces and coking plants, moving away from 
an energy mix based on coal, coke and electricity to mainly use fossil-free electricity to melt scrap and fossil-free 
sponge iron in electric arc furnaces. We do this based on the need to modernize our facilities, maintain our 
innovative edge and meet customer demand for decarbonized steels. We will eliminate about 10% of Sweden’s 
CO2 emissions with this investment. 
The transformation is a business strategy that holds great potential to not only strengthen individual companies 
but also contributes to EU objectives of innovation, competitiveness, climate, resilience, and securing an industrial 
base and jobs in Europe for decades to come. It is also an opportunity for companies to modernize their facilities 
and keep their innovative edge, rather than reinvesting and locking in existing/carbon-intensive technology. 
However, a transition to low carbon or fossil-free steel operation translates into high investments and risks in the 
development of new technology. The current steel industry context is also one characterized by uncertainty and 
global overcapacity.  
It is key that the EU stays the course on decarbonization, and ensures predictable, stable and ambitious climate 
policy. This includes an intermediate target of at least 90% GHG emission reductions by 2040 to provide a clear 
path forward and ambitious benchmark for the post-2030 EU climate framework and also continued focus on 
implementation of the Fit-for-55 package without delay.  
EU 2040 climate target  
SSAB supports an intermediate target of at least 90% GHG emission reductions by 2040. We welcome the 
Commission’s proposal to enshrine the 90% target into EU law. SSAB believes that focus and priority need to be 
placed on reductions in the EU, but has taken note of the flexibilities included in the proposal to which we would 
like to share the following remarks:  
International credits. The Commission refers to a possible limited role of international carbon credits as of 2036. 
The explanatory memorandum accompanying the legislative proposal outlines that such credits should not be 
used for compliance in the EU carbon market. We agree to this approach, which should also be included also in 
the actual legislative text. More broadly, there are also still uncertainties related to how such credits would be 
designed or used in practice, which raises further question marks on their role and suitability for reaching EU 
climate targets.  
Carbon removals. The iron and steel industry will be able to eliminate most emissions, but not all residual 
emissions, from e.g. alloys. The EU climate policy framework will need to take into consideration that residual 
emissions will remain in a 2040-perspective. Hence, there is need for acceptance of the fact that there will be a 
need to compensate for these in view of reaching climate neutrality. In the 2040 climate target proposal, one 
flexibility proposed is to consider “the role of domestic permanent removals under the greenhouse gas emission 
allowance trading system within the Union (‘EU ETS’) to compensate for residual emissions from hard to abate 
sectors.” In the steel industry, biomass is needed as a carbon source in fossil-free steel production. While carbon 
removals could contribute with some flexibility to the ETS, it should also be considered that companies/industries 
shifting to fossil-free ways of production will need biomass as a resource to produce high value-added products. 
In other words, even in fossil-free steelmaking there will remain a need for a (fossil-free) carbon source.

Therefore, negative emissions such as bio-CCS need careful consideration on timing for introduction and with 
constraints both for supply and use. The use of temporary carbon removals should not be allowed under the ETS.  
Enabling conditions and the climate policy framework 
It is key that the EU stays the course on decarbonization and climate policy. A 90% climate target by 2040 helps 
provide reassurance for companies and investors to proceed with certainty and sets an ambitious benchmark for 
the post-2030 EU policy framework. There is also a need for continued focus on implementation of the Fit-for-55 
package without delay. The EU ETS system is a cornerstone in the EU’s climate policy. The phase out of free 
allocations under the ETS, as decided in the Fit-for-55 package, should not be slowed down. Also, the definitive 
period of CBAM needs to start as planned in 2026, and focus must now be on making the instrument effective by 
concrete solutions to ensure a level playing field for CBAM goods exports and expand the CBAM scope to include 
steel-intensive downstream products. 
For SSAB there is no contradiction between decarbonization and competitiveness. However, there should be 
stronger focus on effectively implementing the enabling conditions that need to be in place to support the 
business case for the transformation of industry at a larger scale. As we see it, it is more important than ever to 
decisively implement the Clean Industrial Deal and Steel and Metals Action Plan with a maintained focus on 
aligning competitiveness and sustainability.  
About SSAB  
SSAB is a Nordic and US-based steel company. SSAB offers value added products and services developed in 
close cooperation with its customers to create a stronger, lighter and more sustainable world. SSAB has 
employees in over 50 countries. SSAB has production facilities in Sweden, Finland and the US. SSAB is listed on 
the NASDAQ OMX Nordic Exchange in Stockholm and has a secondary listing on the NASDAQ OMX in Helsinki.

The TÜV Association welcomes the European Commission’s efforts to streamline and simplify the 
EU’s digital regulatory framework. Clear, coherent, and predictable rules are crucial for 
strengthening the competitiveness of the European digital economy and ensuring the effective 
functioning of the Single Market. However, the Digital Omnibus should not be used as a means to 
deregulate and weaken the protection objectives and corresponding requirements, which must be 
adhered to.  
Simplifying without weakening protection levels 
We support the Commission’s intention to address fragmentation and ensure coherence across the 
acquis. Diverging obligations across EU digital legislation, such as different incident reporting obligations, 
are seen as an overly complex burden by many market actors. Harmonised application and consistent 
enforcement are indispensable to avoid regulatory inconsistencies and to provide businesses with the 
legal certainty needed for investment and innovation. 
This being said, it must be emphasized that simplification should reinforce, not weaken, Europe’s 
leadership role in digital legislation. The EU’s digital rulebook is designed to address the most severe risks 
to consumers, businesses and society. Its core principles – notably risk-based requirements and robust 
conformity assessment procedures – are essential preconditions for trust, legal certainty and for a future-
proof EU innovation hub. Weakening these mechanisms in the name of simplification would be 
counterproductive and risk undermining the credibility and global leadership of the EU’s digital 
governance framework. A strong EU framework creates trust among citizens and businesses, which in 
turn drives digital technology uptake.  
No extensive postponement of the AI Act 
As regards the current discussions on the AI Act, the TÜV Association calls for a timely application. We 
reject an extensive postponement as called for by various stakeholders. 
A delayed application of the AI Act would not only leave risks posed by AI systems unaddressed, but also 
hamper the quality-assured market placement of innovative AI systems, especially in high-risk areas. The 
agreed AI Act deadlines, as published in the EU’s Official Journal, ensure that both companies and 
designated authorities have legal and planning security to build up the necessary financial and human 
resources to ensure timely compliance readiness and a functioning AI market surveillance. If deadlines 
become negotiable, legislation becomes unpredictable, competition distorted, and apparent risks 
unaddressed – thus creating a harmful precedent.  
In our view, the expected delay in the availability of harmonised standards is by itself not a sufficient 
reason that would justify a postponement. The AI Act, just as other Union product legislation, allows

providers to place their AI systems on the market through an alternative path of conformity assessment: 
The inclusion of an independent notified body verifying the system’s conformity with the AI Act’s legal 
requirements. The TÜV organisations, just like the entire TIC sector, are ramping up capacities to be able 
to offer certification services in a timely manner. While harmonised standards are a voluntary means to 
demonstrate conformity, there is (and should be) no ‘standards first’ principle where the entry into force 
of legislation is made dependent on the existence of harmonised standards. 
This being said, it must be acknowledged that there are delays in the establishment of competent 
authorities in several EU member states, including notifying authorities. This may lead to practical 
challenges also with regard to the accreditation and notification procedures of conformity assessment 
bodies. In order to allow notified bodies to conduct the necessary assessments for high-risk AI systems as 
stipulated in the AI Act in due time, the TÜV Association considers a short extension of the deadline by 6 
to maximum 12 months to be acceptable. This, however, under the condition that national authorities 
prioritize their efforts to enable an efficient accreditation and notification of conformity assessment 
bodies as soon as possible.

As TÜV Association, we represent the policy interests of the TÜV assessment organisations and 
foster the professional exchange between our members. We are committed to the technical 
safety, digital security and sustainability of products, systems and services. Universally applicable 
standards, independent assessments and qualified training form the basis. Our goal is to maintain 
the high level of technical safety, to build trust in our digital world and to preserve our livelihoods. 
To this end, we are in regular exchanges with policymakers, authorities, the media, companies and 
consumers.

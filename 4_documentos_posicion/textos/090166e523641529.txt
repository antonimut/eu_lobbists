As a premium provider of trusted AI solutions globally, IDEMIA supports the European Commission’s initiative to seek 
simplification in the implementation of the AI Act. With full implementation of the Act scheduled for in less than a year, 
industry needs consistent application of the rules throughout the EU  and legal clarity, in particular when it comes to 
high-risk AI systems. IDEMIA understands that with the Digital Omnibus, the Commission seeks in part to ensure the 
optimal application of the AI Act, and provide legal predictability aligned with the availability of all the necessary 
support and enforcement structures. In addition, IDEMIA understands that the Digital Omnibus seeks to facilitate the 
much needed smooth interplay between the AI Act and other legislation. 
 
In order to meet these objectives, the European Commission should take into account the following elements:  
 
1) The possible impact of the late adoption of harmonized standards on the overall implementation of the AI 
Act 
2) The need to determine an adequate criteria to categorize entities which could benefit from alternative 
measures as part of the implementation process 
3) The consistent implementation of the AI Act across Member States 
4) The EU’s ability to evaluate and benchmark AI solutions developed in Europe 
5) The interplay between the AI Act and the GDPR 
 
 
Possible impact of the late adoption of harmonized standards on the overall implementation of the AI Act 
 
With standardization bodies being late in fulfilling the Commission’s request to provide relevant harmonized  
standards, the successful implementation of obligations on high-risk AI systems is at risk. At this stage of the process, 
it seems unlikely that such standards will be defined sufficiently ahead of time for developers of high-risk AI systems, 
such as IDEMIA, to be fully compliant by August 2026. Despite taking all appropriate measures to comply with the 
obligations of the AI Act by then, some companies might be penalized for not having had adequate time to adapt to 
new standards.  
 
The Digital Omnibus should provide an opportunity to review the AI Acts initial implementation timeline in order to 
ensure that companies, regardless of their size or revenue, not be sanctioned due to delays in the standardization 
process. 
 
IDEMIA believes that the deadline for full compliance should be set taking into account the date of the official adoption 
of relevant harmonized standards.  A rational evaluation of the level of progress of current standardization works 
should be performed jointly by the European Commission and standardization bodies to outline a sound assessment 
of a reasonable date for the issuance of harmonized standards. In parallel, the European Commission with the support 
of the suppliers of high-risk systems should perform a sound assessment of the time needed for high-risk systems to 
be fully compliant to the harmonized standards. 
 
Criteria to categorize entities which could benefit from alternative measures as part of the implementation 
process 
 
Ongoing discussions on possible alternative measures for small and medium -sized enterprises (SMEs) and small 
mid-caps (SMCs) in the implementation of the AI Act are misleadingly assuming that there is a correlation between 
the size and/or revenue of an AI company, and its capacity to infringe on key principles of the AI Act. 
 
In the field of biometrics  (ie. high-risk AI system) , there is n o correlation between the size and/or revenue of a 
developer and the risk its solution(s) might present to personal data and fundamental rights. On the contrary , larger 
companies often have in place structures and processes specifically designed to prevent the misuse of biometric 
data, of which SMEs lack of. While SMEs and start -ups do face unique challenges in implementing the AI Act, their 
size and/or revenue should not be used as a criteria to justify deploying alternative measures.

The Digital Omnibus should provide an opportunity for European and national authorities to agree on a relevant 
criteria. 
 
IDEMIA believes that a  dedicated process for alternative measures to full implementation in case of specific 
circumstances of non-compliance affecting all types of deployers of high-risk AI systems should be devised to ensure 
the overall security of high-risk AI systems, and preserve fundamental rights and core EU values . There is no 
justification to introduce a new layer of risk by proposing compliance-loopholes based on arbitrarily defined criteria 
disconnected from the reality of the AI ecosystem in Europe. 
 
Consistent implementation of the AI Act across Member States 
 
As seen in the chaotic Member State’s process for designating national competent authorities, consistent 
implementation of the AI Act is unlikely. This will create a risk for industry by removing de facto legal clarity across 
Europe.  
 
The Digital Omnibus should provide an opportunity to require Member States to effectively consult industry on the 
implementation of the AI Act on a regular basis. 
 
 
EU’s ability to evaluate and benchmark AI systems 
 
The EU should be in a position to benchmark on its own the quality of the AI developed by its own industry, based on 
its own standards and values. It is imperative to take into account the security implications of AI policies and solutions 
adopted for the EU’s strategic sectors, in line with the principles laid out in the Commission’s ProtectEU Strategy.  
 
The Digital Omnibus should provide an opportunity to start a discussion on what capabilities might be required at EU 
level to facilitate the consistent evaluation of AI systems, including through the JRC. 
 
 
Interplay with other EU legislation 
 
While IDEMIA welcomes the fact that the European Data Protection Board (EDPB) and the European Commission 
are working on guidelines on the interplay between the AI Act and the GDPR, legal clarity is immediately needed 
when it comes to  biometric solutions (considered to be high-risk AI systems).  This is particularly relevant when it 
comes to  provisions of the AI Act on the differentiation between biometric identification and biometric verification.  
 
The Digital Omnibus offers an opportunity to provide clarifications on the interplay and possible enforcement overlap 
between the AI Act and other EU legislation, such as the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), by addressing 
innovation and competitiveness of EU industries by individual sectors.

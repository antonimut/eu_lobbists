About Vorwerk Group:

Platform Work Directive (PWD) – Directive 2024/2831 on improving working 
conditions in platform work  
 
While Vorwerk supports the core principles of the Platform Work Directive 2024/2831 (PWD) - 
protecting and improving platform workers' conditions - we are concerned about its overly broad and 
vague scope. Many European businesses use (basic) electronic tools to collaborate with freelance 
professionals in delivering ancillary or supporting services to their customers. Such businesses share 
very little in common with large gig economy platforms, yet without intervention, they will be subject 
to the same heavy-handed regulations. 
  
Vorwerk exemplifies this situation. Vorwerk sells its branded products (e.g. Thermomix)  directly to 
customers online and through independent commercial agents who showcase products and collect 
orders. Customers who wish to test products before buying can request a demonstration via the 
Vorwerk website or app. A simple postal code -based syste m connects them with local commercial 
agents. This minor, ancillary service is free for customers and unpaid for agents. Vorwerk's primary 
purpose is selling its branded  products, not merely showcasing them. However, it could result in 
Vorwerk being regarded as a digital labour platform under the PWD. 
  
Vorwerk urges the European Commission to use the simplification omnibus regulations to ensure that 
businesses fundamentally different from gig economy  platforms are not burdened by unnecessary 
regulations. The PWD should be amended to  clarify that digital labour platform regulations do not 
apply to businesses  whose main purpose is to sell their own products  and where the organisation of 
the work performed by individuals is a minor and  ancillary component  of such sales – in the spirit of 
recital 20 of the PWD  which explicitly states that  the definition of digital labour platforms “limited to 
providers of a service for which the organisation of work performed by the individual, […] constitutes a 
necessary and essential component, and not only a minor and purely ancillary component. ”

Proposed Amendment – New exception to the definition of Digital Labour Platform (Art. 2 (3) – new): 
 
Amended Text – Article 2 (3) new 
 
3.  The definition of ‘digital labour platform’ laid down in point (a) of paragraph 1 does not include  
providers of a service , whose primary purpose is the sale of their own products, and for which the  
organisation of work performed by individuals constitutes merely a minor and purely ancillary 
component of such sales.

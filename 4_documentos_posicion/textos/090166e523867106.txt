Digital Omnibus  
(Digital Package on Simplification) 
Call for evidence 
  
 
 
Ensuring Europe’s competitiveness and patients’ timely access to innovation depend on reducing and ultimately 
overcoming the current fragmentation of digital compliance requirements.  The European Union’s complex digital 
regulations, which also apply to digital health solutions, were originally framed for the wider digital economy, leaving 
certain sector-specific considerations insufficiently addressed. However, in practice, these regulations often result in 
overlapping, duplicative or inconsistent requirements for sectors such as medical technologies that already comply 
with product-specific safety and performance rules , including the In Vitro Diagnostics Medical Devices Regulation 
2017/746 1 (IVDR) and Medical Devices Regulation 2017/7452 (MDR). 
 
Europe’s medical technology sector, covering connected devices, diagnostic software, and AI-enabled solutions, is a 
key driver of innovation, employing over 930,000 people across more than 38,000 companies, 90 per cent of which 
are SMEs. As the world’s second -largest market, valued at around EUR 170 billion in 2024, it generates over 15,700 
patent applications each year. At the same time , the sector faces increasing challenges from overlapping and 
fragmented digital compliance requirements, which divert resources from innovation . 
 
MedTech Europe , therefore,  welcomes the European Commission’s initiative to review digital regulations  and 
introduce simplification measures that will help maintain Europe’s position at the forefront of medical innovation, 
ensuring that patients benefit from timely advances in healthcare . A more coherent, predictable, and innovation -
friendly EU digital framework is essential to guarantee that the implementation of horizontal digital legislation 
achieves its objectives while respecting the operational realities and regulatory requirements within the medical 
technology sector. In our sector,  simplification is not about lowering standards . Instead, it is about making 
compliance more efficient, predictable and practical so that innovation can reach patients and health systems more 
rapidly. In August 2025, MedTech Europe published a position paper with clear recommendations  for EU 
policymakers3 aimed at simplifying EU digital legislation impacting our sector,  with the goal of ensuring more 
coherent implementation of horizontal digital laws.  
 
Ensure real simplification through a more ambitious package that aligns all digital rules  
The Digital Simplification Package is a timely opportunity  and unique chance to cut through the fragmented and 
overlapping requirements of EU digital laws. But in its current form , it lacks clarity, scope, and ambition. Without 
decisive action and without clear direction, the package risks adding another layer of complexity instead of reducing 
burden stemming from current legal frameworks . MedTech Europe urges policymakers to significantly raise the 
ambition of the Digital Simplification Package by providing a clear mandate to broaden its scope.

MedTech Europe calls on policymakers to support Member States’ call4 for substantive simplification and stronger 
political commitment to deliver concrete improvements that benefit businesses, patients, and health systems . To 
achieve this, MedTech Europe urges the European Commission to: 
• Broaden the scope to include all relevant digital laws: The Digital Simplification Package must address the 
interplay between existing and forthcoming sectoral health ( European Health Data Space  (EHDS) 
Regulation5/IVDR/MDR) and horizontal data-related legislation (Data Act6/Artificial Intelligence Act7).  
• Provide clear and timely guidance: Since EU law does not establish a clear hierarchy between horizontal 
and sectoral legislation, manufacturers are left uncertain about which rules to prioritise when obligations 
clash. Without this clarity, manufacturers are forced to divert resources from innovation to compliance, 
delaying patient access to new life-saving technologies, discouraging investment in Europe, and weakening 
EU’s competitiveness.  
• Do not limit simplification to reducing reporting requirements: Actions need to include streamlining these 
regulations and clarifying their interplay , eliminating redundant obligations, and reducing administrative 
burdens, especially for innovation-driven sectors such as medical technologies.  
• Establish clear actions to harmonise implementation across EU Member States:  Take measures to align 
obligations and provide practical compliance tools to ensure consistent and effective implementation and 
application of digital legislation across the Union. 
 
Ensure competitiveness by a high degree of predictability  
Predictability is key for the medical technology sector, where the product development lifecycle from early design to 
final deployment routinely spans several years due to the complexity of development, regulatory requirements, and 
extended approval processes.  Yet the absence of enforceable deadlines  leaves manufacturers, competent 
authorities, and notified bodies facing uncertainty, contributing to delays (e.g., in conformity assessments), divergent 
decisions, and uneven application of rules . Many countries are still in the process of establishing the necessary 
oversight bodies and competent authorities, raising concerns that the deadlines set out in different digital legislation 
cannot be met . For manufacturers of medical technologies, insufficient  predictability and  time to prepare hinder 
their ability to update devices and systems to meet  new requirements while maintaining safety, effectiveness, and 
market availability. This situation not only raises the costs of compliance but also risks delaying patients’ access to 
both existing and innovative medical technologies.  
 
To address these challenges, MedTech Europe  urges policymakers to ensure a high degree of p redictability to 
enable all stakeholders, including industry, to implement digital legislation effectively and consistently, ultimately 
safeguarding patient access and European competitiveness. 
This will require in particular: 
• Prioritising realistic implementation timelines for digital legislation, thus, ensuring predictability for 
manufacturers of medical technologies. 
• Guided by the principle of legal certainty, avoid ing retroactive effects on products that were placed on the 
market in conformity with existing safety legislation, thereby minimising disruption and maintaining supply 
continuity. 
 
Clarify requirements for medical devices under the Artificial Intelligence Act 
Once the AI Act comes into full effect, medical technologies that incorporate AI systems will be subject to 
simultaneous obligations under both the AI Act and sectoral frameworks. Given that AI-enabled medical technologies

already undergo comprehensive conformity assessments under MDR and IVDR, including risk management, post -
market surveillance and notified body oversight, it is essential that the implementation of the AI Act ensures 
coherence with existing sectoral legislation. If not addressed, this overlap will create duplication, higher compliance 
costs and conflicting obligations, increasing the burden especially for SMEs and risking delays in bringing AI -enabled 
solutions to EU patients. 
 
MedTech Europe urges policymakers to ensure targeted alignment between these frameworks, to avoid duplicate 
conformity assessment procedures, conflicting definitions and overlapping risk management obligations, leading 
to higher compliance costs and potential delays in patients’ access to innovation.  
 
To address these issues, MedTech Europe calls on the Commission to use the Digital Simplification Package to:  
• Adapt application timelines for AI-enabled medical devices already regulated under MDR/IVDR: The current 
AI Act deadline of 2 August 2027 does not reflect the state of readiness of  the regulatory ecosystem. 
Extending the full application date to 2 August 2029  would give manufacturers, authorities, and notified 
bodies the time needed to ensure consistent compliance across the EU .  
• Align definitions, especially of “substantial modification” in the AI Act with “significant/substantial change” 
under the MDR/IVDR. 
• Allow the use of existing MDR/IVDR technology codes for notified-body designation under the AI Act, and 
issue clear guidance confirming that conformity assessment for AI -enabled medical technologies can be 
performed under existing MDR/IVDR procedures. 
• Provide a clear legal avenue for pre-market testing and explicitly exempt investigational and performance-
study devices from AI Act obligations when conducted under MDR/IVDR rules. 
• Ensure that harmonised AI risk -management standards are developed in coordination with existing 
MDR/IVDR standards to avoid duplication  and confirm that compliance with sector -specific (vertical) risk 
standards fulfils equivalent AI Act obligations. 
 
Harmonise cybersecurity rules and transposition of Network and Information Security 
Directive II (NIS 2) and Cybersecurity Act 
Cybersecurity is a fundamental pillar of the digital ecosystem, playing a crucial role in protecting citizens and patients, 
securing electronic health data, and fostering trust in digital health solutions. Achieving these goals requires a 
coherent and harmonised legislative framework that eliminates regulatory overlaps and uncertainty. Such a unified 
approach not only streamlines compliance but also strengthens overall cyber resilience, ensuring that digital health 
innovations can thrive in a secure and trusted environment. 
 
For this reason, MedTech Europe calls on policymakers to leverage the Digital Simplification Package to clarify and 
streamline cybersecurity requirements stemming from the Cybersecurity Act and its revision, as well as  the 
Network and Information Security Directive II (NIS 2). 
 
On the Cybersecurity Act, MedTech Europe urges policymakers to: 
• Reinforce the voluntary nature of EU cybersecurity certification. 
• Refocus efforts on promoting state-of-the-art cybersecurity and supporting innovation practices within the 
European digital ecosystem. 
 
In the context of NIS 2, MedTech Europe calls on policymakers to use the Digital Simplification Package to: 
• Support the harmonisation of NIS 2 transposition across EU Member States to avoid fragmentation. 
• Simplify and harmonise NIS 2 definitions and processes for reporting of cybersecurity incidents .

• Align NIS 2 and the Critical Entities Resilience (CER) Directive, using the catalogue of critical medical devices 
as the basis for designation of important/critical entities in all EU Member States . 
• Leverage the single reporting platform to be introduced under the Cyber Resilience Act so that entities can

Breaking down complexity should include the streamlining of data legislation  
With respect to the Data Act, MedTech Europe is  concerned that th is Regulation does not adequately reflect the 
unique sensitivity of data generated in healthcare settings and risks clashing  with the technical and regulatory 
requirements of medical technologies.  An obligation to provide raw, pre -processed and uninterpreted data access 
outside a secure and sectoral regulated framework, as mandated by the Data Act, may expose patients to unforeseen 
risks, including misinterpretation or loss of clinical context.  
 
To address this, MedTech Europe urges policymakers to create an environment that allows for meaningful data  
sharing with patients and healthcare professionals to enhance healthcare delivery via a sector-specific framework 
(such as the EHDS) , while ensuring legal clarity and avoiding potential overlaps or unintended consequences 
stemming from the horizontal provisions of the Data Act.  
 
In the context of the Data Act, MedTech Europe recommends: 
• Introducing a voluntary approach for the Data Act's data accessibility obligations (Chapter II) for 
medical devices, Electronic Health Record (EHR) systems, and related services.  
• Enhancing the so-called “security handbrake” for products that remain in scope, to implement a 
safeguard that limits data sharing when the access of raw and unprocessed data could potentially 
compromise safety or security, also considering product performance as critical factor.  
• Ensuring clear and consistent legal definitions  across these legislations and clarifying the interplay 
between the Data Act and the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)8.  
• Levelling the protection of  trade secrets and security protections in the Data Act with international 
standards by removing the requirement for serious economic damage as a precondition for withholding 
data sharing, which is a cornerstone of Europe's digital competitiveness. 
 
In the context of the EHDS, M edTech Europe emphasises that genuine digital simplification must take into account 
the sector-specific rules governing data sharing, ensuring that new requirements are truly fit for purpose, rather than 
imposing a one-size-fits-all approach.   
 
MedTech Europe, therefore, recommends: 
• Reviewing the definition of an Electronic Health Record (EHR) system  under the EHDS . This will help 
ensure that devices already regulated under the MDR and IVDR are not inadvertently encompassed, 
reducing the risk of duplicative or overlapping regulatory requirements . Such clarity is essential to foster 
innovation and to streamline regulatory processes within the European health data landscape.  
 
 
Conclusion 
The Digital Simplification Package presents a crucial and timely opportunity to address the fragmented and 
overlapping requirements under the EU’s horizontal and sectoral digital legislation. Yet, its current scope and 
ambition fall short of what is needed to achieve genuine simplification and foster innovation. Now is the time for

decisive action: MedTech Europe urges policymakers to deliver a package with clear objectives, streamlined rules, 
and reduced administrative burden, especially for innovation-driven sectors like medical technologies. 
 
MedTech Europe supports Member States’ call for more meaningful streamlining of existing requirements to reduce 
unnecessary costs and overlaps. Achieving this will require a clear political commitment to practical improvements 
that deliver tangible benefits for businesses, patients, and health systems.  A balanced yet ambitious approach is 
essential, one that upholds European values while ensuring regulatory frameworks remain workable and 
proportionate. MedTech Europe stands ready to collaborate with policym akers to make the Digital Simplification 
Package a cornerstone of Europe’s technological sovereignty and competitiveness, ultimately enabling faster access 
to safe and innovative medical technologies for patients and health systems across the EU.

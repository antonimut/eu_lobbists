  1.  Digital  rulebook:  refocusing  the  P2B  Regulation  in  the  age  of  the  DSA   The  entry  into  force  of  the  Digital  Services  Act  (DSA)  presents  a  crucial  opportunity  to  
streamline
 
overlapping
 
legislation.
 
Currently,
 
the
 
Platform-to-Business
 
(P2B)
 
Regulation
 
imposes
 
reporting
 
obligations
 
that
 
are
 
not
 
only
 
resource-intensive
 
but
 
are
 
now
 
largely
 
redundant.
  For  instance,  the  annual  reporting  mandate  on  the  effectiveness  of  complaint-handling  systems  
(Article
 
11(3))
 
requires
 
significant
 
administrative
 
effort
 
for
 
data
 
collection
 
and
 
publication.
 
This

is
 
duplicative,
 
as
 
the
 
DSA
 
introduces
 
similar
 
transparency
 
requirements
 
concerning
 
ranking
 
parameters,
 
complaint
 
mechanisms,
 
and
 
justifications
 
for
 
account
 
restrictions.
 
This
 
considerable
 
overlap
 
calls
 
for
 
simplification
 
to
 
reduce
 
unnecessary
 
administrative
 
burdens.
  Proposals:  ●  Merge  Requirements:  Consolidate  the  complaint-handling  and  reporting  obligations  of  
the
 
P2B
 
Regulation
 
and
 
the
 
DSA
 
into
 
a
 
single,
 
coherent
 
framework
 
to
 
eliminate
 
redundancy,
 
or
 
provide
 
clear

 ●  Shift  to  On-Demand  Reporting:  Transition  from  a  proactive  annual  reporting  
requirement
 
to
 
a

where

is

to
 
designated
 
authorities
 
only

specific
 
and

 ●  Reduce  the  retention  and  public  disclosure  periods :  Reducing  the  public  retention  
period
 
to

would
 
preserve
 
regulatory
 
oversight
 
while
 
reducing
 
unnecessary
 
storage
 
and
 
publication

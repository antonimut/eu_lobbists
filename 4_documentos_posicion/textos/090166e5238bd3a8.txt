COCIR Response to the European Commission’s Consultation on Digital 
Omnibus 
October 2025 
Introduction 
COCIR, representing the medical imaging, radiotherapy, health ICT, and 
electromedical industries, welcomes the opportunity to contribute to the 
European Commission’s initiative  on the Digital Omnibus Package . We 
support the EU’s objectives of fostering innovation, ensuring patient safety, 
and simplifying regulatory frameworks.  Our response focuses on two 
interrelated areas critical for healthcare innovation:  (1) AI systems in medical 
devices and in vitro diagnostics, and (2) the effective, secure use of health data 
across the EU. We strongly support a sector-specific approach for AI-enabled 
medical technologies , alignment with MDR/IVDR, protection of well-
established products, structured and interoperable health data, and practical 
implementation strategies to support both Member States and industry. 
1. ENSURING A SECTORAL APPROACH FOR AI -ENABLED MEDICAL 
TECHNOLOGIES 
Alignment with MDR/IVDR 
COCIR recommends moving AI systems regulated under MDR and IVDR from 
Annex I Section A (horizontal high -risk AI) to Section B (sector -specific 
legislation). This approach: 
• Avoids duplicate and potentially conflicting obligations between the AI 
Act and MDR/IVDR. 
• Recognises that healthcare operates under a robust, risk -based 
regulatory framework that ensures safety and performance. 
• Acknowledges that AI medical devices (except certain Class I scenarios) 
are already subject to third -party conformity assessment, unlike many 
other high-risk AI products.

Integration of AI requirements in MDR/IVDR 
We propose embedding relevant high -risk AI requirements, such as human 
oversight, data governance, logging, directly into Annex I of MDR/IVDR during 
upcoming revisions or as part of the Digital Omnibus Package. This: 
• Ensures continuity of safety and compatibility of standards. 
• Provides legal certainty and operational clarity for manufacturers, 
regulators, and Notified Bodies. 
• Leverages existing conformity assessment and market surveillance 
mechanisms. 
Postponement and grandfathering 
To enable effective implementation of a sectoral approach, COCIR supports 
extending the AI Act application deadline for MDR/IVDR -governed AI systems 
beyond 2 August 2027. Rather than setting a fixed new date, the timeline should 
start once key prerequisites are in place: such as the integration of the 
requirements into MDR/IVDR, designation of Notified Bodies, implementation 
of the Database, and availability of  harmonised standards and guidance. This 
avoids premature obligations amid overlapping requirements, limited 
capacity, and an incomplete regulatory framework. 
Furthermore, protection of legitimate expectations is essential for devices 
developed before 1 August 2024. The challenge stems from a lack of alignment 
between the MDR and the EU AI Act. Unlike the MDR, which includes 
transitional provisions under Article 120, the AI Act does not offer any form of 
grandfathering for existing AI systems. Article 111 of the AI Ac t states that, 
starting August 2, 2027, all high -risk AI systems falling under the New 
Legislative Framework (NLF) - including those used in medical  devices- must 
fully comply with the AI Act before they can be placed on the market. The latter 
is understood as each individual product, not product type.

requirements – such as those concerning training, testing, validation, or data 
retention. Many algorithms were created a decade ago or earlier, at a time

There’s a significant risk that the availability of certain medical devices – many

Proposed measures include: 
• Applying development -related AI Act requirements only to activities 
initiated after the entry into force of the AI Act. 
• Alternatively, extending grandfathering until 2 August 2032. 
These measures ensure continued market access for well -proven devices, 
respect the principle of legitimate expectation, and address practical 
challenges such as managing retrospective nature of the development-related 
compliance obligations. 
Regulatory simplification and industry collaboration 
COCIR stresses that regulatory simplification and coherence are essential to 
competitiveness. Streamlining overlapping frameworks, removing 
administrative bottlenecks, and engaging with stakeholders  including 
manufacturers, clinicians, regulators, and Notified Bodies, will ensure AI rules 
are feasible, proportionate, and compatible with clinical workflows while 
maintaining patient safety.

2. BUILDING A COHERENT EUROPEAN DATA UNION STRATEGY 
2.1. SUCCESSFUL AND EFFECTIVE IMPLEMENTATION OF THE EUROPEAN 
HEALTH DATA SPACE (EHDS) 
Interoperability as a cornerstone 
Secure and effective data use across the EU, particularly health data, requires 
interoperability across technical, semantic, and organisational levels. 
Interoperability: 
• Supports seamless data exchange and clinical decision -making across 
systems and borders. 
• Reduces duplication and ensures continuity of care. 
• Underpins the safe, secure, and innovative deployment of AI, imaging, 
diagnostics, and digital therapeutics. 
The EHDS should serve as the primary framework for health data governance , 
referencing internationally proven standards such as DICOM, HL7 FHIR, IEEE 
11073, and IHE profiles , and ensuring coordinated adoption across Member 
States. 
Structured data and scope clarity 
A successful European Data Union requires clear guidance on structured, 
usable data. COCIR recommends: 
• Clear definitions and guidance for healthcare professionals on 
structured data collection and sharing to enable secondary use (e.g., AI 
training, research). 
• Guidance should clearly define  structured versus unstructured data , 
covering imaging, lab results, digital pathology, video, PDFs, and free -
text notes. Structured data should reference established standards such 
as CDA, PAS, and similar specifications to ensure interoperability. 
• Application of reference vocabularies (LOINC, SNOMED CT) and 
templates for structured data collection to ensure usability and reduce 
clinician burden.

• In line with the legitimate expectations principle, exclude legacy devices 
from the scope of data sharing obligations under the Data Act  (i.e., 
devices placed on the market before applicability date for Art. 3(1))  and 
recognise product development cycles in the regulation to avoid ad hoc 
changes to products. 
• Protection against unintended exposure of sensitive intellectual 
property or trade secrets during third-party data access. Ensure that data 
sharing under the Data Act becomes voluntary and at the discretion of 
the manufacturer. 
Phased implementation and support for Member States 
 
Effective implementation requires realistic, phased roadmaps that build on 
existing national infrastructures. COCIR recommends: 
• EU-level roadmaps and toolkits that clarify scope, use cases, and 
priorities across domains. 
• Use of existing IHE profiles and conformance testing frameworks (e.g., 
IHE Connectathons) to accelerate standardisation and enable testing of 
interoperability across Member States. 
• Recognition of differences in technical capacity , digital infrastructure,  
and regulatory maturity between Member States to avoid uneven 
deployment and implementation delays. 
2.2. CONSOLIDATION OF DATA ACQUIS   
Remove regulatory fragmentation 
The current EU data legislation landscape (including the EHDS, Data Act, Data 
Governance Act,  Free-Flow of Non -Personal Data Regulation, Open Data 
Directive and GDPR ) is complex and potentially fragmented. COCIR stresses 
the importance of: 
• Better alignment between EU frameworks and practical implementation 
in the context of healthcare.

• Harmonisation of cloud portability provisions in the Data Act with the 
realities of medical devices and EHR systems to avoid compromising 
data integrity, security, or compliance. 
• A coordinated EU-level governance model to support international data 
flows, secondary use, and the secure exchange of sensitive data. 
Conclusion 
The Digital Omnibus Package  presents a significant opportunity to reinforce 
Europe’s digital leadership in health. Realising this potential requires: 
• A sectoral approach for AI  embedded in medical devices  aligned with 
MDR/IVDR, including integration of AI requirements, grandfathering for 
well-established products, and realistic implementation timelines. 
• Clear scope and structured data definitions, aligned with clinical 
practice and secondary use needs. 
• Robust technical frameworks built on international standards, 
supported by phased, practical implementation tools for Member States 
and industry. 
• Coherent regulatory alignment across overlapping EU frameworks to 
reduce burden and enhance competitiveness. 
COCIR remains committed to supporting the Commission in these efforts and 
looks forward to collaborating to ensure a secure, interoperable, and 
innovative European Data Union.

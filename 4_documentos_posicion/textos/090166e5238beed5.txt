Eurofinas: Digital Omnibus 
 
 
 
 
 
Eurofinas, the voice of specialised consumer credit providers at the EU  level, represents finance houses, 
specialised banks, captive finance companies of car, equipment, etc. manufacturers and universal banks . By 
enabling responsible access to credit  for Eu ropean consumers, our members promote social mobility, 
inclusion, and economic growth across Europe. 
 
We strongly support the European Commission’s ambition to streamline and simplify the EU’s digital rulebook 
through the Digital Omnibus and the forthcoming Digital Fitness Check. The financial services sector is already 
among the most highly regulated in Europe, operating under an extensive framework that includes both sector-
specific and horisontal legislation, i.e., for our industry, notably the Consumer Credit Directive (CCD), GDPR, 
cybersecurity legislation, Anti-Money Laundering rules, as well as the advancing AI Act. 
 
Simplification and coherence between these frameworks are essential to safeguard competitiveness, ensure 
legal certainty, and foster responsible innovation. A more coherent digital framework would allow lenders to 
focus on delivering value and innovation for consumers, while maintaining robust safeguards.  
 
To make simplification meaningful, the Digital Omnibus and Digital Fitness Check should ensure:  
 
• Proportionality in the AI Act, ensuring that long -established and transparent statistical 
techniques, such as logistic regression and generalised linear models, remain clearly outside 
its scope.  
 
• Alignment between the AI Act, the GDPR, and financial sector legislation, avoiding duplication 
and ensuring consistency.  
 
• Regulatory coherence and consistency across Member States, preventing fragmentation of 
the Single Market and contradictory obligations.  
 
• Reduced administrative burdens and recognition of already existing requirements.  
 
• A balanced, innovation -friendly regulatory environment that supports competitiveness and 
responsible digital transformation in financial services.

AI Act 
 
Eurofinas fully supports the AI Act’s objectives of promoting trust, transparency, and safety in the use of 
artificial intelligence. However, implementation must remain proportionate, technology -neutral, and 
recognising existing EU and sectoral frameworks, as well as operational realties. The Act should complement, 
not duplicate, the comprehensive governance, risk management, and consumer protection obligations that 
already apply to credit providers. 
 
The Commission’s Guidelines on the Definition of an AI System, published earlier this year, provide vital 
clarification. As set out in those Guidelines, well -established statistical techniques such as logistic regression 
and generalised linear models do not meet the definition of an AI system under  the AI Act. These are 
deterministic, transparent, and human-controlled tools that lack the autonomy or adaptive inference the AI Act 
is intended to regulate. Their exclusion reflects the legislators´ set out intent to target genuinely new and higher-
risk technologies. Eurofinas, therefore, calls on the Commission to explicitly reaffirm this exclusion to ensure 
legal certainty and uniform interpretation across Member States.  
 
While the AI Act classifies the use of AI systems for creditworthiness assessments as high-risk, this designation 
should be strictly limited to the evaluation of repayment capacity. Adjacent and/or supportive functions, such 
as fraud detection, customer service, or marketing, should not fall within this category.  
 
Extending the scope beyond the legislator’s intent would risk over-regulation, reduce credit availability, and 
hinder digitalisation and financial inclusion. 
 
 
 
Interplay with GDPR and sectoral rules 
 
The GDPR already provides a comprehensive and well -tested framework governing data use, transparency, 
bias prevention,  and individual rights in automated decision -making. However, diverging national 
interpretations and overlapping enforcement approaches risk creating fragmentation between data protection 
and AI legislation, as well as with other relevant sectoral legislation. 
 
Eurofinas, therefore, calls for alignment and coordination between the AI Act, the GDPR, and existing financial 
regulation. In particular, compliance with GDPR principles and supervisory expectations under existing 
frameworks should be better recognised, not least in the areas of transparency, data governance, and human 
oversight. 
 
The forthcoming Digital Fitness Check provides a  more comprehensive  opportunity to identify and resolve 
structural inconsistencies across the digital rulebook and its fundamental interplay with the broader applicable 
legislation. A coherent and predictable approach will reduce compliance costs, avoid double regulation, and 
maintain high standards of consumer and data protection.  We encourage the Commission to use the Digital 
Fitness Check to address persistent inconsistencies and overlaps be tween data protection, AI, and financial 
regulation.

Fundamental simplification 
 
The Digital Omnibus provides a timely and practical opportunity to reduce administrative burdens and 
regulatory complexity without compromising consumer protection. For the financial sector, simplification should 
focus on eliminating duplication, aligning supervisory expectations, and recognising equivalence between 
comparable frameworks. This requires a systematic identification of overlapping obligations and 
inconsistencies across existing rules, a clear commitment to proportionality, and consistent interpretation and 
enforcement across Member States. 
 
Eurofinas also encourages strengthen ed coordination among the AI Office, the European Data Protection 
Board (EDPB), and the E uropean Supervisory Authorities (ESAs). A more joined -up and proportionate 
supervisory approach, grounded in coherent oversight and continuous dialogue with the industry , will be key 
to achieving real simplification in practice. 
 
Ultimately, simplification must serve competitiveness and innovation, not merely compliance. By removing 
redundant requirements and reinforcing coherence across the EU’s digital framework, the Digital Omnibus can 
make regulation more agile, efficient, and innovation -friendly, empowering financial institutions to continue 
supporting consumers, the wider economy, and Europe’s twin digital and green transitions.  
 
 
 
 
 
About Eurofinas 
 
Eurofinas is the voice of specialised consumer credit providers in Europe. As a Federation, Eurofinas brings 
together associations throughout Europe that represent finance houses, specialised banks, captive finance 
companies of car, equipment, etc. manufacturers , and universal banks. The scope of products covered 
by Eurofinas members includes all forms of consumer credit products such as personal loans, point of sale 
credit, credit cards and store cards. Consumer  credit facilitates access to assets and services as diverse as 
cars, education, furniture, electronic appliances, etc. Eurofinas members financed around €4 84 billion worth 
of new loans during 2024 with outstanding portfolio amounting to more than €1 trillion euros at the end of the 
year. You can find more information at www.eurofinas.org

Bayer AG Contribution To The Simplification Of Digital Regulation In Europe 
 
 
As a leading EU-headquartered life sciences company with global operations, Bayer is committed to 
transforming health and nutrition by leveraging advanced digital technologies to accelerate product 
development and enhance manufacturing and delivery processes. Guided by our mission, "Health for 
all, hunger for none," we use these technologies in a principled, purpose-led manner. To remain 
competitive on a global scale, we support the efforts of the European Commission towards reviewing 
and simplifying existing digital legislations in the areas of AI, data, and cybersecurity. By doing so, 
European businesses can better utilize AI and data as strategic tools, while ensuring robust 
cybersecurity measures are in place to protect sensitive information and maintain trust. 
 
Data 
 
Harmonization of Data Regulations: We welcome efforts towards harmonizing data protection 
regulations to simplify compliance and promote cross-border data sharing. As AI systems rely heavily 
on personal data for training, organizations must secure a legal basis under GDPR, incorporating 
principles like data minimization and purpose limitation from the outset of system design. This need 
for compliance across multiple regulatory frameworks places a considerable administrative burden 
on businesses, diverting resources—time, personnel, and finances—away from innovation and 
product development. 
 
Considering these challenges, we urge the European Commission to reflect on the potential 
hindrance to innovation caused by overlapping regulations and to work towards clearer, cohesive 
guidance that facilitates both compliance and the advancement of AI technologies. 
 
Transfer conditions should be consistent and aligned with the GDPR, avoiding duplication of 
obligations under the EHDS and the Data Governance Act. By creating a clear legal basis for cross-
border data sharing, the Digital Omnibus can ensure a predictable environment that protects 
individual rights while enabling responsible, cutting-edge research across borders. 
 
Establishment of Interoperability Standards: Effective data management, standardization, 
interoperability, ownership, and sharing among stakeholders are crucial for advancing the digitization 
across key sectors of the European economy to foster further innovation. As it stands, varied levels of 
digital infrastructure across Europe hinder productivity. In agriculture, this complicates the 
widespread adoption of innovative digital farming technology, as rural areas often lack adequate 
broadband and internet connectivity to support such transitions. The lack of data and system 
interoperability also poses additional challenges, such as digital literacy and skills development. 
 
We urge the European Commission to establish common standards and formats to ensure 
interoperability, allowing user communities to access and integrate data from diverse sources in a

Cybersecurity 
 
Incident Reporting: 
Cyber incident reporting is currently cumbersome as Member States and relevant competent 
authorities use different tools and processes. This leads to high effort and complexity for companies 
that operate in several countries. Simplification measures could be: 
- Similar to GDPR establish a one-stop shop approach for incident notifications at EU level for 
companies with business in more than one Member State. 
- Harmonize incident reporting requirements from different EU legislations regimes, such as 
NIS-2, DORA, GDPR, CRA to avoid multiple or conflicting notification obligations. Central 
reporting tool vs. multiple different reporting tools / processes per country/authority. 
 
Complexity of Legal Frameworks: 
Streamlined and consolidated legislation would help to reduce overlaps and ambiguities. The 
instrument of EU-Directives (vs. binding Acts) increases the risk of non-compliance as global 
companies need to deal with increased complexity and country specifics. Furthermore, the fact that 
the standardization process / implementing guidelines are often timely delayed (not in sync with the 
legislation process) creates legal ambiguity and increases effort for companies to ensure compliance. 
 
Bayer encourages the Commission to pursue simplification and legal clarity through the Digital 
Omnibus to ensure consistent cybersecurity expectations across Member States, reducing 
unnecessary complexity and fostering trust in Europe’s digital health ecosystem. 
 
AI 
 
Necessity for a clearer regulatory framework 
Companies in the EU are navigating a more complicated regulatory framework than overseas, 
meaning certain systems are launched outside the EU and unavailable to European citizens. As 
healthcare and agriculture related AI applications are likely to be categorized as 'high risk', that would 
necessitate rigorous compliance with transparency, explainability, and data governance 
requirements. Scaling AI solutions globally and across different markets is complex, requiring careful 
coordination and adaptation to local contexts. We welcome a more predictable regulatory approach 
which would allow us to propose innovative solutions to EU patients and farmers. 
 
Horizontal digital legislation, such as the AI Act, should be consistent with sectoral rules governing 
critical sectors within agriculture, medicines and medical technologies. Regulatory frameworks 
should facilitate—not hinder—scientific research, product development, and innovation across the 
medicine lifecycle.

AI Act exemption for Research & Development 
Art. 2(6) of the AI Act deals with scientific research and development.  We favour an interpretation of 
this provision that would reflect the intentions of EU lawmakers and not unduly burden scientific 
research and innovation in the Union. We are looking for clarity on: 
- whether the pharmaceutical value chain will be able to fully benefit from this. 
- whether AI used for seeking regulatory market approval falls under this exemption. 
- scientific: academic (“non-commercial") versus industry (“commercial”) R&D 
 
The Digital Omnibus offers a key opportunity to clarify how AI systems developed solely for scientific 
R&D are treated. By confirming that AI used across the medicine lifecycle falls under Article 2(6), the 
Commission can safeguard scientific freedom while promoting responsible innovation. 
 
We recommend aligning the interpretation of “scientific research purposes” with existing language in 
GDPR Recital 159 and EHDS Recital 61, which define it broadly to include technological development, 
demonstration, fundamental research, applied research, and privately funded research. 
 
Creation of an AI Cloud Infrastructure 
Creating an EU-managed AI cloud infrastructure would strengthen data sovereignty, security, and 
adherence to European standards. This would promote innovation by offering a customized 
environment that meets European requirements. Although substantial investment and collaboration 
among member states would be necessary to establish and sustain this infrastructure, it has the 
potential to drive innovation by facilitating a specialized setting for AI development. Therefore, we 
would welcome: 
• Prioritized investments to establish a secure and compliant AI cloud infrastructure. 
• Collaboration between public and private sectors to ensure competitiveness and innovation 
while maintaining sovereignty.  
• Simplification of current and future regulatory frameworks to support infrastructure 
development and ensure (data) interoperability with global systems.  
• The development of an AI cloud regulatory framework to ensure provider accountability and 
long-term operational commitments.

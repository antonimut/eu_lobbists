  We  would  like  to  emphasise  that  consent  fatigue  does  not  stem  from  the  use  of  cookies  or  other

  Consent  fatigue  is  driven  by  several  overlapping  factors :   ●  Monotony  by  design:  standardisation,  malicious  compliance,  and  legalese  make  every  banner

 ●  Browser  privacy-washing :  extremely  restrictive  practices  on  cookie  lifetimes  (e.g.  a  7-day  cookie  
lifetime)

or

 ●  Proliferation  of  in-app  browsers:  rather  than  redirecting  users  to  external  browsers,  some  apps

 Any  reform  of  cookie  or  tracking  rules  should  preserve  the  neutrality,  diversity,  and  interoperability  
of
 
consent-management
 
solutions.
 
It
 
must
 
be

that
 
online
 
consent
 
goes
 
beyond
 
cookies.
 
CMPs
 
play
 
a
 
key
 
role
 
in
 
obtaining
 
consent
 
for
 
all
 
non-essential
 
treatment
 
of
 
data,
 
for
 
all
 
types
 
of
 
technologies.
  Centralising  consent  management  within  proprietary  browser  mechanisms  would  risk:   ●  undermining  the  speciﬁcity  of  consent,  which  must  be  contextual  and  granular ,  as  required  
by
 
the
 
GDPR
.
 
Any

that
 
automatically
 
accepts
 
or
 
refuses
 
cookies
 
on
 
all
 
sites,
 
without
 
distinction,
 
cannot
 
be
 
considered
 
respectful
 
of
 
the
 
speciﬁc
 
nature
 
of
 
consent;
 ●  concentrating  control  in  the  hands  of  a  few  gatekeeper  browsers,  risking  tension  with  the  
Digital
 
Markets
 
Act.
 
Integrating
 
an
 
in-house
 
consent
 
system
 
into
 
the
 
browser
 
could
 
reduce
 
space
 
for
 
competing
 
services
 
and
 
bias
 
the
 
quality
 
and
 
neutrality
 
of
 
consent
;
 
 ●  reducing  transparency  and  user  control,  contrary  to  the  principles  of  the  Digital  Services  
Act.
 
Only
 
independent
 
third-party
 
solutions
 
can
 
ensure
 
that
 
the
 
collection
 
of
 
consent
 
is
 
not
 
inﬂuenced
 
by
 
the
 
economic
 
interests
 
of
 
the
 
browser
 
provider
 
and
 
is
 
oriented
 
towards
 
protecting
  
user
 
rights
;
 
and
 
 ●  limiting  innovation  by  restricting  space  for  independent  European  solutions  that  enhance  
privacy
 
by
 
design.
 
  Instead,  the  EU  should  promote  a  model  in  which  users  can  choose  interoperable  tools,  including  
CMPs,
 
that
 
communicate
 
seamlessly
 
with
 
browsers
 
and
 
apps,

Simplification – digital package and omnibus
European DIGITAL SME Alliance - Call for Evidence
The European DIGITAL SME Alliance, representing over 45,000 tech small and
medium enterprises across Europe, supports the EU's digital rulebook
simplification provided it preserves fair competition and market access.
Simplification must empower SMEs while preserving essential rules
aiming at rendering digital markets more contestable, fairer and open to
competition. The proposed package offers a unique opportunity to reduce
fragmentation and align rules with technological progress. If implemented
correctly, it can remove barriers and open the digital market to more innovative
SMEs from across Europe.
However, streamlining must go hand-in-hand with strong, consistent enforcement
ofsomekeyexistingobligationstoensurethatsimplificationdoesnotcomeatthe
cost of fairness or legal certainty. At the same time, and especially when it comes
to the AI Act, the enforcement should be calibrated to the actual readiness of
standards, guidance, and supporting tools, so that SMEs are always equipped to
comply effectively and without disproportionate burden.

Data economy: unlocking access and innovation for
SMEs
Rules designed to encourage the adoption of data sharing mechanisms are often
beneficial for SMEs as they mitigate data gatekeeping by large companies and
original equipment manufacturers (OEMs). In turn, this can improve competition
and innovation in the downstream and after-market sectors. However, these rules
are only effective if they are not overly complex and difficult to navigate. SMEs
often face the challenge of complying with the full regulatory framework while
having much fewer resources at their disposal. Bridging this “cliff edge” – where it
makes sense – would unlock new opportunities and strengthen the
competitiveness of these businesses.

Cyber Resilience Act: smarter applicability for SMEs
Businesses face a considerable burden due to incident and data breach reporting
obligations set out in various EU-level regulations, both horizontal and sector-
specific,andtheirimplementationatthenationallevel.Thischallengeisfrequently
highlighted by SMEs, underscoring the urgent need for measures that simplify
compliance and streamline the use of reporting tools, while maintaining robust
cybersecurity protection.
The Cyber Resilience Act places heavy burdens on SMEs, especially around
vulnerability notifications (Articles 11–12), conformity assessments (Chapter III),
overlapping obligations for economic operators (Articles 15–19), and extensive
documentation (Article 23). Many SMEs lack the capacity for continuous threat
monitoring or maintaining incident response teams, yet must report exploited
vulnerabilities within 24 hours, an impractical demand. To simplify, DIGITAL SME
proposes:clearerthresholdsandtieredobligations,exemptionsorlongertimelines
for low-risk products, and a tailored “SME economic operator” profile to reduce
overlap.

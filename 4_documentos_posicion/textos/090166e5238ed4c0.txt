A. About VAUNET 
VAUNET is the umbrella organization of private audio and audiovisual  (AV) media in Ger-
many. The diverse business areas of the approximately 160 members include TV, radio, 
web and streaming offerings. The trade association aims to create acceptance for the 
political and economic concerns of audio and AV media at national and European level 
and to raise awareness of the great socio-political and cultural importance of the sector. 
B. Horizontal Asks of the Digital Omnibus 
 
VAUNET welcomes the aim to create a simpler, faster and coherent digital framework 
within the EU and equally aims to reduce regulatory overlap and additional burdens that 
impede media pluralism and a sustainable media ecosystem. 
While VAUNET in general is very much in favor of any useful simplification, simplifying 
always risks unintended tightening of current legislation for the sake of “cutting red tape” 
by making regulation more general and less detailed.  
Due to its relevance for democracy itself it is important to continuously keep private me-
dia and its regulatory and refinancing environment  in mind while working on existing or 
creating new regulation and uphold ongoing exchange with stakeholders to properly as-
sess new developments and challenges. This could be done through a mandatory media 
impact assessment for all legislative work. By systematically including mandatory media 
impact assessments in the legislative process – drawing from the principle set out in art. 
21 EMFA and integrating these assessments into the Better Regulation framework – the 
EU would help avoid unintended, collateral harm and policy fragmentation. Furthermore, 
enforcement would improve if the EU consolidated overlapping rules and phased out 
measures that are ineffective or mis-targeted.  
As the Digital Omnibus targets various existing legislation of the digital acquis, VAUNET 
wants to highlight the opportunities as well as the  risks that a Digital Omnibus encom-
passes.  
Key points: 
• Prioritize enforcement of the existing data acquis: Maintain proportionate regu-
lation without tightening the GDPR. 
• Tackle cookie fatigue by addressing root causes: Enable basic network exchange 
& simplify consent requirements.

Enforcement of Existing Law & Alignment of Data Acquis: 
The best way to achieve legal certainty in the current legislative situation is by focusing 
on consistent enforcement and effective application of existing legislation within the 
digital framework. The DSA, DMA, GDPR, AI Act etc.  address data protection more than 
sufficiently and are apt for digital progress and development.  
Hence, VAUNET will reiterate the same position it will take in terms of the upcoming Dig-
ital Fitness Act: New legislation, such as the Digital Omnibus , should focus on effective 
enforcement of existing rules, avoid or eliminate any overlaps by coordination with the 
existing framework, and take the distinct role and workings of journalistic-editorial com-
mercial media fully into account. In order to fulfill its purpose as a democratic pillar and 
source of trusted journalistic -editorial content, private media is heavily reliant on ad -
based and subscription -based financing. These business models are data driven . 
Through art. 5 (2), 24, and 30 of the GDPR, evidence and reporting requirements are im-
posed, causing considerable burdens for media companies that are not proportionate to 
the little-to-none additional protection for those affected. 
To reduce disproportionate burdens while maintaining strong data protection, the Digital 
Omnibus could clarify and operationalize the GDPR by establishing a de minimis threshold 
and guidance for handling excessive requests in line with art. 12(5) GDPR. Thi s would al-
low companies to save resources if a request is clearly abusive. 
However, preserving legitimate interest pathways (in the sense of the GDPR) for low-risk 
purposes (e.g. basic analytics) that serve media pluralism are essential for refinancing of 
media and their existence. Therefore, VAUNET welcomes alignment, enforcement of ex-
isting legislation, and possible deregulation without tightening the current one through 
the Omnibus-back door. 
ePrivacy - Cookies & Cookie Fatigue: 
Art. 5 (3) of the ePrivacy directive establishes a requirement for user consent when data 
is stored or accessed. This requirement has driven, fueled by the broad interpretation of 
the EDPB, the widespread use of cookie banners, and repeated prompts can contribute 
to consent fatigue.  While VAUNET shares the aim to reduce cookie banners and 
strengthen consumer protection, we see the cookie pledge as a sheer treatment of 
symptoms rather than the root cause: An interpretation that requires individual consent 
for basic network exchanges would be not only impractical but increase the number of 
consent banners as well as case-by-case disputes over what is strictly necessary to ac-
cess media content. Instead, consent requirements could be simplified – without lifting 
 
its safeguards – to facilitate compliance and reduce consent fatigue. Furthermore, it is 
important to consider the role of media and the need for its sustainable refinancing to 
safeguard media pluralism. An over-restrictive approach undermines viable funding 
models and even harms the consumer by reducing access to free, diverse media. Hence, 
a clarification of consent requirements for activities that are functionally necessary (e.g. 
privacy-preserving audience measurement of the reach of a service , login, updates, ac-
cessibility, security measures etc.) would increase the media sector’s sustainability and 
competitiveness regarding gatekeeping big -tech platforms and could be laid out by a  
non-exhaustive list.  
Application of the AI Act: 
With the AI Act being a new piece of legislation, not even fully entered into force, it would 
be too early to already amend it, without the chance to be fully enforced or for a proper 
impact assessment. In the case of this new law,  enforcement and refinement through 
codes of practice would help stakeholders more than too early changes of the AI Act. 
Strong copyright protection in the context of AI training remains a key concern for 
VAUNET and needs to be at the heart of further AI Act enforcement, impact assessments 
or guidelines. GenAI must respect the existing copyright framework and training on cop-
yrighted media content should require clear opt-in and adequate remuneration. In addi-
tion, transparency about the data used for training needs to be provided to ensure that 
rightsholders can enforce their rights. 
VAUNET supports proportionate transparency under art. 50 to counter manipulation and 
deepfakes without undermining professional editorial media: Due to the rising costs and 
economic situation media companies face, AI’s efficiency and innovation is a necessary 
tool for media companies. However, excessive labelling rules (under art. 50  (4)) should 
not interfere with journalistic-editorial content and work: where editorial control and hu-
man review exist , leeway for professional providers can be granted . The exception 
granted for press publishers should equally cover private broadcasters. Furthermore, as-
sistive AI that does not change meaning should not trigger labelling obligations.

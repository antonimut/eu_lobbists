          Digital Omnibus Initiative  Position | Digitalisation    Brüssel, 14.10.2025   
    
Introduction  DER MITTELSTANDSVERBUND welcomes the opportunity to comment on the planned Digital Omnibus. Fragmented digital regulation increasingly burdens SMEs, particularly in retail, and hampers innovation. The Omnibus should create clarity, reduce overlap, and ensure proportionality in the implementation of EU digital law.  Recommendations  } Consistent Definitions Across Digital Acts: Definitions in the AI Act, Data Act, and other instruments differ significantly. Clear and harmonised terminology is essential. In particular, the concept of an AI system must be clarified to avoid over-inclusion and legal uncertainty. SMEs need predictable, technology-neutral definitions that reflect practical use cases. } Elimination of Double Reporting and Oversight: Multiple reporting regimes, e.g. under NIS2, the Cyber Resilience Act, and other digital legislation cause duplication without added value.  The Omnibus should ensure alignment of reporting obligations, creating a coherent and non-duplicative compliance framework that reduces administrative burdens.  } Limiting National Overregulation: Excessive opening clauses enable Member States to adopt divergent national rules.  To safeguard the single market, such flexibility must be drastically reduced. SMEs operating cross-border should face a uniform regulatory environment instead of 27 variations of EU law.  } Aligning Data Protection Requirements: The interaction of GDPR, AI Act, and Data Act creates contradictions that hinder data-driven innovation. These instruments must be systematically aligned, ensuring that data use for innovation remains lawful and proportionate. To avoid chilling effects resulting in further losses of competitiveness, further clarifications and limitations to the liability regime of the AI Act are needed. Many SME test ready-to-use AI solutions which are gradually adopted to the given entrepreneurial context. For the time being it remains unclear, at which point the liability regimes „tipps“ and users are held liable pursuant to the producer´s liability regime.

} A targeted reform of the GDPR is necessary to simplify excessive duties, such as processor audits, which overburden small businesses without improving data security.  } Data Act: Adjusting Information Obligations: Under Article 3(2) Data Act, retailers at the point of sale should not be primary addressees of manufacturer information duties. In many cases, they cannot obtain the technical data required. Obligations must lie with those controlling the data generation—manufacturers and importers. As a general rule, the forementioned information requirements should only apply to the manufacturer. Dedicated conformity labels or the CE label should be sufficient to indicate the conformity of the respective smart device. Detailed information should on the applicable data processing should be made available by the producer by digital means (QR code, link to dedicated webpages). The phased introduction of the Digital Product Passport should address the information requirement in the future.   } Proportionality in the Digital Services Act: The DSA imposes extensive compliance duties even on small or cooperative marketplaces, contrary to the legislator’s original intent.  The Omnibus should re-establish proportionality: obligations must scale with platform size and risk. Cooperative online marketplaces (distribution channels only available to members of a given cooperative or group of independent retailers) cannot meet the same compliance levels as global tech corporations.  The thresholds therefore need further adjustment.  } European Digital Reporting System: A European Digital Reporting System (EDRS) should be established to integrate reporting for digital, product, and sustainability legislation.  It should offer a single point of entry and shared interfaces, reducing duplication and ensuring data consistency across EU instruments. The Digital Omnibus is a unique chance to simplify, harmonise, and rationalise the EU’s digital regulatory framework.  For SMEs and groups of independent retailers, wholesalers and service providers, it should mean fewer redundant rules, clearer obligations, and a fair competitive environment.   About us DER MITTELSTANDSVERBUND – ZGV e.V. is a national umbrella organisation for German businesses operating in Berlin and Brussels, representing the interests of around 230,000 small and medium-sized enterprises organised into around 300 associations. With 2.36 million full-time employees, these cooperating SMEs generate a turnover of around 506 billion euros (approximately 12 per cent of GDP) and offer over 400,000 apprenticeships. Individual associations operate under a single brand, e.g. EDEKA, REWE, INTERSPORT, EP: ElectronicPartner, expert and BÄKO. All of them support their members through a wide range of services such as purchasing negotiations, logistics, IT, financial services, consulting, marketing, shop fittings and trend research.

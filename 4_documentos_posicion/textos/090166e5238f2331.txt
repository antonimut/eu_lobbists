 
    
 
Contribution to the Consultation on the Digital Omnibus as part of the Digital Package on 
Simplification 
The Austrian Chamber of Civil -Law Notaries welcomes the opportunity to contribute to the 
consultation on the Digital Omnibus as part of the Digital Package on Simplification, and in 
particular the Commission’s quest to launch a  Fitness Check stress-testing the coherence and 
cumulative impact of the EU digital acquis. 
 
Austrian notaries stand alongside businesses as reliable partners in ensuring efficient and legally 
secure procedures and transactions . They have actively supported digitali sation initiatives that 
enhance efficiency and transparency while safeguarding the principles of legal certainty, planning 
stability, and trust. In this spirit, Austrian notaries are committed to contributing their expertise 
and practical experience to the European Union’s efforts to simplify digital regulation and improve 
the functioning of the internal market, while upholding the value of reliability and legal certainty 
and would thus like to highlight the points below. 
 
The intention to conduct a comprehensive “stress test ” of the EU digital acquis to ensure 
coherence, consistent application, and greater legal clarity is also a step in the right direction. In 
recent years, a multitude of horizontal and sectoral regulations have been introduced, which has 
led to growing comp lexity and implementation challenges. A thorough analysis of overlaps and 
gaps will help streamline digital regulation and support more effective application in practice.  
When undertaking streamlining processes however, well -established national frameworks  and 
practices must be respected. 
 
With regard to the EU's data framework, including the Data Governance Act and the Open Data 
Directive, Austrian notaries particularly stress the importance of data confidentiality and 
reliability. Interoperability and data sharing can only deliver their intended benefits if the accuracy 
and trustworthiness of the data , especially with regard to  public registers, are guaranteed by 
competent national authorities. As trusted public officials, notaries contribute to this by verifying 
facts and ensuring the legal quality of data submitted to registers. Any further steps in digitalising 
access to or the reuse of public -sector data must therefore be accompanied by safeguards that 
preserve data accuracy, protect sensitive information, and ensure legally robust processes  and 
should be done with the involvement of competent authorities only. 
 
On cybersecurity, and in particu lar concerning incident reporting obligations, the Austrian 
notariat emphasises the importance of preventive protection measures. All concerned 
institutions must be equipped with robust digital infrastructure and persons in charge must 
receive ongoing training to ensure that they can securely operate within an increasingly digital 
environment. In this regard, clear delineation of responsibilities and secure communication 
channels are vital, as well as adequate financial and technical support. 
 
Concerning the application of the AI Regulation, Austrian notaries welcome the clear classification 
of risk categories and the framework for responsible AI use. To ensure proper implementation  
and offer the necessary training , the Austrian Chamber of Civil -Law Notaries has introduced an

AI competency certificate. Such initiatives demonstrate the importance of equipping legal 
professionals with the skills needed in a digital environment, while respecting high data 
protection and legal standards. 
 
The Austrian Chamber of Civil -Law notaries welcomes the development of secure and 
standardised electronic identification and trust services , with particular view to supporting 
business transactions . With the ongoing implementation of the eIDAS II Regulatio n and the 
development of the European Business Wallet, it is crucial that national formal requirements and 
competences, particularly in civil law matters, are respected. Austrian notaries already facilitate 
digital company formation through a fully online process. They remain committed to expanding 
these services, but underline that legal certainty, national competence in civil law, and the need 
for a reliable identity infrastructure must remain guiding principles. Any further harmonisation 
must be built on respect for these core values. Concerning the proposal for a EU Business Wallet, 
Austrian notaries see particular potential in the area of digital powers of attorney. To ensure their 
practical and legally sound application, notaries respectively businesse s in the EU must be 
provided with sufficient resources and tools to create respectively manage and store these digital 
documents securely. When moving  away from paper -based processes and establishing digital 
channels for communication between businesses and public authorities, notaries, acting as public 
officials, should be well integrated into this digital transformation and consulted in this process. 
While the Austrian notaries support digitalisation and  simplification endeavors, these must not 
come at the expense of legal certainty or well -established national formal requirements that 
ensure the authenticity, relia bility, and integrity of legal acts.  A balanced approach is needed  - 
one that fosters efficiency and digital innovation while maintaining the high standards that are 
essential to trust in cross -border legal and business transactions.  Austrian notaries stand ready 
to contribute their expertise and digital infrastructure to ensure that these reforms are 
implemented in a manner that balances innovation and legal security.

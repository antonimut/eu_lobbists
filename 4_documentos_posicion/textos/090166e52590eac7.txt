Subject: Response to the public consultation on the Proposal for a regulation addressing the negative 
trade-related effects of the global overcapacity on the European Union’s steel market.   
 
 
Dear Madam,  
Dear Sir,   
 
Pneurop welcomes the opportunity to contribute feedback on  the Proposal for a regulation addressing the 
negative trade-related effects of the global overcapacity on the  European Union’s steel market.   We fully support 
the objective of safeguarding the competitiveness, sustainability, and strategic autonomy of the EU’s metals 
industries. Whereby, it is essential to recognize that this sector goes beyond steel producers alone; downstream 
industries, such as the compressor manufacturing sector, must also be considered.   
 
The implementation of the US tariffs on steel, alumin ium, and derivatives, the expansion of 
Section 232 and CBAM, combined with our industries facing the  third consecutive year of economic decline  and 
other global trade challenges are putting significant pressure on our sector.  Elements that should not be 
neglected when implementing new legislation.    
 
First, as the measure intends to protect the European internal market and the EU steel sector, we propose  to 
exclude all derivative products from the scope of this legislation and only focus on the imports of raw steel 
materials. In this way, the scope of the proposal is  respected and the major negative impact on the downstream 
industry is limited.   
 
Furthermore, we  are concerned that  the current proposal  goes against the  institution’s broader efforts toward 
simplification. The introduction of  the "melt and pour" requirement would impose major administrative burdens, 
as mentioned multiple times when  commenting the current expansion of Section 232.  This proposal 
would also force manufacturers to determine the precise value of the metal content in the concerned products—
a highly difficult task given the complexity of modern supply chains and of our products.    
 
Therefore, Pneurop asks for the removal of the melt & pour obligation or at least the simplification of the process. 
This could include submitting invoices from suppliers or limiting the requirements to the average product rather 
than imposing them on every single product.

Next to this, the current safeguard measures would restrict our access to steel at competitive prices, restrict our 
access to highly  specialized steel, create extra administrative burden and therefore,  impact our competitive 
advantage compared to other international competitors.     
 
According to  Orgalim, the association  representing Europe’s technology industries,  the introduction of 
the current proposal, might cost downstream steel users about 5.4 bn € a year.  Therefore, we deplore the 
absence of a full impact assessment on the downstream steel industry.     
 
Our companies  operate in a globally interconnected and turbulent landscape, where  supply chains are highly 
specialized and cannot be restructured overnight.  As a general comment,  we would like to stress that  our 
sector currently needs to  comply with  a high number of regulations, whereby it is important to focus on the 
possibility of effective and efficient transposition as well as on market surveillance.    
 
 
We therefore urge the Commission to:   
1. Focus its efforts on raw materials and not on the derivatives     
2. Remove the melt & pour obligation or at least simplify the declaration process     
3. Conduct a full impact assessment on the downstream industry     
4. Re-introduce the carry over mechanism    
5. Continue negotiations with the United States.    
  
 
We thank you in advance for your careful consideration and remain at your disposal for any further 
clarification.   
  
 
 
 
  
Luc De Beul

About Pneurop   
Pneurop is the European association of manufacturers of compressors, vacuum pumps, pneumatic tools and 
allied equipment, represented by their national associations. Pneurop members are national associations 
representing more than 200 manufacturers in 7 EU Member States, in the United Kingdom and in Turkey. 
The European market turnover for the business represented exceeds €20 billion.

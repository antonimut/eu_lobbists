10 December 2025 To: European Commission   Procedure: Proposal for a Regulation addressing the negative trade-related effects of global overcapacity on the Union steel market (COM(2025)726)  REQUEST FOR AMENDMENTS IN ANNEX I   Dear Sir or Madam, Members of the European Commission, We represent an ad hoc association of producers of steel tube and pipe butt-welded fittings (‘fittings’) of the European Union. The association of European producers represent a vast majority of the total Union production of fittings, composed primarily of small- and medium-sized enterprises in almost all Member States of the Union. Fittings are made of steel, carbon or stainless steel, and used to connect tubes and pipes. They are essential components of any tubes and pipes system, indispensable to crucial sectors like construction, gas pipelines, hydrogen, water infrastructure, nuclear powerplants, industrial machinery, marine shipbuilding (military), aerospace, and chemical and pharmaceutical infrastructures.  The European fittings industry welcomes the Commission’s initiative to address the critical challenges facing the EU steel industry through the proposed measures laid down in the Regulation Proposal COM(2025)726 (‘Regulation Proposal’).1  The European fittings industry however considers that the omission of carbon and stainless steel fittings from the scope of Annex I of the Regulation Proposal is an error. We call for the inclusion of carbon and stainless steel fittings in Annex I into two additional and distinct product categories.  This omission risks causing serious harm to the European fittings industry. It already suffers injury caused by increased imports. In addition, since August 2025, tube/pipe fittings are explicitly subject to U.S. Section 232 measures, with a 50% import duty applied under HTS heading 7307, likely to result in a diversion of trade to the EU. Finally, the omission of fittings undermines the Regulation’s effectiveness and the broader objectives of existing Union policies.2  1. The EU fittings industry is essential to preserve the EU’s strategic autonomy The European fittings industry welcomes the Regulation Proposal as an indispensable instrument to protect the steel sector. The EU fittings industry is flooded by imports worth more than €1,8 billion The strategic sector of steel tubes and pipes cannot be conceived without fittings. Fittings – classified under HS 73073 – include elbows, bends, reducers, caps and flanges and are manufactured by a set of different industries in the Union. Fittings are an essential part of any piping system. Steel tubes and pipes cannot be connected without fittings.  Similarly to tubes and pipes, the fittings sector is subject to global excess capacities creating negative trade effects. The imports of fittings into the EU correspond to over 360 000 tonnes, representing a value of over 1,8 billion euros per year. The inclusion of fittings is consistent with Union policies The inclusion of fittings in the Regulation Proposal prevents increased dependence on imports for the entire steel supply chain which delivers strategic products, machines and infrastructure. Fittings form an integral and indispensable part of the steel supply chain in the Union. Crucial sectors like energy pipelines (gas, hydrogen, nuclear, etc.), water, chemical and pharmaceutical infrastructures, industrial machinery and marine shipbuilding (military) need both tubes/pipes (included in Annex I) and fittings (excluded from Annex I) to connect the tubes/pipes. They are interdependent.  Without fittings, tubes and pipes cannot be used and essential infrastructures cannot be built. Therefore, extending the protection to fittings is crucial for the EU fittings industry and more importantly, for the entire steel supply chain. The omission of steel fittings from Annex I will result in a dependence on suppliers located in third countries, which will result in uncertainties for EU’s strategic industries to source fittings, undermining, inter alia, the EU’s defence sector and essential gas, and water infrastructure.  The inclusion of fittings is aligned with the stated objectives of the Regulation Proposal and of existing policies to preserve the strategic autonomy of the EU and protect the Union economy in the face of critical trade-related challenges.14  The inclusion of fittings is aligned with the objectives of the ‘Competitiveness Compass’ to act on the EU’s dependence on imports for strategic inputs, to secure the EU’s strategic supply chains15 and, as a result, to enable decarbonisation and industrial competitiveness.16 The inclusion of fittings will deliver on the objectives of the ‘Steel and Metals Action Plan’ (SMAP) which pledges to effectively respond to the global overcapacities that severely threaten the profitability and competitiveness of European industries.17 By including  fittings that face identical overcapacity and competitive pressures to products already included in Annex I, the Regulation Proposal will protect the supply chain and strengthen the Union’s capacity to achieve the SMAP’s core goals of industrial resilience, fair competition, and low-carbon transformation.18 Moreover, the inclusion of fittings would be consistent with Article 11 of the Treaty on

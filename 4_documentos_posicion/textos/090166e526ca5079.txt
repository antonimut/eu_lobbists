  Feedback to the Commission’s consultation on the Proposal for a Regulation addressing the negative trade-related effects of global overcapacity on the Union steel market 7 Steel Nordic welcomes the opportunity to comment on the Commission’s proposed trade measure to address the negative trade-related effects of global excess capacity on the EU steel sector. As a Nordic producer of low-carbon reinforcing steel, we support action to tackle unfair trade. In summary, aligning with the core objective of the proposed Regulation to address the negative trade-related effects of global excess capacity, 7 Steel Nordic calls on the Commission to focus on the following elements for implementing the proposed Regulation: 1. Melt-and-pour can only work with full value-chain traceability: Mill Test Certificates (MTCs) should be required for all imported steel products covered by the measure and ensure that MTC based traceability back to the original heat number is maintained and documented at every transformation step and passed along the value chain for all products placed on the EU market. 2. Quota attribution must follow crude-steel origin, not rolling location, to avoid circumvention: Individual country quotas should be used rather than broad “other countries” baskets and the same melt and pour/MTC logic should apply to semifinished imports that are rerolled and traded intra EEA. 3. Preserve EEA Single Market integrity and avoid new barriers within the EEA: Uphold the special status and integrity of the EEA Single Market from safeguard type out of quota duties and design melt and pour and MTC based traceability rules so that they do not create de facto barriers to intra EEA trade.  1. Melt-and-pour can only work with full value-chain traceability   We particularly welcome the proposed ‘melt-and-pour’ requirement as a cornerstone for determining the true origin of steel products. This requirement will only be effective if it is backed by robust, enforceable traceability across the entire value chain, including all imports and intra-EU trade of products made from non-European semifinished steel. We agree with the Commission’s assessment that a credible melt-and-pour requirement is essential to address circumvention and accurately attribute origin. The effectiveness of this requirement depends entirely on documentation tracing products back to the original liquid steel. The Mill Test Certificate (MTC) is a key, practical evidence of where steel was melted and poured. MTCs contain the heat number, the unique identifier of the original liquid steel, and essential technical data, making them the central document for verifying crude-steel origin.  Issuing MTCs is an existing responsibility of steelmakers, and they are routinely provided by serious producers worldwide, including all European mills. They are indispensable for effective CBAM implementation, which depends on verifiable traceability of production emissions and origin, and hence could be used for the steel measure with minimal extra administrative burden for companies. Today, a significant share of steel flows through complex value chains as billets are sold to rerollers, rerolled products are sold to transformers and fabricators, and finally to distributors and end-users. Without explicit traceability obligations between these nodes, the requirement risks becoming a formal label.  At present, many transformers and fabricators claim that they lack the capability to trace products back to the original heat number. There is no technical reason why this cannot be done and clear, MTC-based traceability rules will ensure that transformers and fabricators adapt quickly.

We emphasize that there should be no transition period for implementing the requirement for MTCs during customs declarations. MTCs are already an industry standard for all steel producers globally, and customs agencies should adopt them immediately once the new TRQs come into force. Delaying implementation would undermine the effectiveness of the measure.  In the rules for melt-and-pour, we urge that: o All imported steel products covered by the measure must be accompanied by MTCs indicating the melt-and-pour location, heat number, and producer.  o At every transformation step, traceability back to the original heat number must be maintained, and quality documentation must integrate key data from the original MTC. Importers and EU economic operators placing products on the EU market must ensure that all relevant documents are passed along the value chain.  o The presentation of valid MTCs should be a legal requirement for customs clearance within tariff-rate quotas. Imports without valid MTCs should automatically fall outside quota and be charged the maximum applicable tariff.   
2. Quota attribution must follow crude-steel origin, not rolling location, to avoid circumvention  A robust melt-and-pour framework must ensure that tariff-rate quotas follow the country where the crude steel was produced, not where it was rerolled, otherwise last-step relocation can be used to circumvent quotas and sanctions. Without correct attribution, material can enter the EU market indirectly via third countries and still count against those third countries’ quotas rather than against their true origin, undermining the effectiveness of the steel measure. The quotas should also be applicable on semifinished products imported into the EU, rerolled in European facilities and then traded between European Countries. Current exemptions for some semifinished products create incentives to replace finished with semifinished imports if MTC-based melt-and-pour traceability is not required through to the final product. If tariff-rate quotas on finished and semifinished products are not clearly established and enforced using their crude-steel origin, safeguards on rebar will simply be bypassed.  Linking the country of melt-and-pour to quota allocation must start immediately after the new TRQs come into force. Introducing a two-year gap before this linkage would significantly weaken the intended impact of the quotas and risk circumvention.  While not directly linked to melt-and-pour, we furthermore acknowledge the Council’s interest in allowing unused quotas to be transferred between quarters.  We therefore recommend that: o Once the MTC is provided, quota deduction must follow the country of melt-and-pour. For example, if rebar rolled in Country B uses Country A origin billets, it should count against Country A’s quota.  o A robust framework will require individual country quotas, hence no broad ‘other countries’ baskets should be applied as it undermines melt-and-pour enforcement.  o The same melt-and-pour/MTC logic must apply to semifinished imports that are rerolled and traded intra-EU as rerollers should be obliged to present MTCs for imported billets.  o Carry-over of unused quota should be limited to a maximum of two consecutive quarters to prevent excessive accumulation and opportunistic behavior by countries with large unused quotas.    
 
3. Preserve EEA Single Market integrity and avoid new barriers within the EEA   We support the Commission’s proposal to exclude EEA/EFTA States (Norway, Iceland, Liechtenstein) from the safeguard measures. Legal certainty on the free circulation of goods within the EEA is essential for investor confidence and for the cross-border investments underpinning Europe’s green industrial transformation. It is therefore crucial that the final design and implementation of melt-and-pour and MTC-based traceability requirements neither create new de facto barriers to intra-EEA trade nor treat EEA/EFTA States as third countries. 4. Industry Consultation We note the Council’s position to hold further industry consultation starting in October and express our willingness to actively support this process. 7 Steel Nordic is ready to participate and contribute expertise to ensure robust and practical implementation.  About 7 Steel Nordic   7 Steel Nordic is the leading Nordic steel company, offering some of the most sustainable and climate-efficient reinforcing steel available on the market. We produce reinforcing steel by recycling steel scrap in an electric arc furnace powered by clean, renewable hydropower—using one of the most environmentally friendly steelmaking technologies available. Our high-quality products include billets, reinforcing steel, and wire rods for sectors such as construction, energy, shipping, and rail. We operate a smelter and rolling mill in Mo i Rana, Norway. Our scrap receiving entity, based in Sweden, sources scrap from across the Nordic region and integrates it into the 7 Steel Nordic value chain. Our cut-and-bend companies carry out final processing and sales of reinforcement products in Norway, Sweden, Finland, and Denmark, providing significant economic activity and green industrial jobs within the EU. Our products are present in major European infrastructure projects such as the Öresund Bridge, the Copenhagen Metro and the Helsinki Olympic Stadium. 7 Steel Nordic employs approximately 500 people in the European Union. Our Environmental Product Declarations (EPDs) demonstrate our commitment to producing some of the greenest steel on the market and to supporting Europe’s green transition and long-term industrial competitiveness.

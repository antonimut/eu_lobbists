KEY RECOMMENDATIONS FOR THE DIGITAL OMNIBUS ON AI TIC Council, representing the independent Testing, Inspection and Certification (TIC) sector, offers the following seven recommendations to ensure effective implementation of the AI Act and the AI Omnibus, enabling Conformity Assessment Bodies (CABs) to support compliant, trustworthy AI systems across Europe. 1. Governance readiness • Establish clear timelines for national competent and notifying authorities to become fully operational under the AI Act.  • Enable CABs to apply for notification without delay so that sufficient Notified Body capacity is available.  • Clarify how existing notifying authorities under sectoral legislation will manage AI-related assessment tasks. 2. Timelines and postponement of high-risk AI provisions • Uphold the hard backstops of December 2027 (Annex III) and August 2028 (Annex I).  • Avoid making postponement decisions dependent on the availability of harmonised standards.  • Prevent further extensions that would increase uncertainty for providers and CABs. 3. Notification framework for Notified Bodies • Maintain the single application and single assessment procedure for designations under the AI Act and sectoral legislation.  • Streamline or remove AI-technology-specific codes in Annex XIV to ensure a practical, technology-neutral approach.  • Provide explicit confirmation that sectoral notifications may be extended to cover AI-specific conformity assessment. 4. Collaborative role of the AI Office and CABs for GPAI assessments • Avoid duplicative pre-market assessments that overlap between the AI Office and Notified Bodies. • Adopt a balanced model where the AI Office oversees consistency while CABs conduct the technical assessments. 5. Integrated assessment for sectoral products • Provide clarity on how integrated assessments combining AI Act and sectoral requirements will operate in practice. • Offer guidance on how Annex XIV codes apply in multi-sector contexts to ensure consistent national interpretations. 6. Smart compliance support for SMEs and SMCs • Ensure compliance facilitation measures remain risk-based rather than size-based. 7. Transparency in risk classification • Preserve a registration or transparency mechanism for systems self-declared as non-high-risk.

INTRODUCTION TIC Council, the international association representing the independent Testing, Inspection and Certification (TIC) sector, welcomes proportionate simplification that enhances clarity and supports the core objectives of the EU’s digital rulebook. TIC Council members, Conformity Assessment Bodies (CABs), have invested significantly in recent years to build the technical expertise, personnel and infrastructure needed to assess AI systems. Our members are ready to support industry and regulators in the implementation and application of the AI Act, and TIC Council looks forward to collaborating with the European Commission, the European Parliament and Member States to ensure that CABs will become fully operational as quickly and consistently as possible across the Single Market. In this spirit, TIC Council offers the following recommendations on the Digital Omnibus on AI1 (hereafter ‘AI Omnibus’) to enable smart, efficient compliance.  1. GOVERNANCE READINESS The effective implementation of the AI Act depends on the timely designation of national competent authorities2, including notifying authorities, in all Member States. These bodies are the basis of the AI Act’s governance structure, enabling CABs to apply for notification and become Notified Bodies (NBs). Once TIC Council members are designated as NBs, they can support industry in complying with high-risk AI requirements. To ensure that this governance structure functions as intended, several elements require clarification from co-legislators: • Designation of notifying authorities: TIC Council calls on co-legislators to include provisions requiring a clear roadmap and timeline for these bodies to become fully operational under the AI Act. This would help avoid delays that could jeopardise the overall governance framework and provide CABs with the predictability and certainty they need to prepare internally. • Notification and designation of CABs: Once notifying authorities are in place, it is essential that CABs can apply for notification without unnecessary delay, so that sufficient NB capacity is available to support manufacturers and deployers of high-risk AI systems. • Readiness for AI-related conformity assessment: A much-needed clarification is how existing notifying authorities under sectoral legislation listed in Annex I Section A are

TIC Council stands ready to share our technical expertise and practical experience to support Member States in designing efficient, robust designation and notification processes, including through guidance, training and exchanges of best practices. 2. TIMELINES AND POSTPONEMENT OF HIGH-RISK AI PROVISIONS In light of the delays in national implementation, which directly affect CABs' ability to support industry, TIC Council backs the proposed hard backstops of December 2027 for Annex III systems and August 2028 for Annex I systems (Art. 1(31)). However, we are concerned that decisions on postponement should not depend on the availability of standards, as this should not become a precondition for applying the AI Act’s high-risk requirements. We urge co-legislators to provide timely clarity and to uphold these hard backstops, as any further extension would only create additional uncertainty for providers and CABs, and could undermine the safety of EU citizens. 3. NOTIFICATION FRAMEWORK FOR NOTIFIED BODIES TIC Council strongly welcomes the proposal to allow CABs to submit a single application and undergo a single assessment procedure for designations under both the AI Act and sectoral legislation (Art. 1(10)). This should reduce duplication for multi-sector CABs and accelerate the notification process for NBs. Further, we appreciate the possibility for NBs to use existing designation documentation and certificates obtained under other EU harmonisation legislation as presented (Art. 1(11)). However, in practice, the reuse of existing documents and procedures is limited, as the conformity assessment of AI systems differs from the methodologies applied under sectoral laws (e.g., Radio Equipment Directive, Medical Devices Regulation, Machinery Regulation. Separately, the introduction of harmonised “codes” defining the scope of notification and detailing procedures for applications and assessments, as set out in Annex XIV, is, in principle, a positive development. Common codes will bring much-needed clarity and consistency to a process that can benefit from clearer guidance from notifying authorities, thereby supporting a more coherent, predictable and efficient notification pathway for CABs. Nevertheless, we note that the list of codes proposed in Annex XIV Section 3 (AI technology-specific codes) is based on an AI model taxonomy which does not align with current technical practice and introduces unnecessary complexity. From our practical experience working with customers and authorities, we believe this specific list of codes could be streamlined or removed entirely without compromising the effectiveness of the notification framework. A more technology-neutral approach would better support smart compliance and adapt more readily to evolving AI technologies.

Against this background, it remains unclear whether an existing sectoral notification can be extended to cover AI-specific conformity assessment through a targeted gap application. While the new approach based on harmonised codes appears to permit such an extension, this is not stated explicitly in the proposal. A clear confirmation from co-legislators would be essential to ensure legal certainty and to enable CABs to plan their designation strategies effectively. 4. COLLABORATIVE ROLE OF THE AI OFFICE AND CABS FOR GPAI ASSESSMENTS TIC Council suggests careful consideration of the proposal for the European Commission to perform pre-market conformity assessments for certain general-purpose AI (GPAI) models used in high-risk systems (Art. 1(25) (1c)). This raises questions about how this approach will operate in practice and how it will interact with the well-established system of NBs, which remains essential for ensuring a robust, expert-driven and independent conformity assessment. It is also unclear whether this would result in two separate conformity assessments, one carried out by the Commission and another performed by a NB, which could introduce duplication and additional administrative burden.  A collaborative model in which the same authority does not simultaneously act as assessor, market surveillance authority and enforcer may better support checks and balances and enhance functional clarity. Leveraging the expertise of TIC Council members would further strengthen the system by providing skilled, independent technical assessment capacities. TIC Council proposes a balanced system in which the AI Office coordinates oversight, guidance and consistency, while partnering with designated NBs to carry out technical assessments. We are committed to collaborating with the AI Office in developing technical guidance, good practices, and assessment methodologies for GPAI models used in high-risk contexts. 5. INTEGRATED ASSESSMENT FOR SECTORAL PRODUCTS TIC Council appreciates the move towards an integrated assessment model for high-risk AI systems covered by sectoral harmonisation acts, under which AI Act requirements are incorporated into existing product conformity assessments (Art. 1(13)). However, additional clarification would be helpful on how this model will operate in practice, particularly regarding the implementation of the quality management systems standard, the interplay with cybersecurity requirements and the role of different authorities. Greater clarity will help ensure consistent national interpretations and enforcement. Enhancing flexibility in notification options within this context would better support CABs operating across multiple regulated sectors and strengthen the ability of competent providers to support high-risk AI compliance effectively. TIC Council therefore suggests the development of detailed guidance on integrated assessments, including how Annex XIV codes should apply in multi-sector contexts.

6. SMART COMPLIANCE SUPPORT FOR SMES AND SMCS TIC Council supports proportionate efforts to ease the administrative burden for small and mid-sized organisations. At the same time, technical documentation, quality management and post-market monitoring requirements for high-risk AI systems should remain risk-based, not size-based. Compliance facilitation for small and medium enterprises (SMEs) and small mid-caps (SMCs) should complement the core safeguards that underpin the AI Act’s risk-based architecture. TIC Council members are ready to support SMEs and SMCs in meeting these requirements through tailored guidance, modular assessment services and practical tools that enable efficient compliance. 7. TRANSPARENCY IN RISK CLASSIFICATION TIC Council is concerned about the proposed deletion of the registration obligation for AI systems self-declared as “non-high-risk” (Art. 1(6), (14), (32)). This mechanism provides an important transparency safeguard, enabling regulators, civil society and market participants to understand and scrutinise why certain systems are exempted from high-risk obligations. Allowing providers to self-declare that their systems are not high-risk, while removing the obligation to justify such decisions in a public register, would reduce regulatory visibility and make misclassification harder to detect. Maintaining transparency would support accurate classification and strengthen overall compliance. TIC Council recommends that co-legislators explore options to preserve robust transparency requirements around high-risk classifications, for example, by maintaining an appropriate registration mechanism and by requiring providers to document and share the basis for their “non-high-risk” assessment. CONCLUSION TIC Council remains committed to working with the co-legislators to ensure that the AI Omnibus strengthens the AI Act through smart, efficient compliance pathways. By advancing the governance readiness in Member States, clarifying the notification framework and Annex XIV codes, enhancing transparency, establishing a collaborative role for the AI Office, and fully leveraging the expertise of CABs, the EU can build a robust and credible framework for safe, secure and trustworthy AI across Europe.

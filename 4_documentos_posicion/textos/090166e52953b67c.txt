1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY 
Proposal for a Regulation amending the AIA. 
On the EC Proposal for AI, Telefonica emphasises the importance of extending the 
timeline for the implementation of high -risk AI obligations, advocating that 
standards and guidance be available at least a year before these requirements take 
effect to ensure legal certainty and realistic compliance. The company further 
recommends aligning the transition deadlines for high -risk AI systems under both 
Annex I and Annex III, proposing a harmonised deadline to simplify regulatory 
processes, reduce complexity, and facilitate effective industry planning. Overall , 
Telefónica expresses strong support for the European Commission's proposal, 
recognising its positive contribution to creating a transparent, predictable, and 
balanced regulatory environment for AI across Europe.

2. Proposal for a Regulation amending the Artificial 
Intelligence Act.  
Telefónica strongly supports the Commission’s efforts to deliver a clear, 
proportionate and workable framework for the AI Act, and welcomes the objectives 
of the AI Digital Omnibus to enhance legal certainty and simplification.  
From Telefónica’s perspective, effective implementation of the AI Act requires, first 
and foremost, the timely availability of standards and guidance. These instruments 
should be in place at least one year before the application of high-risk AI obligations, 
as they are essential to ensure legal certainty, proportionality and realistic 
compliance planning. In this context, Telefónica supports the delay of high -risk 
requirements but stresses that automatic application of obligations in the absence 
of standards should be avoided. 
Telefónica also advocates for aligning the implementation timelines for high-risk AI 
systems under Annex I and Annex III. Divergent transition deadlines introduce 
unnecessary complexity and uncertainty; a harmonised deadline would improve 
coherence, reduce administrative burden and facilitate effective industry planning. 
For simplification and coherence, Telefónica advocates for aligning the 
implementation timelines for both Annexes, setting August 2028 as the harmonised 
limit date. 
Given the original application dates foreseen in the AI Act, Telefónica underlines the 
urgency of swiftly adopting the Digital Omnibus. Any delay risks undermining legal 
certainty and the orderly roll-out of the regulatory framework. 
More broadly, Telefónica welcomes several proposals that contribute to 
simplification and proportionality, including adjustments to AI literacy obligations, 
the removal of registration requirements for non-high-risk use cases, clarifications 
on the process ing of special categories of data for bias detection and mitigation, 
and the introduction of simplified technical documentation templates for SMEs and 
start-ups. Telefónica also supports the proportionality -based approach to quality 
management systems, whi le highlighting the need for clarity on responsibilities 
across complex supply chains. 
The company further supports targeted clarifications on the interaction between the 
AI Act and other EU legislation, as well as additional guidance on key concepts such 
as the definition of safety components, criteria for non –high-risk classification, 
governance of EU -level AI sandboxes, supervisory roles of the AI Office, and the 
allocation of responsibilities in areas such as generative AI content marking. 
Finally, Telefónica considers that regulatory clarity would be further enhanced by 
publishing a consolidated version of the AI Act alongside any amending legislation. 
Providing consolidated texts reflecting all applicable changes would significantly 
facilitate implementation and compliance efforts for all stakeholders.

A coherent, predictable and proportionate regulatory environment will strengthen 
Europe’s global competitiveness and enable strategic sectors, including 
telecommunications, to deploy AI in a safe, responsible and effective manner. 
• Availability of standards one year prior to Implementation of High -
Risk requirements 
Telefónica welcomes the decision to delay the implementation of high -risk 
requirements due to delays in the availability of standards and guidance. These 
instruments are essential to ensure legal certainty, support robust compliance 
processes, and uphold the principle of proportionality. 
Given the complexity of internal compliance processes, these elements must be 
available at least one year before  the corresponding high -risk provisions take 
effect. The current six -month transition for Annex III systems does not provide 
sufficient time for companies to prepare adequately. 
• Alignment of Implementation limit deadlines for Annex I and Annex 
III 
The coexistence of different transition deadlines for high -risk rules under Annex I 
and Annex III introduces unnecessary uncertainty. For simplification and 
coherence, Telefónica advocates for aligning the implementation timelines for both 
Annexes, setting August 2028 as the harmonised limit date. 
Such alignment would reduce administrative burden, increase clarity, and support 
more efficient industry planning. 
• Urgent Approval of the Digital Omnibus 
Given the original implementation date of 2 August 2026 for Annex III high -risk 
systems, it is critical that the Digital Omnibus be adopted without delay. Failure to 
do so risks creating legal uncertainty and undermining the effective roll-out of the AI 
Act.  
• Ensuring technological and stakeholders’ neutrality 
ePrivacy applies only to operators, whereas the GDPR applies across all sectors, 
including operators. If the GDPR is amended in relation to the AI Act, the ePrivacy 
legislation should either be updated to introduce equivalent amendments or be 
withdrawn altogether, given that the GDPR already applies.  
• Interplay with other EU legislation 
We welcome the approach of targeted changes to clarify the interplay between the 
AI Act and other EU legislation. However, harmonization of the AI Act with other legal 
acts will probably only be regulated in the Digital Fitness Check.

Telefónica welcomes the legal basis introduced in Article 4a. However, further 
clarification is required on the interpretation of “necessary and proportionate” 
processing. Without harmonized criteria, there is a risk of divergent enforcement by 
national data protection authorities. Telefónica recommends that future guidelines 
provide concrete examples and proportionality standards to ensure legal certainty. 
• Clarification of criteria for non–high-risk classification.  
Telefónica supports removing the registration requirement for Annex III systems that 
are not high -risk. To ensure consistent application, the Regulation should be 
complemented with operational criteria or examples defining “administrative” 
tasks. This woul d avoid divergent national interpretations and reinforce legal 
certainty. 
• Definition of “Safety Component” and Its Role in High -Risk 
Classification in the high-risk guidelines to be published 
A clear and proportionate definition of “safety component” is crucial to determine 
when an AI system should be considered high risk under the AI Act. Telefónica 
interprets a safety component as an AI system that directly prevents physical harm 
to people or material damage to physical assets or critical infrastructure. Although 
such a system may not be necessary for normal network functioning, it becomes 
essential when its failure could directly cause harm. 
EU law draws a clear line between safety and security: cybersecurity protects 
systems and data against integrity, availability, or confidentiality breaches, while 
safety concerns the avoidance of physical or material harm. For this reason, the AI 
Act specifies that components designed solely for cybersecurity purposes should 
not be treated as safety components. 
In telecom and digital networks, only AI systems that genuinely prevent direct 
physical harm or material damage should qualify as safety components. Systems 
aimed at avoiding indirect or non -physical harm, or harm caused by third parties, 
fall outside this  definition. Likewise, basic connectivity functions such as simple 
data transmission or the ability to initiate calls do not constitute safety functions, as 
they do not directly prevent harm. 
This distinction is key: the AI Act classifies only the AI -based safety component —
not the full system —as high risk. Clarifying this concept is essential to avoid over -
classifying operational or cybersecurity systems that do not play a direct safety role, 
ensuring obligations remain proportionate and aligned with the Act’s objectives.

• Governance and access rules for EU-level AI Sandboxes. 
While Telefónica supports the creation of sandboxes, the proposal will need to be 
complemented within the framework ongoing a consultation regarding participation 
criteria, prioritization, confidentiality safeguards and interoperability with national 
sandboxes. In addition, it is important to explicitly address the need to create 
incentives for participation in these sandboxes. Without an appropriate incentive 
framework, participation risks being limited, which could undermine the 
effectiveness of sandboxes as to ols for experimentation, learning, and regulatory 
alignment. 
• AI Office supervision of article 75. 
Telefónica supports the strengthening of the AI Office’s role. It must be clearly 
regulated where the responsibility of local authorities begins and where the 
responsibility of the AI office ends when it comes to the surveillance of the use of 
those AI Systems by the deployers. 
Additionally, to avoid unintended overlap with the Digital Services Act, Telefónica 
recommends clarifying the criteria of what could be deemed as an “AI system that 
constitute or that are integrated into a VLOP/VLOSE” for the purposes of Article 75” . 
In th is regard, Telefonica considers that AI systems distributed through an 
VLOP/VLOSE should not be considered as constituting nor integrated. Likewise, 
Telefonica also considers that when a provider/deployer other than the VLOP/VLOSE 
provider develops, tests, operates or puts into service an AI system or model using 
a VLOP/VLOSE (for example, an IaaS / PaaS cloud provider), such activities should 
neither be considered as constituting nor integrated in the VLOP/VLOSE.  
• Responsibility for content marking in generative AI systems. 
Clarification is needed on the allocation of obligations between model developers 
and integrators. Telefónica seeks guidance on whether integrators must implement 
or preserve marking solutions embedded in third-party GPAI models.

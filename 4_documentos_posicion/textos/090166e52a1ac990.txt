Amadeus Response 
 
On the EU Commission’s Digital Package and Omnibus.1 
 
Introduction 
Europe’s political focus on competitiveness marks a welcome and necessary paradigm 
shift, which is particularly relevant for the industrial ecosystem surrounding travel and 
transport - one of Europe’s most economically significant sectors in terms of 
employment, contribution to GDP, and long ‑term growth potential .2 Ensuring that this 
ecosystem remains competitive  globally is essential for Europe’s broader economic 
resilience and strategic autonomy. 
Amadeus plays a central role in strengthening Europe's competitiveness: Firstly, by being 
among Europe's top investors into software R&D.3 An area, highlighted by Mario Draghi’s 
report on European Competitiveness, key for closing the competitiveness gap with the US 
and China.4  Secondly, as an enabler for the digital transformation of travel and transport, 
particularly by driving the uptake of digital technologies. Thirdly, by competing globally in 
the IT and digital markets, demonstrating that Europe can host world ‑class digital 
companies operating at scale. 
Maintaining and enhancing  competitiveness will require sustained investment  in 
digitalization, sustainability, and resilience. Achieving this depends heavily on regulatory 
certainty and a modern, agile and future‑proof legal framework. Europe needs rules that 
enable the adoption of cutting ‑edge digital technologies while ensuring robust safeguards 
for European values.  
Amadeus welcomes the first wave of regulatory simplification and  attention given to 
the B2B technology domain, where Europe has well-established competitive strengths. At 
the same time, a second wave of simplification will be essential . This should focus on 
addressing the current fragmentation and institutional imbalance when it comes  to 
enforcement and supervision, and on ensuring that emerging political priorities - especially

General Remarks 
 
The “blanket regulation”  approach for digital technologies based on ex -post legal 
principles has led to a high degree of uncertainty . Instead, a risk-based approach with 
attention to specific cases should be strengthened, which would increase legal clarity and 
facilitate targeted, solution-oriented discussion s.  The wide use of  secondary 
legislation should also be targeted as it poses problems for companies in terms of 
transparency and timely feedback for often deeply technical and complex questions.   
 
On the AI Omnibus5 
 
1. Amadeus supports improvements of regulatory  sandboxes on AI . The omnibus 
proposal should include an EU ‑level fast ‑track mechanism including a licensing 
system and mutual recognition  for companies that successfully complete national 
or EU sandboxes. The goal is to avoid restarting full authorization processes after 
supervised experimentation.  
2. Amadeus supports the stop the clock approach so that rules apply after technical 
standards become available with sufficient time for companies to implement them , 
e.g. 12 months between publication of the standards and application of the 
corresponding obligations of the AI Act.  
3. Amadeus suggest recognizing voluntary ex‑ante governance tools as compliance 
instruments.6 This would reduce duplication, support investment and procurement, 
and bridge self‑regulation with formal regulatory requirements.

On Privacy (Art. 3 in the Digital Omnibus). 
 
1. Amadeus supports the proposal to validate legitimate interest as a legal basis to 
process personal data for the development of AI models and systems .7 It should be 
complemented with clear criteria to understand what will be required for a positive 
balancing test.  
2. Amadeus supports the new definition of personal data  (pseudonymized is not 
personal data for those who can’t re-identify), but further clarification is required on 
the contractual requirements (e.g. will requirements under art. 28 remain applicable). 
Clarifications should also be made on the responsibilities through the chain of 
transfer, or the expected duties of those who transfer , to verify the ability of the 
recipient to re-identify.8  
3. Amadeus supports the additional exception for the processing of biometric data 
for the purposes of uniquely identifying a natural person  introduced. The exception 
should be written in a technology agnostic way . In addition, the wording of the 
proposal should be reviewed (deleting the reference to identity verification) to 
ensure consent remains a valid option for biometrics when identity verification is 
optional.9  
4. Amadeus supports the  new definition of scientific data and the recognition of 
technological development and demonstration  as a key component and societal 
wellbeing in that sphere and would expect further clarification and confirmation that 
product development when for the good of society is within the scope.10 
5. Amadeus suggests expanding the scope of regulatory sandboxes to other 
regulatory domains - including GDPR and special categories of data. Data 
Protection Authorities should be obliged to provide pan-EU regulatory sandboxes to 
enable innovation in a safe and controlled environment, reducing the risks to both 
individuals and companies, enhancing single market integration. 
6. Amadeus supports the additional limitations to the right of access, which reflects 
reality. Such limitations  do not imply restrictions on privacy for individuals but just 
reinforce the intention behind the access right, i.e. that the right shall not be used for 
other purposes in contravention of the law.11

On Data (Art. 1 in the Digital Omnibus). 
 
In principle, the EU’s data framework should aim for maximum clarity and prevent any 
overlaps. Legal uncertainty still exists, for example, regarding contractual clauses for data 
processing services, e.g. on the date of application.  
 
1. Amadeus suggests clarifying that obligations under the Data Act should only 
apply to contracts concluded after entry into force of the Data Act to avoid having 
to reopen negotiations for existing contracts. This also means that the term 
“custom-made” in the provisions on relaxing cloud switching rules should be 
removed to reduce compliance burden. 13  This term describes a  vague concept, 
leading to regulatory uncertainty and commercial ambiguity. 
2. Amadeus rejects any prohibition on charging termination fees, which is critical 
for SaaS.14 For instance, f ixed-term contracts are offered in return for sometimes 
significant customer discounts which providers need to recover in case of early 
termination. 
 
On Cyber (Art. 6 in the Digital Omnibus).

touch on definitions. The proposal does not take necessary steps to address streamlining

also a missed opportunity. It would be desirable if the Omnibus was more ambitious by 
introducing alignment across definitions and timelines.  
 
1. Amadeus supports the Single-Entry Point (SEP) mechanism for incident 
reporting.15 In addition, there should be clear liability protection clauses in NIS2 and 
CRA, as it will reduce th e burden of reporting the same incident multiple times. The 
SEP should be complemented by a harmonization of rules what companies must

Digital Fitness Check. 
 
The digital omnibus is a good first step but should not be the end of the reflection process. 
A deeper, more structural look, in particular on enforcement issues, is needed. Amadeus 
welcomes the concept of a digital fitness check to create a regular and institutional 
process to check whether legislation is achieving the political objectives. 
 
1. Enforcement and supervision: Oversight must be centralized for cross -border 
cases in AI, privacy and data policie s paired with structured coordination with 
national authorities and meaningful involvement of representative business 
associations.

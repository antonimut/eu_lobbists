CEE Ambassador for AI and New Technologies  
POSITION ON DIGITAL OMNIBUS ON AI PROPOSAL 
 
 
CEE general view  
On 19 November 2025, the European Commission presented the Digital Omnibus on AI 
Proposal as targeted amendments to simplify implementation of the AI Act, ease 
regulatory burden and strengthen proportionality. 
From a Central and Eastern European perspective, EU rules must enable the adoption 
and scaling of AI in economies with a strong industrial base, export orientation, and 
complex supply chains, w hile safeguarding trust, security, and fundamental rights. EU -
level reflections, including the Draghi report on Europe’s competitiveness, point to a 
simple need: predictable and workable rules that let companies invest and scale across 
the Single Market. Europe should aim to be a global AI leader again, given its strong open-
source and foundational research legacy in AI. We should build on that legacy with faster 
deployment and wider uptake. 
When obligations are complex or uncertain, deployment slows, R&D i nvestment falls, 
and cross -border collaboration becomes harder. It also makes AI more expensive to 
develop and place on the market, and those costs reach users. The result is fewer 
capable tools, slower upgrades, and growing frustration that Europeans cann ot access 
the same quality of AI as elsewhere. Simplification can reduce these pressures, with 
estimated savings up to EUR 433 million and additional benefits from clearer, more 
streamlined rules. 
We, CEE Ambassador for AI and New Tech, support an ambitiou s simplification of the 
digital and AI regulatory landscape that reduces cumulative compliance burden and 
strengthens predictability, so innovation can flourish without lowering protection 
standards. Safety is not in dispute, but it is not achieved by exce ssive documentation. It 
requires effective oversight and clear technical practices to maintain strong safeguards. 
We consider the Commission’s Digital Omnibus on AI Proposal a first step in the right 
direction, notably on support for SMEs and small mid -caps, alignment with other 
legislation, and providing more time to the stakeholders. At the same time, EU can not 
stop here. It should go further towards significant structural simplification, consolidation 
of the whole digital regulatory landscape and to leg al certainty, so Europe leads in AI 
innovation, not only in AI regulation.

Key recommendations on the Digital Omnibus on AI 
1. Extend the postponement and align the timelines for high -risk 
systems 
Commission proposal (article 113):  
● High-risk AI obligatio ns shall apply following the adoption of a decision of the 
Commission confirming that adequate measures in support of compliance with 
Chapter III are available, from the following dates: 6 months after the adoption of 
that decision as regards AI systems classified as high-risk pursuant to article 6(2) 
and Annex III, and 12 months after the adoption of the decision as regards AI 
systems classified as high-risk pursuant to article 6(1) and Annex I.  
● In the absence of the adoption of the decision within the meaning of subparagraph 
1, or where the dates below are earlier than those that follow the adoption of that 
decision, Chapter III, Sections 1, 2, and 3, shall apply: on 2 December 2027 as 
regards AI systems classified as high-risk pursuant to article 6(2) and Annex III, and 
on 2 August 2028 as regards AI systems classified as high -risk pursuant to article 
6(1) and Annex I.  
 
We propose to: 
• Strengthen legal certainty for stakeholders not by aligning the application dates 
for Annex I and Annex III AI systems with the Commission’s evaluation of 
compliance tools, but rather by aligning them to a single date: 2 August 2028.  
• Set a binding delivery timeline for harmonised standards, common specifications, 
guidance, and other compliance tools - well ahead of the above application date - 
together with transparent progress reporting. 
• If EU tools are delayed, allow compliance using standardised templates. 
 
2. Simplify for SMEs and small mid-caps 
Commission proposal (article 11, broadly 3, 17, 63, 95, 96, 99):  
 Extend regulatory simplifications granted to small and medium -sized enterprises 
(SMEs) to small mid -caps (SMCs), including simplified technical documentation 
requirements and special consideration in the application of penalties  
 
We agree and further propose to: 
• Provide EU -level simplifying templates and standardised documentation 
packages to make such simplification operational in practice. 
• Clarify B2B versus B2C impacts so consumer -facing requirements are not 
automatically applied to purely industrial contexts.  
• Within the upcoming Digital Fitness Check, identify more provisions where 
compliance obligations for SMEs and small mid-caps can be further simplified. 
 
3. Registration: simplify the whole process  
Commission proposal (articles 6, 49): 
 Non-high-risk AI systems falling under article 6(3) should be exempt from EU 
database registration, while providers must still document their classificat ion 
assessment and provide it to competent authorities on request. 
 
We propose to: 
• Reduce administrative burden around registration in general, not only by 
abolishing non-high-risk AI systems falling under article 6(3). 
• For all high-risk AI systems, impl ement only the minimum necessary registration 
requirements with only essential information. 
• Allow the option for authorities to request additional information ex post.  
 
4. EU sandboxes and real-world testing: enable scale-up through cross-
border recognition 
Commission proposal (articles 57, 58, 60 + new article 60a): 
● Stronger and more consistent EU -level cooperation and governance for AI 
regulatory sandboxes, including procedural simplification by combining the 
sandbox plan and real-world testing plan into one document, and enabling the AI 
Office to set up an EU-level sandbox for certain systems.  
● Expand real -world testing beyond sandboxes to additional high -risk AI systems 
covered by EU harmonisation legislation. 
 
We propose to: 
• Explicitly include SMEs and small mid-caps in access to sandboxes and support 
services. 
• Use a standard contractual framework for real -world testing to ensure 
transparency, predictability and consistency. 
• Accelerate EU -level sandboxes and ensure availability well before 2028, at the 
latest when high -risk obligations start applying (regulatory sandboxes are most 
effective when available during the early phase of regulatory application). 
• Participation in AI regulatory sandboxes will be totally cost -free for startups and 
SMEs. 
• The impleme nting acts should support cross -border sandbox projects and 
support recognition of results obtained in joint sandboxes, enabling SMEs to scale 
solutions across the Single Market without duplicating testing efforts. 
• Support an EU -level sandbox under the AI Office and expand real -world testing, 
including cross -border cooperation, as key tools for innovation and practical 
compliance. 
 
5. AI literacy 
Commission proposal (article 4): 
● Replace the current AI literacy obligation for providers and deployers of AI systems 
with a softer approach where the Commission and Member States shall 
encourage providers and deployers of AI systems to take measures to ensure a 
sufficient level of AI literacy of their staff and other persons dealing with the 
operation and use of AI systems. 
 
We agree and further propose to: 
• Clarify that providers’ and deployers’ role in AI literacy is only supportive, 
promoting that people using AI systems have appropriate knowledge and skills.  
 
6. Remove duplication with sectoral legislation: o ne audit and one 
documentation set 
Commission proposal (articles 28, 29, 43):  
● Where high-risk AI systems are already subject to conformity assessment under 
Union harmonisation legislation in Annex I, Section A, the Commission proposes 
using that single se ctoral assessment, with AI Act high -risk requirements 
assessed within the same process. It also enables reusing existing designation 
evidence and introduces a single application and assessment procedure for 
notified bodies seeking designation under both regimes, reducing duplicate steps 
while keeping scrutiny unchanged. 
 
We agree and further propose to: 
• Explicitly state the principle of one conformity assessment, one audit, and one set 
of documentation for both sectoral legislation and AI Act.  
• Support a harmonised standard for the quality management system requirement 
as a prerequisite for proving the conformity. 
 
7. Annex XIV NANDO codes 
Commission proposal (article 30 + new Annex XIV): 
● New Annex XIV with harmonised codes and categories for AI system types used in 
NANDO has been introduced, to standardise and clarify the scope of each notified 
body’s designation. 
● Empower the Commission to update Annex XIV by delegated acts to reflect 
technical progress, and delete Annex VIII, Section B. 
 
We agree and further propose to: 
• Ensure codes are clear, usable, and consistent with the conformity assessment 
framework.  
• Issue EU guidance on the interpretation and practical application of NANDO 
codes to reduce divergent national approaches. 
8. Bias mitigation and sensitive d ata: narrowly tailored and consistent 
with EU data protection law 
Commission proposal (new article 4a): 
● It introduces a legal basis to exceptionally process special categories of personal 
data for bias detection and mitigation (subject to strict safeguards , and where 
necessary and proportionate) also for providers and deployers of all AI systems. 
 
We are aligned with this proposal.  
 
9. Codes of practice: guidance, not a law  
Commission proposal (articles 50, 56): 
● Remove the Commission empowerments in Articles  50 and 56 AI Act to adopt 
implementing acts to give codes of practice for general purpose AI models and 
transparency obligations for certain AI systems general validity in the Union.  
● Codes of practice remain a compliance tool supported at the Union level , but are 
not turned into binding rules via implementing acts. 
 
We are aligned with this proposal.  
 
10. Post-market monitoring 
Commission proposal (article 72): 
● Remove its power to adopt a harmonised Post -market monitoring plan template 
binding for the providers.  
● The Post -market monitoring plan remains required and part of technical 
documentation, but providers have more flexibility to tailor monitoring to their 
system and organisation. 
 
We are aligned with this proposal.  
 
11. Responsibilities for oversight of the Member States 
Commission proposal (article 75):  
● Centralising oversight under the AI Office over a large number of AI systems built 
on general-purpose AI m odels when both model and that system are developed 
by the same provider (except Annex I systems) and over AI systems embedded in 
very large online platforms and very large search engines.  
 
We propose to: 
● Broaden the exception from covering only Annex I  systems to also include AI 
systems referred to in Annex III, point 2 (critical infrastructure) 
 
12. Confirm the exception for research and development 
 
Beyond the Digital Omnibus, we propose to: 
• Confirm that this AI Act does not apply to AI systems or AI models, including their 
output, that are developed, tested or used exclusively within the context of 
research and development activities, including commercial R&D, provided that 
such systems or models are not yet placed on the market or put into service; and 
that research and development covers all stages of scientific and applied 
research, including industrial research and experimental development, prior to 
market placement or putting into service.  
• Examine whether the A I Act’s wording and implementation are aligned with the 
objectives of EU-carried out or EU -funded research and innovation projects, and 
whether they support their successful delivery and cross -border collaboration, 
including strategic initiatives (such as OpenEuroLLM). 
 
13. Practical guidance and ready-to-use compliance templates 
Beyond the Digital Omnibus, we propose to: 
• Provide EU-level guidance together with easy -to-complete templates, checklists 
and model documentation packages that allow providers to demonstrate 
conformity with the Regulation and fulfil key requirements in a consistent, efficient 
and auditable manner.

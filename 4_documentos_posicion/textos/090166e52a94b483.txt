UITP Statement on the Digital 
Omnibus Proposal 
 
The International Association of Public Transport ( UITP) and its European members support the 
Commission’s objective of simplifying and improving the coherence of the EU ’s regulatory 
framework on data through the Digital Omnibus initiative. Ensuring greater legal clarity, reducing 
administrative burdens and streamlining overlapping obligations across EU legislation are 
important steps for strengthening Europe’s competitiveness and enabling the effectiv e 
deployment of digital technologies across sectors. 
Public transport operators and authorities play a central role in Europe’s digital and green 
transitions. Modern public transport systems increasingly rely on data-driven operations, including 
the use of connected vehicles, predictive maintenance tools, smart charging systems, and digital 
mobility services. Access to operational and technical data is therefore essential to ensure 
efficient fleet management, service reliability, energy optimisation and the successful integration 
of zero-emission vehicles. 
While UITP welcomes the overall objective of the Digital Omnibus, it is important to ensure that 
the proposed adjustments to the EU data framework do not unintentionally reduce the ability of 
public transport operators and authorities to access the data necessary to operate and maintain 
their fleets. 
Access to vehicle and battery data 
The Data Act Regulation (EU) 2023/2854  establishes important rights for users of connected 
products to access and use data generated by those products and related services. For the 
public transport sector, this framework is essential to ensure that operators have access to 
operational vehicle data generated by buses and rail rolling stock deployed in their networks. 
Such data include telematics data, diagnostic information, predictive maintenance data, 
battery parameters such as State of Charge (SoC) and State of Health (SoH), degradation curves, 
and smart charging parameters. These datasets are critical for fleet management, safety, service 
continuity and energy optimisation.

EU legislation such as the Data Act, the EU Batteries Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 and the revised 
Renewable Energy Directive (EU) 2023/2413, in particular Article 20a, already recognise the 
importance of access to battery data for system integration and energy optimisation.  
UITP emphasises that the Digital Omnibus should preserve the existing balance of the Data Act 
and ensure that the rights of users of connected products to access operational data remain fully 
effective in practice. 
Trade secrets exception and access to data 
UITP notes that the Digital Omnibus proposal introduces strengthened safeguards for trade 
secrets in the context of data sharing obligations, notably through the proposed amendment to 
Article 4(8) of Data Act. 
While the protection of legitimate trade secrets is important, it is equally essential that this 
safeguard does not lead to disproportionate restrictions on access to operational data required 
for the functioning of public transport services. 
Public transport operators must retain effective access to the data generated by the vehicles they 
operate. This includes data necessary for maintenance, fleet optimisation, battery management 
and smart charging. 
UITP therefore stresses that the trade secrets exception should remain strictly limited to 
exceptional and well -justified circumstances and must not become a general mechanism to 
restrict access to operational vehicle and battery data. Any refusal to share data on the basis of 
trade secrets should be duly justified and notified to the competent authority, as foreseen in the 
Data Act framework. 
Legal certainty for vehicle data access 
UITP welcomes the guidance published by the European Commission accompanying the Data 
Act regarding the scope of vehicle data covered by the Regulation (Commission 
Communication C/2025/5026). This guidance provides valuable clarification for the automotive  
ecosystem and confirms that raw and pre -processed vehicle data fall within the scope of the 
Data Act. 
Maintaining this legal certainty is essential for the development of a competitive and innovative 
mobility ecosystem. Any future legislative adjustments should therefore ensure that the 
interpretation of vehicle data access under the Data Act remains stable and predictable. 
Data access for public authorities 
UITP also notes that the proposal introduces adjustments to the Business -to-Government (B2G) 
data access framework under the Data Act. 
Public authorities and transport operators increasingly rely on data to ensure the resilience, safety 
and efficiency of public transport systems. It is therefore important that the regulatory framework 
continues to enable the use of relevant data in situat ions where this supports public interest 
objectives.

An improved GDPR  
UITP members experience a heavy regulatory burden related to the implementation of the 
General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). We therefore welcome the changes that are being 
introduced by the Digital Omnibus, including the  clearer definition of personal data, the 
clarification on how data subjects must be informed, and the reporting obligations in case of a 
data breach. These changes make the implementation easier and reduce the burden on the 
side of the companies.  
The additional exemption to the processing of data used to confirm the identity of a data subject 
might be useful in certain situations in a transport context. Moreover, the improved protection of 
companies against the abuse of the right of access by data subjects for purposes other than the 
protection of their personal data is welcome.   
Conclusions 
The Digital Omnibus represents an important opportunity to simplify the EU regulatory framework 
on data and improve its coherence. UITP supports these objectives and welcomes efforts to 
reduce unnecessary administrative burdens. 
At the same time, it is essential that the simplification process preserves the ability of public 
transport operators and authorities to access the operational data required to manage their 
fleets, maintain services and support Europe’s green and digital transitions. 
UITP therefore calls on EU policymakers to ensure that the Digital Omnibus: 
• Preserves effective access  for public transport operators and authorities to operational 
vehicle and battery data 
• Ensures that the trade secrets exception under the Data Act remains strictly limited to 
exceptional and duly justified circumstances. 
• Ensures that access to operational vehicle and battery data supports the operational 
independence of public transport operators and avoids situations of technological 
dependency or vendor lock-in.

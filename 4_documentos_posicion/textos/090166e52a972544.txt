“

ˮ

Providing
 clear

 additional  clarification

2.  GDPR  
Decathlon  welcomes  the  proposed  amendments  to  the  GDPR.   
However,  we  would  like  to  raise  concerns  regarding  the  proposed  new Article  88b in  GDPR.  As  
envisaged,
 
Article
 
88b
 
would
 
introduce
 
a
 
framework
 
for
 
automated
 
and
 
machine-readable
 
indications
 
reflecting
 
individualsʼ
 
choices
 
in
 
relation
 
to
 
data
 
processing,
 
and
 
would
 
require
 
website
 
providers
 
to
 
recognise
 
and
 
comply
 
with
 
such
 
indications
 
once
 
the
 
relevant
 
standards
 
have
 
been
 
established.
 
While
 
we
 
support
 
the
 
objective
 
of
 
strengthening
 
user
 
control
 
and
 
promoting
 
harmonised
 
technical
 
solutions
 
across
 
the
 
Union,
 
we
 
are
 
concerned
 
that
 
this
 
provision
 
may
 
create
 
additional
 
compliance
 
uncertainties
 
and
 
could
 
limit
 
the
 
autonomy
 
of
 
economic
 
operators
 
in
 
designing
 
and
 
managing
 
consent
 
and
 
information
 
mechanisms,
 
including
 
content
 
banners.
 
Indeed,
 
we
 
believe
 
it
 
creates
 
a
 
compliance
 
paradox
 
by
 
subordinating
 
a
 
data
 
controllerʼs
 
legal
 
obligations
 
to
 
the
 
automated
 
settings
 
of
 
third-party
 
browser
 
providers.
 
By
 
allowing
 
browser-level
 
signals
 
to
 
override
 
the
 
interface,
 
the
 
provision
 
may
 
technically
 
prevent
 
operators
 
from
 
even
 
displaying
 
a
 
consent
 
banner
 
to
 
explain
 
their
 
data
 
practices,
 
effectively
 
stripping
 
them
 
of
 
the
 
ability
 
to
 
obtain
 
freely
 
given
 
and
 
informed
 
consent.
 
This
 
replaces
 
a
 
direct,
 
nuanced
 
dialogue
 
between
 
the
 
brand
 
and
 
the
 
consumer
 
with
 
a
 
binary
 
software
 
toggle,
 
resulting
 
in
 
significant
 
legal
 
uncertainty
 
and
 
a
 
loss
 
of
 
autonomy
 
for
 
the
 
economic
 
operator.
 
Therefore,
 
we
 
recommend deleting  Article  88b  of  the  GDPR to  avoid  
potential
 
compliance
 
uncertainties.
 
 
Regarding  Data  Evaluation  protocols,  we  find  the  new Article  41a unclear  and  potentially  
burdensome,
 
as
 
it
 
does
 
not
 
address
 
how
 
authentication
 
methods
 
shift
 
over
 
time,
 
creating
 
a
 
risk
 
that
 
data
 
previously
 
classified
 
as
 
non-personal
 
could
 
be
 
reclassified
 
as
 
personal.
 
It
 
also
 
fails
 
to
 
address
 
the
 
legal
 
and
 
retrospective
 
consequences
 
for
 
operators
 
if
 
a
 
previously
 
accepted
 
pseudonymization
 
protocol
 
is
 
later
 
invalidated
 
by
 
new
 
jurisprudence.
 
We
 
recommend deleting  
Article
 
41a
 
from
 
the
 
GDPR
 as  the  Article  creates  legal  uncertainty  and  retrospective  liability  for  
operators
 
without
 
improving
 
data
 
protection.
 
Deleting
 
it
 
would
 
preserve
 
the
 
effectiveness
 
of
 
existing
 
pseudonymization
 
practices
 
and
 
ensure
 
regulatory
 
clarity
 
for
 
operators.
 
 
3.  NIS  2  
Decathlon  supports  improving  cybersecurity  resilience  under  NIS  2  by  creating  a  single  entry  
point
 
for
 
incident
 
reporting
 
obligations.
 
However,
 
there
 
is
 
a
 
need
 
for
 
coherence,
 
alignment,
 
and
 
practical
 
feasibility
 
across
 
the
 
EU
 
regulatory
 
framework
 
to
 
ensure
 
that
 
obligations
 
are
 
implementable
 
for
 
businesses.
 
At  the  moment,  we  may  report  the  same  incident  to  multiple  authorities  with  slightly  differing  
deadlines,
 
increasing
 
complexity
 
and
 
cost.
 
Therefore,
 
we
 
recommend ensuring  alignment  of  
cybersecurity
 
incident
 
reporting
 
obligations
 
across
 
EU
 
legislation
 
to
 
avoid
 
overlapping
 
or
 
conflicting
 
notification
 
requirements.  Additionally,  we  also  call  on harmonising  the  
terminology,
 
definition,
 
and
 
criteria
 
for
 
incident
 
reporting.  Divergent  and  overlapping  terms  
such
 
as
 
"
significant
 
incident
"
 
under
 
NIS
 
2
 
and
 
"
severe
 
incident
"
 
under
 
the
 
Cyber
 
Resilience
 
Act
 
can
 
trigger
 
distinct
 
and
 
complex
 
internal
 
processes
 
for
 
similar
 
events.
 
Additionally  we  call  on  EU  institutions  to  consider  extending  the  notification  deadline  under  
NIS
 
2
 
and
 
the
 
Cyber
 
Resilience
 
Act
 
from
 
24
 
to
 
96
 
hours,  as  currently  proposed  under  the  
GDPR
 
through
 
amended
 
Article
 
33
 
paragraph
 
1,
 
to
 
provide
 
sufficient
 
time
 
for
 
accurate
 
reporting
 
while
 
maintaining
 
security
 
standards.
 
We
 
believe
 
extending
 
the
 
deadline
 
to
 
96
 
hours
 
balances
 
 
timely  reporting  with  the  ability  to  submit  accurate,  meaningful  information.  Harmonized  
reporting
 
reduces
 
administrative
 
burden,
 
improves
 
incident
 
response,
 
and
 
strengthens
 
EU-wide
 
digital
 
resilience.

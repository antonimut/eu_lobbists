FRANCE ASSUREURS POSITION ON THE DIGITAL OMNIBUS PACKAGE 
 
The insurance sector is facing an unprecedented level of regulatory inflation at the European level, characterised by 
the proliferation of Level 1, Level 2 and Level 3 instruments that are often overlapping, sometimes redundant, and 
insufficiently coordinated. This regulatory complexity has itself become a risk, weighing on legal certainty, effective 
compliance and the competitiveness of European undertakings, as well as on the customer journey. It is further 
exacerbated by the insufficient use of robust and systematic ex ante impact assessments, which limits the evaluation of 
the cumulative effects of regulation, its overall coherence, and its practical implementation by undertakings. 
 
In this context, France Assureurs strongly welcomes the European Commission’s initiative aimed at simplifying and 
streamlining the digital regulatory framework, in line with the objective of better regulation, without calling into 
question the fundamental objectives of protecting rights, ensuring financial stability and safeguarding security. 
France Assureurs’ key requests on digital omnibus and digital omnibus AI:  
 Set a predictable and stable date of application: establish a fixed and certain date for the entry into application 
of the AI Act provisions relating to high- risk AI systems. An application date of 2 December 2027 appears 
reasonable and proportionate, provided that the implementing measures and supporting instruments are published 
sufficiently in advance to allow stakeholders to undertake the necessary organisational, technical and contractual 
adjustments. 
 Prioritize the review of the AI Act implementation timeline:  separate the measure postponing the AI Act 
application timeline from the remainder of the omnibus proposal and provide for an accelerated procedure. Failing 
this, the postponement — which is essential to enable undertakings to comply under legally secure conditions — 
risks being delayed by the length of the legislative process, thereby depriving the measure of its beneficial effects 
and creating significant legal uncertainty in the interim. 
 Right of access: clarify the circumstances in which a request may be considered manifestly unfounded or 
excessive. 
 
PRELIMINARY REMARKS 
 
The inflation of European Level 1 legislation (directives and regulations), combined with the multiplication of Level 2 
instruments (delegated acts, regulatory or implementing technical standards) and Level 3 instruments (guidelines, 
guidance, Q&As), constitutes one of the factors explaining the slowdown in European competitiveness, particularly in 
the financial sector. 
 
This inflation has resulted in a very significant increase in the number of legal instruments applicable to the insurance 
sector: the number has thus risen from approximately 12 instruments in 2012 to nearly 70 instruments in 2024 that are, 
or will be, applicable to this sector. The insurance sector is therefore subject to a dense body of sector-specific and cross-
sectoral regulation, some of which has been adopted in response to new challenges, notably digital and sustainability-
related developments. 
 
While France Assureurs fully supports the objectives pursued in terms of financial stability and the protection of 
policyholders, it must nevertheless be acknowledged that the European regulatory framework has become particularly 
complex and difficult to navigate. The multiplicity of legal instruments and the layering of different levels of regulation 
generate overlaps, and at times contradictions, which create legal uncertainty for undertakings.

Digital regulation including the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), the Artificial Intelligence Act (AI Act), and 
the Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA) provide an essential framework for ensuring data protection, 
strengthening European digi tal sovereignty, and promoting the development of responsible innovation. Although 
necessary, their implementation also raises significant operational challenges. The interplay between these different texts, 
which can sometimes be complex to understand in a coherent way, can generate substantial compliance burden and 
mobilize considerable resources to the detriment of innovation, the very exercise of insurers’ core activities and, 
ultimately, the interests of policyholders. 
 
In this context, the insurance sector strongly welcomes and fully supports the European Commission’s 
initiative to simplify the European regulatory framework. 
 
PART 1 : DIGITAL OMNIBUS 
 
I. Data 
 
The European Union has progressively developed a regulatory framework governing access to, sharing and circulation 
of non- personal data, notably through the Regulation on the free flow of data, the Open Data Directive, the Data 
Governance Act and the Data Act. While this dynamic has laid the foundations for a European data economy, the layering 
of these instruments has also generated regulatory complexity for undertakings. The proposals under the Digital Omnibus 
aimed at rationalising and clarifying this legal framework therefore move in the direction of simplification. 
 
France Assureurs therefore fully supports the proposal to establish the Data Act as the reference instrument 
and central framework governing data access and data sharing. This simplification approach should lead to a 
reassessment of related legislative initiatives that are still under discussion. 
 
France Assureurs also welcomes the proposals aimed at harmonising the definitions of key concepts, notably through 
explicit references to the GDPR for the notions of consent and pseudonymised data, thereby strengthening the 
coherence, clarity and legal certainty of the applicable framework. Finally, the provisions allowing data holders to refuse 
the disclosure of trade secrets where there is a high risk of unlawful disclosure or use towards third countries or entities 
subject to jurisdictions offering a low er level of protection than that of the Union constitute a major step forward. 
Extending this protection to situations involving disclosure to third parties appears essential in order to preserve the 
legitimate economic interests of European undertakings while maintaining a balanced framework for data sharing. 
 
II. Personal Data Protection 
 
The application of the GDPR raises significant operational challenges for insurers, notably due to the sometimes complex 
interaction between different legal instruments, the multiplication of obligations, and certain divergences in interpretation 
and implementation across the European Union, which create legal uncertainty. These difficulties may hinder both 
insurers’ effective compliance and their ability to respond efficiently to the needs of their policyholders. 
 
France Assureurs therefore welcomes the European Commission’s initiative aimed at introducing targeted and 
pragmatic measures within the GDPR framework. In particular, France Assureurs welcomes and fully supports 
the following measures: 
o Limiting notification obligations to personal data breaches that present a high risk to the rights and freedoms of the 
data subjects concerned. 
o The notification of personal data breaches through a single incident reporting gateway. 
o The establishment by the European Data Protection Board (EDPB) of a common template for personal data breach 
notifications. 
o The development by the EDPB of unified lists of processing operations that require, or do not require, the conduct 
of a Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA). 
o Clarifying the possibility of relying on automated decision- making where such decisions are necessary for the 
conclusion or performance of a contract, irrespective of the fact that the decision could, in theory, be taken otherwise 
than by exclusively autom ated means. Moving from an approach based on derogations to a framework based on 
enhanced safeguards for processing makes it possible to provide greater legal certainty for the use of automated 
decision-making, particularly in the context of contract performance. The clarification that the necessity of relying

on a solely automated decision must be assessed independently of the existence of alternative non-automated means 
addresses proven operational needs while maintaining the essential safeguards provided by the GDPR for the 
protection of the rights of data subjects. 
 
France Assureurs also supports the following developments, subject to further clarification: 
 
o The easing of the information obligation where the controller has reasonable grounds to assume that the data subject 
already possesses the required information, except in particular in cases involving disclosure to third parties, transfers 
outside the EU, automated decision- making, or processing presenting a high risk, constitutes a welcome measure. 
This development would help limit the over -provision of information to data subjects, which is often 
counterproductive and may undermine the objective of ensuring the clarity and readability of contractual 
documentation pursued by insurers. 
France Assureurs’ requests: 
 Clarify the conditions under which it may be considered that there are “reasonable grounds” to assume 
that the data subject already possesses the required information. 
 Specify the criteria for assessing the existence of a high risk to the rights and freedoms of the data subjects 
concerned. 
 
o The extension of the deadline for notifying personal data breaches to the supervisory authority, from 72 to 96 
hours, is a welcome development. 
France Assureurs’ request: 
 Introduce the same deadline with regard to the obligation incumbent upon the processor to notify any 
personal data breach to the controller (Article 33(2)). 
 
o The proposed developments concerning the exercise of GDPR data subject rights are welcome, in particular: 
o the possibility for the controller to refuse to act on a request or to require the payment of reasonable fees where 
the request is manifestly unfounded, excessive, or submitted for purposes unrelated to the protection of 
personal data; 
o the clarification of the burden of proof, allowing the controller to demonstrate that the request is unfounded 
or excessive where it has reasonable grounds to do so; 
o the clarifications introduced in Recital 35, which explicitly recognise certain situations of abuse, notably where 
the right of access is exercised with the intention of causing harm to the controller, and which recall the 
obligation for the data subject to formulate sufficiently specific requests, with excessively broad or 
undifferentiated requests potentially being considered excessive. 
Indeed, the widespread use of data subject rights for purposes other than those provided for under the GDPR 
entails particularly burdensome obligations for insurers, and notably for data protection officers, including in 
situations where the controller is entitled to refuse the request. This situation is further exacerbated by the practices 
of certain actors who seek to exert pressure — in particular through the threat of complaints to supervisory 
authorities — in order to obtain the disclosure of information. 
 
In order to ensure sufficient legal certainty and the effective functioning of the framework, the proposals should 
therefore be further complemented. 
 
France Assureurs’ requests: 
 Specify the situations in which a request may be qualified as manifestly unfounded, excessive, or 
unrelated to the protection of personal data. 
 In this respect, it would in particular be appropriate to: 
o supplement Recital 35 in order to clarify that excessively broad and undifferentiated requests should, in 
themselves, be considered excessive requests within the meaning of the GDPR; 
o explicitly recognise that certain requests pursuing purposes other than the protection of personal data (for 
example in the context of litigation, an investigation, or a suspicion of fraud) may be subject to 
adjustments, or even refusal, where the disclosure of the information would undermine the purposes 
pursued or the legal obligations of the controller. In order to identify such situations, the text could specify 
the criteria for characterizing a request that pursues an objective other than data protection  
o allow the controller to refuse a request when the data subject already possesses the requested information.

III. Privacy and Electronic Communications   
 
The European ePrivacy Directive on privacy and electronic communications requires, subject to certain exceptions, the 
prior consent of the end user for any operation involving the storage of or access to cookies and other tracking 
technologies. 
 
France Assureurs welcomes the European Commission’s initiative to establish the primacy of the GDPR, by 
clarifying that where the information stored or accessed constitutes personal data, or leads to the processing of 
personal data, the legal basis relied upon under the GDPR prevails and is, in itself, sufficient. This clarification 
of the interaction between these two legal instruments is welcome. 
 
IV. Cybersecurity 
 
The European digital regulatory framework is based on a plurality of sectoral and cross -sectoral instruments (notably 
DORA, NIS2, the Cyber Resilience Act (CRA), the GDPR, the AI Act, eIDAS and the CER Directive), which pursue 
converging objectives but each establish specific incident notification obligations. This layering exposes regulated entities 
to multiple notifications, sometimes in relation to the same incident. 
 
France Assureurs fully supports the proposal to create a single incident notification gateway, which addresses 
previously expressed concerns regarding the complexity arising from the multiplicity of notification procedures 
and deadlines, as well as the div ersity of competent authorities to which notifications must be submitted. The 
creation of such a single gateway would allow undertakings to submit incident notifications required under the GDPR, 
the DORA Regulation, the NIS2 Directive, the eIDAS Regulation and the CER Directive through a single interface. This 
gateway would also enable undertakings to retrieve the information submitted in order to demonstrate compliance with 
their notification obligations. This approach would significantly reduce the administrative burden on undertakings, which 
are currently confronted with a variety of notification formats. It would also allow compliance teams to focus on incident 
management rather than on notification procedures addressed to multiple authorities in different formats. 
 
Furthermore, the time required to classify incidents that must be notified raises operational challenges, as it requires the 
collection of information from numerous business functions in order to assess the criteria established by the DORA 
Regulation. In line with the proposal to extend the deadline for notifying personal data breaches, the sector would also 
welcome an extension of the deadline for cyber incident notifications, in order to allow teams sufficient time to gather 
the information necessary for the proper classification of incidents. 
 
France Assureurs’ proposals: 
 Extend the scope of the single incident notification gateway to include the Artificial Intelligence Act, which 
provides for an obligation to notify serious incidents involving high-risk AI systems. 
 Clarify the implementation timeline for the single incident notification gateway: France Assureurs understands that 
the gateway for incident notifications would only become operational once it has been assessed as ensuring 
adequate functioning, including guarantees regarding the reliability, integrity and confidentiality of notifications. 
As this simplification measure is particularly anticipated, France Assureurs calls for the establishment of an 
indicative timeline for the deployment of this platform.

France Assureurs welcomes the safeguards provided for this mechanism, in particular the interoperability between the 
various notification obligations to  avoid duplication, the consideration of sector -specific features and, from a more

 Data sharing relating to incident notifications between authorities : France Assureurs calls for clarification

this additional functionality. 
 
 
PART 2: DIGITAL OMNIBUS AI 
 
The use of algorithms, statistical models and automated techniques is not new for insurers, as such practices have long 
been governed by strict sectoral obligations, particularly with regard to governance, transparency, risk management, data 
protection and consumer protection. The AI Act (AIA) applies to the insurance sector and is added to an already dense 
and demanding sectoral regulatory framework. The European Commission itself has acknowledged that financial 
regulation provides a solid foundation. However, the AI Act introduces a set of new cross -sectoral obligations that raise 
significant operational and legal challenges for insurers. In this context, the European Commission’s initiative to simplify 
digital regulation appears particularly timely. France Assureurs fully supports these proposals and would like to 
provide additional comments that contribute to the objective of simplification. 
 
The progressive entry into application of the AI Act is taking place even though several key elements of the implementing 
framework have not yet been finalised: 
o not all guidelines have been published; 
o harmonised standards have experienced significant delays; 
o competent authorities at national level have not yet been officially designated in several Member States and, as a 
result, have not been able to express their expectations regarding compliance. 
Under these circumstances, undertakings are placed in a situation of clear legal uncertainty. In the absence of 
implementing and supporting measures finalised in advance - particularly with regard to high-risk AI systems - 
undertakings would have no choice but to attempt to comply with the obligations of the Regulation without having access 
to the reference frameworks, guidance and tools that are nevertheless strictly necessary. 
 
France Assureurs welcomes the proposal to postpone the timeline for the entry into application of certain 
provisions of the AI Act. Such postponement constitutes a necessary measure to allow economic operators to prepare 
under legally secure conditions, in particular with regard to the obligations applicable to high-risk AI systems. 
 
 
6 
 
However, France Assureurs considers that the proposed mechanism - consisting in making the entry into application of 
the obligations conditional upon the effective availability of harmonised standards, common specifications and 
Commission guidelines, followed by a formal confirmation decision by the Commission and a subsequent six -month 
period - would be a source of complexity and legal uncertainty. 
 
Such a mechanism would introduce a variable and conditional date of application, likely to undermine the predictability 
of the regulatory framework and to complicate companies’ compliance efforts. Legal certainty and the proper planning 
of investments instead require a clear, stable and foreseeable deadline. 
 
France Assureurs therefore considers that it would be preferable to establish a pre-determined date of application, while 
ensuring that the accompanying measures (harmonised standards, common specifications and guidelines) are published 
sufficiently in advance to allow for effective and proportionate compliance. 
 
France Assureurs’ requests: 
 Establish a predictable and stable date of application: set a fixed and certain date for the entry into application 
of the AI Act provisions relating to high-risk AI systems. An application date of 2 December 2027 would appear 
reasonable and proportionate, provided that implementing measures and supporting instruments are published 
sufficiently in advance to allow stakeholders to carry out the necessary organisational, technical and contractual 
adjustments. 
 Prioritise the review of the AI Act implementation timeline:  separate the measure postponing the AI Act 
application timeline from the remainder of the Digital Omnibus package and provide for an accelerated procedure. 
Failing this, the postponement - which is essential to enable undertakings to comply under legally secure conditions 
- risks being delayed by the length of the legislative process, thereby depriving the measure of its beneficial effects 
and creating significant legal uncertainty in the interim. 
 With regard to incident notification under the AI Act, it would also be appropriate to: 
o Clarify and secure the arrangements for notifying AI incidents  where the deployer is required to notify 
in place of the provider, particularly where the latter is not in a position to carry out the notification itself. 
France Assureurs highlights the practical and legal difficulty for the deployer to notify a serious  incident 
without having sufficient access to the relevant information held by the provider. 
o Simplify the deadlines for notifying serious incidents involving high-risk AI systems , which currently 
provide for multiple time limits, creating operational complexity and increasing the risk of non-compliance.

ABI Comments 
The Italian Banking Association (ABI) welcomes the opportunity to comment on the 
EU Commission “Inception impact assessment” relating to the initiative 
“Amendments to the Capital Requirements Regulation and the Capital Requirements 
Directive”.  
This represents a unique opportunity to highlight general remarks and to draw the 
attention of the Commission to some elements that the Italian banking industry would 
recommend considering in assessing the impact of the regulatory measures. 
First, ABI welcomes that the Commission is committed to the implementation of the 
Basel III finalisation package “taking into account European specificities and the 
objective stated by the Council and the European Parliament for the reforms not to 
result in a significant increase in the overall capital requirements for the banking 
sector”. ABI fully supports this goal.  
With specific regard to the identification of objectives and policy options in the 
“Inception impact assessment”, ABI would remark that, wh ile “continued and 
adequate financing of the economy” is mentioned along with “ensuring financial 
stability” as broad objectives of the legislative proposal, the breakdown into more 
specific goals shows points pertaining to financial stability rather than directly 
pertaining to the financing of the economy. Anyway, ABI appreciates that the 
Commission explicitly states the objectives of level playing field for institutions - both 
within the EU and vis -à-vis third countries  - proportionality, and reduction of  
administrative burden. 
Still with respect to the identified specific objectives, ABI would highlight the 
importance of the objective “to address inconsistencies or mistakes in the existing 
rules”. In this regard, ABI would respectfully suggest that the Commission takes the 
opportunity of the legislative proposal to address also aspects of the regulatory 
framework - other than those mentioned in the “Inception impact assessment” - 
where the industry might point out, over the next months, the need to fix 
shortcomings of the current regulations. 
Indeed, in ABI’s view it is time to conclude the wave of reforms of prudential 
regulation and to achieve a stability of the framework. Hopefully the forthcoming 
CRR3/CRD6 will therefore represent the last occasion, for  many years, to address 
shortcomings of the existing regulations. ABI would thus encourage the Commission 
to consider, and to carefully assess, for possible inclusion in the legislative proposal, 
also measures outside the scope of the current Inception impact assessment.  
As to the impact assessment, the importance cannot be stressed enough of a proper 
assessment of the likely impact of the measures  under consideration , in order to 
ensure that the benefits, in terms of improved financial stability , outweigh the

negative effects on the real economy , via restrictions in the size and conditions of 
the credit supply.  Needless to say, this is key for Europe, due to the predominant 
role of banks in financing corporate, SMEs and households.  
In this respect, it is worth noting that assessments of the macroeconomic impact of 
the Basel III finalisation package , performed by different entities , led to different 
outcome in terms of whether its net impact on the economic growth in the EU would 
be positive or negative. ABI would highlight the importance of basing the assessment 
on reasonable assumptions, in terms of the capital needs that banks will face and the 
actions they will likely take to restore their capital ratios. Also, when comparing costs 
with benefits, it should be taken into account that the estimation of benefits, in terms 
of reduced probability of financial crises due to increased capitalisation, is subject to 
higher uncertainty - especially if we consider t he current high level of capitalisation 
of banks. 
In addition, ABI observes that, in the Inception impact assessment, the possibility 
that the costs of the proposed measures be outweighed by the benefits is mentioned 
in particular with respect to a long-term horizon. ABI would urge the Commission to 
pay specific attention to possibl e negative s hort-term impacts . Such aspect  raise 
major concerns since these impacts would occur at a point in time when the 
Authorities themselves acknowledge that the key challenge is no longer financial 
stability – also due to the huge increase of banks’ capitalisation over the last decade 
- but rather banks’ profitability, and when the European eco nomy is undergoing a 
period of still positive but indeed modest growth  (which would be put into question 
in case of new regulations with restrictive effects).  
The time overlap with the effects of other measures should also be taken into account 
– reference is made for example to the TLAC/MREL eligible liabilities requirements, 
which will likely determine banks’ recourse to the funding market in the coming years. 
Also, the consistency of the proposed measures with other policies and strategic 
objectives of the Union  ought to  be properly assessed. Policy objectives such as 
ensuring SME and infrastructures financing, as well as full integration of the banking 
market cross-border, development of the Capital Market Union, encouraging banks’ 
investments in technology, should be given appropriate consideration.  The 
application of disproportionate capital requirements could in fact ultimately 
discourage banks from certain businesses and investments. Due attention should be 
paid to enterprises engaged in sustainability and to social enterprises. 
Overall, in order for banks to have time for adapting to the new standards – in respect 
to both the impact on capitalisation and the organizatio nal challenges, ABI 
recommends adopting appropriate phase -in arrangements, not limited in scope to 
the output floor framework.

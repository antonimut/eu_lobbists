EU IPU – MiFID-authorised asset managers 
 
Background 
 
Under the currently in-force (but not yet transposed and applied) version of CRD5 Article 21b, 
the EU IPU requirement does not extend to MiFID -authorised asset managers . This is 
because the CRR2 Article 4(1)(2) definition of “investment firm” specifically excludes MiFID-
authorised asset managers.  
 
We believe this should be the co rrect outcome, and has always been the policy intention.  
Asset managers, whether authorised under MiFID, UCITS or AIFMD, were never intended to 
be in scope of the EU IPU provisions.  
 
Asset managers are very different businesses to broker-dealers and other investment firms – 
they are not at all “bank like”. MiFID -authorised asset managers, or UCITS/AIFMD asset 
managers with MiFID “top up” permissions, have a limited range of MiFID permissions. These 
limited permissions correspond to MiFID II Annex I Section A points (1), (2), (4) and (5): 
 Reception and transmission of orders in relation to one or more financial instruments 
 Execution of orders on behalf of clients 
 Portfolio management 
 Investment advice 
 
The above activities encompass the range of activities that an asset manager may typically 
perform. 
 
The EU IPU requirement must be transposed by EU member states by 28 December 2020. 
In-scope third-country groups will need to implement the requirements by 30 December 2023.

Effect of IFD/IFR amendments t o CRD/CRR, and implications for MiFID -authorised asset 
managers 
 
However, r ecent amendments to CRD/CRR via IFD/IFR bring MiFID -authorised asset 
managers into scope of the EU IPU requirement. This is for two main reasons. 
 
Firstly, IFD Article 62(9) amends CRD5 Article 21b by adding CRD5 Article 21(b)(5)(b): 
 
“For the purposes of this Article [21b], … the term “institution” shall also include 
investment firms.” 
 
Secondly, IFR Article 62(3)(b) amends CRR2 Article 4(1)(2) – the definition of “investment 
firm” for CRR purposes – by deleting the carveouts for local firms and MiFID-authorised asset 
managers: 
 
‘investment firm’ means an investment firm as defined in point (1) of Article 4(1) of 
Directive 2014/65/EU [MiFID II] which is authorised under that Directiv e but excludes 
credit institutions;  
excluding the following: 
(a) Credit institutions; 
(b) Local firms; 
(c) Firms which are authorised to provide the ancillary service referred to in point (1) 
of Section B of Annex 1 to Directive 2004/39/EC [MiFID I], which provide only one 
or more of the investment services and activities in points 1, 2, 4, 5 of Section A of 
Annex I to that Directive, and which are not permitted to hold money or securities 
belonging to their clients which for that reason may not at any time place 
themselves in debt with those clients. 
 
In isolation, these two amendments made by IFD/IFR make sense. But in combination, they 
have the effect of bringing MiFID-authorised asset managers into scope of EU IPU when this 
was never the intention and never originally proposed.  
 
The amendment made by IFD Article 62(9) is intended to ensure that the investment firms that 
would have been subject to the EU IPU requirements under the CRD/CRR regime, will remain 
subject to the EU IPU requirements when they move to the IFD/IFR regime which applies from 
26 June 2021 . The amendment made by IFD Article 62(9) was necessary because the 
CRD/CRR definitions of “institution” and “credit institution” were being changed under IFD/IFR. 
Had the amendment in IFD Article 62(9) not been made, the EU IPU requirements would only 
have applied to credit institutions and “class 1 investment firms”  of in -scope third country 
groups – a narrower range of firms than originally envisaged.  
 
The amendment made by IFR Article 62(3)(b) essentially deletes the “local firms” and “MiFID-
authorised asset managers” carveouts from the CRR2 Article 4(1)(2) definition of investment 
firms. This reflects that local firms and MiFID-authorised asset managers would generally no 
longer be subject to the CRD/CRR regime and would move across to the IFD/IFR regime. 
Therefore, for almost all CRD/CRR purposes (capital requirements, governance, etc), it was 
no longer necessary to maintain the local firms and Mi FID-authorised asset manager 
carveouts. 
 
However, when the effect of these amendments are combined, it means that for the first time, 
MiFID-authorised asset managers would be in scope for the EU IPU requirements. 
 
Inclusion of asset managers in the EU IPU framework creates an unlevel playing field between 
asset managers that are part of a group subject to EU IPU, and asset managers that are not. 
It also creates an unlevel playing field between MiFID -authorised asset managers, and

UCITS/AIFMD authorised asset managers. And as said above, asset managers are not “bank

Furthermore, including asset managers into an EU IPU structure into the same corporate 
structure, making such structures more complex, when third -country groups have m ade 
significant efforts over the last ten years to simplify their corporate structures and align legal 
entities and groups of legal with business activities. Corporate governance and management

Bringing asset managers into the scope of EU IPU would significantly complicate recovery 
and resolution planning for in-scope third-country groups, when significant efforts have been 
made to enhance the resolvability of such third -country groups since the introduction of the 
Bank Recovery and Resolution Directive. 
 
 
Drafting Solution 
 
In order to recalibrate the scope of the EU IPU provision so as to exclude MiFID -authorised 
asset managers, as originally intended and drafted, we suggest the following amendment to 
CRD5 Article 21b(5)(b). This should restore the application of EU IPU to credit institutions and 
MiFID authorised investment firms but excluding asset managers. 
 
In marked-up format (marked up from the version as already amended by IFD Article 62(9)): 
 
Article 21b 
 
Intermediate EU parent undertaking 
 
… 
 
5. For the purposes of this Article [21b]: …  
 
(b) the term “institution” shall also include investment firms , but excluding 
those “investment firms”: 
 
(i) which are not authorised to provide the ancillary service referred 
to in point (1) of Section B of Annex 1 to Directive 2016/65/EU 
[MiFID II] 
 
(ii) which provide only one of more of the investment services and 
activities in points (1), (2), (4) and (5) of Section A of Annex I to 
Directive 2016/65/EU [MiFID II] 
 
(iii) which are not permitted to hold money or securities belonging to 
their clients and which for that reason may not at any time place 
themselves in debt with those clients.

Internal TLAC - scaling 
 
CRR2 Article 92b  (Requirements for own funds and eligible liabilities for non -EU G -SIIs) 
became applicable on 27 June 2019.  
 
CRR2 Article 92b sets the internal TLAC scalar at 90% of the external TLAC requirement that 
would have applied (under Article 92a) if the material subsidiary was a resolution entity. 
 
The FSB TLAC Term Sheet (9 November 2015) recommended an internal TLAC range of 75-
90% (ultimately to be determined by supervisors).  
 
Our concern is  that setting the internal TLAC scalar at 90% may result in extensive pre -
positioning of capital requirements in several jurisdictions, if other jurisdictions take the EU’s 
90% approach into account. In addition, this approach does not align with an overall approach 
of aligning with international standards.

In marked-up format: 
 
Article 92b

G-SII G-SIIs and that is are not a resolution entity entities shall at all times satisfy 
requirements for own funds and eligible liabilities equal to a percentage (as 
determined by the institution ’s resolution authority) 90 % of the requirements for 
own funds and liabilities laid down in Article 92a.  The percentage determined by 
the resolution authority shall be between 75-90 %.

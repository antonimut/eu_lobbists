TREATMENT OF INVESTMENTS IN COLLECTIVE INVESTMENT UNDERTAKINGS (CIUS) 
 
Question 150 
 
What are the proportion and characteristics of the CIUs where a look -through is possible and 
how frequent is this possible? Please provide relevant evidence. 
 
UCITS funds are legally required to publish the full composition of their portfolio at least once a 
year in their annual report. In addition, asset managers typically release monthly and quarter ly 
fund factsheets disclosing additional information on the portfolio composition of the funds they 
manage (allocation percentages across asset classes, top holdings, stock sector weightings, 
etc. …) throughout the year (see for instance: Morningstar fund factsheets). 
 
Question 151 
 
What are the proportion and characteristics of the CIUs traded in the EU for which the mandate 
of the CIU is available and daily price quotes can be obtained? Please provide relevant 
evidence. 
 
All UCITS funds are required to publish certain information in their prospectus as well as in their 
KIID, including a ‘Description of the fund’s investment objectives, including its financial 
objectives (e.g. capital growth or income), investment policy (e.g. specialisation in geographical 
or industrial sectors), any limitations on that investment policy and an indication of any 
techniques and instruments or borrowing powers which may be used in the management of the 
fund. 
 
Alternative Investment Funds (AIF s) are under a similar obligation to disclose the investment 
strategy and objectives of the fund and the techniques it may employ and all associated risks 
(AIFM Directive, article 23). 
   
Examples of typical investment restrictions contained in a fund’s in vestment strategy could for 
instance include: 
o Max. 50% equity investments 
o Up to 25% of the fund's net assets may be exposed to emerging market equities 
o Min 50% in fixed income and money market instruments 
o Bond investments in emerging markets may not exceed 25% of the net asset

UCITS funds must establish the net asset value (NAV) of their units, communicate them to the 
competent authorities and publish them at least twice a month. This is a minimum requirement, 
and in practice, most UCITS (and other open-ended CIUs) establish and publish their NAV daily.  
 
Question 153 
 
Would you consider that the revised approaches for calculating the own fund requirements for 
CIUs in the SA-MR would significantly affect investments in those instruments? If yes, would 
there be any solutions to address this issue prudentially? Please explain and provide relevant 
evidence. 
 
{ YES }  
 
A water-fall of three approaches is foreseen for Fund-related products under SA-MR:   
 
- The full Look-Through approach; 
- The construction of hypothetical portfolios mimicking the funds composition as 
represented by their mandate; 
- The fall-back to the residual bucket conceived to a small cap emerging market equity. 
 
In case, as it seems, the first two approaches were to turn impractical/very cumbersome to 
implement, a Bank will be forced to fall -back to a capitalization of the fund business unde r the 
residual bucket.  This being the case a fund or fund derivative product - despite being fully 
transparent in its composition and mandate and showing daily prices - will be subject to a RW 
that suggests it might be losing 70% of its value in the space of the capital horizon.  
 
This seems disproportioned considering the extremely diversified nature of the assets making 
up the fund which is additionally confirmed by the typically very low realised volatility that is 
much lower than the one of e.g. Equity Indices.  
 
Funds treatment under Default Risk Charge should also be addressed since under the current 
text a Jump to Default 0 (JtD0) assumption is mandated. This is clearly inappropriate if we think 
of a fund a s a portfolio of some hundreds instruments.

EFAMA Recommendation 
 
EFAMA would recommend the fall-back approach to be at least re-directed to the Index bucket 
(RW 15%). Additionally a more realistic Jump to Default (JtD) should be allowed, e.g. 5%.

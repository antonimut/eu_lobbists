This document contains the position of CNA – the National Confederation of Crafts and Small 
and Medium Enterprise s - one of the  most important Italian organizations representing  
craft  and  SME companies, on the European Commission Consultation paper for the 
implementation of the final Basel III reforms in the EU.  
 
General comments 
CNA welcomes the opportunity to comment the consultation document implementing the 
final Basel III reforms in the EU.  
However, we would like to point out our inner concerns about the impac t of these reforms 
on SME financing in Italy.  
Micro and small enterprises of the non -financial business sectors are the backbone of the 
Italian economy. They account for the 99,4% of the total enterprises and with 66, 3% of 
employment they account for the 50,4% of the value added making the 40,2% of the total 
investments (Istat, 2016).  
In these years, the most radical change has been certainly the transformation of the so-called 
Relationship Banking Model which has had peculiar connotations for our smallest 
companies. The multilevel regulatory reforms, including Basel III and its reforms, and a 
context characterized by a permanent economic crisis led the banks to follow more selective 
procedures in granting credit.  
We are still facing today, after a decade, low volumes of SME financing in Italy, which are far 
below the pre-crisis level.  
Besides, the decrease of the interes t rates applied and the subsequent loss of the banks’ 
profitability and fixed preliminary costs make it inconvenient to provide credit to micro and 
small businesses which is so necessary to their working capital financing.  
It should also be noted that the Italian banking sector has already experienced a process of 
significant capital enhancement in recent years, which has substantially improved its 
stability and its ability to support the economy.  
A request for further capital increases could lead to restrictions in the granting of credit - 
especially for micro  and small enterprises - with negative consequences for the economy, 
which in turn could affect the soundness of the banking sector. 
It is therefor e particularly important for our country to prevent credit from being further 
penalised and to provide measures to encourage funding access even for the smallest 
companies.

Specific topics relevant for SME financing 
 
SME SUPPORTING FACTOR  
The risk weight of loans to non -rated corporates / SMEs is in general 100%, which was 
linked in Basel 2 into the requirement for 8% regulatory capital. When Basel 3 requested 
10.5% capital in case of 100% risk weight, the European legislators introduced the so-called 
SME Supporting Factor, which ensured that the capital requirement stays at 8%. The 
Supporting Factor of 23.81% (risk reduction) applied first to SME loans up to 1.5 million has 
been increased this year to 2.5 million. In parallel, the Basel  Committee reduced the risk 
weight for all SMEs to 85% ( - 15%), which already now applies for SME loans above 2.5 
million.  
The new Basel agreement requires the cancellation of the current SME Support ing Factor, 
which would increase the risk weight for all SMEs loans to 85%.   
 
CNA requests the continuation of the SMEs Supporting Factor as defined by Regulation (EU) 
876/2019 (CRR2) in order to avoid any unnecessary tightening of lending to SMEs.  
By eliminating the SME supporting factor there will be a distortive increase of the regulatory 
capital provision subject to SME loans.  
The positive effects of the SME Supporting Factor on loans to Small and Medium -sized 
Enterprises are confirmed by various  studies, some of which are mentioned also in the 
recent Financial Stability Board report: "Evaluation of the effects of financial regulatory 
reforms on small and medium-sized enterprise (SME) financing (November 2019)". 
Furthermore, CNA reminds that SME l oans, even under Basel 2 have not been responsible 
for the banking crisis and sees no need to increase the regulatory capital needed for 
traditional SME loans. 
 
TREATMENT OF UNRATED CORPORATES  
In the standardised approach  for credit risk, in relation to corporate exposures, the Basel 
Committee recognises a  favourable treatment (a risk weight of 65% instead of 100%) to 
companies operating in jurisdictions that don’t allow the use of external rating for regulatory 
purposes and for those qualified as "investment grade". Investment grade companies have 
an adequate capacity to meet their financial commitments in a timely manner, even in 
situations of adverse market conditions and in adverse economic cycle, and issue listed  
securities.  
 
CNA reccomends that in the final implementation of  this provision the use of the rating for 
prudential purposes will be mainteined, as well as the possibility of applying the preferential 
risk weight of 65% for unrated companies, in order to  guarantee a level playing field to

companies at an international level. It would also be useful to qualify the notion of 
"investment grade" differently in order to make it more consistent with the features of the 
European market, by deleting  in particular  the provision related  to the issue of listed 
securities ( in Italy, as in most European countries, the set of companies issuing listed 
securities is very limited). 
 
TREATMENT OF LEASING  
In the standardised approach, as for other unrated  corporate exposures, the Basel 
Committee assigns a 100% risk weight to exposures relating to leasing of physical assets (i.e. 
equipment, machinery and vehicles). 
In this regard, we underline that the real risk for the leasing exposure is lower than other 
forms of financing, due to the positive effect deriving from owning the physical asset. 
 
In the process of transposing Basel III reform, CNA asks for a more accurate attribution  of 
risk weight to leasing of mobile assets because of the importance that the leasing has for the 
renewal of the fixed capitals of the companies, especially for the smaller ones.  
 
INFRASTRUCTURE SUPPORTING FACTOR 
The Basel Committee has assigned different risk weights to project financing according to 
the stage of the initiative, in cases where there is no external rating. 
 
When transposing the Basel III reform in Europe, in addition to the above considerations, it 
is important to confirm  the infrastructure supporting factor set out in CRR2, in view of the 
quality of the initiatives that can benefit from this preferential treatment and the need to 
encourage the development of Europe's infrastructure assets to the benefit of companies’ 
competitiveness.  
 
“GRANULARITY CRITERION” AND ADDITIONAL MEASURES TO ENSURE 
DIVERSIFICATION  
Basel III affirms the need for retail exposures to be sufficiently granular in order to ensure 
adequate diversification of the retail portfolio (to which a risk weight of 75% is applied). In 
this regard, as a criterion for ensuring granularity, the Basel standards propose that no single 
position should exceed an amount equal to 0.2% of the entire portfolio.

This approach appears excessively strict and we believe that the current approach of the 
CRR Regulation for the identification of retail exposures is preferable, as it leaves the bank 
to oversee the proper diversification of the portfolio. 
 
LAND ACQUISITION, DEVELOPMENT AND CONSTRUCTION EXPOSURES  
In the standardised approach, the Basel III reform introduces a new real estate exposures 
category called Land Acquisition, Development and Construction Exposure (ADC). It 
includes exposures for the acquisition of land, for the development and for the construction 
of any residential or commercial property whose ability to service the loan derives from the 
sale of the asset and/or the potential cash flow generated by it.  
 
The risk weight for these exposures is 150%. However, we believe that Basel III duly 
provides the possibility of reduction where there are specific conditions that can mitigate 
the risk, including the existence of pre-sale or pre-leasing contracts for the properties. 
It is therefore necessary to transpose this provision into the European legislation in order to 
allow a better risk assessment. Consideration should also be given to further mitigation of 
any risk resulting from the construction company's autonomous ability to service the loan, 
in which case the counterparty risk weight should be assigned instead of the ADC risk weight. 
 
NEW CREDIT CONVERSION FACTORS  
The Basel Committee envisages the introduction of a new credit conversion factor - i.e. a ratio 
for converting off -balance sheet exposures (e.g. unused credit lines) into  credit exposure 
equivalents - of 10% for unconditionally cancellable commitments, which are currently not 
subject to capital requirements. 
The capital burden deriving from this provision would have negative effects on access to the 
working capital for companies, especially smaller ones.  
 
This factor should therefore not be introduced into European legislation on account of the 
peculiarities of the market in many European countries, where the use of unused credit lines 
by companies is a common practice and is essential to ensure the necessary flexibility to 
meet their financial needs.

CREDIT RISK MITIGATION 
The criteria for recognising non -financial collateral for credit risk mitigation purposes in 
both Basel III and CRR2 are very restrictive and differ depending on whether the 
standardised approach or the internal ratings-based approach is used. 
The reform of the CRR, required by the implementation of Basel III, should be used to better 
define this framework, thus expanding the scope of the area of non-financial collateral other 
than immovable property that can be used to mitigate credit risk.  
This would also be relevant for the purposes of the new regulation on the coverage of non -
performing positions (calendar provisioning), in which different deadlines and provisioning 
ratios are envisaged, depending on whether or not the non -performing exposur e is 
guaranteed by risk mitigation collateral recognised by the CRR. 
 
EXEMPTIONS FROM THE CVA REQUIREMENT 
We recommend confirmation of the current European exemptions from the scope of the CVA 
risk capital charge for derivative contracts with certain types  of counterparties, including 
non-financial firms. The provision of a capital requirement on such transactions would lead 
to an increase in the cost of hedging, discouraging companies from virtuous financial risk 
management practices. 
 
SUSTAINABLE FINANCE 
We strongly support the emphasis on sustainability, but the difficult task is not to set up rules 
that overcharge small actors and that respect the principle of proportionality.  
The level of regulation of the European Financial Market Authority and its in clusion in the 
institutions is already very high in terms of sustainability aspects.  
However, it must be avoided in any case that an overregulation in terms of sustainability is 
made so that even with smaller financings (up to EUR 1 million) of small and medium-sized 
enterprises is constrained by excessive requirements regarding information on companies 
and projects.  
Small financings (up to EUR 1 million) should be completely exempted from the foreseen 
requirements. 
 
OUTPUT FLOOR  
Basel III introduces a lower bound for the regulatory capital requirement of banks using own 
internal models (output floor), parameterised (when fully operating, at 72.5%) to the 
amount of the requirement calculated on the basis of the standardised approach.

In order to avoid an excessive reduction in the risk sensitivity of banks' internal valuation 
models, we ask that the output floor be applied in Europe only at consolidated level (and not 
at individual level).

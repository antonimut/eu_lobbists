Public Consultation: Implementing the Final Basel III Reforms in the EU 
 
 
The Austrian Federal Economic Chamber (Wirtschaftskammer Österreich, WKÖ) represents 
almost all businesses in Austria includings SMEs and financial services companies. WKÖ is listed in 
the EU’s Transparency Register (Nr. 10405322962-08). Farmers and some professional sectors 
like medical doctors and lawyers etc are represented by their own chambers. 
 
The EU implementation of Basel III reforms should be guided by the three key principles that the 
G20 provided in this respect:  
 
 It should not lead to a significant increase in capital requirements 
In their main scenario, EBA recently estimated that the package would increase minimum 
capital requirements by some 23.6% for the EU average. Nonetheless the amount of additional 
capital needed would be most likely significantly larger than what EBA expects, e.g. if banks 
kept their current level of capital buffers, it would require much more than the EBA estimate 
predicts. 
 
A proportionate regime for equity exposure together with politically backed supporting factors 
could partly diminish the unilateral increase in the EU. 
 
 It should better support alignment of risk assessments:  
Counter to this objective the output floor will lead to a widening of gaps between actual future 
losses and regulatory capital requirements. Additionally, the revised ratios for real estate and 
unrated companies do not reflect actual circumstances.  
 
 It should aim for a level playing field at global level 
Unlike in the EU, average capital requirements in the US will be almost unchanged. Against this 
background, it is essential that the output floor should exclude EU-specific buffers, which should 
be recalibrated in general, and only be determined on the basis of internationally agreed 
buffers.

Costs outweigh benefits  
Without doubt there are clear benefits of certain minimum levels for capital ratios. However, 
beyond a certain threshold, benefits are declining. With the level of recapitalisation that EU 
banks have achieved since the crisis, any additional layer of capital adds only insignificant 
improvements to financial stability. Even in the most adverse economic scenarios research done 
by IMF and also the EBA stress test suggest that banks will not run into likely insolvency 
scenarios. The higher levels of required capital will increase the borrowing costs for European 
households and businesses. 
 
The European legislators have to carefully balance the requirements to provide a net benefit for 
the European economy and should not go materially beyond the optimal capitalisation level. 
 
 
Main Issues  
To better align with the G20-principles and to deliver an optimal outcome for the European 
economy, we see the following main issues: 
 
Equity Exposure  
 Inevitably, the sharp increase of risk weights leads probably to unintended consequences. 
Current equity holdings of banks in successful European enterprises would be punished. The 
European legislators should secure strategic capital investments of European investors incl. 
banks.  
 
Therefore equity exposures that are held by institutions and subsidiaries of the institutions for at 
least five consecutive years should receive a preferred risk weight of 100%. At least, it is 
necessary to maintain the current risk weight (100%) in the CRR for existing equity investments 
under the standardised approach. 
 
Furthermore, intragroup equity exposures within a banking group are specific exposure which 
cannot be treated within the general framework. According to Art 49 (2) in conjunction with 
Art 133 (2) CRR, investments in equity or regulatory capital instrume nts issued by institutions 
should be classified as equity claims and re ceive a risk weight of 100%.  
 
Treatment for exposures and equities within an institutional protection scheme (IPS) and to a 
counterparty which is its parent undertaking, its subsidiary, a subsidiary of its parent 
undertaking: In the course of implementing ‘Basel III+’ into European law the current approach 
of Article 113 (7) CRR which provides that exposures between institutions that are members of 
the same institutional protection scheme shall assign a risk weight of 0% should be maintained. 
This should not only be valid for IPS, also to a counterparty which is its parent undertaking, its 
subsidiary, a subsidiary of its parent undertaking 
 
Intra-group Exposures 
 
The maintenance of the possibility to assign a risk weight of 0% for exposures according to 
the current Art 113 (6) of the CRR is of utmost importance.

Output Floor  
The output floor has a significant impact on the European banking sector. Therefore an 
appropriate implementation is necessary in order to achieve an increase in capital requirements 
more in line with the global average.  
 
The output floor should be calculated on the aggregate RWA and at the highest level of 
consolidation level (only). Moreover, the floor should only be calculated on the following Basel 
core capital requirements (CET1): 
 minimum requirement (at least 4.5% of RWA); 
 capital conservation buffer (2.5% of RWA); 
 countercyclical buffer (if nationally applied). 
 
SMEs, SME Supporting Factor  
SMEs are the backbone of the EU's economy. We expect that bank loans and overdrafts will 
continue to be the most important external source of finance for SMEs in the future. The SME 
Supporting Factor has been extended recently in Article 501 CRR II. In order to maintain easy 
access to finance for SMEs, it should at least be kept in its current form. For the same reason a 
proportionate extension in Article 501 CRR should be considered. The SME supporting factor is 
fully justified by the lower risk of SME loans (portfolio/diversification effect of a SME loan 
portfolio). 
 
The new risk weight for unrated corporate SME (85 %) is welcome. 
 
Credit Lines (Off Balance Sheet Exposures) 
 
The proposed removal of 0% credit conversion factor for unconditionally cancellable 
commitments is unduly punitive. It does not adequately reflect the underlying risks. 
Furthermore it is probably not consistent with the actual usage ratios of such credit lines. Due to 
these reasons the 0% credit conversion factor should remain for unused cancellable credit lines. 
 
Granularity Criterion  
The definition of the "granularity criterion" as a relative criterion is as follows: "no aggregated 
exposure to one counterparty can exceed 0.2% of the overall regulatory retail portfolio". This 
definition would affect small entities with relatively small retail portfolios (e.g. under 500 mn 
EUR exposure), so that in many cases a significant share of their retail portfolio would not be 
treated "regulatory retail" anymore. This amount will hardly be achieved by smaller banks of 
which a lot do not have an entire balance sheet up to EUR 500 million. In a large Austrian 
banking sector this would affect over 40 single entities, where in total 14% of the exposure 
respectively 16.5% of the RWA of the Retail portfolio would not fulfill this criterion on single 
institute level. The condition would furthermore conflict with the principle of proportionality. 
Hence a check on group consolidated level would make more sense. 
 
 
Examine a Sustainability  Supporting Factor  
A Sustainability Supporting Factor could promote financing economic activities. It contribute to 
the transition from a brown into a green economy without penalizing whole economic sectors. A 
proportionate relief for green investments should be examined as an instrument for reaching the

sustainability goals without burdening the public budgets. However “non-green” businesses 
should not be penalized through such a factor. Risk considerations should prevail over political 
deliberations. 
 
Real estate Lending 
 
Possible effects on real estate lending have to be examined carefully due to the importance for 
the economy (incl. e.g. indirect effects on construction industry). For instance, the finished 
property criterion would have a negative effect on commercial real estate financing. Therefore 
the loan splitting approach should be kept. Real estate is often an important collateral for SMEs 
– negative effects on SME-financing should be excluded. 
 
Recalibration of EU -specific C apital Buffers  
The implementation of the Final Basel III Framework should be used for a recalibration of the 
following EU-spefic capital buffers: 
 the buffer for other systemically important institutions (O-SII),  
 the systemic risk buffer (SRB),  
 the countercyclical capital buffer (CCyB) and  
 the Pillar 2 requirements to be implemented by the national authorities.  
In addition, national capital guidance (that de facto works as additional capital buffer) could be 
recalibrated. Also the management buffer, which is currently required by competent authorities, 
should be taken into consideration. 
 
Due Diligenc e Process 
Institutions using the standardised approach for credit risk shall not be obliged to perform an 
additional due diligence process for the calculation of credit risk for exposures to corporates and 
banks. 
 
Furthermore we refer to the contribution to the consultation of our Bank and Insurance Division, 
which represents the Austrian banking sector.

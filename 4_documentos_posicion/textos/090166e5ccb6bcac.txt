Executive summary of the ITFA response to the European Commission consultation on implementing 
the finalised Basel III standards in the EU: 
▪ Founded in 1999, ITFA is an association of over 190 financial institutions who are engaged in 
originating and distributing trade -finance related assets and risks across the industry and 
throughout the world. Expanding from its original focus on the purchas e and discounting of 
simple but robust payment instruments, such as drafts and letters of credit, the trade finance 
industry has embraced new instruments and created new risk mitigation structures playing a 
prominent role in supply chain finance at both the local and international level. 
▪ ITFA members welcome the opportunity to respond to the consultation launched by the 
European Commission in view of the future implementation of the finalised Basel III standards. 
▪ ITFA is responding to questions 62, 73, 105, 107, 108 and 109 with a specific focus on credit 
insurance (CRI) as a credit risk mitigant (CRM) as ITFA’s focus is distribution and risk 
mitigation of trade finance related assets of banks and insurance creates one means to 
achieving that goal. 
▪ CRI has been recognised as a CRM by the EBA in the context of the CRR II legislative 
framework. It is an established tool for banks, but most importantly it is an essential facilitator 
for lending to the real economy, enabling monetary flows to companies - including SMEs - and 
supporting many sectors including transport, real estate, and renewable energy projects. 
According to a survey conducted amongst ITFA members, a sample of EUR 90bn of CRI 
support to banks ( representing approximately a quarter of the total  CRI market exposure) 
facilitated €150bn of loans to the real economy. Projected to the scale of the whole industry, 
this means that approximately €600bn of support to the real economy is at risk due to 
potential regulatory changes. 
▪ CRI is a tool predominantly utilised in Europe, valuable across the European financial industry 
and benefiting European borrowers. ITFA believes that Europe should build on its specific 
knowledge, expertise and experience with this tool to boost its competitive advantage in the 
sector of trade finance.  
▪ CRI often is instrumental in facilitating   economically efficient  financing to SMEs from the 
financial sector.  
▪ Both banks (subject to CRD/CRR framework) and insurers (subject to Solvency II framework) 
acknowledge the valued intent of the Basel reforms in treating the exposures to the guarantor 
according to the risk weight function of that guarantor. However, ITFA notes that when CRI 
is used as a CRM, the bank is in the privileged position of being a policyholder vis-à-vis that of 
an unsecured creditor to the insurer. and as such, in case of the insurer’s insolvency, has 
greater protection which makes credit insurance an advantageous and stable credit risk 
mitigant.  
▪ The prudential requirements for the insurers provide (Solvency II, recital 62) that “the SCR 
should reflect a level of eligible own funds that enables (re)insurance undertakings to absorb 
significant losses and that gives reasonable assurance to policy  holders and beneficiaries that 
payments will be made as they fall due.” Whilst not directly secured with collateral, claims of 
banks as policyholders benefit from ring -fencing of assets at the operating company level to 
secure outstanding liabilities to policyholders; bolstered by  increased provisioning 
requirements imposed by insurance regulators for all policy exposures where the insurer has

a potential claim liability in circumstances where the insurer is in distress. This ring-fencing of 
assets for the benefit of banks as policyholders should be recognised with lower LGD floors. 
It is important to note that insurers are not involved in maturity transformation and thus not 
exposed to sudden losses of confidence or ‘runs’ and that multi -line insurers present 
extremely low levels of correlation to the bank’s credit cycle. 
▪ CRI, as a stable and sound CRM instrument, allows banks to reduce risks and further support 
their clients in line with the objective of the BCBS, which is to have a resilient banking sector 
that supports the growth and stabil ity of the real economy. ITFA would appreciate if legal 
certainty was provided in the future European Commission’s proposal implementing the 
finalised Basel III standards, whilst recognising the unique characteristics of CRI.  
▪ There are several solutions suggested in the ITFA response which would bring legal certainty 
to the banks using CRI as a CRM (maintaining AIRBA for CRM tools, adopting a lower LGD 
for CRI based on the characteristics of the product or on the multiple channels of recovery). 
The solutio ns described and substantiated in ITFA’S response have one common goal: to 
recognise the need to differentiate the treatment of CRI from other CRM techniques, to 
prevent the unintended consequences of the shift to FIRBA at 45% LGD for all FIs . ITFA 
believes this was selected to address direct exposures to FIs and mitigate against systemic risk 
in the banking sector rather than inadvertently capture CRI provided to banks by insurers.

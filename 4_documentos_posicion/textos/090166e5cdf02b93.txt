 
 
Waste shipments – revision of EU rules 
 
The European plastics manufacturers support the intention of the Commission to review the current 
Regulation (EC) No 1013/2006 on shipments of waste (WSR), with a view to: 
- simplify and reduce burdens linked to its implementation; 
- ensure a smooth functioning of the EU internal market for waste destined for reuse or recycling, 
and support its transition to circular economy models;  
- better address illegal shipments of waste wit hin the EU and explore measures at multilateral, 
regional and bilateral levels to combat environmental crime, notably in the areas of illegal exports 
and illicit trafficking; 
- strengthen controls of  waste shipments and restrict exports of waste that have harmful 
environmental and health impacts in third countries; and 
- improve the sustainable and environmental safe management of waste in third countries.  
Through its voluntary commitment Plastics 2030, our industry is fully committed to circular economy, and 
aims at increasing re-use and recycling, preventing plastics leakage into the environment, and accelerating 
resource efficiency.    
 
This requires  significant investment in new recycling capacities as well as the maximized and optimal 
utilization of these capacities.  To this end , plastic waste should no longer be landfilled but sent, when 
possible, to recycling, and be processed in the most eco-efficient manner possible.  
 
Transboundary movement of plastic waste plays an important role in achieving an economically efficient 
and environmentally sound recovery of plastic waste. The creation of a “Schengen area for waste” (i.e. an 
area where the free movement of waste is guaran teed) would in fact help overcom ing the lack of 
infrastructure in some EU Member States and overcapacity in others.  
 
It is also important for Europe to be able to offer proper recovery solutions of plastic waste originated 
from 3rd countries, especially in areas and countries with no appropriate waste treatment infrastructures. 
In this way, the risk of plastics leakage into the environment will be reduced and waste resources utilised. 
Stakeholders should be able to import such waste to the EU without excessive administrative barriers.  
  
In a global market, such as the plastics market, where waste is increasingly seen as a valuable resource, 
waste which is legally exported to third countries , where it is  recycled in an environmentally sound 
manner, can contribute to a global circular economy. However, it is crucial that such waste is traded 
through properly controlled channels and recycled in certified facilities operating to quality, environmental 
and safety standards equivalent to those in the EU. Such a certification will help secure a level playing field 
between the EU and other regions of the world . And it will help securing that no waste enters the 
environment uncontrolled.

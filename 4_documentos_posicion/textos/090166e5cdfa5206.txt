08/04/2020
 
 
Based
on
the
Commission's
SWD
evaluating
the
Waste
Shipment
Regulation
(WSR)
and
on
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
the
information
provided
through
the
inception
Impact
Assessment,
Hazardous
waste
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Europe
(HWE)
wants
to
share
views
on
the
Commission's
understanding
of
the
different
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
problems pointed out.
 
 
As
a
preliminary
statement,
we
would
like
to
insist
on
the
fact
that
the
WSR
is
an
adequate
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
piece
of
legislation
at
the
EU
level
to
ensure
safe
shipments
of
waste,
traceability
and
to
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
guarantee
appropriate
treatment
of
waste
at
final
destination.
Consequently,
we
advocate
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
not
giving
in
to
the
temptation
of
oversimplifying
the
current
provisions
that
could
be
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
detrimental
to
health
and
the
environment.
This
temptation
could
occur
behind
virtuous
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
expectations.
Indeed,
the
IIA
introduces
some
of
the
measures
of
the
European
Green
Deal
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
and
of
the
new
Circular
Economy
Action
Plan
that
could
be
connected
to
waste
shipments,
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
namely:
 
●
limiting export to third countries,
 
●
facilitating preparing for reuse and recycling.
 
If
there
are
solutions
to
adapt
the
WSR
regarding
the
objectives
of
the
EU
Green
Deal
and
of
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
the
new
Circular
Economy
Action
Plan,
the
regulation
shall
remain
a
text
developing
the
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
different
procedures
for
safe
transfers
and
treatment
of
waste.
Consequently,
HWE
favours
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
an
adaptation
of
the
WSR
by
clarifications
and
improvements
aiming
at
offering
an
efficient
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
frame
for
Shipment
procedures
(notifications
and
movements)
with
the
ultimate
objective
to
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
find
the
right
balance
between
red
tape
and
the
rigor
of
the
document
that
it
is
dear
to
us
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
to preserve.
 
 
While
WSR
is
part
of
the
legislative
arsenal
for
an
EU
circular
economy,
one
cannot
expect
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
all
and
every
changes
needed
in
this
respect
to
be
passed
through
this
revision.
It
is
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
essential
to
keep
coherence
among
the
different
pieces
of
legislation
and
not
to
ask
WSR
to
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
solve all the issues a circular economy model raises.
 
Before
providing
our
views
on
the
problems
identified
by
the
Commission,
it
is
of
utmost
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
importance
to
ensure
that
a
strict
distinction
remains
between
requirements
applying
to
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
non
hazardous
waste
and
those
applying
to
hazardous
waste.
This
distinction
does
not
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
appear
neither
in
the
IIA
nor
in
the
SDW
evaluation
report.
This
point
is
crucial
to
be
kept
in
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
mind regarding the first problem identified on which we will focus now.
 
 
First problem
 
"The
WSR
does
not
support
the
transition
to
a
circular
economy".
It
is
not
possible
to
regard
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
in
the
same
way
transboundary
movements,
even
for
recycling
within
the
EU,
when
it
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
concerns
hazardous
waste
or
non
hazardous
waste.
Procedures
shall
not
be
softened
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
based
on
the
operation
but
based
on
the
nature
of
the
waste.
Consequently,
what
has
to
be
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
potentially improved is:
 
1.
define correctly annex IIIA and annex IIIB waste and,
 
2.
how to deal with transboundary shipments of annex IIIA and annex IIIB waste,
 
but
certainly
not
to
soften
the
transboundary
shipments
of
hazardous
waste,
even
for
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
recycling,
because
of
the
risks
it
implies
for
health
and
the
environment.
This
includes
the
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
cases
of
the
waste
containing
POPs
(fractions
of
WEEE
or
EoLV
or
Furnitures,
some
plastics,
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
…)
for
which
recycling
is
normally
forbidden
while,
today,
the
rules
are
not
enforced
correctly
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
by Member States.

Second problem
 
Regarding
the
second
problem
identified,
HWE
can
only
agree
and
support
the
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Commission’s
view.
Exports
to
developing
countries
threat
them.
The
adoption
of
the
import
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
ban
at
the
Basel
Convention
level
will
certainly
help
even
if
it
will
not
stop
illegal
shipments
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
which is the third problem in the IIA.
 
 
Nevertheless,
regarding
the
loss
of
valuable
resources
for
the
EU
industry,
we
want
to
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
emphasise
that
it
is
not
because
waste
will
remain
in
the
EU
that
the
risk
of
harming
health
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
and
the
environment
disappears.
And
here,
we
want
to
make
a
link
with
the
likely
economic
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
impacts
described
in
the
IIA.
Yes,
there
is
a
potential
for
retaining
large
quantities
of
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
valuable
resources
within
the
EU.
Such
quantities
could
encourage
investments
in
high
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
quality
recycling
facilities.
But,
HWE
firmly
recalls
that
high
quality
recycling
means
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
automatically
an
increase
of
residual
extracted
fractions,
mainly
hazardous
waste
for
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
which
also
new
treatment
capacities
are
necessary.
These
capacities
currently
don't
exist
in
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
the
EU,
because
treating
such
extracted
unwanted/contaminated
fractions
is
an
issue
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
faced
by
the
countries
of
destination
of
the
exported
waste
flows.
Once
the
export
ban
is
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
enforced,
recycling
facilities
will
certainly
flourish
in
many
EU
Member
States,
but
there
is
a
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
risk
that
the
necessary
new
capacities
for
treating
the
extracted
unwanted/contaminated
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
fractions
will
not
be
developed
in
some
of
these
countries,
because
they
represent
a
pure
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
cost.
Thus,
EU
will
face
a
rebound
effect.
Recycling
will
develop
also
in
countries
where
there
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
are
no
infrastructures
for
safe
treatment
of
the
hazardous
residual
waste
(for
instance
the
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
need
for
decontamination
of
waste
containing
POPs
or
other
substances
of
concern).
These
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
waste
will
not
be
exported
to
EU
countries
where
capacities
exist
because
of
the
burden
and
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
the
cost,
consequently
they
will
disappear
in
the
environment.
We
all
know
these
practices
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
already occur in the EU. The phenomenon will just rise up.
 
 
Today,
when
waste
is
exported
to
third
countries
and
specifically
to
developing
countries,
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
there
is
no
possibility
to
assess
the
environmentally
sound
management
of
the
waste.
The
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
restriction
of
export
outside
Europe
foreseen
here
must
be
both
an
opportunity
for
the
EU
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
economy
and
for
a
better
protection
of
health
and
the
environment.
Europe
can
implement
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
the means to trace the entire management chain in order to avoid loopholes.
 
 
This
is
the
reason
why,
when
waste
is
treated
within
the
EU,
HWE
would
advocate
granting
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
specific
attention
to
decontamination
both
through
investment
and
development
of
high
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
quality
recycling
plants
and
by
ensuring
availability
of
appropriate
facilities
for
the
safe
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
treatment
(disposal
or
recovery)
of
hazardous
residual
waste
generated
from
the
recycling
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
operation.
 
 
This
implies
both
instruction
of
notification
and
Competent
Authorities
consent
to
take
into
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
account
that
enough
capacity
exists
in
the
EU
-
in
the
country
of
destination
or
in
another
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Member
States
-
for
the
safe
treatment
of
the
residual
waste
after
or
during
recycling.
A
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
notification
must
provide
an
overall
answer
in
case
of
recycling
-
namely
treatment
of
final
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
residues
generated
by
the
treatment
operation
-
if
we
want
to
avoid
any
detrimental
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
impacts on health and the environment.

EU
and

state
allocate
than

to
be
solved
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
in the WSR.
 
 
Fourth problem
 
The
last
problem
concerns
the
red
tape.
This
is
really
an
area
where
clarifications
and
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
improvement
of
the
WSR

the
revision

all
stakeholders.

state,

the

try
to

that don't depend on WSR like:
 
●

●
hazardous vs non hazardous waste,
 
●
recovery vs disposal operation.
 
Taking
into
account
the
previous
statement,
HWE
agrees
with
the
four
obstacles
identified
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
and already provided proposals for all of them in previous position papers.
 
 
HWE
wants
to
add
a
point
that
was
never
discussed
before
but
which
popped
up
in
the
past
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
month
because
of
the
outbreak
we
all
face
in
Europe.
It
might
be
very
useful
to
have
a
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
dedicated
article
defining
the
rules
Competent
Authorities
and
notifiers
should
follow
so
in
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
order
to
ensure
the
continuity
of
the
transboundary
shipments
in
such
exceptional
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
circumstances.
 
 
HWE
is
at
the
disposal
of
the
Commission
for
further
discussions
and
continues
to
work
on
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
concrete
proposals
to
adapt
the
WSR
in
the
light
of
the
experiences
of
its
members.
A
recast
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
of the text is the best option according to us.

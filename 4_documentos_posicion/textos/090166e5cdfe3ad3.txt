EuroCommerce feedback to the Inception Impact 
Assessment on the revision of the Waste Shipment 
Regulation 
 
 
In the Communication on the European Green Deal the Commission announced to revise  Waste 
Shipment Regulation (EC) No 1013/2006 to better align the existing rules on waste shipments with the 
concept of a Circular Economy and to tackle the problem of illegal export of waste.  
 
This statement aims to summarise key points from the retail an d wholesale sector on shipment of 
waste in the EU and support the Commission’s work in revising the Waste Shipment Regulation. 
1. Use the revision to enable a well -functioning internal market for 
waste 
We agree with the statement of the Inception Impact Assessment that the Waste Shipment Regulation 
does not effectively support the transition to a circular economy, as some of its procedures and  
inconsistent implementation by the Member States do not facilitate the cross border  shipping of 
waste for recycling within the EU and significant delays can occur as the result of the existing rules. In 
addition, as the recent evaluation of the Waste Shipment Regulation  showed, our members also 
experience different interpretations of the provisions and procedures of the r egulation in different 
Member States.  
 
Therefore, we fully agree that the Commission should use the revision of the regulation to enable 
more circular business activities. In our opinion this work is a big opportunity for the Commission to 
create a real internal market for waste, which will support recycling of waste and re -use of valuable 
materials in the EU and helps to boost markets for secondary raw materials. While shipment of waste 
is just one piece of the puzzle, this regulatory change is needed to support the transition towards more 
circular business models. A transition to a circular economy in Europe will require more cross border 
shipping across EU countries, in order to create the needed infrastructure for preparing to reuse, 
repair, refurbish and remanufacture products. This is due for example to logistic need s and the 
expectation that probably some countries might decide to  share resources and efforts to create 
economies of scale.  
 
The Commission should therefore choose one of the policy options that include at least a modest 
revision of the legislation.  We urge the European Commission to abstain from just issuing guidance 
documents or informational tools, which would not ensure the needed leve l of harmonised rules in 
the EU. 
2. Reduce the administrative burden related to waste shipments within 
the EU 
EuroCommerce also agrees with the objective to simplify and reduce burdens linked with the 
implementation of the Waste Shipment Regulation. We also recognise the need for a stronger 
harmonization in waste definition, including hazardous waste.

We support that the Commission assesses if digital approaches to information exchange, simplification 
of procedures, more harmonised implementation can contribute to reduce unnecessary regulatory 
and administrative burdens. In our view especially simplified notification procedures and harmonised 
waste classifications (hazardous vs non -hazardous waste) would simplify the burdens related to the 
Waste Shipment Regulation without leading to negative effects for the environment or the correct 
treatment of waste.

Waste shipments – Revision of EU rules 
 
Inception Impact Assessment 
 
EUCOPRO approaches 
 
 
EUCOPRO (European Association for Co-processing) is an association composed of waste 
pre-treatment companies that prepare alternative fuels and raw materials mainly from 
hazardous wastes, with the aim of recovering them in co-processing processes, such as 
cement kilns. Members of EUCOPRO operate plants located in several EU countries 
including Belgium, France, Germany, Greece, Italy, Spain, the Netherland, Poland, and 
Romania.  
 
 
Due to its activities, EUCOPRO is therefore following with a great interest all the European 
Commission’s Communication and public consultations linked with the Green Deal, and in 
particular, all concerns on the review of the Waste Shipment Regulation. 
 
As it is mentioned in the document submit to the consultation, “the Waste Shipment 
Regulation lays down procedures and control regimes for transboundary shipments of 
waste. The WSR requires Member States to ensure that shipments of waste ant their 
treatment operations are managed in a manner that protects the environment and human 
health against any adverse effects that might result from such waste”. 
 
Eucopro agrees with this statement: Regulation n° 1013/2006 on waste shipments 
should be strictly maintained as a regulation for procedures for authorization and 
for controlling waste shipment operations. 
 
Adaptation of procedures seems necessary to reduce the administrative burden and to 
better help transition to circular economy models. But such improvement must no lead 
to decrease the existing requirements which frame the notification procedure and 
guarantee a high level of protection for environment and health. 
 
In this context, Eucopro asks to clearly distinguish what is relevant for hazardous 
waste shipment from requirements for non-hazardous waste. 
The concerns related to hazardous waste shipment, always subject to notification 
procedures, are very different from those linked with non-hazardous waste. All the 
concerns, mentioned in the inception impact assessment document, linked with the green 
list procedure, and to the export to third countries are not relevant for hazardous waste. 
Eucopro considers that the notification procedure is suitable for hazardous waste shipments 
and there is no need to deeply modify it. 
 
Nevertheless, the Waste Shipments regulations requires improvements. For 
Eucopro, the focus should be put on how reducing administrative burden for both the waste 
operators and the competent authorities.  
Eucopro is not in favor of a deeply review of the Waste shipment regulation and asks for 
developing electronics tools and implementation guidance as much as possible.

-  First of all, for waste shipment in the European Union, the use of an electronics tool, 
which is in construction since several years, is a major project, as it would enable the 
notifiers and the authorities: 
o to better follow up the files, from their original sending to the receipt of all the 
agreements, and then to facilitate the renewing of the notification,….  
o to ensure the respect of the deadlines fixed by the waste shipment regulation for 
the examination and approval, or rejection, of the notification, and  
o to improve exchange and to facilitate communication between all the actors 
involved in a waste shipment, e.g. in the event of non-answer from the authorities 
when end-deadline is approaching, in order to prevent any damaging economic 
impacts for the notifiers or for the waste operators. 
o to improve the follow-up of the waste shipment, including its traceability. 
o to facilitate the exchanges between the notifiers and the competent authorities in 
case of unexpected change during the shipment, such as a road modification. 
 
- Elaboration of guidance documents to explain procedures as those which will be 
necessary when the electronic tools will be running and/or to facilitate the understanding 
of some requirements (e.g. correspondence table between all the different codes asked 
to fulfill the documents) should be more developed. 
 
- The use of both guidance and electronic tool could also contribute to clarify and 
distinguish non-intentional administrative mistakes from real illegal shipments. The use 
of electronic tool/ dematerialized procedure, as previously mentioned, would facilitate 
exchanges and communication. Dedicated guidance linked with these topics could be 
written. 
 
- Eucopro also requests clarification and consistency in the waste shipments regulation 
requirements within European Union, when end of waste status national procedures 
exist, in order to avoid distortions of competition. 
 
- The lack of uniform enforcement throughout the EU linked with e.g. waste classification, 
“R” and “D” codes, are considered as key issues as these discrepancies may lead to a 
non-level playing field for the implementation of the waste shipment regulation. Work 
need to be done on these discrepancies, but only in the context of the relevant 
legislations, such as the waste framework directive. 
 
 
Eucopro agrees on the need to stop illegal shipment and considers that controls 
and inspections carried out by administrative authorities are a major factor in the 
proper application of the regulation  
 
Controls must be carried out throughout the procedures: during the notification instruction 
and during shipment. The regulation n° 1013/2006 has been recently re-enforced on 
control and inspections. Consequently, Eucopro is in favor that any additional requirements 
should be done in the light of first feedback, taking into account the recent implementation 
of this reinforcement. 
 
In connection with the points mentioned above, Eucopro has worked on proposals to 
improve the regulation on waste shipment and we are at your disposal for any exchange 
you may wish.

In its recently published Circular Economy Action Plan, the European Commission announces its plan 
to address waste exports from the EU . Henkel welcomes this proposal as an opportunity , to  fully 
realize the circular economy by addressing the loss of resources as well as tackle illegal export of waste 
with its negative environmental and social consequences.  
With more than 185 production sites worldwide and around 20 billion Euros in sales in 2019, Henkel is 
not only a European but also a global company. Nevertheless, Europe is the biggest market for Henkel, 
with over 50 production sites and about 20,000 employees, generating roughly half of the global sales.  
Henkel has clearly demonstrated its commitment to circularity from the very beginning: Participating 
in the voluntary pledging exercise of the European Commission, signing the Circular Plastics Alliance 
Declaration, and organizing Circular Economy Missions  to identify obstacles to seizing the full 
potential of a closed loops system and for the way forward promote the circular economy to third 
countries. 
Henkel actively supports that valuable secondary raw materials remain in the economic cycle and do 
not enter the environment as waste. In cooperation with organizations along the value chain, such as 
the Alliance to End Plastic Waste (AEPW) and the global initiative Plastic Bank, Henkel aims at making 
packaging more sustainable and promotes the circular economy. The Plastic Bank reduces ocean plastic 
and provides opportunities for people in poverty by turning waste into currency. Henkel incorporates 
the plastic collected into its product packaging.  
To make the Circular Economy a reality in Europe, substantial technological progress is needed. Henkel 
is actively w orking on further improv ing the recyclability and sortability of its consumer goods 
packaging. This also reflects in our sustainable packaging targets until 2025: 
1. 100 per cent of packaging to be recyclable or reusable; 
2. 50 per cent reduction of plastic from fossil sources in our consumer products by increasing the 
proportion of recycled plastic to more than 30 percent, reducing the plastic volume, and using bio-
based plastics; 
3. No plastic waste in the nature by supporting waste collection and recycling initiatives, investing in 
innovative solutions and technologies  and providing consumers targeted information about 
recycling (e.g. through logos) 
Digital solutions can help addressing the significant challenges that remain in order to improve the 
quantity, quality and sorting of recycled material  and thus drive sustainability . Henkel’s “Black 
Masterbatch” is an example of an innovative recycling solution for black plastic packaging  that 
enables the recycling of bottles with carbon-free black by allowing scanning by near -infrared 
technology for automated sorting at recovery facilities . Another example is a “ digital passport” for 
products, that contains information on the material, the manu facturer and the types of layers used  
(see the Holy Grail initiative under the Ellen MacArthur Foundations’ New Plastics Economy).

To make a Circular Economy a reality in Europe and support European industry in their effort to further 
develop and implement sustainable technologies and business models, Henkel calls on policy makers 
to: 
• Enable a functioning Single Market for secondary raw materials 
To make a more circular and sustainable economy a reality in Europe, a functioning Single Market 
for secondary raw materials that replaces “waste” with “resource”  is a fundamental element. As 
outlined above, Henkel perceives itself as being part of the solution to this key challenge. However, 
technological progress needs to be accompanied by the availability of high-quality secondary raw 
materials. To realize this, it is necessary to ensure better enforcement of existing waste legislation 
and to conduct a critical review of the existing legal framework with a view to detect provisions 
impeding progress on the Circular Economy.  
• Facilitate the import of secondary raw materials of high quality  
A regulatory framework should be set up to import secondary raw materials from regions with 
no recycling systems. These waste imports would need to have the clear purpose to feed into the 
circular economy and be used as valuable raw material for European products. This would address 
the lack of high-quality raw materials in Europe as well as plastic waste in the environment.     
• Safeguard the import of “PET flakes”  
Post-consumer PET is shredded into small flakes, in order to enable shipment. These flakes are 
used as raw material and converted again into a product. To ensure the continuous import is crucial 
for companies, in order to guarantee the availability of secondary raw materials. This facilitates 
companies such as Henkel the transition to a circular economy and enables global initiatives like 
the Plastic Bank to operate.  
• Leverage digital means to facilitate the recyclability of a product  
Beyond the availability of high -quality secondary raw materials, digitalisation can support the 
transition to a circular economy. In its communication on the Green Deal, the Commission states 
that “digitalization can also help improve the availability of information”. We support the view that 
digital means, such as a “digital product passport”, offer an important opportunity to facilitate the 
recyclability of a product and create a Single Market for secondary raw materials.

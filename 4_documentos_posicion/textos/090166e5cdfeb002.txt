Introduction 
The European steel industry supports the efforts of the European Commission towards the goals 
of the European Green Deal and the measures put in place in order to transform the European 
economy into a circular one. 
The steel industry supports also the fact that challenges in dealing with EU waste must not b e 
exported to third countries and addressed within the EU. Moreover, the export of waste should 
occur only when it makes sense from a sustainability perspective  and when this export will limit 
or hamper the access of EU industry to secondary raw materials of the necessary quantity and 
quality. If these conditions are ensured, then the principle of free movement of goods must also 
apply to waste. 
The actual text of the Waste Shipment Regulation has ensured an improvement of the 
environmental protection and of the management of waste transport. However, its potential has 
not been fully exploited due to practical experience seen within EU territory. The effectiveness of 
the Waste Shipment Regulation  has been limited by the following elements: (a) d ifferent legal 
interpretations by the member states  on same waste related topics; (b) several often diverging  
material classifications; (c) a largely incomplete digitalisation of the bureaucratic procedures and 
related info rmation on waste; (d) inadequate controls at the EU borders and waste inspection 
procedures not coherent across EU Member States. 
Moreover, the Waste Shipment Regulation implementation and use suffered by i ts close interlink 
with the Waste Framework. The not harmonised implementation of the Waste Framework 
Directive, of the waste catalogue and of classification criteria of waste created hurdles instead of 
possible synergies. 
Finally, the new wave pushed by t he EU Green Deal dealing with Circular Economy and Climate 
suggests that the Waste Shipment Regulation should be fine -tuned via a limited review on some 
specific topics. The adoption of the Circular Economy action plan in 2015 and the release of the 
new one in 2020 made more evident that discarded materials, end-of-life products and industrial 
residues are secondary raw materials whatever is their legal status, waste, End-Of-Waste and By-
products. 
The European Steel Association – EUROFER – welcomes the possibility of a focused review of this 
regulation as mentioned by the recently released Inception Impact Assessment, actually under 
consultation and on which EUROFER would like to contribute. 
Comments and proposals on how to improve the Waste Shipment Regulation and its 
use by member states 
 To simplify and reduce burdens linked with the WSR’s implementation  and to ensure a 
functioning internal waste market 
1. The inconsistenc ies of waste identification , classification and terminology among OECD 
guidance, Basel convention, EU waste catalogue and CN cod es should be removed. In 
addition, a link between these EU or international documents and the  different national 
situations in which national catalogues are used will enormously help.

2. Harmonisation among the Member States needs the limitation of the room for different 
interpretations of the regulation and contradictions about legal aspects. For instance, it 
will be useful having a definition o f what is an ‘illegal shipment’ . In too many cases, a 
waste shipment has been classified illegal only because waste law was differently 
implemented by the two Member States involved in the shipment. 
Legal uncertainties such as “subject of jurisdiction of a state” , e.g. who is the responsible 
of the shipment in the case of the state of transit, should be resolved as well.  
Moreover, more legal certainty is necessary about the ‘additional provisions regarding 
interim recovery and disposal operations’ covered by the Article 15 of the regulation. In 
particular, a practical legal delimitation of the terms "provisional", "subsequent" or "final" 
is missed at present, while it would help legislators and actors involved in the operations. 
The clarifications of all the legal aspects and definitions included in the regulation, such as 
those above presented,  will substantially help the harmonisation of procedures and 
practices across the member states. 
3. The regulation should foresee the establishment of a list or a register of ‘transport 
companies’ accredited for certain types of materials and in function of the waste 
classification (hazardous / non-hazardous) or legal status of the material. 
4. The increase of the use of electronic tools will help in reducing the burden linked to the 
regulation and thus will make smooth its application and procedures. Electronic tools and 
exchange platforms should be also used for shar ing definitions, practices and cr iteria 
among the member states.  Thus, a simplified administrative burden for companies  and 
industries will allow also lower value waste entering the circularity route , while a 
burdensome administrative process will send these streams to landfill or incineration. 
5. Moreover, it is also essential that a revision of the Waste Shipment Regulation will take 
into account inconsistencies and implementing issues in Member States linked to waste, 
End-Of-Waste and By-products. 
 To facilitate preparing for recycling of and adding value to waste in the EU 
6. To find new outlets for waste recycling and innovative methods for valorising them need 
also testing and trials, often at industrial scale. However, the actual pro visions of the 
regulation will not support innovation in this sense. 
The limit of 25 kg imposed on waste shipment for testing is too low especially when 
referring to industrial scale testing. At industrial scale the quantity of a material batch for 
one test is of the order o f 1 t and usually many tests have to be performed before setting 
up input composition and p rocessing parameters. Thus, for industrial testing usually it is 
shipped one truck l oad of material, i.e. around 20t. Then, it would be  coherent and 
effective increasing the limit from 25 kg to 20 - 25 t (at least) when dealing with industrial 
scale tests. 
Moreover, the tests and analysis requirements for this waste shipment should be relaxed 
for such situations that are business to business related. 
7. More clarity is needed about the procedures mentioned in the Annexes III, IIIA and IIIB of 
the regulation. The annexes want to address the problem of cross -contamination among 
waste types. However, the procedure for classification proposed in the three annexes has 
been found complex by practitioners. Our general recommendation is to follow more 
simple approaches. A simplified approach could also help Member States to implement 
the legislation with a more coherent approach. 
8. The presence of certain substances in a waste or compound is often the essential 
condition for facilitating waste recycling and thus adding up value to waste within the EU 
territory. It should be absolutely avoided restrictions on waste circulation based solely on 
hazardous substances content.

For instance, there are residues from the stainless steel production that have high content 
of Nickel which is classified a substance of concern  by ECHA (skin sensitising). However, 
the presence of such metal  gives a very high economic value  to the material and makes 
economically viable its shipment across many Member States in EU for recycling . This 
example fully belongs and it is functional to EU goals of the EU Green Deal and EU Circular 
Economy. 
It should be taken into account the possibility of creati ng a simplified and fast tracked 
procedure with reduced bureaucratic burden  when the final treatment of the material is 
recycling, whatever is its classification (hazardous or not hazardous). 
 To restrict exports of waste 
9. The export of waste should occur only when positively acting in sustainability terms. 
Therefore, the Article 49 of the Waste Shipment Regulation should take into account that 
an export should happen only wh en the receiving facility operates in a country that 
effectively has standards, measures or legislation protecting the health and the 
environment and mitigating climate change. These standards, measures or legislations 
have to be  broadly equivalent to the European ones. Thus, the revision of the regulation 
should introduce the climate mitigation or protection dimension in the check, in order to 
take into account all the components of the environment and social pillar  of the 
sustainability1. 
This proposal is in line with the new Circular Economy action plan that focusses on losses 
of valuable resource for the EU industry and on those waste streams that can be 
domestically treated. Moreover, taking into account climate objectives and measures 
within the assessment of waste exports conditions is fully in line with the general goals of 
the EU Green Deal and the objectives of the Circular Economy as an ancillary climate 
mitigation tool. 
 To better address illegal shipments of waste within the EU and illegal exports outside the EU 
10. The inspection criteria of the member states should be better enforced and appropriate 
control and tracking systems are better suited to combating illegal waste transport than 
changes to the regulation itself. On this re spect the cooperation among the member 
states should be made more effective.  Practices, cases and experiences linked to illegal 
shipments identifications could be shared using electronic tools (see point 3). 
11. The payment in cash should be forbidden along th e waste value chain in order to 
guarantee better traceability and limiting thus trafficking and illegal practices.

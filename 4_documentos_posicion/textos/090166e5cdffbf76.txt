Eurits welcomes the Inception Impact Assessment (IIA) on the revision of the Waste Shipments Regulation  
(WSR) (Regulation 1013/2006).  As we have previously commented during the evaluation of the WSR we 
believe that the in general the WSR is a good piece of legislation which does not need fundamental revision 
but rather consistent implementation and enforcement.  The primary aim of the legislation is to set out a series 
of necessary controls for the protec tion of the environment when shipping wastes across borders  – this is 
largely achieved ensuring that wastes are shipped safely to the right treatment facilities. 
Commission priorities: 
Transition to a circular economy: much of the IIA talks about the need to support the transition to the circular 
economy – we support this aim, however in most cases the WSR is not the impediment to a circular economy.  
The strictest controls of the WSR are rightly aimed at controlling shipments  of hazardous wast e and these  
should not be relaxed in the pursuit of the circular e conomy. It is a common mistake to mis-interpret all the 
WSR requirements as applying to all types of waste or even to non-wastes eg recovered materials which have 
undergone an end-of-waste process.  Controls of shipments of hazardous waste should not be inadvertently 
weakened by any potential simplification of rules for non-hazardous wastes. 
Exports of wastes outside the EU: Eurits completely supports measures to ensure : that the EU both takes 
responsibility for the treatment of its waste without exporting the waste to non-EU countries (often without 
adequate infrastructure to treat the waste properly) and that the EU takes full advantage of the valuable 
secondary raw materials that are being lost from exports outside of the EU.   Exports of wastes out of the EU 
are too often sent to facilities that wo uld not be allowed to treat such wastes in the EU – as a leader on 
environmental issues the EU should not allow such practices. 
There is a strong possibility that increasing the amount of waste treated correctly in t he EU will have an 
economic cost, however it will also have a n e conomic benefit from great employment and 
development/investment in new and better recycling facilities  (along with facili ties for residual wast e 
treatment – the more waste that is recycled in the EU, the greater the amount of residual waste which will 
need to be correctly treated). 
Enforcement and illegal shipments : we support the aim of the reducing the amount of illegal shipments 
however the current WSR already has sufficient provision but it needs to be enforced by an adequate level of 
resources at the regional and Member State level. 
Administrative burden and clarification: in our view this is the are a with the potential for the most positive 
impact on the smooth oper ation of waste shipments.   The issues identified by the Commission in partic ular 
moving to electronic systems and harmonisation of approaches/interpretations across the Member States are 
the biggest day-to-day barriers that our members encounter with waste shipments. 
Additional remarks: the Waste Shipments Regulation largely functions well for the control of transboundary 
shipment of wastes and we should re member that there are already eas ier procedures for the shipment of

 Who is EURITS? 
• The European Union for the Responsible Incineration & Treatment of Special 
waste  
• An association of hazardous waste management companies across the EU 
• Eurits’ members have a total capacity of high temperature incineration of around 
3Mt/y; this represents 90-95% of the total dedicated hazardous waste incineration 
capacity in Europe 
 
 Main activities 
• Make sure hazardous waste is handled, managed and treated very specifically and 
in a correct manner: 
o To avoid any dispersion of hazards or contaminants into the environment 
o To ensure that the environment and public health are protected 
o To ensure that recovered materials are reliable  
 
 Key role 
• The EU’s leading voice on hazardous waste 
• Over 25 years of experience in hazardous waste treatment 
• Consistent promotion of best sustainable environmental practice 
• Representing the special waste treatment industry in the EU Parliament and 
Commission, in order to create adequate support and policy framewo rks in 
Europe.

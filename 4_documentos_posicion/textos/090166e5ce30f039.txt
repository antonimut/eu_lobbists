CEWEP feedback to inception impact assessment on 2030 Climate Target Plan 
CEWEP welcomes the willingness of the EU to become climate neutral by 2050, having in mind that such an 
ambitious target would require for legislators to make evidence-based decisions, in all sectors. 
CEWEP would like to highlight how waste management in general, and Waste-to-Energy (WtE) in particular, 
can contribute to the European climate mitigation, circularity, and sustainability goals. In 2016 (the most 
recent statistics from Eurostat on all the waste generated), more than 1 billion tonnes of waste were 
landfilled in EU 28, out of around 2 billion tonnes of waste generated. To be precise, 45.7% of the waste 
generated in the European Union has been landfilled.1 
Landfilling waste that could be recycled or recovered for energy means burying valuable resources with 
significant environmental risks. Diverting waste from landfills is the low-hanging fruit for climate mitigation 
in the waste management sector. 
According to the EEA, the greenhouse gas emissions from waste in the EU have fallen by 42% between 
1995 and 2017 (See graph below). This happened mainly through the reduction of waste landfilling. 
 
According to CEWEP calculations, based on this report from German Environment Protection Agency (UBA), 
diverting the 175 million tonnes of non-mineral waste (only municipal and commercial waste) that are 
currently disposed of in landfills,  higher up the waste hierarchy would save at least 153 million tonnes of 
CO2eq. This could be done by: 
o Diverting waste that is currently landfilled to quality recycling and Waste-to-Energy 
o By substituting fossil fuels used for the generation of energy with Waste-to-Energy 
WtE recovers energy (electricity, heat, and steam) from residual, non-recyclable waste and replaces fossil 
fuels. By 2035 WtE plants could produce 190 TWh of energy, enough to supply more than 50 million people 
with heat and electricity and to replace 10% of the energy supplied by the coal sector in the EU today. In 
some urban areas with efficient district heating in place, WtE covers more than half of citizens’ heat

demand – a significant contribution to energy security. Half of the energy produced from waste is 
renewable as it comes from waste of biological origin. At the same time WtE also complements other – 
intermittent – renewable energy sources and delivers reliable base-load energy.  
With regard to climate mitigation, the WtE sector is looking into possibilities of CO2 capture and usage or 
storage where appropriate and sustainable, provided that a necessary framework for its implementation is 
set up. 
Additional climate benefits come from the recycling of metals such as iron, aluminum, copper, and zinc that 
are recovered at the end of the combustion process from the bottom ash, and that could not have been 
recycled in other way. This process saves greenhouse gas emissions of more than 3 million tonnes of CO2 
equivalent every year. 
Waste management is one of the sectors that could help the EU to reach the ambitious climate goals. To 
do so, CEWEP encourages the European Commission and policy makers to couple Climate ambitions with 
future EU waste legislation, that should extend targets for landfill diversion and recycling from municipal 
waste (which is only a small part of the total waste volume) to other waste streams like commercial and 
industrial waste.  
Sustainable waste management contributes to greenhouse gas mitigation. Diverting waste from landfills 
has numerous benefits, including reducing emissions, improving environmental and health impacts, and 
boosting recycling and recovery. 
CEWEP is the umbrella association of the operators of European Waste-to-Energy (incineration with energy 
recovery) Plants. Our members are committed to ensuring high environmental standards, achieving low 
emissions and maintaining state of the art energy production from remaining waste that cannot be 
recycled in a sustainable way.

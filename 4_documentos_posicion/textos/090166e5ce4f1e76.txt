Shell response to the consultation on:  
Inception Impact Assessment: ReFuelEU Aviation - Sustainable Aviation Fuels. 
Shell* welcomes the EU’s Green Deal and supports the target to achieve climate neutrality in the EU 
by 2050.  The decarbonisation of the aviation sector is key to achieve this target, in view of continuous 
growth of passenger and commercial air transport. 
Shell believes that a sectoral approach is needed to decarbonise the av iation sector with significant 
investments and change across the whole aviation value chain:  original equipment manufacturers, 
airlines, fuel suppliers, airports and most importantly consumers.  New low CO 2 fuels and propulsion 
systems will need to be dev eloped, the use of alternative modes of transport optimised, and the 
efficiencies of air transport logistics and infrastructure increased, all in alignment with consumer 
acceptance.  In addition, remaining aviation emissions will need to be balanced through carbon sinks 
such as nature-based solutions to achieve net zero emissions.  As the air transport sector is hard to 
electrify and alternative lower carbon engine technologies are currently not available, sustainable 
aviation fuels (SAF) are the main viabl e technology to curb substantially the sector’s greenhouse gas 
emissions, especially in the shorter term.    
Shell Scenario Sketch: A Climate Neutral Europe by 2050** shows that achieving climate neutrality in 
the EU’s energy system would require tripling the use of biofuels between 2020 and 2050.  Demand 
in the upcoming decade will be largely met using waste-based biofuel feedstocks included in Annex IX 
Part B of the Renewable Energy Directive; whereas the share of advanced biofuels - made from 
feedstocks included in Annex IX Part A of the Renewable Energy Directive - will increase progressively 
and dominate biofuel consumption in Europe in 2050.  SAF based on biofuels will be essential to start 
decarbonising the aviation sector, whereas synfuels are foreseen to be available at scale earliest post 
2040. 
The commercial development and rollout of SAF in Europe needs long term clarity and legal certainty.  
In its assessment of policy options, the Commission should ensure supply side and demand side 
measures go hand in hand to allow the large -scale availability of SAF in the medium to long term.  
Cooperation between the aviation industry, fuels suppliers and governments will be key to address 
barriers to decarbonization in the aviation sector.*** 
Scaling up SAF will require time-limited financial support to reduce costs and help emerging advanced 
biofuel technologies become ready for commercial deployment.  This time -limited support should 
enable investment in novel production pathways of SAF and encourage the construction of a first wave 
of manufacturing plants in Europe in the 2020s.  Shell supports the Commission’s intention to set up 
an EU coordination platform to provide technical facilitation and support initiatives for the approval 
of new production pathways.  
Shell supports an EU wide mandate for the use of SAF, as this will strengthen the long-term certainty 
for investors.  In complement to other demand side policy measures, this mandate should have fuel 
suppliers as obligated parties. To support SAF which deliver the highest greenhouse gas emissions 
reduction, this mandate should be set as a CO2 intensity target on a “well to wing” basis.   
The impact of COVID -19 has provided the aviation sector with unprecedented challenges. Further 
analysis will be needed on the conditions, level and timing for the introduction of a SAF mandate. EU 
and national measures to support the recovery of the aviation sector could play an important role in 
encouraging a sustainable transition, aligned with the Green Deal objectives.

The level of the mandate should be ambitious but consistent with the pace of developing supply 
capabilities and infrastructure. The mandate should be gradually ramped up, as the production 
increases and SAF become commercially available.  However, if SA F volumes are not produced to 
expected levels, then regulators should adjust the mandate downwards to a level that maintains 
ambition, while providing obligated parties with a feasible route to compliance. The mandate should 
also have a clear alternative compliance pathway for example via a compliance fee paid into a fund to 
be recycled to support production or via tendering. Moreover, the system should enable the trading 
of compliance certificates between countries and obligated parties.  
The mandate shoul d initially allow the continued use of SAF made from first generation feedstocks 
compliant with the sustainability and greenhouse gas emission criteria set by the Renewable Energy 
Directive.  Moreover, Shell believes that a clear pathway to advanced feedst ocks and synfuels (e.g. 
electro-fuels) in aviation is needed.  To support this long-term diversification of production pathways 
and feedstocks used, a mandate for the use of SAF should include a sub -target for SAF made from 
advanced feedstocks (Annex IX Part A of the Renewable Energy Directive) and synfuels.  
Shell believes that the stability of the regulatory environments impacting feedstocks availability will 
drive confidence to invest in the production of SAF.  Policy measures supporting the production a nd 
consumption of SAF in Europe should incentivise biofuels for both aviation and road transport in the 
short term and help transition biofuels and power -to-liquids from road transport to aviation in the 
medium to long term.  Shell sees a need for consiste nt sustainability criteria of feedstocks for 
alternative fuels across the transport sector. 
Moreover, should excise duties for aviation fuels be introduced in future, these should be linked to 
the fuels CO 2 intensity to provide an additional incentive.  La st but not least, a robust and rising 
government-led carbon price will contribute to balancing out the higher purchase costs of SAF.  To 
that end, the EU cap in the EU Emissions Trading System should be tightened, and the free allowances 
given to airlines reduced.

Definitions and cautionary note of the New Sketch: A Climate-Neutral Eu By 2050 
On March 4, 2020, the EU Commission proposed the European Climate Law that would estab lish a 
legally binding target of net -zero greenhouse gas emissions by 2050. Shell strongly supports the 
proposed European Climate Law and its binding target of net-zero greenhouse gas emissions by 2050. 
We believe meeting this target will be extremely chal lenging but possible. This report contains an 
assessment of what we believe may be needed to decarbonise the energy system in order for the EU 
to meet the proposed target of net -zero greenhouse gas emissions by 2050. This overview is not 
intended to be proscriptive and there are other pathways for the EU to follow in reaching the target. 
It is important to note that the suggestions contained in this report are those to be taken by the EU, 
and not necessarily Shell. While Shell is supportive of the EU target  of net -zero greenhouse gas 
emissions by 2050, our current business plan is not consistent with the proposed EU target. However, 
as announced on April 16, 2020, Shell aims to be a net -zero emissions energy business by 2050. 
Accordingly, we expect that over  time, our business plan will change as society and our customers 
move toward meeting the goals of the Paris Agreement. We believe that the proposed European 
Climate Law is a significant step in this journey.

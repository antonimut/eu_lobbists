Revision of the Industrial Emissions Directive 
Cerame-Unie’s feedback to the inception impact assessment  
 
 
 
Cerame-Unie, t he Europea n Ceramic Industry Association , welcomes the start of the Impact 
Assessment on the revision of the Directive 2010/75/EU on Industrial Emissions  (IED), with an 
inception impact assessment. We believe it is an important and necessary step, in order to assess the 
framework of the impact assessment. Regarding the proposed roadmap, we would like to highlight  
some key issues which need to be taken into consideration by the European Commission. 
 
Timeframe 
The Ceramic industry BREF process under the IED only recently started. We are therefore concerned 
that premature evaluations might paint a distorted picture o f the expected status. The IED is one  of 
the key pieces of environmental legislation that r egulates industrial activities and s uch legislation 
requires time for deployment and for its effects to take shape. It is our strong wish to actively 
contribute to t his process and we hope that the impact assessment  will be based on the best 
available data to allow the right conditions to design fit-for-purpose Directives.  
 
Differences amongst Members states 
In general, we strongly welcome the IED Business as usual scenario, as this was reflected by industry in 
the evaluation of the IED.  We support the primary objective of the IED to create a level playing field in 
Europe in the environmental performance of installations . However, this depends on the execution of 
the local authorities. When preparing the impact assessment , attention should be paid to the cost -
benefit ratio of certain techniques and to the difference of implementation, conditions and 
reporting among member states. 
 
IED for proper choice of decarbonisation technologies 
Furthermore, we remain committed to developing and implement ing GHG emission abatement 
measures that contribute to the achievement of a climate -neutral EU economy. We also support a full 
coherency with the IED and its integrated approach based on the BAT concept. However, we reject the 
option of regulating GHG emissions under an IED permitting regime.  GHG emissions are regulated 
under the EU ETS and we object any double regulation,  as the EU ETS is a well-functioning tool 
implemented throughout energy intensive industries. 
 
IED with a Circular Economy 
In the ceramic industry, the use of secondary material is already  widespread and will be incentivised 
even more under the new EC new Circular Economy strategy.  As a result, mandatory rates are not 
necessary as  they will lead to problems with the product quality as well as  with the supply and

Inception Impact Assessment 
Comments 
LanzaTech, Inc. 
LanzaTech commends the European Commission for its consideration of policies in support of aviation 
greenhouse gas reductions and increased supply and offtake of sustainable aviation fuels in the EU. 
We welcome the opportunity to respond  to the Objectives and Policy Option s put forward in the 
Inception Impact Assessment (IIA).  The following outlines our feedback and recommendations on  
some of the specific policy mechanisms put forth in the IIA. 
• SAF Blending Mandate 
We support the concept of an EU-wide SAF blend mandate with the following recommendations:  
a) The mandate should come into force after a minimum of three years to enable 
implementation of commercial SAF projects and to allow airlines, airports, and other 
stakeholders time to prepare for SAF use.  
b) To be effective, the cost of non-compliance should exceed and possibly be tied to the cost 
differential between SAF and conventional fuels. 
c) The SAF blending mandate should persist for a minimum of 15 years in order to provide 
sufficient assurance for project financing, both equity and debt. 
• Revision of the Multiplier 
We support the upward revision of the multiplier with the following recommendations: 
a) It would be helpful for the Commission to develop EU-wide guidance on transposition of the 
multiplier into national legislation, including interaction with other elements of RED II, such 
as double-counting of fuels from certain feedstocks. 
b) To have the desired effect of stimulating SAF production, the multiplier should be set at a 
level that reflects the increased cost of SAF production relative to sustainable ground 
transport fuels. 
• Funding Mechanism 
We agree that new and expanded funding mechanisms will be needed to ensure expand capacity 
for SAF production and to close the price gap as SAF production economics improve. 
a) EU funding mechanisms should prioritize deployment of SAF pathways that expand the 
feedstock base, as the oil-based feedstocks required for the HEFA pathway, currently 
operating at commercial scale in multiple locations, are limited. 
b) EU funding mechanisms should be reserved for facilities that produce SAF as a significant 
part of their product slate to ensure that increased volumes of SAF volumes are consistently 
produced.  
c) EU funding mechanisms are needed to reduce the risk of investment in the first few 
commercial plants to implement new SAF technologies that are ready for commercial scale 
production. Funding can be in the form of capital grants and/or facilities for guaranteed, low-
cost debt.

d) EU funding mechanisms are needed to support scale up and commercial demonstration of 
new SAF technology pathways so that they may develop the technical and financial data 
needed to design and finance commercial-scale plants.  
e) EU funding mechanisms are needed to support the price gap between SAF and conventional 
jet fuel as SAF technologies come down the price curve. One possible mechanism is a feed-in 
tariff, similar to those already in place for green electricity from PV installations or wind 
energy. In this system producers (or buyers) get a set contribution to cover (a part of) the 
additional costs of SAF relative to fossil jet. This support should be made available for an 
extended period to provide predictable economics for SAF project developers and off-takers.  
f) EU funding mechanisms are needed to support the build out of infrastructure needed for 
expanded SAF utilization, including SAF transport, storage and blending.  
g) The ‘State Aid rules for environmental protection and energy’ need to be revised as the 
current Guidance only lasts until the end of 2020. It is important that the revised version of 
these State Aid rules allows governmental and/ or EU support for the development of the EU 
SAF industry in parallel with a policy incentive (such as a blend mandate). These rules should 
not limit the amount of support given to SAF projects by the governments/ EU. 
• Prioritization 
We agree that it is appropriate to establish priorities for both funding and feedstocks. 
a) As noted under Funding Mechanisms above, we recommend that priority be given to funding 
of SAF pathways that diversify the SAF feedstock pool and facilities producing SAF as a 
significant fraction of total output. 
b) In addition, we recommend prioritizing production of new SAF feedstocks and collection of 
waste feedstocks to ensure that there is sufficient supply for expanded SAF production.   
c) As with any fuel production process, feedstock is the largest single cost associated with SAF 
production. Therefore, both feedstock prioritization and financial support for SAF production 
could be accomplished with some form of financial incentive for purpose-grown and waste 
feedstocks that are sold for SAF production. 
• Monitoring 
We agree that common SAF definition and reporting requirements for all EU States is desirable to 
ensure a consistent and complete monitoring of the SAF production and use in the EU. The 
reporting requirements should be harmonized with those of CORSIA to minimize the burden on 
producers and end users. 
• Sustainability 
We recommend that part of any policy to promote and expand SAF production contain clear 
sustainability guidelines and leverage mechanisms for sustainability certification that have been 
previously applied to road transport fuels. In addition, we recommend that: 
a)  As noted previously, SAF policies should promote diversification of pathways and feedstocks. 
From the standpoint of sustainability, priority should be given to pathways using waste and 
residue feedstocks, including agricultural and forestry wastes, unrecyclable municipal wastes, 
and waste industrial process gases. 
b) Sustainability certification schemes that address all aspects of sustainability, including 
environmental, social, economic impacts should be consulted in developing EU SAF policies 
and their principles incorporated into financing decisions.

Revision of the Industrial Emissions Directive   
European Aluminium preliminary views  
17 April 2020  
 
 
Preliminary response  from the aluminium industry   
European Aluminium thanks the Commission for welcoming participation in the Industrial Emissions Directive  (IED) 
revision. After having followed the process since the IED evaluation , we would like to share our views on the 
Inception Impact Assessment and create a direct communication line with our industry.  
These are the main items we would like to bring to the attention of the Commission:  
• The Industrial Emissions Directive (IED) has at its core the integrated approach. It is paramount that this 
philosophy prevails through subsequent steps of the revision as abatement is only workable when 
considered in the whole co ntext of the installation. As described by the Commission, the permits must take 
into account the whole environmental performance of the plant 1. In this line of thought, we advise to 
consider this the point of departure of any future work on the IED Directive.  
• In our understanding of the recast exercise of the Integrated Pollution Prevention and Control (IPPC) 
Directive2, the IED was an instrument that excluded green-house gases (GHG) as specified by Article 9  
(without prejudice to the article 9.1) . European Aluminium understands that the policy instrument for 
regulating GHG emissions to date is the ETS system , under which the aluminium industry is considered. The 
IED and ETS have different scoping which could result in even more complicated regulation systems. We urge 
the Commission to go back to those conclusions and at least use them as basis for the Inception Impact 
Assessment. 
• Circular Economy can adopt multiple facets when industrial systems are addressed. Industrial systems are 
often ruled by the principle of process efficiency 3: a system is considered ‘optimised’ when the maximum 
benefit is obtained via the reduction of inputs and outputs. Therefore, it is paramount to address the 
systems as a part of the industrial process itself, and to mak e the circular economy  potential of a site 
workable within the context of the installation by considering what is already optimised.  
European Aluminium and its members remain available for any further clarification on the above and reiterate their 
wish to remain an engaged stakeholder through the revision process and beyond.  
 
About European Aluminium 
European Aluminium, founded in 1981 and based in Brussels, is the voice of the aluminium industry in Europe. We 
actively engage with decision makers and the wider stakeholder community to promote the outstanding properties 
of aluminium, secure growth and optimise our metal’s contribution to meeting Europe’s sustainability challenges4.

 
IATA 21 April 2020 
 
 
ReFuelEU Aviation : Sustainable Aviation Fuels - Inception impact 
assessment consultation. 
The International Air Transport Association (IATA) welcomes the opportunity to comment 
on ReFuelEU Aviation Inception Impact Assessment. 
IATA is the global trade association of the world’s airlines representing more than 290 
airlines.   In 2009, IATA along with the global aviation industry set ambitious de-
carbonization goals including carbon neutral growth from 2020, and halving net CO2 by 
2050, relative to 2005 levels.  Meeting these targets will involve a suite of measures, 
including new aircraft and engines, more efficient flight and operating procedures and 
improved airport infrastructure. Since these goals were established, fuel efficiency has 
improved at an annualised rate of 2.3%, exceeding the 1.5% target.  However, IATA 
recognises the requirement for greater achievement.   
IATA has been an active and strong proponent for the ICAO Carbon Offsetting and 
Reduction Scheme for International Aviation (CORSIA), aiming to deliver carbon neutral 
growth from 2020.   Longer term, it is widely accepted that halving net CO2 by 2050, 
relative to 2005 levels will require substantial replacement of traditional jet fuel with 
substantial quantities of Sustainable Aviation Fuel (SAF) and this represents one of the 
most important pillars of the industry’s efforts to reduce carbon emissions.  
IATA notes that the production cost of SAF is currently at least twice as high as that of 
conventional jet fuel and higher than that of sustainable alternative fuels used in other 
transport modes, depending on the pathway. Due to these higher costs, SAF are, in 
absence of support, not an economically viable substitute to conventional jet fuel. The 
price volatility of the feedstock (important cost component of the final fuel price) 
contributes to the market uncertainty and acts as a disincentive to invest. In the absence 
of a long-term, stable policy framework with sufficient and appropriately designed 
incentives, the necessary confidence for major investments in large scale SAF production 
will be challenging.   
 
IATA supports the REFuelEU Aviation objective of designing effective policy to help 
reduce the environmental footprint of the aviation sector.  Specifically, the incentive-
based approach is favoured which can support stable market demand and incentivise 
production.  This approach can enable end users to make purchase commitments at 
prices which are competitive to conventional fuel.

All policy options need to be carefully evaluated to ensure they achieve the desired 
purpose without unintended negative consequences, both environmental and market 
distortions.  Two approaches that contain significant externality risk include aviation taxes 
and mandate obligations on airlines.   
 
Aviation taxes represent a policy instrument more closely linked to demand management 
than addressing CO2 reduction.  Historically taxes have been environmentally ineffective 
as they aim to lower demand by raising general tax revenue, while not focusing directly on 
addressing CO2.  
 
Mandates can present similar risks.  Aviation operators are highly sensitive to competitive 
price distortions.   In 2019, world-wide airline net post-tax profit per departing passenger 
was $6.12 USD.   A competitive aviation sector delivers significant value to both 
consumers and economies, however the sensitivities to market distortion needs to be 
understood to ensure these benefits do not evaporate in a mandate distorted aviation 
market.   
 
IATA believes there are a number of key criteria that need to be carefully assessed when 
designing mandate policies, some of which include;  
• Appropriate mechanisms to incentivise investment 
• To minimise competitive distortion and carbon leakage 
• Reflect availability of different feedstocks 
• Support industry accepted sustainability standards 
Policies such as revising the renewable energy directive multiplier (1.2x) for aviation is a 
mechanism that has the potential to put the production of sustainable aviation fuel on a 
more equal footing with the production of sustainable fuel for ground transport. 
Given aviation is expected to require a liquid energy fuel into the foreseeable future, 
prioritizing feedstock for aviation is strategically important.  IATA supports robust 
sustainability standards and these should be verified by third-party organisations with 
such expertise.  Feedstock options should not be restricted if sustainability can be 
demonstrated.   
Overall, IATA believes there are many aspects within the REFuelEU Aviation – Sustainable 
Aviation Fuel proposal that can be developed into positive and productive mechanisms to 
advance the commercial deployment of sustainable aviation fuel in Europe.   
IATA looks forward to working constructively with the European Commission to ensure the 
objectives of this initiative are delivered and SAF can play a substantial role in overall 
European emissions reduction objectives.

INDUSTRIAL EMISSIONS DIRECTIVE REVISION  
Euroheat & Power contribution to the IIA Roadmap 
The IED’s main purpose is to set environmental protection standards for the industry and provide a framework 
for industrial operations to meet the identified best available techniques. Euroheat & Power (EHP), representing 
the District Heating and Cooling (DHC) sector in Europe and beyond, support the ambition to achieve a high 
level of environmental protection. EHP has actively participated in the LCP and WI BREFs elaboration processes 
which requirements are under implementation.  
The IED has been a driver for the effective reduction of industrial pollutants emissions. The Directive keeps its 
relevance in view of achieving climate -neutrality by 2050, as the transformation to a fully decarbonised and 
sustainable economy will require good regulation of all emissions that may harm our environment, not only 
GHG. The DHC sector has committed to pursuing the full decarbonisation of its networks in Europe before 2050, 
to contribute to the goals set out in the Paris Agreement1 and in line with the EU Green Deal. 
An evaluation of the IED has been carried out in the course of 2019, to which EHP provided feedback. However, 
the Staff Working Document communicating the conclusions of the evaluation process, as well as the final 
consultant report have not been published yet while the revision process of the IED  is moving forward . This 
appears to contradict the logic underpinning the better regulation principle.  
Despite this information gap, EHP would like to make the following comments with regards to the upcoming 
Commission impact assessment (IA) for the revision of the IED: 
The IA should explore options to improve the BREF and BAT conclusions elaboration process to make it more 
transparent, objective and streamlined. In particular: 
• The identification of Key Environmental Impacts (KEI) should be consistent, following a set of defined 
criteria and efficient, focusing on parameters likely to deliver noteworthy additional environmental 
benefits. A good measure would be to lift this task from the individual TWG of each BREF revision to 
the Article 13 Forum level. Such an approach should ensure a harmonised prioritisation and more 
efficient resource allocation. 
• A systematic methodology should lead to the derivation of BAT-AELs. 
• Sound economic analysis of compliance costs with the requirements of the IED and BAT conclusions 
should be carried out. Based on member feedback, EHP estimates that LCP costs of compliance have 
been very significant, and the process of implementation is still ongoing. 
• The review of the BREFs and BAT -C should be aligned with the economic and investment cycle s of 
industrial installations in order to minimise stranded assets. 
• Legal certainty should be ensured, during the revision of the IED and no BREF review should be initiated.  
While the IED should remain the backbone for environmental protection with regards to industrial pollution, 
the climate-neutrality objective will only be achieved with a combination of policies.  The IA should  analyse 
interactions with other EU legislation  and ensure full consistency of EU environment, climate and energy 
frameworks.

The DHC sector has invested in upgrading its plants from technological, and environmental perspectives to meet 
the increasingly higher level of exigence.  Many networks have already switched to renewable energy and 
recover waste heat, thus increasing sustain ability, efficien cy and ultimately reducing their environmental 
impacts. 
To align with the EU energy and climate objectives and support the transition towards a carbon -neutral 
economy, the IED and BREF process need to support the role of biomass2 to replace fossil fuels. 
Synergies between EU policies could be explored in the IA. However, the IED should not replace or overlap with 
dedicated legislative or market instruments such as the circular economy framework or the EU-ETS. 
• The heat market is not a level playing field. DHC plants’ GHG emissions are regulated by the EU-ETS 
while fossil fuels are given an unjustified advantage . Indeed, individual small-scale heating systems 
below 20 MW using fossil fuels are not covered by the EU ETS.   
• In the same way, the IED has set a number of requirements impacting DHC operators and weighting on 
their competit iveness in the heating sector that are not affecting individual less efficient heating 
solutions.  
• While the IED should not regulate CO 2 emissions, it aims at protecting the environment and should 
leave the possibility for other GHG to be regulated in permits when significant local pollution is caused. 
• The IED and permits regulate plant performance, activities on -site and environmental effects caused 
by those activities. The circular economy is a value chain approach not particularly suit ed for the IED. 
The existing provisions on secondary raw materials, resource efficiency, waste reduction and industrial 
symbiosis seem to sufficiently serve circularity, as the IED is not the main tool to drive this policy. 
In conclusion, the Impact Assessment should: 
• Investigate policy scenarios backed by cost-benefit and evidence-based analysis, the IA should draw on 
the experience acquired implementing the IED and the BREF BAT Conclusions.  
• Avoid any risk of double double -regulation. The IED can only play a supportive role to the main 
instruments dealing with GHG reduction and circular economy.  
• Follow the subsidiarity principle. The conditions of operation at BAT level should be uniform throughout 
the EU but should remain under the control of national authorities. 
The evaluation of the IED was not timely  and we consider that the IED should not be opened for changes . As 
repeatedly pointed out, there is a lack of return on experience; only about half of the BREFs have been updated 
under the IED, of which less than half have entered into force.  
Moreover, given the severity of the crisis the European industry is currently experiencing and its likely lasting 
impact, EHP would consider wise to hold the process for the time being . It would allow stakeholders to focus 
their resources on recovery. This would not only benefit the quality of the IA process but also allow to take into 
account some of the profound changes in the industrial-economic landscape induced by the pandemic, in the 
interest of all parties.

Sustainable aviation fuels – ReFuelEU Aviation – Position 
of German Aviation Association 
The German Aviation Association very much welcomes the initiative “ReFuelEU 
Aviation – Sustainable Aviation Fuels” of the European Commission. In addition to the 
UN aviation organization ICAO, which should set the global framework to push the 
market launch of Sustainable Aviation Fuel (SAF), it is the European Union, which 
should set the EU-framework to avoid national patchwork regulations for an industry 
which works in a strong global competition. Therefore the main challenge for 
ReFuelEU Aviation will be to push SAF into the market without market distortions 
between European and non-European Airlines. German federal ministries and states 
are working together with plant manufacturers, fuel producers and aviation industry 
on the development of a joint roadmap for electro-fuels. 
Until now, synthetic fuels from renewable energy sources have not been available in sufficient quan-
tities but are mainly produced in very small amounts as part of research projects and are still too ex-
pensive. With current oil prices at historic lows, the price of SAF is still 5 to 6 times higher than fossil 
kerosene. Should the after-corona-recession persist, this price differences could prevail. Neverthe-
less, the technology for production and usage of synthetic fuels is proven and feasible. The most im-
portant starting point is therefore to set up industrial plants for the production of SAF, in particular 
electro-fuels, in order to produce sufficient quantities and to leverage scaling and learning effects. 
The German aviation industry is prepared to participate in pilot projects. Expecting that cost efficient 
electro-fuels will not be produced in Europe because of missing renewable energy, we support and 
participate in the Global Alliance Power Fuel, an association of the energy industry, fuel producers, 
plant manufactures, aviation, and the automotive industry. Because the question of whether and 
when such fuels can be used is for sure not a matter of technical feasibility but of an empowering en-
ergy policy framework, ReFuelEU Aviation is of utmost importance. 
SAF blending mandate 
Today, synthetic fuels cost several times more than conventional kerosene. A binding quota for 
blending synthetic fuels with fossil kerosene would be a preferable option at a global level as it would 
affect all airlines in the same way. Thus, any attempt to set such a quota in regional exclusive agree-
ments such as EU could affect competition in air transport. 
Fuel costs already account for around a quarter of an airline’s operating costs. If it was stipulated 
that if European airlines have to use a blend of fossil kerosene with 10 percent power-to-liquid fuel, 
the operating costs of European airlines would increase by 11 percent. With a 50 percent quota, op-
erating costs would increase by 55 percent. Corresponding to the increase of operating costs for Eu-
ropean airlines would be an increase of distortion of competition in favour of non-European air carri-
ers. 
On the other hand, the effects on airlines with their main operations abroad would be marginal. Even 
if non-European airlines would have to use blends accordingly, this would have only a minimal effect

on their whole operating costs. Such unequal costs could not be passed on to customers by the Euro-
pean airlines in the form of higher prices and would therefore result in a clear competitive disad-
vantage. 
It is essential, that a blending mandate reflects the SAF production capacity and is technologically ag-
nostic and it should include a special mandate for electro-fuels. Without such a sub-mandate, the 
mandate will only focus on existing technology whereas progress on electro-fuels is pivotal, espe-
cially in the long-term due to limited availability of biomass meeting high sustainability criteria. 
In any case, an internationally coordinated roadmap is needed to ensure that plants are built and 
that fuel is sold at marketable prices. Any quota regulations should ideally be defined globally analo-
gous to the internationally agreed CO2 pricing instrument CORSIA. The EU should therefore also take 
the issues forward at global level within ICAO. 
Any quota should focus on reliable sustainability criteria and allow sufficient lead time for the respec-
tive production facilities to be set-up. An overly ambitious quota regarding the mandated SAF share 
or regarding the timeline will either divert bio material streams from other uses or promote non-sus-
tainable production practices. It should furthermore focus on supporting development of the right 
technologies to reducing aviation emissions in the long-term rather than aiming for short term emis-
sion reductions that are not sufficiently scalable in the long-term. 
Revision of the multiplier 
The multiplier for aviation with 1.2 is lower than for other sectors: 1.5 for railway-electricity and 4 for 
electricity used in cars. A factor of 1.2 is not a real incentive for fuel manufacturers. It must therefore 
be increased in order to accelerate SAF production. The impact assessment should examine which 
factor is sufficient to create a real incentive and if there is a necessity to implement a different multi-
plier for electro-fuels and advanced biofuels. 
A central auctioning mechanism 
We support a central auctioning mechanism. 
Funding mechanism 
Financial support should aim for reduction of SAF cost by evaluation of the best production concept 
followed by initial scale-up. Large-scale production outside of the EU should be anticipated due to 
feedstock and energy potentials as well as lower production costs. 
To bridge the cost gap between fossil fuel and SAF, the aviation industry proposes for Germany, that 
the federal government uses the revenues from the national aviation tax in future for the production 
and market introduction of electro-fuels. In the past years, the government has collected - as a sub-
stitution to a kerosene tax - around €1.2 billion in air travel tax per year without using the tax reve-
nue specifically for climate policy purposes in aviation. This April the tax rate was significantly in-
creased. Starting this year a part of the taxes will be used to scale-up the SAF-technology. We recom-
mend harmonisation at an European level in this respect, including the use of funds (eventually a cer-
tain share of a harmonised aviation tax) and coordinated investment/expense strategy

It should be noted at this point that an air transport levy/tax is more suitable as a financing instru-
ment in terms of climate policy and competition than a kerosene tax because the air transport 
levy/tax is related to the final destination, i.e. independent of a stopover, and a kerosene tax is only 
effective until the first stopover. Additionally an European funding, e.g. budget resources from the 
European Green Deal, should be used to accelerate the scale-up and market introduction of ad-
vanced SAF – in particular electro-fuels.  
Prioritisation 
Decarbonisation in aviation is more expensive than in other modes of transport. Besides moderniza-
tion of the aircraft fleet, the optimization of operational processes and the realization of the Single 
European Sky (SES) aviation has no alternative to reduce CO2 at large scale than to use SAF. There-
fore, production processes for alternative fuels should preferably address high potential feedstock / 
energy resources, aim for high GHG savings and in mid-term enable a higher-than-current blending 
ratio as today (max 50 percent). 
Voluntary agreements 
The cooperation of all stakeholders is essential. A European platform is therefore very useful. But in 
order not to create new structures, existing platforms should be used. For example, the Global Alli-
ance Powerfuels (www.powerfuels.org) already exists, which is dealing with the market ramp-up of 
electro-fuels, among other things. In this context, voluntary purchase commitments for sustainable 
aviation fuels produced are possible. 
Simplification and/or administrative burden 
A book-and-claim-concept is sure to facilitate carbon accounting and reduce administrative burden 
while enabling all EU airlines to purchase SAF even if there is no SAF-supplied airport in their individ-
ual route network. With book and claim, the SAF will be accounted for by certificates like green elec-
tricity in the common supply grid. This is essential, because it would eliminate the administrative bur-
den for the SAF logistics upstream of the airport tank farm and reduce logistics-related emissions by 
avoiding a parallel fuel logistics network for SAF. 
Other aspects to be taken into account 
While the transport of SAF is mostly accepted by almost all means of transport (ships, trains, trucks), 
there is still reluctance to accept it in the Central European Pipeline System (CEPS). This supply chain 
is of utmost importance for a secure and timely supply of the major European Hubs in Belgium (BRU), 
Germany (FRA) and Switzerland (ZRH). The acceptance must be agreed by all five CEPS Nato Mem-
bers. Today this is missing. To solve this problem the roadmap of ReFuelEU Aviation should keep it on 
the agenda. 
With regard to the sustainability criteria of SAF we need to mitigate investor’s risks through robust, 
stable, science-based sustainability criteria for all feedstocks, processes and fuels. These sustainabil-
ity criteria should be harmonised with other sectors/legislations (RED, FQD etc.) and easily applied to

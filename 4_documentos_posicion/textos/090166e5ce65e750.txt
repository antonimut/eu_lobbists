 
 
Climate Action Network (CAN) Europe feedback on EU rules on industrial emissions 
 
Summary  
Climate Action Network Europe  welcomes the European Commission found the review of the IED necessary and 
the need to improve its implementation, delivery and effectiveness on its objectives.  
In order to align with the Paris Agreement 1.5C goal, the EU economy needs to undergo a major transformation to 
become a circular, decarbonised (by 2040) and zero pollution economy. The Industrial Emissions Directive (IED) has 
a key role to play in driving this transformation, particularly for industry sectors with high emissions levels to all 
environmental media. 
Further work is needed to lead  to the transition towards climate-neutral, resource efficient and non -polluting 
industrial and energy production in the EU. The IED provides the opportunity to implement an ‘integrated approach’ 
meaning to address all environmental impacts and to set the best available standards to prevent negative impacts 
from industrial activities. It should prevent pollution by fully integrating the circular economy and decarbonisation 
policies in order to put us on track towards zero-pollution industrial production.  
 
Thus, we welcome the conclusion by the European Commission that a review of the IED is necessary, and we support 
the preliminary list of problems identified. 
Detailed comments: 
1.1. Implementation issues 
We support the findings of the European Commission as stated in the Inception Impact Assessment document. We 
would particularly highlight the following aspects:

- Ignoring the interplay of relevant Environmental Quality Standards (Art 18) both at the level of defining the 
BREF benchmarks and in the implementation phase; 
- Various methods and cost benefit assessments used to decide on Art 15.4 derogations, uneven way of ensuring 
early and effective public participation in the procedures; 
- Excessive use of Art 15.4 derogations ,1 unclear definition of Art 15.4 a) and b) criteria – “geographical 
location”, “local environmental conditions” and “technical characteristics”; Undermining of the energy 
efficiency BREF requirements , mainly due t o EU -ETS (Article 26/ Art 9 of the IED) and the non -binding 
character of BAT-AEELs. The Article 9.2 of the IED needs to introduce minimal binding (and not optional) energy 
efficiency standards based on best in class solutions within a given industrial activity (e.g. electricity, heat 
generation, steel, cement); 
- Significant shortcomings in enforcement (sanctions and penalties); 
- Vague definition of (non-)substantial permit change which leads to overuse of the non-substantial change of 
permit regime, which severely limits any effective public participation; 
- Improvements should be achieved through the revision of the legal provisions, especially a change of legal form 
to “Regulation” would significantly improve the manner of implementation by Member States as well as ensure 
a level playing field for the stakeholders throughout the EU. 
1.2. BREF process 
We support the findings of the European Commission as stated in the Inception Impact Assessment document. 
Improvements should be achieved through the revision of the legal provisions and the BREF review rules. 
1.3. Access to information, participation in decision making and access to justice 
We support the findings of the European Commission as stated in the Inception Impact Assessment document. We 
would especially point out the issue of lack of transparent data on emissions and the measurement uncertainty 
subtraction, which has an adverse impact on emission limits compliance assessment. Improvements should be 
achieved through the revision of the legal provisions, relevant Commission Implementing rules (2018/1135/EU and 
2019/1741/EU), revision of the EU PRTR and Global UNECE Protocol (ongoing).  
1.4.  Interaction with decarbonisation of industry 
We support the findings of the European Commission as stated in the Inception Impact Assessment document. We 
advocate for a combined approach of market-based tool (EU-ETS) with command and control performance-based 
tool (IED). Decarbonisation is to be included in the revised framework and the ongoing BREF reviews, BAT -AEELs 
and/or other forms of carbon emission standard or other fuel switching obligations should be  binding for all 
relevant industrial sources.  
Including greenhouse gas emissions within the scope of the IED does not entail duplication of regulation vis -a-vis 
the ETS Directive, as stated in IED recitals 9  , because both frameworks are complementary and follow different 
approaches, and the EU ETS has not lead to any meaningful industrial greenhouse gas reductions for almost a 
decade. In addition, the EU ETS sets a price level on carbon allowances which is no t based on the Best Available

Improvements should be achieved through the revision of  the legal provisions (change to “Regulation”) and

energy (electricity, heat or mechanical energy). 
Therefore, we call for: 
● Deletion of IED Art. 9.1 in order to enable a n integrated approach of command and control (IED)  and market-
based instruments (EU ETS); 
● Introduction of GHG performance standards to achieve a complete coal and lignite phase -out in the European 
power and industrial sectors by 2030, and industrial decarbonisation in line with  achieving climate neutrality by 
2040; 
● Introduce a Paris Agreement compliance test for new industrial investments and major  refurbishments in 
environmental permitting. This measure would avoid a lock-in of investments into high emitting infrastructure. 
1.5. Coherence with other EU legislation 
We support the findings of the European Commission as stated in the Inception Impact Assessment document, but 
we would argue against limiting the assessment to the E-PRTR only.   
We welcome the intention to improve external and internal policy coherence and provide added value and 
effectiveness in their implementation. Coherence should be improved so that the revised IED framework provides 
added value on the following aspects to be addressed under the integrated approach: Air quality, Water quality, 
Circular economy, Waste management, Sustainable use of re sources, Chemical risks and hazards, Energy 
and Climate change (decarbonisation).  Any legislation referred to under Article 18 (EQS) should be carefully 
assessed and the revised IED framework adapted/improved so as to deliver better on those objectives. 
Inconsistencies/incoherence with EU legislation have been identified on the following: E-PRTR, Aarhus Convention 
and EU ETS/climate change agenda. Improvements should be achieved through the revision of the legal provisions 
(change to “Regulation”) and possible review of EU legislation e.g. EU -ETS Directive (deletion of Article 26) and 
revised E-PRTR.

 
 
 
Inception Impact Assessment- Enerkem Comments 
 
Enerkem produces advanced biofuels and renewable chemicals by converting non-recyclable, 
mixed wastes into methanol, ethanol and other widely -used chemicals. Headquartered in 
Montreal (QC), Canada, Enerkem operates a full -scale commercial facility in Alberta as well as 
an innovation centre in Quebec. 
  
The Company is currently developing a bio-refinery in Rotterdam, the Netherlands that will convert 
up to 360,000 tons of waste into 220,000 tons (270 million litres) of bio - methanol – a chemical 
building block that is used to manufacture a broad range of everyday products, as well as being 
a renewable fuel. This represents the total annual waste of mo re than 700,000 households and 
represents a CO2 emission savings in excess of 200,000 tonnes per annum when compared to 
the production of methanol from fossil fuels. Enerkem has also entered into a project development 
agreement covering initial investments in to an advanced carbon molecular recycling facility in 
Tarragona, Spain. Th is facility will be the first of its kind in Spain dedicated to the commercial 
production of green methanol from secondary raw materials derived mostly from pretreated non-
recoverable waste. 
 
Enerkem welcomes the opportunity to provide the following comments regarding the Objectives 
and Policy Options put forward in the Inception Impact Assessment (IIA) of the European 
Commission to enable a supportive policy environment for an increase in  the production and 
offtake of Sustainable Aviation Fuel (SAF): 
 
1) SAF BLEND MANDATE 
 
a) Enerkem supports a n EU wide SAF blend mandate . However, we strongly urge the  
Commission to increase the RES-T target if this mandate is made part of the REDII.  
 
b) A SAF blend mandate should be agreed and announced at  least three to five years before 
implementation. This gives companies time to develop new production  capacity and secure 
investments. Furthermore, a smooth transition between current SAF  supporting policy  
mechanism and newly announced supporting mechanisms should be ensured. 
c) The EU-wide SAF blend mandate must be in place for a minimum of 15 years to generate a 
strong business case for investors and to create confidence amongst shareholders.  Enerkem 
would like to stress that only stringent implementation of the current REDII in member states will 
enable both sustainable feedstock supply and technology development tha t are needed to 
produce SAF in larger quantities. Many of the plants under construction or under consideration to 
produce advanced biofuels for road transport sector will be crucial in the coming decade to supply 
also fuels for the aviation sector. 
 
d) Enerkem recommends that the obligated party for  the proposed SAF mandate be the fuel 
suppliers as it is in the RED II for consistency sake and to ensure stability amongst the supply 
chain.

e) Recycled Carbon Fuels  should be taken into account when setting the mandates for the 
aviation sector. These fuels can help in reducing the amount of carbon emitted by the aviation 
sector. 
 
f) In order to ensure swift and proper uptake  of an EU -wide blend mandate, a cost for non-
compliance (set higher than the cost of compliance) should be implemented. This will guarantee 
the obligatory parties of the mandate to adhere to the offtake or production of the mandatory 
amount of SAF. 
 
2) REVISION OF MULTIPLIER 
 
Enerkem does not support a multiplier as the sole policy mechanism to stimulate the  production 
and offtake of SAF in Europe. It is recommended that the multiplier function as  an intermediate 
policy mechanism until an EU-wide policy mechanism comes into place. While the multiplier is in 
place, we rec ommend the Commission  release EU - wide guidance on how to interpret and 
transpose the SAF policies of the RED II into national legislation. An overarching approach should 
be adopted to prevent varying approaches to the legislation. For example, it is advisable to clarify 
how the multiplier will be applied in the different policy frameworks such as a bio-ticket system 
versus a blend-mandate system. 
 
3) FUNDING MECHANISM 
 
a) EU funding mechanisms are needed to financially support new SAF technology pathways with 
lower technology Technology-Readiness-Levels (TRLs) as well as those which are already higher 
on the TRL scale (pilot and demo-scale) and which still need to be commercialised. 
Pathways being developed from demo to commercial scale have the most significant price gaps 
compared to existing fuels and therefore require the most support.  
 
b) Many European countries have fixed price support mechanisms in place such as feed-in tariffs 
for green electricity from PV installations of wind energy and contracts-for-difference mechanisms 
for sustainable power production from waste . Fixed price  mechanisms could prove useful  
mechanisms for SAF as well, based on actual supply. Under these systems, producers (or buyers) 
get a set contribution to cover (a part of) the additional costs compa red with fossil fuels. This  
support should be made available for a longer period of time and could (partially) come from  
national budgets as aviation fuels may count towards their renewable energy targets and support 
them in their energy transition from fossil to renewable. 
 
c) The evaluation of any new funding mechanism should take account of the wider impact on the 
feedstock market and other, existing biofuel production pathways.  It is important to ensure that 
the introduction of new incentives leads to additional advanced biofuel production and does not 
simply displacement existing production by distorting feedstock markets.  
 
d) The ‘State Aid rules for environmental protection and energy’ need to be revised as the current 
Guidance only lasts until the end of 2020. It is important that the revised version of  these State 
Aid rules allows governmental and/ or EU support for the development of the EU SAF industry in 
addition to a policy incentive (such as a blend mandate). Furthermore, these rules should not limit 
the amount of support given to these projects by the governments/ EU. This is currently the case 
(for example, no financial support can be given for biofuel facilities  which exceed the production 
 
 
of 150.000 tonnes per year in case of an active policy incentive for biofuels in an EU Member 
State) and should be amended for all road transportation fuels as well. 
 
4) MONITORING 
 
A common SAF definition and reporting requirements for all EU States should be put in place  to 
ensure a consistent and complete monitoring system of SAF production and use in the EU. 
 
5) SUSTAINABILITY APPROACH  
 
Enerkem strongly recommends having  clear guidelines on the sustainability approach through 
which sustainable growth and diversification of the technology and feedstock portfolio of the SAF 
industry in the EU will be built. Feedstock development policies should stimulate a diverse and 
sustainable feedstock portfolio for SAF. The focus should be on waste  residues such as those 
found in Annex IX Part A.

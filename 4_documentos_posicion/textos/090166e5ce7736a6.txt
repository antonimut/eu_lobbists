Feedback to FuelEU Maritime – Green European Maritime Space 
 
The Port of Rotterdam welcomes the FuelEU Maritime – Green European Maritime Space initiative and 
would like to express its support for the forthcoming proposals to enhance the uptake of sustainable 
alternative fuels in maritime transport by creating a clear pathway for the demand of low and zero -
emissions sustainable alternative fuels and power.  
 
As Europe’s largest bunker port and the first port in Europe where ships were able to bunker Liquefied 
Natural Gas (LNG), we are determined to play an important role in ensuring the availability of climate 
friendly fuels in the future. In our port area, the production, trade, storage and transshipment of fuels 
come together. Next to five oil refineries with a combined distillation capacity of 58 million tonnes, several 
biofuel production facilities are located in the port area with room f or future growth to accommodate 
clean hydrogen production and the production of other sustainable shipping fuels.  
 
In order to deliver to our high ambitions to prevent climate change  and promote clean air in the direct 
port area, we actively stimulate the use of sustainable fuels in shipping. Next to discounts on port dues, 
we have set up an Incentive Scheme for Climate-Friendly Shipping to create demonstration projects 
in which new sustainable fuels are used in sea -going shipping. Until 2022, € 5 million is available for 
shippers, shipping companies, fuel producers and suppliers, manufacturers and vessel owners that want 
to experiment with climate friendly fuels in our port.  In addition we are looking for ways to initiate and 
scale up the production of new alternative sustainable fuels together with our land-based industry clients.  
 
The Commission rightfully acknowledges that, despite ongoing incentives, the current uptake of clean 
marine fuels remains low. In order to scale up the use of sustainable alternative fuels and power beyond 
pilot and demonstration phases, we agree that stimulation of demand is necessary . We moreover 
support the conclusion that pushing the supply side without addressing demand  will not automatically 
lead to the desired uptake of alternative fuels and power by the market. To fully achieve this, fiscal 
stimulation of alternative  energy and power  is necessary together with  accompanying regulatory 
measures to regulate the CO2 emissions of shipping and, where necessary, air pollution by ships.  
 
We are glad to see that the Commission is considering, amongst others, a goal based approach in its 
roadmap. In our view, the climate targets of the European Green deal should be leading in such an 
approach, accompanied by a clear pathway stating which emissions reductions are necessary by when. 
 
We would like to emphasize that most experts point to a future fuel mix based on a broad range of 
marine fuels and energy carriers and that we must prepare for a more complex world that requires a 
lot of flexibility, whilst at the same time we must ensure that sufficient certainty is provided for market 
players to invest. Ideally, a roadmap with applicable fuels/on shore energy supply for different 
shipping segment/ship types is developed by the Commission in cooperation with all stakeholders 
concerned. Such a sectoral roadmap should encompass the preferred fuels and on shore power 
solutions for that segment, supplemented by clear emission targets and the applicable timelines.  
 
In our view, policy instruments like these can improve the use of (new) alternative fuel infrastructure and 
the uptake of on shore energy supply in ports. At the same time cleaner fuels and onshore energy supply 
should be exempted from all energy taxation in order to create viable business cases. The transition 
towards the use of more sustainable fuels in shipping  further requires a massive amount of investment 
that cannot be achieved without stimulus from the public sector.

European financing instruments like the Connecting Europe Facility and Motorways of the Seas can 
help bridge financing gaps, although the  effects thus far seem  to be limited as synergies between 
(alternative fuel) infrastructure investment and the financing of new vessels  and ships that operate on 
new fuels or plug into clean on shore energy supply is lacking in current calls. We therefore call for 
further integration of alternative fuel infrastructure and ship financing and a supply chain approach in 
EU financing programmes order to foster the uptake of alternative fuels and power.  
 
The Port of Rotterdam furthermore welcomes the proposed revision of State aid guidelines  in order 
to deliver to the policy objectives of the European Green Deal and ensure a level -playing field in the 
internal market (especially for the deployment of on-shore power infrastructures).  
 
Last but not least, we support a broad scope of fuels in the initiative, including LNG . In our view, 
the market uptake of LNG (with a clear pathway to bio -LNG or carbon captured LNG should be further 
promoted in order to gain from momentum in the market and provide investment security to the market. 
An important condition of the new legislative framework for alternative fuels and power is that it should 
be consistent over time and provide long term certainty to investors. This condition is not met when LNG 
is excluded from the scope of the FuelEU Maritime initiative.  
 
As an active member of the European Sustainable Shipping Forum, we are committed to be actively 
involved in setting ambitious, but realistic and workable objectives  and discuss ex ante the impact of 
specific measures under the proposed policy options. In addition, we will continue to share our insights 
about the significant investments and associated risks that must be shared by many parties in the value 
chain, and which cannot be seen outside the context of the energy transition in our port industrial and 
petrochemical cluster.

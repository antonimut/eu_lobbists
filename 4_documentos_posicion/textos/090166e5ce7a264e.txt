     Wind Propulsion Technology Solutions Key Contribution for Decarbonising Commercial Shipping   Key Points  1. Wind energy is an abundant renewable, zero emissions energy source delivered free of charge to the point of use onboard the ship without any additional infrastructure.  2. Wind propulsion solutions are significant propulsion providers, not fuel efficiency measures and the cost of this energy is fixed at zero for the lifetime of the vessel.  3. Wind propulsion technologies (WPT) are available in the market today along with a significant pipeline of viable and certified systems in late R&D and pre-market stages.  4. Direct wind propulsion is still regularly excluded or not adequately incorporated into decarbonisation pathways due either to the lack of information or exclusive focus on longer-term alternative fuel options.   5. Wind propulsion systems can be installed as retrofits or on optimised new build vessels.  6. As retrofits, WPT’s can deliver between 5-20% fuel and associated emissions savings (with no change in vessel operational profiles), with the potential to reach 30%.  7. WPT systems integrated into optimised new builds offer substantially higher savings with designs that offer well above 50% savings. The primary wind propulsion vessels with auxiliary engines are options for various sizes and designs of vessels.  8. All fuel and emissions savings and calculated using motor vessel operational profiles, but savings can be optimised by using weather routing, speed changes etc. and there is potential for supplementary onboard zero carbon fuel generation adding further fuel autonomy.   9. The uptake of expensive alternative and low carbon fuels will be accelerated if the requirement for those fuels is reduced by up to 30% (or beyond) thus reducing the total amount of fuel required, but also reducing the storage space and potentially the installed or operational power requirement of the vessel.  10.  Wind Propulsion Technology Solutions Key Contribution for Decarbonising Commercial Shipping   Overview  The development of wind-assist and primary wind propulsion solutions has been gaining significant momentum in the shipping industry, especially over the last 18 months since the release of the IMO initial strategy on greenhouse gases in April 2018. We are seeing the involvement of shipping majors, such as MOL, Maersk Tankers, Louis Dreyfus Armateurs, K-lines, Oldendorff, Viking lines & Scandlines, Vale etc. along with major shipbuilders Chantiers de l’Atlantique, DCIS, Oshima Shipyards as well as the involvement of all of the major classification societies in both classifying technologies, but also their involvement in wind propulsion facilitation projects. Non-shipping players are also increasingly engaging with wind propulsion projects and technology providers and these include Renault group, Airbus, the European Space Agency, Drax to name a few.  The International Windship Association (IWSA) was established in 2014 to communicate and help facilitate the uptake of these technologies and has grown from 12 founding members to over 100, including technology providers and projects, shipowners, shipyards, research organisations etc., which in itself is also a clear indication of the growing momentum in the sector.    We are however concerned that wind propulsion, an increasingly credible, viable and immediately available zero-emissions technology, is not receiving the same level of consideration and promotion that other longer-term and more costly solutions, such as eco-fuels development, are receiving in the industry and among policy makers. This is compounded by a lack of access to concise and up-to-date information being made available to decision makers on this fast developing segment and that there are a number of clear regulatory and non-regulatory barriers that can be addressed to remove unnecessary restrictions on the uptake of this innovative and highly beneficial technology grouping.   Wind propulsion solutions offer a near-term opportunity for significant reductions in GHG emissions in line with the declared IMO initial strategy on GHG. Wind propulsion systems (sails, rotors, kites etc.) can deliver between 5-20% fuel and associated emissions savings as a retrofit option (with no change in vessel operational profiles), with the potential to reach 30%. Systems integrated into optimised new builds and those used for primary propulsion will offer substantially higher savings.  The use of primary renewable energy (wind) delivered directly to vessels, with: (i) zero-emissions or extremely low emissions in operation (ii) a stable zero (or extremely low) fuel cost for its contribution to the propulsion requirements for the operational life of the vessel and (iii) no need for additional infrastructure, is a critically important technology option for building a resilient, flexible and low carbon fleet. Moreover, wind propulsion technologies deliver a substantial reduction in the overall demand for more costly low carbon eco-fuels, potentially reducing storage space requirements for these alternative fuels or installed power requirements and extending the vessel range thus bringing increased flexibility for bunkering of these new fuels (reducing supply issues and potentially price).

  As noted, these wind technology solutions are increasingly available today, with an increasing number of demonstrator vessels already in commercial operation, sea trialling or in late stage R&D. These systems are fully automated and will be integrated into the energy management systems of the vessels. Wind propulsion can be deployed either as wind-assist for primarily motor vessels or as a primary propulsor for newly built vessels outfitted with auxiliary engines.  There are seven categories of wind propulsion technology: Rotors, Hard or Rigid Sails, Suction Wings, Kites, Soft Sails, Turbines and Hull Form. There are substantial technical variations within these categories with 30+ technology providers and developers at present across the categories. Discussion Points: Wind Propulsion – Innovative Technology & Primary Renewable  The installation of wind propulsion technologies reduces the overall demand for zero-emission Eco-fuels, which will be in demand from all other economic sectors and all research suggests, will be significantly higher cost than fossils, therefore negatively impacting the cost of shipping to consumer. Wind helps to mitigate these costs, the uncertainty and availability risks of eco-fuels.  The use of renewable energy to produce these fuels is an inefficient process (as noted in figure 1) and the industry should aim to use wind to provide thrust directly wherever possible to maximise cost and environmental efficiency.    The cost of the wind is only pegged to the cost of the technology itself and all innovation developments reduce in cost and improve in efficiency and we can state with certainty that the costs of maritime wind technology will follow the same cost reduction trajectory once the we have crossed the initial 'innovation hump'. Eco-fuels on the other hand will continue to be traded on commodity markets which are inherently volatile and in turn reduce operational cost certainty and negatively impacts the ability to strategically plan across the industry with any confidence.   IMO MEPC 75 – Wind Propulsion Information Paper Submission: The wind propulsion info paper submitted by the Comoros flag has been accepted as MEPC75 Inf.26 and is now available to all delegates and is also publicly available through IMO docs. The Executive Summary reads: “The decarbonization of shipping is the defining issue of the coming decade; however, currently, one of the leading decarbonization technologies, direct wind propulsion, is receiving only very limited consideration in this critical debate over the future of shipping. Direct thrust from wind propulsion technologies offers a technically and commercially viable near-term solution that can already save 5% to 20% of fuel and associated emissions as wind assistance, with the potential for much higher benefits as the technology develops or is deployed on optimized newbuild ships. Wind solutions are cost-effective, do not depend on alterations to port infrastructure and ensure shipowners have improved operational autonomy in mitigating the risks and uncertainties of being commercially dependent on the unknown cost and availability of alternative fuels. Therefore, the adoption of wind solutions will greatly assist the global fleet in reducing net emissions in the short-term, reducing the carbon intensity of the whole fleet, and better enable to meet IMO GHG reduction targets.”         NOTE: Further technical submissions will be made at subsequent IMO MEPC sessions.  Hybrid Solution: the lack of inclusion of wind propulsion solutions in EU, IMO and other policy platform deliberations and the explicit integration of WPT into decarbonisation strategies or simply their inclusion as add-on solutions rather than central and integrated into the approach would appear to stem from the view that these solutions function as an energy efficiency measure and not the significant propulsor that they are, but it also seems to stem partly from this lack of ‘fuel’ commodification which can reduce interest from bunker and investment interests. ClassNK (Sep 2019) : Guidelines Download from Class NK website DNV GL (Nov 2019): Guidelines Download PDF  Facilitating & Support Technologies & Projects: Along with the development of wind propulsion systems there has also been steady progress in the development of new testing procedures and performance criteria, energy management systems, weather routing software development, and further work is underway including new digital/satellite enabled wind data and routing systems etc.  Fleet Segments & Operations: Almost all segments of the world fleet can immediately benefit from installing wind propulsion technologies. Each segment will have its own challenges such as limited deck space on container vessels, loading/unloading requirements for geared bulkers, air draft restrictions for cruise vessels etc. Thus, each segment places requirements on the systems deployed and these are incorporated in the various designs available with wind propulsion technology developers working closely with the relevant stakeholders developing systems that are: retractable, auto-furling, hinged, modular, multi-positional, limited deck footprint etc.   Proven Savings: As stated earlier, the current fleet of large demonstrator vessels have provided confirmed savings data, averaged out over the full operating profile over periods of 12 months. Publicly available results already confirm the projected savings, an example with the rotor installations reporting 6.1% savings for two small retrofitted rotors on RoRo vessel in operation since 2014, 8.2% for two larger rotors installed on an LR2 product tanker and approx. 20% achieved with a relatively large single rotor installed on a small general cargo vessel. These results are scalable and will be further optimised in time. Other wind-assist technology category sea trials are reporting similarly expected results.     Additional Considerations: With increases in range, less restrictions around bunkering for fuel and lower operational costs afforded to vessel operators by wind propulsion installations, there is the opportunity to change the economic dynamics surrounding speed reductions or lowering engine power requirements at existing speeds. Also these dynamics alter on existing less economical routes and hold the potential to reopen previously  uneconomical ones, possibly relieving congestion, opening up additional trade opportunities, increase the volume of trade moved by the world fleet and critically enabling SIDS and other developing countries to provide far more economical and sustainable shipping services both domestically and internationally.    Increased resilience, reduction in fuel insecurity, the reduction of upstream & downstream emissions and the potential for modular or leasing options for installations of wind propulsion technologies are all important considerations. These highly visible technology choices will also be viewed as a very public statement of intent to decarbonise, telling a positive shipping story to a wider audience. Wind Propulsion Facilitation Projects & Organisations  There are several multi-stakeholder projects and organisations that are currently working on these issues and that will submit reports, generate third party validation and support facilitation activities and technologies.

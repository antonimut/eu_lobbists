Fuel EU Maritime Inception Impact Assessment – A.P. Moller-Maersk   
 
A.P. Moller-Maersk would like to thank the European Commission for the opportunity to 
comment on the Inception Impact Assessment (IIA) of the Fuel EU Maritime initiative.   
Generally, action in the field of alternative fuels and technologies is welcomed. Maersk 
has set a company efficiency target aiming for a 60% reduction in CO2 relative to cargo 
moved by 2030 and the ambitious target of net-zero emissions by 2050 
Time is short if shipping is to reach the necessary green transition and contribute to the 
goals of the Paris Agreement . Promoting alternative power for shipping can  therefore 
not be considered a long -term effort. In order to secure commercially viable carbon 
neutral shipping in this decade, action must be taken in the short term, as there is a 
time lag with design, ordering and building of vessels.  
At the same time, action must be carefully analyzed to ensure actual effect and avoid 
unintended implications. The IIA points to interdependencies with other EU initiatives.  
It is essential that new initiatives do not only seek to ‘fill the regulatory gap’ but that 
such initiatives will also be carefully al igned with existing EU regulation on renewable 
and alternative fuels. Moreover, action for alternative fuels should not be solely viewed 
from the perspective of the maritime sector in isolation. The real market for alternative 
fuels must be created in the energy sector. Initiatives to promote alternative fuels  in 
the maritime sector should therefore also aim to send a strong signal to the energy 
sector of significant future demand. There is then a technical and practical role for EU 
regulators to ensure tha t once such solutions are broadly developed, the European 
energy network and not least port infrastructures and processes are ready to support 
the uptake from maritime transport.  
 
I. General principles for action   
Maersk would like to emphasize some general principles, which should underpin any 
new initiative aimed at reducing GHG emissions from shipping.  
• First of all , the nature of shipping is global. Therefore, any initiative must be 
complementing the global approach from the International Mar itime 
Organization, IMO. 
• Secondly, the IIA states that the initiative is intended to be flag-neutral. This is 
an essential point for Maersk. If prescriptive measures were only applied to EU-
flagged ships this would result in unlevel playin g field by inducing competitive 
advantages for non-EU flagged ships and ultimately carbon leakage.

• Thirdly, it is essential to reward  first-movers. As such, any initiative should 
reward companies which have already now taken initiatives to reduce emissions. 
This includes securing stable investment frameworks and ensuring that all EU 
legislations are fully aligned so as not to create uncertainty for the substantial 
investments needed.   
 
II. Scope and impact  
While several elements of the IIA are described in very concrete terms, the actual scope 
is left largely undefined. It does not seem clear which emissions are to be targeted and 
on which basis, for instance emissions at port or sea, from fleet or vessel, by voyage or 
year etc. Further information is in this regard needed in order to have a more concrete 
discussion.  
As to the scope of the Impact Assessment , it should also be considered to assess the 
expected change in import/export costs to and from EU of possible new requirements 
for EU s hipping, including whether there may be impacts for EU companies and /or 
consumers.  
 
III. Deployment and supply of fuels  
Today, one of the main issues seems to be deployment, which impedes both market 
supply and demand in developing.  Moreover, the EU should aim at broadening the 
supply basis of alternative fuels . Today, there are only a very limited number of 
suppliers of for instance certified biofuels in the EU. But this can only be done if the EU 
also pushes for a strong enforcement on the quality of these fuels.  
R&D continues to be a prerequisite to promote carbon neutral shipping. In this regard 
the International Maritime Research & Development Board suggested by ICS, WSC et 
al. in the IMO’s GHG work provides a valuable solution where the shipping sector itself 
would contribute to the green transition.    
 
IV. IIA suggested policy options  
As to the suggested policy options , Maersk is looking forward to a further elaboration 
and dialogue with the European Commission. Maersk would like to emphasize  the 
following initial comments to the presented policy options, with a reservation as to the 
concrete composition of the options:   
• Possible support measures should be focused on deployment. For many years the 
EU has offered support funding for e.g. R&D on alternative fuels. This has 
undoubtedly been valuable but does not seem to have solve d the ‘Chicken and 
Egg’-issue. The initiative mentions target tax incentives to promote the use of

sustainable alternative fuels . This could be a way forward , but only onc e 
alternative fuels can actually be supplied and a gap between fossil and non-fossil 
is identified. The for use of subsidies or cross-subsidization should be considered 
in this regard. 
 
• Prescriptive requirements should be considered carefully based on a nu mber of 
criteria including cost -efficiency. Such initiative will be highly dependent on 
strong enforcement and should be accompanied with stringent requirements as 
to the documentation of the bunkered fuel mix. Prescriptive requirements should 
moreover be focused not on mandating the use of specific identified fuels but the 
results thereof. The energy sector will largely lead the transition of fuels and 
maritime fuels will be one part of that. It is unrealistic to expect that a solution 
covering solely the maritime sector will be developed.   
 
• Goal-based performance is also the focus for discussions in the IMO. As such, 
there is good basis for alignment between IMO and EU measures. However, a 
goal-based approach may be short-term solution to improve carbon intensity but 
does not seem enough to push deployment and ensure a market for alternative 
power.  
 
It is possible that a combination of the approaches is the appropriate  solution. Once 
again, further information from the European Commission on the content of the policy 
options would be welcomed.   
 
V. Emissions at berth  
The initiative also covers emissions at berth and shore side electricity is emphasized as 
a possible solution. Maersk fully supports reducing emissions at berth and in ports. 
However, focus should be on the goal, i.e. reducing emissions, rather than prescribing 
the means. There continues to be uncertainties with the supply of shore side electricity 
including the sustainability of the energy source, the investments needed and not least 
safety issues. Also, there are some practical questions to be solved including the fact 
that the electricity can only be connected an hour after arrival and an hour before 
departure, which reduces the potential for reductions. Shore side electricity may be a 
solution in the future , batteries may be another  or a third option  altogether. An 
approach to emissions at berth is welcomed but should be open to any future solutions. 
Finally, shore side electricity is not only one possible solution to address GHG reductions 
but as much (and perhaps more) a means to reduce other particles than CO2 and 
prevent local pollution. It should be clarified specifically what the desired outcome of 
looking at shore side electricity is, which problems the EU wishes to solve in this regard  
and why it is relevant in the scope of this initiative.

VI. Life cycle approach 
The IIA proposes a well -to-wake approach . Sustainability of fuels must be  clearly 
accounted for including in the Bunker Delivery Note and ship owners should be rewarded 
for taking decisions in this d irection. At the same time, a well -to-wake approach by 
nature includes other areas than only the maritime when looking at the production, 
distribution etc. of fuels. It is essential that the EU when considering well -to-wake in 
this regard ensures alignment with the approach of other areas. Work on LCAs is already 
going on in international fora and an aligned approach is needed. 
Finally, the IIA mention s that m arket-based-instruments, such as ETS, may help 
bridging the price gap between conventional and alt ernative fuels. It is important to 
note, that is uncertain whether a possible EU ETS will have this effect, since it is 
impossible today to estimate the competitiveness gap between renewable and fossil 
fuels. Moreover, as we see today such prices will fluctuate. An initiative which leads to 
regulation of fuel prices in the EU and aims to avoid advantages of bunkering outside 
the EU demands a very thorough impact assessment as shipping operates globally and 
there are legal and practical elements which are fundamental to respect. 
A.P. Moller -Maersk is available to answer any possible questions to the above and 
looking forward to continuing the dialogue with the European Commission.

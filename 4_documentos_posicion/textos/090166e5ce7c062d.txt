This is a response from the European Fluorocarbons Technical Committee (EFCTC) to the Inception 
Impact Assessment on the revision of Regulation No. 1005/2009 (Ref. Ares (2020)  1782671 - 
26/03/2020). 
 
EFCTC notes that the Commission evaluation of Regulation No. 1005/2009 carried out as part of the 
Regulatory Fitness and Performance programme (REFIT) in 2019 noted that “the Ozone Regulation is 
generally fit-for-purpose and is effective in achieving its objectives and that the main pote ntial for 
improving the Regulation is related to enhancing its efficiency, coherence and clarity by fine -tuning 
specific measures.” 
 
EFCTC notes that in this context, the future of the revision of Regulation No. 1005/2009 will have a 
very limited impact either on climate change or on the attainment of carbon neutrality by 2050 by the 
European Union.  
In fact, currently, the EU is prohibiting all uses of ODS subject to a number of exemptions, where ODS 
alternatives were not (yet) available in 2009. 
As such, we believe that, to ensure the appropriate targeting and use of limited resources both within 
the Commission, Member States, the European Parliament and the industry sector, it would be 
beneficial to give a higher priority to the review of Regulation No. 5 17/2014 on fluorinated gases as 
this will have a larger impact on future climate change through the control of hydrofluorocarbons 
(HFCs). The review of Regulation No. 517/2014 is planned for a similar time period. Improvements in 
implementing and in the en forcement of this regulation will assist the European Union in moving 
towards carbon neutrality, and also to control and prevent illegal activities, which currently undermine 
the environmental goals. 
Two possible options would be to either: 
• keep in place the current regulation No. 1005/2009 on ozone depleting substances until after 
the completion of the review of the F-gas Regulation No. 517/2014, or 
•  to consider the inclusion of the remaining elements of the ODS Regulation within the review 
of the F-gas Regulation.  
We expect that an expedient revision of the current F -Gas Regulation will have a substantial 
positive impact on the EU’s Green Deal goals , whereas the benefits of a revision of the ODS 
Regulation will be marginal at best. EFCTC therefore expresses a clear preference for the first 
option. 
It is important for industrial planning that the option chosen is made clear early in the process.

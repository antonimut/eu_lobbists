EDISON’S PRELIMINARY FEEDBACK ON THE INCEPTION IMPACT ASSESSMENT 
OF THE FUELEU MARITIME – GREEN EUROPEAN MARITIME SPACE INITIATIVE 
 
Edison welcomes the opportunity to share some preliminary views on the roadmap related to the initiative 
“FuelEU Maritime - Green European Maritime Space ” launched by the European Commission . We strongly 
believe this initiative will contribute to developing a comprehensive framework for a substantial uptake of 
sustainable alternative fuels in maritime transport.  
Many initiatives are being launched at international and European level to implement and support the 
development of sustainable alternative fuels and related logistic chains and infrastructure. The most recent 
initiatives launched under the International Maritime Organization (IMO) confirm the importance attached 
to the development of sustainable solutions for the maritime sector in the process of r eaching the goals of 
the Paris Agreement. For its part, t he Commission has strongly supported the development of sustainable 
alternative fuels through the Directive 2014/94/EU ("DAFI") on the deployment of alternative fuels 
infrastructure; but also, throug h initiatives such as the Blue Corridors , the Motorways of the Sea and 
through dedicated programs in support of investments under the Connecting Europe Facility (CEF Transport 
and CEF Blending).   
Supporting the goals of the Paris Agreement and supporting the Green Deal's ambition for climate neutrality 
in 2050, we hope that the Commission will take forward, as it has done so far, the deployment of all fuels 
capable of substantially reducing the impact of all emissions (SOx, PM, NOx and CO2) in the maritime sector. 
These sources include electricity, hydrogen, biofuels, natural gas (including biomethane) in gaseous (CNG) 
and liquefied (LNG) form. Giving lead to the most cost-effective ones can cater for an immediate contribution 
to a substantial im provement of the environmental profiles in the maritime transport, while others will 
require long term efforts before being implemented by all the players involved.  A stable framework for 
investments in this perspective remains crucial in our view.  
In thi s context of development of the sector, we hope that the Commission can  also preserve and give 
continuity to initiatives such as the Directive 2014/94/EU on the Deployment of Alternative Fuels 
Infrastructure (DAFI) as the uptake of alternative fuels  is a c rucial enabler for the decarbonization of the 
energy and transport sectors, only if supported by adequate infrastructures. 
Related to this, another driver which would help the growth of LNG as Maritime Fuel, is the harmonization 
in all EU of the procedures to enhance operators to start LNG bunkering as well as standard authorization 
procedures and operational criteria for LNG bunkering operations themselves. 
It will also be of paramount importance, in order to continue supporting the development of solutions able 
to reduce emissions in the maritime transport sector, that in the ongoing reform of Directive 2003/96/EC on 
the taxation of energy products a favorable framework for less polluting fuels is maintained, as in the case of 
natural gas also in the form of liquefied natural gas, including in the form of  the tax exemptions envisaged 
under the current Directive. 
Another important aspect would be the harmonization of the emissions criteria in sea areas. Accordingly, the 
implementation of the ECA (Emission Control Area) in the Mediterranean Sea would represent a crucial step 
forward in developing a comprehensive framework to supp ort the development of sustainable alternative 
fuels in maritime transport, considering that in Nordic & Baltic Sea ECA has already been implemented. 
The FuelEU Maritime initiative will play a key role in catalyzing synergies across all the initiatives put in place 
over the last  years. We therefore hope that it will be able to make a clear policy contribution to the 
development of sustainable alternative fuels, taking into account the important progress that the maritime 
sector has made in recent years, thanks also to the close cooperation with the players of the energy sector.

An adequate support to integrated logistic chains and infrastructures, including for ports and coastal deposits 
for alternative fuels and small-scale LNG projects, remain crucial in this regard.  
Last but not least, i t will be crucial in our view  to ensure a robust dialogue , based on information and 
experiences sharing,  with all the relevant stakeholders during the evaluation of the FuelEU  Maritime 
initiative, in particular with regard to the development of the related Impact Assessment and new proposals.

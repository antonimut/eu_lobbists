CLIA Europe feedback to the roadmap for the FuelEU Maritime Inception Impact 
Assessment 
 
CLIA Europe welcomes the intention of the ‘FuelEU Maritime’ initiative to increase the use and 
availability of sustainable alternative marine fuels and energy sources. The development of 
alternative technologies and fuels is a necessary enabler towards decarbonization of the 
shipping sector. Therefore, implementa tion of this initiative should be closely aligned with 
the other relevant processes of the EU Green Deal including the 2030 Climate Target Plan and 
the European Climate Law. 
 
CLIA Europe represents the cruise lines operating in Europe and is the European section of 
Cruise Lines International Association (CLIA). CLIA members represent 95% of the cruise 
industry. Our industry fully supports the long -term commitments of continuous ly reducing 
the carbon intensity and making progress toward a climate neutral cruise sector in line with 
the commitments at the IMO. These already agreed commitments and timelines are 
ambitious and challenging for our sector. 
 
We look forward to providing further input to the detailed impact assessments supporting 
any legislative proposals in 2021 and to receiving feedback from the Commission on how our 
input will be taken on board and, if applicable, why certain suggestions can't be taken up. This 
initiative, and a possible revision of Directive 2014/94/EU, should provide clear opportunities. 
 
Unprecedented consequences from COVID-19  
We would like to draw you r attention to the unprecedented consequences from COVID -19 
and the constraints this puts on the cruise sector. It would be wrong to assume that it will be 
business as usual soon and that there would be no effect on investments and innovation.  
Supporting the ambitions of the sector and expectations of society , the development and 
operationalization of maritime fuels require a strategy and investments in R&D. The global 
R&D fund as proposed by shipping industry could be a key enabler for innovative financing 
solutions. 
 
CLIA Commitment 
In this light, we would like to stress that o cean-going cruise line members of CLIA are 
committed1 to reducing carbon intensity by 40% across the cruise industry by 2030 . This 
directly supports international industry-wide goals and closely aligns with both the IMO Initial 
Strategy for GHG Reduction  and the EU MRV. However, this will require both considerable 
investments and cooperation among segments of the maritime industry. The EU could 
provide a very positive contribution facilitating this process while acknowledging our goals 
and the characteristics of specific segments of the shipping sector. 
  
Importantly, we would like to call on the EU to support these CLIA commitments and the vast 
technological innovations and operational developments required to reach these shared

Problem the initiative aims to tackle  
Regarding the problems this initiative aims to tackle and specifically the lack of predictability 
and high risk of investment choices and technological aspects and price factors, we would like 
to share the following: 
 
Cruise ships have a very long life cycle , going beyond 30 years , and this should not be 
compromised. Investments are being made now, incorporating the best available technology 
in ships that will still be  operational in 2050 and beyond . Realistically, enabling them to 
continue operating with existing infrastructure, and to see out their longevity, these ships will 
need to rely on drop-in alternative fuels to decarbonize. At the same time, these ships operate 
globally and therefore measures and fuels to support this reduction need to be implemented 
globally as well.  
 
The Inception Impact Assessment recognizes that the number of sustainable alternative fuels 
and technologies available is extremely limited.  Currently, no climate neutral fuel is 
sufficiently mature or commercially available at scale to be applied in the international 
maritime sector while pilot testing is only now starting. A significant amount of funding is 
required for research, development and application of innovations and solutions in the short 
and medium term.  
 
We advocate access to funding mechanisms and for policy support to enabl e 
commercialization and align the industry to the benefits currently afforded to other forms of 
transport and ground-based industry. This concerns the total value chain and maritime sector, 
including its suppliers and other partners to ensure a safe, resil ient and reliable supply chain 
of cleaner fuels. The priority to develop these innovations. 
 
New technologies and fuels need to become available globally as quickly as possible . The 
shipping and maritime sector should be incentivized to make the transition  to these new 
technologies and fuels as soon as they become globally available , recognizing that drop-in 
fuel solutions should be a priority for existing fleet. It must be stressed that wider efforts from 
economic sectors well beyond the maritime industry are crucial for the development, 
production and proliferation of alternative fuels from renewable sources. 
 
Significantly, the international associations representing the shipping sector recently 
submitted a joint proposal to IMO to establish a US $ 5 billion maritime R&D fund financed by 
the shipping sector which we look forward to further presentation and discussion with IMO 
and EU Member States. We expect this proposal to play an important role in supporting 
innovation, even during and in the aftermath o f the current crisis, and therefore should be 
supported by the EU.

At berth, shore-side electricity is one of many solutions to reduce local CO2 and air-emissions.  
Although not widely available to meet the energy demand for cruise ships, shore power has 
the major advantage of being mature and, with further research, could play an exciting dual-
role of charging battery -assisted vessels. The technology is, however, only as clean as the 
energy consumed.  
 
CLIA Ocean Going Cr uise Line Members are already commitment to implementing 2 
alternative fuels and continue to invest in new fuel technologies as well as innovation: 
• 44% of ne w capacity of cruise ships are committed to rely on LNG for primary 
propulsion, with the goal of a transition toward  net-zero GHG emissions utilizing 
liquefied bio-methane or synthetic natural gas as fully compatible alternatives when 
they become available at scale 
• 30% of global capacity are fitted to operate on shore power (up 10% over 2018) while 
88% of new ships will be fitted with shore power systems or configured to add shore 
power in the future 
  
Unfortunately, in Europe, cruise ships can only use shore power in two ports; much fewer 
than th e 30 available in TEN -T core and comprehensive ports  as mentioned . This list is 
dominated by cargo-operations and not in line with the list of largest European passenger and 
cruise ports and most importantly, specific berths. 
 
International effort 
The global shipping industry has  submitted a proposal to the IMO to form the world’s first 
collaborative shipping R&D programme to support the deployment of low -carbon and zero-
carbon fuels, energy sources, propulsion systems and other new technologies necessary to 
reduce the sector’s GH G emissions. This proposal 3, based on a financial contribution per 
tonne of marine fuel purchased for consumption by shipping companies worldwide, is 
expected to generate about USD 5 billion in funding over a 10 -year period. It is a practical 
solution tow ards ensuring that new fuels and technology are available in time to reduce 
shipping emissions within the ambitious timeframe agreed via the IMO.  
 
This timeframe committed by the Member States to the IMO aims to reduce the total annual 
GHG emissions from ships by at least 50% by 2050 whilst pursuing efforts towards phasing 
them out completely during this century. This objective will not be possible without the rapid 
deployment of zero -carbon technologies a nd propulsion systems, including sustainable

Dependency on land-based infrastructure for low-carbon fuels 
While shipowners would invest in new or retrofitted vessels and on-board equipment, these 
efforts are reliant on external investments in land -based infrastructure and production 
facilities for low-carbon fuels4. We therefore welcome efforts to stimulate the production of 
new fuels (such as Bio and Synthetic Methane, Ammonia, H2)  on a larger scale and reduce 
the price gap with current technologies, as recognized in this inception impact assessment. 
Moreover, it is very positive that the Green Deal Communication 5 of December 201 9 has 
recognized the need to develop commercial applications of breakthrough technologies in 
Europe, also in the shipping sector.  
 
To support the cost-competitiveness of new fuels and energy sources, the European shipping 
industry also supports a taxation exemption for all energy carriers to ships (i.e. fuels and 
electricity) in order to close the cost gap between conventional marine fuels and alternative 
fuels and electricity6.  
 
Opportunity for the EU 
Almost all cruise ships are built in European yards and have European technologies on board. 
The EU has an opportunity to serve as test and demonstration centers for the deployment of 
new fuel types and technologies and support a critical mass of existing solutions. All with a 
view to be up scaled to a global level. This will have the potential to provide the full breadth 
of the EU maritime sector a competitive ad vantage in new green technologies, creating 
opportunities for jobs and growth and providing a first-mover advantage. 
 
Technology neutral and goal-based 
It could be said that the maritime sector is at cross-roads. There is no certainty on which 
specific fuels or technology represent the future for the industry and it is likely that different 
shipping trades will require different technologies . We encourage the forthcoming proposal 
to be subject to an impact assessment and be technology neutral to leave open the possibility 
of emission-reduction technologies that are not even yet known.  
 
We therefore welcome goal-based objectives with non-prescriptive requirements on the type 
of fuel or technology used.

investment choices as barriers towards shipowner deployment of new technologies. A

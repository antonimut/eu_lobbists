Contribution from FuelsEurope 
to the Inception Impact Assessment on the development  
of post-Euro 6/VI emission standards for cars, vans, lorries and buses 
  
FuelsEurope appreciates the EU’s objectives to improve the standards governing road vehicle emissions 
in the EU .  This initiative provides an opportunity to simplify the standards, take advantage of new 
emissions control technologies  and remove any ambiguities around emission control applying in all  
circumstances. Affordable mobility is a key contributor to the quality of life of European citizens and is an 
important parameter of a healthy economy: in proposing new limits for pollutants, the Commission should 
carefully look at their impacts on air quality while assessing their social and economic impacts. 
 
1. Emissions levels of currently regulated air pollutants: 
 
Those levels shall not only be achievable though the use of the best available technologies, in accordance 
with the CLOVE consortium work in the frame of the AGVES, but shall lead to a measurable improvement 
of the air quality: 
 
 Evaluate the effect of the current fleet turnover on air quality, and assess how (and if) more stringent 
emissions standards would improve air quality in the next 10 years. In other words, the idea is to shift 
from “zero emissions” to “zero impact emissions” approach. This moves the focus from tailpipe levels 
to the final goal of how they impact the air quality. This leads to a more scientifically based approach 
where internal combustion engines achieving very low emission levels are similarly good for air quality 
as Battery Electric Vehicles , as Concawe studies have shown  (Concawe report 8/18 ). Those 
conclusions may be extrapolated to Hybrid Electric Vehicles (HEV) or Plug-in HEV. 
 Simulation studies show that more stringent standards on NOx and PM e missions are predicted to 
have either no, or a minor effect on air quality in terms of NO2, PM10 and PM2.5 compliance in 2030 
due to the current turnover of Euro 6/VI vehicles (Concawe report 8/18).  
 Recent advances in PM treatment techniques, such as the rollout of the Diesel Particulate Filter from 
the Euro 5 /V standard onwards have proven to significantly reduce the relative contribution of 
vehicles to PM10 and PM2.5 concentrations. In addition, “Re al time analyses” have shown that a 
significant drop in road transport, as a result of the spread of Covid -19, does not seem to lead to a 
consistent reduction of PM concentrations in ambient air (see: EEA report on air quality and COVID -
19 ) 
 Similarly, due to the natural turnover, Euro 4 /IV, Euro 5 /V and Euro 6b diesel passenger cars will 
progressively be replaced by Euro 6d/VI cars/trucks and buses, which is expected to lead to very low 
contribution to NO2 levels in 2030 (Concawe report 8/18)  
 
2. Currently non-regulated air pollutants:  
 
The assessment should p rioritise the pollutants that have an impact on health and where solutions to 
improve the air quality are technically and economically available. 
 
Measurement uncertainties must be taken into account to avoid setting any limit values below the 
detection threshold or the experimental confidence interval (in accordance with the CLOVE consortium 
work in the frame of the AGVES)

Fuels parameters impacts on emissions:  
 
 Recent studies show the diesel fuel parameters have no to minor impact on the tailpipe pollutants 
emissions of modern Diesel vehicles (Concawe Report 2/19, Concawe Report 5/20, SAE paper 20FFL-
0213 (submitted)). This is due to the very high efficiency of modern after treatment  systems, 
efficiently converting NOx, CO and HC while filtering close to 100% of the engine-out particles. 
 Similarly, gasoline parameters have no to minor impact on the tailpipe NOx, CO and HC emissions of 
modern gasoline vehicles. However, it is recognize d that they may have an impact on particulate 
number emissions, although the parameters responsible for this are  not fully identified and 
understood. The latter is still an active field of research, and we are of the opinion that this should be 
discussed in the frame of the FQD definition or the CEN fuel standardization activities. 
 A benefit of reference fuels is to give a level playing field for vehicles homologation. While pointing 
out these benefits of reference fuels, certification emissions should be a chievable in all real world 
scenarios, thus with any fuel meeting the specifications. 
 
 
3. CO2 emissions:  
 
 Renewable liquid fuels reduce by at least 50% the Well-to-Wheel CO2 emissions compared to a fossil 
fuel baseline, and this reduction can reach more than 90% if produced with the right feedstock and 
the right process. Hence, once the supply is ensured, renewable liquid fuels are able to 
instantaneously provide the whole existing fleet with CO2 abatement, without having to wait for a 
long fleet turnover. For this reason, renewable liquid fuels are among the fastest solutions to reduced 
CO2 emissions. 
 Taking into account what is written above, renewable liquid fuels, especially when used on hybridized 
powertrains, provide a good solution to comply both with air quality requirements and with climate 
neutrality. 
 
4. Non-CO2 greenhouse gas emissions:  
 
 Non-CO2 greenhouse gases emissions should be regulated together with CO2. As an example, this is 
the approach followed in the USA with N2O and CH4 emissions.

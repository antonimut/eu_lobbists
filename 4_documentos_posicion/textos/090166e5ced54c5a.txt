 
Feedback to the AFID Inception Impact Assessment
 
 
May 2020
 
Fastned’s mission is to accelerate the transition to electric mobility, by giving freedom to electric
 
drivers. Our goal is to develop and operate a European network of fast chargers for EVs along high
 
traffic corridors. The main barriers we face with the roll-out of our network have to do with
 
regulations and policies. A proper revision of the AFID is therefore crucial for the transition to
 
e-mobility.
 
 
The first and most important barrier in this regard is that the most suitable locations for fast
 
chargers (on highways and high-traffic corridors) do often not have an allocation or tender process in
 
place to make land available for fast charging. Furthermore, in several Member States, the
 
government only allows existing petrol stations to place fast chargers on these highway and
 
high-traffic corridors, which means it is very hard or not possible for new and innovative players to
 
enter the market. We therefore urge the European Commission to include the following updates in
 
the revision of the AFID, with the purpose to create an accessible and transparent market, and a
 
level playing field:
 
 
●
Site-allocation strategy. 
​
Thus far the AFID has failed to ensure adequate availability of fast
 
charging stations on locations where consumers need it most: highways and high-traffic
 
corridors. Member States should be instructed to come up with a site-allocation strategy for fast
 
charging along highways and main traffic corridors (this is a main barrier at the moment to the
 
roll-out of fast charging stations). This is needed to ensure sufficient fast charging capacity is
 
available in convenient locations for the consumer.
 
 
●
Transparent permit allocation process and access for new players.
​
 The process to gain a permit
 
on these locations should be open, transparent, and designed in such a way that new players
 
(with no petrol experience) have a fair chance to enter this market. Today this is often not the
 
case. A tender process should be organized for fast charging separate to other fuels.
 
●
Do not require petrol stations to build alternative fuel infrastructure.
​
 It is truly detrimental to
 
the market if petrol stations are appointed or required to provide alternative fuel infrastructure.
 
If the AFID will be turned into a regulation, this is even more important. It is seemingly an
 
efficient way to accelerate the roll-out of fast charging infrastructure but this measure makes the
 
transition to electric mobility dependent on incumbant petrol stations. This approach would be
 
comparable with a situation where it is only possible to build a wind turbine on the site and in
 
sub-contract with a coal power plant. This would undermine the business case and level playing
 
field of the wind turbine. Instead, an open and transparent process should be organized so that
 
new players can also access the market (see points above).
 
 
 
Furthermore, the following issues should also be considered.
 
 
●
Differentiate between different types of charging. 
​
In the directions provided by the AFID
 
differentiation should be made regarding public slow and public fast charging points, wherever
 
possible. These types of charging serve an entirely different purpose, have a different business

case, and therefore each require a different approach as to the implementation and all aspects
 
considered in the AFID.
 
 
●
Ensure non-discriminatory and transparent pricing.
​
 EV infrastructure companies which benefit
 
from subsidies/public money and/or occupy land allocated to them by the government or a
 
public authority (e.g. rest areas or refueling areas next to highways) should not be allowed to
 
offer exclusive services, or a reduced tariff to EV drivers of specific OEMs only (or their related
 
charge cards). This is currently the case with at least one large EV infrastructure consortium. We
 
think this is not only unethical and it should not be supported with public funds/measures, it also
 
leads to unfair competition and a distortion of the charging market.
 
●
LNG/CNG and any other fossil fuels should be excluded from the AFID.
​
 The environmental
 
benefits of these fuels are increasingly debunked (some studies even show they are negative).
 
Investing in this fossil fuel infrastructure is not in line with climate goals and leads to lock-in
 
effects. It is unacceptable to spend public funds towards this end or to require governments to
 
support this anno 2020. These fuels should therefore not be promoted by the AFID in any way, it
 
would take away the credibility of what AFID stands for: paving the way to a sustainable mobility
 
sector.

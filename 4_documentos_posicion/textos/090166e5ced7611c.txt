Position on the European Commission’s Revision of the Directive 2014/94/EU on the 
Deployment of Alternative Fuel Infrastructure 
 
Decarbonizing the EU economy is a pivotal and urgent challenge that all sectors and market actors need 
to respond to. Especially in the transport sector,  significant emissions reduction efforts are required. 
EnBW Energie Baden-Württemberg AG fully supports the 2050 decarbonization target  of the European 
Union and it  is in  this context , that we endorse the European Commission’s revision of Directive 
2014/94/EU on the Deployment of Alternative Fuel Infrastructure to expand the EU’s network for 
recharging and refuelling stations for alternative vehicle fuels , to increase interoperability and to 
encourage the use of low- and zero-emission vehicles.  
EnBW is one of the largest energy supply companies in Germany and Europe with a workforce of 
approximately 21,000 employees and 5.5 million customers. We have been at the forefr ont of the 
transition to electric mobility for several years already so that today EnBW is the largest operator of fast-
charging stations across Germany. Additionally, the EnBW mobility+ app provides customers access to 
more than 40,000 charging points. We have made extensive investments in electric mobility and have a 
clear understanding of the market needs and customer demands. 
To sustainably respond to the development of the transition to electric mobility and to successfully 
contribute to the aims of the European Green Deal, EnBW suggest s the revision of the Directive to 
highlight the following aspects: 
1. Free choice of customers regarding the authorization and payment method used at the recharging 
station to enable seamless charging all over Europe. 
Customers shall be able to freely choose the way in which they authorise and pay at the charging station. 
Moreover, direct payment through ad hoc charging shall be possible at every charging station in Europe 
in an easy and simple manner , whilst there shall be  no obligation for credit or debit card terminals or 
the use of Plug & Charge through ISO 15118. Digital payment means based on a deposited credit or debit 
card shall be considered as an equal method of payment. Additional security measures for RFID cards 
may also be achieved using coupling of charging cards to e-mobility provider (EMP) apps. 
2. We believe in a competitive market for e-mobility and a market-based price formation mechanism. 
Prices are set in a competitive market environment. Therefore, there is no  need for any retail price 
regulation related to recharging tariffs  in the current premature market . However, price transparency 
and information are essential for the customer before charging so that this information shall be 
displayed through digital means to the customer (via the mobility app or on the charging station display). 
3. Standardisation must be accompanied by rules for a level playing field and fair competition that 
guarantee the free choice of customers and prevent anti-competitive behaviour. 
ISO 15118 is an important enabler for e -mobility. However, customers shall not be restricted in the use 
/ non-use of ISO 15118 or the choice of their EMP and must be able to choose the preferred EMP before 
each charging session . As the version ISO 15118 -2 Ed.1 only allows one charging contract for Plug & 
Charge per vehicle, it is potentially highly damaging to market competition. The application of this version 
shall therefore not become mandatory. The installation and regular up date of charging contracts must 
in principle be possible for all EMP s without delay at transparent and fair conditions in all vehicles. The 
public key infrastructure (PKI) should be operated by a neutral entity and ensure cross -border 
interoperability. Market participants - especially those with more than one market role - shall neither 
establish closed corporate ecosystems nor restrict competition in the market.

4. CPOs shall continue to grant EMPs fair  and adequate  access to their recharging infrastructure 
based on fair tariff negotiations and mutual agreements. 
It shall be ensured that EMPs have fair and adequate  access to charging stations of charge point 
operators (CPO). Access should be organised within the fram ework of competition rules  to the extent 
that access is based on bilateral negotiations between t he market players and permitting differentiated 
pricing models of CPOs for EMPs . Roaming platforms provide a good opportunity to connect EMPs and 
CPOs Europe-wide. 
5. Static and basic dynamic data of charging stations and vehicle batteries shall be easily available 
for customers. Customers shall freely decide on the use and transmission of the vehicle data. 
With the consent of the user, charging related and other relevant data and functionality from the electric 
vehicle must be shared and made available to other market participants (e.g. charging power during 
charging process, state of charge of the battery, thermal preconditioning) to the extent that vehicles must 
support the transmission of as much data as possible. Minimum data security standards need to be 
established. 
CPOs own the data of their char ging stations. Static data and basic dynamic data (vacant, occupied, in / 
out of operation etc.) shall be freely accessible to customers and other market players. Premium 
dynamic data (quality, charging history etc.) and basic dynamic data related to indiv idual charging 
sessions shall be marketable by CPOs/EMPs to other market players through bilateral negotiation. 
Lastly, there shall be no state-run platform(s) for data transmission as private platforms will develop in 
a competitive environment. 
6. Intelligent bundled European -wide, national and regional funding schemes based on long term 
targets and accelerated administrative processes advance the dissemination of recharging 
infrastructure – rather than mandatory targets. 
Mandatory national targets for chargi ng infrastructure and a fixed electric vehicle to charging station 
ratio lead to an inaccurate allocation of capital. Rather, dissemination targets for charging infrastructure 
shall be reasonably linked with dissemination targets of BEV and PHEV and the deployment of non-public 
charging infrastructure. Aside from competitive market analysis, scientific tools may help to implement 
a Union-wide charging infrastructure. 
7. Whilst customer-friendly charging provides a competitive advantage for CPOs, there is a need for 
regulatory requirements regarding technical standards. 
Customers need to be able to locate charging stations easily (web, app, traffic signs) and have 
transparency of the status of charging stations (vacant, occupied, in / out of operation etc.) b efore 
choosing a charging station. Additionally, a clear description of the charging process needs to be 
displayed at the charging station. Whilst minimum requirements for technical standards, such as the 
socket location on the car and a recommended cable length, are needed, minimum requirements for the 
quality of charging (SLA, availability ratio etc.) are not feasible since, in competition, every CPO will aim 
for excellent quality to attract customers and EMPs.

Position on the Negative Effects of Implementing the ISO 15118 Standard 
1 Background 
ISO 15118 “Road vehicles – Vehicle to grid communication interface” is an international standard defining 
the communication, identification, authentication and authorisation between electric vehicles and the 
charging infrastructure for energy management and Plug & Charge. This standard will be supported by 
almost all new electric vehicles from 2020 onwards. Consequently, a rapid and almost complete market 
penetration can be expected in the next years. Communication based on ISO 15118 will replace DIN SPEC 
70121, the current de facto standard for DC charging in Europe. 
Currently, the standard is being discussed  often in the contexts of "V2G Communication" and “Plug & 
Charge”, whereby the latter concerns the automated authentication and authorization of charging 
sessions and billing thereof without other identification means, such as an additional RFID card or e-
mobility charging app. Furthermore, the implementation of the standard offers improved possibilities for 
energy management between the electric vehicle and the charging infrastructures, and as such also with 
customer or home energy management systems. 
Particularly due to smart charging through the improved energy management, the standard is an 
important factor for both the market ramp -up of e -mobility as well as  for the sector coupling in the 
transition of energy and transport systems. As a result, ISO 15118 is an important enabler for e-mobility 
at large and not a premium functionality. 
However, the way the standard is being implemented by the electric vehicle manufacturers (OEMs) may 
lead to undesirable market distortion and anti -competitive effects in the coming years , resulting in  
negative effects on the market ramp-up of e-mobility. Therefore, a cross-industry discussion with policy-
making and regulatory bodies is required immediately to counteract impending market dis tortions and 
anti-competitive effects already at an early stage. 
2 Assessment 
The ISO 15118 standard defines the technical implementation of the communication between the electric 
vehicle and the charging station . However,  it allows for variations in the realised functionality of the 
specific implementation. It is precisely this latitude that has a decisive influence on the upstream and 
downstream business models of the various market players, the available choices for the users and, 
consequently, the competition in the e-mobility market. 
a) Restriction of the customer’s freedom of choice and distortion of competition through pre -
selection of an e-mobility service provider (EMSP) by the vehicle manufacturer (OEM)  
A crucial aspect of the implementation of Plug & Charge through ISO 15118 is that charging contracts 
can be installed  directly in the e lectric vehicles as digital certificates and automatically be used to 
authorise charging processes . Critically, t he current version of ISO 15118 -2 Ed.1 only allows for one 
charging contract to be installed in the vehicle and therefore only a single e-mobility service provider 
(EMSP) per vehicle. The EMSP, whose charging contract is installed in the vehicle is thus au tomatically 
the only choice of the vehicle user. Furthermore, this installation could happen already during the 
production process or upon delivery of the vehicles at the dealership. Both the restriction to one charging 
contract and the pre-installation of the charging contract and thus pre-selection of an EMSP by the OEM 
represent a step backward s compared to the status quo, where every customer can use different 
charging contracts, e.g. by using multiple charging cards or mobility apps, easily, at any time and without 
complications. 
b) Market separation through the establishment of closed ecosystems by the OEMs 
Many OEMs adopt – mainly through their own subsidiaries, joint ventures or exclusive agreements and 
cooperation – (in)directly the market roles of charge point operator s (CPO/CSO) and EMSP s. They can

thus control several central market roles along the value chain aiming at vertical integration. In addition, 
the vehicle manufacturers are already working intensively on making their own back -end systems 
connected to the telematics link of the e -vehicles increasingly indispensable for the vehicle users, thus 
creating closed ec osystems around the vehicles (e.g. Mercedes me Charge, MyPorsche and Porsche 
Charging Service, Audi myAudi and e-tron Charging Service). Furthermore, additional bundled products 
offered by the OEM during the vehicle sale, e.g. the temporary exemption from the annual subscription 
fee for the charging service, reinforce the use of their services and subsequently the associated long -
term “lock-in” effects for customers. As a result, customer access for other market players, especially 
for competing mobility service providers, is severely hindered from the outset.  
c) Unclear application of public key infrastructures (PKI) at the expense of competition  
The public key infrastructure (PKI) is a digital certificate infrastructure and forms the technical 
foundation of the standard ISO 15118. The PKI, or several PKIs, provides the means to use encryption 
and digital certificates to enable secure communication between the e -vehicle and the charging station 
as well as the generation of charging contracts for the vehicles. The implementation of the PKI has not 
yet been clarified concerning their number, specific operators  and governance, non -discriminatory 
access for all market participants and cross-border interoperability. There is currently no obligation for 
OEMs to make the ir selected PKI open and interoperable. Furthermore, the OEMs are not obliged to 
provide the vehicle provisioning certificates , which are necessary to generate the charging certificates. 
Both factors lead to a fragmentation of the European market and a situation in which EMSPs other than 
those associated to OEMs simply cannot issue charging certificates. 
3 Core Demands 
Currently, there are no legal requirements for vehicle manufacturers regarding the implementation of 
ISO 15118 that prevent them from exploiting their own position of power vis -à-vis other market 
participants. Since numerous vehicle models with ISO 15118 are scheduled to  be intr oduced to the 
market in 2020, proactive measures must now be taken. Therefore, we demand: 
1. Customers shall not be restricted in the use / non-use of ISO 15118 or the choice of their e-mobility 
service provider. 
For each charging operation, a customer must be able to choose simply and directly whether to use 
Plug & Charge or another method of authentication and billing. Furthermore, the customer's 
freedom of choice when choosing an e -mobility service provider for a Plug & Charge charging 
contract in the vehicle shall not be restricted by unnecessary technical restrictions and requirements 
imposed by the vehicle manufacturers or by the establishment of closed ecosystems. It must be 
ensured that during the vehicle purchase, a customer is made aware of the offers of other e-mobility 
service providers without discrimination and is informed about his right to choose the mobility 
service provider without restriction. 
2. The version ISO 15118 -2 Ed.1 only allows one charging contract for Plug & Charge per vehicle and 
is therefore potentially highly damaging to market competition. The application of this version shall 
therefore not become compulsory. 
The restriction to a single charging certificate in the vehicle represents a significant step backwards 
compared to the current situation. The customer must continue to be able to choose and change 
between different e -mobility service providers easily and at any time,  including for Plug & Charge.  
Furthermore, the customer shall not be limited by a technical standard to only one charging contract 
of a certain mobility service provider pre-set by the OEM. The future version of ISO 15118-20 provides 
the technical possibility of multiple charging contracts in the vehicle and therefore offers a necessary 
basis for fair competition. Currently i t is assumed, but not verified, that the same applies to the 
revision of ISO 15118-2 as Ed.2. Since the first vehicles in which the anti-competitive ISO 15118-2 Ed. 
1 is implemented are already introduced to the market this year, vehicle manufacturers must be

obliged to offer appropriate solutions that allow  for the implementation of several cha rging 
certificates by other market participants in the electric vehicle. 
3. Market participants - especially those with more than one market role - shall neither establish 
closed corporate ecosystems nor restrict competition in the market.  
The key element for a successful energy transition in individual mobility is a functioning market 
competition of the best services and customer offers. ISO 15118 provides multiple opportunities for 
an implementation, which is potentially damaging to the market and restricts the freedom of choice 
for customers. Such market separation by individual market participants must therefore be 
prevented by appropriate legal measures. 
4. The installation and regular update of charging contracts must in principle be possi ble for all e -
mobility service providers at transparent and fair conditions in all vehicles.  
The installation of charging contracts for Plug & Charge and their regular update shall neither be 
prevented nor restricted by the vehicle manufacturers. The charg ing contracts must be installed in 
the vehicle in a customer -friendly, simple and immediate manner. It must be possible for every e -
mobility service provider to issue Plug & Charge charging contracts for every e lectric vehicle in 
Europe and to have them installed without delay at transparent and fair conditions. This should 
possibly be monitored by an independent organisation at European level.  
5. With the consent of the user, charging related and other relevant data and functionality from the 
electric vehicle must be shared and made available to other market participants.  
The data sovereignty lies with the customer. The customer decides with whom and to what extent 
the data is to be shared. Data required for processes relevant to the energy industry, intelligent 
management of charging or the development of innovative services and offers must be made 
available to all market participants. There shall be no pre -selection, restriction or manipulation of 
data by the vehicle manufacturer. In addition, charging -related functionalities, such as the thermal 
preconditioning of the battery, must be directly accessible to customers without navigation via the 
on-board unit, which may lead to a preferential treatment of certain charging stations. 
6. The public key infrastructure (PKI) should be operated by a neutral entity and ensure cross -border 
interoperability. 
Proprietary and closed public key infrastructures (PKIs) of individual market participants must be 
prevented. If there is more than one PKI, it is imperative that these are interoperable. The PKIs 
should be operated as sensitive and critical infr astructure by a neutral and independent authority, 
thus ensuring non -discriminatory access for all e-mobility service providers and cross -border 
interoperability – at least within Europe.

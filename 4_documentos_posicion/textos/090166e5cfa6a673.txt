 
Triodos proposal amending annex ii MiFID – May 2020 
Introduction to proposed amendments 
Semi-professionals that wish to opt-up, in situations where the enhanced retail-investor protection rules 
are overbearing and not necessary for them, are confronted with varying Private Placement regimes (PP) 
across the EU and hampered by admin burdens to an extent where they may choose not to invest, 
especially across borders. Clarifying MiFID Annex II would solve this barrier and facilitate semi-profs 
wishing to be regarded and invest as a professional investor for defined causes.   
In all PP regimes across the EU, the same elements can be found, namely those listed in art 25.2 MiFID II 
(2014/65/EU), but enriched with factors and thresholds that are similar but varying.  
The current MiFID Annex II lists the same factors but without clear thresholds and as such without legal 
certainty to investment firms or clients with respect to the adequacy of their own assessment. This is 
currently hampering parties from opting up, also in situations where opting up would be an excellent way 
forward for all involved.  
The legal uncertainty regards the scope of the opt-up, the appropriateness of risk profiles and the 
assessment of fitness. Both investment firms and clients could be considered wrong and thus liable.  
 
To overcome this legal uncertainty, Annex II must be more specific about the elements of the assessment, 
and possibly cater for certain very common situations that occur across member states. These are:  
- family offices, wealthy individuals with  
- more than adequate funds and  
- excellent knowledge and experience,  
- investing part of their funds in portfolio or financial instruments in which their maximum risk is 
loss of their investment. 
 
The attached suggested amendment builds on Art 25.2 MiFID II, which relates to servicing a (retail) client 
for the first time, when this assessment has to be made for any non-professional investor. This obligatory 
assessment can thus also be used for a discussion on ‘opting up’ by and with the client, if that would be 
appropriate. This would save time and costs by both investment firm and clients, while it would ensure 
that the client-base systems that investment firms use also will store this type of information.  
ANNEX II – MiFID II (2014/65/EU) 
ANNEX II PROFESSIONAL CLIENTS FOR THE PURPOSE OF THIS DIRECTIVE  
Professional client is a client who possesses the experience, knowledge and expertise to make its own 
investment decisions and properly assess the risks that it incurs. In order to be considered to be 
professional client, the client must comply with the following criteria:  
I. CATEGORIES OF CLIENT WHO ARE CONSIDERED TO BE PROFESSIONALS  
(1) Entities which are required to be authorised or regulated to operate in the financial markets. The list 
below shall be understood as including all authorised entities carrying out the characteristic activities of 
the entities mentioned: entities authorised by a Member State under a Directive, entities authorised or 
regulated by a Member State without reference to a Directive, and entities authorised or regulated by a 
third country:  
(a) Credit institutions;  
(b) Investment firms; 
(c) Other authorised or regulated financial institutions;

(d) Insurance companies;  
(e) Collective investment schemes and management companies of such schemes;  
(f) Pension funds and management companies of such funds;  
(g) Commodity and commodity derivatives dealers;  
(h) Locals;  
(i) Other institutional investors;  
(2) Large undertakings meeting two of the following size requirements on a company basis:  
— balance sheet total: EUR 20 000 000  
— net turnover: EUR 40 000 000  
— own funds: EUR 2 000 000  
(3) National and regional governments, including public bodies that manage public debt at national or 
regional level, Central Banks, international and supranational institutions such as the World Bank, the 
IMF, the ECB, the EIB and other similar international organisations.  
(4) Other institutional investors whose main activity is to invest in financial instruments, including entities 
dedicated to the securitisation of assets or other financing transactions.  
The entities referred to above are considered to be professionals. They must however be allowed to 
request nonprofessional treatment and investment firms may agree to provide a higher level of 
protection. Where the client of an investment firm is an undertaking referred to above, the investment 
firm must inform it prior to any provision of services that, on the basis of the information available to the 
investment firm, the client is deemed to be a professional client, and will be treated as such unless the 
investment firm and the client agree otherwise. The investment firm must also inform the customer that 
he can request a variation of the terms of the agreement in order to secure a higher degree of protection. 
It is the responsibility of the client, considered to be a professional client, to ask for a higher level of 
protection when it deems it is unable to properly assess or manage the risks involved. This higher level of 
protection will be provided when a client who is considered to be a professional enters into a written 
agreement with the investment firm to the effect that it shall not be treated as a professional for the 
purposes of the applicable conduct of business regime. Such agreement shall specify whether this applies 
to one or more particular services or transactions, or to one or more types of product or transaction. 
 
II. CLIENTS WHO MAY BE TREATED AS PROFESSIONALS ON REQUEST 
II.1. Identification criteria 
Clients other than those mentioned in section I, including public sector bodies, local public authorities, 
municipalities and private individual investors, may also be allowed to waive some of the protections 
afforded by the conduct of business rules and be treated as professional on request. The waiver shall 
specify whether it applies to one or more particular services or transactions, or to one or more types of 
product or transactions, and which protections are waived. 
Investment firms shall therefore be allowed to treat any of those clients as professionals provided the 
relevant criteria and procedure mentioned below are fulfilled. Those clients shall not, however, be 
presumed to possess market knowledge and experience comparable to that of the categories listed in 
Section I. 
 
II.1.2.  
Any such waiver of the protection afforded by the standard conduct of business regime shall be 
considered to be valid only if an adequate assessment is carried out by the investment firm. It shall 
contain  all of the information elements listed in Article 25.2) of this Directive: 
a. the expertise, experience and knowledge of the client,  which undertaken by the investment firm 
gives reasonable assurance, in light of the nature of the transactions or services envisaged, that 
the client is capable of making investment decisions and understanding the risks involved. 
b. the financial situation of the client, including ability to bear losses; 
c. investment objectives of the client, including the client’s risk tolerance 
d. the nature of the transactions, type financial instruments or services as envisaged and defined 
in the waiver  
If the client’s and investment firm’s assessment of the information elements referred to in letter a, b 
and c substantiates that the investor is at all times adequately protected by their expertise, experience 
and knowledge, financial situation and investment objectives, then a waiver for the information in d 
can be allowed. In such case, the waiver agreement between investment firm and investor does not 
need to include a limited set of transactions and type of instruments and services. 
 
II.1.2.a: Expertise, experience and knowledge of the client 
The fitness test applied to managers and directors of entities licensed under Directives in the financial 
field could be regarded as an example of the assessment of expertise and knowledge. In the case of small 
entities, the person subject to that assessment shall be the person authorized to carry out transactions on 
behalf of the entity. 
In the course of that the assessment, as a minimum, one two of the following criteria shall be satisfied 
with respect to the expertise, experience and knowledge of the client: 
- the client has carried out transactions, in significant size, on the relevant market at an average 
frequency of 10 per quarter over the previous four quarters, 
- the size of the client’s financial instrument portfolio, defined as including cash deposits and 
financial instruments exceeds EUR 500 000, 
- the client works or has worked in the financial sector for at least one year in a professional 
position, which requires knowledge of the transactions or services envisaged. 
Member States may adopt specific criteria for the assessment of the expertise and knowledge of 
municipalities and local public authorities requesting to be treated as professional clients. Those criteria 
can be alternative or additional to those listed in the fifth paragraph. 
II.1.2.b: Financial situation of the client 
- the size of the client’s financial instrument portfolio, defined as including cash deposits and 
financial instruments exceeds [EUR 500 000], and  
- commits to investing at least [100,000 Euro] [per investment or portfolio] 
II.1.2.c: Investment objectives including risk tolerance  
- the investors’ preferences for financial and non-financial investment criteria 
- the investors’ ability and willingness to bear losses. 
II.1.2.d The nature of the transactions, type financial instruments or services 
A waiver can be confined to  
 
- one- or a certain type of  investment service as defined in Annex I, section A and/or 
- or particular transaction, and/or  
- one- or a certain type of transaction or product, or a specific financial instruments as in Annex I, 
section C of this directive, and/or 
- other limitations, for instance in time or jurisdiction. 
In this situation the assessment can focus on the relevant information within the elements a,b,c 
thereto. 
The assessment as in II.1.2 can be positively concluded  if the elements mentioned under a, b, and c are 
met and if the nature of the transactions, type financial instruments or services as envisaged and 
defined in the waiver  only regard investment advice, portfolio management and/or execution or 
reception and transmission of client orders with or without ancillary services, and would result in 
maximum risk of loss of the investment, unless information is available or should reasonably come to the 
attention of the investment firm, that from an investor protection point of view the waiver should not be 
allowed. 
II.2. Procedure 
Those clients may waive the benefit of the detailed rules of conduct only where they state in a written 
agreement with the investment firm following procedure is followed: 
a) that they wish to be treated as a professional client, either i) generally or ii) in respect of a 
particular investment service or transaction, or type of transaction or product;  
b) whether the waiver was given in general or in respect of a particular service or transaction, 
type of transaction or product;  
c) that the investment firm includes the information on the client and assessment thereof as 
referred to in section II.1.2 of this Annex; 
d) that the investment firm must has given a clear written warning of the protections and investor 
compensation rights they may lose, and 
e) in a separate document from the contract, that they are aware of the consequences of losing 
such protections. 
ESMA shall adopt guidelines with regard to the content and form and/or a format for the agreement 
and statement as mentioned in this paragraph, 
Before deciding to accept any request for waiver, investment firms must be required to take all 
reasonable steps to ensure that the client requesting to be treated as a professional client meets the 
relevant requirements stated in Section II.1. 
However, If clients have already been categorised as professionals under parameters and procedures 
similar to those referred to above, it is not intended that their relationships with investment firms shall be 
affected by any new rules adopted pursuant to this Annex. 
Firms must implement appropriate written internal policies and procedures to categorise clients. 
Professional clients are responsible for keeping the investment firm informed about any change, which 
could affect their current categorisation. Should the investment firm become aware however that the 
client no longer fulfils the initial conditions, which made him eligible for a professional treatment, the 
investment firm shall take appropriate action.

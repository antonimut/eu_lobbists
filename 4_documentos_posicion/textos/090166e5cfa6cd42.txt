Vanguard Asset Management Limited: Consultation Response Summary – European Commission: 
Public consultation on the review of the MiFID II/MiFIR regulatory framework 
 
A. About Vanguard  
 
The Vanguard Group, Inc. - Vanguard’s parent company  - was founded in 1975 and is based in 
Pennsylvania. Vanguard’s global assets under management (AUM) were just over €4.8 trillion (as of 31 
March 2020). 
 
In Europe, Vanguard has nearly 700 employees based in London, Frankfurt, Paris, Dublin, Amsterdam 
and Zurich. Further European expansion is a strategic objective for Vanguard. Vanguard’s UCITS funds 
are domiciled in Ireland, with  assets under management of just under €130 billion (as of 31 March 
2020). Our investors range from direct  retail investors to institutional and intermediary investors, such 
as banks, pension funds, independent financial advisers and discretionary wealth managers. 
 
Vanguard’s core purpose is to take a stand for all  investors, to treat th em fairly, and to give them the 
best chance fo r investment success.  One aspect that sets us apart from the rest of the asset  
management industry – and lets us always put  investors first around the world – is the ownership  
structure of Vanguard in the US. Rather than being  publicly traded or owned by a  small group of 
individuals, Vanguard is owned by the US mutual funds and ETFs it manages. Those funds, in turn, are 
owned by their  investors, meaning Vanguard is structured as a  “mutual” fund company. This unique 
structure aligns our interests with those of our investors – benefiting investors worldwide – and drives 
the organisation’s culture, philosophy and policies. 
 
Our inspiration is to change the way the world invests.  For more than 40 years we have advocated for 
the Vanguard way of investing – clear goals, balance, low  costs, and discipline.  These enduring 
principles have helped millions of people save for retirement, fund their higher education, or buy a new 
home. The investment industry has  followed our lead, resulting in better outcomes for  many more 
investors. Indeed, while investors can’t control the markets, they  can control the cost of investing. The 
funds we offer are  amongst the lowest -cost funds available in their peer  groups; this is not a pricing 
strategy for us, it is how we  do business and is good for the returns of our clients.  Vanguard’s scale 
also helps to keep costs low. As our assets increase, we can reduce costs for those that invest with us. 
 
Vanguard is supportive of the E uropean Commission’s Capital Markets Union (CMU) initi ative, which 
aims to integrate capital markets to put European savings to better use, improving the efficiency through 
which savers and borrowers are matched, and increasing the performance and competitiveness of the 
EU economy. Vanguard believes that reta il investor engagement is a critical component of the 
development of a stronger capital market in the EU. This requires greater confidence among retail 
investors, and transparency to help investors to make the right investment decisions and for EU citizens 
to benefit from investment opportunities from around the world.

B. Summary of response  
 
Vanguard believes that the implementation of MiFID II has successfully enhanced the EU’s financial 
regulatory framework, particularly in terms of enhancing investor protection. Nevertheless, we believe 
that more can be done to increase retail participation in the EU capital markets  and ensure that these 
investors get a fair deal . To this end, in our response to this consul tation on the review of the MiFID 
II/MiFIR regulatory framework (Consultation) we are proposing that the Commission make further 
amendments to the  investor protection and market structure provisions within the MiFID II/MiFIR 
framework.  
 
Proposed revisions to investor protection provisions 
 
(1) Complete Ban on Inducements. Building strong and integrated capital markets that operate to the 
benefit of retail investors requires the further removal of conflicts of interest in investment distribution 
chains that exist in the EU . Where consumers’ money is invested should not be influenced by the 
existence of payments to intermediaries by produc t providers. Alongside a number of EU consumer 
groups [notably Better Finance and BEUC],we believe that the commission-based sales models that 
dominate distribution of retail investment products in the EU give rise to potential conflicts of interest 
that misalign the interests of investment fund managers and intermediaries, with those of the investo rs 
they serve. The payment of commission to intermediaries can cause product bias in favour of those 
products paying higher commission and away from those paying less, or no, commission, even if this 
is not in the consumer’s best interests. Whilst we do no t dispute that increased transparency and 
disclosure of inducements under existing MiFID II provisions has the potential to mitigate the most 
adverse impacts of product providers paying commissions to intermediaries, we strongly support a 
further revision of MiFID II to ban  the receipt and retention of all commission and inducements by 
intermediaries on the basis that these detrimental conflicts can only truly be addressed where the 
payment of commission by product providers to intermediaries ceases to exist. In our experience, where 
such prohibitions have been implemented, such as in the Netherlands and the UK, they have: 
 
• Reduced the risk of intermediary conflicts of interest affecting the products that are purchased 
by/for end investors; 
• Increased product access and competition, meaning a wider variety of products are available 
for investors to purchase; and 
• Increased cost transparency 
 
 
(2) Consistent and Comparable Disclosure of Cost and Product Information. Despite 
improvements pursuant to MiFID II, issues persist with regards to EU consumers’ access to consistent 
and comparable product and servic e material. In order to enable more consistent and comparable 
information to be available to consumers in the EU, we support further simplification and harmonisation 
of disclosure rules . More specifically, retail investors would benefit from being able to access 
understandable and consistent information across different products (UCITS, PRIIPs and non -MiFID 
comparable products) and services. Even where that is currently theoretically possible (e.g. costs 
disclosures), the lack of a prescribed cost calculation methodology under MiFID II, acts as an obstacle 
to genuine comparability. We are also very supportive of the idea of developing an online tool to allow 
EU consumers to filter, select and compare retail investment products in order to help them make better-
informed investment decisions. Recognising the current lack of (standardised information) across all 
EU retail investment products, this could be undertaken on a phased basis, starting with products for 
which a comparison is currently possible (e.g. UCITS funds).

Proposed revisions to market structure provisions 
 
In order to enable better outcomes for all investors, greater market efficiency should also be promoted 
through further revisions to MiFID II/MiFIR  
 
(1) Consolidated Tape (CT).  We believe that a CT will improve trade execution and firms’ ability to 
comply with their best execution obligations by providing a comprehensive, trusted data source to 
monitor transaction cost analysis. A CT is also integral to delivering the CMU as it wo uld empower 
European retail investors by providing them with an accurate source of information on trading activity. 
In addition, a CT could support the establishment of a level playing field with regards to market data 
and promote further competition and t ransparency within EU markets . In light of different levels of 
demand and complexity, we favour a phased-in introduction of a CT. T he first phase would focus on 
post-trade data for all products, with pre-trade data following at a later stage.  
 
(2) Share Trading Obligation (STO)/ Derivatives Trading Obligation (DTO)/ Double Volume Cap 
(DVC). We consider the STO to be damaging to the competitiveness of European equity markets and 
consumer outcomes as it forces participants to trade on pre-specified venues rather than the potentially 
best venue at the time of execution. Similarly, the introduction of the DVC has contributed to increased 
complexity of European markets without clear benefits for market participants and consumers with 
regards to transparency and market efficiency. We consider there to be strong grounds for both the 
STO and the DVC mechanism to be repealed. At minimum, the cross-border equivalence provisions of 
the STO need to be reconsidered in order to not inappropriately restrict EU investors from accessing 
liquidity and improved pricing on third-country venues, such as those in the US and UK. The same 
applies to the DTO mechanism which , although  it has improved eleme nts of European derivatives 
markets, risks fragmenting global derivatives trading markets and restricting cross-border capital flows 
unless cross-border equivalence with non-EU DTO regimes is further refined.  
 
(3) The EU’s Digital Future . This MiFID II/MIFIR Review affords a timely opportunity for the 
Commission to consider further calibration of  MiFID II/ MiFIR requirements to the ever-increasing 
digitalisation of the financial sector. In particular, accommodating and encouraging the adoption of 
digital technology (e.g. technology -enabled advice, direct -to-consumer online platforms) and the 
electronic delivery of information will foster innovation and competition, while helping increase 
consumers’ access to valuable investment products and services and improve their long-term financial 
standing.

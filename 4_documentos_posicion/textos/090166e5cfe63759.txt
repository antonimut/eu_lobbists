Combined Evaluation Roadmap / Inception Impact 
Assessment of the Development of post Euro 6/VI 
emission standards for cars, vans, lorries and buses 
May 2020 
 
IBERDROLA believes that the following are the main points that should be considered 
for a definition of new emission standards for road transport: 
1. Setting the EU vehicle emission limits to the lowest possible level based on best 
available technologies and applying these lim its to all powertrain technologies 
within the light duty and heavy duty categories without exception. 
The limits set must help ensure that the WHO’s Air Quality Guidelines  (to be 
reviewed next year) will be met across the EU at all times and should be zero for all 
new vehicles from 2030.  
Limits of all pollutants must be respected by all types of vehicles no matter its 
technology, including compressed natural gas , plug-in hybrids or vehicles which use 
future advanced/synthetic fuels, as is already the case in China and the U.S. 
Post Euro 6/VI regulation should be the last allowing any dangerous pollution from 
vehicles in Europe. From 2030 all new vehicles should be zero pollutant. 
2. Regulating all pollutants that are harmful to public health and the environment 
Many pollutants emitted from the tailpipe are completely unregulated but have a 
negative impact on public health  and the environment. The following pollutants 
need to be effectively regulated in the future: 
 Solid particles smaller than 23 nm: Tiny solid particles emitted in large amounts from 
all vehicles with a combustion engine, including those powered by natural gas. These 
particles could potentially be the most harmful due to the high efficiency with they 
are deposited in the lungs and their potentially higher toxicity. 
 Ammonia: contributes to air pollution; each 1mg of ammonia is estimated to 
contribute 1mg to particle air pollution smaller than 2.5 micrometres (PM2,5). The 
emissions of this pollutant are only regulated for heavy duty veh icles, but certain 
passenger cars can have also high ammonia emissions. 
 NO2: It is currently only regulated as part of a total NOx limit. As the fraction of NO2 
varies significantly between different vehicles, an independent emission limit must 
be established for this pollutant

 N2O and CH4: These are greenhouse gases with a very high global warming potential. 
Methane emissions need to be tightened for heavy duty, and for light duty this 
pollutant is only covered as part of total hydrocarbon limit. No emission limit exists 
for nitrous oxide. To effectively limit the emission of this pollutants, direct emission 
limits applicable under all driving conditions must be established. Additionally, to 
account for their climate warming effects, they must be counted in the revised CO2 
regulation as CO2 equivalents. 
 Formaldehyde and acetaldehyde: They are cancer-causing chemicals emitted from 
cars and vans which have ethanol blended into the fuel. Especially high amounts of 
these dangerous pollutants are emitted from vehicles that use high proportions of 
ethanol, like flex fuels which run on up to 85% ethanol that are sold in the EU. 
Therefore, emission limits for both formaldehyde and acetaldehyde need to be 
established, as it already happens in the U.S. 
New regulation must capture and control full environmental and health impact of 
pollution from road transport 
3.  Ensuring that all vehicles meet the emission limits for all pollutants under all 
driving conditions throughout the whole lifetime of the vehicle 
This should be achieved by: 
 Extending durability requirements to truly reflect real vehicle lifetimes. The testing 
requirements need to extended to reflect the whole lifetime of the vehicle, not just 
between 15,000-100,000 km and up to 5 years which is required as present.  
 Improving testing procedures to ensure that all possible driving conditions are 
covered. The type-approval testing procedures must be designed in such a way as to 
ensure that emission limits are respected by  all vehicles under all possible driving 
conditions.

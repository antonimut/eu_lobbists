Coherence and alignment of legislation is important in order to achieve cost-effective solutions and
 
a predictable regulatory environment. The new Directive should therefore be developed in full
 
consistency with other existing legislation such as the Clean Vehicles Directive (CVD) and relevant
 
ongoing regulatory procedures, such as the RED II implementation, review of the Energy Taxation
 
Directive (ETD) and the technical assessment of the Fuel Quality Directive (FQD). In addition,
 
Reference Fuel and vehicle CO2 Regulations should be aligned with AFID and other
 
aforementioned directives by supporting all the available low-emission solutions, including
 
synthetic and paraffinic fuels, with well-to-wheel based assessment. CVD counts heavy duty
 
vehicles using neat biofuels as “clean vehicles”. Therefore developing the AFID framework such
 
that it would support the objectives of the CVD, would seem logical.

Renewable paraffinic diesel (HVO) demand is expected to be more than 20 million tons annually
 
based on existing and proposed regulation in Europe and North America. The currently identified
 
waste and residue feedstock pool in 2030 is globally around 35 million tons a year. There is
 
significant growth potential in scalable new technologies e.g. lignocellulosics, municipal solid waste
 
and algae oils. New scalable feedstocks enable deeper decarbonisation of transport.
 
 
Rapid growth and cost reduction in renewable electricity also drives the development of renewable
 
hydrogen and Power-to-Liquids. They enable the production of sustainable liquid fuels besides
 
biomass by utilizing CO2 as feedstock. In the longer term the commercialization of these
 
technologies will help to tackle the scarcity of biofuel feedstocks. Hence, internal combustion
 
engines (ICEs) remain a viable technology using alternative and renewable liquid fuels that
 
contribute to the decarbonization of transportation and decrease the dependence on crude oil.

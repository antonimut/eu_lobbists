FRONIUS COMMENTS ON THE DIRECTIVE 2014/94/EU 
24.06.2020 
General terms 
The revision from the European Commission to strengthen the directive on alternative fuels refuelling/ 
recharging infrastructure is welcomed by Fronius. To reach a European climate neutrality by 2050, the 
transports´ emissions need to decrease by 90%. Therefore, the deployment and further use of alternative fuels 
is an important step towards mitigation of environmental impacts and European independence from oil-based 
fuels.  
 
As one of the goals of alternative fuels infrastructure is to mitigate environmental impacts, it would be relevant 
to mention more specifically sustainability aspects, in coherence with the current challenges and the recent 
European Green Deal. In this sense, a framework for certificate/guarantee of origin (for electricity, hydrogen, 
biofuels…) should be developed and implemented at a European level. The further implementation of analytic 
tools (for example LCAs, environmental labels…) can also support the shift to a more sustainable mobility 
sector. Some projects have already started tackling this issue such as CertifHy (for hydrogen).  
 
A consistent and comprehensive action should be conducted for the development of alternative fuels. The most 
relevant solutions – taking into account the sustainability criteria: environment, social and economic – should be 
implemented depending on the final needs. Special attention should be made on promising alternatives that 
could be put aside and their potentials wasted (avoid a favour treatment for a specific option).  
 
One relevant aspect would be to ensure a harmonised European framework on standards regarding the 
alternative fuels (electricity, hydrogen…). More clarity on the relevant standardization organisations to be 
considered would be helpful. Which standardization organisations will be considered? How will th ey be 
selected? At which scale: European-level or more regionally? How will the information be communicated? 
 
Hydrogen as a fuel (mostly Article 5) 
When the different alternative fuels are considered, the hydrogen option is the least developed one especially in 
comparison with other sections (for example on the natural gas). More information should be provided and 
clarified, regarding for example the different usages, data communication, and others.  
 
The distinction between green (based on renewable energy sources) and blue hydrogen (based on steam 
methane reformation or coal gasification with a Carbon Capture System) is ne ver mentioned. This distinction 
should to be clearly defined and stated. Green hydrogen should be strongly supported as an alternative for fuel 
production since it can be produced without CO 2-emissions. Furthermore, it is promoted in several recent 
initiatives, proving a will from stakeholders to develop a hydrogen economy. Since May 2020 the European 
Commission has launched a new hydrogen strategy initiative and the paper “The EU hydrogen strategy: 
Hydrogen Europe´s top 10 key recommendations” asks in Point 6 to revise directive 2014/94/EU, in order to 
promote hydrogen in the mobility sector.  
 
A possibility connected to hydrogen would be to support the development of Hydrogen Refuelling Systems 
equipped with renewable energy systems (for example solar energy). This way, hydrogen can be locally 
produced and distributed to vehicles and promote the EU-added value with local business opportunities and job 
creations.  
 
Connections between hydrogen and the Trans-European Transport Network (TEN-T) should be clearly 
established and defined. Furthermore, a European framework for the technical requirements or compatibility of 
hydrogen systems is required in order to shift significantly to alternative fuels and improve the user´s 
experience.   
 
A clear definition of a roadmap and numerical goals (e.g. number of recharging and refuelling points per 
Member State or region) would strongly support the implementation of an alternative fuel infrastr ucture, tackling 
the problem of the related market´s low-level of penetration.

Miscellaneous 
Some updates/changes would be required for the references´ numbers.  
The annex mentions the "technical specifications of the ISO/TS 20100 Gaseous Hydrogen Fuelling 
specification." The norm´s name should be ISO 19880-X:YYYY (X goes from 1 to 8, YYYY is the year: 2019 or 
2020). 
 
Finally, regarding fuels costs, a better calculation and consideration of externalities (positive and negative) 
would strengthen a transition to a more decarbonised fuel infrastructure and service. The relative contributions 
of the different fuels would then be more explicitly indicated, with the CO2 emissions or the amount “saved”, the 
use of critical materials, etc. A better understanding of fuels origin and influences would be helpful to build an 
improved European fuel infrastructure.

 
 
 
Herbalife Nutrition feedback to the Inception Impact Assessment on the review 
of the requirements for packaging and other measures to prevent packaging 
waste 
 
Herbalife Nutrition values the European Commission’s intention to review the requirements for 
packaging and other measures to prevent packaging waste and welcomes the opportunity to submit 
feedback to this initiative. We agree that there is a need for clearer and more specific requirements on 
the composition of packaging. Introduction of harmonised rules on packaging among Member States is 
crucial to support the transition towards more sustainable circular economy and ensure well-
functioning internal market. 
 
This statement aims to summarise our comments on the essential requirements for packaging and 
support the Commission’s work in revising the Directive 94/62/EC on Packaging and Packaging Waste. 
 
 
Specific comments based on the structure of the Inception Impact Assessment 
 
A. EU Problem Definition (selected points) 
a. In 2017 only 42% o f plastic packaging waste generated in the EU was being recycled, 
compounded by the fact that the amount of materials used for packaging are growing 
in Europe as stated by the EU commission.  EU commission states that this is due to the 
cost of recycled ma terials, however a main contributing factor is the lack of 
appropriate waste streams for recycled materials. Presently, mature waste streams 
exist for glass, aluminium and PET, while demand for other packaging is much lower. 
Recommend that the EU Commissio n study and create incentives for additional uses 
for recycled packaging, and optimised recycling processes.  Legislating the requirement 
for recyclable packaging will not solve the demand problem. 
b. Another suggestion is partnering with corporations for voluntary goals (i.e. CONEG in 
the US). 
c. Harmonising definitions for recycled and recyclable will allow for a more focused 
effort.  
d. Light weighting of packaging should be further analysed. The conclusion that this is 
done at the expense of recycling may not consider the net environmental effect.  Mass 
or source reduction is usually preferred as it reduces the amount that needs to be 
recycled. It usually can also reduce the overall carbon footprint through transportation 
efficiencies. 
 
B. EU Objectives and Policy Options (selected points) 
a. Requiring all packaging to be reusable or recyclable:  
i. Definitions are needed as indicated above. It is crucial that harmonised rules 
are implemented in the same way among Member States. This can be achieved 
by providing an enforceable definition that is interpreted in the same way. 
ii. Waste streams need to be optimised or recycling will not have the desired 
effect.  More capacity is required in the various waste streams in order to 
facilitate more recycling.

ptimising the recycling processes will require engineering and investment,  
and this can be facilitated by tax or fee incentives. It is not efficient to commit 
significant resources into the redesign of packaging if the packaging waste is 
not collected or sorted in an adequate manner. 
b. Restricting use of packaging materials to certain applications or eliminating packaging:
i. Packaging, besides conveying the product to the end user, is also protection for 
the product through the transportation channel. This needs to be considered,  
or the assumption that these initiatives will reduce cost might be inaccurate.
ii. When examining possible restrictions, the Commission should be aware that  
reusable packaging can be challenging in long and international supply chains.  
We believe that the option of ‘ re-use by a consumer’, without the product  
being returned to a system for refill/reloading, should be explored within the  
Packaging and Packaging Waste Directive for packaging whose product design  
characteristics and performance allow such re-use.
c. Reducing the complexity of Packaging, number of material and polymers:
i. There may need to be an exemption for products that require moisture and  
oxygen barrier – layers are required in packaging of such products in order to  
achieve the barrier and allow for machinability.
ii. The assumption that cost will decrease may again be inaccurate as  
manufacturability of the new configurations may be affected.
iii. We consider that reduction of complexity of packaging materials, including the  
number of materials and polymers,  should be addressed within product  
specific legislation instead of horizontal legislation. For food contact materials  
this should be covered within the scope of food contact materials legislation to 
ensure that product safety isn’t jeopardised.
d. Introducing recycled content targets for specific packaging formats:
i. Introduction of recycled content targets for all packaging formats could be  
challenging and unfeasible to meet. We believe that such requirements should 
be given under product specific l egislation to ensure that product-s pecific 
characteristics are considered.
ii. Lack of authorised recycling processes for plastic food contact materials at EU  
level i s currently preventing introduction of recycled content to such  
packaging. We ask the European Commission to take authorisation of recycling 
processes as a priority to allow the industry to start introducing recycled  
content to their packaging.
iii. Assessment of supply of approved recycled materials for food contact  
materials application should be conducted.
 
 
 
C. EU Preliminary assessment of Expected Impacts  
a. The assumption that a shift toward reusable packaging will require initial investment 
but result in operational savings is not an accurate assumption. Product categories, 
transportation channels, and conflict with existing regulations needs to be evaluated. 
b. Reinforcement of essential requirements will potentially focus recycling efforts, but 
they will need to be expanded for capacity. Financial inducements, tax incentives and 
other instruments may be helpful. 
c. The assumption that these new regulations will result in job creation is not an accurate 
assumption and needs to be studied in greater detail. Several of the initiatives require 
initial investment by industry, but the return potential as stated is vague and  may not 
be achievable. Again, tax or fee incentives to offset these costs may be required.  
d. It is assumed that the administrative burden for public authorities will be reduced, this 
may not be an accurate assumption, especially if the intention is for enforcement. 
e. It is not clear how the Commission concluded that a more competitive market for 
secondary materials will ensue as a result of these regulations. In other regions, few 
companies have entered the business causing transition to these materials to  result in 
an increased cost.  
f. It is correct that the case of food contact materials must be studied and may need to 
be exempted.  
g. It is correct that the administrative burden for industry will increase, especially if it is 
required to designate the packaging status on labelling causing a significant cost and 
inventory obsolescence. 
 
 
D. EU Evidence Base, Data collection and Better regulation Instruments 
a. It is critical that the Commission conduct further review with relevant industry 
stakeholders as it appears  that certain assumptions have been made that may not be 
completely accurate, especially the cost basis of the proposals. 
b. The “have your say website” and further stakeholder interviews will assist the 
Commission in completing this directive with optimum value.

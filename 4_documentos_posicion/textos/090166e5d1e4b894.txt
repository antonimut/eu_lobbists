Considerations Reducing packaging waste roadmap consultation 
 
Cefic, the European Chemical Industry Council, founded in 1972, is the voice of large, medium and small 
chemical companies across Europe, which provide 1.2 million jobs and account for about 17% of world 
chemicals production. Cefic is devoted to promoting a thriving chemical industry that is broadly recognised 
to: 
• provide sustainable solutions to meet the challenges for future generations, 
• provide safe and resource efficient solutions  
• foster prosperity, growth and investments in Europe, 
The chemical industry provides the wheels for a circular economy. Whether you look at paper, battery, or 
plastics recycling, the process for the production of these products and recycling of these products is using 
chemistry. Transitioning towards a circular economy can contribute to addressing  our global resource 
challenge, reduce greenhouse gas emissions through  better use and reuse of the materials that alre ady 
exist in the economy,  reduce environmental littering, create new meaningful jobs, and spur  economic 
growth. Going more circular means contributing to meeting the Paris Agreement and the broader UN 2030 
Sustainability Development Goals. 
The faster we can develop and deploy circular solutions and technologies, the faster we can make the EU 
economy fully circular. For example, chemical recycling, a set of technologies developed by the chemical 
industry, can be a game changer for plastic waste management an d drive the creation of a “ new plastics 
economy”. The next step is to create the right conditions for our industry to develop this and other 
promising solutions on European soil. This includes the creation of a European Single Market for Waste  
and progressive transition from a waste-oriented to a resource-oriented policy framework. 
In this respect we like to point at the Circular Plastics Alliance (CPA) – an industry voluntary initiative set in 
collaboration with the European Commission. CPA signatories work together to realise the full potential of 
recycled plastics in the circular economy and help the European market for recycled plastics grow steadily 
in the future, with policies and me asures that include facilitating the free movement of plastic waste for 
recycling and recycled plastics in the EU.  
The CPA aims to bring at least 10 million tonnes of recycled plastics – in products and packaging – back to 
the market by 2025. This target directly links to Recycled Content in products, and one of the metrics to 
monitor progress in achieving the circular economy for plastics 
Recycled content is obtained from  “following” mass flows of recycled plastics streams. A too prescriptive 
or specific definition or measurement method based on mass flows would unduly discriminate alternative 
approaches associated with new recycling techniques. For instance chemical recycling, a set of technologies 
converting plastic waste into secondary feedstock materials for the production of new products including 
plastics.

As these new recycling technologies come to the market plastic mass -flow may not be sufficient to cover 
all recycling options . T he ability to ‘follow’ recycled plastics streams may become difficult or even 
impossible.  Cefic therefore would like to invite the E uropean Commission and like to work with the 
Commission to develop and implement metrics compatible with t he common understood principle of 
“what goes in goes out ” and attributing recycled-plastics-feedstock to selected products using a chain of 
custody Mass-Balance Approach. 
We therefore call the European Commission to assess the impacts of the policy scenarios it will envisage 
and to continuously consult the chemical industry in the development of recycling metrics, specifically 
introducing recycled content targets  for specific packaging formats  and the contributions chemical 
recycling of plastic waste can make in this respect.  
We support a recycled content assessment made at EU level, which also considers the evolution of 
packaging design, recyclability, material innovation, collection, sorting, and recycling technologies.  
 
While contributing to this consultation we are very aware we are experiencing unprecedented times, with 
events none of us have lived through before. Many governments and institutions around the world are 
taking major actions  to address the Covid -19 crisis, and introducing large -scale policies which will have 
significant impacts for years to come. We will continue to support Europe’s Member State governments 
and institutions in their efforts to overcome the socio -economic impacts of the crisis. When investing in 
the future, industry, governments and institutions will have to ensure investments align with the policy 
targets of a climate neutral Europe by 2050. All this also means that the attractiveness of Europe as a re -
investment destination, and re -shoring industry back to Europe, will depend more than ever on a 
favourable policy framework that manages ever-growing differences between the world’s regions. We look 
to the European Commission to undertake the appropriate assessme nts and to include these wider 
considerations in the future framework that will be developed.

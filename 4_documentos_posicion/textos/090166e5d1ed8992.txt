 
 
How a Waste Recycling Energy Plant 
can boost Climate and Circular 
Economy 
 
INTRODUCTION 
Europe stands at a crossroad. The EU urgently needs to face its complex climate challenges In order to 
achieve the 2050 climate targets, innovative policies and support for circular initiatives are essential.  
This requires a realistic attitude: aiming high should be the goal, but this should not prevent policy 
support for incremental innovation that can serve as a  catalyst for further development towards a 
climate-neutral Europe.  
AVR has always taken environmental policy as an opportunity. AVR operates  ‘Waste Recycling Energy 
Plants’ (WREP) that recover energy and secondary raw materials from waste. AVR  also works towards 
circularity by capturing CO2 and delivering it to nearby greenhouses . In order to be able to effectively 
combat climate change and boost the circular economy, we urgently call on European policymakers to 
support two decisive developments: establish a viable market for recycled CO2 and establish a well -
functioning market of secondary raw materials. 
 
THE MISSING LINK: A CO2 MARKET 
CO2 emission s are amongst  the biggest challenges our society faces . An increasing number of  
organisations are working together on reducing or capturing their CO2 emissions. AVR is the first 
European Energy-from-Waste facility to capture CO2 at large scale, delivering it to nearby greenhouses1. 
This provides a  clear example of how economic players from different, and usually not interrelated, 
sectors can jointly decarbonise society.  
However, relying on CO2 reduction and Carbon Capture Storage (CCS) alone will not be enough to reach 
and sustain the 2050 targets. What Europe desperately needs are targeted support for Carbon Capture 
and Utilisation (CCU) initiatives and the creation of a well-functioning CO2 market in Europe. The lack 
thereof reduces the eagerness of European businesses to invest in innovative capturing techniques.  
Currently, CO2 produced from fossil sources is far cheaper than recycled CO2 which hinders the 
establishment of a true European recycled CO2 market. Therefore, we ask for support for the  
development of the carbon capture industry . Eventually, we need to move to wards a market where 
supply and demand determine a realistic price for recycled CO2 that can compete with CO2 from fossil 
sources. Otherwise, businesses will continue to rely on fossil resources. 
AVR urges European policymakers  to support the creation of this m arket by stimulating the use of 
recycled CO2, investing in research on both short- and long-term alternative use cases for recycled CO2

THE CIRCULAR TRANSITION 
EU Member States are moving higher up in the waste hierarchy by diverting waste streams from landfill 
to energy recovery, to recycling and reuse of products. AVR welcomes this commitme nt but is 
concerned that the lack of a well-functioning market prevents waste to be treated in the most circular 
way possible.   
It is often forgotten that we are currently unable to recycle all materials that we bring  back into the 
economy, despite our best efforts. This means that , for the time being, Europe needs to deal with 
residual waste that cannot be recycled or re -used and is forbidden to be landfilled . The existing 
European Energy-from-Waste facilities fill-in this gap.  
There are only a handful ‘Waste Recycling Energy Plants’ (WREPs) that work according 
to advanced principles of recycling and energy production from waste. These WREPs 
retrieve plastics, metals, drinking cartons, and minerals as raw materials from residual 
waste and convert the remainder of the residual waste into steam, heat and electricity 
with a (very) high energy efficiency according to the European R1 energy efficiency  
formula2.   
However, this type of Energy-from-Waste facilities are geographically unevenly dispersed. Ideally, EU 
Member States should cooperate and support each other in a free internal waste market. This could be 
done by allocating the available residual waste to the best possible facility in the area, thus avoiding the 
creation of redundant capacities and incentivising the existing Waste-to-Energy facilities to upgrade.  
From an efficiency and environmental perspective it is most preferable to divert the purely residual 
waste to the most efficient treatment facility in a region and, regardless of national borders . This 
requires an open European market for residual waste transport. A realistic approach is required which 
balances the environmental gains of the most efficient treatment and the footprint generated by 
transporting residual waste to the most efficient facility and sharing best practices on waste transport.3  
AVR therefore calls on European policymakers to stimulate waste streams to be treated at the most 
efficient location in the region, thereby avoiding the creation of unnecessary capacity.

How can the EU close the loop?  
1. Establish a viable market for recycled CO2 by further researching alternatives uses of 
recycled CO2 and developing policy support for this emerging industry within the 
framework of the EU ETS, non-ETS and CCS regulation. 
2. Establish a well-functioning market for secondary raw materials by reducing restrictions 
on cross border waste shipments, thus encouraging the waste to be treated in the most 
efficient and circular facilities.

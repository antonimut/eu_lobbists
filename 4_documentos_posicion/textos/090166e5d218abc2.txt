BDSI answer to the Inception Impact Assessment on the Review of the 
requirements for packaging and other measures to prevent packaging waste 
 
BDSI welcomes the opportunity to contribute to the consultation on the Review 
of the requirements for packaging and other measures to prevent packaging 
waste. 
 
The BDSI represents the economic interests of over 200 mostly medium-sized 
German confectionery companies. The German confectionery industry is the 
fourth-largest sector of the German food industry, accounting for around 10% of 
overall sales. It is particularly characterised by its strong focus on exports. 
Germany’s confectionery manufacturers have an overall workforce of around 
50,000 employees.

The German confectionery industry has a strong interest in handling the 
required res ources in a fo rward-looking and efficient manner.  Packaging 
material has been continuously reduced by the manufacturers for a number of 
years. The continuous improvement in the material efficiency of packaging saves 
resources and CO2 emissions. 
Sustainable production and the use of sustainable packaging is of key importance 
for the German confectionery industry . Hence the confectionery sector is 
constantly deliberating sust ainable ways of handling water, energy, waste, and 
logistics, and is thus also focuss ed on the eco -friendliness of the packaging used 
and the conservation of resources.  Since the introduction of the German 
Packaging Ordinance in 1991, German confectionery manufacturers have been 
continuously reducing the amount of packaging material used.  Plastic packaging 
has become ever lighter since then.  As a result of technical progress in the 
manufacturing sector, the use of plastic packaging is now 35% lower than wha t 
counted as state -of-the-art technology in 1991.  The German Association for 
Packaging Research (GVM) has calculated:  if Germany had stayed with the 
packaging of 1991, instead of going on to use state -of-the-art plastic packaging in 
2013, then 
- this would have generated 955 kilo tonnes more plastic 
and 
- this would have resulted in approx. 2. 6 (2.5 -2.8) million tonnes more of 
CO2 emissions. 
 
Germany is a pioneer in the field of recycling and also that of plastic packaging.  
The recycling rates Germany has achie ved, i.e. the share of recycled packaging in 
the overall volume collected, are unpara lleled worldwide.  According to the 
European Waste Framework Directive, at least 50% of packaging waste from 
households or, if applicable, other household -like sources must  be recycled. This 
target was already exceeded in 2010, when the recycling rate reach ed 85%, and 
this trend has continued over the last few years.  In addition, all BDSI member 
manufacturers participate in one of Germany’s Dual Systems (DSD) for waste 
avoidance and recycling.  The recycling systems could even improve if consumers 
managed to curb the flow of materials by not carelessly throwing away plastic 
packaging but sorting it correctly. 
 
 
In many cases the confectionery industry still has no alternatives  to plastic 
packaging. They fulfil various functions to protect food in the interests  of the 
consumer and hence enable the pleasure and enjoyment of a food consumption 
that is safe. 
Packaging serves to perform various tasks.  The following functions can be 
achieved by making a requirements-optimised selection of plastics for tailor-made 
packaging solutions: 
- Protection of food from contaminants for hygienic reasons 
- Protection against microbial spoilage (e.g. germs/fungi) 
- Prevention of the absorption of undesirable and volatile substances  
(e.g. mineral hydrocarbons) 
- Shelf life 
- Protection from light 
- Protection against oxidation 
- Longer shelf life 
- Avoidance/reduction of temperature fluctuations 
- Prevention of the absorption of extraneous odours 
- Prevention of grease transfer to the packaging 
- Protection against water absorption for dry products and also in areas with 
high humidity for other products 
- Protection against insect damage in some areas 
- Transport protection 
- Aesthetics and recognisability of products 
- Increasing the supply of goods oriented to the needs of the consumer 
through consumer-oriented sizes 
- Providing information to the consumer. 
 
 
Plastic packaging makes an important contribution to environmental and 
climate protection, as a greater volume of food would spoil without efficient 
packaging. 
Food often contains many times more resources a nd energy than the packaging 
material. Food must therefore be packaged in keeping with the requirements so 
as to reduce food losses.  Food packaging prevents the loss of resources and 
spoilage of food.  They ther efore contribute to achieving the target of a 50% 
reduction in food waste by 2030, as called for by EU parliamentarians. 
 
 
The BDSI is committed to promoting and accelerating the technical development 
of waste sorting and recycling systems. 
The recognition  of the different materials coming from the ye llow bag or the 
yellow bin during sorting must be continuously improved so as to be able to 
recycle more materials. According to the today’s state of the art technology, many 
composite materials are not, or onl y to a limited extent, recyclable, although 
many of these materials are essential for the purposes mentioned.  Although 
packaging made using mono -materials can be recycled easily in principle, this is 
currently often not done by waste management companies d ue to their lacking 
the necessary sorting tech nology. Polyethylene terephthalate (PET) is a 
thermoplastic that is well suited for recycling plastic bottles, for example.  By 
contrast, PET thermoformed trays, used in many food packaging applications and 
today even contain recycled material, are currentl y not being recycled.  Only by 
creating an even better infrastructure and improving the recycling structure and 
technology can the recyclability of plastic packaging for food and confectionery be 
increased and hence lead to increased recycling rates. 
 
 
Recyclable packaging must meet the food safety requirements of the respective 
products. 
According to Art.  14 of the Regulation (EC) 178/2002 laying down the general 
principles and requirements of food law , food tha t is not safe may not be sold in 
the market. Foodstuffs are considered unsafe if it can be assumed that they are 
harmful to health or unsuitable for human consumption.  Packaging, especially 
plastic packaging, serves to fulfil this legal requirement for foo dstuffs. To this end, 
packaging must comply wi th the requirements of the EU Regulation on materials 
and articles intended to come into contact with food (1935/2004) and the EU 
Regulation on good manufacturing practice for these materials (2023/2006).  In 
achieving the objectives of dispensing with pack aging material or only using 
recyclable packaging material, these legal obligations nevertheless have top 
priority. Frequently the objective of ensuring the safety of the food and its 
packaging, together with the currently commercially available shelf live s, can only 
be achieved by using composite materials that are hardly, or not at all, recyclable 
using current state-of-the-art technology. 
 
 
Research into the recovery and recyclability of packaging must be int ensified. 
The energy balance, carbon footprint , and environmental footprint must be 
taken into account in the kind of recycling. 
In Germany, approx.  49% of the plastic waste is thermally recovered and 
approx. 51% is materially recovered, i.e. recycled.  According to the German 
Packaging Act, the recycl ing rate for plastic packaging came to 58.5% in 2019 and 
is expected to increase to 63% in 2022.  This means that as early as in 2019, 
Germany’s recycling rate for plastic packaging is expected to exceed the Eur ope-
wide target of 55% in 2030 as laid out in the EU Packaging Directive.  Both the 
European Single U se Plastics Directive and the German Packaging Act require 
packaging to be recyclable.  All plastic packaging throughout Europe is  to be 
recyclable by 2030. In Germany, the fees for participation in a du al system are to 
be scaled according to the respective packaging’s recyclability since 2019. A switch 
to so -called bio -plastics as packaging is not a viable soluti on, as biodegradable 
plastics usually do not decompose any faster in nature than other plasti cs do and 
are hence not returned to the cycle.  Nor are they easily compostable.  Hence it is 
rather the packaging industry that is called upon to offer solutions to  the users of 
plastic packaging via which the demand for recyclable packaging can be realised . 
Innovation must be the driving motor for achieving higher rates of recyclability 
and improved degradability.  But research must also focus on the factors energy 
balance, carbon footprint, and the environmental footprint of recycling or thermal 
recovery.

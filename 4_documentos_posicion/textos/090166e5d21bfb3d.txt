In general, the beverage carton industry supports control of hazardous waste and sound environmental 
management of waste as requested by the Waste Shipment Regulation. The members of ACE are 
committed to support recycling of beverage cartons in Europe and all over the world.  
Used beverage carton are classified as “laminated paperboard waste” (code B3020) which is green listed 
and hence can be shipped without notification or prior consent for recovery or recycling. It is rightly so as 
beverage cartons are recyclable and recycled at scale. Recycling of beverage cartons in Europe (EU-28) 
has grown steadily over the last number of years, with a recycling rate of 49% of all cartons sold in Europe 
being recycled in 2018, with some countries, like Belgium or Germany, having rates over 70%. The 
recycling of beverage cartons will increase thanks to both the new requirement under EU waste 
legislation to separately collect all packaging materials, and the industry’s newly formed pan-European 
recycling platform, EXTR:ACT, that seeks to better coordinate and support collection and recycling.  
To reach an increased recycling rate for beverage cartons it is however essential that the infrastructure 
and legal requirements are in place so as to allow collection and shipment of waste to recycling facilities. 
Packaging waste is a commodity that should be tradable in view of sound treatment. Exports of  non-
hazardous waste to OECD countries for recycling should remain possible without having to go through the 
lengthy and costly notification procedure. A too strict approach to the tra de of plastic waste may hinder 
recycling.  
The status of the different waste is not harmonised across the MS which creates unnecessary cost and 
burden for operators and recyclers. It also hampers recycling. MS should be obliged to agree on 
harmonised status for the waste streams so as to facilitate control and treatment.  
The notification procedure especially for transit countries should be simplified.

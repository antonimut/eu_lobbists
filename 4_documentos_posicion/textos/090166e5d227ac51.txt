PlasticsEurope response to the public consultation on the revision of the Waste 
Shipment Regulation (Regulation (EC) No 1013/2006).  
 
 
July 2020 
 
 
PlasticsEurope is one of the leading European trade associations with centres in Brussels, 
Frankfurt, London, Madrid, Milan and Paris. We network with European and national plastics 
associations and have more than 100 member companies, who are responsible for producing 
over 90% of all polymers across the 27 member states of the European Union, plus Norway, 
Switzerland, Turkey and the United Kingdom.1  
 
PlasticsEurope support the intention of the European Commission to review the Waste Shipment 
Regulation (hereafter WSR), with a view to simplify and reduce burden related to the WSR 
implementation and strengthen its enforcement across EU Member States in line with the New 
Circular Economy Action Plan.  
 
PlasticsEurope welcome the opportunity to provide feedback as part of the public consultation 
and has the following recommendations on the three identified policy objectives: 
 
 
1. The WSR should support the transition to a circular economy in the EU more 
effectively – fully support but guidelines must be developed under the Waste 
Framework Directive not the WSR 
 
PlasticsEurope fully agree that the revision of the WSR should support the uptake of the 
circular economy in Europe whilst providing the opportunity to promote innovation and 
the deployment of new technologies, such as chemical recycling. With this in mind, it is 
important to remove some of the current administrative burdens to waste shipment, 
including by simplifying existing notification procedures.  
 
In addition, PlasticsEurope strongly believe that a consistent interpretation of the end of 
waste criteria and waste classification at EU level would contribute greatly to achieving 
circularity in Europe. The scope of the WSR is to regulate the shipment of waste and it is 
therefore not the appropriate legal instrument to tackle interpretation linked to the end of 
waste criteria and the classification of waste which both fall under the scope of the Waste 
Framework Directive. Instead, we would recommend developing further guidance to 
support consistent implementation of the Waste Framework Directive and to clarify the 
links between the different types of legislation and classification of waste. In case of 
disagreement between member states, the creation of an arbitration board maybe an 
option to explore especially if this allows decisions to be taken within a short timeframe 
thus preventing delays/blocking of shipments. 
 
2. Restrict the export of EU waste to third countries – support instead a globally- led 
certification platform for recycling to enhance the uptake of the Circular economy 
for plastics

For the circular economy to become reality it is important that recoverable waste be 
regarded as a resource if they can be safely recovered. The Basel Convention on the 
control of transboundary movements of hazardous wastes allows for the export of mixed 
plastic waste for recovery to third countries under the prior and informed consent 
procedure and in compliance with the principle of environmentally sound management 
(ESM) of waste. The WSR should align with the Basel Convention and allow for the 
shipment of recoverable plastic waste to third countries since this will enhance recovery 
of waste and support the uptake of the circular economy. 
 
It is also crucial that waste be traded through properly controlled channels and recycled 
in facilities operating to quality, environmental and safety standards. With respect to this, 
we support the development of a new procedure in the WSR which would allow waste 
resources to move more freely, subject to strong quality conditions such as for example 
to certified facilities.  
 
It’s not appropriate for the EU to implement controls in non-EU countries (such as 
inspections and certification of waste management facilities in non-EU countries). A more 
appropriate mechanism would be a globally-led system of ESM (e.g. a certification 
platform for recycling facilities either run via a fully independent and global organisation 
that sets the rules to audit and verify practices in relation to waste shipments would also 
provide for reliable traceability, certificates and statistics or/and a standardisation body 
such as ISO), rather than setting up an EU agency and the Basel Convention has the 
appropriate instruments to provide technical support and capacity building to third 
countries to help them implement the Basel Convention.  
 
 
3. Strengthen the enforcement of the Waste Shipment Regulation’s provisions – fully 
support strengthening compliance with the WSR and eliminating illegal activities 
 
Strengthening the enforcement of the WSR across the European member states is 
essential to stop illegal shipment of waste within and beyond the EU the achievement of 
the objectives mentioned above, and crucial to the prevention. PlasticsEurope therefore 
welcome this objective and support the proposed measures aimed at strengthening 
compliance with the WSR.

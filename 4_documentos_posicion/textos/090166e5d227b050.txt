Feedback on Impact Assessment of Waste Shipment Regulation - Regulation 1013/2006 
The goal of the European Green Deal is for the EU to become climate neutral by 2050. The new Circular 
Economy Action Plan is one of the most important corner stones to reach this goal. The new CE Action Plan 
takes a life cycle perspective aiming to keep valuable resources in the economy as long as possible.  
PolyStyreneLoop highly welcomes this approach. PolyStyreneLoop is an initiative by the entire polystyrene 
foam value chain bringing together over 70 members and supporters across 18 EU countries. Together we 
recycle polystyrene foam demolition waste containing the flame retardant HBCD, which is classified as POP 
waste. Our solvent-based purification technology allows the isolation of the HBCD from the polystyrene. 
Over 99% of the polystyrene is thereby recycled. The bromine in the HBCD is also recovered while the HBCD 
is safely destroyed. PolyStyreneLoop thus closes the polystyrene and bromine resource loops. We are 
strong advocates of bringing the circular economy into action.  
As the PolyStyreneLoop plant, which will be operational as of Q2 2021 in the Netherlands, is dependent on 
polystyrene foam demolition waste coming from across Europe, we welcome the impact assessment on 
the waste shipment regulation (WSR) especially with regards to facilitating recycling of waste and reducing 
the burdens of the WSR.  
Based on our current insights and discussions with regards to the transport of polystyrene foam demolition 
waste containing HBCD across the border, we foresee the following three bottle necks which we would like 
to bring to your attention. First, we would welcome a clear link in the WSR to the POP regulation with 
regards to the concentration as of when a POP waste needs to undergo notification procedure. To date this 
leaves still ambiguity and therefore uncertainty. Second, while PolyStyreneLoop functions as a pre -
treatment in which the POP is isolated from the remaining waste, we would welcome an explicit clarification 
that a POP-isolating pre-treatment plant (like operated from 2021 on by PolyStyreneLoop) can already be 
recognized as target plant for cross border waste shipment (like planned for polystyrene foam demolition 
waste containing HBCD ). Finally, we would like to ensure that the waste treatment operated by our 
PolyStyreneLoop plant is EU-wide uniformly classified as a recovery operation (R3) by the respectively 
competent waste authorities to facilitate the transport of waste across the border.  
We see the risk that if the above-mentioned legal uncertainties are not dismantled, the transport of 
polystyrene foam demolition waste containing HBCD across the border and therefore the recycling of this 
waste stream is endangered which will negatively impact bringing the circular economy for polystyrene and 
bromine in action meaningfully.  
We welcome a dialogue to further explain our position.

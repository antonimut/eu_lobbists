RREUSE feedback: Waste Shipment Regulation 
– Public Consultation 
RREUSE is an international network representing social enterprises active in re -use, repair and 
recycling. RREUSE members currently divert around 1 million tonnes of goods and materials on an 
annual basis from landfill through re-use, repair and recycling.  
RREUSE welcomes the proposal for a regulation on Waste shipments and supports the idea that, to 
speed up the transition towards a circular economy, the EU should prevent the export of valuable 
products outside the EU.  The social enterprises that RREUSE represents already follow this strategy 
by prioritising local re-use over exportation of reusable waste items collected from the waste stream. 
This also concerns products collected from donations.  
However, because of the saturation of the market with new, cheap and low-quality products, as well 
as the lack of staff and financial means to sort out the totality of the collected waste items, it is difficult 
for re-use social enterprises to maximise the proportion sold locally. To extend the lifetime of second-
hand products that cannot be sold locally, exportation remains the best solution, even if compare to 
local recycling. For example, textiles collected in Europe have a local re -use potential of 5 - 15 % and 
a further international re-use potential of at least 30 – 40 %. Another 30 - 40 % is destined for recycling 
and 10 – 15 % must be disposed of at the cost of collectors. The social and economic impacts of these 
exportations ar e positive on the receiving countries while exporting countries can reach their 
preparing for re-use objectives.   
Most reuse social enterprises are small and medium enterprises potentially having difficulties to 
understand and comply with the WSR, especia lly given its non -harmonised interpretation among 
member states. The different rules concerning the end-of-waste status makes the whole process even 
more complicated, with uncertainties concerning the nature of the products exported. RREUSE is 
therefore particularly in line with the approach taken by the Commission “to further harmonise the 
implementation of certain procedures and provisions and measures to reduce unnecessary regulatory 
and administrative burdens”.  
In order to encourage the preparing for r e-use and re -use of waste and products locally, RREUSE 
advises the Commission to take the following steps in the implementation of the WSR, as well as other 
circular economy and trade policies which relate to the exporting of waste: 
● Develop a transparent, user-friendly, harmonised and reliable way for exporters of waste, 
being large or small companies, to identify the importers that are respecting the waste 
legislation and are able to prepare for re-use or safely recycle waste. 
● Given the environmental benef its of preparing for re -use instead of recycling, prioritise 
exportation for preparing for re-use over local recycling.  
● Harmonise end-of-waste criteria in order to clarify what has to be exported as waste or as 
products. Inspiration can be found in Austria where end-of-waste criteria for prepared for re-
use items have been recently developed.

● Prevent the use of the repair loophole (more info on this here). 
To conclude, RREUSE encourages the Commission to limit the exportation of potentially reusable 
waste products to the strict minimum by improvin g the design of products put on the EU market, 
making waste collection points fit to collect reusable products, and encouraging consumers to buy 
second-hand via preferential taxation. The intense marketing of new products on the EU market is the 
main reason why the illegal exportation of waste is happening.   
RREUSE remains available to discuss any of the points made in this feedback paper.

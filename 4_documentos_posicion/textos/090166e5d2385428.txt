Consultation: Reducing Plastic Packaging 
 
Feedback on the review of the requirements for packaging and other measures to prevent 
packaging waste 
August 2020 
 
Chemical recycling is one of the key solutions contributing to the circular economy of plastics. In 
addition to increasing the recycling rates of some plastics currently not being recycled, it is creating 
recycled content of virgin-quality which will be essential to fulfil the strong demand incentivized by the 
EU Plastic tax, the Circular Economy Action Plan or the EU Green Deal. This move toward a circular 
economy will reduce oil extraction necessary for the creation of virgin plastic, and reduce GHG in line 
with the Paris Agreement.  
 
Chemical Recycling Europe represents the chemical recycling technologies necessary to enable the 
conversion of plastic waste into new chemical substances and/or products, supporting the circular 
economy for plastics and complementing traditional mechanical recycling.  
 
We strongly support the Commission’s initiative to prevent packaging waste, promote recycling, and 
increase and incentivize the use of recycled content and its quality.  
 
We would suggest the Commission to work along side the industry and platform such as the Circular 
Plastic Alliance (CPA) as its works overlap with the current goals and work necessary for this initiative.   
 
Design for recyclability should create a level playing field for recycling technologies and 
materials choice 
• To revise the packaging design and recyclabili ty, this review needs to account for all 
technologies for recycling and innovation, this includes chemical recycling. Some packaging 
can be recycled through chemical recycling currently but not through mechanical recycling. 
Without accounting for chemical recycling, increase in recycling rates will be limited. Taking 
the recyclability based only on traditional existing infrastructure would close some opportunity 
and disincentivize investment in new technologies for recycling.  Chemical recycling can 
indeed enable some flexible packaging for instance to keep their functionality, lightweight, 
while still being recyclable. Chemical recycling still has some specifications and we encourage 
the Commission to work together with Chemical Recycling Europe to ensure t he packaging 
design is adapted to all recycling methods.  
• These revisions on packaging design and recyclability should align with the application of eco-
modulation fees to all recyclable materials, no matter the technology used. This would create 
a level p laying field in terms of recycling methods and optimize EPR fees directed towards 
recycling and packaging material recyclable according to the new assessment.  
• These two previous points would effectively shift the plastic packaging from a waste to a 
valuable resource and incentivize circular solutions. 
• Finally, a holistic LCA and general impact analysis will be essential for companies to assess 
packaging from the intersection of variables including recyclability, climate change, 
functionality & safety. Recyclability should not be considered independently of the other factors 
in the choice for packaging material. 
 
Harmonization in recyclability and collection will be key to support the recycling industry

• Packaging directive essential requirements harmonization across EU countries and clarity will 
support recycling provide clarity for the private sector and investors to invest in appropriate 
sorting and recycling infrastructure. 
• However, this harmonization should go hand -in-hand with an harmonization of collection. If 
collection is partial, it then will not be adapted to the sorting and recycling of all plastic 
packaging. The harmonization of collection, starting with the collection of all plastic packaging 
waste will be essential to harmonized and improve sorting and recycling. Recycling solutions 
will develop faster if they know there is a clear market for a specific technology, and they can 
secure the packaging waste. In addition, if efforts are made on design by packaging producers, 
we should ensure that these are collected, sorted properly to ensure it is recycled at its highest 
possible grade. 
 
Development of policy and methodologies for recycled content to ensure the good functioning 
of the internal market 
• Effective and harmonized EU end-of-waste criteria application across countries will ensure the 
strengthening of the internal market for waste plastics and secondary raw materials.  
• The development of adapted measurement methodology for the recycled content from 
chemical recycling will be crucial to the development of this promising recycling method. We 
would suggest the Commission to work with the us, the chemical recycling industry, as well 
as with the chemical and plastic industry on the use of the Mass-Balance Approach.   
• Finally, the official confirmation that recycled content from chemical recycling are suitable for 
food-grade application will increase availability of virgin -quality recycled content, currently in 
high demand with new plastic taxes and circular economy action plan.

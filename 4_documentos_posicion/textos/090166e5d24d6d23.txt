European Paper Packaging Alliance 
Response to the Inception Impact Assessment for the  
Review of the Packaging and Packaging Waste Directive 
 
 
 
 
The European Paper Packaging Alliance (EPPA) is a not-for-profit food and foodservice packaging 
association.  EPPA’s priority is to provide efficient environmental , low carbon  and health-safe 
products to the European population, with improved recycling solutions.  
 
EPPA supports the efficient use of resources in all its forms and recognizes the major role that 
packaging, as an essential infrastructure, can play in that common objective.   
 
The Alliance believes it is important that the review of the Packagi ng and Packaging Waste 
Directive delivers in three areas: 
 
1. The critical role of packaging ’s functionality , including maintaining product safety and 
hygiene, reducing food waste and loss, enabling transportation and supporting consumer 
accessibility to food. 
Summary 
 
The European Paper Packaging Alliance requests that the Inception Impact Assessment:  
 
• Supports a more precise and detailed description of: 
o Packaging’s functionality; including product quality, safety, hygiene and 
accessibility, which play critical roles.  
o The importance of upholding policy and operational integrity of the Internal 
Market in order to avoid fragmentation and conflicting market consequences. 
o Any effects on policy coherence and consistency, with particular emphasis on 
Green Recovery, net-zero and safety and hygiene. 
• Supports an end of life definition of ‘waste’ for materials as understood in the Circular 
Economy model, which promotes recycling, value-retention and economic well-being.  
• Correctly a pplies the Waste Hierarchy so that final ‘waste’ is identified and can be 
reduced avoiding unintended consequences, which result in lower environmental 
performance. 
• Assesses reusable options on a systems basis, including raw materials, production, 
logistics, sanitation and operability, to quantify the full environmental impact. 
• Assess policy options , inclu ding using an evidence-based environmental impact 
assessment, to ensure they are not contrary to the Green Recovery and a net -zero 
economy, and do not harm European innovation and investment. 
• Incorporates formal opinions from the European Food Safety Authority and the 
European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control on policy options which may affect 
food safety and hygiene.

• EPPA believes it is fundamental that policy options must not place product safety and 
hygiene at risk, and that the European Food Safety Authority and the European Centre 
for Disease Prevention and Control should be consulted as part of the policy process.  
 
2. Maintain the policy and operational integrity of the Internal Market in line with Article 
114 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. 
 
• EPPA therefore welcomes the Commission’s recognition that harmonization of the rules 
on packaging can support sustainability.  The Alliance trusts that the PPWD review will 
address existing barriers (including infrastructure): “The amendment to Directive 
94/62/EC on Packaging and Packaging Waste is based on Article 114 of the Tre aty on 
the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU). It is necessary to strive for full 
harmonization of rules on packaging across the internal market to preserve its integrity 
and allow for a smooth free movement of packaging and packaged goods. 
Uncoordinated national measures to address sustainability aspects of packaging result 
in obstacles to the free movement of goods and hinder the development of markets for 
secondary raw materials.” 
 
3. There should be policy coherence and consistency. 
 
• EPPA believes it is critical that policies should not conflict and that impact assessments 
should be holistic and evidence-driven, ensuring wider objectives are incorporated.  For 
example, ensuring that policies suppo rt innovation and the objectives of a Green 
Recovery and a net-zero-carbon economy are not jeopardized. 
 
Objectives and policy options 
 
EPPA notes that the real issue in relation to packaging ‘waste’ is  too narrow a focus of what 
‘waste’ is, which the Alliance considers is contrary to its understanding of circularity: “In a 
circular economy, the value of products and materials is maintained for as long as  possible. 
Waste and resource use are minimized, and when a product reaches the end of its life, it is used 
again to create further value. This can bring major economic benefits, contributing to innovation, 
growth and job creation.”1 
 
In our view,  it is fundamental that the Waste Hierarchy is correctly applied so that the use of 
materials is optimized, and true ‘waste’ is identified and can be reduced.  The Alliance therefore 
believes article 4 of the Waste Framework Directive must be fully and rig orously applied in the 
impact assessment and in any strategy aiming to reduce packaging waste.   Within this , both 
“underpackaging” and “overpackaging” needs to be clearly defined.   
 
Again, policy options should not deliver outcomes which may be contrary to the Green Recovery 
and a net-zero economy.  This includes overly prescriptive requirements for packaging design, 
which harm innovation and therefore investment in Europe. 
 
Impact assessment 
 
In addition to the areas identified in the inception document, EPPA would request that the 
following areas are considered:

Likely economic impacts 
• The financial impact on industry should  be included in the assessment, with sufficient  
consideration given to any impacts on investment and innovation. 
• The impact on product costs, including food pr ices, should be assessed – including 
establishing the cumulative impact of related policies e.g. extended EPR fees, EU and 
national taxes and levies, national charges as a result of the SUPD implementation 
• Product and food waste prevention should be taken into account, being the most 
efficient ways to both improve wider resource efficiency and to reduce the specific 
environmental impacts of packaging. 
 
Likely social impacts 
• EPPA believes it is important that the impact on food prices, and the opportunity costs 
resulting from any restrictions on accessibility are fully assessed.  In particular, the 
impacts on lower income groups should be examined to ensure there are no negative 
impacts on the ‘Just Transition’ foreseen by the European Commission in the Green 
Recovery programme. 
• The association believes it is important that that policy options must not place product 
safety and hygiene at risk, and that the European Food Safety Authority and the 
European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control should be consulted as part of the 
policy process . When considering the potential mandatory use of recycled materials 
hygiene and food safety aspects must not be compromised.  
 
Likely environmental impacts 
• EPPA welcomes the focus on the impact on food waste. 
• The Alliance believes the waste hierarchy should be used, especially when considering 
the overall impact of reusable packaging systems, addressing the production, use and 
disposal phases of a complex lifecycle. 
• Assessments should also include consideration of the raw materials that are used for 
packaging, especially where raw materials themselves can mitigate against climate 
change. The benefits of renewable raw materials should be recognized and promoted.   
 
EPPA welcomes the opportunity to continue to engage with policymakers on the review of the 
requirements for packaging and other measures to prevent packaging waste; and to ensure 
proposals avoid harmful unintended consequences.

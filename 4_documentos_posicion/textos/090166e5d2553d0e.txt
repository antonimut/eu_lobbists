 
 
Kotkamills position on Review of the requirements for packaging and other measures to prevent 
packaging waste - Ares(2020)3041578 
Kotkamills is made of three separate but united business lines working together as a forerunner of 
the circular economy. Kotkamills Wood produces high quality timber products and deliver raw 
materials as a by-product for other businesses. Absorbex Oy is a world leading producer of 
saturating base kraft utilising saw dust and recycled fibre as a raw material. The Consumer Boards 
businesses serve the consumer packaging industry with plastic free AEGLE and ISLA products which 
are designed for easy recycling.  
To provide excellent protection for food products, the properties of AEGLE and ISLA boards can be 
enhanced for high grease and liquid resistance, with water-based, aqueous, dispersion barriers. 
Environmentally-sound food packaging and disposable cups, which meet all necessary food and 
safety requirements, are therefore a conscious, sustainable choice for the food industry.  
Kotkamills warmly welcomes the Commission’s ambitious Green Deal and its targets for reducing 
emissions, promoting safe and sustainable chemicals, European Forest Strategy and further 
developing the circular economy. By creating a comprehensive and clear guideline for Circular 
Economy, the Commission released its Circular Economy Action Plan in spring of 2020, which 
included high ambitions for packaging and packaging waste.  
We believe packaging plays a major part in modern society and is heavily dependent on feed stocks 
that are renewable, easily recyclable and/or biodegradable. As part of the Commission’s circularity 
drive, the essential requirements for packaging, listed in Annex II of Directive 94/62/EC, are currently 
being reviewed.  
In order to ensure the bio-based economy continues to play an important role in producing 
sustainable, circular packaging in Europe, Kotkamills would like to highlight the following 
recommendations for improving the essential requirements of the Packaging and Packaging Waste 
Directive:  
• Create clear, standard-based and technology-neutral definitions for biodegradability and 
compostability of packages. We are pleased to see this is recognised by the recent Eunomia 
report on the effectiveness of the requirements of the PPWD as a main area that needs 
strengthening, and that they push for the consideration of compostable packaging in certain 
applications;  
• Create clear and predictable standards for what is considered recyclable and taking into 
account recycled fibres and their benefits;  
• Clear definitions between words plastic and polymer.  
 
Sustainable and safe chemicals also play a major role in packaging. Actions against plastics are 
extremely welcomed, but it cannot be replaced by materials that create unsustainable or potentially 
hazardous impacts to human health. Therefore, we also wish to highlight the following:  
• Fluorinated chemicals have unknown impacts to human and animal health. We therefore 
advocate for the introduction of regulatory measures to reduce the use of fluorinated 
chemicals within the PPWD or during the upcoming revision of the Food Contact Materials 
Ref. Ares(2020)4137178 - 06/08/2020
 
regulation. We welcome efforts by countries like Denmark to restrict their use and 
encourage the EU to make this Union-wide.

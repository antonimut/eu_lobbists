EXPRA’s feedback on the EU public consultation on “Reducing 
packaging waste - review of rules” 
06 August 2020 
This paper outlines EXPRA’s views on the Commission’s consultation on “Reducing packaging waste 
- review of rules”, as part of the revision of the Packaging and Packaging Waste Directive (PPWD – 
Directive (EU) 94/62 amended by 2018/852).  
Regulatory context 
Recital 21 of the PPWD (Directive (EU) 2018/852) highlights the need to review the Essential 
Requirements (ER), as laid out in Directive 94/62/EC, in order to boost prevention of packaging waste, 
reduce its environmental impact and promote recycling materials of high quality, while also 
safeguarding the functioning of the internal market, avoiding obstacles to trade and  preventing 
distortion and restriction of competition. 
Article 9(5) of the PPWD further states that the European Commission shall, by 31 December 2020, 
“examine the feasibility of reinforcing the essential requirements with a view to, inter alia, improving 
design for reuse and promoting high quality recycling, as well as strengthening their enforcement”. 
In a ‘circular economy’, waste is reduced and goods are re -used and recycled as much as possible. 
This means that packaging waste should also be reduced, and packaging should be made easier to 
recycle. 
The Commission will review the requirements on packaging and packaging waste in the EU. This will 
include assessing how to: 
• improve packaging design to promote reuse and recycling 
• increase recycled content in packaging 
• tackle excessive packaging 
• reduce packaging waste. 
General remarks 
The Extended Producer Responsibility Alliance (EXPRA), representing industry -owned, non-profit 
packaging and packaging waste recovery and recycling organi sations, welcomes the opportunity to 
provide feedback on packaging waste prevention and essential requirements as part of the EU 
consultation on the PPWD review. The comments  below address EXPRA’s views and main policy 
asks with regards to measures aimed at reducing the generation of packaging waste by increasing 
packaging circularity as well as  updating and improving the essential requirements and their 
enforcement. 
EXPRA supports prevention goals  within the PPWD , which the essential requirements should 
promote by, inter alia, reinforcing packaging design circularity. 
Packaging waste generation represents less than the 4% of the total waste generation, of which 80% 
fed circular economies (67% via recycling and 13% via recovery) in 20171.

We believe that packaging has an important role in meeting the objectives of the Green Deal as well 
as climate neutrality targets through material efficiency and reuse/recycling . However, we deem it 
important that any measure proposed as part of  the PPWD review is proportional to the “problem”  
and cost-effective, avoiding increased complexity, or additional burden, for the industry.  
Regarding Waste Prevention  
EPR organisations not only contribute to meeting the ambitious EU objectives on packaging and 
packaging waste recycling and recovery ; they already undertake and support  packaging waste 
prevention measures adopted both at national level and across the EU. 
As part of said rem it, EXPRA members develop a wide range of activities focused on preven ting 
packaging waste at source, including promoting reuse . They also place great value on  packaging 
design, collection and sorting infrastructure as a means to minimise the environmental impact of both 
the product and its packaging. Relevant measures include: 
- Developing packaging prevention plans at national level; 
- Devising tools and LCA studies on packaging design to incentivi se waste prevention based 
on “packaging functionality”; 
- Undertaking information and communication activities to promote waste prevention at national 
and local level; 
- Gathering data on packaging quantities put on the market as well as on recycling and reuse. 
This information lays the foundation for  developing eco-design tools that assess the 
environmental impact of packaging. 
One example of EXPRA activities is the “Packaging for Recycling” (P4R)  tool. The project was 
developed by EXPRA’s Sustainability and Packaging Working Group and aims to raise awareness of 
recyclability, informing of innovative packaging trends and strengthening the link between packaging 
producers and recyclers. The tool allows users to self -assess the recyclability of packaging via a 
roadmap based on several critical points (design, labelling, collection and sorting). More information 
about the project can be found here: https://www.packaging4recycling.eu/  
Further information on waste prevention activities carried out by EXPRA members can be found here.  
Assessing carefully the increase in packaging waste generation 
EXPRA has contributed largely to the study carried out by Eunomia to support the Commission in the 
revision of the PPWD 2. The study points out to the increase in packaging waste generation despite 
the decline in weight of individual packaging units.  
The study identifies a number of factors responsible for such increase, which EXPRA believes are 
not supported by scientifically sound data. In particular, the study fails to take into account important 
factors, such as the functionality of packaging, changes in demographics to 1-person households and 
shifts in consumer behaviour towards on-the-go consumption, which are an explanatory condition for 
the fact that there may be more packaging per kg than before. 
Therefore, we recommend the European Commission ensures that the identified trends in packaging 
waste generation are carefully assessed and its conclusions supported by scientifically  sound data 
and analyses. Furthermore, they should not propose measures that are not beneficial for the 
environment when considering the packaging an d the product together. LCA methodologies based 
on CEN standards are best suited for this purpose.

Regarding the Essential Requirements  
EPR organisations already play an important role in implementing the  essential requirements to 
improve packaging design. In this regard, packaging optimi sation efforts are a priority: these  are 
directed to  minimise the life-cycle environmental impact  of packaging  while guaranteeing its 
functionality vis-à-vis the product  it is aim s to protect . In addition, EPR organisations promot e 
environmentally-friendly packaging design, recyclability and reusability. 
While welcoming  the Commission’s work on the revision of the essential requirements, EXPRA 
believes that it is crucial to adopt a measurable, documented approach that follows a “plan, do, check, 
act” rationale and that addresses the following issues. 
• Headline requirements 
EXPRA considers that the concept of “packaging functionality” should be at the core of the new 
approach, meaning that the overall environ mental performance of packaging , including that of the  
product it contains, should be duly assessed. Failing this, the application of recyclability and reusability 
would have undesired effects: it would lead to market barriers for packaging that performs better from 
an overall environmental perspective, but which is less recyclable or reusable. This would also have 
an impact on the way EPR fees are modulated.  
In addition, while the existing essential requirements and their accompanying CEN standards may no 
longer be entirely fit for purpose, we do not believe that they should be removed. Instead, these should 
be revised in order to better refle ct existing needs and realities, and subsequently better enforced. 
The structure of CEN standards (which should remain harmonised) should also enable producers and 
importers of packaged goods to set up an administrative system, making use of environmental tools 
such as LCAs to evaluate various effective and functional packaging alternatives and their 
environmental performance. 
• Definitions of recyclable and reusable 
First of all, EXPRA believes that it is important that any definition of recyclable or reusable 
distinguishes between what is considered cost-effective and lower cost. This should crucially avoid to 
link the legally-binding essential requirements with EPR fee modulation guidelines, especially as fee 
modulation is not related to packaging design alone, but also to the design of the (local) packaging 
waste management system, EPR investments and market trends.  
Instead, EU guidelines for eco-modulation should remain general at this stage and adapted once the 
essential requirements had been agreed upon and integrated into EU legislation. 
Definition of recyclable 
With regards to the definition of recyclable, EXPRA believes that a “technical, qualitative”, rather than 
quantitative, harmoni sed standard should be adopted. The introduction of a generic definition, 
instead, already accounts for the fact that practical interpretation will vary from one Member State to 
another. 
Packaging is recyclable if its successful post-consumer collection, sorting and recycling is proven to 
work in practice. A quantitative threshold system, as proposed by E unomia’s study on Essential 
Requirements, not only is unclear but also inapplicable, given that the recycling process and the 
recycling rates are based on materials or material mix and not on packaging types or formats. 
Overall, any definition of recyclability should be fully aligned with the definitions and provisions set out 
in the Waste Framework Directive and Decision 270/2005, as amended in 2019 for PPWD recycling

target reporting. In general, any solution propose d that is not coherent with current legal provisions

When defining “high quality” recycling, the European Commission should not link this to a threshold 
related to the number of use-cycles. In practice, what material is of high quality is defined on the basis 
of end-product applications and their market value. As such, adopting a requirement based on use -
cycles would be neither effective nor feasible. 
Definition of reusable 
With regards to the reusable definition, EXPRA believes that the European Commission should avoid 
setting arbitrary thresholds which would undermine the very rationale of reusability. Any assessment 
determining reuse alternatives (environmentally and economically -viable solutions) must take into 
account, inter alia, product and consume r safety, and conducted according to LCA methodologies  
based on CEN standards. 
• Enforcement and reporting requirements 
We support the European Commission’s objective to improve the application and enforcement of the 
essential requirements. However, it is key that enforcement and reporting requirements do not place 
additional burden on the industry or on the system in general. 
For this reason, we would, in principle, support a proposal to self -certify producers or fillers via an 
online compliance form or the implementation of  harmonised CEN standards. However, given the 
administrative burden on producers, we believe that compliance alternatives, such as accrediting 
specific tools aimed at assessing packaging sustainability (certification tools), should also be explored 
as a complementary step to self -certification. These could automatically assess the conformity with 
the essential requirements as part of wider packaging sustainability assessment. 
Some EPR organisations, including EXPRA members, support enforcing essential requirements via 
recalling CEN harmonised standard3 compliance and requirements in the implementation of different 
prevention programmes as well as in fee modulatio n requirements. Worth mentionin g, too, are the 
several guidelines drafted to support the obliged compani es to comply with the  essential 
requirements. More details are available here: http://www.expra.eu/en/Sustainability/sustainability-
drops-news-from-expra-members 
Limiting the role of EPR organi sations regarding essential requirements ’ enforcement to fee 
modulation alone will undermine the efforts and results achieved so far as well as any further potential 
improvement of EPR services and drivers for reaching common goals.

About EXPRA 
EXPRA is the Extended Producer Responsibility Alliance – the organisation for packaging and packaging waste recovery 
and recycling systems which are owned by obliged industry and work on a not -for-profit basis. EXPRA acts as the 
authoritative voice and common policy platform representing the interests of its members, which are all founded and run by 
or on behalf of obliged industry. O ver the past 25 years, our members across 20 countries thereof 17 EU Member States 
have co-organised the collection, sorting and recycling of used packaging (with a focus on household packaging) on behalf 
of industry, to fulfil their legal take back and recycling obligations, serving over 200 million inhabitants and recycling over 20 
Million tons of packaging per year.

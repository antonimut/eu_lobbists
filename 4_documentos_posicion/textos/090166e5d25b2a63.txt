 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
OCEANA’S RESPONSE TO THE CONSULTATION ON THE REVIEW OF THE 
REQUIREMENTS ON PACKAGING AND PACKAGING WASTE IN THE EU 
 
 
August 2020 
 
 
Oceana is the largest international organisation dedicated solely to the conservation and 
defence of the oceans. Oceana focuses its efforts on restoring marine richness and biodiversity 
around the world and on implementing science-based policies in countries that control one third 
of the world's fish catch. Our campaigns are producing results, as shown by more than 200 
victories in the fight against overfishing, habitat destruction and pollution, and supporting the 
conservation of endangered species such as turtles and sharks. Restoring the oceans would 
mean that a billion people could enjoy healthy fish today, tomorrow and forever.  
 
Oceana supports measures intended to prevent waste and encourage reuse. In line with our 
campaign efforts to minimise single-use plastics reaching the ocean, we would like to draw 
attention to two specific aspects that should be considered in relation with the Packaging 
Directive: 
 
 
1. Reduce e-commerce plastic packaging 
 
The Packaging Directive must take into account recent changes in consumer shopping 
behaviour. E-commerce has increased over the past years, and the COVID-19 pandemic has 
forced millions of people into lockdown, further increasing e-commerce sales
i. Part of this 
increase is expected to consolidate in the near future, which will have a significant impact in the 
expansion of packaging. 
 
EU citizens should use their purchasing power to influence the sustainability of global e-
commerce practices. In this line, all products purchased in the EU should contribute to EU and 
national objectives to reduce single-use plastics, regardless of the country where the retailer is 
headquartered.  
 
Background: Oceana’s findings 
 
Public opinion researchii sponsored by Oceana in the United States, Canada and the United 
Kingdom has shown that Amazon customers are buying more online due to the COVID-19 global 
pandemic. They are overwhelmingly concerned about plastic pollution and its impact on the 
ocean, and they want major online retailers including Amazon to give them plastic-free 
packaging choices. Our findings for the three countries are very consistent and we believe that 
these concerns are likely to be shared by EU citizens.  
 
YouGov found that for the Amazon UK customers surveyed: 
• 94% were concerned or very concerned about plastic pollution and its impact on the 
ocean and the environment;

• 89% said they either strongly or somewhat agreed with the statement, “I would use a 
plastic-free choice/alternative packaging if offered;” 
• 61% strongly and/or somewhat agreed with the statement, “the coronavirus (COVID-19) 
outbreak has forced me to shop online more than I did before the virus;” and 
• 56% strongly and/or somewhat agreed with the statement, “I'm bothered by all the extra 
plastic packing I am now receiving due to increased online shopping because of the 
coronavirus (COVID-19) outbreak.” 
 
In 2019, according to news accounts, Amazon shipped approximately 7 billion packages 
worldwide– which would be equivalent to nearly one package for every person living on earth. 
These shipments are often packaged with plastic, which can end up in the oceans and devastate 
marine life. 
 
E-commerce companies have alternatives to plastic readily available. They can move fast when 
legislation starts being implemented, as they do in India, for example. Amazon has removed non-
recyclable plastic packaging from fulfilment centres in India, and is also testing reusable crates 
for delivery of packages there. In the United States, Amazon is already using reusable packaging 
for their food deliveries to Prime customers. Amazon’s packaging and materials lab has already 
created lightweight plastic-free paper packaging, including a new mailer that the company 
reports to have been used 100 million times. It is time for EU legislation to catch up and require 
sustainable alternatives to plastic packaging by e-commerce operators selling in the single 
market. 
 
 
Oceana recommends:  
• Extended Producer Responsibility schemes to guarantee that e-commerce companies 
carry the full costs. caused by the plastic they use, throughout their lifecycle.  
• An obligation to report the amount of packaging used annually by e-commerce 
operations, per country and material. 
• A ban on the use of non-recyclable plastic for the e-commerce industry. 
• Clear industry-wide reduction goals for plastic packaging, with the goal to phase out 
plastic packaging. 
• An obligation to implement e-commerce delivery systems based on reusable packaging. 
• An obligation to increase the information and decision power of consumers during the 
purchasing process, including: 
o Allowing consumers to easily choose a plastic-free packaging option, without 
increasing the retail price.  
o Information on how many times the packaging can be recycled into the same 
packaging material before being downcycled. 
o Information on where the packaging should be disposed of for its correct 
recycling, based on the recipient’s country infrastructures (e.g. recycling centres, 
yellow bin, etc.), and the environmental impact of improper disposal. 
 
 
 
2. Quotas for reusable bottles  
 
Deposit Return Schemes (DRS) and refillable bottles should be strongly promoted to minimise 
the amount of waste products reaching the ocean. Increasing the use of recycled plastic is a 
helpful measure but it is not enough, as it does not prevent bottles, cups and other recipients 
 
   
 
from being dumped and ending up in the sea. Once these reach the seabed, removing them 
becomes difficult, costly or simply impossible.  
 
Oceana considers that DRS discourage improper disposal, and that they are aligned as well with 
the goals of the Single-Use Plastics Directive. 
 
Background: Oceana’s findings 
PET bottles are one of the items most commonly found as plastic debris in the ocean. Reusable 
bottles for soft drinks are widely used in Germany, in most Latin American countries and some 
Asian countries like the Philippines. Because of their high rate of reusability, refillable bottles 
also have lower carbon emissions per litre.iii  
 
Oceana analysed packaging market data for the non-alcoholic beverages industry, the market 
share of PET bottles, and plastic marine pollution data in 76 coastal countries around the world. 
Our analysis
iv found that between 21 and 34 billion PET bottles become marine pollution every 
year, out of 445 billion litres of non-alcoholic beverages sold in PET bottles.  
 
We also found that increasing the market share of glass and PET refillable bottles by only 10% in 
all coastal countries (replacing single-use bottles) could reduce PET bottle marine plastic 
pollution by 22%. This would keep 4.5 billion to 7.6 billion PET bottles per year out of the ocean. 
A 20% increase in refillable market share of glass and PET bottles (replacing single-use PET 
bottles) could reduce marine plastic pollution by 39%, keeping 8.1 to 13.5 billion PET bottles out 
of the ocean every year, based on 2018 data. 
 
Surprisingly, European Union countries, where consumers are thought to be more environment-
conscious, are at the lowest end of the refillable spectrum, with some of the lowest market 
shares for refillables globally. Finland has only 2% market share of refillable soft drink bottles; 
France, Sweden, and Ireland have only 3%; Denmark, the Netherlands, and Greece 4%; Romania 
and Portugal 5%; and Spain 6%. 
 
Some of the largest Coca Cola bottlers in the world have already turned around and now work 
actively to increase their market share for refillable bottles. Coca Cola Femsa, the largest Coca 
Cola bottler in the world, shared in its second quarter earnings call in July 2020 that it increased 
its share of refillable bottles to 45% in Mexico (from 35%) and to over 20% in Brazil (which was, 
the company noted, a 25% increase) in the midst of the COVID-19 pandemic. This shows that 
reusable bottles are regarded a safe and viable solution by the soft drink industry and by 
consumers, even during the pandemic.  
 
Oceana recommends: 
• To implement and strictly enforce a share of 70% refillable bottles at individual producer 
and at and retailer level.  
• To establish or raise mandatory container deposits, including establishing different 
deposit levels for refillable and single-use bottles.  
• Implement fees on single-use bottles.  
• Require the use of standardised bottles to simplified logistics for refillable bottles.

SITA  welcomes the opportunity to provide feedback to the European Commission’s public 
consultation on the impact assessment on possible revised rules on Advance Passenger Information 
(API). 
 
SITA fully supports this revision and as an organization that helps hundreds of airlines and dozens of 
governments on all continents deliver, process and analyze passenger data, we remain willing  to 
contribute and assist the Commission accordingly. We agree with the Commission’s objectives to 
‘ensure effective processing of API data with clear rules and transparency ’. Based on this and the 
desired outcomes of harmonization, interoperability, improving data quality, driving innovation and 
facilitating legitimate travelers, we make the following observations: 
 
1. Scope of API Data Collection 
Experience in other countries has shown that widening the scope of API data collection to include 
‘inter-state’ travelers has helped improve border security, reduce illegal immigration and prevent or 
detect criminality. As noted, with the increasing demand for passenger data around the world, and on 
the basis that “one nations outbound is another nations inbound”, we would recommend controlled 
and cooperative sharing of API data between nations, minimizing the financial and operational impact 
on carriers. We note that several Member States have chosen to implement the PNR Directive for 
Intra-Schengen flights. Any expansion in API data collection must of course be balanced with the free 
movement rights enjoyed within the EU. 
 
General and Business Aviation has long been a gap in the passenger data for nations and remains a 
risk in terms of border control evasion, criminality and smuggling. Whilst operating procedures and 
systems are very different to scheduled carriers, technology solutions already exist to support their 
submission of API. The Commission should make itself aware of these solutions during any 
subsequent assessment phases. 
 
In line with many other countries, collecting traveler information for all border crossings, including 
Land, Maritime and Fluvial borders, represents a significant step in closing security gaps. We would 
therefore recommend expanding data collection beyond the aviation sector to close these gaps and 
help prevent those with ill intent from seeking rail or ferry services as the perceived “weaker border”.  
 
2. API Errors and Data Quality 
We recognize and support easyJet’s feedback that 100% error-free collection and submission of API 
data is not possible. Further, that fines should only be imposed as a last resort and when there is a 
failure to cooperate. SITA recommends a collaborative approach between government and carriers to 
establish and maintain submission of API. This should include a “feedback” process where data errors 
(including lack of submission and/or receipt), should be notified to both carrier and government as 
near to real-time as possible to allow for resolution or rectification.  
 
  
 
 
3. Technical Solutions 
There are many technology providers assisting carriers and governments in the submission and 
processing of passenger data. Indeed, some carriers have effectively outsourced this obligation to 
service providers. We would recommend that the Commission work with these service providers to 
understand what existing technology can be used to more quickly and efficiently assist the community 
in improving data acquisition. Similarly, machine learning and AI techniques are being used to ident ify 
potential data quality errors and assist in their rectification.  
 
In response to the Covid-19 crisis, governments have found that using interactive API (iAPI) solutions, 
with real-time analysis and denial of boarding capability, has enabled them to ra pidly protect their 
borders and citizens. Examples of this include Australia and New Zealand utilizing their deployment of 
Advance Passenger Processing (APP) iAPI. In addition to the IATA/WCO/IATA Guidelines and Annex 
9 to the Convention on International Civil Aviation, APP (the most widely used interactive API 
standard in the industry) has been evolved over more than 20 years in consultation with many 
Government agencies to become a stable and fully encompassing format that fulfils the needs of 
diverse Government stakeholders and is well established and supported in the Industry. This format 
also supports all document types, modalities and associated checks such as VISA checks. The use of 
the APP standard would provide optimum coherence across industry, Governments, and modalities. 
 
Interactive API solutions are now in place in approximately 20 countries and evidence shows that the 
quality of the data they receive is significantly better than that in traditional or batch API. We would 
strongly recommend the Commission consider the extended use of iAPI solutions, particularly as both 
EES and ETIAS are expected to have an interactive data element. Carrier impact will be minimized 
and take-up quicker if existing solutions are adopted. 
 
Implementing APP saves the industry money as noted in the Audit Report from the Australian 
National Audit Office (https://www.anao.gov.au/sites/g/files/net616/f/ANAO_Report_2005 -2006_34.pdf) 
which quotes: “The APP system has benefited all parties. It provides greater border security, it 
resolves many issues around the ‘credentialing’ of passengers before they arrive at the border and it 
has reduced the cost to airlines and shipping companies of arrivals without proper documentation. For 
example, in 2000–01 fines totalled around $23m but are estimated to be around $3m in 2006 –07.” 
 
Supporting the need to minimize the technical, financial and operation impact on carriers, we would 
encourage the Commission to review existing technical solutions, rather than creating new systems.

INTRODUCTION 
UNESPA (Spanish Association of Insurers and Reinsurers) appreciates the opportunity to comment 
on the European Commission Inception Impact Assessment on the 2020 Review of Solvency II.  
UNESPA is the representative body of 195 private insurers and reinsurers that stand for approximately 
the 96% of the Spanish insurance market. Spanish insurance undertakings generate a premium 
income of more than € 65 bn, directly employ 48.000 people and invest more than € 324 bn in the 
economy.  
UNESPA, as member of Insura nce Europe has taken part in all Insurance Europe documents linked 
with this project and firmly supports the Insurance Europe position on this matter. Our comments 
should be considered together with the European industry position.  However, in addition to t he 
Insurance Europe position, we would  like to highlight the following issues included in the Inception 
Impact Assessment. 
KEY MESSAGES 
Long-term guarantee measures. 
One of the long-term guarantees (LTG) measures which was introduced in the Solvency II framework 
was the matching adjustment (MA). This LTG measure has proven to be a very powerful and valuable 
tool which has provided many benefits for policyholders, and insurers. It has also benefitted the wider 
financial system through ensuring the supply of long-term capital and mitigating systemic risks.  
The MA provides good risk management incentives that eliminate or substantially mitigate some 
market risks (interest  rate risk, reinvestment risk, spread risk and liquidity risk). MA matching 
requirements also promote undertakings to monitor ALM and ensure that asset and liability cashflows 
are closely matched. 
EIOPA, in its opinion on the 2020 review of Solvency II, su pports the retention of the MA framework 
within the Solvency II framework as a separate and distinct measure which recognises the ability to 
hold assets to maturity that are not exposed to real forced -selling risk and the rigorous asset liability 
management (ALM) techniques that insurers employ to manage these portfolios.  
EIOPA also proposes to remove the restriction on diversification between MA and non -MA portfolios 
in the standard formula.  Removing the restriction will ensure a level playing field, impr ove 
transparency and comparability and avoid unjustified constraints to the availability of long -term 
guaranteed insurance products and the ability of insurers to make long -term investments (for those 
insurers who meet MA requirements). 
The MA has enabled insurance undertakings who apply the measure to continue to offer guaranteed 
insurance products, such as annuities, which are highly valued by consumers and are a valuable 
product offering given the demographic trends in Europe. It has also permitted insurance undertakings 
to continue in their role as long -term investors. This enables MA insurers to provide capital to long -
term financing. 
Preserving the ability of the insurance industry to provide long-term guarantees should be a priority of 
the review. The demand for guaranteed products remains very high on average at EU level.  
A recent Insurance Europe pan -European pension survey, interviewing 10 000 citizens a cross 10 
members states, clearly confirmed a strong appetite for certain features typically offered by insurers 
including guarantees. By far the highest priority when saving for retirement was the security of the 
money invested (60% of respondents). Survey  respondents also overwhelmingly chose investment 
safety over performance of investments (73% of respondents).

Proportionality 
The principle of proportionality, one of the overarching principles of the Treaty on European Union, is 
a vital element of the Solvency II Directive that should be kept and improved within the framework and 
applicable for all the undertakings irrespective of their size. The proper application of the principle of 
proportionality should be the tool, the solution that helps insurance  undertakings not to bear 
unnecessary costs of regulatory requirements that are disproportionate compared to their risks.  
The principle or proportionality applied in practice, without undermine Solvency II concepts and 
policyholder protection, gives enough  room to calculate in a simpler and flexible way (on a case by 
case basis) risks that are not significant for insurance undertaking. 
However, from the entry in force of Solvency II there has not been an optimal application of 
proportionality in practice, a situation that has not prevented insurance undertakings from 
unnecessary complexity and costs. 
Sometimes National Supervisory Authorities could consider any simplification or deviation from legal 
texts as not permitted if they are not explicitl y mentioned in the regulation. However, the purpose of 
the proportionality principle is precisely to be able to over -ride some specific detailed requirements 
when they are disproportionate in relation to the objective sought.  
It should be made clear in th e legal texts that supervisors are required to consider and allow, and in 
fact facilitate, deviations from the specific texts based on the application of the proportionality principle, 
when justified by the insurer’s scale, nature and/or complexity. This s hould be made clear in the 
Solvency II Directive, for example in Article 29 “General principles of supervision” but also in the 
respective National Supervisory Authority Regulation. 
Threshold for exclusion from Solvency II (article 4 from the Directive 2009/138/EC) 
This issue should not be considered as proportionality in strictly manner since it´s a pure exclusion of 
companies from the regular Solvency II requirements according the thresholds established in the 
Directive. Those insurance undertakings are r egulated under the national framework established in 
their respective Member State. 
 
UNESPA considers that same risks should lead to same rules, something that ensures the same level 
of protection to policyholders and beneficiaries. However, if heterogeneous national regimes coexist 
in Europe UNESPA is not firmly convinced that the same level of protection could be achieved.  
Having said that, UNESPA considers that the current thresholds for the application of Solvency II are 
suitable however we do not oppose to update them according to inflation criteria. On the other hand, 
UNESPA consider that those insurance undertakings that are out of the scope of Solvency II should 
publicly disclose this fact in the same way that companies under Solvency II have to disc lose a huge 
amount of information in the Solvency and Financial Condition Report. 
Climate and environmental risks 
UNESPA would like to point out the need for Solvency II, as a prudential framework, to remain risk  
based and avoid imposing investment incentives. Only if there is evidence that E, S, G factors impact 
the risk profile of an investment, could these elements be reflected in the regulatory framework.  
 
For instance, according to the EIOPA Opinion on Sustai nability within Solvency II (EIOPA-BoS-
19/241) there is no meaningful difference in the risk profile of sustainable equities investments 
compared to other equities. EIOPA also stated that depending on additional data which may become 
available, including on brown assets, it may be possible to better differentiate between the risk profiles 
of assets based on their sustainability characteristics, but at a later date.

Cross-border activities and IGSs 
As our European Federation (Insurance Europe) appreciates, UNESPA confirms that IGSs currently 
in place vary significantly across EU but work generally well within their local context and laws. Some 
Member States, as is the case in Spain, currently have arrangements equivalent to an IGS that protect 
policyholders, whereas other Member States do not have an IGS but consider that policyholder 
protection is nevertheless sufficient. In any case, we deem that national authorities are in a better 
place to know and understand the implications of a local insurance company entering into problems 
in their markets. Consequently, they must be able to organise themselves in the most efficiently way. 
Nevertheless a “home country control principle” should be applied any way: the authority responsible 
for the supervision of the insurance company being winding up should be the one paying “the bill” at 
the end. 
 
We would like to insist that are some IGSs in place (as is the case in Spain) that have being operating 
properly during decades. It will be unreasonable to force a change in them if they have been protecting 
policyholders appropriately until now. Hence, the solution finally decided on this area should respect 
it.  
 
UNESPA also believes that, as regards cross -border tran sactions, information and collaboration 
between home and host supervisors should be reinforced and improved. Based in our proper 
experience, in the case of the so called “empty shells” (where a significant part of the overall business 
of the insurance or r einsurance undertaking, in the meaning as provided for in  Article 354 of the 
Delegated Regulation, is carried out in the form of a branch operations or FoS activities in the Host 
Member State), a specific early warning alert system might be established.

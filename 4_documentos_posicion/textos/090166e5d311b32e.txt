ISEAL feedback to the EC roadmap on ‘Strengthening the role of 
consumers in the green transition’ & ‘Substantiating green 
claims’ 
 
ISEAL Alliance is the global membership organisation for credible, collaborative, and 
transparent sustainability systems. ISEAL currently brings together a range of different voluntary 
sustainability standards which manage reliable labels or b2b claims. At the core of our 
membership is commitment to a common set of principles and concrete practices that ensure 
ISEAL members are credible and create positive sustainability impact.  
We welcome the EU’s initiatives on ‘empowering consumers’ and ‘substantiating green claims’. 
ISEAL was established to address many of the challenges identified in the roadmaps. Therefore 
we call on the Commission to; 
• Strengthen existing best practices in terms of managing and substantiating sustainability 
claims based on ISEAL principles and resources. In particular, the ISEAL Codes of 
Good Practice, should be used as a reference point. 
We believe the legislative initiatives on ‘empowering consumers’ and ‘substantiating green 
claims’ can be effective and impactful if they;   
● Provide a mix of measures that incentivizes credible claims in the marketplace but do 
not lead to a restrictive environment which can limit the overall awareness and 
availability of information on sustainability issues. We argue that blacklisting certain 
‘greenwashing’ terms is unlikely to be effective and there is a need to assess each claim 
on whether it is substantiated or not.  
● Avoid that EC policies on consumer information and green claims have a narrow focus 
on climate or environmental issues, as these are often directly related to socio-economic 
challenges and broader sustainability goals 
● Ensure a focus on all types of sustainability claims placed on products and services, 
including those based on, for example, in-house company progammes. 
● For the empowering consumer agenda; 
○ We recommend building on existing initiatives that provide clear information to 
consumers on which labels and claims are trustworthy and credible. Examples at 
national level (Siegelklarheit in DE and Keurmerkenwijzer in NL), and 
international level (ITC Standardsmap). Using new technology and partnerships

with ‘enablers’ (supermarkets, online retail) are important to make this 
information easily available and actionable by consumers. 
● For the substantiating green claims agenda 
○ we recommend that the Commission assesses how the product environmental 
footprinting methods and rules can complement and integrate existing, credible 
standards and certifications. 
○ ISEAL is committed to support to the Commission in defining what a credible and 
market-worthy sustainability claim should be, and share insights into how this can 
be assessed 
In addition, we want to emphasize that; 
• ISEAL code compliant members are independent organisations that manage their labels 
to provide reliable and robust information. This is based on their independent assurance 
systems (ie. verification and certification procedures), and traceability or chain of custody 
systems that allow for accurate, product specific claims. 
• Through this, ISEAL members have been effective in shaping and enabling consumer 
choices and driving market-driven sector transformation. 
• ISEAL members can support the Commission to work torwards it’s goals under the 
circular economy and climate agenda, as credible sustainability standards are effective 
tools to not only reduce the environmental and climate footprint of material and products, 
but also to shift consumers preferences towards more sustainable choices, covering 
both environmental and social considerations. 
• ISEAL members and their independent labels are widely referenced and used by EU 
governments in their own purchasing and procurement policies. They are considered the 
most credible and time-efficient tool to demonstrate that requirements are being 
addressed. 
Furthermore, we wish to stress that different private standards and certifications can effectively 
coexist and complement each other. In any given industry or product category, a combination of 
different standards, methodologies, and tools is necessary to achieve sustainability goals, and 
diverse incentives are needed at different points in production and supply chains. A single label 
approach is unlikely to practically and effectively address such complex realities across all 
industries, product categories, and sustainability goals, or satisfy all consumer preferences. 
Despite this, there is a need to focus on harmonized, joined-up approaches that provide clearer 
sustainability information, and ISEAL and it’s members are keen to support the Commission on 
developing these.

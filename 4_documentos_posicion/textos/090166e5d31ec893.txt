Empower	 customers	 to	 make	sustainable	choices	 through	transparency		September	2020			H&M	Group’s	initial	contribution	to	the	European	Commission’s	legislative	initiative	to	empower	customers	for	the	green	transition		H&M	 Group	applaud	the	 European	 Commission’s	 ambition	 to	enable	customers	across	the	 European	 Union	to	choose	more	sustainable	products	based	on	trustworthy,	clear,	comparable	and	harmonized	information.			We	share	the	European	Commission’s	objective	to	address	a	lack	of	common	effective	enforcement	at	European	level,	 as	 well	 as	customers’	confusion	due	 to	a	 multiplication	 of	 labels	 and	 claims.	We	 believe	the	 European	Commission	should	also	aim	at	rebuilding	customers’	trust	in	sustainability	claims.			We	believe	the	policy	initiatives	driven	by	the	European	Commission	at	various	level	could	drive	positive	change	taking	into	account	the	following	principles:			- Ensure	one	 common	and	 consolidate	 transparency	framework	 and	 approach	at	 the	 European	level	encompassing	product,	brand	and	value	chain	transparency	initiatives;	- Consider	both	social	and	environmental	dimensions	while	empowering	customers;		- Design	 the	legislative	initiatives	 keeping	 at	 their	 core	 the	principles	 of	 comparability	 and	building	customers’	trust	in	the	system	to	be	defined;	- Build	 on	fashion	industry’s	 experience,	research	and	 testing	campaign,	 as	 well	 as	sectorial	initiative	(such	as	the	Higg	Index	suite	of	tools1)	to	drive	customers’	sustainable	choices.			H&M	Group’s	transparency	vision	and	achievements			H&M	Group’s	vision	is	to	use	our	size	and	scale	to	lead	the	change	towards	circular	and	climate	positive	fashion	while	 being	 a	 fair	 and	 equal	 company.	 We	 want	 to	 make	 sustainable,	 good	 quality	 fashion	 available	 to	 as	 many	people	as	possible.			At	 H&M	 Group,	 we	consider	transparency	 as	 a	 driving	 force	for	 change.	Transparency	 contributes	 to	 our	sustainability	vision	in	three	important	ways:		- It	will	enable	informed	customer	 choice.	Connecting	customers	to	the	story	 behind	 our	 products,	 so	 they	can	make	choices	aligned	with	their	values	and	be	inspired	to	shop	more	sustainably;	- It	will	build	trust	in	public	opinion	on	brands	and	industries’	claims	related	to	sustainability	performance;	- It	will	accelerate	sustainable	change	in	the	textile	industry.	Sharing	our	processes,	plans	and	performance	to	identify	joint	challenges	and	synergies	with	our	peers,	and	accelerate	more	sustainable	practices.			Transparency,	 through	 trustworthy,	comparable	and	 clear	 information,	can	 empower	 customers	 to	make	sustainable	choices	for	the	environment,	people	and	communities.			At	H&M	Group	we	are	walking	the	talk:	- Supplier	 disclosure:	We	 were	 one	 of	 the	 first	 to	 publicly	 disclose	 all	 our	 suppliers	 in	 2013,	and	 are	 now	disclosing	all	our	suppliers’	factories	and	around	400	factories	in	tier	2;	- Corporate	disclosure:	We	have	been	ranked	in	the	top	5	companies	by	the	Fashion	Transparency	Index2	for	the	past	years.	We	have	been	rewarded	the	top	position	for	our	transparency	efforts	in	2020;	- On-Product	 Transparency,	 i.e.	 comprehensive	 disclosure	of	 information	 at	 product	 level:	Lately	 we	focused	 on	 one	 solution	 to	 disclose	 all	 at	 once	 product,	 value	 chain	 and	 brand	 information	 to	 customers	through	the	so-called	on-product	transparency.	Our	brands	H&M,	H&M	Home	and	ARKET	now	share	details	such	as	production	country,	supplier	names,	factory	names	and	addresses	as	well	as	the	number	of	workers	in	 1 https://apparelcoalition.org/the-higg-index/ 2 The	Fashion	 Transparency	 Index	rates	how	 transparent	 brands	 are	 with	 policies,	 goals,	 suppliers	 etc.	It	 reviews	200	 of	 the	 biggest	 global	fashion	brands	and	retailers	ranked	according	to	how	much	they	disclose	about	their	social	and	environmental	policies,	practices	and	impact.

The	following	policy	recommendations	are	based	on	the	experience	and	lessons	learnt	throughout	the	past	years.				Create	more	legal	certainty	for	both	businesses	and	customers			We	recommend	 the	 European	 Commission	 to	address	 misleading	sustainability	 claims	 through	option	 1:	amending	 the	 Unfair	 Commercial	 Practices	 Directive	(UCPD)	by	developing	 a	guidance	 document.	This	would	provide	clarity	in	the	short	term	on	to	develop	sustainability	claims	and	so	rebuild	customer’s	trust	in	the	information	received	while	purchasing	their	goods.			Ambitious	 criteria	to	 communicate	sustainability	claims	shall	 be	 developed	 in	 collaboration	 with	 the	industry.		Existing	tools	and	labelling	schemes	that	meet	these	criteria	(e.g.	the	Higg	Product	Module	-	Higg	PM	and	 Higg	 Brand	 and	 Retail	 Module	-	Higg	 BRM)	 should	 be	 listed	 in	 the	annex	 to	 the	 guidance	 document.	Using	these	 criteria	in	 consumer	 communication	 have	 the	 potential	 to	 drive	 informed	customers’	 choices,	 as	 well	 as	acknowledge	leading	companies	and	products.			One	comprehensive	transparency	framework		• A	common	understanding	of	transparency		To	 ensure	 a	 successful	 outcome,	 the	 European	 Commission	 should	clearly	 differentiate	 transparency	and	traceability	throughout	the	legislative	process	and	in	the	final	policy	to	be	developed.			At	H&M	Group,	we	define	both	concepts	as	follow:		o Traceability	is	not	transparency	but	the	act	of	knowing,	about	finding	out;	to	know	where	our	fibre	comes	from	and	who	was	involved	in	making	our	products	crating	visibility	to	the	company	internally.	It	helps	the	company	to	take	more	informed	business	decisions.		o Transparency	as	the	act	of	sharing	and	disclosing	information	relevant	for	a	specific	audience	in	an	open,	accurate	 and	accountable	manner,	 with	 the	 purpose	 of	 creating	 trust,	 enabling	 the	 audience	 to	 make	informed	decisions	and	scaling	sustainable	impacts.		We	recommend	 the	 European	 Commission	to	refer	to	 transparency	 as	 defined	 above	and	 place	 it	at	 the	core	of	the	European	Commission’s	initiative	to	empower	customers	for	the	green	transition.			• A	comprehensive	and	inclusive	approach		We	welcome	the	European	Commission’s	high	priority	to	transparency	through	the	various	legislative	initiatives	launched	 in	 2020	for	 green	 claims	and	empowering	customers.	We	 also	 understand	transparency	will	 be	addressed	 under	 the	due	 diligence	initiative	and	 the	 revision	 of	 the	 non-financing	 reporting	 directive.	 To	drive	positive	change,	we	urge	the	European	Commission	to	ensure	a	strong	collaboration	and	alignment	between	services	to	 establish	one	common	framework	for	sustainability	 transparency	building	on	these	policy	initiatives.			Such	 framework	should	address	 transparency	 from	 a	global	 angle	–	including	both	 social	 and	 environmental	dimensions,	as	well	as	product	and	value	chain	transparency.					To	 ensure	 a	 global	 outreach	 and	 a	 long-term	 change,	 a	 common	 measurement	 for	 all	garment	and	 footwear	products	 is	 needed	 to	 allow	 customers	 to	 compare	 in	 a	 consumer	 friendly	 and	 accessible	 way.	Such	measurement	 methodology,	 and	 its	related	 communication	vehicle,	 shall	 be	 scalable	 and	 usable	 by	 all	types	of	companies	and	in	all	sub-sectors	of	the	garment	and	footwear	industry	to	ensure	the	possibility	for	widespread	adoption	and	comparability	for	customers.

Build	on	fashion	industry’s	experience	At	 H&M	 Group,	 we	 believe	 in	an	 industry	 benchmarck	 driving	 impact	by	 enabling	 brands,	 suppliers	 and	customers	to	consciously	choose	who	they	do	business	with	and	what	they	buy.			The	garment	and	footwear	industry	has	been	gathering	experience	on	sustainability	claims	and	the	best	tools	to	deliver	 them	 through	 the	 European	 Product	 Environmental	 Footprint	(PEF)	pilot	 and	 transition	 phase	 (since	2013)	 as	 well	 as	 the	 Higg	 Index,	suite	 of	 tools	supporting	brands,	 retailers	 and	 manufacturers	 to	 integrate	sustainability	 and	 circular	 economy	 into	 their	 daily	 activities.	The	outcomes	 of	 these	industry	experiences	shall	be	takeninto	account	to	drive	impactful	transparency.			These	 projects	 provided	 clear	conclusions	 and	 directions	 on	 the	 type	 of	 information	 customers	 needed	 and	wanted	to	receive	and	through	which	specific	communication	vehicle3.	Based	on	these	studies,	and	on-going	tests,		H&M	Group,	together	with	the	Sustainable	Apparel	Coalition,	is	developing	an	on-product	transparency	approach	aiming	at	a	 scalable	tool	 for	 the	 whole	 industry,	 providing	 relevant	 information	 from	 a	 customer	perspective	in	an	accessible	way,	with	the	Higg	Index	Transparency	effort.	It	will	enable	informed	customer	choices	will	lead	to	positive	sustainability	impacts	in	supply	chain,	design	and	consumer	habits.			Conclusion		H&M	 Group	support	the	European	 Commission’s	will	 to	enable	 transparency	 and	 empower	customers	 to	 make	sustainable	 choices.	The	 legislative	initiative	for	 “Empowering	 the	 consumer	 for	 the	 green	 transition”,	 well-coordinated	 with	 the	 one	 regarding	“Substantiating	 green	 claims”,	 will	 represent	the	 right	 foundation	 for	ambitious,	comprehensive	and	EU-wide	legislative	framework.			We	look	forward	to	contributing	to	the	creation	of	this	level	playing	field	to	drive	transparency	in	the	industry.

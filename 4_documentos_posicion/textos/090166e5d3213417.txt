Comment of the European Heating Industry as input for the Roadmap 
regarding the ‘Review of EU rules on fluorinated greenhouse gases’ 
As manufacturers of heating equipment containing fluorinated gases (F-gases), members of 
the European Heating Industry (EHI) are affected by the provisions of the current European 
F-gas Regulation (517/2014). EHI member companies develop  and put on the market  
innovative, highly energy efficient space heaters, such as heat pumps, which are essential for 
the decarbonisation of the entire building stock in Europe. 
Heat pumps for space and/or water heating usually operate with F-gases. EHI members invest 
significant amounts in the development of new efficient products, which use alternative or low-
GWP (Global Warming Potential)  refrigerant gases, to contribute to the phase down of F -
gases, and which use electricity for heating in a highly efficient way, thereby  cutting CO2 
emissions. 
Heat pumps used for space heating or domestic hot water production are key technologies for 
the decarbonisation of buildings and help to achieve the EU’s 2030/2050 climate targets. 
Alongside other highly efficient heating technologies, heat pumps should be  promoted to 
replace the old and inefficient stock of heating equipment across Europe. Whilst most heat 
pumps use hydrofluorocarbons (HFCs) to operate, their benefits for reducing CO2 emissions 
far outweigh any global warming effect linked to direct F-gas emission. Firstly, thanks to their 
very high efficiency . Moreover, because F-gas circuits are tight and are installed and 
maintained by trained installers (i.e. F-gas accredited specialists); therefore, their leakage rate 
is very low. Finally, at end of life, heat pumps are in scope of the WEEE regulation which also 
requires the end of life recovery of gases including F-gases. 
While we recognise the importance of phasing down the use of F -gases across Europe, in 
accordance with the European Green Deal objectives, any potential revision of the current F-
gas regulation should not harm the development of heating and domestic hot water heat 
pumps – precisely to attain the Green Deal objectives. In other words, the transition towards 
lower- or even ze ro-GWP refrigerant gases should not outweigh the objectives of reducing 
CO2 emissions from energy use, required to achieve the EU Green Deal objectives. 
As regards the planned review of the European F -gas Regulation, EHI would also like to 
highlight the following points: 
o Alternative refrigerant gases with a low er GWP should ensure that today and future 
products achieve or exceed the current and future energy efficiency requirements of 
Ecodesign and relevant policies (i.e. EPBD). 
o A high level of safety should be ensured through strict application of EU safety directives 
(i.e. Low Voltage Directive, Machinery Directive, Pressure Equipment Directive and 
General Product Safety Directive), preferably combined with EU harmonized standards 
updated according to international standards and adequate training of installers and 
maintenance companies, also for non HFC-refrigerants. 
o Lastly, the increasing use of alternative refrigerants should avoid having a counter effect 
on market price levels of heat pumps and/or installation and maintenance costs for the

About the European Heating Industry 
The European heating industry brings together companies that are leaders in the production 
of efficient heating systems. Our members produce advanced technologies for heating in 
buildings, including: heating systems, boilers, heat pumps, burners, components and system 
integrators, radiators, surface heating & cooling. For tomorrow’s changed energy system, our 
members are innovating in hybrid and digital solutions. They develop high -efficiency and 
renewable-based systems. These innovations will improve Europ e’s homes and our 
environment. EHI members employ directly more than 125.000 people in Europe and invest 
over 1 billion euros a year in energy efficiency.

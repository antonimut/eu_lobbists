Siemens fully supports the European Commis-
sion’s 2020 Green Deal for climate action, sus-
tainable production and conservation of natu-
ral resources by investing in environmentally -
friendly technologies and supporting industry 
to innovate. 
 
Siemens develops its products, s olutions and ser-
vices using a lifecycle perspective and sound eco -
design standards. 
Modern electrical power distribution systems uti-
lizes state -of-the-art metal encapsulated switch-
gear. Those provide low transmission losses by 
high voltages, conserve resources by small dimen-
sions and ensure a reliable and stable power net-
work in Europe, all at the same time. Moreover, 
the existing technology ensures personal safety 
for operators and availability of electricity supply 
for citizens, industry and  transport across the en-
tire EU. 
 
SF6 is under regulatory control and covered – 
among many other fluorinated substances – by 
the Regulation (EU) No 517/2014 on fluorinated 
greenhouse gases. 
In the electricity sector SF 6 is also widely used in 
high-technology electrical distribution installa-
tions. European manufacturers are delivering 
leading technology equipment, ensuring mini-
mized SF6 emissions during the entire lifecycle . 
Nevertheless, SF 6 emissions from electrical equip-
ment also contribute to the CO 2 equivalent emis-
sions in Europe, but as a  minor contributor. 
The training of personnel handling SF 6, already in-
itiated in the repealed F -gas regulation (EC) No 
842/2006 has considerably risen the awareness of 
keeping SF 6 in a closed cycle and allowed to re-
duce the emission of SF 6 significatively. But also, 
further obligations of regulation (EU) No 
517/2014 helped to reduce SF 6 emissions. Im-
provements are still possible, in particular in the 
area of emissions monitoring and product’s label-
ling. 
According to the Annual European Union green-
house gas inventory 1990-2016 and inventory re-
port 2018; Annex V, Europe’s overall CO 2 equiva-
lent emissions added up to 4,300 Mt in 2016,  
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
whereof 2.83% were caused by all kinds of fluori-
nated greenhouse gases.  
SF6 from all electrical and non -electrical appli-
cations contributed to 0.16% (6.8 Mt CO 2 equiva-
lent) of Europe’s overall CO2 equivalent emissions. 
Specific SF 6-emissions from electrical transmis-
sion & distribution (T&D) equipment were re-
ported to 94 t SF 6 (2.14 MT CO 2 equivalent) acc. 
to the study of German Federal Ministry for Envi-
ronment, Nature Conservation and Nuclear Safety 
and German Federal Environment Agency, con-
ducted by Ecofys/ETH Zurich (“Concept of a SF 6 
free transmission & distribution of electrical en-
ergy”)2. Those emissions decreased over the years, 
now representing less than 0.05% of Europe’s 
overall CO2 equivalent emissions.  
Generating an easily interpretable image: In 2016 
approximately 225 million cars ran in the EU-28. If 
those do not exceed the vali d CO 2 exhaust limits 
in average, the entire T&D industry’s SF6 emissions 
equal to less than 0.5% of this cars population 
(based on a mileage avg. 15 000 km/year and car). 
 
Despite of its low impact to the overall CO 2 
equivalent emissions, the European elec trical 
industries committed itself to take actions to 
further minimize SF 6 emissions from equip-
ment and to take research and development ef-
forts for SF 6-free alternatives.

Siemens AG is furthermore prepared to contrib-
ute actively in the consultation phase  for any 
recast of the European F -gas regulation  and 
likes to address three topics that will have addi-
tional impact on SF 6 emissions: 
1. In those countries that adopted an additional 
voluntary self -commitment and  a target on 
emission reduction measures, the reduction of 
SF6 emissions from the 2.G.1 sector 1  was sig-
nificantly higher than in other EU countries. We 
recommend implementing the content of 
these well -working voluntary self -commit-
ment into a recast of the EU F -gas regula-
tion2. 
Those are e.g. measures by manufacturers and 
asset owners to limit emissions, establish selec-
tion criteria for power transmission & distribu-
tion (T&D) switchgear equipment, data collec-
tion and targets for SF 6 application in electrica l 
T&D equipment , as well as a detailed monitor-
ing and reporting on applied SF 6 and emis-
sions. 
2. Reclaiming – also for re -use during mainte-
nance procedures and for recycling - of the 
SF6 is mandatory , both during any mainte-
nance procedures and at the equip ment’s end 
of life. Although this obligation has already 
been introduced in the F -gas regulation (EC) 
No 842/2006, its national implementation shall 
be closely monitored, also due to the fact that 
that fines for violating the F -gas regulation ob-
ligations d iffer significatively different be-
tween EU countries.  
Depending on the voltage level, the region and 
the number of the switchgear units managed 
by the asset owner, the reclaiming obligation’s 
implementation may vary. It shall be consid-
ered that SF 6 equipment, started being in-
stalled in larger scales beginning of the mid 
1980’s, is now reaching its end -of-life- Me-
dium-voltage hermetically sealed products en-
able a designated closed loop recycle process 
without gas release into the atmosphere.

Innovation follows a path towards a viable, 
market-oriented portfolio.  
A first 5-years phase 1 consists of:  
(a) Basic development to get initial solutions,  
(b) selected application developments which 
then are piloted with clients/end users.  
Followed by a minimum 5 -years phase 2: 
(c) Main variants development and start of an in-
dustrialization process,  
(d) customized portfolio extensions,  
(e) homologation processes  with clients/end us-
ers, 
(f) application developments by system integra-
tors. 
Especially the medium-voltage switchgear vari-
ants developments, the customer -specific portfo-
lio extensions and its industrialization for series 
production require much more time a nd effort 
than known from typical high -voltage (>52 kV) 
applications, namely typically more than 5 years.  
For e.g. medium -voltage secondary switchgear 
there are no customer “standards” in Europe, even 
not for Ring-Main Units (RMU), how to arrange its 
various functions, how to connect to the network 
lines, how to arrange the transition to the power 
user, for its rated performance, etc., This results in 
thousands of different design variants that will 
have to be qualified (type tested) and mapped to 
a sound series production.  
Considering those facts, Siemens supports a 
regulation driving a stepwise SF 6 phase-down 
in medium-voltage equipment, that is aligned 
with the technological progress and industrial-
ized availability of a broad, market -oriented 
and market -accepted portfolio.  This progress 
shall be reviewed and assessed at regular inter-
vals. 
 
 
Step 1: Stepwise phase-down of medium -voltage 
SF6 (primary) equipment in high-voltage/me-
dium-voltage substations and large industrial 
applications up to 24 kV  starting 2022 until 
2027. Stepping into SF 6-free technologies is facili-
tated by a broader availability of SF 6-free alterna-
tives and the potential to “mix” specific SF 6-free 
switchgear with existing SF 6 equipment in case of 
replacements or extensions.  
Step 2: Stepwise phase-down of medium -voltage 
(secondary) SF 6 equipment in medium-volt-
age/low-voltage substations in urban and rural 
areas (e.g. Ring Main Units) up to 24 kV starting 
2025 until 2030. In Europe, 12 kV application only 
cover a small niche market, therefore a separate 
12 kV regulation is not adequate.  
Step 3: Stepwise phase-down of medium-volt-
age SF6 equipment for >=36 kV for all applica-
tions starting 2030.  
Exemptions shall be considered for specific niche 
applications and installations where replacement 
or extension by SF 6-free switchgear alternatives is 
not feasible (e.g. due to performance exigencies, 
space restrictions or by replacing just a cubicle 
within a larger installation) or if homologation 
processes with clients/end users are still in pro-
cess.

Conclusion and outlook  
Electrical equipment manufacturers have been 
working successfully for years to reduce SF 6 emis-
sions in its products and to research for alterna-
tives. Products and pilot installations with alterna-
tive extinguishing and insulating gases are already 
available. 
The only sustainable path is avoiding  fluori-
nated g ases or gas mixtures  (see also study of 
Fraunhofer Institute IEE and Grenoble School of 
Management of May 2020: “Impact assessment of 
F-gas free medium voltage GIS applications”, 
www.f-gas-free.eu) and using vacuum switching 
technology with new design prin ciples as the 
technical optima, which shall be established as a 
new mainstream and industry standard.  
In order to support Europe’s ambition of climate -
neutrality by 2050, all F -Gases (or mixtures) with 
a Global Warming Potential (GWP) greater than 1 
should be examined equally in view of its obliga-
tions and consequently they should be listed in 
the F-gas regulation annex 2.  
F-gas free  technologies require additional ef-
forts and material to compensate dielectric 
properties of fluorinated gases, however, at 
the same time, provide the market a positive 
and distinct path forward over the entire prod-
uct lifetime , including the end -of-life treatment. 
SF6-free solutions, completely renouncing fluori-
nated gases or gas mixtures, are now possible.  
All SF6-free solutions today serve limited applica-
tions in medium -voltage distribution networks 
and in the lower segment of the high -voltage 
transmission system. To cover the full range of SF6 
insulated products and systems, significant prod-
uct development and gaining long-term experi-
ence in operation is required.  
 
A clear, effective regulatory framework and eco-
nomic support for development and a staggered 
market introduction should be the goal for or-
dinary applications  of new SF6-free technologies 
from 2022 onwards. Economic concerns of manu-
facturers and operators must be considered, par-
ticularly since clear market signals are currently 
missing for a complete, cost -intensive, complex, 
time-consuming overall industrialization of a port-
folio and for a further portfolio d evelopment ap-
plication by application.  
Doing this, the leading industrial position of Euro-
pean T&D equipment manufacturers, that also ex-
port outside EU, will have to be taken in consider-
ation, ensuring employment in the EU which is 
also an important aspect  of European Commis-
sion’s Green Deal. Any regulatory framework 
shall avoid jeopardizing the leading position of 
this industry sector in Europe .  
 
To avoid delays for uncertainty and therefore sup-
porting EU to become climate -neutral by 2050, 
this signal should come as soon as possible.

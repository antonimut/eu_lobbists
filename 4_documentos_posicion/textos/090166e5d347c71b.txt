JRAIA response to F-gas Regulation Roadmap / Inception Impact Assessment 
 
The Japan Refrigeration and Air Conditioning Industry Association (JRAIA) represents companies 
that are active in developing, producing and marketing air conditioning and refrigeration products 
and heat pumps. Appliances can sometimes also be used for vent ilation and heating purposes. 
JRAIA member companies have been active in Europe for decades, and are obviously very 
committed through significant European investments in research and development, production 
and distribution.  
JRAIA very much welcomes the elaborate legislative process that has started off with the making 
available of the combined Evaluation Roadmap/Inception Impact Assessment.  
JRAIA has actively and constructively contributed to the process that led to the adoption of the 
current 2014 F -gas Regulation. We fully support and are committed to attaining the goals and 
objectives of both, the 2014 EU F -gas Regulation in terms of EU HFC phase d own, and the 
Montreal Protocol in terms of EU HCFC phase out.  
We are of the opinion that the 2014 F -gas Regulation is working well in the light of HFC phase 
down. Additional GWP regulations would not, therefore, be vital in the form of affecting the 
regulated HFC phase down schedule before 2030. It is pivotal to exploit the full potential of the 
current EU F-gas Regulation without any further changes. Regarding “seeking alignment with the 
Montreal Protocol”, which is one of the objectives and policy options of EU Commi ssion, JRAIA 
suggests that the Commission execute a thorough assessment comparing carefully EU F -Gas 
targets and Montreal Protocol targets as baseline and contents are not the same. 
At the same time, JRAIA welcomes the Roadmap’s identifi cation of implementation problems 
which this legislative initiative aims to deal with. We would like to mention important 
implementation problem s which seriously hampers the uptake of lower -GWP refrigerants  and 
proper use of the quota system. Illegal trade, improper use, and the recurring problem of national 
building codes that makes use of A2L refrigerants all but impossible will arouse concerns.  
Finally, a higher uptake of energy savings technologies, such as heat pump technology, will help to 
curb CO2 emissions. In addition, enhancing recovery, recycling and reclaim of HFCs will contribute 
to alleviating the future strain on the HFC availability caused by EU HFC phase down.  
JRAIA is very much looking forward to providing more details in upcoming consultations.

About JRAIA 
The Japan Refrigeration and Air Conditioning Industry Association (JRAIA) was originally established in 
February 1949 as the Japan Refrigerating Machine Manufacturers Association which was thereafter 
reorganized in February 1969 to become an incorporated association and renamed as it is at present. 
JRAIA is the industry association representing over 160 manufacturers of refrigeration and air conditioning 
equipment in Japan. We, the members of JRAIA, have so far been dedicated to offering quality products to the 
markets of EU. JRAIA aims to promote and improve production, distribution and consumption of refrigeration 
and air conditioning equipment and their applied products, as well as auxiliary devices and components, 
automatic controls and accessories and thereby contribute to the steady development of Japanese industry 
and the improvement in people's standard of living. 
For more information, please see JRAIA’s website: www.jraia.or.jp

Siemens Energy  welcomes the opportunity to give feedback on the review of the F luorinated 
Greenhouse Gas Regulation (F-Gas Regulation, 517/2014) regarding the phase out of SF6 in high-
voltage (HV) applications. Siemens Energy fully supports the European Union’s ambition  to 
become the world’s first carbon -neutral continent by 2050 , the Paris Agreement and the UN 
Sustainable Development Goals . The European Commission’s Green Deal must be a deal for 
carbon-neutrality, but also a deal for more competitiveness and investments in environmentally 
friendly and safe technologies. 
 
Why phasing out SF6 is necessary 
 
SF6 is the most harmful greenhouse gas.  SF6 stands for sulfur hexafluoride with a global 
warming potential of the equivalent of 23 500 CO2 equivalents. This means that 1kg of SF6 leaking 
into the atmosphere has the same GHG effect as 23 500 kg of CO2. Additionally, SF6 has a very 
long atmospheric lifetime of up to 3200 years. Once released to the atmosphere, SF 6 is there to 
stay.  
 
Existing b an of SF 6 in most applications . Because of its high GHG potential and its long 
atmospheric lifetime, the EU decided in the F -Gas regulation in 2014 to ban SF 6 for most 
applications. However, with one important exception: the electricity sector. The key reason at the 
time was a lack of alternative s that were both technologically and economically viable. Today, 
alternatives are available in HV applications, and the industry is ready to phase out SF6 step by step 
and use SF6-free alternatives in electrical equipment.

Phasing out SF6 is the most effective way towards climate -neutrality. A recent study by the 
industry association ZVEI 1 concludes that the installed base of SF 6 gas and SF 6 emissions during 
operations could only be achieved with a phase out of SF6 and an immediate introduction of SF6-
free technologies as soon as available . Limiting emissions with state -of-art SF 6 technology with 
lower gas quantities and leakage rates is not enough. Instead , in mid -term, it will lead to an 
increase in SF 6 emissions driven by electrification and the rapid development of decentralized 
energy systems and e-mobility infrastructure.  
 
Keeping jobs and technology leadership in Europe.  European-based companies are at the 
forefront of the technological development. For standard SF 6 equipment, the innovation gap is 
closing and price risks dominating international competition. Europe’s global success is based on 
technology leadership and s trong sustainability credentials. HV SF6-free equipment  is a huge 
industrial growth opportunity for Europe to regain our innovative edge, expand our technology 
leadership and keep jobs in Europe. To fully leverage our first mover advantage, and to translat e 
years of research and development into jobs, growth and competitiveness, Europe will need to act 
quickly and boldly.  
 
EU leads on ambition but lacks on action . Europe leads by example with its ambition to be the 
first carbon neutral continent by 2050. The ambition level is clear, the actions still need to follow. 
Currently, the EU is clearly lacking behind in phasing out SF 6, where alternatives are available. 
South Korea is already working on a law to phase out SF6 and California is in the final stages to do 
the same with planned entry into force of the law in January 2021. It is therefore paramount that 
the European Commission prioritizes the review of the F-Gas regulation and comes forward with 
a legislative proposal by June 2021.  
 
Clean Air will be the future – for Europe and globally. Siemens Energy has invested massively 
over the last decade to develop chemical-free alternatives to SF6. Our solution works simply with 
clean air thanks to innovative adaptations to the design of HV equipment. No emissions, low 
handling costs and no health risks to workers . This innovation in clean and safe technologies is 
one of the building blocks for the EU to achieve the promised carbon neutrality by 2050 and keep 
its technological leadership and therefore jobs in Europe. A clear regulatory framework is needed 
to go ahead with the phase out of SF 6 starting with HV equipment to provide certainty to the 
European industry. Europe can take this opportunity to act as a role model for all re gions 
worldwide to invest in a SF6 free future society.

Our policy recommendations for the way forward 
 
Phase out of SF 6 in the high-voltage electricity sector. To achieve carbon neutrality by 2050, 
the European Commission announced to align existing regulations with this overarching target 
and to promote the necessary innovations. The F -Gas regulation is an important legislation to 
achieve this target and needs to be reviewed and aligned with the promise of the Green Deal  as 
soon as possible.   
 
SF6-free technologies are ready and are introduced stepwise in the HV sector. With more than 10 
years of operational experience, they are ready for industrialized production and can become an 
industrial growth opportunity (Figure 1).

We therefore recommend four priorities when reviewing the F-Gas regulation with a differentiated 
roadmap to phase out SF6 in HV applications.  
1) Existing installations: all HV SF6 equipment that is older than 40 years should be replaced 
by SF6-free equipment, taking the timeline for new installations into account. 
 
2) New installations: stepwise phase out of SF6 in new HV equipment

3) SF6 alternatives: A phase out of SF6 goes hand in hand with the phase in of new alternative 
gases. This requires full transparency on the emissions of these new alternative gases. We 
recommend that all F -gases replacing SF 6 in the electricity sector with a GWP > 1 are 
included in the European inventory of GHG and follow the same reporting requirements. 
Also, the US CARB regulation is very clear in this regard and covers all gases with GWP > 1 
in its draft version from July 2020.2 
 
4) Timely actions needed: Operators currently face high uncertainty about whether to invest 
in SF 6-free equipment. This delays the transition to carbon neutrality without need. 
Therefore, the European Commission should give a clear signal to grid oper ators that 
Europe’s future will be SF 6-free. We recommend reviewing the F -Gas regulation by June 
2021 at the latest. Any further delay buys international competitors time to catch up.

Response from the European Fluorocarbons Technical Committee (EFCTC)  
to the Inception Impact Assessment on the revision of Regulation No. 517/2014 
(Ref. Ares (2020) 3402178 - 29/06/2020) 
 
EFCTC understands that the revision of Regulation No. 517/2014 aims at having a substantial positive 
impact on the EU’s Green Deal goals.  
Improvements in implementing and in enforcing the Regulation will indeed assist the European Union 
in moving towards carbon neutrality. In this process preventing and controlling illegal activities will be 
key, as they currently undermine the climate goals. 
EFCTC stresses the value of lower-GWP HFCs, HCFOs and HFOs with short atmospheric lifetimes, which 
will need to be part of the solution and are already gradually being introduced while the higher GWP 
HFCs are being phased down. This shows the current F -gas Regulation market -based system and 
schedule works and needs to be maintained. 
In addition, based on 6 years of exp erience with the implementation of Regulation No. 517/2014, 
EFCTC sees this revision as a perfect opportunity to include some improvements, e.g. provisions to 
enhance enforcement, the removal of certain exemptions and exclusions, some slight changes in the  
reporting system, etc. They will all have a positive effect on reaching the EU’s Green Deal goals. 
 
EFCTC notes that the Commission wishes to update the F -gas Regulation in the light of international 
obligations, the European Green Deal objectives and technical progress made, and lessons learned 
during the implementation of the Regulation over the past 6 years.  
More specifically: 
• The last phase-down step of the F-gas Regulation is in 2030, whereas the Montreal Protocol’s last 
reduction step is in 2036 with a more ambitious target. Therefore, the EU risks of becoming non -
compliant with the Montreal Protocol after 2030. 
• Recent technological developments may permit further savings in support of reaching the European 
Green Deal objectives more easily. 
• Some challenges related to the implementation and enforcement of the F-gas Regulation have been 
identified over the past years. These concern e.g. illegal imports and misuse of the quota system as 
well as more comprehensive monitoring and a need for better policy coherence to exploit synergies 
with the “Single Window for Customs”.

While reviewing the Regulation in the above spirit, EFCTC requests the Commission to take the 
following into account: 
Alignment with the Montreal Protocol 
• The EU Regulation and phase-down of HFCs entered into force well ahead of the Kigali Amendment 
of the Montreal Protocol. Comparison of the two regimes on a 1:1 basis is difficult as not all the 
criteria are identical. 
 
• Taking this into account, it is, therefore, essential to undertake a detailed analysis for a correct and 
meaningful comparison.   
 
• EFCTC supports ensuring flexibility in any measures proposed to align HFC production to enable  
continuation within the EU where economica l and with environmentally responsible production 
as set out in Art 7 of the Regulation. 
How the revision can help reaching the climate neutrality goal  
• Over the past years, there has been a major adoption of lower GWP energy efficient solutions. 
There have been significant improvements in refrigerant and equipment technology, local recovery 
and recycling of HFCs impacting both direct and indirect emissions. Focus has increased on energy 
and resource efficiency whilst safety remains an absolute priority. This shows that, in the context 
of the Green Deal and decarbonisation, HFCs, HCFOs and HFOs can be part of the solution.  
 
• Viewing the growth in the use of heat pumps and other refrigerant-requiring applications, the value 
of lower -GWP HFCs, HCFOs and HF Os with short atmospheric lifetimes, must continue to be 
recognised. 
 
• The current F -gas Regulation market-based system and phase -down schedule for the quantity of 
HFCs (in CO 2e) that can be placed into the market , is shown to be working. To allow a smooth 
transition to lower GWP solutions, it should be maintained, whilst aligning the regulation with the 
2036 reduction target of the Montreal Protocol1. 
 
• The reductions for greenhouse gas emissions proposed in the context of the Green Deal go beyond 
“business as usual” scenario as they aim to reduce drastically net emissions of greenhouse gases by 
2050. In order to reach this target, all viable options such as lower GWP HFCs, HCFOs and HFOs and 
their mixtures will be required. 
 
• To enable a more thorough understanding of the speed and breath of the transition away from 
higher-GWP HFCs in the EU market, quantitative reporting needs to be optimised  and should be 
expanded to include all solutions for replacing  high GWP  HFCs (including non-fluorinated 
substances).

How the F-gas Regulation can support the circular economy 
• In the context of circular economy, recovery & reclamation needs to be better integrated and 
prioritised in the market. This can, for example, be realised by ensuring enforcement of the ban on 
the use of non-refillable cylinders, including action to prevent circumvention of the rules by one-
way sales of returnable cylinders. 
 
• Operators and natural persons trained and certificated under Article 10 of the F-Gas Regulation will 
also use their knowledge and expertise to handle HFOs and HCFOs in order to prevent t heir 
emissions and for recycling and re-use.   
 
The revision as an opportunity to improve implementation and enforcement 
• EFCTC has raised major concerns over the illegal import of HFCs into the EU and the misuse of the 
HFC quota system. A number of short recommendations are included in an Annex I to this response. 
 
• While EFCTC accepts the need to provide opportunities for New Entrants, the current system has 
resulted in a significant fragmentation of the HFC market and disrupting compliant, well-established 
supply networks that have invested in handling and recovery of HFCs. It is also creating major 
bureaucratic issues in controlling the import of HFCs in the EU and should be revised to be 
enforceable. A requirement for effective vetting of all companies that are placing HFCs on the EU 
market, regardless of quantity, including those from outside of the EU, and verification and auditing 
of their annual F-Gas reporting, could be a perfect first step. 
 
How the revision can help strengthening obligations for MS and companies   
• The revision can strengthen the obligation for Member States to ensure that the accurate records 
are kept under the F-gas Regulation through inspections. 
 
• Currently producers or imp orters of less than 100tCO2e per year are exempted from the phase -
down schedule [article 15.2] and reporting requirements [article 19] . As this exemption has been 
misused by players on the market who combine several imports below the threshold, it should be 
removed for imports of HFCs in containers. 
 
• The verification obligation should also apply below the current 10 000tCO2e threshold [article 19.6], 
especially since the next phases of the phase-down schedule will decease allocated quota volumes. 
 
• The use of F-gases with a GWP  2 500 should be prohibited to service or maintain refrigeration 
equipment irrespective of the charge size. Currently a threshold is set at  40 tonnes of CO2e [article 
13.3] 
 
EFCTC and its member companies look forward to taking part in the stakeholder consultation and will 
be active during the process of the revision in order to provide balanced comments on proposals that 
are forthcoming.

Annex I 
• EFCTC supports the setting of minimum penalties by the Commission and the inclusion of guidance 
to address the down-the-value-chain legal compliance. Germany, for example, already drafted an 
amendment to the national legislation. 
 
• Action must be taken to stop the abuse  of the T1 Transit process, which has been identified as an 
important route for illegal imports of HFCs. 
  
• Appropriate articles of the F -gas Regulation should cover the issue of sales of non -compliant 
cylinders through e-commerce platforms.  
 
• Harmonised minimum requirements for dealing with confiscated material should be set for 
Member States 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
While contributing to this consultation we remain very aware we are all still experiencing 
unprecedented times. As Cefic, we aim to maintain a high standard in our responses. Europe’s 
industries will have to cope with the impacts of this crisis while ensuring investments support a 
climate-neutral Europe. We therefore look to the European Institutions to undertake the 
appropriate assessments and set a framework ensuring the EU’s post -crisis attractiveness as an 
investment destination while achieving EU Green Deal objectives.

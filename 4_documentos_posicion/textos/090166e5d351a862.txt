 
 
Xylem Position on Inception Impact Assessment on the Revision of the Urban Waste Water 
Treatment Directive 
 
Xylem welcomes the opportunity to provide feedback on the roadmap for the revision of the Urban Waste 
Water Treatment Directive (UWWTD). As a leading technology and solutions provider for the wastewater 
sector, Xylem actively contributed towards the REFIT evaluation of the UWWTD and, in light of the 
evaluation’s conclusions, welcomes the Commission’s decision to revise and update this long-established 
legislation. A revised Directive could drive more sustainable decision-making by WWTP operators, thereby 
improving environmental performance and building resilience to emerging challenges. 
Xylem’s key proposal is that the Commission should ensure that a revised directive should  include policy 
measures that ensure the modernisation of Europe’s wastewater infrastructure. We would like to stress that 
this can be achieved by integrating digital solutions (software & digital technologies) into WWTP operations 
without replacing existi ng hardware  and equipment  – reducing high investment costs, while improving 
environmental and operational performance.  On this basis, Xylem would like to make the following 
recommendations for the Commission to consider during the UWWTD review process. 
1. Small agglomerations should be included within the scope of a revised UWWTD , ensuring access 
to sanitation 
The new Article 13 on access to water in the recently agreed recast of the Drinking Water Directive 
constitutes an important step towards fulfilling the objective of the Right2Water initiative and UN Sustainable 
Development Goal 6 on access to clean water and sanitation. Xylem calls on the Commission to mirror this 
provision in a revised UWWTD, ensuring access to sanitation for all in Europe. The Commission could 
consider EU -wide objectives for connection rates. Where centralised wastewater management is no t 
feasible, Member States could be required to prepare decentralised wastewater management strategies to 
mitigate environmental impacts. Meanwhile, the Commission should ensure adequate standards at 
agglomerations of all sizes, by encompassing small agglomerations in the scope of the revised UWWTD. 
2. Monitoring obligations need to be updated to support deployment of innovative, digital solutions , 
which would also enable surveillance of pathogens in wastewater 
In light of the Commission’s intention to explore additional monitoring obligations and modernisation  of 
reporting requirements, Xylem would like to highlight the importance of supporting the uptake of innovative 
monitoring technologies including IoT  solutions for alarm management and real -time decision support 
systems, which is not currently encouraged by the current Directive1. Revising the monitoring methodology 
through digitization could encourage data access, traceability and compliance to Utili ties. Extending 
monitoring obligations to effluent and environmental waters could also contribute to pinpointing sources of 
pollution. Digital monitoring and surveillance tools would also enable Member States  to improve their 
resilience to future pandemics  through early detection of pathogens in wastewater. To this end, the 
Commission should consider the option of introducing a requirement for national plans for using wastewater 
surveillance as a pandemic control measures, as well as the formulation of a watchlist of critical pathogens 
for large agglomerations. 
3. Strengthened spatial planning requirements with a focus on smart infrastructure would allow for a 
holistic management of collection and treatment 
Xylem supports the Commission’s intention to consider preventive measures for reducing pollution at 
source and limiting quantities of water in the collection systems via improved spatial planning. We would 
especially like to highlight the potential benefit s of introducing mandatory requirements for regional spatial 
planning to include a mapping of different types of infrastructures, classified e.g. as ‘grey’, ‘green’ and 
‘smart’ infrastructure.  A comprehensive database of our all infrastructure  would contribute towards more 
data-driven optimization of the system, and thereby enabling a more holistic management of collection and

treatment of wastewater and stormwater, e.g. optimisation of available hydraulic volume and 
decentralisation of treatment. 
4. EU-harmonised measures regarding s tormwater overflows & urban run -off in a revised UWWTD 
would bring significant environmental benefits 
Given the increased pressures on wastewater infrastructure from increasing urban development and 
intensity of rainfall events, Xylem believes that a EU -wide harmonised approach to stormwater overflows 
would bring significant environmental benefits. This sho uld include requirements for separation and 
balancing of flows in new or upgraded infrastructure, as well as mandatory assessment of stormwater 
overflows at national level. Based on these assessments, Member States should be required to set 
mitigation and management plans for stormwater overflows, including frequency targets and coliform limits, 
and promoting digital management solutions that can significantly reduce overflow events  and limit the 
investment plan into new civil infrastructure. 
5. Contaminants of Emerging Concern should incrementally be included within the scope of a revised 
UWWTD 
Xylem recommends that Contaminants of Emerging Concern (CECs), including pharmaceuticals, 
endocrine disruptors and microplastics, should be included within the scope of the directive, ensuring that 
they are adequately removed during treatment processes before treated wastewater is discharged into the 
environment. At a minimum, CECs should be included in treatment requirements for large agglomerations, 
where there is a risk that they are present in certain concentrations. In addition, this expansion of treatment 
requirements could support the increased reuse of sewage sludge.  
6. Given the energy efficiency potential of UWWTPs, the Commi ssion should consider measures to 
capitalise on this potential in the UWWTD to overall reduce greenhouse gas emissions.  
Xylem welcomes  the Commission’s recognition the energy efficiency potential of Europe’s wastewater 
sector. To capitalise on this potenti al and to move an energy -neutral wastewater sector, Xylem would 
recommend that the Commission introduces adequate  energy audit and transparency requirements for 
individual WWTPs, ensuring consistency with the EU’s broader energy efficiency objectives, including load-
based parameters of energy consumption. Beyond energy savings, requirements for the reduction of NOx 
emissions from anaerobic digestion would contribute significantly to climate change mitigation. To enable 
future energy neutrality, it is also necessary to ensure the ability of energy producing WWTPs to feed energy 
into the grid. Xylem would like to underl ine that digital solutions in the wastewater sector can achieve 
significant energy savings with limited investment needs. 
7. A mixture of incentives and requirements could be considered to promote resource recovery  & 
reuse in order to contribute towards the circular economy 
Xylem recommends that the Commission uses the revision of the Directive to ensure a closer alignment 
between water legislation and the EU’s circular economy objectives through promotion of resource 
recovery, and non-potable and potable water reuse from wastewater. To achieve this, the Commission 
should construct an enabling framework which provides certainty of the quality of recovered materials, and 
consider further harmonised standards for recovery and reuse of raw materials from sludge. Recovery and 
reuse could also be further stimulated through the introduction of conditionality clauses for EU funds for 
wastewater treatment projects. 
8. Financing strategies for the European wastewater infrastructure need to be updated in light of the 
need to address existing wastewater pollution & emerging challenges faced by the sector 
Xylem considers that the Commission should facilitate sound financing strategies for the wastewater sector 
in Member States through setting minimum requirements for the contents of national strategies, employing 
outcome- or performance-based models and respecting the polluter-pays principle. 
 
Xylem hopes that the comments above can be useful in supporting the Commission’s work on the UWWTD 
review. We remain available in case of any further questions or comments.

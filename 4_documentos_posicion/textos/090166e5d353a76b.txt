UNESID’S FEEDBACK ON THE INITIATIVE TO REVIEW THE URBAN WASTE 
WATER TREATMENT DIRECTIVE 
The Spanish Steel Association, UNESID, in representation of the Spanish Steel Industry 
welcomes the opportunity to give feedback on the initiative which aims to assess the 
suitability of reviewing the Urban Waste Water Treatment Directive. 
The new environmental performance aspiration of the European Commission involves 
updating the way of managing the en vironmental aspects  as a whole, and the way 
wastewater is treated and managed should not be an exemption. We think that the 
review and alignment of the Directive to this new trend would involve significant 
support to the co mpletion of climate neutrality, p ollution minimization and circularity 
ambitions, among others. 
The Spanish Steel Industry is deeply compromised both with making completely circular 
our operations, while enabling that other economic sector s become more circular  
themselves. For that, we provide permanent materials (steel) and we promote the use 
of our by-products/waste as secondary materials (slag, among others) which may be an 
alternative for other less sustainable primary materials. 
Thus, during the last years, we have been focusing on how to make more circular parts 
of our production processes that can still be optimized. One of the key lines of action 
identified is the use of water in our processes , mainly for cooling purposes. The use of 
regenerated wastewaters coming f rom Waste Water Treatment Plants (WWTP) with 
tertiary treatment has been identified as an optimization action and its potential 
extensively assessed (always assuring that required quality constrain are fulfilled ). The 
industrial water consumption mains are  often close to the cities /towns, they have  
similar order of magnitude of consumption comparing with  the urban waste water in 
many countries, they can be easily monitored, and generate a higher GDP/quality jobs 
than other wastewater consumption alternatives. Therefore, it should be a must to 
adapt the WWTP output quality to the industrial needs. 
As the Urban Waste Water Treatment Directive approved in 2011 does not cover (from 
our point of view) these kind of alternatives as it should do in order to  align e fforts 
towards the European Green Deal and the new Circular Economy Action Plan  (among 
others), we believe that it will be advisable to review and update it , upgrading the 
objectives it aims to tackle and the associated investment schemes. 
We think that, within the ambition of trying to include circularity criteria into this 
Directive, efforts needed shall be balanced. The Inception Impact Assessment pays 
special attention to the reuse of the sludge, while the reuse of treated wastewaters has 
gone, to some extent , unnoticed. We believe that reusability and recyclability shall be 
promoted, independently of the subject material. Additionally, the reuse of regenerated 
wastewaters cannot only be focused on the agricultural se ctor, as there may be other 
economic sectors with equal or higher potential to reuse this water. This shall be

In some cases, Urban WWTP are also receiving indust rial effluents. If , for whatever 
reason, a mixed Urban an d Industrial WWTP is not capable to  manage the declared 
indirect industrial effluent (which flows through the sewage network) from a plant which 
will/is covered by an Integrated Emission Directive pe rmit, the WWTP Manager should 
inform of such situation to the Authorities before issuing/reviewing the permit. These 
kind of situations  shall be considered on the evaluation of potential alternatives, such 
as the design of  specific pre-treatment processes for any of the pollutants within the 
industry, to adapt the WWTP to such effluents (among other cases). 
The operator/service provider should have good knowledge about the pollutants that 
could be emitted to the collector/sewage system , that could potentially affect the 
management of the WWTP. If an exotic/specific pollutant can generate/star generating 
problems for the entire management of the WWTP or the receiving water body, it should 
be consider ed what the source is, what monitoring practices are needed , and what 
measures need to be carried out over the sources and/or in the WWTP. These situations 
shall be assessed on a case by case basis, applying a cost benefit analysis. Otherwise, it 
should be accepted that the waste water service is correctly dimensioned and managed 
to appropriately treat the incoming wastewater. 
In conclusion, we consider that the review of the Urban Waste Water Treatment 
Directive may involve an interesting opportunity to align the  efforts made in terms of 
wastewater management with other high-level European aspirations, such as climate 
neutrality, pollution minimization and circularity, among others. 
 
 
 
 
About UNESID 
The Spanish steel industry is fundamental for the metal-mechanic industry’s value chain: for automobiles, 
all tips of machinery, infrastructures, etc. The sector produces mor e than 13,6 million tonnes of steel per 
year and exports the amount of 8.000 million eu ros. Innovation, efficiency, sustainability and safety are 
the principles that guide the  strategy of the sector, which is  recognized as one of the main levers for the 
new sustainable economy. Every tonne produced involves 7 euros of investment in sustainability matters. 
The Spanish steel industry leads the recycling scheme, as more than 11 million of steel tonnes are recycled 
every year. The steel ind ustry carries out a n efficient use of resources, making use of around the 80% of 
the waste and by-products generated by the main process. 
UNESID is the business association of the Spanish steel industry, that brings together 46 companies, which 
directly employ around 60.000 people, additionally to another 20.000 that are involved in the collection of 
ferrous scrap. The sector has a turnover of 14.000 million euros.

 
 
EDISON’S CONTRIBUTION TO THE PUBLIC CONSULTATION FOR THE FUELEU MARITIME – GREEN 
EUROPEAN MARITIME SPACE INITIATIVE 
 
Edison welcomes the opportunity to share our views  related to the initiative “FuelEU Maritime - Green 
European Maritime Space ” launched by the European Commission. We strongly believe this initiative will 
contribute to developing a comprehensive framework for a substantial uptake of sustainable alternative fuels 
in maritime transport.  
Many initiatives are being launched at international and European level to implement and support the 
development of sustainable alternative fuels and related logistic chains and infrastructure. The most recent 
initiatives launched under the International Maritime Organization (IMO) confirm the importance attached 
to the development of sustainable solutions for the maritime sector in the process of reaching the goals of 
the Paris Agreement. For its part, the Commission has strongly supported the development of  sustainable 
alternative fuels through the Directive 2014/94/EU ("DAFI") on the deployment of alternative fuels 
infrastructure; but also, through initiatives such as the Blue Corridors, the Motorways of the Sea and through 
dedicated programs in support of investments under the Connecting Europe Facility (CEF Transport and CEF 
Blending).  
Supporting the goals of the Paris Agreement and supporting the Green Deal's ambition for climate neutrality 
in 2050, we hope that the Commission will  take forward, as it has done so far, the deployment of all fuels 
capable of substantially reducing the impact of all emissions (SOx, PM, NOx and CO2) in the maritime sector. 
Taking into consideration only the reduction of the CO2 impacts would result in a  partial and, thus, not 
entirely effective approach to tackle all the environmental pollutant agents in the maritime sector.  The 
sources capable of reducing the impact of all emissions include electricity, hydrogen, biofuels, natural gas 
(including biometh ane) in gaseous (CNG) and liquefied (LNG) form . This initiative will be crucially 
instrumental in driving the uptake of alternative fuels from 2030 onwards, as some of the alternative fuels in 
the above-mentioned list are not ready yet and their process of technological maturation needs to be framed. 
Therefore, giving lead to the most cost-effective technologies can cater for an immediate contribution to a 
substantial improvement of the environmental profiles in the maritime transport, while others will require 
long term ef forts before being implemented by all the players involved. In fact, we believe that the 
development of alternative fuels, LNG first and foremost, has a well -plotted path for the next  future, 
considering that LNG is also the only fuel which is already tech nically mature but also able to make a 
transaction towards cleaner fuels such as Blue Hydrogen.  
In this context of development of the  sector, we hope that the Commission can also preserve and give 
continuity to initiatives such as the Directive 2014/94/EU on the Deployment of Alternative Fuels 
Infrastructure (DAFI) as the uptake of alternative fuels is a crucial enabler for the decarbon ization of the 
energy and transport sectors, only if supported by adequate infrastructures. 
Related to this, another driver which would help the growth of LNG as Maritime Fuel, is the harmonization 
in all EU of the procedures to enhance operators to start LNG bunkering as well as standard authorization 
procedures and operational criteria for LNG bunkering operations themselves . Moreover, it should be also 
guaranteed access to incentives to all the ships entering in the EU market regardless the applicant. 
It will also be of paramount importance, in order to continue supporting the development of solutions able 
to reduce emissions in the maritime transport sector, that in the ongoing reform of Directive 2003/96/EC on 
the taxation of energy products a favorable framework for less polluting fuels is maintained, as in the case of 
natural gas also in the form of liquefied natural gas, including in the form of the tax exemptions envisaged 
under the current Directive.

Another important aspect would be the harmonization of the emissions criteria in sea areas. Accordingly, the 
implementation of an ECA (Emission Control Area) in the Mediterranean Sea would represent a crucial step 
forward in developing a comprehensive framework to support the uptake of sustainable alternative fuels in 
maritime transport, considering that in Nordic & Baltic Sea ECA has already been implemented and brought 
positive results. 
The FuelEU Maritime initiative will play a key role in catalyzing synergies across all the initiatives put in place 
over the last years. We therefore hope that it will be able to make a clear policy contribution to the uptake 
of sustainable alternative fuels, taking into account the important progress that the maritime sector has 
made in recent years, thanks also to the close cooperation with the players of the energy sector. Nevertheless 
we strongly believe that this initiative should recognize, as already done in the past by the Commission, the 
prominent role of the International Maritime Organization (IMO), and its standard, not to isolate the EU from 
the rest of the world and to avoid over-regulation on the matter, in order not to lose trading activities and 
maritime tourism.

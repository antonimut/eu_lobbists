 
 
LanzaTech Response REDII Consultation. 
 
This consultation seeks to find opportunities to update EU policy frameworks to support cost effective 
deployment of solutions in line with the European Green Deal (EGD) and the post COVID Economic 
Recovery Package. The impact assessment does highlight promotion of other low carbon fuels, however, 
this needs to be explicitly called out as an opportunity for the EU to increase its sustainable fuel pool, for 
the hard to decarbonize sectors, such as aviation.  
Key focus areas for eligible fuels in the Impact Assessment include:  
• Preserve biodiversity 
• Do no Harm 
• Reduce Air Pollution 
• Increase renewable energy across multiple sectors.  
While these are all commendable focus areas, the scope of the revised RED is still very much focused on 
Renewable solutions, with the recent exception of including Recycled carbon fuels. This inclusion, 
however, is still fraught with challenges at an implementation level because it is an opt in solution for 
Member States and has additional technical details to finalize.  
For any revision of energy policy, we must treat deployment of new fuels to address climate change in the 
same way as COVID vaccines – pursuing all possible technology solutions with an eye to the future.  This 
is the model that has been followed for Electric Vehicles, enabling the EV market to grow and driving down 
costs of production, in parallel with the grid transition to 100% renewable. If Electric Vehicle development 
and deployment had been forced to wait until the grid was sufficiently green to fully realize the ir 
anticipated benefits, we would now have decades to wait for the sector to emerge. 
Similarly, we do not have the luxury of time before starting to put steel in the ground for production of 
new sustainable fuels. 
To meet any climate and decarbonization ta rgets, the scope of all policy frameworks should be broader 
with a focus on outcomes rather than prescribed inputs. The challenge with the RED is that its primary 
focus is only on  solutions with r enewable inputs rather than all sustainable solutions that c an delivery 
carbon savings and promote the goals of the EGD.  
By increasing the scope of the RED, and changing its name to encompass broader sustainable solutions, 
the EU would be able to increase its level of ambition and meet its targets. Revisions to any Energy policy 
need to adopt a future-oriented view – just like that taken for EVs. For example, sustainable fuel policies 
should focus on emissions savings as grid emissions change over a project’s lifetime, rather than using 
historical or current grid e missions to establish eligibility. Just as the EV market was encouraged in 
anticipation of a greener grid, sustainable fuel projects should be encourage based on future grid 
projections. This approach will help create a market for fuels from new technologi es, encourage 
investment and offtake agreements, and importantly allow producers to build, produce sustainable fuels 
and create jobs in the EU today. If potential producers must evaluate projects based purely on current or 
historical grid emissions, they will have to wait for the local grid to catch up and this could delay project 
deployment for 10 years or more in some Member States. This is especially relevant today with the

We all want to avoid unintended consequences of new technologies, but safeguards can be built in 
through monitoring and the use of staged GHG reduction thresholds to ensure that technologies are 
improving year on year. 
With this in mind, we support Option 4 in the Impact Assessment to: Amend REDII to translate into legal 
measures the actions proposed in other energy strategies of the EGD  
We also believe there are elements from Options 2 and 3 that can be included, including revisions to the 
targets and sub targets in Option 3.  
Option 4: We especially support measures to foster electrification, which will improve the GHG footprint 
of all fuel’s technologies and the support for better use of waste streams and low carbon fuels in transport.  
A key aspect for Future Fuels technologies is the current challenge that some categories of fuels are not 
included in some of the targets (Recycled Carbon Fuels for example), and so any revision under Option 4 
and 3 should open up eligibility so that new solutions are not excluded. The existing opt in clause to RED2 
leaves a gap in fuels that can contribute to the targets and so creates an unnecessary barrier to 
deployment and fulfilment of volumes to meet the set ambition.  
Any revisions should be looked at in terms of creating a technology neutral and level playing field for all 
sustainable fuel approaches to contribute. Imbalances such as allowing some feedstocks to be eligible for 
double counting, while others are not can cr eate challenges to getting volumes to the market from a 
diversified feedstock pool and can also create a market distortion because double counted fuels mean 
that we are not actually increasing the volume of fuels in the market (and reducing the associated fossil 
fuels required by the consumer).  
A holistic approach should be taken for all fuels, using identical criteria and going beyond GHG reductions.  
Non regulatory measures as outlined in Option 2 will also play a part in supporting the sector to ensure 
the public is aware of the benefits, successes (not just the failures as are highlighted in the media today) 
and support acceptance of these types of fuels. In addition, a supportive financial landscape is critical for 
new technologies to deploy in the EU and policy must support this by creating a market with stable policy 
frameworks that investors will get behind.

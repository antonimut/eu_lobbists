Ref: Revision of Directive (EU) 2018/2001 on the promotion of the use of energy from renewable sources   
 
Finnish Energy’s comments on the Revision of Directive (EU) 2018/2001 on the 
promotion of the use of energy from renewable sources   
 
Finnish Energy, the association representing Finnish companies that produce, acquire, transmit and sell 
electricity, gas, district heat and district cooling and offer related service, thanks for the possibility to give 
feedback to the roadmap for the impact assessment on the revision of REDII. 
 
Finnish Energy supports commissions target to make Europe the first climate neutral continent in the world 
by 2050 and agrees on the role of renewable energy on helping to achieve the target and to make our 
energy supply more affordable and secure by reducing the EU’s dependency on imported fossil fuels.  
We agree on and are happy to notice that Europe has made significant progress in decarbonizing its 
electricity production through renewables and there really is a need to ensure that all sectors (inc. all 
heating methods) fully contribute to decarbonization.  
 
As said previously on the consultation response of the EU 2030 Climate Target Plan, we however do not 
support the rapid recast of RED or EED. Both these directives include detailed demands which affect 
directly to current investments. The climate framework should provide clarity and long-term certainty 
when reaching the 2050 carbon neutrality target. Now planned revision of REDII comes immediately after 
the national implementation, which is now still ongoing, and there is yet no valid data on the effects the 
revised directive has.  
 
Concerning biomass use, the focus should now be on the implementation of the existing stringent criteria 
on sustainability set out in REDII and related compliance guideline. When discussing about the use of 
whole trees for energy production, it should be kept in mind that whole trees do contribute to biodiversity 
in many cases, but not always. The term “whole trees” is undefined, and when decreasing the usage of 
fossil fuels and peat, the role of whole tree for energy security increases. 
 
Finnish Energy has given a national climate pledge, in which we endorse Finnish Government's efforts to 
make Finland carbon neutral by 2035. We have committed to foster climate and energy policy, which 
halves the GHG current emissions from electricity and district heating by 2030 and the renewables already 
have an important role on achieving this goal. However, carbon neutrality and rapid electrification of 
industry need all CO2-free sources and therefore nuclear (incl. SMR) should be available in energy strategy.    
 
Energy transition is already happening in Finland and energy companies are diversifying their business 
strategies. The main driver is the CO2 price signal from the EU ETS. The current EU climate target has 
indicated that the EUA will cost 30-40 e/t in early 2030s. Energy companies have established their emission 
reduction strategy based on this and several companies have declared to reach carbon neutrality in 2030s. 
These strategies include changes in whole business model and expansions, for example, to circular 
economy. Strategies need careful planning, time and resources in order to take care of energy security. 
Finnish Energy is concerned that the recast of the 2030 climate target and related regulation such as 
revision of the REDII cause uncertainty. 
 
We can see the transition happening in Finland, for example:

• heating markets have developed and are further developing on market terms, customers are able 
to freely choose their heating method.  It is also possible for third parties to produce heat and 
cooling to networks, which is also a realized example of energy sector system integration. District 
heating companies are actively mapping and utilizing waste heat sources from industry, tertiary 
sector and even residential buildings. 
• the share of renewables and other carbon neutral energy sources in energy production is already 
high (82% of the electricity and 53% of district heating, year 2019) and the amount is still increasing 
in the coming years, driven by the CO2 price signal from the EU ETS. 
• We can promote the progress of using renewables and other carbon neutral technologies without 
the revision of REDII and focus on the non-regulatory measures mentioned in option 2 and by 
finding funds for research and development. 
 
When some of the actions proposed in several initiatives, strategies (for example Energy System Integration 
Strategy and the Hydrogen Strategy)  and plans that have been adopted or will be adopted under the EGD, 
bring up the need to revise REDII, revision should only be made to necessary parts or amendments. These 
revisions could be done at the same time with the evaluation of REDII in 2023.

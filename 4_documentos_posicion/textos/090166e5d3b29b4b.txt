VCI-Position: 
Inception impact assessment on the revision of Directive 
(EU) 2018/2001 (RED II) 
 
Competitiveness is a driver for sustainability 
VCI welcomes the consultation of the roadmap on the revision of RED II. Industry is 
ready to support the transition of a carbon neutral energy supply system with 
innovative processes and products. However, many innovations based on renewable 
energies are largely not yet competitive and partly not even on an operational 
readiness level. In addition, needed solutions to deal with intermittency of renewable 
energies and to buffer periods lacking renewable energy production are not in place 
yet. Implementation requires increased investment, operating and production 
expenditures. Nevertheless, in the course of the desired transformation, the European 
industry must not lose competitiveness. Industry is an important element of Europe’s 
value chains and contributes significantly to economic welfare. The transition costs 
related to the Green Deal facing industrial consumers have to be efficient and on a 
competitive level. A further key to competitiveness is a cost-efficient roll-out and market 
integration of renewable energy sources, as outlined in the roadmap. Incentives for a 
cost-efficient roll-out are particularly required in non-electricity sectors. 
 
Technology neutrality and guarantees of origin (GO) 
RED II focuses on renewable based fuels and gases as a priority. However, in the 
transition phase other low carbon pathways such as blue hydrogen may be considered 
as well to satisfy the demand for low carbon hydrogen. VCI stresses a technology 
neutral approach, since in the end, only the carbon-footprint of hydrogen production 
technologies matter. Technology competition favours a market launch at economically 
optimised costs. A political and regulatory limitation to certain technologies leads to 
inefficiencies and unnecessarily high system costs. Furthermore, the large amounts of 
hydrogen and other low carbon gases and fuels required in the future prohibit the 
exclusion of suitable technologies. Therefore, all technologies revealing a marginal 
carbon footprint should be considered as equal options for the future technology mix, 
also in the long term, in order to prevent hampering development of efficient 
technological pathways in the future.  
The market mechanisms should be further decoupled from physical energy flows. 
While GOs for green electricity can already be traded in the electricity sector in order to 
decouple the supply options for renewable electricity from physical delivery, an 
analogous development for gases with a low carbon footprint is still required. 
Corresponding GOs must be valid across the internal market and must be applicable to 
all climate neutral gases while maintaining technology neutrality.

Effective sector integration 
Hurdles to sector coupling have to be removed. This requires a revision of the levies 
systematics in the member states. A mayor share of the energy consumption in the 
chemical industry is used as process heat and cold. Industrial heat supply can be 
realised via several different technological pathways (e.g. power-to-heat, fuel-based 
by, for example, using climate neutral gases or waste biomass). The latter are to be 
treated in a technology neutral manner, while considering limited availability (e.g. waste 
biomass). Support regimes for CHP and heat/cold networks should be further 
expanded. 
 
Use of renewable electricity for renewable fuel production 
To develop an efficient and fully integrated energy supply system, access to renewable 
electricity via the electricity grid infrastructure has to be ensured. Art. 27 III RED II 
enables a physical access route only if the criteria outlined in recital 90 are fulfilled. 
These criteria are very restrictive, e.g. the principles of temporal and geographical 
correlation, restrict the use of renewable electricity to produce green hydrogen and 
thus hamper an effective market launch and investments. This conflicts with the aims 
expressed in the European Hydrogen Strategy launched in July 2020. Therefore, the 
criteria should be removed, softened or implemented in a pragmatic manner, e.g. by a 
dynamic approach enabling a less restrictive environment during the initial market 
launch.

Naturgy response to EU energy review rules consultation 
The revision of the Directive 2018/2001 will facilitate the achievement of a new 2030 emission 
reduction target. It’s a window of opportunity to bring untapped emission considering that  
much of the energy & climate legislation covering the 2021 -2030 is in implementation phase, 
complicating its update simultaneously to the national transposition and definition of policies.  
 
Gas systems will be critical to embrace new forms of renewable and decarbonized gases 
which will progressively replace natural gas – which will remain as a key energy source to 
facilitate the decarbonization of the economy-. Gas supports the deployment of intermittent 
RES in the electricity sector, provides irreplaceable firm energy to industry processes, 
affordable energy to households and SMEs and it also yields important energy savings 
(industrial CHP) and significant emission abatement replacing more carbon-intensive fossil 
fuels in all sectors -in addition to the reduction of pollution  
 
The Renewable Directive should facilitate in particular the gradual transformation of the gas 
sector via the integration of new technologies and gas sources (renewable gas, hydrogen and 
mobility solutions). As pointed by major long term analysis, renewable gas will be largely 
needed in large scale to meet our LT targets, achieving significant emission reductions at an 
efficient cost. Stakeholders will thus need 
 Political and regulatory certainty to favor investments to deploy and to scale up new 
gas technologies, mirroring the experience and instruments deployed in the electricity 
sector. Many 10-year NECPs might lack of a sufficient perspective aligned to 2050 
horizon, selecting and promoting short-term winners only, name renewable electricity. 
For instance, Spain has one of the largest renewable gas and P2G prospects in the EU 
but its NCEP lacks of concrete and quantified commitments in this regard. The revision 
of NCEPs should have a look to the way in which they facilitate the uptake of 
subsequent decarbonization strategies beyond 2030. MS should establish 2030 
renewable and decarbonized targets in the natural gas sector at the latest during the 
review of the PNIECs in 2023. 
 The impact assessments should consider all the positive externalities of renewable 
gases (economic, social, environmental) : impacts on EU employment; on technology 
diversification and autonomy (vs. dependence of global supply chains), environmental 
benefits; security of supply (e.g. resilience, cyberattacks risks); impact on vulnerable 
consumers or social acceptance (including consumer preferences and behavior). 
Employment factors will have to be closely considered, focusing on the creation of 
jobs in the EU. Global events such as the covid19 crisis stress the importance of fostering 
local employment without undermining the climate change agenda. Many 
decarbonization technologies depend on supply chains highly concentrated (mainly in 
Asia). The EU should assess local/foreign employment factors/impacts and technology 
dependence of diverse technologies . Biomethane and renewable hydrogen might 
contribute significantly in terms of local employment (+ 1.7M jobs in 2050 according to 
studies). Further asse ssment of EU employment of decarboni zation gas strategies 
should be included. 
 A thorough analysis of infrastructure developments and raw materials associated to 
direct electrification. Optimizing the use infrastructure and materials, balancing direct 
and indirect electrification (e.g. to produce renewable hydrogen for instance) and 
promoting the development of non-electrical renewable and decarbonized energy 
sources is necessary to achieve a least cost decarbonization.

 Energy sector integration as an opportunity for renewable production. The production 
of electricity is increasingly expected to be above the demand in many times. Storing 
electricity to be released at peak times would be an option to monetize electricity. 
However it limit s the access of renewable electricity to electrifiable consumption  and 
for relatively low volumes of surplus. According to a recent EU analysis1 there might be 
a risk of overinvestments in electri city storage facilities based on 10 year NCEPs 
compared to the forward looking perspectives which relies upon the existence of units 
converting renewable electricity into other energy carriers, mainly hydrogen. The 
existence of infrastructure connecting el ectricity and gas systems, might facilitate 
trading, competition and market opportunities. As part of the upcoming revision of the 
internal gas market legislation, facilitating the planning and development of power -to-
gas capacity will likely be crucial to facilitate the integration of large shares of renewable 
electricity production increasing market opportunities. 
 Clear and equal rules for guarantees of origin , sustainability certificates and target 
accountability for all fuels. 
Last but not least, the review of the renewable energy legislation should have to consider the 
links and the integration of bioenergy in horizontal policies  such as the modernization of  the 
Common Agriculture Policy, the Circular Economy Action Plan –where it has been barely 
addressed- or the State Aid Guidelines.

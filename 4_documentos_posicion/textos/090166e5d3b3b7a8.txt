RENEWABLE ENERGY DIRECTIVE  
 EHP contribution to the IIA Roadmap 
Delivering sustainable heating and cooling is fundamental to achieve Europe’s climate neutrality 
ambition. District heating and cooling (DHC) can contribute  to advancing this goal, supporting the  
delivery of the energy and climate objectives outlined in the EU Green Deal.  
Achieving net-zero carbon emissions by 2050 implies the deep implementation of existing rules , 
particularly given that REDII adopted in 2018 is still being transposed in most EU Member States. It could 
also trigger the need to look at further means (financial support) to reach a higher level of ambition and 
possibly revise legislation.   
Euroheat & Power, the association representing the district heating and cooling sector in Europe and 
beyond, welcomes the European Commission’s initiative to assess the possibility to rev ise the 
Renewable Energy Directive (REDII) and would like to make the following recommendations for the 
impact assessment. 
1. Set an ambitious renewable heating and cooling decarbonisation target for the entire sector 
EHP supports an increased ambition to decarbonise the heating and cooling sector (article 23), as was 
mooted in the 2030 Target Plan.  To ensure fair competition among different heat supply options, t his 
target should apply to the entire heating and cooling sector. Waste heat, as well as RES based electricity 
used in DHC systems, should be fully eligible to be counted towards the achievement of the target and 
adequate financial support should be made available (see the following points). 
2. Strengthen the financial support for the development of modern district heating and cooling 
As acknowledged by the Energy System Integration Strategy and the 2030 Climate Target Plan, DHC can 
“connect local demand with renewable and waste energy sources, as well as the wider electric and gas 
grid – contributing to the optimisation of supply and demand across energy carriers”. Support for district 
energy infrastructure development should be strengthened , covering both new networks and the 
modernisation of existing systems . Support should address emerging applications such as power-to-
heat, large-scale thermal storage and renewable gases and be available across the relevant financing 
EU instruments. 
3. Better enable the use of locally available yet largely unused waste heat potential 
EHP welcomed the recognition of waste heat in the current directive to increase circularity and energy 
efficiency. We support a stable and enabling framework to promote waste heat across EU legislation. 
While it is too early to measure the imp act of R EDII in this field , treating waste heat on par with 
renewables will be beneficial to encourage Member States to advance waste heat recovery, in line with 
the energy efficiency first principle. Additional focus should be put on waste heat from the tertiary 
sector, as well as ensuring recovery of the vast potential recovery from hydrogen production.

4. Clarify the accounting of renewable energy in the heating and cooling sector (article 23) 
There should be a n adequate methodology to determine the share of  renewable energy used in the 
heating and cooling sector, in particular concerning the accounting of renewable gases and electricity 
used in power to heat applications. Such necessary changes will contribute to accelerate energy system 
integration.   
5. Focus on the implementation and compliance with the biomass sustainability criteria 
Sustainable biomass will continue to play an important role in  the energy transition and contribute to 
the decarbonisation of DHC. It is one of the available solution s to cut emissions and reduce the 
dependence on fossil fuels. Achieving the general and renewable heating and cooling targets will require 
predictable and stable conditions fo r investments. Therefore, the focus should be on t he 
implementation of the existing stringent sustainability criteria set out in RED II and related upcoming 
compliance guideline . This  will ensure that the biomass, used for efficient district heat production, 
meets a high standard of environmental protection while maintaining the legal certainty required for 
the development of new projects.  
6. Consider the interface and synergies with other key legislation of the EU energy framework  
The recently adopted energy package, in particular, the revised Energy Efficiency Directive and REDII 
ensure a better  alignment of the key provisions for the decarbonisation of the heating and cooling 
sector. This approach should continue to prevail.   
7. Setting a uniform CO2 price across the heating sector to ensure a level -playing field and drive 
decarbonisation  
A necessary change to drive decarbonisation, in support of the dispositions of REDII, is to ensure a level-
playing field in the heating sector via the introduction of a price for CO2 – either via fiscal means (in the 
forthcoming Energy Taxation Directive review) or the extension of the scope of the EU ETS to buildings, 
as outlined in the 2030 Climate Target Plan.

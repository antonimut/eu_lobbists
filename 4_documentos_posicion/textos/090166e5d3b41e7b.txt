ENERGY EFFICIENCY DIRECTIVE  
 EHP contribution to the IA Roadmap 
Euroheat & Power, the association representing the district heating and cooling sector in Europe and 
beyond, welcomes the European C ommission initiative to assess the effectiveness of the Energy 
Efficiency Directive (EED) in delivering the energy and climate objectives announced in  the EU Green 
Deal.   
While the implementation of the 2018 EED is still ongoing in most of the EU Member States, in light of 
a potential revision of the directive, we believe that the following points should be addressed: 
• The energy efficiency first principle should be at the core of all the EU energy and climate policies. 
More than ever, primary energy savings across the whole energy supply ch ain are needed at the 
EU and national levels to recover from the current economic crisis. 
 
• A better alignment of the provisions of the EPBD, EED and RED is needed to address those barriers 
that hamper the deployment of energy efficiency, particularly in the heating and cooling sector. 
The comprehensive assessments (CAs) and long-term renovation strategies (LTRS) should become 
a joint exercise. Improving the energy performance of buildings and promoting sustainable heating 
and cooling are the two sides of the same coin . The two types of measures will need to work in 
tandem to decarbonise the energy sector as quickly and cost-efficiently as possible. 
 
• Article 14 has helped to put heating and cooling on the agenda of Member States. This is especially 
true for countries typically dominated by gas and oil heating. In the perspective of the Green Deal 
implementation, a stronger link between the identification of the potential for  efficient heating 
and cooling, and policy measures taken at national level, is needed.  
 
• Waste heat should play a bigger role in the revision : the identification and access to information 
on waste heat recovery potential and sources is key and should be highlighted, for instance, in the 
work carried out by Member States under the EED article 14. These analyses will need to translate 
into national strategies to ensure the use of  waste heat, as an integral part of decarbonisation.  
Additional focus should be put on waste heat from the tertiary sector, as well as ensuring recovery 
of the vast potential from hydrogen production. 
 
• With regards to article 5 on the “exemplary role of public buildin gs”, the obligation to renovate 
should be extended to all public buildings and the “district approach” concept should be introduced 
in this article  to encourage members states to develop a more holistic approach . The d istrict 
approach refers to the holistic renovation schemes which are applied to a number of buildings in a 
pre-defined spatial area, such as districts or neighbourhoods. Such an approach has numerous 
advantages compared to building -by-building renovation measures , as it provides a  clearer 
overview of the needs of that particular district/neighbour hood and allows for a more rational 
energy planning that effectively matches available carbon free sources (RES, waste heat ) with local 
demand.

• On article 15: high-efficiency CHP - coupled with power-to-heat applications and thermal storage 
– has a significant role to play to complement the increasing share of intermittent renewable 
production. Any revision of the article should recognise the positive contribution these 
technologies can play in increasing efficiency , lowering costs for infrastructure deployment 
(efficient production close to consumption) , balancing the power system  and easing capacity 
constraints in urban areas. 
 
• On Primary Energy Factor (PEF): any review of the value should be based on a solid assessment of 
the evolution of the power mix. It should be in line  with standard FprEN 17423 to determine PEF 
and CO2 emissions.

 
 
EU RENEWABLE ENERGY RULES REVIEW 
LANDWÄRME CONTRIBUTION 
Landwärme is fully committed to reaching a carbon-neutral European Union and welcomes the inception 
impact assessment of the European Commission aiming to target sectors such as transport, heat and gas 
where the process of de -fossilization has been slow so far. Binding targets and measures are needed in 
these sectors to accelerate the GHG emissions reduction and to ensure that renewable energy is deployed 
across all sectors throughout the continent. 
OUR VISION FOR MAKING EUROPE A CLIMATE NEUTRAL CONTINENT BY 2050: 
HORIZONTAL BASIC PRINCIPLES 
Level Playing Field for renewable gases: Define technology and source neutrality  
Technology and source neutrality for all renewable gases should constitute a top priority within all strategy 
and policy documents as well as for regulatory frameworks, incentive systems and in the development of  
new gas networks.  
Equal treatment of renewable energy carriers 
All renewable energy carriers should be treated equally in terms of funding, demand stimulation and tax 
benefits. Any quota system (in the fuel or heating sector) must treat renewable energy carriers equally, while 
at the same time clearly distinguishing them from fossil energy sources such as natural gas.  
Avoid a one-size-fits-all process  
Defining these above principles would enable Member States to follow different pathways  to climate 
neutrality, depending on their respective starting points, ressources and policy choices. 
SMART COMBINATION OF MATURE TECHNOLOGIES AND UPCOMING NEW TECHNOLOGIES 
New technologies such as power -to-gas and hydrogen are still in a phase of immaturity. Thus, their 
contribution to reaching the 2050 targets will be more significant than those for 2030 . In the meantime , 
mature technologies such as biomethane and biogas are ready to deliver 2030 targets at the lowest possible 
cost and with the utmost impact.  
Biomethane represents the most suitable energy carrier for successful sector coupling. Belonging primarily 
to the agricultural sector, it sustainably secures jobs in rural areas. Across the entire value chain up to  
600 000 jobs can be creaated and preserved, thus strengthening regions suffering from economic decline. 
RECOMMENDED ACTIONS 
To meet the Union’s 2030 and 2050 climate objectives, while also guaranteeing sustainable, secure and  
affordable energy for consumers, we suggest approaching “Option 5” of the Inception Impact Assesment.  
SHORT-TERM MEASURES 
Reshape binding EU targets  
 Increase of the minimum EU targets  for renewable energy  as well as the sub -target for advanced 
biofuels

 A binding renewable gas target of 11% in final gas consumption within the EU by 2030 combined with 
the targets on installed capacity: 40 GW of green hydrogen (as included in the EU’s Hydrogen Strategy) 
and 46 GW of biomethane (in line with the realistic potential of 370-375 TWh by 2030) estimated in the 
Gas Decarbonisatio n Pathways study  of the Gas for Climate Consorstium. Each renewable energy 
source (such as biomethane ,green hydrogen as well as biogas ) shall contribute at least 25%  to the 
target in order to ensure equal treatment of renewables. 
 A binding quota to increase the share of renewable energy in the heating sector as an annual average 
calculated for each of the periods 2021 to 2025 and 2026 to 2030. All fossil fuel  consumptionin the 
heating sector should respectively be obliged to meet this minimum share of renewable energy.  
 Removing the multipliers (douple or multiple counting) for renewable energy supplied to the transport 
sector and clarifying the mass -balancing rules for traceability  purposes; i generally simplifying the 
framework for the use of renewable energy in the transport sector while also avoiding over-regulation. 
 A minimum share of renewable energy in specific industy sectors (e.g. steel or chemicals). 
 Member States need to be given a scope of action in order  to develop different types of renewable 
energy carrie rs according to their national potentia l but at the same time should be prohibited to 
exclude any of the potential renewable pathways. 
Create one integrated renewable energy market without barriers 
 Legal barriers prevent cross-border trading of biomethane: Despite the European Court ruling in 2017, 
several EU countries continue to implement import restrictions that do not only threaten current 
biomethane flow but would also put any future power -to-gas and hydrogen flows at risk. Mass -
balancing via gas grids within and across national boarders must be fully implemented and accepted 
in national and EU regulation Additionally, it should be legally clarified how greenhouse gas emission 
targets and the renewable energy targets are accounted for when renewable energy is traded across 
borders. 
 Calculation of the share of energy from renewable sources in EU Member States must be revised. The 
current statistical practice in relation to biomethane injected into the gas grid does not comply with the 
recent developments of the cross-border trade with biomethane. 
Avoide double or multiple incentives  
 State aid rules and/or internal energy market legislation have to ensure that any ongoing support is  
granted in a way that promotes free competition and market integration. Recital (123) of the current 
Renewable Energy Directive must be amended to cover all aspects leading to distortive effects caused 
by interfering subsidy schemes of different Member States.  
 Market players shall be in a position to switch between support schemes, or to pay back the subsidy in 
the case of export o f renewable gases .This would result in avoiding  multiple subsidi zation or any 
distortive effects such as price decline caused by the trade with subsidy volumes. Export and import 
control mechanism must be established if DG COMP approves a support scheme of a Member State 
with regional or international effects on the renewable gas market 
Promote and financing existing solutions: 
 Individual regional biogas grids: for example in Baden-Württemberg (Germany), that are detached from 
the puplic natural gas grid, have to be promoted. Corresponding solutions enable direct transportation 
of biogas from rural areas to CHP plants in urban regions . Without the need for upgrading to 
biomethane this is highly cost effective. 
 
 
 Sustainable city concepts (Linköping, SWE): district heating is connected to excess heat sources from 
industry and CHP plants deploying bioenergy or from waste water treatment  plants. Organic waste 
(from households, industry and agriculture etc) or wastewater is separated and fermented to produce 
biogas. This isthenupgraded to biomethane to fuel public transport and CNG cars and/or injected into 
the grid for further use (e.g. liquification for heavy transport use) . Nutrients are recycled as organic 
fertilizer, as well as processed sewage.  
 Sale of 100 % biomethane has recently become available at CNG filling stations in Sweden and in the 
Netherlands. Germany will follow by the end of 2020. 
MID-TERM MEASURES 
Prioritize CO2 taken from the atmosphere in CCS solutions 
 CCS is currently primarily performed in connection with fossil energy carriers, which unsustainbly 
extends fossil fuel production. CCS can be accomplished in connection with bio energy (BECCS) or by 
directly extracting CO2 from the atmosphere (Direct Air Capture, DAC) . These methods  a far more 
effective for decarbonisation as negative emissions are acchieved by actively removing CO2 from the 
atmosphere instead of reducing emissions from outdated fossil sources . Assessing CCS solutions, 
BECCS and DAC should always have first priority over fossil CO2. 
Exploit synergy effects of different ongoing EU strategies 
 Promoting the benefits of sector coupling via biomethane: bridging the energy sector and agriculture, 
waste management industry as well as the food industry.  
 Promoting the positive effects from obligatory fermentation of organic waste and manure: prioritize 
fermentation over waste combustion in value chains. 
 Promoting projects for digestate use as organic fertilizer s and melioration: The greenhouse gas 
emission savings related to the replacement of fossil fertilizers should be fully taken into account in  
GHG calculation  of biogas production – organic fertilizers help to recycle essential nutrients, thus 
contributing to a circular economy.  
Finally, soci o-economic impacts of the preceding measures must be accounted for as well. Biomethane 
represents the perfect energy carrier for successful sector coupling also in terms of economic development. 
Biomethane production itself is a very work force intense energy sou rce. Thus, the more the biomethane 
sector is strengthened, the higher the impact for employment creation and securing especially in rural areas. 
Across the entire value chain, biomethane production can provide up to  
600 000 jobs and therefore support regions suffering from economic decline.

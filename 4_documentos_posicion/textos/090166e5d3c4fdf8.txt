 
Feedback to the EU renewable energy rules – review 
 
 
Introduction 
The EC has announced that the proposed increase of Europe’s greenhouse gas emissions reduction target from 40 to 
55% by 2030 may require a revision of the Renewable Energy Directive (RED II). Transport is mentioned as one of the 
key sectors where more renewables could be deployed and a more ambitious target for renewable transport could be 
required. The Port of Rotterdam, as a renewable energy hub, supports a higher ambition for renewable transport to 
unlock the full market potential for renewable fuels, provided that the following preconditions are met to ensure effective 
functioning of the RED. 
 
Revision of RED II: A higher target required for renewable transport  
The Port of Rotterdam supports option 3 in the inception impact assessment for the review of the RED for the 
renewable transport target  (art. 25). The RED II target for the share of renewables in transport determines the market 
uptake of these fuels between 2021 and 2030. In the Netherlands the current RED II ambition of 14% by 2030 will be 
easy to meet and a higher national target has already been set. A higher European obligation of 24% for the transport 
sector is feasible and will stimulate companies to make onward investments in renewable transport.  
 
A more ambitious target is feasible, provided that possible unwanted market effects related to the RED implementation 
are prevented. Some of the legal measures as presented in option 4 could contribute to a higher share of renewables 
and a more efficient and harmonised structure of the RED. The impact of these measures however is still difficult to 
assess and can be both positive and negative. A clarification of these measures is needed to enable a thorough market 
consultation.  
 
RED III: A longer term perspective for the ambition in 2040 
Next to a higher short term ambition for 2030, the Port of Rotterdam also recommends to give direction to the midterm 
ambition for 2040 to give market players the confidence to further invest in the necessary production capacity. An 
ambition of 40% by 2040 would be needed to meet the climate objectives of the Paris Climate Change Agreement. The 
period up to 2030 is required to create the right policy context to enable the required scale-up of renewables.  
 
A potential sub target for different modalities 
A higher general target for renewable transport can only be achieved if the EU provides targeted support for all energy 
carriers and all modalities. The general target for renewable transport could possibly be combined with a sub target per 
modality. A customized approach is needed both for modalities that have been working on implementation over the 
years and modalities that are still in an early stage. Shipping and aviation, for instance, are not yet included in the Paris 
Climate Change Agreement. In view of their energy consumption, however, these sectors can achieve significant GHG 
emissions reductions when they deploy more renewables. In the Netherlands both sectors are prepared to move in this 
direction. Since they do not yet have an obligation yet, it seems logical to define a realistic growth path in consultation 
with the sector. Obvious, these sectors need time to take the step towards more renewable and low carbon fuels.

Transparent and unequivocal implementation  
The Port of Rotterdam aims to become a key renewable energy hub in Europe.  This calls for a rapid development of all 
types of biofuels, electrification, hydrogen and low carbon fuels. To realise this ambition a clear outline is needed of the 
national implementation schemes of the RED to ensure cooperation and fair competition between the member states. 
There are wide variation in the national implementation of RED II, for instance in the double counting schemes. Due to 
these variations some fuel categories and feedstocks will have a higher value in some member states than in others, 
which will result in all kinds of unwanted side effects. To avoid unnecessary movements of feedstocks and fuels and 
other system inefficiencies harmonization of targets and instruments is essential to create a level playing field on the 
European market.  
 
Harmonisation of the policy aim: energy or CO2  
The RED focuses on the share of renewable energy and the related energy value of renewable energy categories. This 
focus has been effective to increase the share of renewables over the last decade, but does not provide sufficient 
insights in the related GHG emissions reduction. The current  implementation of RED II shows that some member 
states are therefore shifting to a policy focus on CO2 reduction and a derived energy value. These diverging policy 
approaches are undesirable.  Germany’s policy focus on CO2 reduction will for instance provide a pull for fuels with the 
lowest carbon footprint. A potential increase of the ambition of RED II requires a harmonized policy focus and a single 
unit of measurement. Since CO2 reduction is the ultimate aim of the RED, the focus could be shifted to CO2, starting 
with potential new agreements under RED for modalities like aviation and shipping under RED. 
 
REDII as part of an integrated mix of instruments 
A well-balanced system for renewables and low carbon fuels requires a close cooperation between market players 
throughout the value chain. The current RED system intends to increase the share of renewables, but does not 
stimulate all steps in the value chain, including production, storage, trade, infrastructure, modality and end-user. RED or 
other related instruments should aim for a more integrated system and incentives for market players in different stages 
of the supply chain. 
 
With a higher target for renewable transport an integrated mix of  market instruments becomes even more import. It is 
necessary to assess the RED instruments in relation to the ETS, the CO2 tax and a set of other instruments. It should 
also be guaranteed that the revenues of these instruments are earmarked and channeled back to the market parties 
concerned to ensure future investments in renewable and low carbon transports. The impact assessment should 
assess and outline how the different instruments can be employed to stimulate the uptake of renewables.  
 
Impact Assessment  
The aforementioned recommendations about the functioning of the RED  are essential to work towards an effective 
system for renewable transport. A thorough Impact Assessment is required to provide a clear view per energy carrier, 
modality and feedstock category. Market players in the value chain, including production, storage, trade, infrastructure 
and modality, need to be involved.

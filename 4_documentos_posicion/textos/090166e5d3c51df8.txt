Revision of Directive (EU) 2018/2001 on the promotion of the 
use of energy from renewable resources 
 
1. VDB as representative of the biofuel producers in Germany 
The Association of the German Biofuel Industry (Verband der Deutschen Bio-
kraftstoffindustrie e. V. - VDB) represents the interests of 15 biofuel producers 
in Germany with a production capacity of 2.3 million tons of biodiesel (and 600 
GWh of biomethane). This equals approximately 60% of the total biodiesel ca-
pacity in Germany in the transport sector.  
 
2. Remarks on the initiative 
VDB welcomes the Commission’s initiative to revise RED II. 
As the European Green Deal sets up the target to achieve a reduction of 90% in 
transport-related GHG emissions by 2050, investments in clean technologies 
and sustainable alternative fuels are essential to reach the transition that is 
needed. Therefore, it is necessary to define the regulatory framework for the 
use of climate-friendly energy sources. Biofuels, electromobility, electricity- 
based fuels and enhanced energy efficiency together make climate protection in 
transport possible. Without renewable fuels not enough GHG reductions can be 
achieved in road transport and the GHG budget will be exhausted.  
Biofuels such as biodiesel, bioethanol and biomethane have saved around 9.5 
million tonnes of greenhouse gases in 2018 in Germany alone. It is evident that 
the GHG balance of the transport sector would be even more devastating if bio-
fuels did not make a substantial contribution to climate protection. Compared 
to fossil fuels, biofuels reduce greenhouse gases by up to 90%. The entire pro-
duction process is considered here, starting with the emissions from the agricul-
tural cultivation, transport, and the industrial processing of the raw materials. 
Biofuels in the EU are obliged to comply with the EU sustainability criteria.

Considering the backlog of the transport sector concerning GHG abatement and the limited effect of 
measures taken until now, it seems appropriate to make use of several instruments - biofuels being 
an integral part of the solution - to achieve an appropriate contribution of transport to the climate 
targets of the European Union:  
• The recast of the Renewable Energy Directive (RED II) remains insufficiently ambitious: A higher 
sub-target of 27% for the transport sector is mandatory if the EU’s climate targets should be like-
ly to be met. It is vital that the targets align with scenarios that comply with the required savings 
of GHG emissions as fixed in the Effort Sharing Regulation (ESR), taking into account realistic pro-
gress in transport (vehicle efficiency, change of modal split and reduction of transport demand) 
as well as realistic contributions of the other ESR sectors. With a GHG reduction target of 55% 
pending, a higher target than 27% RES must be reached. 
• Least would be to make sure that the physical share of renewable energy in the transport sector 
will be significantly higher in 2030 than in 2020. Due to multiple counting, out of the nominal 
14% target a physical share of only 7.5% remains for Germany, resulting in practically no increase 
in comparison to 2020. As the 14% target can even be reduced by 7 percentage points by Mem-
ber States that renounce to the use of crop-based biofuels, nominally 7% renewable energies 
remain that make up less than 3,5% as physical share. This means halving renewable energy in 
transport until 2030 which is absurd.  
• As crop-based biofuels have by far the biggest effect to reduce GHG emissions in the existing 
vehicle fleet until 2030, regulations are necessary to maintain their volumes. A reduction of the 
iLUC-free cap - as foreseen in the RED II - must be abandoned. 
• The possibility according to Article 26 (1) that Member States may reduce the minimum share 
referred to in the first subparagraph of Article 25(1) by a maximum of 7 percentage points has to 
be relinquished. 
• Adequate promotion of new types of renewable fuels: Safeguarding and sustainably extending 
the contribution of established as well as advanced biofuels must be key elements of the revised 
RED II transport target. Therefore, a phasing out of crop-based biofuels is unacceptable from an 
investment security point of view and detrimental for the GHG reduction in road transport on 
the whole. 
• The inclusion of new feedstocks into Annex IX must be reconsidered. The Appendix “Suggested 
way forward for evaluated feedstocks”, pp. 6-15 of the consortium in charge (E4tech et al.) al-
ready clearly shows that there are substantial concerns within the consortium and stakeholders 
with respect to the inclusion of certain feedstocks into the Annex. The establishment of guiding 
principles for the introduction of new feedstocks has to be considered seriously: These feed-
stocks must not have relevant alternative uses (direct use as animal feed or use as chemical 
building block, e.g.) and must not be manifestly susceptible to fraud. 
• VDB advocates a continuation and enhancement of the Fuel Quality Directive (FQD). The Di-
rective provides the legal basis for GHG-specific regulations in Article 7a. This regulation interacts 
with the transport target of the RED II: The FQD fosters the use of biofuels with high GHG sav-

ings. In such a way, the FQD works as an efficiency tool as with the same volume of biofuels more 
GHG can be reduced. Therefore, the RED II should be accompanied by a recast of the FQD Art. 7a.  
• Emission reductions of fuels are indispensable to address the huge vehicle fleet stock with inter-
nal combustion engines (ICE) still on European roads in 2030. In Germany for instance, scientific 
calculations estimate an ICE fleet of approximately 35-40 million in 2030. Achieving 2030-GHG 
targets requires decarbonising the fuel used in these vehicles. A one-sided political focus on elec-
tric mobility will therefore by far not be enough. Instead, in addition to 6.5 Mio. electric vehicles 
a share of 20% renewable liquid and gaseous fuels is needed for Germany according to the esti-
mation of the German Federal Government (Energiereferenzprognose) in 2030. 
• The strong expansion of e-mobility, hydrogen and other electricity-based fuels will significantly 
reduce the amount of fuel counting in the denominator according to Article 27 para. 1 lit. a in the 
future. This will also decrease the proportion of renewable fuels, since the mandate is expressed 
as percentage shares. An increase in the percentage of renewable fuels is necessary merely to 
keep the overall amount of renewable fuels stable.  
• However, since additional GHG savings are necessary to comply with the enhanced climate tar-
gets of the European Commission, an increase of the overall amount of renewable fuels is inevi-
table. Consequently, a significant elevation in the mandate for all categories of biofuels is neces-
sary. Crop-based biofuels are the fundament of this development. Looking at the low share of 
renewables in road transport today in most member states, it is obvious that EU policy should 
not be about replacing one sort of biofuels by another - but about adding new biofuels (and oth-
er renewable fuels) to the existing supply.

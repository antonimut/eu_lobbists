Air   Liquide’s    Feedback  
 
 
Air   Liquide   is   acting   globally   in   gases,   technologies   and   services   for   Industry   and   Health.  
 
The  Group  takes  active  measures  for  the  climate  in  its  operations,  with  its  customers  and  ecosystems  by                  
offering  low-carbon  solutions.  We  contribute  to  the  development  of  a  low-carbon  society,  in  particular  by                
developing  biomethane  and  by  promoting  hydrogen  which  will  play  a  key  role  in  the  energy  transition  and                  
the  fight  against  climate  change.  With  its  customers,  the  Group  is  committed  to  working  towards  a  clean                  
and  sustainable  industry.  Thanks  to  its  essential  molecules  management  (oxygen,  hydrogen,  carbon             
dioxide...)   and   the   in-depth   knowledge   of   its   customers’   processes,   Air   Liquide   offers   technologies   which  
allow   them   to   improve   the   energy   efficiency   of   their   industrial   processes   and   reduce   their   emissions.  
 
 
AL  welcomes  the  initiative  to  revise  the  RED  II  in  the  context  of  the  European  Green  Deal  (EGD)  and  of                     
the   post   Covid-19   Economic   recovery   package.  
 
The  Renewable  Energy  Directive  needs  to  provide  a  framework  to  achieve  a  very  ambitious  decrease  of                 
the  GHG  emissions  at  acceptable  cost  for  the  society.  We  believe  the  cost  of  a  transition  relying  fully  and                    
only  on  renewable  energy  is  too  heavy  to  achieve  the  ambition  within  the  timing  required  to  control                  
climate  change.  The  cost  to  achieve  GHG  emission  targets  should  be  minimized  by  incentivizing               
simultaneously  the  increase  in  renewable  energy  production  and  the  best  use  of  still  abundant  fossil                
resources.  Fossil  resources  have  a  very  significant  role  to  play  to  allow  an  economically  and  industrially                 
sustainable   transition   provided   that   GHG   emissions   resulting   from   their   use   are   minimized.   
 
We   believe   that   a   revision   of   the   RED   2   should   be   done   according   to   option   5   combining   options   2,   3   and  
4,   taking   into   account   the   following   considerations.  
 
Increasing   the   targets   and   setting   the   objectives   in   terms   of   GHG   emissions   reduction  
Raising   the   ambition   level   /   Increasing   the   targets   is   crucial   in   the   context   of   the   EGD.   
Significantly  more  ambitious  GHG  reduction  targets  in  line  with  the  EGD  shall  be  accompanied  by  the                 
possibility  to  count  the  contribution  of  renewable  and  non  renewable  low  carbon  energy  in  order  to                 
achieve  the  desired  climate  impact.  We  welcome  the  idea  to  include  measures  to  increase  the  penetration                 
of   low   carbon   fuels   as   mentioned   under   option   4.  
 
Providing   a   level   playing   field   for   all   pathways  
Under  the  current  RED2,  the  objectives  are  set  as  a  %  of  RE  in  the  supplied  or  produced  energy.  For                     
transport  targets,  Multipliers  are  used  to  ensure  level  playing  field  between  different  energies  with               
different  Tank  to  Wheel  efficiencies.  Providing  a  level  playing  field  for  the  evaluation  of  the  different                 
energy  pathways  with  regards  to  the  evaluation  of  their  contribution  to  GHG  reduction  is  of  paramount                 
importance.  
Using  Well  to  Wheel  emission  reduction  targets  instead  of  RE  content  targets  would  achieve  such  a  level                  
playing   field   while   providing   better   readability.

The  emission  assessments  should  also  take  into  account  so  called  CAPEX  emissions  in  order  to  provide                 
a  fair  evaluation  of  the  GHG  reduction  benefits.  This  is  especially  true  for  RE  electricity  from  wind  or  PV.                    
The  emissions  related  to  CAPEX  may  have  a  very  significant  impact  depending  on  the  efficiency  of  the                  
processes  used  to  convert  it  to  fuels  (RFNBO).  CAPEX  emissions  must  be  considered  to  make  sure  the                  
actual   impact   of   those   pathways   are   properly   taken   into   account.  
The  impact  of  RE  Low  carbon  gases  (Biomethane  and  other  BioGases)  when  used  as  intermediate                
products  for  the  production  of  conventional  fuels  should  be  recognized  along  with  RFNBO  to  allow  a  fair                  
market   competition   between   the   different   pathways.  
 
Stimulating   the   demand   -   Establish   market   for   environmental   attributes  
The   revised   RED2   should   aim   at   creating   market   pull   for   RE   and   low   carbon   energy.  
It  should  promote  a  market  based  approach  for  the  commercialization  of  low  carbon  and  renewable                
energy  through  a  Guarantee  of  Origin  system  that  can  be  used  to  evidence  the  environmental                
characteristics   of   energy   consumed/supplied   for   the   purpose   of   complying   with   consumption   targets.   
Creating  market  pull  must  result  in  demand  for  RE  energy  exceeding  the  offer.  This  will  increase  the                  
willingness  to  pay  for  RE  energies  helping  to  finance  its  deployment.  As  soon  as  demand  exceeds  the                  
offer,   additionality   will   automatically   be   achieved.  
A  robust  and  fraud  resistant  GO  system  is  key  to  reach  those  goals.  The  possibility  opened  in  RED2  for                    
member  states  to  extend  GO  to  non  RE  electricity  and  gases  should  become  an  obligation  as  well  as  full                    
disclosure  so  that  consumers  of  highly  emitting  energy  bear  the  responsibility  of  the  corresponding               
emissions.  
The  temporal  correlation  mentioned  in  RED  II  should  apply  only  to  electricity  as  Gas  and  Hydrogen  are  by                   
nature   storable.  
We   recommend   to   suppress   the   reference   to   geographical   correlation,   physical   link   and   grid   congestion.   
Indeed,  if  a  RE  energy  producer  is  able  to  have  GO  issued  for  a  given  amount  of  energy,  it  means  that  it                       
has  been  able  to  inject  it  in  the  distribution  infrastructure  and  that  no  congestion  prevented  the  said                  
amount  of  energy  to  be  used.  Therefore,  the  RE  energy  claimed  at  point  of  use  thanks  to  the  cancellation                    
of   the   corresponding   GO   effectively   corresponds   to   energy   produced    and   consumed .  
Stimulating  circular  economy  involves  giving  a  value  to  production  outputs  considered  before  as  waste               
(referred   to   as   unavoidable   and   unintentional   in   the   current   RED   2).  
As  soon  as  they  have  an  economic  value,  such  output  should  be  considered  as  products  and  bear  a  GHG                    
foot   print   established   through   allocation   methods   compliant   with   international   standards.  
 
 
Align   with   the   EU   ETS   directive,   Energy   System   Integration   Strategy   and   the   Hydrogen   Strategy   with  
regards   to   the   guarantees   of   origin   for   renewable   and   decarbonized   hydrogen.  
 
Further  clariﬁcations  on  guarantees  of  origin  for  renewable  and  decarbonized  hydrogen  are  necessary  as                            
a  basis  for  reduction  of  emissions  reported  under  EU  ETS  (in  analogy  to  biomass  -  (EU)  2018/2066,                                  
Article   38.2)   and   in   alignment   with   the   Energy   System   Integration   Strategy   and   the   Hydrogen   Strategy.   
 
Directive  (EU)  2018/2001  (RED  II)  provides  a  deﬁnition  and  criteria  of  the  guarantees  of  origin  for                                
biomass  (e.g.  biogas)  that  result  in  “zero”  emission  factor  in  reported  emissions  under  EU  ETS  (EU)                                
2018/2066.  As  per  analogy,  the  renewable  and  decarbonized  hydrogen  which  could  be  blended  within  the                              
natural  gas  grid  mix  (conﬁrmed  by  guarantees  of  origin),  should  be  recognized  as  well  under  the                                
Monitoring  and  Reporting  of  emissions  at  revised  EU  ETS  directive  as  a  means  to  reduce  reported                                
emissions   and   to   avoid   double   counting   of   already   reported   emissions   in   case   of   decarbonised   hydrogen.  
 
RED  II  provides  already  a  solution  in  recital  (59)  and  in  Article  19.7b(ii),  allowing  Member  States  to  extend                                    
the  guarantees  of  the  origin  system  to  renewable  gas  (e.g.  biogas-based  or  electricity-based  renewable                            
hydrogen)  as  well  as  energy  from  non-renewable  sources  (e.g.  fossil-based  hydrogen  with  carbon                          
capture).  However  further  clariﬁcations  in  revision  of  RED  II  are  necessary  in  qualiﬁcations  criteria  and                              
deﬁnitions  of  labels  for  different  types  of  hydrogen  as  described  in  Hydrogen  Strategy  and  Energy  System                                
Integration  Strategy.  Those  shall  be  further  reﬂected  in  the  revised  Monitoring  and  Reporting  Rules  of                              
emissions   under   EU   ETS.

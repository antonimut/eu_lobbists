Roadmap on Revision of Directive (EU) 2018/2001 on the promotion of the use of energy 
from renewable sources 
 
21/09/2020 
Repsol as a global multi -energy provider, shares the world’s ambition to reach climate neutrality in 
the framework of the Paris Agreement as well as the EU’s 2050 carbon neutrality objective. In this 
sense, on December 2 nd, 2019, Repsol published its aim to become a net zero carbon emissions 
company by 2050, making it the first oil & gas company in the world to assume this ambition . This 
aim continues being valid amidst the current extraordinary conditions. Our  new business 
opportunities include renewable generation, low-C fuels, gases and chemicals production, as well as 
other technologies that will contribute to achieve this goal.  
Repsol’s refineries are engaged in a number of innovative projects that are directly impacted by the 
Renewable Energy Directive . These  projects will deliver low -carbon fuels and, under the right 
regulatory conditions, can be scaled up to deliver significant quantities of low-carbon liquid fuels that 
will contribute to the 2050 climate neutrality objective. The first project, earmarked with €60 million 
initial investment, involves building one of the largest net zero emissions synthetic fuel production 
plants in the world, based on hydrogen generated with renewable energy.  
The second project will represent an initial investment of 20 million euros. It will consist of a plant for 
generation of gas from urban waste. The generated gas will be used to replace part of the traditional 
fuels currently used in one of our refineries. 
Moreover, Repsol is continuing to innovate in the biofuel production, and recently has successfully 
completed the production of t he Spanish market's first batch of aviation biofuels. This milestone 
enables the Company to advance in the production of low -carbon fuels for industries such as 
aviation, where alternatives like electrification are not viable today. More batches of aviation biofuel 
will continue to be manufactured at other facilities of the Group across Spain and through initiatives 
using biofuels derived from waste at a later time. 
Since EU leadership in R&D will be indispensable in further expanding the range of available  
technologies, we strongly support the EU’s stated aim to create a nurturing investment environment, 
mindful of the long life cycle of industrial projects and to de-risk the capital raised by early movers. 
Repsol welcomes the European Commission’s (E C) intention to review the RED II , as well as the 
opportunity to provide feedback to this Initial Impact Assessment. 
Considering all the points above and after reviewing the EC’s document we would like to share our 
thoughts and recommendations: 
We acknowledge the context for the revision of this Directive , mainly the need to align the REDII 
objectives with the ongoing revision of Climate Target for 2030 . We also share the main concern of 
increasing the share of renewables in those sectors that have had a slow er decarbonising progress,

despite our understanding that there is still quite large room for the power generation to be 
decarbonised (mainly shifting from coal to natural gas and renewable sources). 
In absence of the outcome of EC’s impact assessment, the  best option to fulfil with the proposed 
objectives in the Roadmap seems to be Option 5 – Possible combinations of Options 2, 3 and 4. 
Regarding the Option 3 – Raising the ambition level of REDII targets, we would like to highlight that 
any such increase of the share of renewable energy should be market -driven and cost-effective and 
strengthen rather than weaken the competitiveness of EU’s industry. 
For Option 4 – Amend REDII to translate into legal measures the actions proposed in other energy 
strategies of the European Green Deal, our suggestions are: 
- Ensure a neutral technological approach  - The revision of the RED  II should stimulate 
competition among various suppliers and technologies and bring down the cost of renewable 
energy sources, while providing  sufficient investment certainty in Europe. In this sense, setting 
targets for specific technologies would not promote the most cost -effective and sustainable 
solution for meeting decarbonisation objectives. 
- Ensure a clear and stable regulatory framework - The full-scale development of technologies for 
the production of low-carbon fuels requires policies providing clear, stable and strong regulatory 
signals to investors . It is important that possible changes introduced reinforce certainty for 
investors and do not introduce disruptive elements to the long -term perspective for investment 
provided by the current RED II (e.g. Annex IX can be reviewed to add but not to remove 
feedstocks). Also, first movers should be protected by appropriate ‘grandfathering’ or ‘no 
retroactive’ clauses so that the fuel they produced that fully meets the requirements of the 
mandate can be continued to be considered compliant regardless of any su bsequent changes to 
the mandate. 
- Promote climate neutral carriers and feedstock  – The revision should take into account carriers 
such as clean hydrogen (as defined in the Hydrogen Strategy), sustainable biofuels and synthetic 
liquid and gaseous fuels, not only for the hard -to-decarbonise sectors, but for road transport, 
too. 
- Provide sustainability criteria that are robust and supportive of the targets set - We would 
welcome an assessment of how the R ED II could be used to promote the use of low -carbon fuels 
such as those using hydrogen produced in combination with carbon capture and st orage, while 
avoiding any double counting. 
- Develop a terminology and European -wide-criteria for the certification of renewable and low -
carbon fuels where consistent criteria on a GHG emission basis are applied across all 
renewable and low -carbon fuels . We support the continued use of voluntary schemes to 
evidence that sustainability is accredited and to provide certification as required under t he RED 
II. In this context we support taking well-to-wheel considerations into account. 
- Develop a market for low-carbon fuels through sufficiently high and stable carbon price signals to 
support business cases for alternative fuels for transport. 
- Consider the increasing role of circularity of resources and related technological solutions.

We would like to underline the  current co -existence of two overlapping mandates on the fuel 
supplier after 2020: one is covered by the RED II as the renewable energy use obligation in road 
transport, while the other resides into the Fuel Quality Directive (FQD) as an obligation to redu ce the 
carbon intensity of the road fuels used (FQD – Article 7a). This dual regulation should also be 
assessed to avoid any double fuel regulation on fuel suppliers. 
We look forward to continue participating actively in the development of this legislative procedure, 
by providing further input to upcoming consultations and addressing other information requests the 
Commission may need.

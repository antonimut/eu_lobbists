 
European Data Centre Association 
EUDCA supports a higher 2030 target  for renewables as DCs already run on 50 -100% of renewable energy 
and are highly energy efficient . However, apart from an increased target, EUDCA thinks that renewables 
should be increasingly deployed into the sectors such as heating and cooling.  Being sustainable for a DC 
consists of two parts: (I) energy that a DC procure; (II) the way that a DC is being operated i.e. how the cooling 
and heating is provided.  
 EUDCA would like to highlight several points  which could help the EC identify how DCs can contribute to a 
more ambitious RED and higher overall GHG reduction targets: 
 
1. Better use of waste heat for a more circular energy system:  In order to improve DCs’ sustainability 
and integrate their waste heat into the district heating, DCs would like to work together with the EC 
on how to explore the uses of waste he at, create more awareness and start steering more into the 
direction of the EU Code of Conduct for Data Center Energy Efficiency under which all DCs have to 
be liquid cooling ready. In that way, high caloric heat could be directly used for energy reuse without 
the current requirement of additional refrigerant compressors and heat pumps. Optimised use of DC 
waste heat will not only accelerate the GHG reduction goals  but will also reduce the load  on the 
electricity grid.   
2. Promote further development and use of sustainable hydrogen as energy storage for wind and solar 
and other low-carbon fuels – Hydrogen operating fuel cells have an advantage of high temperature. 
Coupled with the liquid cooling, this could be the most sustainable solution for the DC industry.  DC 
operators are alread y testing hydrogen fuel cells technology as local energy storage of green 
hydrogen in order to replace diesel fueled back -up generators and get rid of any possible electrical 
usage coming from fossil energy.  Hydrogen technology as energy storage would enable the DCs to 
go off the grid when necessary and even let them deliver energy, thus stabilising the energy supply. 
The storage of large amounts of hydrogen on the DC premises is an issue which we need to further 
study as we continue. To run sustainably there must be a competitive market for green hydrogen - 
the  generation of hydrogen from renewable energy sources.  Moreover, hydrogen that is pumped 
into the pipelines should be clean – if it gets polluted by residual left in the pipes, it will not be able 
to be used in the fuel cells.  
3. Development of the European super grid - The goal is not just to have a few companies building their 
own renewable energy plants and becoming themselves power companies, but rather the complete 
adaptation of sustainable energy by everyone living in Europe. Power purchase agreements and on-
site generation are not yet commonly available in the EU. This causes a mismatch between 
expectation and capability. In Western Europe there is a super grid where all sustainable energy is 
connected. Roughly 50% of the sustainable energy is hydro, which is not in the vicinity of the typical 
DC and therefore susceptible to fraud. If the premium we pay on top of the energy bill for sustainable 
energy would really be reinvested in new production plants for sustainable energy, we would have a 
more acceptable situation accepting hydro – as sustainable energy source - injected into the Western 
European super grid.

Bioenergy Europe’s Reply to the Public Consultation on Inception 
Impact Assessment  
 
Revision of Directive (EU) 2018/2001 on the promotion of the use of energy from 
renewable sources 
 
Bioenergy Europe welcomes  the European Commission initiative evaluating the possible revision of 
the Renewable Energy Directive ((EU) 2018/2001).  
The achievement of carbon-neutrality objective by 2050 will require a prompt adjustment of the mid-
term climate and energy objectives measures. For this reason, Bioenergy Europe welcomes the 2030 
Climate Plans commitment to increase deployment of renewables to achieve higher GHG emissions 
savings.  However, the sector regrets the non -technical wording describing sustainable bioenergy 
contribution in such Communication, as it provides a misleading image of the sector. Indeed, the term 
‘whole trees ’ already misused in the biodiversity strategy, remains troubling. ‘Whole tree ’ is an 
arbitrary designation that does not relate to a particular forest product or grade of wood, rather it 
appeals to emotions. Energy wood is produced as part of forest management, but not driving 
harvesting. Bioenergy improves the economic viability of  low range timber, and wood decayed or 
damaged as a result of disturbances (wild-fires; pests and storms). 
Bioenergy accounts for almost 60% of the current EU renewable energy consumption and according 
to the European Commission Long Term strategic vision 1, and other scientific literature, its 
contribution to the EU energy mix can grow in the next decades  with an improved mobilisation of 
sustainable feedstocks. A recent literature review2 (Faaij, 2018) demonstrated that the contribution of 
sustainable bioenergy can triple, without increasing the dependency of raw material from third 
countries. Analogous to the European Commission Vision 2050, Faaij’s review underlines that a better 
mobilisation of agricultural and forestry residues and utilisation of energy crops, e. g. fast growing 
perennial energy crops, will be necessary for a sustainable growth of the bioenergy sector.  
The review of elements of the Renewable Energy Directive to achieve an alignment to the EGD 
initiatives should be discussed by legislators, following a thorough evaluation of the existing measures 
and requirements. Nevertheless, it should be noted that adoption of RED II was the result of inclusive 
legislative works , and reflects a valid political compromise reached between Member States an d 
European Parliament in 2018. While realignment of energy and climate targets are necessary, a full-
fledged revision of the Directive at this stage seems not to be neither needed nor justified    Further, 
such revision could easily create drastic uncertainty, while market-actors need long-term perspective 
to promote investments and ensure further renewable energy uptake.   
The proposed focus on those sectors like transport and heating and cooling, where renewables have 
not been deployed as successfully as in the electricity sector , is appropriate.  Furthermore, measures 
incentivising bioenergy will have positive economic effect, spilling over to other  agricultural; forest 
based and industrial sectors. Indeed, the bioenergy sector employs more than 700 000 workers and its 
value chain is predominantly European, with more than 45 000 business units (mostly innovative small

Mandatory targets are proven tools to scale up renewable energy on the market, adequate carbon 
pricing could further support their competitiveness . The upcoming revision of the  Energy Taxation

Among the policy options briefly outlined by the European Commission’s roadmap, Bioenergy Europe 
would favour Option 5 entailing a smart combination of Options 2, 3 and 4.  Bioenergy Europe supports 
the following combination:  
1. Option 2  
Non-regulatory policy instruments could improve the performance of bioenergy value chains  and 
facilitate the deployment of new chains that would be needed to rea ch higher RES targets . Through 
training and information campaigns forestry operators and farmers could improve their management 
practices and logistics operators could further reduce their footprint. Best practices in responsible 
sourcing, should be shared  at European level to inspire other stakeholder to adopt similar methods. 
Adequate training programmes would also be key to upskill installers and maintenance operators, 
updating their skills and knowledge to fully participate in the energy transition  Support to small 
medium enterprises and energy communities in improving logistics (e.g. new harvesting machines, 
new storage depots, etc.) could leverage further agricultural residues mobilization. 
2. Option 3 
Increased a mbition and  revision of  targets and sub -target design will be instrumental to the 
achievement of higher Greenhouse gas emissions savings. Special attention should be dedicated to the 
heating and cooling sector that, representing half of current energy consumption, is still dominated by 
fossil fuels ( 79%).  In 2018  bioenergy accounted for 85 % of renewable heat  in the total heat 
consumption. Bioheat is providing decarbonised heat and enabling Member States to meet their long-
term climate objectives. Bioenergy Europe would favour a binding target to incentivise renewable heat 
and have at least half of the heat consumption provided by renewables by 2030 . This target will be 
even more within reach if energy efficiency measures are properly implemented.   
 
With increased investments in district heating and combined heat and power production, biomass, can 
be used in an efficient way to produce both heat and electricity.  
 
Europe needs a binding target to timely lower the emissions from its heating sector: from the analysis 
of the final national energy and climate plans, very few member states focused on the decarbonisation 
of heating and included the required 1.3 pp yearly increment (art.23 REDII) when calculating their 2030 
objectives.

To achieve climate neutrality, a steep reduction in transport emissions is needed by 2050.  All transport 
sectors will need to contribute to the reduction, with a prompt shift to low -emission mobility crucial 
to achieving the EU Green Deal’s ambitious decarbonisation goals.  
For this reason, the 2030 target for renewables in transport must be increased (art.25).   
Biofuels – already the largest renewable energy source in transport – will continue to be a concrete 
and viable solution to reducing road, aviation, and waterborne transport emissions.

3. Option 4 
Bioenergy Europe believes the existing policy framework should be evaluated to make sure it is aligned 
with the objectives of the European Green Deal initiatives. Sustainable bioenergy will enable a  more 
efficient building and heating stock, an energy sect or allowing efficient use of resources and higher 
renewables integration, a truly circular economy (for example through the utilisation of lower quality 
biomass and adding value to ashes as fertilisers ) and increased resilience of our environment .

Furthermore, the existing policy framework should be evaluated to ensure a timely uptake of negative 
emission technologies. The sustainability and GHG emissions requirements for solid biomass will be 
implemented in Europe, starting from next year. The implementing  guidelines for REDII are not yet 
finalised.  An appropriate fitness-check is meant to evaluate if measures are fit for purpose by assessing 
their performance with respect to the policy objectives. At present, such an evaluation cannot be 
carried out as the sustainability requirements for biomass are not yet implemented.  For this reason, 
Bioenergy Europe rejects the revision of articles 29-31 of REDII before their application. With operators 
gearing up to demonstrate compliance and voluntary schemes being set up to certify sustainable 
practices, being able to count on a stable legislative framework is necessary to ensure a smooth 
implementation of the rules. When assessing the preliminary environmental impacts of the Directive, 
the Commission mentions the r isk of unintended incentives to unsustainable biomass. Sustainability 
criteria that will be implemented as of July 2021, will ensure that incentives and public support will 
only be granted to sustainable biomass.

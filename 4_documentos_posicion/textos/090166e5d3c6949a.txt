Feedback on the inception impact assessment regarding the 
 
EU renewable energy rules – review 
 
 
 
September 21, 2020 
 
 
 
The Austrian Federal Economic Chamber is the legal representative of the entire Austrian business 
community and represents all Austrian companies – some 517,000 businesses drawn from the areas 
of Crafts and Trade, Industry, Commerce, Banking and Insurance, Information and Consultancy, 
Tourism and Leisure, Transportation and Communication. 99,6% of our members are SME with less 
than ten employees.

Crisis management and economic survival first 
The Austrian Federal Economic Chamber (Wirtschaftskammer Österreich/WKÖ) supports 
the goal of European climate policy to achieve greenhouse gas neutrality by 2050. The 
economy can make a significant contribution, but it needs a fair, plannable, and 
competitive legal framework in order to continue to implement investments in Europe. 
Due to the current tense economic situation caused by the COVID-19 pandemic  companies 
will not be able to deal with any additional administrative or regulatory burdens. Many 
companies are having their backs to the wall and are fighting for their survival. New 
obligations, restrictions and burdens cannot be handled. In addition, Member States have 
to cope with enormous financial burdens. Businesses must be given the best possible 
support for the necessary recovery and transformation towards a low-carbon economy. 
Unilateral tightening of European framework conditions, which could weaken the 
international competitiveness of our companies, must therefore be strictly avoided. At the 
same time, for establishing new trends, such as strengthening the role of climate-neutral 
gases an EU wide framework is needed.  
 
Businesses needs predictability 
We oppose the proposed increases in climate and energy policy targets for 2030. The 
economic burdens on companies are at their limit - but the current technical and economic 
potential does not offer more. Member states have just completed their National Energy 
and Climate Plans, which intend to show the way to achieving the (current) 2030 targets - 
in particular to increase the share of renewable energy. In the following, the respective 
legal acts at member state level need to be drafted and implemented. By specifying 
suitable measures and instruments companies will then be lead towards the way to 
economic prosperity and climate neutrality.  
The effects of a target increase in the area of renewable energies by 2030 at this stage 
must be subjected to a more in-depth examination - especially with regard to the 
international competitiveness of the European economy. The impact assessment has to 
prove, that the goals are achievable and how. Additional targets and measures should be 
agreed not only with the Member States, but must be coordinated with the very limited 
room for maneuver in the sectors concerned. 
It must also be taken into account that the planned massive expansion of renewable 
energy sources will have a considerable impact on grid stability, security of supply, and 
energy costs. Both, security of supply as well as competitive energy prices have to be 
guaranteed. In addition, all possibilities for reducing energy costs should be exploited at 
European level. This also applies to all fossil energy sources and in particular to gas, which 
will continue to play an important role as a bridging technology during the transition phase 
towards a low-carbon industry for the foreseeable future. 
Instead of technology bans and corporate obligations, incentives, subsidies, and open-
technology approaches should be provided for financing instruments. In the sense of a 
European level playing field, measures must be taken to realize the most cost-efficient 
expansion potential. Renewable energy targets should therefore be set at European level 
and remain indicative.

Holistic approach supports renewable energy expansion 
In order to make our energy system climate-neutral in a cost-efficient way, existing and 
new technologies as well as existing and possibly new infrastructure must be used in an 
economically and ecologically optimal interaction. Especially in the field of renewable 
energies, we have to think outside the box and focus not only on renewable electricity but 
also on climate-friendly gas and climate-friendly heating/cooling. Only through an efficient 
and comprehensive linking of the generating sectors electricity, gas, heating/cooling can 
the consuming sectors such as mobility, production, services and agriculture be 
decarbonized. Following the principle of technology neutrality, a sustainable and 
competitive level playing field must be created. 
As part of a holistic approach, regulatory interactions and existing conflicting objectives 
must be addressed and mitigated by prioritizing the objectives. The European Green Deal 
(EGD) provides a good framework strategy in which initiatives must be developed in a well-
coordinated manner. For example, non-energy policy projects aimed at raising awareness 
and training and further education of skilled workers should be supported as well. It should 
not be overlooked that the primary objective is to reduce greenhouse gases. Appropriate 
instruments must be developed to promote the expansion of renewable energies, whereby 
we see a need for action especially in the non-regulatory area. 
 
Strengthening the role of climate-friendly gases 
The role of renewable and climate-friendly gases and the use of existing gas infrastructure 
can be considered in the context of economic reconstruction after the COVID 19 pandemic 
and to strengthen the EU's competitiveness. Targets at EU level for renewable gas and 
climate-friendly gases are trend-setting and give investors planning security.  
In order to achieve the above-mentioned goals, a stronger focus on climate-friendly gases, 
such as bio methane, green hydrogen or synthetic gas, is needed. They connect the 
sectors, integrate and store renewable energy and are therefore relevant in all sectors 
(space heating, mobility, electricity). In the space heating market, green gases offer a 
quick and comparatively cheap substitution possibility for fossil gas. However, it must be 
considered that European Member States have limited potential.  
Moreover, the extended use of existing storage possibilities (for mainly climate-friendly-
gases) helps managing the security of supply. Other energy sources cannot exploit the 
entire storage potential and therefore have adverse effects on the degree of supply 
security. Combining renewable and decarbonized gases with the existing gas infrastructure 
(networks, large-volume gas storage facilities) enable balancing  the increasingly volatile 
power generation by increasing their share at EU level. 
To establish climate-friendly gases, a uniform EU-wide framework is needed: 
 for the design of an EU-wide uniform support system to increase the share of 
renewable and climate-friendly gases in the gas network. This should create legal 
certainty and a European market. 
 for guarantees of origin and certificates for climate-friendly gases. For example, 
the guarantees of origin should not have an expiration date for the storage of these 
gases.

 for the "blending" of hydrogen. EU-wide standards and minimum thresholds for the 
feed-in and gradual addition of hydrogen to the gas network should be promoted, as 
should the implementation of necessary adjustments at the transfer points. This 
should prevent market fragmentation and enable cross-border trade in renewable 
and climate-friendly gas. 
 
Additional focus on renewable heat 
It is important that a revision of the Renewable Energies Directive takes greater account of 
the accountability of waste heat or puts it on an equal footing with renewables. There is 
enormous potential, for example, in data centers whose waste heat can be transferred to 
district heating networks. Waste heat from industrial processes or cooling circuits can be 
fed into existing and new heating networks - provided that it is of a certain quality - and 
thus reused in the interests of resource conservation and recycling. It is important to 
remove market barriers to the use of alternative fuels derived from waste. The revision of 
the Renewable Energies Directive gives the opportunity to recognize the positive role that 
waste management activities play in avoiding CO2 emissions in the entire energy sector. 
Finally, heat pumps and solar heat in conjunction with heat storage systems must also be 
stimulated and taken into account and credited when reaching the targets. The possibility 
to feed in waste heat must be given. 
The Commission's proposal for a possible revision, scheduled for 2021, should create 
incentives right from the start so that technical-economic potentials can be implemented 
and research measures supported in order to increase the share of renewables.

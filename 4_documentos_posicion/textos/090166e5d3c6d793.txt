“Inception impact assessment – Renewable Energy Directive II” 
This is the reply of the European Heating Industry to the roadmap consultation launched by 
the European Commission on the review of the Renewable Energy Directive. 
 
Accounting for the largest share of final energy consumption in European buildings (about 
80%) and buildings being the highest single source of CO2 emissions in the EU, achieving 
carbon-neutral heating will be crucial to achieve the EU 2050 climate targets. As suppliers of 
efficient, smart and renewable-based heating solutions, European heating manufacturers can 
drive the use of renewable energy in our homes, providing the cost -effective technological 
solutions to reach climate-neutral buildings in 2050. 
No matter whether the current Renewable Energy Directive (REDII) will be reviewed or not, 
implementation is key to deliver on EU policies to promote renewable energy . This 
should be coupled with additional measures to accel erate the replacement of inefficient 
heating systems with modern, efficient, and renewable -based ones. Scrappage schemes 
should also  be created under the Energy Efficiency Directive to stimulate investments in 
renewable-based and efficient heat equipment, e.g. heat pumps, solar thermal systems as 
well as boilers and micro-CHP units working with renewable and decarbonised sources. Such 
schemes should also promote hybridisation, i.e. coupling already existing efficient heating 
systems with additional renewable energy technologies. 
Should the Commission decide to amend the Renewable Energy Directive, we 
recommend to: 
• Strengthen the role of renewable and decarbonised gases in the heating sector; 
• Continue to support the uptake of heat pumps; 
• Promote hybrid heating; 
• Promote decentralised and building-based heating systems. 
Promote green gases used for heating 
To decarbonise buildings, renewable and decarbonised gases can play a decisive role, 
alongside efficient and renewable -based heating appliances. Already  today, gas heating 
appliances installed in European buildings are ready to work with biomethane . Moreover, 
modern condensing technology allows for the use of  up to 20% hydrogen (best available 
technologies work with up to 30%). These solutions fit both for new-built and renovation, while 
making optimised use of existing energy infrastructure.  Heating manufacturers are also 
developing and testing appliances running with 100% hydrogen – e.g. boilers, mCHP (internal 
combustion engines and fuel cells).  
Hence, any revision of the REDII should: 
o promote the use of renewable and decarbonised gases in buildings: demand from all 
economic sectors (i.e. not only heavy-industry and transport) is a key driver for supply; 
o introduce EU-wide targets for the product ion and distribution of decarbonised and 
renewable gases; 
o support the installation of technologies working with new gases and technical rules 
defining the interaction between these gases and heaters. The roll -out of appliances

Keep up the support of heat pumps 
Heat pumps are key technologies to decarbonise buildings and should be promoted by EU 
legislation, together with other heating technologies using renewable energy. There are many 
different heat pumps technologies, suited to different types of buildings , local conditions and 
uses, including for cooling. Electric and thermally-driven heat pumps use electricity and fuels 
for heating extremely efficiently and increase the share of renewable energy sources. Hybrid 
heat pumps (an electric heat pump combined with a condensing boile r) are excellent 
technologies for existing buildings. 
Promote hybrids 
A revision of the renewable energy directive should promote hybrid heating systems. Hybrids 
(e.g. heat pumps combined with condensing technology) are excellent means to introduce 
renewable energy in buildings. They can be easily installed without prior adaptation (staged 
renovation) and allow the system to switch to other energy carriers where needed. This helps 
to alleviate stress on the grid and reduces infrastructure costs.  Moreover, they allow to  
efficiently use renewable gaseous and liquid fuels. 
Promote small and decentralized heating  
To increase the use of renewable energy sources in district heating, it is important to make it 
easier for third parties producing heat fr om renewables to supply heat to existing heat 
networks. Currently many restrictions limit this right of third parties (Art. 24.4). Moreover, any 
revision of the Directive should make it easier for consumers to disconnect from an inefficient 
district heating system and switch to an efficient, renewable, building -based heating system. 
For example, by not making customers pay for the physical disconnection and the initial 
investment in the district heating infrastructure. Finally, large, existing district heating systems, 
when maintained, should be based on renewable sources and become more efficient (through 
Energy Efficiency Directive). 
 
About EHI, the Association of the European Heating Industry 
EHI represents 90% of the European market for heat and hot water generation, heating controls 
and heat emitters, 75% of the hydronic heat pump market, 80% of the biomass central heating 
market (pellets, wood) and 70% of the solar thermal market. EHI Members produce advanced 
technologies for heating in buildings, including: heating systems, burners, boilers, heat pumps, 
components and system integrators, radiators, surface heating & cooling and renewable energy 
systems. In doing so, they employ about 120,000 people in Europe and invest over a billion 
Euros per year in energy efficiency. www.ehi.eu

 
 
 
 
 
 
 
GEN-I Group Position Paper on Comprehensive Green Deal Implementation: 
 
Ensuring Active Consumer Empowerment for the Green Revolution 
 
 
 
The EU's climate and energy policy framework has evolved dramatically in the last decade since the 2009 Third 
Energy Package, through the establishment of the Energy Union Framework starting in 2015, the adoption of the 
Clean Energy for All Europeans Package in 2018 and 2019, and with renewed momentum and ambition by the 
announcement of the European Green Deal in December 
2019. Most recently, on 17 September 2020, the European 
Commission Communication on ”Stepping up Europe’s 2030 
climate ambition Investing in a climate -neutral future for 
the benefit of our people ” raised the 2030 emissions 
reduction target to 55% and has further emphasised the need 
to step up efforts through a comprehensive, coordinated 
approach in all relevant sectors. The common trajectory of 
policy development has been an ever-increasing and wholly 
appropriate focus on putting citizens and consumers first, at 
the core of the clean energy transition, and to empower 
them to participate in, and reap the benefits of, the 
fundamental transformation of the energy system  through 
the green energy revolution. 
 
 
 
The GEN-I Group welco mes and fully supports the European Commission's commitment to tackling climate 
and environmental-related challenges through such a clean energy transition which puts the citizen and the 
consumer at the centre . In the face of the climate crisis, putting th e consumer first means more than secure 
supply at affordable prices; it means having a vision to be a driver of the green transformation and promote 
the sustainability of the entire energy system.  
 
 
 
 
 
Consumers will play a decisive role in the green energy transition.  By enabling them to make more informed 
choices and decisions regarding their energy consumption through data access and digitalisation possibilities 
provided by  smart meters, they can unlock potential revenue streams as active consumers , reducing their 
energy costs while supporting the integration of renewable energy through demand response, e-mobility, and 
other innovative flexibility solutions . Further, they ca n directly contribute to renewables deployment by 
becoming renewables self-consumers, which represents an economically attractive, stable and secure long -
term investment, not only in financial terms but also through its direct contribution to ensuring a cleaner future 
for younger generations. They can do so individually, and also collectively, through citizen or renewable energy 
communities, which will leverage concern for the local environment into tangible economic and social benefits 
at local level, in cluding job growth, thereby strengthening social cohesion , providing economic opportunity, 
and contribute to addressing energy poverty at a community level . In doing so, active and renewables self -
consumers will also activate all available sources of decentralised flexibility, which will support more efficient 
distribution network operation and enable further renewable energy integration while conserving resources by 
reducing the need for costly and unnecessary grid expansion.   
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
To realise the green transformation, it must recognised that not only is the necessary technology 
available, but also that more and more consumers are aware of the possibility to become active 
co-creators of the green energy transition – and, most importantly, are eager to do so.

However, in our experience, consumer participation at a massive scale and through all possible mechanisms 
(including in particular renewable energy communities) is hindered, if not completely prevented, by: 
  
 the persistence of unnecessary administrative burdens (such as commercial activity registration 
requirements for renewables self-consumers);  
 time-consuming procedures (including lack of clarity regarding response timeframes of public 
authorities and grid operators); 
 too slow smart meter deployment, including deployment of ‘smart’ meters not fit-for-purpose; 
 consumer-unfriendly data access provisions (purchase and installation of a smart meter at the cost of 
consumers who want to become active consumers);  
 purchase and installation of a standardised int erface into a smart meter enabling near - real-time data 
transfer at the cost of consumers; 
 uneven readiness of distribution system operators for flexibility resource integration, due to which the 
market value of flexibility does not accurately reflect its real value to the system, and  
 the absence of clear, appropriate, and justified support mechanisms (such as reduced tariffs, taxes and 
levies for active and/or renewables self-consumers)  
 
 
 
 
It is our firm belief that removing these obstacles and simultaneous unlocking of participatory consumer 
potentials is crucial to kick-start a self-reinforcing virtuous cycle of consumer empowerment, by which: 
 
 
 
 
Importantly, this is not a linear process – it represents the catalyst for  a self -reinforcing virtuous cycle , as 
increasing consumer empowerment through active renewable self -consumption and its economic and 
environmental benefits motivate further consumer engagement and participation in the clean energy transition 
(provided that flexibility at the distribution system level is appropriately valorised ). Thus, the convergence of 
these dynamics results in an ever -increasing share of renewables deployment enabled by ever -increasing 
activation of decentralised flexibility resources, as passive consumers become not onl y active consumers but 
active renewables self -consumers, both at individual and collective levels  through citizen and renewable 
energy communities: 
In practice, consumer participation and empowerment is hindered by: 
 Unnecessary and excessive administrative burdens and barriers 
 Time-consuming and unclear administrative procedures 
 Too slow, costly smart meter deployment and unfriendly data-access provisions 
 Lack of distribution system operator readiness for flexibility resource procurement 
 Absence of clear, appropriate, and justified support mechanisms

In order to achieve such a virtuous cycle, the only path forward is a concerted effort to ensure coordinated and 
effective policy intervention that will require: 
 
 Mandatory policy targets in the form of legally binding targets at EU -level, set at a mandatory 
minimum which is uniform for all Member States, covering: 
o A mandatory share of renewable energy sources in electricity, of which a mandatory minimum 
share (uniform Union-wide) must be achieved through renewable self-consumption, covering 
renewable self -consumption, jointly acting self -consumers, and renewable energy 
communities 
o A mandatory network efficiency target, of which a mandatory minimum level (uniform Union-
wide) must be achieved through market -based procurement of flexibility by distribution 
system operators 
o A mandatory smart meter roll -out target, which includes a mandatory minimum technology 
standard (uniform Union-wide) and clear, reasonable yet rapid deadlines for deployment 
 
 Mandatory enabling measures, with a 
minimum degree of harmonisation Union -
wide, in the form of reward and incentive 
measures, including but not limited to: 
o Lower network tariffs, charges and 
levies for renewables self -
consumption and active 
consumers, both at individual and 
collective level, to reflect their 
contribution to environmental 
sustainability and network 
efficiency 
o Regulatory measures such as  
temporal net metering (in Member 
States where technologies have not yet reached grid-parity) or locational net metering (in 
Member States where technologies have already reached grid-parity) 
o Financial measures such as state aid support via direct subsidies and favourable loans, grants, 
and favourable tax treatment for investments supporting ren ewables self-consumption and 
active consumers, both at individual and local level 
 
 'Quick win' measures to improve administrative efficiency, targeting administrative obstacles that are 
both easy to remove and prevent consumer engagement and participation at mass scale, including but 
not limited to: 
o Removal of administrative obstacles such as the requirement for commercial activity 
registration for renewables self -consumption and active consumers, both at individual and 
collective level 
o Ensuring clarity and transparency, such as reducing administrative response times to facilitate 
efficient deployment by imposing clear maximum response times

The GEN-I Group fully supports the recent European Commission Communication on “Stepping up Europe ’s 
2030 climate ambition Investing in a climate -neutral future for the benefit of our people ” that the increased 
ambition for emissions reduction “requires adjustments to the current policy framework ” and that “all policy 
instruments relevant for the decarbonisation of our economy must work in coherence in order to achieve our 
objectives”.  
 
In view thereof, and as all the minimum required policy proposals presented have consumer empowerment as 
their core catalyst, an appropriate and effective approach is needed, an approach that would send Member States 
a clear message of what is required – and a clear message to consumers of what is possible: 
 
A virtuous cycle that leaves no consumer behind and ensures a common green future requires 
simultaneous and comprehensive enactment of a minimum level of mandatory, legally binding 
Union targets and measures  that will avoid fragmented, delayed, and unnecessarily costly 
implementation. We therefore strongly urge the European Commission to consider the above policy 
recommendations as the core of a new over -arching Regulation on Active Consumer 
Empowerment for the Green Revolution, which will put the European Green Deal firmly in the 
hands of every European citizen. 
 
 
KEY TAKE-AWAYS: 
 
 Consumers will play a decisive role in the clean energy transition by participating in decarbonisation 
through decentralisation  enabled by digitalisation , with significant direct economic, social, and 
environmental benefits for individuals and communities 
 
 Consumers are increasingly aware of the possibilities of renewables self -consumption and active 
participation through flexibility provision, and are eager to take advantage of these opportunities , but 
lack all the tools necessary to unlock their potential (in particular smart meters and data access) 
 
 Consumer empowerment must be promoted a t EU -level through legally binding Union targets and 
comprehensive, mandatory enabling measures  in all relevant policy areas ( renewables self -
consumption, distribution-level flexibility, and smart metering deployment)

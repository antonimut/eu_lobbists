1. Introduction 
Business and Science Poland welcomes the possibility to comment on the early revision of the Directive 
2018/2001 on the promotion of the use of energy from renewable sources. We recognize the need to 
ensure compliance of several initiatives under the EDG scope with the ongoing ambitious climate policy, 
which is relevant for elements of the RED II rules.  
It should be indicated that any attempt to increase the already ambitious climate goals in RED II must 
be preceded by the identification of problems which the Member States’ economies may already have 
to deal with . T herefore, appropriate solutions must be found for all countries, so that the energy 
transition is cost-effective, socially sustainable and adapted to the specificity of individual countries and 
their inhabitants. In this context, the priority should be to "fair", not equal efforts of individual countries 
to achieve the declared energy savings. Thus, the benefits of increasing Europe's energy security due to 
the growing share of renewables in the EU energy mix, from the consumer’s point of view and the total 
cost of electricity incurred by him, may be different in each Member State. With this in mind, the review 
of the regulations should also take into account the issues related to the total costs of the energy 
transition for end users.

3. Energy storage. 
The most optimal development of renewable energy should be carried out with the parallel 
commercialization of energy storage technologies on a different scale. In the transitional phase, the key 
stabilizing role of the system should be given to flexible and highly efficient gas units, which in the long 
term may be powered by the gas -hydrogen mixture in various proportions. In this context, h ydrogen 
seems to be the most promising technology for a large-scale and inter-season energy storage. 
We would like to underline that during the transition phase it will be indispensable to support the supply 
of the hydrogen market by the deployment of low -carbon hydrogen, utilizing fossil fuels as a raw 
material in combination with Carbon Ca pture and Utilization (CCU) and Carbon Capture and Storage 
(CCS) technologies.  
With this in mind, r enewable energy storage is a key element in reducing costs. It ensures stability of 
energy supply and contributes to improving the profitability of the low and zero-emission sector and 
increasing Europe's energy security.  
 
4. Renewables in transport. 
The targets for the suppliers of fuel for transport, as set out in the RED II Directive, are very ambitious. 
First of all, it should be emphasized that the growing ambitions regarding the share of renewable energy 
in the transport sector must be compatible with the availabili ty of tools . Only this would  enable the 
achievement of these goals without disturbing the financial stability and competitiveness of the entities 
indispensable for Europe’s transition process. One of the biggest challenges to achieve the goals of the 
renewable energy sources in the transport sector are resources constraints, in particular regarding 
feedstock for the production of advanced biofuels. 
We welcome the consultations already conducted by the European Commission on the extension of the 
catalog of raw materials for the production of advanced biofuels, which are meant to result in amending 
of Annex IX a of the RED Directive. At the same time, we consider that appropriate changes to the RED 
II Directive may contribute to increasing the role of alternative fuels used in transport. 
In this regard, we propose considering: 
1) LPG, LNG, CNG, gray hydrogen  as a denominator (Article 27(1)a of RED II)  - for the calculation 
of the minimum shares of renewable energy in the transport sector,  
2) 4 times enhanced multiplier for hydrogen (as in case of electricity), 
3) 2 times enhanced multiplier for: 
• bioCNG and bioLNG, 
• biofuels in aviation.  
In our opinion, it would be an effective tool to stimulate demand for alternative fuels in transport.

5. Obstacles. 
We would like to point to  the necessity to eliminate  all barriers which may jeopardize the 
implementation of hydrogen in production and in the use of projects in transport and industry. In this 
context, the abandonment of the additionalit y criterion should be considered. Moreover, the priority 
should be given to the recognize of the origin of electricity used for the production of hydrogen in the 
electrolysis process as sufficient to determine the hydrogen as a renewable. 
It should be emphasized that fuel suppliers are subject to overlapping regulations -  on the one hand 
there is an obligation to reduce emissions in the fuel life cycle (pursuant to Art.7a of the Fuel Quality 
Directive), and on the other hand, an obligation regarding the share of renewable energy imposed by 
the RED II Directive. In our opinion, this approach causes excessive regulatory burdens, which is why 
we postulate a horizontal harmonization of the approach to climate regulation for the fuel industry. 
 
6. Financial aspects.  
It is crucial to stimulate the RES market appropriately through support systems, financial instruments 
and funds in parallel with dedicated regulations. We recognize that current regulations do not provide 
support for both investments and use of alternative fuels for transport sector. Production of these fuels 
is more expensive than conventional one, hence the demand for it remains negligible. In our opinion 
financial support is indispensable, and it should accompany the policy framework to up scale 
investments, volume of production and finally, demand for these fuels.  
We also would like to point out that current targets for the share of renewable energy sources in the 
energy mix and the emission reductions targets are sufficiently ambitious for the proper development 
of the market, and their increase may lead to too stringent requirements of  financial and technical 
involvement for some Member States. 
 
7. Stability of regulations. 
Predictability and stabilization of legal regulations are of great importance as they reinforce certainty 
for investors in a long-term perspective. We would like to emphasize, that regulations of RED II are still 
in the process of transposition into national legal systems. We are of the opinion that regulations 
currently envisaged in RED II are  appropriate to further promote the use of renewable and other low -
carbon fuels including sustainable biofuels, synthetic liquid and gaseous fuels and renewable and low -
carbon hydrogen.  
In this regard, we support the continued use of voluntary schemes that evidence compliance with the 
sustainability and greenhouse gas emissions saving criteria for biofuels, bioliquids and biomass fuels as 
required under RED II.  
However, if Annex IX of the Directive is amended, it  should add feedstocks to the actual list and not  
remove them.

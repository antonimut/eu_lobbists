Feedback to Inception Impact Assessment 
Revision of Directive (EU) 2018/2001 on the promotion of the use of energy from 
renewable sources 
 
To be on a pathway consistent with achieving climate-neutrality by 2050, SolarPower 
Europe supports the European Union to review upwards its 2030 greenhouse gas 
emissions target to at least 55% and to increase the ambition of its 2030 renewable 
energy target accordingly by June 2021, before the upwards review initially established 
by the RED II for 2023.  
 
Despite the increasing cost-competitiveness of solar, achieving higher ambitions will 
require a robust European regulatory framework, going beyond or adapting the existing 
provisions of the RED II. This would imply a combination of non-regulatory measures, 
raising the ambition of the REDII, and amending REDII legal measures (Option 5 of the 
Inception Impact Assessment).  
 
SolarPower Europe proposes the revision of provisions included in the scope of the 
inception impact assessment (Articles 3, 15, 23, 25, and 27) and urges the European 
Commission to extend the scope of the revision to provisions that were not included in 
the scope of the impact assessment (Articles 16, 19, and 21). 
 
RED II Articles in the scope of the Impact Assessment 
 
Article 3 - Binding overall Union target for 2030 
 
The binding overall Union target for 2030 for renewable energy should be increased 
to at least 40%, to align with the target of achieving at least 55% CO2 emission reduction 
by 2030. SolarPower Europe’s “100% Renewable Europe” report shows that an 
accelerated transition to a 100% renewable energy-based system can deliver the 
sharpest decline in GHG emissions of 60% by 2030 and down to zero by 2040 in a cost-
effective way. 
 
 This review should be initiated by June 2021, before the 2023 upward review 
initially established by the RED II.  
 
Article 15 - Administrative procedures, regulations and codes 
 
Despite the good provisions put forward by the Clean Energy Package, administrative 
procedures still hinder the development of renewable energy projects across the EU.  
 
 Lengthy and complex procedures and the multiplicity of contact points create a 
barrier for project developers. These barriers result in additional project 
development costs and a higher risk for project delays, resulting into higher 
financing costs.   
 Heavy procedures, such as the requirement of construction permits, can  slow 
down the deployment of rooftop PV installations. This is not in line with the 
ambition set by the Renovation Wave initiative to be presented by the European 
Commission.

 Member States have largely ignored the requirement to remove administrative

renewable energy sourcing. 
 
Administrative procedures are a critical barrier to the ambitious deployment of solar 
capacities in Europe. The European Commission should revise the RED II to further 
strengthen the provisions of article 15, and in particular: 
 
 Introduce better safeguards for the development of explicit measures to simplify 
administrative procedures for distributed and large-scale installations, as 
part of the National Energy ad Climate plans. 
 Complement the current simple notification procedure (15.1) for authorisation of 
decentralised devices with new measures, such as the explicit exemption from 
construction permits for rooftop. Such a measure could boost the market for 
rooftop PV, harvesting the huge potential for clean electricity generation (680 
TWh of electricity production annually according to the JRC) and accelerate job 
creation in the many SMEs active in this sector.  
 In addition to this, legislative action is needed to require Member States to 
ensure building regulations and codes require the use of solar in all buildings 
with suitable conditions. In the NECPs, Belgium, Luxembourg and Spain 
introduced targets for the deployment of rooftop PV on their public building stock.  
 
Article 23 - Mainstreaming renewable energy in heating and cooling  
 
The heating and cooling sector represents almost half of the EU energy consumption, 
and the current regulatory environment does not incentivise cost-optimal deployment of 
renewables in heating, cooling and hot water use. The sector remains dominated by fossil 
fuels and therefore dependent on imports. 
 
The pathway of electrifying heating and cooling using renewable energy requires the 
combined deployment of efficient appliances for heating and cooling with the expansion 
of renewable energy plants, with both utility scale and distributed-scale renewable 
generation such as rooftop solar PV in buildings and industry. 
 
 Beyond the need to increase EU ambitions for renewable energy in heating and 
cooling, re-opening of the REDII should focus on clarifying the usage of 
renewable electricity for power-to-heat applications and how it can be 
accounted for in the sub-targets for the share of renewable heat. A simple 
and appropriate methodology should be established which should also be 
applicable for various reporting requirements. 
 
Article 25 - Mainstreaming renewable energy in the transport sector 
Electrification of transport, driven by the falling costs of batteries, opens increased 
perspectives for a higher penetration of renewables in transport, particularly in the road 
and rail transport segments.

electricity in the transport target (Belgium, Cyprus, Latvia, Spain) or do not provide a

Article 27 - Calculation rules with regard to the minimum shares of renewable 
energy in the transport sector 
 
In order to provide visibility to investors in renewable hydrogen, the European 
Commission should quickly clarify certain elements of this article, in the context of the 
future delegated act establishing a Union methodology setting out detailed rules by which 
economic operators are to comply with the requirements laid down in the fifth and sixth 
subparagraphs of this paragraph. The delegated act should be agreed as soon as 
possible, and questions should not be shifted in the RED II revision which would delay the 
process.

RED II Articles not in the scope of the Impact Assessment 
 
Article 2. 15 Jointly acting self-consumers 
 
The current definition of “jointly acting self-consumers” in the RED II limits the concept to 
consumers located in the “same building or multi apartment bock”.  
 
However, the potential of collective self-consumption, where energy is shared even 
beyond the premises of a building, is developing in Europe. This has an important 
potential to democratise self-consumption business models, improving access for 
consumers in urban areas or with lower revenues. Since the adoption of the Renewable 
Energy Directive in 2018, several countries have introduced frameworks enlarging the 
scope of collective self-consumption business beyond buildings and multi-apartment 
blocks such as in France, Spain, Italy, the Netherlands. 
 
The definition of jointly acting self-consumers should be revised. The limitation of self-
consumption “in the same building or multi-apartment blocks” should be removed. This 
will enable more Member States to fully harness the potential of collective self-
consumption, in line with existing national best practices listed below.

Article 19 - Guarantees of origin for energy from renewable sources 
 
Corporate PPA’s will be critical to support higher ambitions on renewable energy 
deployment by 2030. A well-functioning framework for GOs is critical to the development 
of corporate renewable PPAs and, therefore, SolarPower Europe calls on the inclusion of 
Article 19 in the RED II review.     
 
 The framework should increase the level of transparency and information 
contained in GOs to support those consumers seeking to report on additional 
attributes, including on additionality.     
 Member States should issue GOs for all renewable electricity, reinforcing 
transparency, and empowering renewable energy producers to market their 
electricity.  Rules to guarantee the traceability and to ensure issuance of GOs to 
all renewable electricity producers, should follow a consistent approach across all 
Members States. Member States should provide a robust certification scheme for 
renewable energy projects to incentivize investments in renewable energy.  
 Member States must also remove barriers that inhibit GO assignment from 
behind-the-meter projects to building owners or operators.   
 Projects coupling renewable energy generation and renewable-based 
hydrogen should receive GOs, thereby ensuring that hydrogen has been indeed 
produced from renewable sources (including through PPAs).  
 
Article 21 - Renewables self-consumers 
 
Self-consumers represent a very high potential for solar in Europe. The potential on 
rooftop is huge, with a technical potential corresponding to one quarter of the current 
electricity consumption of the EU, while avoiding public acceptance issues related to land.  
In the context of the Renovation Wave and the Recovery Plan, reopening the provisions 
on self-consumption could allow the European regulation to incorporate best practices 
from national countries while boosting the SMEs active in the self-consumption market.    
 
The prosumer market is still facing the barrier of accessing cost-competitive financing, 
specifically in countries that have implemented retroactive measures.  
 The introduction of targets for the development of prosumers or rooftop PV, as in 
five NECPs, could significantly boost the market.  
 
Despite the provisions highlighted in Article 21.3 of the RED II, several Members States 
still apply disproportionate administrative procedures for C&I self-consumers. For 
example, in France, on-site self-consumption above 100kW (C&I scale) is awarded

Non-regulatory measures 
 
Clean Energy Package Implementation Body 
 
To successfully deliver on this ambitious objective, we strongly encourage the creation 
of a Clean Energy Package Implementation Body to bring together the European 
Commission, Member States, and industrial and civil society stakeholders with the 
objective to review progress on the implementation of Directive (EU) 2018/2001. This 
forum should also serve to share best practices and technical guidance.

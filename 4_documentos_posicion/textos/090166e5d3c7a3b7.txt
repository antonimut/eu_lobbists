Consultation on EU renewable energy rules – review 
 
Comments by Falck Renewables 
 
 
 September 2020 
 
 
 
The start of an overall review process of the energy and climate legislation, including the 2018 
Renewable Energy Directive (“REDII”), aimed at ensuring that the EU renewable energy policy 
effectively continues to contribute to the cost-effective deployment of renewable energy sources is 
welcome. This initiative is an attractive opportunity to improve certain elements of the existing 
regulatory framework to ensure that renewable energy sufficiently contributes to the achievement 
of the higher EU climate ambition in the changing context of the European Green Deal (EGD). 
However, we believe that the ongoing assessment on the REDII should be part of a wider review 
process of energy regulations also including the Internal Market for Electricity Directive and 
Regulation, along with the EU competition law. The EU regulatory framework needs to take a 
holistic approach that considers the interdependence between different markets and other 
government energy policies while fostering both demand and competitive supply of low-carbon 
solutions. The EU should review its state aid policies and the market liberalization and competition 
policies established in the 1990s and 2000s as the deep decarbonization of the power system 
cannot happen in the current market environment.  
 
Falck believes that the ongoing impact assessment should look at a possible combination of 
different options: non-regulatory measures (option 2) , raising the ambition level of the REDII targets 
and subtargets in line with the 2030 Climate Target Plan (option 3), and amending REDII to translate 
into legal measures the actions proposed in other energy strategies of the EGD (option 4). 
Key elements of the REDII provisions that should be considered according to Falck are listed below.

Increase the 2030 target for renewable energy  
All 2030 targets foreseen by the RED should be revised upwards to be in line with the new political 
realities of the Green Deal and Carbon-neutrality commitment. However, raising the targets 
makes sense only and exclusively if the market conditions will be set for their efficient and 
effective development. First of all, although it is not the subject of this consultation, we 
would like to underline the need for a revision of the market design to provide for a full 
integration of renewables, storage and consumption units. We also think that new targets 
should be increased taking into consideration the available data of energy production and 
consumption at a European level and also considering the emergency due to the spread of Covid-
19. 
 
Increase in electricity consumption 
The review process should also focus on the compatibility of such increased renewable targets with 
a necessary growth of the Renewables share in energy consumption.  
Today there is a lack of regulation to implement a higher renewable target. We believe it is essential 
to act on the consumption side to bridge the existing regulatory gap. In particular, Falck strongly 
supports the introduction of specific mechanisms aimed at providing an obligation for both public 
administration and end-consumers to acquire renewable energy. 
 Measures widely adopted in the 
United States that might be appropriate for this purpose are the green procurement for public 
administration and the Renewable Portfolio Standards. 
 
 
Identification of areas unsuitable for the installation of renewable plants 
Article 15, paragraph 7, of the RED II allows Member States to identify “ 
areas suitable for low-
ecological-risk deployment ” included in the assessment of their potential of energy from renewable 
sources. We consider that this specific provision should be modified in order to leave the possibility 
to Member States to carry out only a spatial analysis aimed at identifying unsuitable areas  for the 
installation of new renewable power plants.  
 
 
Foster electrification and renewable fuels such as green hydrogen 
The current iteration of the directive was drafted and agreed on in a different context and spirit to 
that of today. The European Green Deal (EGD) has set a clear pathway towards climate neutrality, 
and firmly underlines the importance of a well-bala nced RED and a concerted regulatory 
environment that enables a positive investment environment for existing players and market 
challengers, to enable the scaling-up and deployment of hydrogen and hydrogen technologies.  
The production of hydrogen and derived renewable and low carbon gases from renewable energy

decouples the deployment of renewable energy from grid bottlenecks unlocking the full potential 
of renewable energy to replace fossil energy carriers in all sectors of our economy, not just in the 
power sector. Hydrogen and derived renewable and lo w carbon gases, liquids and chemical 
feedstocks allow integrating ever growing amounts of renewable energy into so called “hard to 
abate” sectors such as steel, chemicals, and transport, including refineries, maritime and aviation. 
As highlighted by the EU’s Hydrogen Strategy and th e Communication on Energy System 
Integration, carbon-neutrality across all sectors of the economy requires hydrogen and derived 
renewable and low carbon gases, liquids and chemical feedstock as one of many solutions. In order 
to enable the achievement of EU’s climate targets in 2030 and 2050, hydrogen needs to start 
being deployed, at large scale, across different sectors as soon as possible. 
In order to underpin the production targets envisioned in the EU’s Hydrogen Strategy and the 2x40 
GW initiative, it is necessary that specific targets for renewable hydrogen are introduced in the 
transport and in the heating and cooling. In addition, specific sub-targets for so called “hard to 
abate” sectors. (e.g. steel production, aviation and maritime) may also be considered in the 
upcoming revision of the RED to further incentivize and speed-up deployment and adoption of 
renewable energy in specific sectors and industrial segments. 
 
Corporate power purchase agreements (PPAs) 
Corporate renewable energy power purchase agreement s (PPAs) are a crucial driver for market-
based investments in new renewable projects in the European Union, supplementing national 
support schemes and providing renewable suppliers with reliable revenues.  
Notwithstanding Article 15.8 already asks Member States to remove barriers to the development 
of corporate renewable PPAs, a revision of the REDII is still needed to actively support the uptake 
of these contracts. In this regard, Falck welcomes the introduction of new measures aimed at 
providing more extensive safeguards for both PPA contracting parties, by detailing in each National 
Energy and Climate Plan (NECP) how corporate renewable energy sourcing will be deployed. 
To further encourage the development of PPAs, new m easures aimed at streamlining and 
shortening both the permitting and grid connection processes should be included in the Directive. 
Such interconnection between renewable electricity sourcing and permitting (i.e. between Art. 
15.8 and 16) should be clearly defined.  
 
Streamlining the permitting process  
Permit-granting procedures for new and repowered renewable energy plants remain too slow and 
complex, notwithstanding Article 16 of the REDII asks Member States to streamline and speed up 
them.  
Lengthy processes, also due to lack of adequate personnel in the national administrations, prevent

the development of the most innovative technologies which should allow the decarbonisation at 
the lowest cost for the community. Unclear national  regulatory frameworks and delays in

We, therefore, highlight that without these new investments, the volumes of renewable energy

A new framework for Guarantees of Origin (GOs) 
The revision of the REDII shall include a well-functioning and more transparent framework for 
Guarantees of Origin (GOs), as evidence of the renewable share of electricity consumption. GOs 
also represent an essential tool for the uptake of corporate PPAs in the European Union.  
Falck strongly supports the introduction of standardized rules at European level aimed at designing 
a clear certification system able to guarantee traceability and ensure GOs issuance to all renewable 
producers. 
The revision of the GO system shall also cover rene wable hydrogen, thus, ensuring direct 
compatibility between renewable support schemes and GOs to encourage the development of an 
EU renewable hydrogen market.   
 
EU carbon pricing  
 Furthermore, even if is not the  subject of this consultation, we strongly suggest that targeting 
climate neutrality by 2050 will require not only choosing cost-competitive options while 
minimizing stranded assets, but also giving the right technologies the investment perspective and 
deployment scale that it is needed to reduce costs. 
EU carbon pricing will need a restructuring to cover all sectors and increase in a progressive and 
predictable manner. For carbon pricing to be effective, the EU will also need to ensure that 
inefficient fossil fuel subsidies are removed (e.g. direct funding, tax exemptions e.g. on diesel fuel, 
etc. accounting in the EU only at around €55 billio n per year) and that environmental 
considerations are included in all national schemes. In doing so, external dimensions will need to 
be identified and addressed. Otherwise, the risk for the European Union will be a loss in 
competitiveness, a weakening of its economies and companies, with imports replacing carbon-
intensive production in the EU (“carbon leakage” affecting energy intensive industries - e.g. steel, 
chemicals; pulp and paper, etc.) and external actors leveraging on EU’s innovations and 
technologies.

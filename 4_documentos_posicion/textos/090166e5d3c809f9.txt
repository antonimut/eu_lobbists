Danfoss input to the European Commission’s roadmap on 
The Energy Efficiency Directive 
August 2020 
 
 
Danfoss welcomes the evaluation launched by the European Commission on the Energy Efficiency 
Directive. As a global leader in engineering solutions that allows the world to use resources in smarter 
ways, energy efficiency is at the core of what we do.  
 
With the presentation of the European Green Deal and the newest climate objectives for 2030 and 
2050, we agree with the Commission that the Directive must be revised , and it is also clear to us the 
ambition must be revised upwards so that it is fully aligned with the EU’s commitments. At the same 
time, it is crucial that we continue to focus on ensuring the provisions of the current EED  are properly 
implemented and enforced.   
 
A first analysis of the National Energy and Climate Plans submitted by the Member States carried out 
by your own services show that progress on energy efficiency has been slower than anticipated, and 
that the 2020 target for energy efficiency will not be achieved. The same is true for the 2030 target of 
32.5% unless there is further intervention, such as  properly considering the Energy Efficiency Fir st 
principle by streamlining energy efficiency in all other policies and actions.  
 
Danfoss believes that the f ollowing recommendations can help close the a mbition gap for the 2030 
target and set the EU on track to achieving climate neutrality by 2050. 
 
• The energy efficiency first principle should be at the core of all EU energy and climate policies. 
Ensuring this will increase energy savings, create jobs, stimulate growth and therefore 
contribute to recovering from the current economic crisis.  
• The EU Energy Efficiency target for 2030 should be updated, and binding. The current target 
is only indicative, which weakens the effectiveness of the Directive and makes Member States 
more reluctant to set the necessary policies for diminishing energy consumption.  For this 
reason, Danfoss is in favour  of an EU wide mandatory target . To be aligned with the 2030 
Climate Plan and the assessment of the Nation al Energy and Climate Plans , the EU should 
increase the target to 40%. 
• All public buildings should be in scope and lead by example. Article 5 of the EED applies to 
“buildings with a total useful floor area over 250m² owned and occupie d by the central 
government”. The scope should be expanded to include buildings from all public authorities  
and the “district approach” concept should be introduced in this article to encourage members 
states to develop a more holistic approach . Cost-effective measures that quickly lead to high 
energy sa vings with a short payback time , notably the optimization of technical building 
systems (as mandated by the EPBD) should be prioritized and rolled-out at large scale in public 
buildings. 
• Public procurement can drive the market and energy savings. Article 6 of the EED states that 
Member States shall ensure that central governments purchase only products, services and 
buildings with a high energy -efficiency performance. It will also be encouraged for regional 
and local authorities. Danfoss believes that requesting the mandatory calculation of total cost 
of ownership in public procurement can be an efficient way to drive the market and energy 
savings beyond the minimum requirements  in line with the Public Procure ment Directive. 
Clear performance levels should  also be set for buildings newly built or rented by public 
authorities: they should all meet nearly nZEB and healthy indoor climate standards. And, again 
the scope should be extended to apply to regional and local authorities.  
• Energy efficiency obligation schemes (EEOS) : In view of the increa sed target ambition  and 
considering the EU is set to introduce new measures in a number of sectors delivering energy 
savings, we ask the Commission to look into issues of eligibility and additionality of measures.

of active energy efficiency measures (e.g. with building management systems such as building 
automation and contr ols) in the list of eligible measures. Additionally, in order to enhance 
supply side efficiency, Article 7 should include Combined Heat and Power (CHP) and District

assess the impact of changing EU minimum energy tax ra tes on delivering additional energy 
savings as part of the revision of the Energy Taxation Directive. 
• Efficient heating and cooling. Article 14 has helped to put heating and cooling on the agenda 
of M ember States. This is especially true for  countries typically dominated by gas and oil 
heating. However, we need  a stronger link between the identification of the potential for 
efficient heating and cooling and policy measures taken at national level . Waste heat should 
play a bigger role in the revision: the identification and access to information on waste heat 
recovery potential and sources is key and should be highlighted, for instance, in the work 
carried out by Member States under the EED Article 14. These analyses will need to translate 
into national strategies to ensure the use of waste heat as an integral part of decarbonization. 
Additional focus should be put on waste heat from the tertiary sector, as  well as ensuring 
recovery of the vast potential from hydrogen production. 
• The potential of energy efficiency in motors is significant. Applying intelligent drives in electric 
motor systems make sure that motors only run when it is necessary by matching the process 
requirement and the motor speed . We ask the European Commission to assess the energy 
savings potential from drives. This could save as much as  8% of total global electricity 
consumption and reduce industry application emissions up to 40% according to IEA figures. 
• Final customers deserve to  know exactly what they are paying for . Article 9 provides that 
Member States shall en sure that consumers are provided with individual meters that reflect 
their energy consumption and provide information on actual time of use. We believe that 
article 9 should specify that consumers be charged for their actual con sumption and not by 
square meters. This creates a much stronger incentive for behaviour change.  
• Energy efficiency is possible only through a skilled labour force. Articles 16 and 17 should be 
revised to make sure Europe has access to competent workers, sim ple and verified 
accreditation and certification schemes.  
• Facilitate access to funding and investments . The economic crisis , consequence of the 
coronavirus pandemic, have made resources scarcer. A mandatory energy efficiency target 
would steer investors t o direct more funds to energy efficiency projects. Member States 
should also use the recently proposed “Next Generation EU” scheme to significantly increase 
the level of public funding for energy efficiency, like building renovations, financing district 
heating and cooling networks and investing in clean transportation.

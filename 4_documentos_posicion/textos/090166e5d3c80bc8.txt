Danfoss input to the European Commission’s roadmap on 
The Renewable Energy Directive 
August 2020 
 
 
Danfoss welcomes the inception impact assessment presented by the European Commission on the 
Renewable Energy Directive (RED). The European Union has settled to increase its climate ambition for 
2030 and to become c limate neutral by 2050 . This requires  a significant increase of the share of 
renewables in our energy mix , especially in the industry, buildings and transport sectors. As 
manufacturers of technologies that create renewable energy throughout the supply chain, from 
generation to the demand side, Danfoss encourages the Commission to take this opportunity to revise 
the RED upwards. 
 
In order to achieve the newly adopted climate targets, the Commission should revise the Directive to 
tackle fundamental problems that persist within the energy sector  as well as increase the overall 
ambition level. At the same time, it is also crucial that the provisions of the current RED continue to be 
properly implemented and enforced. 
 
Danfoss believes that the following recommendations can help increase the share of renewable energy 
in the mix, having in mind the objective of achieving climate neutrality in the EU by 2050: 
 
• The EU targets for renewable energy should be updated . We are calling on the European 
institutions to back an increase for renewable energy to a binding 40% target (Art. 3). Danfoss 
also call s on the Commission to s et an ambitious renewable heating and cooling 
decarbonisation target  (Art. 23) , with waste heat eligible to be counted towards the 
achievement of the target. Additionally, the current target for Renewables in transport should 
also be updated  (Art. 25 ). As the Commission’s assessment of the NECPs recognises,  
consumption of renewable energy  has to reach 24%  in the transport sector to achieve the 
55% GHG reduction target by 2030. Furthermore, maritime transport remains the only sector 
with no specific EU commitments to reduce greenhouse gas emissions. More provisions should 
be added to increase the share of renewable energy in ships.  
 
• Increase the deployment of renewables in the power, heating and cooling, industry and 
transport sectors  through sector integration.  With intermittent renewables gaining more 
weight in Europe, the key issue is how much energy we can integrate in the system.  Sector 
integration will facilitate a higher share of renewables in th e system. Integrating heating and 
electricity with the help of district energy and large-scale heat pumps can solve two challenges 
at once: the challenge of decarbonizing the heating supply  and the challenge of integrating 
growing shares of volatile electricity into our energy system. 
 
• Better use of waste heat in a more “circular” energy system . A more efficient and circular 
energy system should take into account the reuse of unavoidable waste streams for energy 
purposes, with studies estimating that between 300TWh and 800TWh of industrial waste heat 
could be recovered per year in the EU. The reuse of waste heat from the tertiary sector should 
also be promoted, especially from data centres and supermarkets.  
• Better integrate renewables in buildings in the context of the Renovation Wave initiative .
Buildings are the single largest energy consumer sector in Europe, with 40% of total energy 
consumed. Optimizing the energy performance of buildings reduces the future need for 
renewable electricity to heat and cool our buildings which can then free up electricity to be 
used in other parts of the grid, such as for the electrification of transport, or reduces the need 
for energy production capacity extensions.  Demand-side management response allows for 
shifting the energy consumption from peak hours to off -peak times and thus helps match 
renewable generation with energy demand.

 
 
 
Swedish Wood-Fuel Association´s reply to the Public Consultation on Inception 
Impact Assessment Revision of Directive (EU) 2018/2001 on the promotion of 
the use of energy from renewable sources. 
 
The Swedish Wood-Fuel Association is an organization for Swedish producers of solid wood fuels 
within the forest and recycling industry. Our members have experience of renewable energy supply 
from the development after the oil crisis in the mid-1970s of solid residual products from the forest 
and forest industry to the production of heat and electricity. The development was very fast during 
the first 15 years and has since continued to increase at a declining rate for about 30 years. There is 
still great unused raw material potential for further development, but the rate of increase is now 
low. 
The Swedish Wood Fuel Association has the following comments on the heading document and 
initiative for a revision of the Renewable Energy Directive (RED II).  
General comments 
We strongly recommend not to open a revision of the RED II. It was adopted not long ago after 
thorough negotiations. The directive has not yet been implemented in the members states. It will 
cause uncertainty for the markets actors if the directive should be revised at this stage. Article 29-31 
should not be changed. The market needs stable and long-term policy instruments for making the 
transition to renewable energy.  
Policy instruments should in general be neutral in technology and let the market and consumers 
decide on the best solution. Therefore, should detailed regulations be avoided. This will result in the 
most cost-effective solutions. Also cascading principles causes non optimal solutions for the market 
and the EU citizens. Instead general instruments like emission trading and carbon tax will reduce 
fossil emissions in a cost-effective and market-oriented way.   
Bioenergy is the largest domestic EU energy source. Only 5 percent is imported. Bioenergy have a 
great potential to increase energy independence and at the same time create jobs in the whole of 
EU, also in rural areas.  
 
A. Context, Problem definition and Subsidiarity Check. 
We note that the revision of REDII is part of the European Green Deal, EGD, and that the President of 
the Commission has given this initiative the highest political priority. We find the initiative well 
justified with regard to, among other things for example to the EU's current climate policy ambitions 
and goals. Based on our horizon and experience, the goals are urgent but very high with regard to 
the incentives, competitive conditions and other conditions that can be expected in the energy 
markets. 
We believe and assume that the development must in all essentials be based on market economic 
driving forces combined with simple, uniform and consistent management based on environmental 
requirements. As an environment-related instrument, we in Sweden have good experience of carbon 
dioxide tax on fossil energy. We believe that this type of environmental management should be used

to a greater extent because it is effective, equal for all and does not require additional administrative 
work in the companies. Such governance should, as far as possible, replace governance with 
government subsidies. 
The simplicity and universality of carbon tax management provide incentives for all types of 
companies to develop the production and use of renewable energy. This applies not least to small 
companies that dominate the primary production of energy raw materials in European agriculture 
and forestry. In this way, carbon tax management contributes to meeting the EU's requirements for 
subsidiarity. 
If the political governance is not largely based on general instruments for environmental governance, 
we believe that it will be difficult to tackle the problem that renewable energy in many cases 
becomes expensive compared to fossil energy. 
B.  Objectives and Policy options. 
 
Option 2 
Non-coercive instruments such as education, and information can be good if it is carried out in a 
cost-effective way. 
Option 3 
Option 3 means that the ambition for greenhouse gas reduction will be sharply increased to 50 to 55 
% by 2030 and that parts of REDII (Articles 3, 23, 24 and 25) will be revised to cope with the increased 
level of ambition. 
The Swedish Wood-Fuel Association doubts that it is possible and appropriate to achieve the target 
of 50 to 55% with the policy instruments mentioned in section A. In order to achieve the proposed 
target levels, we believe that a higher degree of general environmental management needs to be 
used. 
Option 4 
Option 4 means that large parts of RED II are revised (Articles 7, 15, 23, 24, 25, 27, 29, 31) so that 
they can be used for legal action to implement actions proposed in other strategies within EGD. 
We do not have a total overview of all other strategies within EGD. We must therefore abstain from 
commenting further on Option 4. However, we fear that there may be several different requirements 
and ideas for control. In that case, EGD risks leading to a very complex, difficult-to-understand and 
cumbersome whole. We can only repeat that we believe that EGD should to a greater extent rely on 
more general instruments than what appears from the document Inception Impact Assessment.  
Option 5 
Option 5 means opening for combinations of Option 2, 3 and 4. 
We refer to what has been said above for each option. We advocate general policy instruments like 
emission trading and carbon tax instead of detailed government of the market.  
 
 
 
 
C.  Preliminary Assessments of Expected Impacts. 
  
The Swedish Wood-Fuel Association definitely wants to advise against experiments with "cascade 
control" ("circular economy measures") which we believe in the long run leads to non-optimal 
market solutions and more expensive energy for EU citizens. We advocate market economy and 
environmental management as we stated under A. above. 
With an increased supply of domestic European renewable energy, production will to a large extent 
take place in small and medium-sized enterprises, as the document describes in section C. 
Significantly more jobs will be created in Europe. To enable such a positive development, it is 
important that new requirements do not lead to increased administrative work in small companies 
(with, for example, certification). We want to emphasize the importance of what is said last in 
section C of the document: "This initiative will aim to reduce administrative burdens… ..". 
Under the heading social aspects, it is said that the initiative is likely to lead to declining energy prices 
for consumers. We believe that this can apply to some sectors and to areas where adjustment can 
already take place with good finances. However, a large part of the adjustment that can be made 
with good finances has already been carried out. But when high-level conversion is to be pushed 
forward in all sectors with the funds proposed, we believe that the costs will increase significantly. 
We see the high ambition as good, but fear that the costs will be higher than what the initiative 
expects. 
Under the heading environmental impact, it is noted that the initiative has positive environmental 
effects. We agree with this. 
In conclusion, it is noted that there is a risk that incentives may be misused for unsustainable 
biomass. The Swedish Wood-Fuel Association advises against incentives via state aid. If the initiative 
chooses appropriate incentives, no measures are needed to avoid abuse of incentives. If measures 
still need to be put in place to counteract the abuse of inappropriate forms of incentive, this will lead 
to an increased administrative burden. It will hurt small market players that are of great importance 
for the implementation of the initiative. 
D.  Evidence Base, Data collection and Better Regulation Instruments.  
We have above devoted a large part of its comments to the issue of Better Instruments. We believe 
that governance must be based on driving forces for market economy combined with environmental 
governance instruments with simple design and general application.

Summary 
GRI’s contribution below includes three recommendations (points 6-8): 
 
• Alignment with international standards 
• Mandatory human rights and environmental due diligence  
• Complementarity with the revision of the NFRD 
 
Contribution 
1. GRI strongly supports the stated objectives to “align better the long-term interests of 
management, shareholders, stakeholders and society” and improve “the framework to 
incentivise corporate boards to integrate properly stakeholder interests, sustainability 
risks, dependencies, opportunities and adverse impacts into strategies, decisions and 
oversight.” We believe that this initiative will help the EU progress in the achievement of 
its sustainable finance ambitions, as set out in the Green Deal, as well as the attainment 
of an environmentally and socially sustainable economy.  
 
2. GRI enables organizations to be transparent and take responsibility for their impacts. We 
are the global standard setter for impact reporting through an independent and multi -
stakeholder process. We maintain the world’s most comprehensive set of sustainability 
reporting standards, which are available as a free public good. The GRI Standards and 
reporting framework enable companies of all types to disclose their impacts on people, 
economy and the environment and thereby their contribution (positive or negative) to 
sustainable development. 
 
3. Embedding sustainable practices into corporate strategies is critical to reaching the 
objectives of the Green Deal. The Commission showed that one of the catalysts for 
change towards sustainable finance is transparency. GRI’s view of sustainability and 
materiality is closely aligned with this thinking, in terms of determining what should be 
disclosed by organizations. The GRI Standards, with their focus on the environmental, 
economic and social impacts of the activities of a company, are the only sustainability 
reporting standard that embodies the outward impact component of the ‘double 
materiality’ principle as articulated by the Commission. Companies around the world 
regularly use the GRI Standards as the basis for reporting financially material 
environmental, social and governance (ESG) issues as well as issues that impact people 
and the planet. Experience has shown that the impacts that are important to society today 
are a leading indicator of impacts that could become financially material to the company 
over time. Further, the Green Deal and the GRI Standards are grounded in the same core 
belief that the environmental and social impacts of economic activities need to be 
managed so future generations may live prosperously.  
 
4. In this context, GRI strongly supports the idea that sustainability should be further 
embedded into corporate governance frameworks. We endorse: 
a. a vision of corporate governance that embraces ‘stakeholder capitalism’ (i.e., that 
requires directors to have regard for all relevant stakeholder interests) and wh ich 
encourages corporates to account for their adverse sustainability impacts; and, 
therefore,  
b. the Commission’s ambition to foster convergence and complementarity with the 
revision of the non-financial reporting directive (NFRD).  
Together, this will create an enabling environment for a financially healthy private sector 
that focuses on long-term sustainable value creation rather than short-term benefits.

5. We appreciate the opportunity to share our initial reactions through the following 
recommendations on three key topics: 
 
6. (i) Alignment with international standards: GRI strives for policy coherence and 
alignment with widely recognized international instruments for responsible business 
conduct. The GRI Standards are aligned with the OECD Guidelines for Multinational 
Enterprises, the UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights, and the ILO 
Tripartite Declaration of Principles concerning Multinational Enterprises and Social Policy. 
In developing a sustainable corporate governance framework including due diligence 
requirements, we recommend that the Commission builds on these existing authoritative 
instruments. 
 
7. (ii) Mandatory human rights and environmental due diligence: GRI acknowledges the 
importance of effective due diligence to support sustainable corporate governance. We 
see transparency as an enabler for behavioural change and therefore disclosure 
requirements as a key component of any effective due diligence legislation. We therefore 
strongly support the Commission’s plan to develop a corporate obligation to carry out due 
diligence as well as mandatory disclosure on human rights and environmental due 
diligence aligned with widely recognized international instruments for responsible 
business conduct. In line with this position, in the review of the Universal Standards, GRI 
has proposed more extensive reporting on how a company meets the requirements of 
“responsible business conduct” including due diligence, covering not only human rights 
but also impacts on the economy and environment. 
 
8. (iii) Complementarity with the revision of the NFRD: GRI recommends making the 
revised NFRD the “hub” for non-financial reporting requirements. We believe that the EU 
should strive for convergence of similar reporting requirements in various legislative 
initiatives focused on sustainability topics and matters of responsible business conduct. 
Ultimately, the NFRD would be the logical place to bring related reporting requirements 
together to foster alignment and minimize the reporting burden for companies. Concrete 
goals and targets can be part of separate legislations, all referring to the NFRD for the 
way to report on them (standards as a reporting language) to ensure comparability and 
usability of the data. 
 
9. In view of the above points, we look forward to actively engaging and sharing our 
expertise throughout the process leading to a new European sustainable corporate 
governance framework.

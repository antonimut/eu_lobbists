Statement on the Evaluation of the eIDAS Regulation 
 
General Information 
In Germany, the eIDAS Regulation is widely unknown and has not yet been transposed 
appropriately into German law. As a result, eIDAS eID and Trust Services are rarely used and 
are simply circumvented in practice (an overview of the transposition of the eIDAS Regulation 
Trust Services in Germany is provided in Bundesdruckerei's study "Vertrauensraum in der 
Digitalisierung"1). The second EU Payment Services Directive (PSD 2) shows how compulsory 
use can be achieved: it is a good example of how the eIDAS Regulation Trust Services have 
been effectively integrated into legal text and how an extension to the private sector has been 
made possible. A similar approach should be adopted in other sectors, like transport, energy, 
or justice. Here, regulatory obligations and incentives for companies in particular can lead to 
greater use of the eIDAS eID and Trust Services. In addition, all national laws containing 
identification and authentication procedures should require the use of eIDAS means. This 
leads to the following overarching recommendations: 
 
 Promote eIDAS eID and  Trust Services in additional sectors through legal 
requirements (see PSD-2 directive for reference) or incentives 
 National legal requirements for identification and authentication procedures must 
respect eIDAS eID and Trust Services. 
 
 
1) Extend use of national eIDs in the private sector and use hardware 
security modules as a technical basis for EU-wide digital application 
The eIDAS Regulation should be developed further to provide a usable, secure and 
interoperable digital identity for public and private online interactions in the European Union. 
Revisions aimed at improving the coherence, consistency and interoperability of the existing 
eIDAS framework, for example through guidelines on liability principles or through simplifying 
the notification procedures for Member States, should be pursued in accordance with this. 
More private use cases in a wide range of services can generally strengthen the use of national 
eIDs. In this context, the use of national eIDs can be further promoted through incentives 
that encourage a wider voluntary acceptance of national eIDs by companies. Mandatory use 
in some areas could also be envisaged. Meanwhile, the concrete design of the national eID 
systems should continue to be left exclusively to the Member States which must assume 
liability for the authenticity of an identity and validate the national eID.  In addition, 
Bundesdruckerei suggests establishing common rules for the use of hardware security 
modules (either in the form of Universal Integrated Circuit Cards (eUICCs), also known as

eSIM, or embedded secure elements, also known as eSEs). Modern smartphones are equipped 
with these modules, which are perfectly suited to support digital applications, e. g. for digital 
identities in a secure and trustworthy way. The a im should be to establish a union -wide 
technical minimum standard for hardware security modules that harmonizes basic security 
mechanisms while giving Member States discretion with the implementation of country -

of eSE and eSIM and to oblige Original Equipment Manufacturers (OEMs), respectively the 
Mobile Network Operators (MNOs) to open their hardware security modules for third parties. 
This uniform standard would be a big step for digital sovereignty within the European digital 
single market demonstrating the security and trustworthiness of mobile ID solutions, while 
helping to make the topic more transparent and comprehensible for citizens and service 
providers alike. In this context, the OPTIMOS 2.0 project initiated by the German Federal 
Ministry for Economic Affairs and Energy is a promising approach that could be used 
throughout the EU. It  creates the basis for reliable, unambiguous identification and 
authentication in the digital world while complying with the highest security and data 
protection standards, thereby providing  preconditions for further digital applications.  
Recommendations are as follows: 
 
 Increasing the number of use cases by incentivising the use of national eIDs in the 
private sector 
 Concrete design of the national eID systems should continue to be left exclusively to 
the Member States  
 Establishing technical minimum standards for the use of hardware security modules  
 Obliging original equipment manufacturers, respectively mobile network operators to 
open their hardware security modules for use of ID applications  
 
2) Greater Commitment to eIDAS Trust Services Usage 
To increase confidence in the European IT infrastructure, commitment should be created 
at national level. All public institutions at national and European level should therefore be 
required to protect their websites with a Qualified Website Authentication Certificate  
(QWAC). The QWAC is a European standard used to verify and transparently display 
website operator identities for consumers. All browsers currently ignore this standard, as 
their interfaces do not contain references to QWAC s. Browsers should be required  to 
publicly display a QWAC, accept it as a European standard, and support the widespread 
use of the EU-Trusted List. For websites of public interest, the imprint obligation (website 
provider identification) should be complemented by the use of QWACs, as these already 
provide the means for identification. To ensure the interoperable use of Trust Services in 
software products, the European Commission should also be able to issue regulations on 
applications (such as operating systems or browsers) if they use  eIDAS Trust Services. 
Furthermore, the validity of signatures, seals , and time stamps is currently defined 
exclusively through a technical check. To date, there is no legal statement as to when a

 Introduce binding coordination between supervisory authorities 
 
 
3) Stronger Harmonisation 
To avoid competitive disadvantages within the EU, greater harmonisation of certification and 
audit requirements for Trust Services is needed. To this end, all references to national 
regulations should be removed from the eIDAS Regulation. Conformity Assessment Bodies 
accreditation as well as Conformity Assessment Body (CAB) work with the Supervisory Bodies 
and Trust Service Providers (TSPs) should be carried out uniformly throughout the EU. For 
example, the requirements for TSPs set up by the national supervisory authorities with regard 
to audit procedures and documentation vary among Member States and should be harmonised.  
For example, audit procedures can take varying amounts of time and depth depending on the 
Member State they are conducted in. The working methods of the assessment and supervisory 
bodies should also be harmonised. In order to prevent disadvantages for individual market 
players in the EU, further harmonisation of means of identification and identification features 
or identification attributes is necessary. This applies in particular to video-based identification 
on the “substantial” level that can be achieved by harmonising the application of Article 24 
(1).  At the same time, storage periods for certificates and identity data need to be defined in 
concrete terms. This could be ten years after the end of the certificate validity. Such a 
regulation can be achieved by clarifying the scope of Article 24 (2)a. In this case, exceptions 
may be regulated by national legislation. Lastly, a uniform catalogue of algorithms for the 
generation and validating of Trust Services can counteract the current wide scope for 
interpretation. Recommendations are as follows: 
 
 Remove references to possible national schemes 
 Harmonise approval of Trust Service Providers (TSPs)  
 Harmonise the requirements of Supervisory Bodies for TSPs 
 Harmonise audits of TSPs

4) Further Remarks 
Implementing acts are a method for making use of eIDAS Trust Services mandatory and 
of achieving the necessary harmonisation. In general, we welcome the introduction of as 
many implementing acts as possible to create a level playing field in the market.  In the 
case of the current Protection Profile (CEN EN 419 241 -2 - Trustworthy Systems 
Supporting Server Signing Part2: Protection Profile for QSCD for Server Signing) on the 
subject of remote signatures,  no implementing act  should be introduced. Most current 
implementations of remote signatures do not support this architectural design.  The 
integration of this Protection Profile into an implementing act would lead to large 
investments by trust service providers  and threaten the small, emerging market for 
remote signatures.  Furthermore, during the technical transposition of the PSD  2, it 
became clear that a reference EU Trusted List would have greatly accelerated technical 
implementation for the financial market. Usually, reference environments are repeatedly 
created in the private sector to test changes or extensions. For this reason , a reference 
EU Trusted List should be provided  by the same institutions , similar to the previously 
productive EU Trusted List. Recommendations are as follows: 
 
 No consideration of Protection Profile CEN EN 419 241-2 
 Provide a reference EU Trusted List for a better testing environment

   
 
 
Introduction of higher security measures 
 
For a couple of public procedures such as the issue of national ID documents or the notarial 
authentication procedure, national law requires face -to-face interaction between the applicants and 
the public authority in order to meet highest identification st andards. These standards are necessary 
in particular to prevent identity fraud (e.g. by passing on an eID plus PIN) and to ensure the accuracy 
and reliability of public registers (e.g. national business registers and land registers) which are 
endowed with public faith in many Member States.  
In order to allow that the eIDAS Regulation can also be used in future for the aforementioned 
authentication procedure and other public law procedures with the highest identification 
requirements, the existing three ass urance levels in the eIDAS Regulation should be supplemented 
by higher security measures, which add  to the existing assurance level “high” a videoconferencing 
identification procedure. The latter must make sure that an official photograph of the owner of t he 
eID is being transferred to the national public authority. In fact, this is the only way to ensure that 
electronic identification procedures are of (at least) the same standard as the existing procedures using 
an identity card or passport in the physical world. 
This is demonstrated by the example of the online formation of a limited liability company. In this 
case, the existing eID procedure and the aforementioned videoconferencing identification procedure 
must be combined in an online authentication pro cedure in order to ensure the most reliable 
identification of the parties involved. The party must first authenticate himself/herself – as required 
by the Digital Tools Directive (EU) 2019/1151 – by an electronic identification means (eID) with the 
assurance level "high" according to the eIDAS Regulation (e.g. by way of an identity card with an 
electronic identification function or an EU residence permit or by a full digital ID level high, if 
applicable). The notary can see via his system that the party involved has a valid eID and read out the 
necessary data. In doing so, the notary must also be able to receive the party’s official photograph 
which is sent to him via the national eID system in order to compare it with his/her video image 
during the videoconference. This is precisely not possible in a conventional videoident procedure, as 
it only allows a comparison with the ID card held in the camera. The advantage of combining the two 
methods is that the weaknesses of the respective other method are mitigat ed. The weakness of the 
(mere) videoident procedure is that the authenticity of the ID card held in the camera cannot be 
reliably verified. The weakness of the (mere) eID procedure is that it is impossible to detect the use 
of eIDs which are stolen or deli berately passed on for abusive purposes – as well as psychological 
pressure and legal incapacity. These weaknesses are significantly mitigated by the combination of 
the current eID procedure with the aforementioned videoconferencing identification procedure, since 
both the eID and the declaration of the party can be assigned to a specific person at the moment of 
their submission. 
The current assurance level “high” would not meet these identification requirements, which are 
necessary to prevent identity fraud and to ensure for example the accuracy of business registers and 
land registers. On the other hand, if the revised eIDAS Regulation provided for the proposed high er 
security measures , Member States could use the eIDAS Regulation in future also for the 
aforementioned authentication procedures and other public law procedures with the highest 
identification requirements, which currently require the personal appearance of the applicant.

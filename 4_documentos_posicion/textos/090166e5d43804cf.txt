The eIDAS regulation, as a common basis for trusted digital identities and trust services, definitely strengthens the EU digital single market.  AFSC supports its objective to make easier and safer the use of online services while giving people more control over their personal data and privacy. We are now into a digital world where Digital Identity is becoming an essential requirement and where the technologies for the e-ID systems are evolving rapidly. It is key that these systems are interoperable across Members States to make sure that we allow innovation and digital transactions to grow in a secure manner. A harmonised e-ID system would significantly reduce the cost of compliance for digital businesses and would offer new opportunities for companies to meet their compliance obligations.  Baseline scenario, with current Regulation unchanged, is a no-progress option, and we do not sustain it.   Option1, reinforced baseline scenario, allows for further enhancements and extended usages of eIDs under eIDAS and will finish clarifying the eIDAS security requirements and Levels of Assurance.  Revisions aimed at improving the coherence, consistency and interoperability of the existing eIDAS framework, for example through guidelines on liability principles to promote the use of eIDs in the private sector, should be pursued. Abandoning the doctrine of technological neutrality, if it leads to the adoption of open and already recognized international standards and specifications, will reduce the technical difficulties encountered today in ensuring the interoperability of solutions.  Option2, extending the scope of eID regulation under eIDAS to the private sector, maybe through a dedicated regulation, will define the necessary harmonized standards for the reuse of notified eID schemes by the private sector. This extended/dedicated regulation should also identify or request the development of a European Certification Scheme, under the CyberAct, when it comes to the evaluation of private eID schemes, and provide the necessary means to ensure a clear legal framework and liability for private actors. A harmonised EU wide online (i.e. non-face-to -face) framework would facilitate the introduction of a truly cross-border identification services market, and markedly reduce the cost of compliance for digital businesses.  Option 3, the European Digital Identity scheme (EUid), set up a parallel scheme to the already existing eID schemes, adds complexity to the eIDAS ecosystem and presumes additional funding. The quick option, to add a EU label on top of national eIDs notified by Member States, could be more effective.  Conclusion. Also necessary, Option 1 may not be sufficient. We sustain to use a combination of Option 1 and Option 2, to significantly improve the current situation and make eIDAS more unambiguous and transparent.   Related to the general update of the eIDAS Regulation, we have the following observation: In its current form, the Regulation uses, in certain cases, the same terms, “electronic signature” and “advanced electronic signature”, both in the case where it refers to the category of electronic signatures, respectively of advanced electronic signature, and where it refers to types of electronic signatures, namely, advanced electronic signatures, types that are identified by the specific method of signature creation, and this leads to confusion. A distinct observation related to the updates on the Regulation is on the quality of the text translation in different languages. For example the Romanian translation have a few errors that need corrections. Like the following: • Article 3 (2) instead of ‘for an online service’ the romanian translation is for ‘of an online service’  • Article 8 (2) (a) instead of ‘provides a limited degree’ the romanian translation is for ‘provides a substantial degree’ • Article 33 title, where instead of ‘validation service’ the romanian translation is for ‘preservation service’  • Article 43 (2) where instead of ‘using a qualified electronic registered delivery service’ the romanian translation miss the qualified attribute.

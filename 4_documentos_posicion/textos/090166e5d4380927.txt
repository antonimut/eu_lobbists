Twilio response to the European Commission consultation  
on digital identity and trust: the eIDAS regulation 
 
Twilio welcomes the opportunity to provide comments on the Commission’s review of the 
eIDAS Regulation and on the drivers and barriers to the development and adoption of eID 
and trust services in Europe. 
 
Introduction to Twilio 
 
Twilio is a leading global cloud communications services provider. Twilio's products and 
services allow all types of organizations – including businesses, governments and non-profit 
organizations - to embed communications capabilities in their web, desktop, and mobile 
applications, enabling them to communicate more efficiently and effectively, and reinvent 
how they engage with their customers. Organizations have used Twilio to allow their end-
users to contact their teacher or students, alert the public about an emergency, video chat 
with their doctor, speak with their rideshare driver, make a bank transaction, shop online, 
authenticate an account, and interact with elected officials, among many other activities.  
 
As a business to business (B2B) company, Twilio currently handles more than 800 billion 
interactions every year for more than 200,000 customers globally. Twilio customers range 
from small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) to the world's largest corporations and 
come from a broad range of industries including education, healthcare, logistics, retail and 
financial services. Twilio is paid by organizations for the services it provides and does not 
monetize consumer data. 
 
Founded in 2008 and headquartered in the United States, Twilio now has 26 offices in 16 
countries with the infrastructure to support communications worldwide. Trust and privacy 
have been core principles for Twilio since the company’s founding. The relationships 
between brands and consumers constantly evolve, but trust and privacy remain critical to 
successful customer engagement. 
 
Twilio’s interest in the eIDAS regulation as a user AND a provider 
 
Twilio views the eIDAS Regulation from two distinct perspectives:  
● as a multinational cloud communications company active across the EU, Twilio uses 
electronic identification tools of the public sector in various member states, for 
instance to fulfill certain regulatory requirements as an electronic communications 
service provider (ECS).  
● as a trusted service provider offering authentication services. While Twilio is not 
formally qualified under the eIDAS regulation because its authentication products rely 
on two-factor verification rather than the trust services covered by the current eIDAS 
regulation, it supports high global standards for electronic authentication and is 
contributing with its products to that end.

Twilio’s experience as a user of eID and trust services 
 
Twilio offers services that require secured interactions with public authorities, such as to provide 
legally required information on its operations in various member states. Twilio has registered as 
an ECS in the majority of EU member states and, as such, needs to meet various obligations, 
including those on regulatory reporting and verification of customer identity. Additionally, when 
entering into contracts with public authorities, some authorities offer the use of electronic 
identities and of trusted services for the execution of those contracts and other contractual 
requirements. Twilio’s regulatory and contracting teams therefore make regular use of electronic 
ID and electronic signatures. 
 
In Twilio’s experience, the eIDAS framework is an excellent concept in theory, but has not been 
implemented in a way that provides the anticipated benefits. Cross-border interoperability does 
not exist in a number of member states, and some member states still do not allow the use of an 
eID for certain activities. For instance, a member of Twilio’s team had to be present in person in 
Luxembourg and at a Hungarian consulate in order to obtain the tools necessary to enable Twilio 
to fulfill various reporting obligations in those countries. In order to be able to acquire telephone 
numbers in Estonia, an Estonian eID had to be obtained - no other European eID was 
acceptable. The process for obtaining the eID is cumbersome, with a process that originates with 
a face-to-face meeting at an Estonian consulate and can then take up to 8 weeks. Sweden, on 
the other hand, is a positive example because it accepts other European eIDs. 
 
From a user perspective, Twilio welcomes the European Commission’s idea of developing a 
single and universally accepted digital identity scheme valid across Europe. Twilio believes that 
an obligation for the public sector across Europe to recognize and accept eIDs and other trust 
services, no matter from which member state such services originate, is necessary to advance 
the value of the framework. 
 
Twilio believes that such a program would help private and business users to conveniently 
interact with public authorities, augment trust for citizens in digital interactions, and bring down 
the costs associated with these interactions, which can be significant, especially in the case of 
multinational companies such as Twilio.  
 
Twilio’s perspective as a provider of identification services 
 
Twilio provides authentication and verification services that build on top of an initial identification. 
While Twilio is not formally recognized as a trust service provider under the eIDAS framework, as 
it does not currently fall into any of the existing eIDAS categories of trust services, the European 
Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA) has listed Twilio in its overview of the technological 
landscape of eID solutions as part of its report on eIDAS-compliant EID solutions (March 2020).  
 
Twilio’s Authy/Verify product is a two-factor Authentication/Verification API solution that allows 
customers to elevate their security and enhance user experience via multiple authentication 
channels, such as SMS/voice/email one-time passcode. The Authy App uses soft tokens and 
Push Authentication, Google Authenticator or push authentication via an SDK. Authy is 
configured globally out of the box, optimized and translated for 200+ regions, with built-in

Twilio believes that the broadening of the eIDAS framework to recognise a broader range of trust

consumer confidence in the digital ecosystem, as well as further uptake of the eIDAS framework 
by service providers and consumers alike. 
 
Extension of the eIDAS framework to the private sector 
 
Twilio answers this question from the perspective of a service provider. As mentioned above, the 
eIDAS framework is an excellent concept in theory, but its implementation has major flaws, 
especially when it comes to cross-border interoperability. While an eventual extension of the 
framework to the private sector could be desirable, a number of issues need to be addressed and 
resolved first: 
 
Voluntary participation 
● The use of trusted services under the eIDAS framework must remain voluntary. 
Businesses must be able to select which type of authentication is most appropriate for 
their purpose. To mandate specific services would create a disproportionate burden on 
businesses to redesign systems to accept eID solutions in use across all member 
states, potentially before they reach ‘critical mass’ in public sector adoption and achieve 
sufficient uptake and familiarity among EU citizens. 
 
Alignment with global standards 
● Twilio recommends that any EU program, including the potential development of a 
common EU eID system, should build on and contribute to global or multiregional eID 
solutions to avoid barriers to cross-border trade from emerging, especially at a time 
when interacting in person for certain transactions runs counter to medical advice 
relating to COVID-19. 
 
Interoperability is key 
● Interoperability of services should be mandated and facilitated where adoption occurs. 
A major intended benefit of the eIDAS framework was the facilitation of cross-border 
electronic identification. This would be highly beneficial especially for multinational

Openness to technological innovation 
● An important consideration for the use of the eIDAS framework is the need to create a

for technical innovation. Security tools for electronic interaction are becoming 
increasingly sophisticated and user-friendly. The rapid digitization in response to the 
COVID-19 pandemic has created an even greater momentum for developing new and 
improved solutions for electronic authentication. While such services should adhere to 
the highest standards, the eIDAS framework should remain open for such innovation.

October 2020 
 
The European Confederation of Police (EuroCOP) supports the Commission’s initiative to 
strengthen automated data exchanges under the Prüm framework.  
 
As a body that has vast first -hand experience in the area, EuroCOP acknowledges the 
existing shortcomings of the framework, and fully supports a revision of the system to make 
it better applicable to the present day.  EuroCOP believes that the current lands cape of 
different instruments, channels and tools is complicated, leading to inefficient use of the 
framework. Improving cross-border information exchange is not an end in itself. The purpose 
is to be able to tackle organised crime more efficiently. For this purpose, information sharing 
has to become quicker and will have to be done in a more comprehensive way at European 
level. 
  
Administrative burdens: 
As set out in the impact assessment, the future framework should take into account the 
existing administrative burden on law enforcement and the need to balance these tasks with 
operational police work. In the Netherlands for example, the same crime needs to be reported 
and entered into three different systems, a procedure which takes 1.5 hours to complete. The 
revised framework should replace, rather than complement national systems where 
appropriate, to avoid excessive administrative burden. 
  
Applicability of Prüm post-Brexit: 
With the transition period for the UK’s exit ending in a few months, EuroCOP is concerned 
about the effects of Brexit on criminal organisations. The applicability of Prüm Decisions that 
the UK has opted into is a key concern for EuroCOP.  
 
If these Decisions are to become void, and the data exchanges stop, the ability of police 
officers to complete their work, both in the UK as well as in connected countries, will be 
significantly hindered. Whilst the data exchanges will stop in such a scenario, criminal 
organisations will continue operating.  
 
Therefore, a serious consideration in the review of the Prüm framework must be the process 
for third countries (which the UK will become) to become parties to these Decisions.

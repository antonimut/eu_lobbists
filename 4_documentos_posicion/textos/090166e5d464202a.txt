1. Executive summary 
Assonime welcomes the opportunity to present its feedbacks on the European 
Commission Inception Impact Assessment.  
Assonime supports the European Commission intention to favour the evolution of the 
EU company business es toward a sustainable and resilient corporate growth. In this 
light, we believe that s ustainability practices – developed in line with the internatio nal 
standards set forth by the UN and OECD  – can help to reconcile economic growth, 
social progress and the protection of  the environment  and should be carefully 
considered when assessing any legislative initiative at EU level.  
However, Assonime believes that the choice of policy tools for implementing such 
a goal should be carefully considered, as they can have unintended consequences 
on the incentives of companies to invest  and on the  effectiveness of capital 
markets to attract companies and to support them to grow.  
As a matter of fac t, w e think that capital markets can pla y a crucial role also in 
stimulating and supporti ng companies’ evolution toward sustainability. 
Therefore, any policy action by the EU concerning company law should be aimed to 
enhance such a role and not to frustrate it. 
In this perspective, the view – which appears to be the underlying fil rouge of the Ernst 
& Young  study (hereinafter “EY study”) – of a general polarization between fatally 
‘short-term’ oriented shareholders and fatally ‘long-term’ oriented other stakeholder s 
appears simplistic and misleading. 
On this regard, we observe that compared to its ambitious mandate (“assess the root 
causes of short termism in corporate governance” in order to “support the European 
Commission in identifying the need for a  possible reform in corporate law and board 
duties to foster sustainable corporate governance ”), th e EY study appears to be 
rather lacking and its conclusions unconvincing , as it is  based on an ideologically 
biased conceptual framework and on a methodologically questionable empirical 
analysis.  
As a matter of fact, t he EY study builds on two main assumptions, which are revealing 
of its  biased approach:  first, corporate decision -makers focus on short -term results; 
second, this short -termism reduces the long -term economic, environmental and social 
sustainability of European businesses. 
The short -term assumption, which is the necessary condition for the second one, is 
asserted being supported by an empirical analysis of companies’ pay -outs and capital 
and R&D investments over the last 30 years . We believe that the methodological and 
substantial weaknesses of th at empirical analysis are very substantial and should 
prevent their use as a basis for any policy conclusion.

Our main concerns about the EY study, fully described in the attached note,  can be 
summarized as follows. 
The data sample used to assess short-termism is not transparent and it appears 
strongly biased in its composition:  
- the inclusion of UK companies is inconsistent with the policy-oriented goal of the 
study, as an impact analysis for future policy options should not consider the 
market features of a co untry that will no longer be part of the EU . In fact, other 
parts of the study treat the UK as a ‘third country’. Moreover, because of the size 
of the UK market, its inclusion may well distort the sample representativeness  
(we estimates UK c ompanies represent between one forth and one third of the 
sample, but a n accurate assessment is prevented by the op acity of the sample 
composition);  
- the selection of only 15 member countries out of 27 appears neither clear nor 
reasonably consistent: the i nclusion of som e very small economies and stock 
markets, such as Slovakia and Slovenia, and the exclusion of some larger  
economies and stock markets, such as Denmark and Ireland, has no justification 
and is potentially misleading, if not instrumental. As a  matter of fact , Slovakia 
largely comes out as the most short-term oriented country. 
Both pay-out and investment findings suffer from estimation problems, as the EY 
figures systematically: 
- overestimate the actual pay-out of the European companies , as they neglect 
inward capital flows (capital increase and debt);  
- underestimates their actual long -term investments , as they neglect investment 
realized through M&A. 
Even accepting EY figures, their interpretation in the report seems inconsistent:  
- the current pay out for Europ ean companies is reported around 55%, a level 
which is 40% lower than for US companies , so it can hardly be considered an 
‘excessive’ level, at least in comparative terms 
-  the increase in pay out in Europe is concentrated in the decade preceding the 
financial crisis, while the decre ase in investment is co ncentrated in the decad e 
following the financial crisis, challenging that there is a causal link between them, 
as supposed by the short-termism hypothesis, and that other possible reasons for 
their evolution should be considered. 
Other concerns can be raised with r espect to the consultation with stakeholders 
and to the cost-benefit analysis realized by EY, where the same problems of opacity 
and methodological bias are found.

1. Main concerns about the analysis of the EU businesses ’ (supposed) short 
termism 
The EY study is built upon the assertion that companies tend to favour the short -term 
maximisation of shareholder value to the detriment of the long-term development of the 
company. 
These conclusions are based  on a lacking analysis of the EU econom y trends and a 
superficial understanding of the functioning of capital markets. 
General methodological remarks about the sample 
The study does not provide suffici ent information a bout the  selection and the 
composition of the sample , which appears in contrast with international common 
practices. In fact, studies promoted by international organi sations in  order to  orient 
policy making normally give stakeholders, which could be affect ed by po licy choices, 
enough information to assess the quality of the empirical analysis that supports such 
choices and provide further evidence.

The selecting criteria of the sample do not seem appropriate, such as: 
- UK companies – which reasonably represent a significant portion of the sample -

- The study systematically favours the more stringent legislative interve ntions,

preceded by and adequate disclosure about the analysis that is actually needed 
to draw such conclusions; 
- The study does not provide a clear overview of the contributions submitted by the

Substantial remarks about the data analysis 
The study’s conclusions – namely that EU businesses are short -term oriented due to 
the increase of dividend payments  and share buyback s and to the decreas e of

2. About the m easures aimed to address adverse sust ainability impacts 
through the value chain 
The Inception Impact Assessment sta tes the EC intentio n to further explore the 
evolution of th e due di ligence measures along the supply chain , in order to design a 
duty “by building on existing authoritative guidelines using well -established definitions 
as developed by the UN and later expanded by the OECD” .  
The issue of an EU framework on the  due diligence through supply chain  is of crucial 
importance. Therefore, Assonime is eager to work together with the European 
Commission to improve the way companies operate and calls for close cooper ation 
between all s takeholders involved in order to tackle this highly complex and global 
challenge.  
Considering the increasing voluntary steps taken by companies to ensure a better 
control of risks linked  to human rights and sustainability along their su pply chains and 
the numerous sectorial initiatives in this regard, we believe that any m easure in this 
field shou ld carefully consider  some crucial issues in order to ensure a pro per and 
effective EU framework, through realistic, proportionate and flexible criteria.  
For this purpose, we would like to underline the following challenges which should be 
taken into account by any future European initiative. 
As to their scope of any measure to address adverse sustainability impacts through the 
value chain, we believe that any EU measure invite the European Commission to:  
- focus on relevant supply chains only, to be understood as the supply c hain on 
which the company has a significant market power . This concept co uld be  
assessed, for example, by considering the significance of the supplier’s revenues 
from the company in comparison to its total revenues. 
- focus on the first tier of the supply ch ains only, where the companies are in a 
position to exercise leverage thr ough the contractual relationship. Any further 
obligation down the supply chain would make it legal ly uncertain and practically 
impossible for companies to contro l and enforce their internal due diligence 
guidelines. 
- cover larger companies and company grou ps only , who are the ones that 
have an effective m arket power over their supply chain. In this line, for instance, 
the O ECD Due D iligence Guidance for R esponsible Business Conduct r efers 
only to multinational enterprises and multinational group. It would b e 
inappropriate to extend same i nitiatives to  SMEs. In this perspective, a strong 
proportionality approach is to be  adopted, following what is provided for by the 
UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights.

- cover also non-EU large companies and company groups providing goods or 
services in the EU.  Imposing rules on EU companies only would lead to a 
competitive disadvantage for EU companies, while their competitors from outside 
the EU would continue to enter the EU market with products and services 
possibly not respecting social and environmental minimum standards.  
As to the contents of any initiative on the due diligence in the  supply chain, we invite 
the Euro pean Commission to consider the following remarks in order to avoid legal 
uncertainty: 
- focus on more sever e and specific risks only , considering for examples 
specific matters (e.g. human rights , environmental risks). Developing a material 
approach for the selection of se vere risks is important to ensure legal certainty, 
considering that it is impossible to mitigate every single risk on the supply chains. 
- the publication of the companies’ due diligence strategy should respect  the 
principle of materiality  and be compatible with the preservation of commercial 
secrets. 
- limit the EU company liability  to the exten t they have directly caused the 
damage or intentionally contributed to it . In this light, UNGP and OECD 
guidelines – mentioned in the IIA – are perfectly clear that due diligence does not 
shift responsibilities, neither from governments to comp anies, nor from suppliers 
or subcontractors to the  companies placing the order. Each State or company 
has its own responsibil ity for the action it takes and the adverse impacts it may 
have. The companies’ duty of care should nei ther remove the liability of  each 
local actor for its acts nor place the burden of liability on EU companies.  
Therefore, we invite the European Commission to consider in any further initiative on 
the due diligence in the supply chain: 
- companies’ instances for a clear, proportionate and effective framework  
in this field; 
- the relevant com petitive issues that could arise by the  limitation of the 
mentioned measures to EU companies only.

Company interests 
National frameworks already provide rules on directors' duties and responsibilities that 
ensure the evolution of the compan y’s purpose, in line with the emerg ing best 
practices. In most national legal frameworks, definitions of the company interest are 
“open” and are generally supplemented by ca se law to allow for a better consideration 
of long-term view and stakeholders’ interests. 
Furthermore, a significant complementary source for a complete view on the concrete 
evolution of the co mpany’s interest is re presented by the Corporate Governance 
Codes, which already recommend the board to pursue long -term value creation and to 
consider also the interest of other st akeholders that a re relevant for the company's 
business. Such an approach is already observable in a number of national jurisdictions 
such as France, the UK, Italy, Belgium, Germany, and the Netherlands. 
As to the Italian  context, the new Italian Corporate Governance Code has a general 
and overarching principle recommending the board to  “pursue the sustainable success 
of the company’s activity” and defines it as “the objective that guides the action s of the 
board of direct ors and that consists of creating l ong-term value fo r the benefit of the 
shareholders, taking into account the interests of other st akeholders relevant to the 
company”. 
To this end the Italian Corporate Governance Codes promotes the board’s  “dialogue 
with shareholders and other stakeholders which are releva nt for the company ” to be 
developed “in the most appropriate way”.

Sustainability risks 
The first mission of  a board i s to determine the strategy o f the  company taking into 
account the risks it is confronted to and identified opportunities. 
Integrating and reporting on risk factors ar e thus key components of corporate 
stewardship and have been included in EU leg islation for long: in accordance with t he 
Accounting Directive, the management report shall include “a fair review of the 
development and performance of the un dertaking's busine ss and of its position, 
together with a description of the principal risks and uncertainties that it faces”. 
Furthermore, even in thi s field, an important complementary s ource is represented by 
corporate governance codes, which have developed a link between corporate purpose 
and the development of the company’s strategy . It is theref ore unjustified to consider

Directors’ remuneration

companies include in the remuneration policy non -financial performance crit eria 
relating to corporate social respo nsibility, where they are material f or the company’s 
strategy. This provision already ro utes the de finition of directors ’ remuneration in 
accordance with the sustainability goals that contribute to the company’s long -term 
performance thus offering companie s sufficient room to set appropriate  incentives for 
its management.

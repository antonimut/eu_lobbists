SUSTAINABLE CORPORATE GOVERNANCE AND THE ROLE OF THE INVESTMENT INDUSTRY 
EFAMA, the voice of the European investment management industry, welcomes the Commission’s 
initiative to improve the EU regulatory framework on company law and corporate governance. 
Encouraging companies to focus on long-term, sustainable value creation has the potential to promote a 
cultural shift in the way companies operate, and  unlock sizeable investments towards a sustainable and 
just transition. Asset managers show a strong commitment to this  objective, not only by mobilizing and 
channelling capital towards sustainable investments, but also by exercising the tools at their dis posal to 
promote change in the corporate governance and business practices of the companies they invest in. A 
long-term perspective and a careful integration of sustainability considerations in the investment strategy 
are essential to creating durable value for the clients and to future-proof investments. 
Investment stewardship plays an important role in encouraging long -term, sustainable value 
creation. It consists of voting and engagement by investors , e.g. asset owners or asset managers on 
behalf of their clients, with investee companies. Asset managers conduct stewardship activities as part of 
their duties as fiduciaries. They engage with investee companies on issues relevant to the long -term 
performance of the firm, typically including firm governance, executive remuneration, and corporate 
performance issues. 
Investment stewardship raises the bar on corporate governance, by holding Boards to account.  
Asset managers have strong incentives to engage with the companies in which they invest their clients’ 
capital, so as to ensure the long -term potential of those holdings to preserve and add value for clients. 
This involves conducting analysis and research, as well as setting out clear expectations to the investee 
companies about the ESG standards they are expected to adhere to. This has led to the cr eation of 
effective tools to assess the due diligence policies and business practices enacted by investee 
companies. 
Asset managers undertake a wide range of engagement activities as stewards of investments, all 
with the goal of promoting long term investm ent success.  EFAMA’s members have a range of 
different approaches to governance and broad stewardship activities, with procedures in place to provide 
transparency to investors. Many asset managers publish material online to provide transparency on their 
engagement activities (incl. internal principles, guidelines and summary of voting activity). At the moment, 
there are more than 20 stewardship codes and initiatives worldwide which encourage or require 
signatories to be active in investment stewardship, including EFAMA’s Stewardship Code1, which collects 
European best practices. As the stewardship initiatives demonstrate, investors (asset owners and asset 
managers acting on their behalf), are expected to be active in shareholder engagement . 
While not involved in the day to day management of the companies they invest in, asset managers have 
a number of options to pursue if concerned about decisions taken by the Board . Asset managers 
can seek regular communication or meetings with the Board, as part of ongoing relationship management 
and to voice any concerns privately, or express their views by voting on resolutions at general meetings.  
If a company fails to address asset managers’ concerns, at the risk of material impact to its long term 
financial performance, managers of active investment strategies can decide to sell their holdings. 
However, the impact of these tools is sometimes limited by the ownership structure of EU listed 
companies, where financial institutions only account for a minority share. 
Stewardship supports the long-term objectives of the ESG & long-term investment agenda. Asset

managers engage with companies to mitigate ESG risks in their portfolio. For many companies, 
engagement is also accompanied by frequent publications (e.g . CEO letters, thought leadership papers) 
on key issues, annual prioritisation process and detailed client reporting. The value of stewardship is 
becoming increasingly well understood by ever more asset owners, asset managers and policymakers. 
Several managers have committed to the United Nations Principles for Responsible Investment (PRI) 
which include the principle of ‘engaging asset owners on ESG issues and providing appropriate disclosure 
on ESG issues to investors’. 
Effective engagement is important to both managers of active and index investment strategies. 
For the first, engagement is a means to undertake fundamental analysis, driving their investment strategy, 
and remain engaged with companies on issues that impact value over the long term.  Index investing, on 
the other hand, prevents investors from exiting their position by selling companies’ shares . Investors 
remain shareholders for as long as the company stays in the index and , for that, they  engage with 
companies to help enhance their long-term performance. 
Beyond S RD II, EU regulatory action might further enhance long -term engagement between 
investors and their investee companies .2 Shareholders, to perform their role as stewards of the 
companies they invest in, need to be equipped with proper tools. To further promote shareholder 
engagement, including on sustainability topics,  the Commission could consider initiatives that facilitate 
shareholders’ access to the entire Board. We also note that companies with majority or controlling 
shareholders have fewer incentives for a dialogue with their minority shareholders, as management 
proposals can go through approval with the sole voting power of their majority shareholder. With regards 
to voting, we also find that directors’ (re-)appointment provides an effective mechanism for shareholders’ 
engagement and it makes directors more responsive the concerns raised . In addition, i t is important  
shareholders can effectively propose resolutions on the agenda of general meetings and that there are 
sufficient minority shareholder protection safeguards. 
It is important to ensure a consistent and pragmatic approach to setting new rules on sustainable 
corporate governance. If carefully monitored, and if gold-plating is avoided, we believe that a consistent 
transposition of SRD II across Member States has the potential to equip shareholders with more effective 
tools to perform their function. Regarding directors’ remuneration, we note that SRD II, once implemented, 
should not only improve the transpar ency around directors’ remuneration, but also give more  powers to 
shareholders in this respect. EFAMA champions a real shift in corporate thinking, encouraging Boards to 
design a well-thought-through and consistent sustainability approach. 
In conclusion, we wish to highlight that i nvestor engagement has proven to be one of the most effective 
means to foster better corporate , environmental, social and governance practices. The industry is 
committed to its role in fostering sustainable corpora te governance and we support in principle the 
development of an EU framework. At EFAMA we look forward to taking part in the consultation on the 
upcoming legislative initiatives and we stand ready to  support the Commission’s work to develop an 
ambitious, effective and pragmatic framework.

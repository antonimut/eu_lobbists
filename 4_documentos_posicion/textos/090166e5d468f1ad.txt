protection and economic role of investment, corporate governance, investor

As a preamble, it seems essential to us to recall the long- term perspective of investors, 
which converges towards that of stakeholders. We believe it is important to promote soft 
law and dialogue given the great diversity of listed companies, whether in t erms of size or 
sector of activity.  
 
Investor dialogue with listed companies helps them move towards greater transparency and 
better consideration of the environment in which they operate. The sole focus on the 
payment of dividends is restrictive, especially as there is a diversity of shareho lders, and it 
seems reductive to consider that investors are the cause of an excessive dividend payment. 
Furthermore, the dividend is also important for individual shareholders and should not be 
overlooked. 
 
In any event, prior to the implementation of any  new regulations, it would seem useful for 
an assessment of the implementation of the various components of SRD2 in all European 
countries and more generally of the latest European regulations on these topics 
(transparency directive, Market abuse regulation…).  
 
 
 
1 Duties of directors and corporate interest  
Regarding the duties of directors and the social interest, France has put in place rules with the 
PACTE law. We suggest similar rules to be adopted at the national level of other European 
countries. 
 
2  Investor pressure  
 
Far from exerting short -term pressure, investors are increasingly committed to encouraging 
long-term issuers with their engagement. They are careful to ensure that the issuers' objectives 
are not short -term, even though they publish their results on a quarterly basis. It would be 
advisable to keep flexibility on these aspects by favoring soft-law in application of the spirit of 
SRD2. 
 
 
3   Integration of sustainability into the strategy  
 
On aspects of sustainability in business strategy, it seems that soft law is the most appropriate.

4 -5  Remuneration of the Board of Directors  
 Composition of the Board of Directors  
 
The issues of remuneration and composition of the Board of Directors must be adapted to the 
size of the company, its capital structure, as well as its maturity. It is better to keep flexibility in 
adapting to various situations without risking unnecessary complexity even though, through 
dialogue, investors contribute to the positive development of these compani es in a long-term 
perspective.  
 
This dialogue, combined with investors’ vote, enables concrete progress in European 
companies.  
 
The independence, diversity and complementarity of expertise within the Board of Directors 
are difficult balances to achieve. There is no magic formula for board composition that would 
fit all companies. However, these objectives are on the agenda of investors attentive to 
corporate governance as a guarantee for the establishment of checks and balances on 
management in the long-term interest of the company. 
 
 
 
6  Involvement of the company's stakeholders  
 
Provisions on stakeholders must be part of an overall logic of coherence, and in particular 
reflections at European level on the duty of vigilance. France is already ahead on these 
aspects.  
 
 
 7 Application of the duties of directors  
 
Integration into the duties of directors to act in the long- term interest of the company seems 
essential to us and, as an exception to the other topics above, should be integrated into hard  
law.

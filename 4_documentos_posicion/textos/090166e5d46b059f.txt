World	Fair	Trade	Organization	Europe	(WFTO-Europe)	Rue	Washington	40,	1050	Brussels	www.wfto-europe.org		-		Phone:	(0)2	640	63	86		Input	 for	 the	 Public	 Consultation	 on	 the	Commission	Inception	 Impact	Assessment	on	Sustainable	corporate	governance		Brussels,	8th	October	2020					WFTO-Europe	warmly	 welcomes	 the	 European	 Commission’s	 focus	 on	sustainable	 corporate	 governance	 and	 for	 recognising	 several	 of	 the	 issues	directly	related	with	profit-maximisation	as	the	dominant	business	model	of	late.	Indeed,	 WFTO-Europe	 believes	–	as	 is	 demonstrated	 by	 the	 efforts	 of	 our	members,	all	mission-led	businesses	fully	practicing	Fair	Trade	–	that	addressing	the	 governance	 model	 of	 companies	 holds	 the	 key	 to	 ensuring	 long-term	environmental	 and	 social	 sustainability	 in	 Europe	 as	 well	 as	 in	 third	 countries	with	 which	 the	 EU	 trades	 and	 from	 where	 EU-based	 companies	 source	components,	products	or	raw	materials.				We	see	several	issues	that	call	for	clear,	unambiguous	regulation	of	corporate	governance,	 in	 particular	 to	 address	 sustainability	 issues,	 among	 them	 in-work	poverty	and	human	rights	violations	directly	linked	to	the	purchasing	practices	of	 companies.	EU-wide	 legislation	 is	 necessary	 to	 address	 these	 issues,	 as	voluntary	 initiatives	–	as	 the	 Commission	 has	 itself	 established	–	are	 not	sufficient.	 Mandatory	 and	 binding	 legislation	 on	 due	 diligence	 on	 human	 rights	and	 the	 environment	(HREDD	 legislation)	in	 the	 entire	 supply	 chain	 of	businesses	 is	 needed	 and	 it	 must	 be	 cross-sectoral	 and	 complemented	 by	binding,	setor-specific	guidelines	for	high-risk	sectors.					While	this	legislation	must	cover	all	companies	–	including	SME’s	and	financial	institutions	–	operating	 on	 the	 EU	 market,	 due	 consideration	must	be	 given	 to	proportionality	in	 obligations	based	 on	 the	 size	 and	 impact	 of	 the	 company.	Additionally,	 provisions	 must	 be	 put	 in	 place	 to	 ensure	 that	 costs	 related	 with	compliance	are	distributed	fairly	within	the	supply	chain	and	are	not	passed	on	to	the	primary	producers,	workers	or	artisans,	who	often	have	little	bargaining	power	 against	 big	 retailers,	 buyers	 and	 processors.	On	 this	 point,	 we	urge	 the	Commission	 to	 carefully	 consider	 the	 costs	 for	primary	producers	(e.g.	 small-scale	 farmers)	to	 adopt	 social	 and	 environmentally	 sustainable	 production	practices,	 and	 how	 they	 can	 be	 aided	 to	 invest	 in	 such	 practices,	 in	 the	 impact	assessment.	 The	 externality	 costs	 of	 weak	 or	 no	 legislation,	 in	 terms	 of	 social,	environmental	and	human	costs,	must	similarly	receive	considerable	attention.					For	 HREDD	 legislation	 to	 be	 effective,	 particular	 attention	 must	 be	 paid	 to	protecting	the	rights	vulnerable	groups	of	workers,	producers	and	artisans,	e.g.	children,	youth,	migrant	and	seasonal	workers,	and	especially	women	and	girls.	It	 is	 key	 to	 acknowledge	 and	 address	 the	 unequal	 power	 relations	 in	 supply	chains	 as	 a	 root	 cause	 of	 environmental	 abuse	 and	 human	 rights	 violations	 as	buyers	 unilaterally	 impose	 asymmetric	 terms	 and	 conditions	 on	 producers,	suppliers	 and	 their	 workers	 with	 lack	 of	 economic	 alternatives	 (for	 reference:

Thus,	 companies	 must	be	required	 to	re-evaluate	their	 purchasing	practices,	 in	 particular	 pricing,	 and	living	 wages	 and	 living	 incomes	 (in	 the	 case	 of	farmers)	 must	 be	 recognised	 as	prerequisites	for	the	fulfilment	of	basic	human	rights.				Further,	 it	 is	 vital	 that	 all	 relevant	 stakeholders	 are	 involved	 at	 every	 step	 of	HREDD	 processes,	 like	 civil	 society,	 trade	 unions	 and	 human	 rights	organisations.				In	order	to	create	a	new	economy	that	is	good	for	people	and	planet,	we	deeply	encourage	companies	to	take	the	path	of	social	and	environmental	sustainability	through	mission-led	 business	 models,	 like	 those	 found	within	 the	membership	 of	 the	 World	 Fair	 Trade	 Organization	 (WFTO).	We	 urge	the	Commission	 to	 support	 such	corporate	governance	 models	with	 enabling	policies.	Such	models	ensure	that	the	internal	structures	of	the	whole	company	is	based	on	social	and	environmental	principles	so	the	interests	of	all	stakeholders	–	and	the	environment	–	are	considered	and	respected.	This	further	ensures	that	the	 core	 business	 of	 such	 enterprises	 contributes	 to	 social	 and	 environmental	sustainability,	rather	than	employing	a	CSR	approach	to	make	up	for	the	adverse	social	and	environmental	effects	of	the	core	business	of	many	profit-maximising	companies.	This	 in	 turn	 contributes	 to	more	 resilient	 business	 and	 economy	(please	 refer	 to:	https://wfto.com/jointhebusinessrevolution/).	 If	 the	governance	structure	 remains	focused	 on	profit-maximisation	instead	 of	 social	values,	human	rights	violations	will	continue	to	occur.					We	 invite	 the	 Commission	 and	 other	 interested	 parties	 to	 look	 at	 concrete	examples,	 from	 within	 the	 WFTO-Europe	 membership,	 of	 enterprises	contributing	 actively	 to	 the	 SDG’s	through	 their	 core	 business,	 which	 is	 made	possible	by	deeply	imbedding	sustainability	practices	in	their	governance	model.	These	 serve	 as	 good	 examples	 of	 what	 sustainable	 corporate	 governance	 looks	like	 in	 practice	 (please	 refer	 to:	https://wfto-europe.org/wfto-europe-review-2019-fair-trade-as-a-step-towards-the-sustainable-development-goals/	as	 well	as	to:	https://wfto-europe.org/members-review-2020/)				We	 support	 the	 EU	 initiative	 and	 the	 EU	 Green	 Deal	 on	 helping	 these	companies	 transiting	 step	 by	 step	 into	 a	 more	 sustainable	 model,	 in	a	 constant	improvement	 that	 will	 only	 be	 reached	 in	 a	 medium	 to	 long	 term.	 We	acknowledge	 that	 there	 are	 social,	 economic	 and	 environmental	 benefits	 in	mission-led	business	models,	and	that,	in	spite	of	initial	extra	costs	of	transition,	these	models	are	viable	and	profitable.	We	invite	the	Commission	and	interested	ethical	 business	 to	 make	 use	 of	 our	 recent	 toolkit,	 which	 gives	 concrete	recommendations	 for	 how	 enterprises	 can	 combine	 Fair	 Trade	 Principles	 with	Circular	 Economy	 practices	 (please	 refer	 to:	https://wfto-europe.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/WFTO-Europe-Circular-Economy-Toolkit-2020.pdf).

 
 
CFA Institute feedback on sustainable corporate governance 
 
CFA Institute welcomes the opportunity to provide comments on the upcoming European 
Commission initiative on sustainable corporate governance. We are in favour of legislative 
measures encouraging a better alignment of long -term interests among companies, i nvestors 
and other stakeholders, including society’s needs. CFA Institute argues that it is important that 
organisations increasingly adopt long -term strategies, taking into account all interests, risks, 
opportunities and adverse impacts in the decision-making process. 
However, current market pressures are still incentivizing companies and their management to 
take a short -term approach  (CFA Institute recently published a report assessing progress 
addressing short-termism) in their decisions and business strategies, with a focus set on realising 
short term capital gains. It is therefore difficult to impose on companies to have a long -term 
horizon in their business strategies and decision s without giving them anything in return. Firms 
that are willing to make a move towards long -term and more sustainable policies need to be 
motivated from EU governments (for example, in the form of tax incentives). We understand that 
fiscal and taxation policies fall within the remit of national member states ; nevertheless, fiscal 
incentives would be the best way to encourage organisations to shift to more sustainable and 
inclusive strategies. We therefore call for a  coordinated policy action from the EU and member 
states. In addition, country -by-country reporting also should be a key priority for the EU as it 
would help enhance corporate transparency and provide investors with better information for 
their analysis, including sustainability metrics. 
CFA Institute also underlines the importance of shareholder engagement with companies. 
Greater dialogue among management, the board of directors, and investors is a key step to make 
interests and needs aligned in the final corporate decisions. Through enhanced communications 
and more robust relationships with shareholders, company management can better understand 
shareholders’ interests and preferences, and integrate them in the strategy and decision-making 
framework of the organisation. Better communications with stakeholders also would be 
necessary to find convergent interests among company, its shareholders and the wider society. 
We believe s takeholder interests can be satisfied without diluting investors’ rights and needs. 
This approach also would help directors establish a longer -term time horizon in their corporate 
decisions, taking into account all parties’ preferences.  
These practices are  already developed in som e EU markets,  but are still lacking in others. We 
suggest that the European Commission better encourage organisations to have greater dialogue 
with shareholders and stakeholders, and make this process more transparent.

However, we would like to remark  that both companies and investors should play a bigger role  
themselves in building more  shareholder engagement. Companies should understand and 
include investors’ preferences in their decision-making process in order to develop a long-lasting 
relationship and mutual trust, while investors need to interact more with companies so that the 
latter can operate in a more sustainable manner, taking into account investors’ needs and 
interests.  
We also believe that progress towards a more sustainable corporate governa nce could be 
encouraged by the European Commission with the introduction of a monitoring exercise with the 
purpose of gauging the state of governance frameworks developed in the EU member states. The 
Commission also should ensure that member states develop  their own mechanism, with clear  
and appropriate  indicators, with the aim to push companies and investors to comply with 
governance guidelines or, if they do not, to provide a valid and clear explanation. 
CFA Institute would be happy  to further discuss with the European Commission how to 
encourage the proper integration of sustainability interests into corporate governance 
frameworks and foster European companies to take a long -term approach in their investment 
decisions.

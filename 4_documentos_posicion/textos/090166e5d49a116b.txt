Creating new momentum for ELTIFs 
 
 
Possible lines of approach for a successful revision of the ELTIF regime 
 
Introduction 
 
ELTIFs are investment vehicles geared towards long-term financing of the European economy and 
offer the advantage of being able to invest, in particular, in the capital of unlisted companies (including 
SMEs), in private debt or in real assets requiring significant initial investment, such as infrastructure.  
 
However, the ELTIF regime has not developed as expected, partly because of its overly restrictive 
regime and difficulties in marketing ELTIF-labelled funds to retail investors. 
 
Making the characteristics of ELTIFs more compatible with the needs of retail investors would be a 
powerful lever to direct more individual savings towards a fund with a large pool of (less liquid) long-
term assets
1, in a context where retail investors' demand for long-term investments is increasing. 
Retirement savings plans, in particular, are set to continue to grow in the coming years.  
 
By constituting an attractive savings placement solution in addition to UCITS, the new ELTIF will 
contribute more broadly to long-term financing and the recovery of the European economy. To achieve 
this objective, the new ELTIF, like UCITS, will have to offer a flexible generalist structure (with assets 
that can be more diversified if the manager deems it necessary, and with adequate liquidity, even 
though redemption possibilities will be less frequent than for UCITS). On the other hand, it may offer a 
higher expected return than UCITS, and a lower volatility due to its long-term investment horizon. 
 
In order to achieve these objectives while remaining attractive to institutional investors (who do not 
necessarily have the same needs in terms of diversification or liquidity, for example), the ELTIF must 
be sufficiently flexible to allow the manager to set up a structure adapted to the needs of its investors 
and to its underlying assets. In concrete terms, depending on the type of investors targeted, the 
manager may adapt the following aspects in particular: 
 
- general structure of the fund: closed-end fund (as in the current regime) or open-end fund (with 
regular subscription/redemption possibilities during the life of the fund, but less frequent than for 
UCITS); 
- structure of the fund's assets: greater or lesser proportion of long-term assets, greater or lesser 
diversification, etc.; 
- liquidity risk management tools: setting up side pockets or redemption gates, for example.

In concrete terms, for example, if the management company is targeting institutional investors, it may 
choose to remain on a closed-ended fund structure and invest heavily in less liquid long-term assets. 
On the other hand, if it is targeting retail investors, it may offer an open-ended fund, with regular 
opportunities for subscriptions and redemptions over the life of the fund. On the other hand, the fund 
may hold a smaller proportion of long-term assets than the ELTIFs dedicated to institutional investors. 
However, even for funds accessible to retail investors, the ELTIF must hold more than 50% of long-
term assets
2 (less liquid). Given this significant proportion of long-term assets, and the high number of 
potential investors, the objectives of financing the economy in the long term can be achieved. 
 
This objective of flexibility guided the reflections of AFG’s members, which led to the recommendations 
presented below. 
 
Finally, these amendments to the ELTIF regulation will also provide an opportunity to clarify the rules 
for managing conflicts of interest. 
 
 
 
Recommendations for amendments to the ELTIF regulation 
 
 
Liquidity structure of the fund 
 
As the regulation currently stands, since the ELTIF is a closed-end fund or a fund with limited 
possibilities of redemptions, its investors may not request the redemption of their units or shares before 
the end of the life of the fund other than by way of an exemption.  
 
The AFG recommends providing for the possibility for the fund to offer regular subscription and 
redemptions windows at a frequency appropriate to the fund's assets (for example, weekly, bi-monthly 
or monthly). 
 
Such a scheme would make it possible to increase the size of the fund (and thus the possibilities for 
diversification) as new subscriptions are made, and would meet the need of distribution networks to 
have products that are permanently open for collection, without having to manage a specific 
subscription period. 
 
In addition, the redemption possibilities would meet the aspirations of retail investors, who might be 
reluctant to lock in their investments for a long period of time with no possibility of redemption. 
 
Restrictions linked to the ELTIF asset base 
 
 
The AFG recommends that the following changes be made, in particular in order to make the ELTIF 
as adapted as possible to the expectations of retail investors and their distribution networks:

 changes in fund assets: 
 
 
In accordance with article 10 of ELTIF regulation3, eligible investment assets are : 
i. equity or quasi-equity instruments or debt instruments issued by a qualifying portfolio 
undertaking (which is not a financial undertaking, and not admitted to trading on a regulated 
market or a multilateral trading facility, or is admitted to trading and has a market cap lower 
than €500m) 
ii. loans granted by the ELTIF to a qualifying portfolio undertaking with a maturity no longer 
than the life of the ELTIF 
iii. units or shares of one or several other ELTIFs, EuVECAs and EuSEFs 
iv.      direct holdings or indirect holdings wia qualifying portfolio undertakings of individual real 
assets with a value of at least €10m. 
 
It would be useful to proceed to the following modifications, notably in order to broaden the scope of 
eligible assets and to modify eligibility conditions: 
 
o it would be desirable to allow the ELTIF to invest in funds other than ELTIFs, EuVECAs 
and EuSEFs. Via a fund of funds strategy, the ELTIF may thus offer the subscriber 
exposure to a wide variety of assets: unlisted companies, real estate, infrastructure, etc. 
(to do this, the fund of funds strategy appears relevant because in practice, the expertise 
of the various asset classes is often implemented by different teams).  
 
In addition, any investment in an eligible asset within the meaning of the ELTIF 
regulation should be able to be made indirectly : 
- via a fund

 in the case of a fund open to subscriptions and redemptions, it will be difficult to

 in the case of a closed-ended fund, this ratio will be difficult to comply with not

It therefore seems necessary to provide more flexibility regarding this rule, or even to

 changes aimed at ensuring the fund's liquidity: 
 
o under the current regime, the ELTIF must invest at least 70% of its capital in eligible 
investment assets defined by article 10 of  ELTIF Regulation. This excessively high 
threshold does not make it possible to attract investors interested in funds that are more 
diversified and offer more liquidity for both subscriptions and redemptions. Moreover, 
the example of national funds invested in real assets and intended for retail investors 
shows lower proportions of long-term assets versus liquid assets.  We therefore 
recommend lowering this minimum threshold to 50%, with the manager naturally being 
allowed to decide to go beyond this, depending on the structure of the fund's liabilities 
and the liquidity risk management tools in place.  
 
This lower threshold will make it possible to broaden the base of fund types that can 
qualify as an ELTIF. For example, OPCI (French real estate collective investment 
undertakings) must reach a quota of 51% of non-listed real estate assets. As the ELTIF 
regulation currently stands, the minimum rate of 70% of eligible assets makes many 
OPCI ineligible for the ELTIF label. Similarly, FCPR (French venture capital mutual 
investment funds) are subject to a minimum investment quota of 50%. 
 
o Provide for the possibility for the manager to implement the most appropriate liquidity 
risk management tools, both on the asset and liability sides: 
 on the assets side: it will be useful for the regulation not to set a maximum “one 
size fits all” percentage of illiquid assets or “eligible assets” on the assets side 
of the fund. However, the manager, depending in particular on the results of the 
stress tests that it will carry out when setting up the fund or during the life of the 
fund, may define in a transparent manner a maximum threshold applicable to 
the ELTIF that it manages. 
 on the liabilities side: depending, in particular, on the various criteria already 
mentioned (percentage of illiquid assets on the assets side, frequency of 
subscription/redemption opportunities, etc.), the manager may, if necessary, 
provide for liquidity risk management tools: redemption gates, swing pricing or 
anti dilution levies (ADL), side pockets, mandatory and incentive redemption 
notices, settlement periods, selling of fund units on a secondary market / 
Multilateral Trading Facility, lock-up period, redemptions in kind, suspension, 
etc.

Restrictions in terms of distribution 
 
Regarding the distribution of ELTIF to retail investors, Article 30 of ELTIF Regulation states, in 
particular, that if the portfolio of financial instruments of a potential retail investor does not exceed 
€500,000, then the manager or distributor shall ensure that: 
- the retail investor does not invest more than 10% of their portfolio of financial instruments in ELTIF; 
- and that the minimum initial amount invested in one or more ELTIF is €10,000. 
 
These rules limit the possibilities of marketing ELTIF to retail investors, who often subscribe through 
scheduled payments rather than through a single subscription. As a result, the unit subscription amount 
has limited relevance. These limits are also particularly complex to monitor from an operational point 
of view, as the targeted investors are often multi-banked. As a result, advisers often prefer to offer 
national, non-ELTIF-labelled products that are not subject to these limits.  
 
Moreover, Article 30 also stipulates that retail investors may cancel their subscription at least two 
weeks after the date of subscription of ELTIF units or shares. In practice, however, the distribution 
channels generally do not provide for the cancellation of a subscription after its completion.  
 
These various rules are therefore complex to follow and penalising. Moreover, they lose their relevance 
if the ELTIF changes to a regime that is open to redemptions and subscriptions, which we believe is 
necessary to ensure its success.  
 
The AFG therefore recommends that these rules be removed (it being understood that for retail 
investors, the ELTIF will continue to be marketed within a framework requiring investment advice). 
Indeed, the manager of the ELTIF or any distributor must carry out the suitability test and provide 
appropriate investment advice. The manager of the ELTIF or the distributor must be authorised to 
provide the services referred to in Article 6(4)(a) and (b)(i) of Directive 2011/61/EU(14), including 
investment advice. In addition, the fund documentation must legibly state, alongside the recommended 
investment period, that investors are advised to invest in an ELTIF only a part of their overall investment 
portfolio. 
 
 
Clarification of the rules relating to the management of conflicts of interest 
 
In addition to the changes recommended above, the revision of the ELTIF regime will provide an 
opportunity to clarify the wording of the provisions relating to the management of conflicts of interest.  
 
Article 12 of ELTIF Regulation on conflicts of interest stipulates that “An ELTIF shall not invest in an 
eligible investment asset in which the manager of the ELTIF has or takes a direct or indirect interest, 
other than by holding units or shares of the ELTIFs, EuSEFs or EuVECAs that it manages”. It would 
be useful to clarify what types of conflicts of interest are referred to here.

Indeed, it should be clear in the Article, that the following schemes are not covered. These schemes, 
which are very common in the asset management industry, do not constitute situations of conflict of 
interest: 
- case of “seed-money” or “initial capital” contributed to the ELTIF by the manager, in order to 
facilitate the development of the fund. In this case, the manager's interest is perfectly aligned with 
that of the investors of the ELTIF. 
- case of co-investment in the same asset between the ELTIF and another fund managed by the 
same management company (fund that might have received “seed money” or “initial capital” by the 
management company). If both funds make the same type of investment (for example, both funds 
invest in capital, or both invest in debt, then the interest of the unitholders of both funds is well 
aligned). This possibility of co-investing would facilitate investment in large assets.

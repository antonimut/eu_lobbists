B. Challenges arising from digitalisation and other issues 
Additional information in support of ABIR consultation responses 
 
(Q42) Should the European legislation introduce enhanced requirements for insurers to monitor and 
manage information and communication technology (ICT) risks, including cyber -risks as part of their 
risk management practices (‘Pillar 2’)?   
We do not believe that enhancing the requirements for insurers in relation to ICT risks under Pillar 2 would 
be beneficial. It is our view that ICT risks are already sufficiently covered under Pillar 2 of Solvency II, under 
operational risk. We would ask t he EC to take a more principle -based approach, and to avoid an overly 
prescriptive approach. Existing security and governance requirements, such as those laid out in Solvency 
II, should be clarified whilst allowing firms the flexibility to adapt to changin g cyber risks. From an 
international perspective, we would stress the importance of consistent definitions (an agreed 
‘taxonomy’) to ensure clarity for insurers while writing policies. 
 
(Q43) Should the European legislation consider that cyber-insurance is a distinct class of insurance, 
which would need to be subject to its own authorization process by public authorities? 
 
The cyber insurance market is continuing to evolve, and new risks are emerging all the time. It is difficult 
to standardise cyber-insurance cover given the pace at which cyber-risk changes, the needs of 
consumers and the varying levels of cover that are purchased. We are of the view that cyber-insurance 
should not be a distinct class of insurance that would need to be subject to its own authorisation 
process.

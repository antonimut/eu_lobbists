ADDITIONAL COMMENTS TO THE QUESTIONNAIRE 
QUESTION 8 
Solvency II, together with the prolonged low -interest rate environment, are the main drivers that 
explain the shift of investment risks to policyholders in many European  countries. But this shift 
started in many countries even before the arrival of the low interest rate environment, because of 
the influence of Solvency II.  
In Spain, this shift is much lower (90% of products are guaranteed products) because the matching 
adjustment (MA) has enabled insurance undertakings to continue to offer guaranteed insurance 
products, such as annuities, which are highly valued by consumers and are a valuable product 
offering given the demographic trends in Europe. EIOPA, in its LTG Report 2018, found that 94% of 
firms surveyed indicated that they use the MA in the design and pricing of their products, allowing 
them to offer more competitive prices  
Notwithstanding the above, and even the usefulness of the MA to keep the offer of products  with 
long-term guarantees, the very low -interest rate environment has adversely affected the ability of 
insurers to provide this kind of valuable -as the Covid -19 crisis has demonstrated - products for 
consumers. 
QUESTION 37 
Even though UNESPA supports the idea that climate change should be properly managed, we 
believe that any regulation needs to be sufficiently flexible to allow insurers to embed risks within 
their specific organisations and risk areas.  
In this context the Solvency II framework, as it is, is enough to the integration and measurement of 
climate change. So, when it comes to considering climate change, insurers are already required by 
Solvency II to use up-to-date data in the valuation of liabilities.  
In our opinion, this assessment is not of high importance, for both Life and for non-life insurance and 
specific rules on climate change trends in data in the valuation of liabilities are not necessary . 
QUESTION 38 
In the same case as for the valuation of liabilities, climate change should be properly managed, but 
we believe that any regulation needs to be sufficiently flexible to allow insurers to embed risks within 
their specific organisations and risk areas.  
Insurers´ internal model design and calibrations, generally take into account the climate -related 
evolutions via their effect on other variables included in the internal models, and, in our opinion, 
specific rules on of the data the used in an internal model corresponding to climate change trends 
are not necessary.

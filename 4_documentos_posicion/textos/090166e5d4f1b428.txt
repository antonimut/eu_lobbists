 
 
 
Consultation on the Review of Solvency 
II 
 
About NFU 
NFU – Nordic Financial Unions is an organization that promotes the interests of the Nordic 
financial trade unions in Europe. Through a high level of competence and dialogue, NFU 
contributes to shaping a sustainable financial sector, fundamental for job creation. Currently, 
NFU represents seven trade unions in the bank, finance and insurance sector in Denmark, 
Finland, Iceland, Norway and Sweden.  
For more information, please visit www.nordicfinancialunions.org    
 
Additional information 
1. It is imperative that the insurance sector in the EU/EEA continues to function well, having 
in mind the several roles that the insurance sector plays  for our societies – of 
protector/professional risk taker; investor; and employer. The insurance landscape in Europe 
is varied and consists of insurers that operate from large scale to very niche environments. 
Therefore, a leveled playing field for insuran ce companies in a regulatory sense should be 
kept/advanced. 
2. As an organization representing the views of finance sector employees, including 
employees in the insurance sector, we find that the employee perspective  is largely 
missing in this consultation  document. Consumers interests and general competition rules 
are only one part of the complex environment of the insurance sector. The connection 
between consumers and the insurance sector employees is central and essential for ensuring 
personalized advice, quality service, sound consumer protection and sustainable insurance 
sector. Given that employees are in daily contact with especially retail consumers , they 
represent a  resource that is often neglected when developing new regulation aimed at 
protecting the retail customer. The objective of guarding the interests of policyholders can

only be achieved by involving and establishing dialogue with employees in risk management, 
regulatory and governance processes.    
3. While NFU is in support of measures that contribute to stability in the sector, we find it 
essential that the impact of these measures is also examined from a wider sectoral 
and social context, including its eff ects on employment and working conditions  
in the insurance sector. On a more general note, attention needs to be given that the cost 
of measures and policies that are supposed to strengthen the sector are not leveled with 
reduced personnel, thus affecting customer service and the capacity for regulatory 
compliance in general.  
On a more specific note, the impact assessment to this consultation identifies the potential 
social impacts with impacts to policyholders only. No reference has been made to any costs, 
time or other resources needed for , for example,  appropriate training and competence 
development of employees, nor potential impact  and changes to the working conditions as 
a result of the implementation of different policy options.  
We therefore encourage the Commission, in the scope of the further work with Solvency II 
and in general, to include impact assessments on insurance sector employees when 
assessing different policy options and their implementation. 
4. The insurance sector plays an important role of a n investor, with a significant amount of 
managed assets under control . These investment powers can be used for advancing the 
sustainable finance agenda , while also ensuring that the appropriate levels of risk and 
financial stability are not jeopardized. Th e review of Solvency II has the possibility to 
facilitate the insurance sectors´ role in delivering on the sustainable finance 
agenda, the European Green Deal and Capital Markets Union . Given that Solvency 
II is an evidence-based framework, bigger consideration of data to support the correct risk 
assessment of both (environmentally) sustainable and unsustainable economic activities is 
needed. In connection to that, we encourage the use of scenario analysis and other forward-
looking assessments, given the nature of sustainable finance activities. 
5. The insurance sector also holds an important role of an employer . With a changing 
landscape and business models, it is important to maintain the notion of employees as assets 
in securing long -term consumer relations, and as catalysts for advancing the sustainable 
finance agenda. 
 
 
 
Sustainable finance as such is much more than offering sustainable products or making 
decision based on ESG considerations – it is also about sustainable governance practices and 
internal policies that invest in people. In recent years, in the Nordic countries there has been 
a tendency of outsourcing, especially IT -related function s, to other countries  where, 
regardless if it is intra -group or external outsource, the working conditions could be such 
that they leave the employees worse off than their Nordic colleagues. Over the long term, 
the reasoning behind such decisions, and the consequences to socie ty at large are also 
questions of sustainability.  
In the context of this question addressed in the consultation – it is essential to differentiate 
between the outsourcing, and to i nvolve employees and/or their representatives in the 
carryout of the outsou rcing process while ensuring social dialogue/collective bargaining is 
possible.  
6. Consumer/policyholder protection  remains a priority not only for the Solvency II 
framework, but also for the daily work of employees in the insurance sector, whose focus is 
to ensure sound and personalized advice, particularly to retail consumers. The COVID -19 
pandemic had led to an increased number of client meetings being held digitally, as well as 
intensive hours of consumer support to navigate the specific circumstances of clients.  
In these occasions, it has been noted that consumers have not been fully certain of the 
different characteristics of the products, including guarantees and risk properties. While 
understandably, the specific nature of the situation  invoked by COVID 19  could call for 
reassurance, it is important, over the long term to consider this effect on consumer 
protection, particularly when it comes to pension savings.   
7. Ultimately, Solvency II has also brought about a significant amout of regulation. The 
purpose of regulation, in line with risk-based approach and stability purpose is of course not 
questioned, but the amount of regulation and its impact on administrative burden, 
compliance costs , possible conficts of interest and customer s ervice is. Particularly now, 
when an increased focus is being placed on sustainable finance and a new set of regulations, 
we encourage for more policy coherence  and streamlining of the different reporting 
requirements, as also mentioned in the consultation reply.

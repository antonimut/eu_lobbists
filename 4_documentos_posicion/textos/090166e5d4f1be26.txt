The current crisis has confirmed that Solvency II is a very robust framework.  
For AXA this review is the opportunity to adjust some of the elements in the framework which have 
gradually impaired insurers’ capacity to fully play their role of long-term investors.  
The unprecedented low rate environment, the ambitious goals set to move towards a more sustainable 
economy and the pressing need to provide long term protection to policyholders form the context in 
which this review must be thought. 
In that regard, we see that some of the proposals tested by EIOPA in their Holistic Impact Assessment 
end up being  counterproductive for the p eriod that opens, whereas t his review needs to be 
contextualized providing the many challenges ahead of us.  
We believe that based on EIOPA’s proposal tested twice in 2020, the overall package does not produce 
a balanced outcome for the industry and will lead to an overall increase of solvency capital required 
and/or a decrease of eligible own funds. 
Also, some of the proposed measures lead to introducing some more volatility in the framework , 
especially those measure relating to the design of the risk-free rate yield curve as well as the proposed 
changes to the fundamental spread calculation used in the volatility adjustment. 
While we trust that a sound risk based and economic approach is the right path to maintain ing the 
high level of protection Solvency II is offering, we believe that a dose of pragmatism needs to be used 
for this review. 
Furthermore, we believe that the right solution is not the building up of capital buffer to mimic the 
banking framework, which has a different business model  and different conc eptual p rudential 
framework. The solution should be the removal of procyclical aspects in th e current framework and 
notably through the needed improvements on Risk Margin and Volatility Adjustment. 
Solvency II is already considered as one of the most (if not the most) conservative framework in the 
world. Maintaining in the framework oversized layer of prudence such as the risk margin or introducing 
additional ones such as the building up of additional capital buffer while not providing the right tool to 
cope with the excess of market volatility by improving the VA can only lead to adverse development. 
First it would trigger an even more pronounced structural disengagement from certain asset  class (e.g. 
equity se curities) and trigger conjunctural mechanism such as  the simultaneous disposal of some 
assets at the worst time of the crisis to manage solvency level. 
Second, this will also inevitably lead to drying up the supply of long-term products to policyholders and 
will further limit the capacity of insurers to contribute to the financing of the transition and the green 
agenda in general.

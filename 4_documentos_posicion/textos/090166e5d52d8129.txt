Business and Science Poland would like to raise the attention to the below key aspects that should be 
considered in the context of sustainable aviation fuels in the following areas: 
 
I. Stability of regulatory framework 
It should be emphasized that ensuring stability of the regulatory f ramework in a sufficiently long -term 
horizon is extremely important in order to provide SAF manufacturers with the necessary investment 
certainty, and for the airlines to conduct a long-term and effective policy in the use of this type of fuel. 
A permanent regulatory framework may therefore help to reduce the investment risk in this area.  
 
II. Commercial maturity of technology 
We understand that a technology is commercially mature when it has sufficient volumes for widespread 
use on the aviation market due to its availability, economic profitability and an appropriate quality of a 
low-emission end product. Reliability of delivery for the fuel is an important question. 
 
III. Economic and environmental profitability 
The price difference between SAF and fossil jet fuel remains a major market obstacle for guaranteeing 
profitability. It should be taken into account that the availability and, consequently, the degree of use 
of raw materials for SAF production is different across geographic regions, which may feed into the final 
environmental effect of SAF. It is necessary to take into account the costs and risk of generating CO2 
emissions, which are related to the import of raw materials for the production of SAF, unavailable in a 
given region. In this context, it is essential that the range of SAF raw materials is as wide as possible. In 
this sense, the need for raw materials for the production of sustainable alternative fuels in all modes of 
transport also needs to be taken into account. 
Apart from the above, an import ant element is the insufficient price competition possibly stimulating 
demand. Consequently, a lack of demand causes a lack of supply. 
 
IV. Multiplier as an incentive to use SAF  
We consider that appropriate changes to the RED II Directive may contribute to increasing the role of 
alternative fuels used in transport. In this regard, we propose considering increasing multiplier for 
aviation. In our opinion, it would be an effective t ool to stimulate demand for alternative fuels in 
transport.

where it is possible to supply fuels in non-EU airports. In the event of significant price differences, being

Therefore, we are of the opinion that aviation fuels should keep exemption from taxation.  
Furthermore, any environmental levy or tax imposed on aviation would primarily affect consumers, as 
its cost would be passed on to passengers. Apart the increase in  ticket prices, it would reduce the 
number of travelers and significantly affect mobility, as well as social expansion. Finally, it would 
inevitably harmfully impact the tourism sector.

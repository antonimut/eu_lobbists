  
 
October 27, 2020 
 
Subject: Airbus response to the EC consultation related to ReFuelEU Aviation. 
 
Together with improved air traffic management and technological advances such as hydrogen 
and electric power  and e arly fleet renewal  the use of sustainable aviation fuels (SAFs) is 
considered as an effective means to reduce CO 2 emissions from aviation in the next decades.  
Airbus believes that the European Green Deal and the ReFuelEU Aviation initiative can play an 
important role in speeding up the development, production and use of drop-in sustainable aviation 
fuels in Europe.  
We believe also that , as Aviation is a global economic activity and air traffic is international by 
nature, there needs to a very close harmonisation with global policy  and potential blending 
mandates (only if supply volumes from the right feedstock / raw materials have reached significant 
levels) to ensure the full climate and enviro nmental improvements can be realized.  Regional 
climate measures should also avoid creating competitive disadvantages and market distortions 
for airlines. 
Sustainability should be at the core of these new types of jet fuel. As aviation is a global industry, 
it is then important for the value chain stakeholders to consider and use widely and commonly 
agreed sustainability standards and criteria to ensure a harmonized approach. 
The aviation industry, including Airbus, has made major progress by contributing to the technical 
certification (ASTM standard) of SAF allowing their use for commercial flights. This important work 
must continue to bring new SAF pathways to the market. It would be relevant for this purposes to 
establish a network of labs and research institutes at EU level in order to support technical 
preparation for the certification process to contribute to process de -risk, lead time and cost 
reduction. 
In all cases, dedicated policy and legal frameworks need to be harmonised with the existing ones 
to ensure that inconsistencies will  not become an obstacle. As an example, further clarification 
and harmonisation of accounting rules when using SAF could be helpful.  
Sustainable Aviation Fuel should also to be recognised by the Platform on Sustainable F inance 
as offering substantial climate mitigation benefits for the Aviation sector. As the Taxonomy will 
drive the upcoming sustainable finance agenda this would be an important element to consider. 
Finally, SAF production needs to be seen in a complete ecosystem. Accordingly, the co-products 
of the production and its valorisation should be considered.

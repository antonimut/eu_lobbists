Danfoss input to the European Commission Consultation on 
Sustainable Products Initiative 
October 2020 
 
 
Danfoss welcomes the European Commission’s new Sustainable Products Initiative and strongly 
encourages a common European approach to this important field.  As a global leader that provides 
energy-efficient  solutions to save energy and costs in a sustainable and resource efficient way, Danfoss 
offers a wide range of products and services used for cooling food, air conditioning, heating buildings, 
controlling electric motors and powering mobile machinery.  
 
A more circular economy is a vital step to increase the resilience of our economy and the sustainable 
use of natural resources. While aiming for more sustainable products, it is important to provide a 
holistic systems overview, as products are always part of an ecosystem and as circular economy effects 
can only be achieved on a systems level. Therefore, we believe that any product regulation should be 
developed within this systems framework to unleash the full potential for sustainable product systems. 
 
The Ecodesign Directive has served as a useful tool for increasing  the energy efficiency for energy 
related products and this could be further developed to support material efficiency within a broad 
range of product groups, while preserving the strengths of the directive. To ensure the right focus and 
to handle any trade-offs between different aspects of energy and material efficiency, it is important to 
build on the “least life cycle cost” principle with  a focus on the me asures that result in the most 
sustainable use of resources. 
 
Any new regulation should provide level playing fields and be enforceable, and it is important to ensure 
both effective market surveillance and the tools for assessing the compliance. European harmonized 
standards are strong tool s to support European legislation and based on the newly developed 
horizontal CEN -CENELEC standards for material efficiency, the appropriate product -group specific 
standards should be developed to provide sector-specific measures. 
 
Therefore, Danfoss calls on the European Commission to: 
• Propose long term regulatory certainty  through a harmo nised approach on requirements, 
methodologies and standards.  
• Ensure a level playing field for businesses, manufacturers and other commercial operators 
supporting responsible organisations’ interest in developing more sustainable business 
models. 
• Build future regulation on analysis on a systems level and based on the least life cycle cost 
principle. 
• Ensure timely delivery of harmonized standards though improved processes and better 
cooperation between the European Commission and the European Standardization 
Organizations.

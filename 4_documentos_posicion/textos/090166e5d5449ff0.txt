EU Sustainable Products Initiative 
 
PlasticsEurope’s view on the Inception Impact Assessment 
October 2020 
 
PlasticsEurope supports the EU’s ambition to accelerate the transition towards a climate-neutral 
and circular economy  in the context of the Paris Agreement, and supports the European 
Commission’s objective to ensure that products placed on the European market fully contribute to 
such a transition.  
 
PlasticsEurope is committed to contributing to this transition with viable, concrete and science -
based solutions. We call on the European Commission to ensure that product-related rules allow 
manufacturers that produce in or import into Europe to create better performing, long-lasting, safe 
and environmentally friendly products.  
 
Objectives and policy options as identified by the European Commission 
 
The role of plastics in contributing to the manufacturing of more sustainable, durable and 
circular products 
 
Plastic is a strong and durable material, highly flexible, light and versatile with excellent 
technological properties in terms of ductility and malleability that allow it to be easily processed 
and transformed at low temperature. The durable nature of plastics allows products to last longer 
and to be used and reused several times, enabling them to be resistant to damages and external 
modification. For example, their anti -corrosion properties provide them with an impressive life 
span, extending to more than 100 years for plastic pipes and 50 years for underground and exterior 
cables. The combination of these properties makes plastics suitable for all types of consumer 
products, ranging from packaging to applications including building and construction, furni ture, 
automotive, electronics, medical equipment, etc.1 
 
Furthermore, plastics throughout their different applications contribute  to the manufacturing of 
more sustainable products by optimising the use of natural resources, minimising the amount of 
waste and reducing energy consumption and GHG emissions 2. For example, in the automotive 
industry, the use of plastics components allows cars to be lighter, leading to a decrease in fuel 
consumption and, as a consequence, reduced CO2 emissions. In the building and construction 
sector, plastic insulation materials across their life cycle consume approximately 16% less energy 
and emit 9% less greenhouse gases than alternative materials3. In addition, the use of plastics in 
packaging prevents food waste  - by protecting food from physical damage in transport and 
handling while extending its shelf -life - and has led to a reduction of the overall volume of

Innovation and cross -industrial collaboration are key to support increasing circularity, 
boosting the markets for recycled content and mainstreaming new recycling technologies 
 
The plastics industry is working on several initiatives to increase the reusability and recyclability 
of plastics and is committed to helping develop high-quality recycling processes for plastics waste 
and to achieve more long-lasting and circular products. Among these initiatives, PlasticsEurope is 
actively involved in the Circular Plastics Alliance  (CPA), which brings together the entire value 
chain to increase the rate of recycled plastics used in products to 10 million tonnes by 2025. 
 
To this end, PlasticsEurope is committed through the CPA to  contributing to several activities 
linked to priority sector groups (e.g. Automotive, Packaging, Construction and EEE) which will  
identify - among others - the enablers and barriers for the uptake of recycled cont ent and to 
determine the economic incentives to further improve separate collection, sorting and recycling. 
These kind of initiatives are crucial to increase the sustainability of the plastics industry and of all 
the sectors involved while ensuring their competitiveness. The outcomes of the actions undertaken 
within the CPA specifically addressing the uptake of recyclates in different products should be the 
ones guiding new European policies and requirements on the use of recycled content. 
 
Improving existing recycling technologies while unleashing the potential of new ones is also 
essential to boost the uptake of recycled content in products and to improve recycling rates. 
PlasticsEurope members are investing and working to harness the full potential of innovation. 
Defining clear requirements on the incorporation of recycled content into new products can favour 
considerably the uptake of secondary raw materials and the circular economy transition. Th ese 
requirements will need to continue to guarantee product and consumer safety as well as achieve 
a net environmental product improvement. 
 
In this regard, chemical recycling will help close the loop of carbon and material cycle s in an 
optimised and energy efficient manner. Chemical recycling can also help solve current or potential 
obstacles in the recycling of products, such as the presence of legacy additives and substances, 
or the presence of contaminants and impurities and produce a virgin -grade recycled material . 
Therefore, it is essential that the Eur opean Commission provide a clear policy and regulatory 
framework to foster investments in such new technologies. 
 
 
Chemicals in plastic products: avoid stigmatization and ensure coherence  with the 
Chemicals Strategy for Sustainability 
 
PlasticsEurope understands the importance of addressing the presence of hazardous chemicals 
in products, underlined in the European Commission’s Inception Impact Assessment. In parallel, 
the European Commission has announced initiatives on the “Safe and Sustainable by Design” 
issue in the context of its Chemicals Strategy for Sustainability, including the development of 
specific criteria addressing chemicals. We believe it is important to avoid substance stigmatization, 
as chemicals have an important role to play to ensure climate change ambitions are met. This is why

Consumer safety and overall positive environmental impacts must guide the adoption of 
Eco-design criteria and sustainability principles. 
 
The design phase is crucial to improve products’ sustainability and we acknowledge the need to 
extend the scope of the Eco-design Directive to cover wider environmental considerations such 
as durability, repairability and new business models.  We consider that a harmoni sed and 
broadened eco-design framework would be an important contribution to a level -playing field for 
the assessment of different products and material applications. To this end, we call on the 
European Commission to ensure that the measures being developed under the Sustainable 
Product Policy Initiative maintain coherence with the Eco -design Directive and all existing EU 
policy instruments so as to guarantee legal certainty and predictability. 
 
Eco-design and related sustainability  criteria should be supported by solid, science -based 
methodologies which take in to account the specificity of each product and the related market 
sector (packaging, automotive, building and construction, etc.) as well as products’ functionality.  
In addition, they should ensure that the overall impact of the product on the environment  is 
minimised and that all the products and their applications are in compliance with consumers’ health 
and safety requirements5.  
 
Furthermore, the establishment of overarching product sustainability principles should be guided 
by a life-cycle assessment, with a view to promoting the best material and/or product combination, 
balancing several aspects including: reduction of waste, efficient use of resources and carbon and 
environmental footprint of a material/product. The assessment should also take into consideration 
the benefits from a product in its use phase, including  health and social impacts of these 
sustainability measures.  
 
 
Green Public Procurement and economic incentives 
 
To effectively achieve the objective to ensure that products placed on the market are sustainable, 
circular and resource efficient, rules on environmental  / sustainability performance of products 
should be accompanied by economic incentives to favour those with the best sustainability 
performance, based on sound and full life-cycle science.  
 
This would increase consistency between the supply and the demand side along environmental 
goals. With the same aim, public procurement requirements should fit environmental policies. 
More harmonized EU rules for minimum requirements in GPP,  based on a sector -by-sector 
assessment, could be developed. Public authorities should be exemplary and favo ur those 
products which meet the best performance level in terms of sustainability, when considered in a 
holistic manner which takes into account sustainable use of resources,  products’ safety 
requirements, as well as carbon, environmental and social impact.

Digitalisation of product information / mandatory sustainability labelling 
 
Communication requirements for products covered by eco-design mea sures, including the 
information to be placed on the related labels should be clearly defined with a view to  providing 
adequate information to consumers in terms of product performance and durability, to improve the 
repair of products, and correct household waste sorting for collection and recycling. 
 
Communication along the value chain  has a different purpose: to  ensure safe and efficient 
recycling operations . In this framework it  is therefore important to  ensure transparency and 
facilitate information sharing along the value chain particularly on topics related to the presence 
of substances of very high concern. To achieve this goal, the potential of digital solutions requires 
careful consideration, in order to ensure adequate protecti on of companies’ Intellectual Property 
Rights (IPR) and preserve Confidential Business Information (CBI).  
 
 
Assessment of expected impacts and evidence base  
 
The Sustainable Products Initiative must preserve the integrity of the Single Market and 
ensure the competitiveness of the European economy. 
 
One clear and consistent framework for products across the EU Single Market remains critical to 
European industrial competitiveness and the industry’s ability to innovate at scale and provide 
solutions to deliver on the EU Green Deal.  
 
PlasticsEurope believes that the future policy developments must preserve the integrity and well-
functioning of the EU Single Market and welcomes the European Commission’s intention to adopt 
the initiative based on Article 114 TFEU. 
 
To this end, product-design requirements should take into account sectoral specificities and any 
additional sustainability measure must be clearly defined and fully harmonised at EU level so as 
to ensure a level playing f ield among all the products placed on the EU market. In addition, the 
European Commission should maintain ownership in the implementation process, including 
monitoring and follow-up processes with Member States. This will enhance legal certainties for 
economic operators and will prevent any fragmentation of the internal market. Any necessary 
guidance or implementation plan must be foreseen and delivered well in advance of the deadline 
for transposition by the Member States. 
 
Furthermore, to safeguard not on ly competitiveness among the Member States but also of the 
whole EU economy vis -a-vis third countries, the European Commission should consider the 
adoption of measures aimed at ensuring that the same sustainability requirements apply to 
products imported from outside the EU. Market surveillance should therefore be reinforced. 
 
 
Need for a thorough impact assessment 
 
Any legislative initiative should also consider the alternatives which may arise due to substitution 
driven by legislation, including the benefits of products to society in instances where alternatives 
are not readily available, and the  possibility of material substitution  arising from legislative 
requirements, and must carefully consider the likely environmental, social and economic impacts 
of any potential substitution. The European Commission should ensure that any sustainability 
policy instrument should be identified on the basis of a holistic evidence-based approach so as to 
prevent misleading and discriminatory information which could lead to the substitution products 
and materials with less circular and less carbon neutral solutions.

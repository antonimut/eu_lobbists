 
 
 
 
Feedback to the Commission’s Inception Impact Assessment on the 
Sustainable Products Initiative 
 
 
Introduction 
Covestro welcomes the Commission ’s Sustainable Products Initiative and its ambitious aim  to 
make products placed on the EU market more sustainable, reducing their environmental footprint 
over the complete life cycle, increasing their lifespan, reducing waste and improving use rates of 
recycled and circular material. 
 
Having carefully analysed the Inception Impact Assessment, we would  like to make the following 
comments for the upcoming Initiative: 
 
 
I – The contribution of the plastics to products’ prolonged use, resource efficiency and 
circular manufacturing 
 
High-tech polymers offer outstanding performance properties that allow to increase products’ 
lifespan, safety, repa rability and reusability, whil st optimising the use of natural resources and 
minimising waste. 
The excellent technological properties of polymers make products strong, durable, light, flexible 
and versatile. They allow the products to be  reused several times, be resistant to damages and 
external alteration and easily adaptable to any shape . This unique combination makes them the 
optimal choice of material for numerous consumer products. 
 
 
II – Role of investment and infrastructure to deliver a sustainable and circular product policy  
 
With the EU recovery plan, Europe has a unique opportunity to overhaul the infrastructure required 
for the full implementation of circular economy. 
Over the past years, environmental policy has been mainly focused on producer responsibility and 
product design. Whilst it has often been successful, this approach does not go far enough to solve 
all issues of circular economy, thus holding it back. 
National and EU recovery plans are a rare opportunity to define the needs of Europe’s circularity 
infrastructure, such as state of the art recycling plants, efficient transport for reverse logistics, etc., 
and ways to finance those projects. 
 
 
III – Sustainability of key value chains  
 
Covestro would like to caution against using a one -size-fits-all approach  with regard  to the 
development of overarching product sustainability principles.  
Instead we believe that durability, reusability, upgradability, reparability, recyclability and resource 
efficiency must be addressed individually for each  value chain. In addition, the criteria must be 
approached holistically, considering the inev itable trade -offs occurring in the effort to balance 
different sustainability goals.

We therefore urge the Commission to develop criteria suited to individual products, ensuring that 
these are supported by solid, science-based methodologies which take into account the specificity 
of each product, its market sector and functionality as well as the CO2 footprint of the final products. 
Each identified “Key Product Value Chain”  should go through a thorough impact assessment  
involving all stakeholders. 
 
 
IV – Revision of Ecodesign 
 
Covestro supports the need to  extend the Ecodesign Directive, as the design phase is crucial to 
improve products’ sustainability. Nevertheless, in order to avoid unnecessary duplication of efforts 
and legal uncertainty, it is important to ensure consistency with existing legislations. 
The Ecodesign framework has been an effective tool, that in our view does not need any radical 
changes. Still, we understand it may require some adaptations. 
We would like to stress that Ecodesign criteria and the hierarchy applying to them must first and 
foremost be based on clearly defined life -cycle assessments, with a view to promot ing the best 
material and/or product combination to reduce waste, preserve goods and resources and cut CO2 
emissions, ensuring at the same time compliance with consumers’ health and safety requirements. 
Favouring one particular criteria to the detriment of others would result in less environmentally 
friendly products on the market. 
 
 
V – Recycled content incorporation 
 
Covestro agrees that increas ed requirements on incorporation of recycled content into new 
products c ould favour the uptake of secondary raw materials and the refore the  transition into 
circularity. Nevertheless, this incorporation should not jeopardize product and consumer safety, nor 
be detrimental to the environmental footprint of the product. 
We believe that there should not be mandatory requirements on minimum recycled content 
incorporation for all products. Instead, the uptake of recycled content for each specific value chain 
should rather depend on the preparedness of the recycling technologies, the availability of waste 
and the consistent implementation of end-of-waste criteria. 
Chemical recycling could be another way to create high quality recycled content, closing the loop 
of carbon and material cycle in an optimized and energy efficient manner. This process could also 
contribute to solving current potential recycling obstacles due to the  presence of hazardous 
substances. 
We believe that the uptake of recycled content could be fostered through targeted incentives like 
eco-modulation fees within extended producer responsibility schemes and other fiscal measures. 
Furthermore, t he development of specific standards could increase confidence in recycled 
materials. 
 
 
VI – Presence of chemicals 
 
Chemical substances have an important role in production and supply chains, and they will continue 
to be key to digitalization, climate change ambitions and other Green Deal goals. Substances that 
do not represent a concrete risk for health and environment, based on scientific assessment, should 
not be phased out.   
Equally, m aterials containing hazardous substances should not be generally excluded from 
recycling. Instead, it must be examined on a case-by-case basis regarding each planned recycling, 
which input materials are foreseen and which process is applied to produce marketable recycl ed 
materials.

VII – Transparency of information for consumers and value chain 
 
Covestro believes that the communication requirements need clearer definition with regard to the 
concerns they address. 
On one hand, we welcome the improved labelling reflecting the achieved sustainability parameters 
as this encourages consumers to buy more sustainable products. The focus of this i nformation 
should be to help consumers make informed choices, whilst avoiding misleading details.  
On the other hand, it is crucial to communicate transparently along the value chain to ensure safe 
and efficient recycling operations. This includes communication on the presence of substances that 
might impact recycling or remanufacturing.  
The potential of digital solutions for tracking and tracing within this framework, such as blockchain 
technology, digital passports, etc. requires further exploration and consideration. 
 
 
VIII - Green Public Procurement (GPP) rules must be ambitious but realistic  
 
In order to achieve the policy objectives, rules should favour products with the best environmental 
and sustainability performance, so that customers can identify and select them. With the same aim, 
public procurement should be oriented towards environmental policies.  
We believe that it would be key to further harmonize EU rules for minimum requirements in GPP, 
based on a sector-by-sector assessment and taking into consideration realistic market conditions 
and developments. 
Public authorities should be leading by example selecting  products that holistically show best 
performance levels in terms of sustainability, resource efficiency and CO2 footprint.

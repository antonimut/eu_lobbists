2nd November 2020, Brussels 
Cepi response to the Sustainable Products Initiative 
The Confederation of the European Paper Industry (Cepi) and its Members welcome the EU  Commission’s 
Sustainable Products initiative, acknowledging the importance of ensuring the placement of  more sustainable 
products on the internal market to support Europe’s transition towards a more circular economy  and achieve the 
goals set under the European Green Deal. 
We support the development of products fit for a climate neutral, resource efficient and circular economy, which will 
also help Europe reduce its  waste generation. Sustainable products start  with sustainable raw materials. In this 
context the European pulp and paper industry has  developed a “recycling environment” which allowed us to reach 
a relevant recycling target while  not being not far from reaching  the physi cal limit. At the same time we also 
invested in the sustainable sourcing of virgin fibres. Furthermore t he European forests and the forest -based sector 
provide integrated solutions to the global climate challenge on a very large scale . A s a matter of fact , the 
contribution of forest-based pro ducts is as important as the forest sink.  Each year forests and forest -based 
products reduce the European Union ’s emissions by 447 Mt  of CO2 today1. By being a viable alternative to many 
fossil- based products and being produced mainly with renewable energy, each year fibre based-products prevent 
the additional release of 410 Mt CO2 emissions. This material substitution is not yet accounted for  in CO2 climate 
frameworks.  
1. Sustainable forest: 
While recycling is a cornerstone of sustainable resource management - by increasing the capacity of  retaining the 
value of materials in a controlled circular loop for as long as possible  - the overall sustainability of prod ucts is also 
determined by the renewable origin of the material and proven sourcing from sustainable sources. For wood-based 
products, this means that they contribute to sustainable forest management . The sourcing of forest biomass is 
framed by the EU and national forest &  nature policies and legislations as well as market based certification 
systems (i.e. FSC and PEFC certifications)  in place. For the large scale energy use of forest biomass the forest 
management is subject to criteria, which de facto are also valid for products  as forests are not managed for only 
one end use.   
As the European Commission is setting a legislative framework that will allow Europe to become the first cl imate 
neutral continent by 2050 it is important to recognize, protect and enhance the crucial role forests and forest -based 
products have as a natural way to reduce emissions . European forest and forest -based industries have been 
striving to find equilibrium between biodiversity preservation, climate protection and economic output . Thanks to 
sustainable forest management, Europe can proudly claim that it currently has more forest resources than it did a 
century ago. Total w ood harvesting in the EU accounts for less than the annual growth rate  of forests , hence 
actively increasing the  carbon storage of our forests and providing habitat s for species important to biodiversity. 
The value chains of the forest and forest-based sector provide nearly 4 million green European jobs, mostly located 
in rural areas. In 2019, 74% of the wood bought  by Cepi Members was certified and the CO 2 emissions reduction 
resulting from their activities decreased by 48% compared to 1990 .2 To close the loop, thanks to its commitment to 
recycling, i n 2019 57.5 million tonnes of paper and board were collected  in Europe and recycled by the paper 
industry, reaching an overall recycling rate of 72%.

2. Circular products and sustainable raw materials: 
We believe that, in order to achieve its goals, Europe must cut its dependence on fossil -based products and 
endorse the uptake of low carbon footprint products made from renewable and recyclable materials such as wood 
fibres and pulp, which are better fit for a truly circular economy. 
Although we recognize the importance of reducing the footprint of primary raw material  consumption, we 
encourage the Commission to differentiate between f ossil-based primary raw materials  and r enewable r aw 
materials. A high percentage of the fibre -based products which are currently placed on the European market are 
made of recycled fibr es. Although the circularity of a product cannot be measured by one metric  and recycling 
alone will not improve our production a nd consumption patterns , we believe that the Commission should 
acknowledge that materials from renewable sources that are able to build an efficient recycling system should be 
prioritized as building blocks of the new circular economy.  Being a sector which already demonstrates to be able to 
build such  a business environment, we also call upon the Commission  to consider product -specific and sector 
specific needs. The paper industry has already developed a system where any fibre collected is recycled and each 
single fibre is allocated to its best use . W e strongly believe that the introduction of an EU -wide mandatory 
requirement for recycled content in fibre -based products would challenge an industry that is already championing 
recycling1, by placing additional financial and administrative burdens on it  and would not deliver on the final goal of 
increasing its circularity.  Paper recycling could be much better supported by further developing a high quality 
collection system and helping the industry in the management of unavoidable recycling residues.  
3. Accounting for the lifecycle of circular and sustainable products: 
The efforts European forest-based industries have put into developing a circular and sustainable business model in 
place should be properly accounted for in many different processes, starting from the Product Environmental 
Footprint methodology. This will ensure environmentally friendly practices become the new norm, that European 
businesses, who champion sustainability, will be properly protected from unfair business practices and that 
European consumers will be given the opportunity to make well informed sustainable choices. Cepi agrees with the 
Commission that currently many produc ts related externalities are not properly accounted for, hindering the 
transition towards more circular patterns of consumption and production. To overcome such problem s and 
effectively support the sustainability commitment of our Members, our organization launched in July a PEF SME 
tool, fully based on the Intermediate Paper Product PEF Category Rules (PEFCR), the most complete of any EU 
PEF Pilot Category Rules, which allows users to calculate their overall environmental performance and back their 
sustainability related claims.  However, we still feel that there are areas that the Commission should be improve in 
the PEF methodology  before mainstreaming its use . In particular:  (1) further development of the land use impact 
methodology that reflects sustainable forest management realistically and the circularity formula; (2) setting of rules 
on how intermediate product footprint results will be linked to the final product calculations ; (3) updating data for 
several product categories; (4) apply the same methodology for EU products and imports.  
4. Supporting products that are safe for consumers and the planet: 
The European Commission, revealed in its Communication «Chemical Strategy for Sustainability 2» the intention to 
minimise the presence  of substances of concern in products by introducing requirements for specific product 
categories, including food packaging, as part of the Sustainable Product s Policy Initiative. Since one of the most 
prominent uses of fibr e-based products is they role as food contact materials (FCM), we also wanted to express 
our views on the topic. 
Recognizing that the abil ity to ensure consumer protection should be the sine qua non before placing any product 
on the internal market, we are looking forward to the development of harmonized requirements to assess the safety 
of all products intended to come in contact with food. When in May the Commission unveiled its “Farm to Fork 
Strategy”3, C epi and its Members  welcomed the ambition to support the use of innovative and sustainable 
packaging and improve food safety and public health . The lack of a harmonised EU  legislation is strongly 
penalising the use of recycled paper products in the internal market. The commitment of our industry to protect the 
health and safety of our consumers, has als o been expressed through the  «Food Contact Guidelines for the 
Compliance of Paper & Board Materials and Articles»4, a set of principles defining good manufacturing practices for

FCM, which our Industry has been updating over the years to keep up with the latest scientific evidence. 
Acknowledging both the importance and the limits of voluntary industry commitments, we would like to recall the 
Commission on the importance of providing industry with a clear  regulatory framework, essential to support 
business decisions and investments, through the appropriate means  and we would use this opportunity to ask the 
Commission to address the safety of food contact materials t hrough the revision of  the food contact materials  
legislation, rather than the Sustainable Products Policy Initiative.  
Cepi welcomes the Commission’s commitment for a more circular Europe and we welcome the policy initiatives 
which will be providing the new regulatory framework to enable this trans ition to happen. However, we need to 
point out that in this transition a “one size fits all” approach might not deliver positive results, and that while 
overarching sustainability principles can be a good starting point for all European Industries, only through more 
sectorial and tailor ed approaches, which  will take into account the needs and characteristics of the different 
industries, European businesses will be able to contribute to the green transition and champion it.

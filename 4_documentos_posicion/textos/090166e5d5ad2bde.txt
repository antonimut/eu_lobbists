- New Euro standards would be appropriate to further reduce air pollutant emissions from cars and

‘Neither agree nor disagree’ is chosen due to the caveat that the appropriateness of any new Euro

12.1 Regulated air pollutants for Euro standards.  
Firstly the effect of the current fleet turnover on air quality should be evaluated; assess how (and if) 
more stringent emissions standards would improve air quality in the next 10 years. In other words, 
the idea is to shift from “zero emissions” to “zero impact emissions”. This moves the focus from 
tailpipe levels to the final goal of how they influence air quality.   
 
Emissions related to climate change such as N2O and CH4 should be measured and their impact should 
be assessed in terms of CO2e before deciding on the appropriateness of regulation.  
 
12.2 Inclusion of regulated air pollutants for Euro standards. 
- Please explain why NH3 should be added to the Euro standards: 
We agree ammonia should be selected since it is a precursor for secondary PM in atmosphere. Given 
that there is not limit for the time being current technologies are reducing other pollutants at the 
expense or significant increase of ammonia 
- Please explain why brake emissions should be added to the Euro standards: 
Increased risk due to electrification of road transport. Brake emissions are relevant part for particulate 
pollution, independent from power-train technology.  
- Please explain why tyre and road-wear emissions should be added to the Euro standards: 
Increased risk due to electrification of road transport. Tyre and road wear emissions are relevant part 
for particulate pollution, independent from power-train technology.

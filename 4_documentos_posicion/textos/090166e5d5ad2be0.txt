VERT 
Recommendations for  
                                                
Post Euro 6/VI Priorities     
                                                 
VERT, the Association for Emission Reduction Technology has started its fight for best available emission 
technology for all combustion engines 1994 with the introduction of highly efficient particle filters for all Diesels in 
Swiss Tunneling NEAT. VERT certification is now worldwide adopted by many governments and organizations.  
The milestones on this way: 
- Introduction of the particle number criterion PN 10-500 nm 1996, adopted by EU 2007 
- DPF health oriented certification protocol with dynamic quality criteria to reflect technology progress 1997 
- Limitation of defined chemicals like NO2, EC-particles etc. instead of undefined groups PM, NOx ,total HC   
- Limitation of all toxic secondary emissions produced by emission technology 1990  following US CAA 202   
- Introduction of benzene free fuel 1998 and metal free lubricants 2010 for two stroke handheld engines     
- Non-road machines must be as clean as on-road vehicles: VERT input to EU NRMM directive 2014  
- PTI by PN measurement at idle to control DPF quality for non-road 2012, on-road 2018 
- Research and technical proposals for emission reduction of handheld motorized tools to EU 2016 
- Proposal of method for high emitter elimination in order to clean the petrol fleets worldwide 2018 
VERT has assisted governments to implement best available technology in Canada, USA, M exico, Columbia, 
Chile, China, Israel, Iran and served as consultant to the European commission , member-states and UN-ECE 
VERT publishes a list of certified emission reduction technologies for worldwide application since 1997 and co-
organizes the ETH-Conference on Combustion Generated Nanoparticles since 1997. 
 
Called in for its expertise with respect to further reduction of combustion emissions and emission quality control, 
VERT has served in hearings for the BRD parliament 2008 and 2016, many times for the EU commission and for 
the European Court of Auditors 2018 on the question of how to further improve emission quality and emission 
quality control methods beyond the level given by Euro 6/VI. 
 
 
Based on 25 years research and application experience VERT proposes the following priorities for after Euro 6-
emission legislation for on-road and non-road combustion engines in order target zero health impact  
 
- No further reduction of NOx emission, but introduce additional limits for NO2, N2O, NH3 
- Reduce the PN-limits by one order of magnitude in the size range 10 nm– 2500 nm (WMA 2014) 
- Include limitation of PN emission from tires and brakes in addition to PN from engine exhaust  
- Establish a general limitation for toxic secondary emissions as part of homologation (US CAA 202) 
- Establish a general limit for carcinogenic particle bound PAH (Trojan horse toxics)  
- Strengthen limits for handheld engines, introduce alkylate fuel and accelerate electrification   
- Implement emission control during PTI starting with PN at idle for Diesel and Petrol engines  
 
To reach the highest possible effect to reduce health effects from traffic emissions the last requirement is by far the 
most important. It also has a very strong effect on the total fleet with little delay and at very limited cost.

Ferrari S.p.A. - ADDITIONAL CONTRIBUTION TO POST-EURO 6/VI  
EC PUBLIC CONSULTATION 
 
Ferrari S.p.A. has decided to provide its own contribution to the online questionnaire 
and to integrate some of the answers with more detailed considerations and policy 
recommendations. 
With reference to question number 11, Ferrari S.p.A.  would like to highlight that 
despite acknowledging the complexity of the existing legislation (i.e. gradual EU RO 
6 phase-in stages), the current regulatory framework somehow guarantees flexibility 
and proportionality which may be beneficial to Small Volume Manufacturers 
(SVMs). Initial simplification hypothesis under discussion could be 
counterproductive, resulting in an additional burden for SVMs (i.e. single mandatory 
introduction date for all new vehicles). 
With reference to question number 13, Ferrari S.p.A.  would l ike to point out that 
existing potential inefficiencies do not necessarily stem from the legislation as such, 
and that stricter regulation may not be the most effective solution to reduce pollutant 
emissions. Other measures that could be even more effective are: 
- Better enforcement from national authorities to prevent tampering; 
- Smarter traffic management s chemes to reduce congestion, pollutant 
emissions and improve air quality in urban areas.

Road transport should not be considered as the only source of environmental 
pollution. A single regulatory initiative would not be effective unless is part of a 
comprehensive set of measures, including positive fiscal and incentive schemes t o 
promote cleaner technologies. 
Moreover, post-EURO 6 legislation should prevent from imposing a specific design 
requirement and should ensure that the technological neutrality is maintained to 
reflect the different vehicle segments. The European Commission should refrain 
from (directly or implicitly) mandating certain technologies for specific vehicle 
segments. The European Commission should focus on measurable targets and the 
Industry should be able to choose how to reach them. 
Last but not least, Ferrari S.p.A.  would like to stress that it is o f paramount 
importance for SVMs that medium and long -term regulatory stability is provided to 
enable sustainable planning of future investments and guarantee a reasonable 
industry lead-time. 
The European pollutant emission regulations should be based on setting measureable 
targets based on cost -benefit and cost -effectiveness that will allow industry to 
achieve those targets by taking into consideration many factors. For SVMs, 
considering their overall negligible impact, the effort required by pollutant 
emissions’ Regulation should be proportionate to the share of emissions for which 
they are accountable. 
For all the above-mentioned reasons, Ferrari S.p.A. is confident that future pollutant 
emission legislation will include specific provisions that take into account the needs 
and peculiarities of European SVMs, which would otherwise be challenged by 
economic, technical and logistic excessive burden.

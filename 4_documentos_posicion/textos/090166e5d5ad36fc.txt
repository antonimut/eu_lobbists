Effects of Emission Aftertreatment Systems  
on Health, Economy and Global Warming  
Is there an inherent vicious tradeoff?  
  
 
1. Trade-off Limitations of Classic Engines  
With classic internal combustion engines we narrowly describe engines without exhaust gas 
aftertreatment. For gasoline engines this time was definitely over after US -Clean air act 
1970 and the TWC 1983. Before that time the gasoline engine was a highly polluting engine 
with respect to NOx, CO and HC and compared to gasoline engines the Diesel engine was 
regarded to be a clean engine. The breakthrough came with the three -way catalyst TWC 
the outstanding invention of John J. Mooney [1] which however requires to control 
stoichiometric conditions by the Lambda-sensor in very narrow bounds and this is not at all 
the optimal economic operation condition for the gasoline engines. Thus fuel consumption 
and CO2-emissions of today’s gasoline engines are higher than possible and this has not 
changed since. The Diesel engine a s a lean combustion system was always good with 
respect to HC and CO (and therefore underground only Diesel were permitted) but NOx 
was high and stepwise reduction was required by law from 1992 in the USA, from 1993 in 
Europe. Although DPF and SCR-catalysis were known and applied to engines since 19 82 
resp.1987 [2, 3], the engi ne industry tried to avoid aftertreatment until Euro 5/V , partly 
because fuel sulfur content was high. The emission reduction potential of the engine itself, 
however, is very limited to mainly injection timing and EGR. Using these parameters a 
strong tradeoff was unavoidable as the following 2 graphs show

enforced after the introduction of the real stringent particle number criterion PN with Euro V 
 
2. Decoupling - the essential difference between the classic and modern Diesel   
Emission aftertreatment EA S permitted to optimize the engine combustion more or less 
independent on emissions. Higher NOx was no longer a problem , so the engine could be 
optimized for better fuel consumption thus lower CO2. CO, HC and even PM were no longer 
a problem since filter and catalysis took perfectly care of this - much more efficient than the 
classic engine could do with its trade-off parameters. Higher combustion pressure became 
possible and the new (electronic) tools with high injection pressure, flexible injection timing 
and common rail could be used to fulfill the customer’s request for  higher power, higher

3. The Potential of EAS for health risk reduction 
The emission reduction potential of modern wall flow filters is 4 -5 orders of magnitude for 
all size particles from 1 nm to anything in the aerosol size range. This is not only valid for 
solid particles like BC (soot) and toxic ash clusters (from lubricati on oil packages, engine 
wear and additives) but also for the toxic substances attached to these particles like PAH 
and Nitro PAH, which will become important to avoid the so-called Trojan horse effects [7]. 
These filters also provide a powerful high surfac e substrate for catalysis for oxidation and 
redox processes. Important to also recognize that  filtration is independent on engine 
operation conditions and that these particle filters can  be used to upgrade the existing fleet 
in particular the HDV, construction machines and stationary energy generators.  
NOx-reduction by SCR can reach 2 orders of magnitude but is limited to operation 
conditions since it only becomes sufficiently active above exhaust temperature of ca. 225 
°C. This means that today the OEM has to add EGR in order to cover the low charge range 
like city driving at high traffic density. EGR has negative effects on fuel economy and should 
therefore be avoided. The question must be permitted, which limit values must be reached 
to protect human’s health. While in the case of the UFP the limit values are still far too high 
[8] the NOx limits might already now be on a limit which requires no further reduction as the 
author has demonstrated [9]. With this we can say that EAS of modern Diesels have the  
potential to literally detoxify the exhaust gases.  
 
4. The contribution of the Diesel to Global Warming and mitigation by EAS 
Diesel soot is black and provides a very high specific surface; it therefore effectively collects 
sunlight and heats the atmosphere or the glacier on which it is deposited. Climate forcing 
potential of Diesel soot is about 800’000 times higher than CO2 per Kilogram. Wildfire soot, 
being brownish “only” 600’000 times. Since residence time of soot in the atmosphere is 
much shorter (weeks) than CO2 (20 years) its climate forcing factor is actually reduced to 
be 4400 times higher than CO2 [10]. Including additional cloud effects due to soot particles 
the overall climate forcing by black soot is 1.2 W/m2 compared to 1,6 W/m2 with CO2 [11].; 
compared to this the actual contribution of engine waste heat is only 0.03 W/m2.

Black soot from combustion is the second largest climate forcing substance. And an 
important additional message: the reduction of soot emission becomes immediately 
effective while CO2 may stay in the atmosphere for over 20 years. Thus this very strong

warming is completely and immediately eliminated by application of  DPF + GPF and this 
would basically be possible for all new vehicles and also for a very big part of the worldwide 
in-use fleet.

5. Interaction of EAS and Engines  
An influence of the EAS like DPF , SCR, DOC on the engine is only possible by its 
backpressure. There is no other mechanism of influence so we must discuss which 
backpressure levels can be generated, to which extend they influence the engine and how 
they can be monitored and controlled in the context of modern electronically controlled 
engine systems. 
The opposite influence of the engine on the EAS on the other hand is manifold: by flow, by 
temperature, by oxygen content, by the content of reactive gases, by combustion generated 
particles, by wear particles, by lube-oil leakage and by mineral particles not filtered by the 
engine inlet filter. Not to forget also the operation profiles of the engine, altitude and climate 
effects. But modern EAS technology takes care of these influences and guarantees 
emission quality during whole engine life. 
One important element however, is the fact that the EAS is “masking” the  engine: signs of 
malfunction of an engine like smell, smoke and noise are absorbed by the EAS system. 
OBD of these engines helps to control but inspection and maintenance of these modern 
engines becomes crucial including a regular periodic technical inspection.

on average 100 – 150 mbar. The resulting increase of fuel consumption and CO2 emission 
remains below 2% for normal 4 -stroke engines with controlled EGR and moderate 
Turbocharging.     
 
 
7. Backpressure increase in operation by EAS   
SCR and DOC should not increase the backpressure much in operation but the DPF is 
continuously filled by soot and either continuously or periodically cleaned by regeneration 
during which the elemental carbon EC and the organic carbon OC will be converted to CO+ 
CO2 by normal combustion or catalytic flameless combustion or during the famous 
continuous regeneration system CRT by the NO2-Reaction [15]. The carbon should thereby 
leave the filter substrate without any residues w hich means that backpressure increases 
during soot loading and should come down to clean conditions  after regeneration . In 
addition to the carbon compounds, however, we have metals in the system from lubrication 
oil packages (Ca, S, P, Zn ..), metals from wear (Al, Fe, Ni, Cr …), which burn to ash when 
passing the combustion zone, form ing ultrafine clusters and leave the engine either 
attached to soot particles or as a separate aerosol population. There might be metal 
additives in addition, eventually used to a ccelerate the regeneration and also minerals 
cannot be excluded, passing the engine intake filter.  
 
During the pilot phase with retrofits on buses and construction machines we were 
confronted with high ash load and had to clean filters routinely when reac hing 200 mbar 
after about one year of operation or after a lubrication oil loss of 15 -20 liters.  When PSA 
started DPF in series production with the fuel borne catalyst FBC in 2000 [16] they expected 
that a ash cleaning would be required after 80’000 km.

Cleaning might however still be needed in case of emergency plugging, which can happen 
at very unusual operation conditions, at high altitude and with some engine failures which 
lead to high soot production, low oxygen availability or extremely high lubric ation oil 
consumption like in case of the turbocharger bearing failure. But also in these cases we 
have the alarm at 200 mbar so excessive fuel consumption or CO2 increase is excluded.

10. The effects of future fuels    
Assuming that future fuels are synthetic Diesel fuels based on solar generated hydrogen 
synthesized with ambient air derived CO2 using the  Fischer-Tropsch process (invented 
1925) as it is now demonstrated by more and more research centers [23] and will hopefully 
soon reach a commercial production and cost level. Using these fuels the Diesel vehicles 
will operate CO2-neutral and the persuading argument is of course that this fuel could be 
used by the whole in-use fleet without any need of infrastructure adaptation. These engines 
would however still emit soot and ash particles so the health effects due to ultrafine particle 
emission and the climate effect due to back carbon particles would not disappear. We also 
have to assume that these new fuels will  generate toxic substances in the gaseous form 
and the formation of Nitrogen to NO and NO2 is also not avoidable as long as air is used for 
combustion. This simply means that the EAS systems now developed will remain part of 
the future engine technology and with this we will be able to eliminate the health risks as 
well as the climate forcing due to the use of Diesel engines. What remains is the actual 
waste heat disseminated into the atmosphere but this is a very small contribution and engine 
development is working on it.  
It should, however, be noted : future fuels will be more expensive than today’s fuels. The 
efficiency advantage of a future Diesel engine of >30% over a gasoline engine (including 
high supercharging, downsizing and intelligent hybridization) for the same car will become 
a very important argument of economy.       
 
 
11. Benefit / Cost of EAS Application   
Combustion soot is not only carcinogen, also premature mortality due to cardiovascular 
and cerebrovascular impacts are  well documented and dose/effect functions are widely 
accepted; since mortality is liked to exposure dose and time , also the monetary health 
benefit of avoided soot emission is well documented [25, 26]; according to this 1 kg PM2.5 
emitted in Switzerland triggers health effects equivalent to a monetary value of about 460 
Euro; however, within this kg PM2.5 only the ultrafine soot particles have this very strong 
toxic effects and 1 kg of PM2.5 only contains only < 20% of soot particles, so the monetary 
value of 1 kg of engine emitted soot particles might be as high as 2’500. - Euro. For the 
following calculation we only use a value of 1’250 Euro/kg soot , as emitted by combustion 
engines as solid carbonaceous ultrafine (nano-) particles.  
 
As shown before, combustion soot is also a very strong global warming substance and the 
CO2-equivalent of 1 kg combustion soot is 4400 kg CO2 [10]. Assuming a “price” for CO2 of 
50 Euro per ton CO2 (somewhere between the Swedish 110 Euro/ton and the new German 
35 Euro/ton covering the GW-effects triggered by soot particles,  the GW damage of 1 kg 
soot comes to 220 Euro. 
 
So avoiding 1 kg of combustion soot emitted as black ultrafine solid particles represents a 
monetary value of 1’470 Euro.     
 
A HD-truck without filter emits during his 1 Mio km lifetime about 100-150 kg soot, which 
means the damage on heath and global warming of this truck is 147’000 – 220’000 Euro, 
which is about the investment cost for this truck [27]. 
A LD Diesel car without filter emits during his 200’000 km lifetime about 10 kg  soot, which 
means that the damage on heath and global warming of this car is 14’000 – 22’000 Euro, 
which again is about the investment cost for this car.

If the investment for a DPF-system for the truck is 8’000 Euro and for the car 3’000 Euro 
the Benefit /Cost comes to 18-27 for the truck and 4.6 – 7 for a car, which is well in the 
range large studies of the US-EPA [28]  and ICCT [29] are reporting.    
In case of retrofit or upgrade the investment cost is higher, maybe twice as high as OEM-
products but as we see from these figures the benefit/cost is still very attractive. 
 
In addition to soot w e also avoid some CO 2 by “decoupling” (see chapter 2) because the 
engine efficiency can be optimized without any tradeoff for emissions. This might be at least 
10 %. For a HDV the overall CO 2-emission might be 800 tons per life (see Fig.7) and 10% 
of this will have a monetary values of 40’000 Euro, in case of the LDV it might be 40 tons 
monetized 2000 Euro. Adding this to the above mentioned benefit by soot reduction we 
come up with 187’000 – 260’000 Euro for the HDV and for the LDV 16’000 to 24’000 Euro 
which increases to overall benefit/cost to 23-32 for the HDV and for the LDV to 5.3-8.  
 
In case of deNOx-systems like SCR used in today’s vehicles the benefit/cost calculation 
is not possible since there is very little incidence for mortal effects due to NO2 exposure at 
levels below 400 µg/m3 [30] and proven dose/effect functions are not availab le.                           
Non-scientific approaches that falsify the health effect by confusing solid soot particles with 
water-soluble nitrate particles [31] are to be strongly rejected.  
So for deNOx-EAS the benefit/cost factor might be very low if at all.   
 
 
12. Summary  
Emission Aftertreatment Systems EAS of modern internal combustion engines like filters for 
ultrafine solid particles DPF or GPF and catalytic systems like DOC and SCR for the 
oxidation of CO and HC as well as for the reduction of NOx are state of the art. They provide 
a degree of reduction of >1:100 for the ultrafine particles and > 1:20 for NOx, CO, HC. This 
technology has the potential to further improve filtration to 99,99 %, catalytic oxidation to 
99% and catalytic reduction of NOx to 99% for the complete operation range and for the full 
life of the vehicles. These EAS systems ar e also able to perfectly cope with future fuel 
requirements. This means that these EAS systems detoxify vehicle exhaust gases and 
eliminate climate forcing due to soot particle elimination at Benefit/Cost levels of 30 for LDV 
and 8 for LDV.  
Backpressure of EAS cannot be neglected but can be controlled to levels < 400 mbar even 
for future developments. If EAS is retrofitted on an existing engine this backpressure must 
be compensated by additional engine work thus fuel consumption and CO 2 emission will 
increase 1-2 %. Modern engines however with integrated EAS use the decoupling potential 
of the system  to more than overcome this effect , the engine will be developed to  best 
possible fuel economy and lowest noise, use downsizing and downspeeding [24] potentials 
and the EAS will perfectly take care of the emission problems . At close-to-zero emission 
level fuel economy improvement has already be demonstrated to be improved by up to 10 
% and future development will double this figure soon.  
This modern EAS integrated engines are best candidates for future CO 2-neutral fuels and 
offer a way to convert the existing fleet to a zero -tox and zero -carbon level much faster , 
more efficient, more sustainable and socially acceptable than e-mobility.

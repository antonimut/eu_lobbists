Subject: Sustainable Products Initiative – Roadmap Consultation 
Inception Impact Assessment – Legislative Proposal 
  
 
 
Plastics Recyclers Europe (PRE) welcomes the initiative taken by the EU Commission to 
develop a policy framework on sustainable products to complement the Green Deal 
ambitions and act on the deliverables of the new Circular Economy Action Plan. 
 
PRE would like to contribute to the public consultation by providing the perspective of the 
plastic recycling industry, the core industrial process enabling the circularity of plastics. Our 
industry links waste management operations with industrial production processes, the last 
step in the end-of-life of products, and the first in the production of circular, sustainable, 
plastic products. 
 
In a linear model, recycling represents a waste management process at the last step in the 
life-cycle of a plastic product1, where the output of a recycling operation is placed back on 
the market in the form of a raw material to be used again. 
 
In a circular model, on the other hand, recycling is also the first step of a production 
process, whereby virgin materials are gradually substituted by recycled ones (recyclates), in 
a wider set of end-applications. To tackle the sustainability of products in those sectors 
subject to the public consultation, (i.e. electronics, ICT and textiles, but also furniture and 
high impact intermediate products such as steel, cement and chemicals), means to 
approach recycling processes both as waste management operations and production 
processes for raw materials. Products made with recyclates are sustainable, not only for 
what it concerns the environment and the management of waste, the less use in raw 
resources and energy, but because they create new market opportunities for a wider set of 
end-applications, leading to job creation and circular business models. 
 
The success of recycling and use of recyclates in new products depends on the whole life-
cycle of a product, from when it was designed, labelled, placed on the market, consumed, 
collected, sorted, and recycled, which necessitates the contribution of the whole value-chain 
to the same objective, i.e. increasing the circularity of plastic products. 
 
To shape the European market according to sustainable paradigms represents a long-term 
commitment by the industry. Industry-led standards represents an effective instrument to 
channel the required needs to adjust current practices and make them more sustainable.  
 
The Circular Plastics Alliance (CPA), with the support of CEN-CENELEC, represents in our 
opinion the right way ahead.  There, working groups are focusing on providing the right set

1. Sustainable recyclable products – the key components  
 
- Design for recycling: compatibility with existing waste streams 
Sustainable products must be placed on the market with circularity by design. PRE has 
developed in the years a toolkit for brand-owners to assess the recyclability of their 
packaging. Recently, this toolkit has become a stakeholder platform named RecyClass2, 
where the value-chain of plastic packaging tests, according to scientific protocols, the 
compatibility of plastics used in packaging for existing waste streams. Guidelines are 
published regularly according to innovation in technologies and materials, leading to a 
tangible transition to greener plastic packaging being placed on the market. The way ahead 
is to extend this approach to plastics used in other industry sectors, such as the ones 
subject of this public consultation. 
 
- Collection systems 
Waste management systems that collect waste from households greatly differs among 
regions and municipalities in the EU. A harmonization will help creating a more uniform 
waste streams for types of plastic items that are to be collected in the plastic bin bags. PRE 
believes that, despite the different consumer choices available at European level, 
harmonisation in terms of products available on the market between the Member States 
allows for a harmonisation in the collection of plastic waste. 
 
- Watermarking 
Once enhanced separate collection at Union level raises the offer for good quality waste 
materials, to separate into the different plastic waste streams, innovative technologies for 
the recyclability of products by design include marking the surfaces of products and 
packaging with information on the composition of materials used. This allows for automatic 
sorting operations that are easily implementable by recycling facilities (modular-based 
technologies). This process creates the push for a more efficient sorting leading to cleaner 
input waste materials to recyclers and by consequence a cleaner, cheaper to process, output 
materials to be used in a wider set of end-applications.

- Digitalisation of sorting operations 
Watermarking can only work with an overall digitalisation of sorting operations. Investments 
shall be directed towards the establishment at European level of modern sorting centres 
capable of exploiting new technologies and digitalization of sorting operations. 
 
- Labelling and consumers' behaviour 
The proper disposal of plastic products and packaging makes the whole process 
functioning. Objective to have sustainable products is to inform consumers on the correct 
practices to handle them when these reach the end-of-life status. Marking on packaging 
shall further be extended (recycled content, recyclable, which bin) to inform citizens on the 
correct disposal for a wider set of products. 
 
2. Sustainable products made with recyclates 
 
When the above conditions are developed and met, the use of recyclates in a wider set of 
end-applications would mean closing recycling loops for a variety of plastic products and 
packaging. Moreover, by using recyclates, it would participate in reducing the carbon 
footprint of plastic products. 
 
Re-usability of plastic packaging and recycling together will help place on the market 
sustainable products while keeping in Europe generated waste that can be reintroduced in 
the economy. 
 
- Food-contact application 
Legislation on food-contact applications shall be ready to be adapted to improved waste 
management systems and technological improvements. Without a legislative stable 
environment, mid-long-term investments required to create a wider market for food-contact 
recyclates will strive to come in time with the deadlines and objectives set by the Green Deal 
and new CEAP.  
 
- Recycling technologies, not all are the same 
Sustainable products are made with sustainable industrial practices. When the boundaries 
between chemical production and recycling will become thinner because of new 
technological advancements in the handling of waste (i.e. reducing polymers to their base 
chemicals through pyrolysis), these industrial practices must be proven to be sustainable, 
as it already happens today with virgin production. To this regard, we welcome the EU 
Taxonomy as setting the right way ahead, where recycling and GHG emissions' reduction 
are linked with each other, to be called sustainable practices. 
 
- Recycled content, an additional parameter for the Ecodesign Directive 
 
The expansion of the scope of the Directive beyond energy-related products would enable 
the assessment of a wider scope of products.

Despite the Directive already prescribes the use of materials issued from recycling activities, 
as one of the parameters to evaluate the potential for improving environmental aspects 
(Annex I), it lacks a specific parameter on recycled content. A greater focus on this 
parameter should be engrained into a revised framework Directive. In each new Ecodesign 
implementing measure, a minimum recycled content requirement should be included based 
on what is technically/economically achievable. 
 
PRE remains available for the responsible team at the Commission to further elaborate on 
the points raised in this position paper. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
About  
Plastics Recyclers Europe (PRE) is an organization representing the voice of the European plastics recyclers who reprocess 
plastic waste into high quality material destined for production of new articles. Recyclers are important facilitators of the 
circularity of plastics and the transition towards the circular economy.  
Plastics recycling in Europe is a rapidly growing sector representing €3bn in turnover.

Cefic views on the Commission’s Sustainable Products 
Initiative 
We welcome the opportunity to provide feedback on  the Commission’s 
Roadmap on Sustainable Products Initiative. 
The European chemical industry is highly committed to bringing products 
to the market that are safe and have a low environmental impact, while 
maximising value for the consumers and the society. Innovation 
continues in search of more resource efficient and circular products. 
Cefic welcomes the Commission’s intenti on to expand the scope of the Ecodesign Directive 
beyond energy-related products to increase resource efficiency and circularity, focusing on the 
key products identified for their highest potential consistent with the ambitions of the CEAP and 
remaining attentive to the need to avoid double-regulation for products that are already subject 
to product-specific legislation. 
Safe and sustainable by design 
✓ Chemical innovations in the design of materials can enable downstream users to supply the 
market with more sustainable and circular products . ‘Safe and sustainable by design’  is an 
innovation approach towards designing chemical products and technologies th at deliver societal 
value, accelerating the transition towards a circular and sustainable society.  
✓ Employing a life-cycle approach is an essential prerequisite in delivering solutions which serve 
their intended function, are  environmentally safe, cost -effective, and positively contribute to 
society over the entire lifecycle; 
✓ Life cycle approaches are multidimensional, needing a balanced approach on weighting different 
aspects and specific cases. When setting a balance between these aspects, one needs to recognise 
commonly agreed benchmarks, which can be adapted and featured per product category.  
✓ The Commission’s Communication on the Chemicals Strategy for Sustainability says that c riteria 
will be developed to secure a common understanding of safe and sustainable-by-design chemicals 
and that financial support will also be provided. Cefic continues to support the implementation of 
safer and more sustainable products that contribute to lower CO2 emissions, step up safe recycling 
and re-use, and reduce overall environmental footprint.  
 
Secondary Raw Materials and sustainable products

demand for secondary materials and a market for sustainable products in general (including from

virgin materials ).  The inclusion of recyclability and recycled content requirements should be 
considered on a case by case basis in the context of a lifecycle approach , and the availability and 
quality of raw materials 
✓ To this end secondary raw materials standards would help increase downstream customers and 
consumers’ confidence in recycled materials 
✓ A consistent implementation of a single market for waste  is key to facilitate and promote waste 
recovery (including recycling) while ensuring a high level of environmental protection. 
✓ Thorough impact assessments of the Ecodesign measures on ‘Key Product Value Chains’ in the EU 
Circular Economy Action Plan 1, as well as the promotion of cross-sectoral Value Chain Platforms 
like the European Circular Plastics Alliance ; these are important to the success of the circular 
economy  
Tracking & tracing of information on problematic substances along the value chain 
✓ The development of a policy framework or industry led initiatives to ensure communication in all 
supply chains and to waste operators  should be a balanced and inclusive exercise, involving a 
cross-industry alignment effort with a strong focus on feasibility and effective implementation. 
A policy framework would need to be proportionate to address the needs of each sector; 
✓ This approach can benefit from the following steps: 
− Establish sectorial approaches for priority waste streams 
− Assess the feasibility of digital solutions  for tracking & tracing  substances throughout 
their lifecycle including current efforts and the potential of voluntary initiatives  
− Ensure that such digital solutions can be integrated with existing data systems  most 
used and an adapted phase-in/grace period 
− Support industry and LCA database providers to develop  the required LCA datasets and 
avoid discrepancies between different database providers  
− Ensure coherence with ongoing initiatives in the EU Digital Strategy 
− Support the creation of advanced detection methods to assess the presence of 
substances in materials and products 
− Develop standardised requirements regarding the communication on relevant 
chemicals in products (CiP) along the value chain 
In conclusion: 
We call upon policymakers to put a supportive, holistic, consistent and forward -looking policy framework 
in place, th at enables industry to deliver on the EU Green Deal  and prioritises the value chains with the 
highest recycling potential.

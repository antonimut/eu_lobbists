CONSULTATION ELV 
 
ADA is the most representative car -dismantler’s association in Italy, and welcomes the path taken 
by the Commission to assess the status of the situation concerning  ELV’s, after 20 years  from 
the enactment of Directive 2000/53/EC, with involvement of all the stakeholders. 
The directive had marked an important turning point for our sector  for the reason that, with the 
Italian transposition, in the Legislative Decree 209/03,  the companies have done many structural 
and operational interventions to their own plants with very important investments. 
We believe, however, that we must absolutely find a solution  for some issues  to support the 
continuous growth of our companies in  environment respect: otherwise, the maintenance of 
activities could risk not being economically sustainable for a long time. 
TRANSPOSITION IN EUROPEAN COUNTRIES 
It is recommended to take into due consideration the need for the directive to be implemented in 
European countries in a homogeneous  way, so not to favor companie s in some countries rather 
than others. In Italy, unfortunately, we record different behaviors of the responsabile environment 
authorities that release authorizations that produce unfair competition between companies placed 
in different regions/provinces.  
Furthermore, it should consider the need to collect and analyze data from the states in a 
homogeneous way, based on the same parameters. 
The assessment of the achievement of the objectives is difficult to compare between European 
countries if the same methods of data collection and reprocessing are not used. 
ERP 
First of all, we believe that manufacturers should comply with the ERP guaranteeing their dismantler 
networks, verifying their requirements and monitoring their goals  in terms of reuse, recycling  and 
recovery. They should also make sure to collect all vehicles, placed on the market, at the end of their 
life by implementing the networks and making them more efficient.  Besides, vehicle design should 
keep in greater consideration the next steps on f or dismantling. It’s very important improving

ILLECIT EXPORT 
It is well known that many vehicles, even if they are end -of-life vehicles (waste, you know), are 
destined to export to EU and non -EU countries with the  only purpose by traders and private 
subjects to  recover spare parts from these vehicles,  without being subjected to the operations 
required by the Community rules. And regardless of the waste export legislation. 
In Italy, a first attempt to solve the problem  has been made, entering the obligation of  a positive 
vehicle-test, not older than 6 months, as a requirement to request the cancellation from the public 
register for export. (Thus preventing the export of vehicles that are too old or damaged).  To our 
total disappointment, this rule only remained in force for a few months: now (from  September 15) 
it is enough if the vehicle is in accordance with the inspection normal obligations. 
Although in Italy there is a public register in which all  vehicle’s dossiers are transcribed, there are 
still data that reveal the lack of control over the entire national vehicle fleet: in addition to the

ILLICIT VEHICLE ABANDONMENT 
The abandoning  of vehicles must be discouraged. In  Italy the main cause  of abandoning  is 
the “administrative detention”, a fiscal measure that blocks the circulation of the vehicle until the 
debts with the public administration are paid. Unfortunately,  in cases where the value of 
vehicles does not justify this  administrative detention (vehicles over 10 years old with 
administrative detention of tens of thousands of euros),  vehicles can’t be unregistrated and their 
abandoning is encouraged. 
INTERACTION WITH OTHER DIRECTIVES 
It would be necessary to coordinate the ELV directive with the directives that could involve the 
ELVs (POPs, REACHs for example) so as not to create interpretative difficulties that could put in 
risk the sustainability of interventions on the vehicle by car-dismantling companies. We refer in 
particular to the issues related to the recycling of plastics. 
It should also take into consideration the fact that scientific progress, which makes possible to 
limit the use of dangerous substances during the production of vehicles, cannot have reflections 
on vehicles in circulation and that the average life time of a vehicle is over 10 years.

REPLACING SPARE PARTS 
In order to favour the re-use of spare parts, consistently with the purpose provided  by the waste 
directive, it  should be definitively made clear that these, having set the safety condition for the 
vehicle, leave the waste area and that their market must be encouraged, including export.  
At the same time, the online sale of spare parts by private and unqualified subjects must be 
controlled, because often these parts are coming from exported vehicles from western EU countries 
to EU countries in Eastern Europe and, in any case from vehicles that have not been 
managed according with EU regulations. 
So, the traceability of spare parts would be necessary to ensure that they c ome from 
vehicles dismantled in authorized center , excluding those pertaining to safety.  In Italy, from 
September 27,  it is mandatory to guarantee the traceability of registered spare parts (currently 
engines and batteries of electric / hybrid vehicles). 
ILLEGAL ACTIVITIES 
Inspections against illegally managed activities should be intensified: besides working 
without respect for the environ ment, they impede free competition and negatively affect the 
market. 
CERTIFICATION 
Companies should be encouraged to obtain and maintain a quality certification, but the 
requirements necessary to obtain it  must be standardized: in fact, on the market the re are 
institutions that issue certifications that are not comparable to others, in qualitative and economic 
terms.

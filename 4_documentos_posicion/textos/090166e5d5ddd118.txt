pinfa input to public consultation on the European 
Commission’s “Sustainable Products Initiative” 
(Inception Impact Assessment) 
 
The Phosphorus, Inorganic and Nitrogen (PIN) Flame Retardants Association, pinfa, www.pinfa.eu supports 
the objective of the Sustainable Products Initiative (SPI) to move forward product sustainability,  
circularity and energy and materials efficiency, including addressing chemicals. 
This Initiative should improve coherence between policy tools: Ecodesign, Ecolabel, Green Public 
Purchasing, RoHS Directive , WEEE Directive and chemicals policy (REACH, POPs , and the Green Deal 
Chemicals Strategy 14/10/2020). 
pinfa welcomes the proposal to widen the scope of the Ecodesign Directive beyond energy -related 
products. 
The approach to chemicals in products should not be limited to the “ presence of harmful chemicals ” 
(consultation website): the benefits of chemicals’ functions should be considered , as should their LCA , 
role in product durability and impacts on product recycling.  Agreed frameworks and methodology for 
assessment of both chemical benefits and impacts should be developed. 
We welcome the identification of electronics/ICT, textiles and furniture as priority product groups . For 
each of these gro ups, fire safety is important . Flammable polymer -based advanced materials and 
composites are essential for electronics for electrical safety and performance (circuit boards, encasements, 
insulation, cables …). Both natural and synthetic textiles and furniture materials are flammable.  
Despite progress over the last forty years, and in the absence of any collated EU statis tics, it is estimated 
that fire kills around 6 000 persons/year in Europe, and injures ten times more 1. The continuing increase 
in the ‘fire load’ of flammable materials (plastics, textiles, carpets, furnishings …) in our homes means 
that ‘escape time’ (the time between a fire starting and temperatures reaching 600 – 1 000 °C) has fallen 
to 1 ½ - 10 minutes2, that is five times lower3 than in the 1970’s. 
Fire has significant environmental impacts which should be taken into account in the Sustainable Products 
Initiative: toxic emissions from burning materials, replacement of lost and damaged property. 
pinfa wishes to cooperate in continuing to improve the health and environmental profile, and recyc ling 
compatibility of non-halogenated flame retardants, with the objective of enabling products to respond to 
societal demand for safety, whilst progressing towards product sustainability . We believe that it is 
important to develop increasingly sustainable chemical solutions to ensure fire safety , in advanced 
materials and natural materials, wherever  it is essential : such as electronics/IT (can cause fires  by 
overheating, arcing, battery failure …), furnishings or textiles (in public places, for people with mobility 
difficulties (hindering escape), in transport applications …) . Even steel needs fire safety protection in

pinfa notes that the inclusion of “ intermediate products such as steel, cement and chemicals ” in the SPI 
requires a different approach from consumer (finished) product groups. A specific action under the SPI 
could duplicate the Green Deal “Chemicals Strategy”. Coherence should be ensured between the Chemicals 
Strategy, the SPI approach to chemicals, and SPI approaches to chemicals in finished product groups, taking 
into account the functions delivered by chemicals in these products. 
We call for a supportive, holistic, consistent and forward-looking policy framework, to enable the fire safety 
industry to deliver on the Green Deal.  
While contributing to this consultation we are very aware we are experiencing unprecedented times, with 
events none of us have liv ed through before. Many governments and institutions around the world are 
taking major actions to address the Covid -19 crisis, and introducing large -scale policies which will have 
significant impacts for years to come. We will continue to support Europe’s Member State governments and 
institutions in their efforts to overcome the socio -economic impacts of the crisis. When investing in the 
future, industry, governments and institutions will have to ensure investments align with the policy targets 
of a climate neutral Europe by 2050. All this also means that the attractiveness of Europe as a re-investment 
destination, and re -shoring industry back to Europe, will depend more than ever on a favourable policy 
framework that manages ever-growing differences between the world’s regions. We look to the European 
Commission to undertake the appropriate assessments and to include these wider considerations in the 
future framework that will be developed.

About pinfa 
pinfa is the Phosphorus, Inorganic and Nitrogen (PIN) Flame Retardants Association (a Sector Group within Cefic, the 
European Chemical Industry Council). www.pinfa.org We bring together (with pinfa North America and pinfa China) nearly 40 
companies who manufacture or use non-halogenated flame retardants, smoke suppressants and synergists, based on 
chemistries of one or more of phosphorus, nitrogen and inorganics.

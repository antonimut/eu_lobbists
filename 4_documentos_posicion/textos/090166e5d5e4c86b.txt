 
 
EURATEX’s Feedback to the „Sustainable Product Initiative” 
 
EURATEX, the European Apparel and Textile Confederation  welcomes and supports the roadmap on the 
Sustainable Product Initiative published by the European Commission. We are pleased to see that the 
European Commission is moving forward on this policy. EURATEX also recognizes this initiative is 
necessary in achieving the Circular Economy Action Plan and the Green Deal targets as well as to implement 
the necessary circular design in the textile industry supply chain . Behind the statement made into the 
roadmap, there is a huge shift in the business model of the industry and it has to be driven in a way to 
generate value in the EU, to offer opportunities of the proximity business model adding value to products. 
This initiative is important to ensure a level playing field across the EU, an opportunity to harmonized rules 
and standards and facilitate the scaling-up of products. 
1. Eco-design directive must be well eco-designed 
Ecodesign - or rather Circular Design - plays an important role in establishing a true circular economy. 
Statement such as imposing monofibres usage should be avoided, as this is not feasible for technical reasons 
and, in the fashion sector, for usage and quality performance reasons.  Appropriate circular economy 
strategies or sustainability criteria cannot be determined at a general level - there must always be a specific 
look at the product and its materials itself and the usage scenario.  The role of produ ct design needs to be 
strengthened and it has to be assured, that provisions do not hinder developments. 
Taking into account these elements, EURATEX would like to stress that, it is very important that textiles 
and clothing get a sector-specific Eco-design directive. 
2. Special attention should be given to definition  
One important concern that EURATEX would like to address is the definition problem. We should clearly 
define the term sustainability in order to avoid target conflicts . Definitions of sustainability principles or 
specifications for circular design must be able to be applied to product-specifically and adapted to the 
corresponding use scenario. If new requirements on ecodesign/sustainability/circularity aren´t set up in a 
proper, clear and scientific based form, greenwashing won´t be avoided . Every product is different and 
therefore, different aspects needs to be looked at.  Applied technologies and materials, legal requirements, 
intended use, etc. must be taken into account when finding the appropriate Circular Economy Strategy. 
3. Technology challenges, shared knowledge and research  
Provisions and goals of the sustainable product policy must be in line with actual technological availabilities. 
Many processes are still in their early stages or have not yet established themselves on an industrial scale.  
The transition to a circular economy can only be achieved together and if all stakeholders along the product 
life cycle are involved. Practical guidelines for circular design can only be created if actors in the production 
phase and actors in the EoL phase work together. (e.g. product designers, material specialists and recycling 
engineers) 
4. Digital Product Passport 
Traceability is important therefore we need to have a clear passport of the product, containing origin, exact 
fibre composition and the chemicals used. The Commission should seek to implement a user friendly 
system and should give sufficient time and funding to SMEs to fulfil the new requirements. A debate on 
the energy consumption of such digital storage should also happen resulting in a cost benefit analysis.

5. Numerous standards and labelling  
EURATEX would like to highlight the high number of sustainability standards for textiles and labelling to 
track supply chain, that are also largely spread across Europe under QR codes. It is important not to create 
additional labels but, instead, clear recommendations on which standards should be used or accepted must 
be formulated. To achieve the formulation of this recommendation the Commission should provide an 
impact assessment stressing how consumers can be provided with information, how much will it costs and 
estimating the risks of oversimplification of the information.  
6.  Proportionality  
SMEs constitutes a concern.  There is a potential high burden that could fell on the SMEs with th e new 
requirements. Not all companies have the same starting position when implementing new requirements or 
new business models. New requirements should be realizable in a target-oriented way and serve the desired 
goal (e.g. closing material cycles). Here, the needs of SMEs must also be taken into account and practicable 
and effective solutions must be proposed.  
7. Level-playing field 
EURATEX would like to raise  the awareness of the Commission concerning non-EU importers not 
following the same EU requirements, it could also appear as a certain burden for SMEs. To avoid any further 
unfair advantages, the requirements that are enforced in the EU should also be extended to products that are 
imported into the EU.

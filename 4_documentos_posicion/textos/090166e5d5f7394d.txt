In General 
The Association of the Automotive Industry (VDA) represents more than 600 companies in the 
automotive industry -manufacturers of motor vehicles and their engines, set-ups, installations, 
containers and vehicle parts and equipment -which produce in Germany . The automotive industry 
is the highest-turnover branch and generated a revenue of more than 435 billion euros in 2019. 
Approximately 833,000 employees manufactured about 4.7 million cars in Germany -from 16 
million cars worldwide. This does not include commercial vehicles (trucks and buses) produced by 
our member companies. Together we research and produce for a clean, safe, and sustainable 
mobility of the future. 
 
The German automotive industry is fully aware of its responsibility throughout the entire cycle of a 
car’ s production and use. Therefore, the VDA supports the goal of the European Commission to 
reduce waste and foster the reuse of resources. All political initiatives should be directly linked to 
the primary goal of the European Union to achieve climate neutrality until 2050.  
 
Overall, the current EL V Directive is a success story. Only slight improvement is needed to sustain 
the Directive for future requirements. The VDA will support this process. 
The automotive industry is pioneer for circular economy 
Due to the EL V Directive a high-quality , certified take-back and recycling network for EL Vs has 
been established in Europe by car manufactures and its business partners. In this network take-
back is free of charge for the last holder and an environment friendly treatment of EL Vs is 
guaranteed. 
 
The products of car manufacturers and suppliers are pioneer for circular economy and are 
benchmark for other industries due to  
▪ high amounts of recycled steel and NF metals in new vehicles,  
▪ established remanufacturing processes with high volumes,  
▪ very good reparability,  
▪ longevity of their products and 
▪ significant reduction of hazardous materials.

Guiding principle for the ELV-revision 
The revision of the EL V Directive should reinforce its existing strengths, should foster innovations, 
and should trust in existing market mechanism.  
 
1. Passenger cars are no ordinary consumer products 
Passenger cars are different from other consumer products due to their complexity and longevity. 
A vehicle consists of about 5.000 to 7.000 components and a multiple subcomponents. It is 15 to 
22 years in use while being exposed to a temperature range of 100°C for the car interior, guarantee 
crash safety , flame retardancy and high material stress during the whole lifetime. 
 
2. The ELV Directive should focus on passenger cars 
The heavy-duty vehicle (HDV) should remain outside the scope of the EL V Directive. Unlike in the 
passenger car segment, there are no ‘standard’ vehicle models in the HDV segment. 
 
3. The ELV Directive should cover the different requirements by the member states 
Waste systems are designed by national authorities and are valid within the limits of the national 
borders. There is no European economic area for waste and one-size-fits all solution might be 
difficult. 
Instead, future ELV legislation should be flexible regarding national requirements and status (e.g. 
population density, number of collection points, decentralized take-back and recycling networks 
and different age of ELV in member states). 
 
4. The traction battery is crucial for a self-sustaining business model in future 
Take-back and treatment of ELVs is a self-sustaining business model today . The value of an EL V 
sufficiently covers the cost of all necessary treatment steps throughout the entire recycling value 
chain. Raw materials are highly valuable goods. Therefore, automotive manufactures have already 
established closed loops of materials, re-use, and re-manufacturing applications. This trend will be 
enhanced in the next years and will be boosted if the traction battery is – in a legal perspective of 
the ELV Directive – a component of the vehicle. 
 
5. A future-proof recycling system by digitizing deregistration and certificate of destruction 
Key factor for an effective governance of the EL V is the obligatory submission of a certificate of 
destruction for final deregistration and its consequent control. Deregistration and submission of a 
certificate of destruction is mainly an analogue and time-consuming process with a high error 
rate. Digitizing these processes and certificates has enormous potential for innovation and 
business. It is key for a better enforcement of existing legislation by preventing unauthorized 
operators and illegal recycling activities. Valuable resources which are missing today will be led 
back to legal markets and ensure a self-sustaining business model also in future.  
 
6. The ELV Directive should not contradict the climate protection goals by the European 
Union 
Recycling of passenger cars makes up for just 1 percent of the whole CO2 emissions of a 
passenger car along its life cycle. Therefore, design for sustainability is in the focus by the industry. 
This includes production, use phase and recycling of the vehicles. The car manufactures and their 
suppliers needs a mix of technologies including advanced materials and lightweight design to 
achieve climate neutrality. 
Furthermore, it should be considered that the increasing number of substance prohibitions 
hamper future recycling, re-use, and re-manufacturing. The upcoming revision on the EL V 
Directive today should be open for future recycling technologies. 
 
7. Complex technologies need flexible use of advanced materials  
The German automotive industry supports the circular plastic alliance (CPA) and is increasing the 
applications of recycled plastic continuously. In addition, the industry is exploring sustainable 
alternatives to oil-based plastic, if this is technically feasible and economically reasonable. 
Nevertheless, research and development must consider the complexity and the high requirements 
to passenger cars (see above). To ensure innovation and future reliability of passenger cars the 
industry depends on flexible use of advanced materials. Specific material recycling quotas or 
quotas for the use of recycled plastic would contradict this approach.

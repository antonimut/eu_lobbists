ECGA statement to the roadmap on the Revision of the End-of-life vehicles directive 
 
The European Carbon and Graphite Association (ECGA) is the representative association of EU carbon and 
graphite producers, including the EU based graphite electrode producers going into Europe’s steel and 
foundry industry, electrodes and cathodes for the aluminium and ferroalloy industry as well as a wide 
variety of  specialty graphite and carbon products for applications ranging from electric motors to modern 
battery technology. 
 
Graphite and carbon materials are an integral part of the EU’s strategy for decarbonisation, performing 
as reliable and safe mat erials for applications in the automotive sector, including batteries and electric 
motors.  
 
ECGA welcomes the opportunity to submit its views on the revision o f the End-of-life vehicles Directive 
(ELV Directive). The revision of the Directive, foreseen for Q2 2022, should allow the automotive sector  
and stakeholders of the value chain  to operate in a clear, consistent and transparent regulatory 
framework which encourages the development of cutting-edge and low-carbon technologies to support 
Europe’s climate objectives and resource efficient and circular ambitions.  
 
To achieve this, ECGA and its members submit the following key observations:  
 
▪ Support carbon-neutral innovation and technology  via consistent and coherent policy objectives 
and legislation 
The review of the ELV Directive should not hinder the development of innovation, new business models 
and products. Industry, long-term investments, and innovative solutions depend on the existence of a 
legislative framework providing predictability and flexibility, where the coherence between the different 
sectoral legislative instruments regulating the automotive sector and its components is guaranteed. 
 
The revision of the ELV directive should follow the better regulation principles and adequately 
complement existing EU regulatory frameworks, including REACH, RoHS and the Waste Framework 
Directive (WFD), and coordinate with upcoming initiatives, specifically the batteries regulation.  
 
These regulatory initiatives must be harmonised, coherent and coordinated to avoid overregulation, 
overburden of industry, legal uncertainty, and conflicting obligations, all of which undermine the global 
competitiveness of the EU automotive industry.  
 
▪ Avoid material specific targets  
The ELV Directive already features ambitious targets for the re-use and recycling (85%) as well as re -use 
and recovery (95%) of components from ELVs. The EU automotive sector and its value chains have already 
successfully delivered outstanding results.

The introduction of  material specific targets, in terms of re-use, recycling efficiencies and mandatory 
recycled content, is undesirable and will not provide any improvement regarding the overall targets, nor 
for the environment . These measures will , instead, negatively affect the image and use of the specific 
material on the market as well as arbitrary force users along the value chain to substitute the material.  
 
Indeed, the fulfillment of substance specific targets depends on product design, collection, dismantling, 
sorting and the quality of recycling operations, underpinned by  economic and technical viability. In 
absence of these cumulative conditions, users will be encouraged to substitute materials that feature 
recovery, re-use and recycling challenges , with less efficient materials, impacting product performance, 
sustainability and durability. 
 
Additionally, substance specific targets lack flexibility and feature complex and challenging monitoring 
and measuring systems that are difficult to implement and economically questionable.  
 
Overall, specific re -use target s, recycling efficienc y targets and recycled content obligation s seem a 
disproportionate burden on industry compared to existing combined-substances targets which allow for 
higher rates as well as economic feasibility. 
 
▪ Support the use of innovative raw materials 
Premature targeting, restrictions and bans on the use of certain raw materials in the automotive sector 
could jeopardise the achievement of carbon-neutral objectives and hinder investments.  
 
When considering hazardous substances/substances of concern, an overall risk assessm ent of the 
substance should be conducted to avoid unnecessary regulation and industry burden based on the sole 
hazardous classification of the material, in lack of an existing and substantial risk for the workers, for the 
environment and/or for the end users.  
In addition, conformity, consistency and coordination with the REACH Regulation and the Chemicals 
Strategy for Sustainability must be achieved.  
▪ Guarantee global competitiveness and level playing field 
The review of the ELV directive should support a prosperous European automotive sector that will be able 
to continue to substantially contribute to the achievement of emission reduction targets while competing 
against strong, existing markets outside the EU on a level playing field.  
 
Consequently, any revision or introduction of a new provision should consider the ELV directive’s impact 
on the global trade of vehicles.

In conclusion, any amendments proposed by the European Commission under the review of the ELV  
directive should aim to support and strengthen the existing system and the progress already made by all 
stakeholders in the value chain, improve the implementation and enforcement of the current legislation 
in Member States, and not create new legal obligations nor disrupt the smooth functioning of the current 
scheme.

1. IDENTIFICATION OF PROBLEMS NOT COVERED BY DIRECTIVE 53/2000 / EC 
 
1.1.- TYPES OF ENGINES. -  The regulations must include the new generation of vehicles that is 
entering the market, to prevent these vehicles from escaping control (vehicles that circulate by 
motor on the road regardless of the propulsion system they use, whether it is electric, LPG, CNG, 
hybrid or diesel combustion) in order to be treated in the ATF, with facilities that guarantee 
handling with full environmental guarantees. 
1.2.- VEHICLE IDENTIFICATION.- Although art. 2.2 of Directive 53/2000 / EC defines ELV as that 
vehicle that is considered waste1, and it could then be interpreted that all vehicles when their 
owner has the intention or obligation to detach from him acquires the status of waste, however 
Article 2.1 defines "vehicles" as those classified as M1, N1 in Directive 70/156 / CEE2, and motor 
vehicles included in Directive 92/61 / CEE3, excluding from the control the rest of vehic les, 
including vehicles. industrial, agricultural buses, minibus, and the vast majority of 2 and 3 -
wheelers. In fact, in Spain both in the transposition of Directive 53/2000 / CE through the 
publication in the Official State Gazette (BOE) on January 3, 2003 of Royal Decree 1383/2002 of 
December 20, as well as in the current standard for the management of end-of-life vehicles (ELV) 
Royal Decree 20/2017, of January 20, published on January 21, 2017 in the BOE, only requires 
the delivery of vehicles identified  in art . 2.1 at CATs (authorized ELV treatment centers). This 
lack of control can be seen in the statistical data published by the General Directorate of Traffic, 
which out of a total of definitive deregistrations recorded in Spain in 2019: 1,087,975 Vehicles, 
of which almost 20% would cover excluded vehicles (see attached document "Report lost 
vehicles"). 
1.3.- EXPANDED RESPONSIBILITY OF THE VEHICLE PRODUCER.-  In Spain, as happens in different 
countries, we have recycling problems for certain vehicle components, such as glass, tires, or 
plastics, and although work is being done to improve, there is a fundamental problem that 
prevents finding a satisfactory solution . Although the Directive in its art. 3 .1 expresses within 
the scope the correct management of ELVs and also their components and spare parts, and that 
in article 5.4 second paragraph , the obligation on producers to pay for the collection and 
treatment of ELV and its components is indicated the lack of an extended liability system in 
Spain, and the lack of regulations that define the obligations of the producer of the product , 
is causing the responsibility for the collection of ELV components, (reused tires), to fall on the 
weakest link, ATF, we understand that the principle of extended  producer responsibility of the 
product is being breached ”(see attached document "Extended Producer Responsibility") 
1.4.- DEFINITION OF COSTS OF RECYCLING THE ELV AND ITS COMPONENTS. - The life cycle of 
the ELV, begins in the manufacture of the vehicle and its components, continues during its useful 
life in which various repairs take place and finally its collection and treatment including the 
treatment of the components at the end of its useful life regardless of whether they have been 
reused. The current  Directive does not define how the cost of treating ELVs and their

current State regulations, these costs are calculated from the result obtained from the 
difference between the sum of the treatment costs and the profit from the sale of its waste, 
excluding the transport of the vehicle to the center and without going into evaluating whether 
in each sector involved in recycling it could obtain negative values. In other words, in the ELV

the ATF sector, especially in vehicles older than 10 years, which represent in 2019 also 81% of

of more than 10 years that would reach an average treatment value of € -152.20/ ELV (see 
attached document "Preliminary study of ELV management costs in ATF") 
1.5.- ELV MISSING.- During the life of the Directive, clandestine activities have found  a way to 
evade the obligations arising from the regulations and which are summarized in the 
disappearance of 4 million per year of ELV in Europe. This disappearance, which is identified in 
the aforementioned report of the evaluation of the Directive, by t he fraudulent use of 
Deregistration for the export of used vehicles, and by Temporary Deregistration, the latter 
representing an incidence in Spain close to 50% of the total of deregistrations that are noted. 
(See the attached document "Report lost vehicles") 
 
 
 
1.6.- INDUSTRIAL FABRIC PROTECTION.- It is consistent for the administrations that guarantee 
the protection of the environment to plan inspection actions on authorized facilities (ATF) that 
are registered in the administrations, and that result in t he control and protection of the 
environment. However, these inspections are also an offense if we are aware that the activities 
that illegally dismantle cars that are not subject to any control and do not even exist for the 
administration as they are not registered. Let us remember that clandestine dismantlers by not

investing in facilities, equipment, or registries as established in the directive and the standards 
transposed in each member country, economically unbalance the ATF sector, and destroy the 
possibility of improving investment and achieving objectives on the reuse and about recycling . 
(They buy the ELV at a price unattainable by the ATFs and sell the components below the market 
price, and they force the ATF sector to include the transport costs of the ELV to try to compete). 
This fact causes the activities of “clandestine demantlers” to be included in the submerged

understanding that there are sufficient means and procedures for Member Countries to 
establish an annual planning for the search for clandestine activities that results in a

1.7.- ACCESO A LA INFORMACIÓN DEL PRODUCTOR.- Although in art. 8 of the Directive 
establishes the obligation for producers to provide information to ATFs to provide information 
that allows the reuse and recycling of  ELV and its components, the system available in Spain 
"IDIS" a software with basic information on the vehicles, which only serves for the 
decontamination of ELVs,  does not currently suppose an updated system that provides 
references and the necessary info rmation for both the reuse and recycling of components,  
even when we encounter problems with certain electronic components which are an encoding 
that prevents its reuse. 
1.8.- PROBLEMS FOR REUSE.- Although in the Directives we do not find any time limitati on for 
an ATF to keep the waste stored in its facility before it is delivered to an authorized manager, 
the truth is that regulations are being published in our Country that limit the temporality, in 
order to to avoid among others ; the generation of clande stine landfills, but which is not 
contemplating in these regulations the possibility of allowing longer storage periods for 
certain wastes of “complex products” to promote the reuse of their components  and the 
approach to the circular economy, when these f or technical reasons or economic cannot be 
removed from the ELV until a request or demand is generated. With technological advances, this 
problem will be increasing, due to a large number of complex products that will be incorporated 
into the market such as ELVs, which could see their ability to reuse certain components reduced, 
and achieve high reuse objectives if they are not included a text that clearly prioritizes the 
possibility of establishing exceptional storage times for ELVs (160106) in ATFs in ord er to 
promote their reuse of components. 
1.9.- VERSATILITY IN REUSE.- One of the problems for the reuse of components is the use in the 
design of vehicles of almost unique components for their models and with difficulties for the use 
of components in different brands and models. It could be interesting to study the possibility of 
improving the versatility of the components between the different models and brands, so that 
the reuse of components can be promoted.

2. PROPOSALS FOR THE MODIFICATION OF THE DIRECTIVE 
 
2.1.- REPLACEMENT OF THE DIRECTIVE BY A REGULATION.- In our country, as is the case in the 
vast majority of member countries, vehicle producers have a great power of influence in 
governments, due to their great weight in economies and jobs. We consider that there could be 
a real impossibility for governments to impose the old and new measures on producers, as they 
imply changes that woul d entail cost increases, especially at this time of transition in vehicle 
propulsion systems, and we consider that it would be more effective if said regulation were 
replaced by a European Regulation. 
2.2.- CREATION OF A EUROPEAN MONITORING GROUP .- For this or other regulations to be 
effective, we also propose the creation of a working group in the European Commission, which 
meets periodically, and which could even be held virtually (video -conferences / webinar, etc.), 
in which a representation of all the s ectors involved participate. Administrations, Producers, 
ATF, Fragmenters, to be able to detect failures and to be able to apply corrections agreed with 
the sectors. 
2.3.- DEFINE THE EXTENDED LIABILITY OF THE PRODUCER.- The lack of foresight, updating and, 
ultimately, of regulatory application, cannot be an excuse for the member countries not having 
a system that guarantees the adequate treatment of ELVs, their components or these when they 
reach the end of their shelf life after reuse, Therefore it is necessary to define in the regulations 
that the scope of the extended responsibility of the producer of the product, which includes 
the collection, transport, treatment of the ELV, as well as its components when they reach the

end of their useful life, including these after their reuse, and that Member States are allowed 
to adopt measures to avoid transferring this obligation to reusers and recyclers , such as 
allowing the application directly by the government of systems of financing systems of the 
treatment system, such as the application of an Ecotax system in the acquisition of a vehicle, to 
cover those treatment costs when they are not being covered by the producers. 
2.4.- EXPAND THE TYPOLOGY OF VEHICLES .- To prevent vehicles not included in art.  2.1 are 
dismantled out of the flow controlling ATF, it is necessary to include in the definition of vehicles 
all motor vehicles that circulate on the road , from mopeds, motorcycles, industrial and 
agricultural vehicles, and that new systems are also inclu ded e propulsion (electric vehicles, 
hybrids, CNG gas, LPG, etc.) 
 
2.5.- DEFINE WHEN AN ELV HAS NEGATIVE VALUE. - Within the obligations of the producer of 
the product, it must include taking charge of the costs of the treatment of the ELV and of the 
vehicle components, when the global calculation of the treatment is negative, but also in those 
sectors that intervene in recycling whose cost of treatment is greater than the benefit 
obtained from its management. And include within the concept of costs the tra nsport or 
transfer to the ATF. 
2.6.- PLAN DE VIGILANCIA DE DES MANTELADORES ILEGALES.- Regardless of the surveillance 
and control plan that exists for ATFs, the Member States must carry out an annual detection 
plan and actions against clandestine dismantlers, and this information must be included in the 
information that is transmitted by the Member Countries to the European Commission. 
2.7.- VERSATILITY FOR REUSE.- Producers should try to facilitate the possibility of reusing their 
components for different models and brands according to the versatility objectives that are 
agreed with the producers, and these objectives are reviewable and upgradeable. 
2.8.- ALLOW EXCEPTIONAL STORAGE TIMES. - The possibility of exempting decontaminated 
ELVs from tempo rary storage should be indicated (currently a limit of 2 years in Spain) when 
they contain reusable components in the ATF that cannot be dismantled for reasons of economic 
sustainability, until it is generated. the demand. 
2.9.- COMMUNICATION OF PRODUCER I NFORMATION TO ATF .- A review mechanism should 
be established to ensure that ATFs are being duly informed by the producer, and that such 
information reaches the facilities, whether or not they are included in the Extended 
Responsibility System.

As established in Royal Decree 1383/2002, of December 20, the Authorized Treatment 
Centers (ATFs) of End -of-Life Vehicles (ELVs) are the only facilities capable of receiving, 
decontaminating and eliminating ELV .  
 
Royal Decree 20/2017, of Januar y 20, reinforces this obligation, establishing in its 
article 7.3 a) second paragraph the following: 
 
"The extraction of parts and components for their preparation for reuse and marketing 
can only be carried out in an ATF ...". 
 
During the  validity of the previous Royal Decree, the controlled decontamination 
processes of the ELVs carried out by the authorized treatment centers (ATF) have guaranteed 
the correct extraction of hazardous waste from vehicles and the disassembly of their 
components in perfect con dition (reusable parts ), tasks that were complemented by the 
telematic annotation of the definitive deregistrations of vehicles in the registry of the General 
Directorate of Traffic (DGT). Instruction 16 / V -122 of the General Directorate  of Traffic 
extended the obligation to deal in an ATFs, in order to process their definitive deregistration, 
to other vehicles (mopeds, industrial, agricultural, etc.) that were not included in the RD 
1383/2002. 
 
The companies and their facilities authorized as hazardous w aste managers ELVs, 
called ATFs, are the only ones that have the necessary authorizations and adequate facilities 
for the reception and treatment of  vehicles, the management of the waste they contain, the 
annotation of deregistration final in the DGT registry and the sale of reused parts.

In Andalusian Association of Dismantlers, which brings together more than 130 ATFs, 
we have observed how the number of vehicles treated in our facilities has progressively 
decreased. At the same time, we appreciate t he proliferation of advertisements on the 
internet where vehicle parts are offered by the same businessman, without the latter having 
the authorization as ATFs. 
 
These clandestine dismantlers hide their illicit activity in facilities that do not have 
adequate c onditions for the decontamination of ELVs, and offer vehicle owners temporary 
deregistration in the DGT registry . The registration of the temporary deregistration stops 
generating obligations in terms of road taxes for the owner of the vehicle. But th e temporary 
deregistration is foreseen so that the vehicle returns to circulation in a certain time, not so 
that the vehicle is disassembled and sold for parts in a way that makes it impossible to return 
to circulation. It is therefore a fraudulent use of temporary deregistration. 
 
During the treatment of ELVs made by ATFs, hazardous waste such as oils, antifreeze, 
etc. are first removed, and then reusable parts such as radiators, engines, braking systems, 
etc. are removed. The competent Ministry of Andalusia (CMAOT) regularly inspects these ATFs 
and they are obliged to submit annual reports of the waste manager and producer detailing 
the amounts of waste produced and the authorized managers who have taken charge of 
them. 
 
Clandestine dismantlers proceed in a very di fferent way, directly disassembling 
reusable parts and dumping hazardous waste from ELVs in an uncontrolled manner, since they 
neither have the adequate facilities to follow the correct procedure nor can they direct said 
waste to authorized managers to collect it. and direct them to the treatment that corresponds 
to them. In effect, as clandestine dismantlers do not have authorization as a PR manager, they 
cannot hire authorized managers to collect these hazardous waste for them as they would be 
in breach of their obligations and, consequently, they are dumping them uncontrollably.  Thus, 
according to Art. 146.1 h of Law 7/2007, of July 9, it is considered a serious offense:

We are very concerned about the fate of these hazardous waste, among which is the 
uncontrolled dumping and the release of greenhouse gases into the atmosphere. 
 
Clandestine dismantlers also do not offer guarantees in the custody of vehicle 
documentation, which can have an impact on the proliferation of document fraud activities or 
cases of document theft for various illegal purposes and on an international scale. 
 
From the economic point of view, these clandestine dismantlers represent an 
insurmountable unfair competition for ATFs, because they escape any economic obligation, 
clandestinely exercising the dismantling of vehicles, and escaping any control by th e 
authorities by not appear in any registry. This advantage allows them to invest in the purchase 
of vehicles and also in the sale of extracted parts at a price that is out of the market for any 
ATF, and is destroying the industrial fabric. 
 
In terms of Prevention of Occupational Risks and in relation to measures against fires, 
it happens in the same way. Cl andestine dismantlers completely escape the control of Law 
31/1995, of November 8, on Occupational Risk Preventi on and Royal Decree 2267/2004 of 
December 3, which approves the Fire Regulations, so they not only put endanger their own 
safety, but also the people who are near their facilities. 
 
The same breach should be expected in relation to the fiscal and tax oblig ations of 
these clandestine facilities, as they cannot prove the sale of reused parts. 
 
Finally, we want to point out the violation of consumer rights, both for those cases in 
which the owner hands over his vehicle to the clandestine dismantler thinking that it is an ATF 
and that the definitive deregistration has been noted in the DGT registry. As for those 
consumers who purchase a second -hand component in these clandestine scrap yards that do 
not offer guarantees, causing fraud by selling defective items w ithout the possibility of filing 
any claim.

We must be aware of the chan ge that waste has undergone as our technologies have 
evolved, which without entering into dangerousness, has changed the typology of waste from 
being simple objects and substances to incorporating complex waste, from the manufacture 
of products made up of various components (ELV / WEEE), whose products when they reach 
the end of their useful life, implies the obligation to reuse the components in order to avoid 
the environmental impact of the manufacture of those same components and get closer to 
the circular economy. 
 
As stated in Directive 53/2000 / CE itself, it is those who manufacture or import them 
to a member country who are responsible for their products and components when they 
reach the end of their useful life, although in the case of Spain there is  no has officially 
registered an Expanded Responsibility System (EPR). 
 
 
 
 
All the costs of the management and treatment of ELVs and their components due to 
the lack of Expanded Responsibility Systems in Spain have fallen since 2003 on rec yclers, who 
have had to pay and financially support their companies from the sale of waste and 
components with positive value that could be obtained in the treatment. However, this 
support is no longer possible. 
 
From AAD we have been observing in recent yea rs, how ATF costs have increased in 
waste management (oil, glass, tires, etc.) and how the benefits obtained from the sale of 
reusable components have been reduced and the sale of the metal fraction , due to unfair 
competition from clandestine scrap yards a nd the constant drop in the price of scrap metal. 
Likewise, precisely in order to try to combat the scourge of illegal activity, the ATFs must 
assume the cost of the transfer of the ELV to the center and increase the cost of acquisition 
of the ELV.  
 
All this is causing in the ATF sector that citizens deliver ELVs to clandestine 
dismantlers, or that centers have to allocate the profits obtained from the sale of the most 
modern ELV components to sustain the costs of treating the ELV of 10 years on, causing losse s

Recently in Spain Royal Decree 731/2020, of August 4 , has been published (published 
in the BOE Friday, August 7, 2020) this regulation is drawn up in order to cover the costs of 
collection and treatment of End of Life Tires that are generated in flows other than the new 
spare tires which do have their own EPR (Signus, ..).  As it is understood, the end -of-life tires 
that are generated after reuse in AT Fs do not have an EPR and therefore a collection and 
treatment system, which are being paid for by the EPR of new spare parts causing economic 
imbalances, and in order to avoid this, the obligation is established for ATFs that do not 
mount second-hand tires to register in the product producer register , and to adhere to the 
EPR of the new spare part that implies the imposition of a new eco -tax on the used tire 
before sale . This situation is aggravating the reuser and making the reused producer more 
expensive, in addition to clearly applying to the recycler an obligation that clearly falls to the 
vehicle producer, assuming a future risk when more ELV components begin to present 
negative value. 
 
The lack of an EPR for vehicles and their components in Spain that cov ers the costs of 
treating ELVs and their components after reuse is causing a situation of economic 
unsustainability in ATFs. 
 
 
 
 
It is a fact, the great weight that vehicle producers reach in the political decisio ns of 
the member countries, and the difficulty that the countries may be encountering for the 
correct transposition of the directives, therefore one of the measures that we consider may 
help to the creation of the appropriate EPRs would be the modification  of the Directive 
through a European Regulation. 
 
The clarification and definition of the Extended Producer Responsibility , which 
includes the obligation of the producer to pay for the collection and treatment of the 
vehicle and its components, including these after reuse. 
 
Include within the  planning of the EPR the annual calculation for the financing of the 
system in which the costs of transferring the ELV to the ATF are included, and also the 
obligation to take charge of the treatment costs when independently that the total value is

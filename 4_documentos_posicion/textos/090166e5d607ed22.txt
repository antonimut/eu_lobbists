 
 		CEWEP	feedback	on	End-of-life	vehicles	–	revision	of	EU	rules		CEWEP	welcomes	the	Commission's	new	initiative	on	the	Revision	of	Directive	2000/53/EC	on	end-of-life	vehicles,	in	order	to	update	this	current	legislation	and	to	improve	its	coherence	with	the	aims	of	the	green	deal	and	the	circular	economy	action	plan.	CEWEP	would	like	to	highlight	the	importance	to	maintain	a	holistic	approach	and	to	strike	the	right	balance	 between	 policy	 goals	 (e.g.	zero	 pollution	 and	 circular	 economy).	 This	 balance	 is	 extremely	important	 to	 ensure	 that	 the	 overall	 aim	 of	 tackling	 environmental,	 health	 and	 climate	 impacts	 is	achieved,	 avoiding	 the	 risk	 of	 undermining	 the	 overarching	 sustainability	objectives	included	 in	 the	Green	Deal.		We	would	like	to	highlight	the	following	for	the	foreseen	review:	• Vehicles	are	made	of	various	material	fractions	and	different	types	of	waste	streams	are	extracted	from	them	at	the	end	of	their	life.	These	streams	should	undergo	treatments	that	are	in	line	with	the	whole	waste	treatment	hierarchy,	e.g.	not	excluding	energy	recovery	when	waste	streams	are	not	suitable	for	reuse	or	recycling.	The	integrated	approach	helps	to	avoid	any	harm	to	the	environment	or	human	health,	in	accordance	with	the	ambitions	of	a	non-toxic	environment.	• When	assessing	the	possible	improvements	of	recycling	specific	waste	streams	such	as	plastics,	it	is	important	that	recycled	content	calculations	be	based	on	solid	methodologies	and	not	theoretical	allocations	that	do	not	provide	sufficient	trust	in	green	claims.	• Definitions	contained	in	the	ELVD	should	be	adapted	to	be	consistent	with	the	Waste	Framework	Directive,	e.g.	recycling	should	exclude	production	of	fuel.	Brussels,	19th	November	2020									Contact	:	info@cewep.eu;	www.cewep.eu	CEWEP,	 Confederation	 of	 European	 Waste-to-Energy	 Plants,	 is	 the	 umbrella	 association	 of	 the	operators	of	Waste-to-Energy	(incineration	with	energy	recovery)	plants,	representing	about	410	plants	from	 23	 countries.	 They	 make	 up	 80%	 of	 the	 Waste-to-Energy	 capacity	 in	 Europe.	 Its	 members	 are	committed	to	ensuring	high	environmental	standards,	achieving	low	emissions	and	maintaining	state	of	the	art	energy	production	from	remaining	waste	that	cannot	be	recycled	in	a	sustainable	way.

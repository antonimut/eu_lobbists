 
 
 
 
   
 
Contribution to EU Commission’s 
Roadmap to Review of the ITS Directive 
  
The Volkswagen Group welcomes the plan of the EU Commission to review the ITS Directive 
in regard to its effectiveness and its goals in order to harmonize and coordinate the deployment 
of respective technologies in Europe and appreciates the opportunity to  provide input  on the 
policy options spelled out in the Inception Impact Assessment.  
 
We share the Commissions ’ assessment that a successful uptake in deployment has the 
potential to improve road safety significantly, traffic efficiency and comfort by helping transport 
users to take the right decisions and adapt to the traffic situation. Hence, the number of fatal 
accidents can be reduced. Moreover, it will contribute to the decarbonization of the road 
transport sector by reducing negative externalities such as congestion, air pollution, and CO2 
emissions. Thus, a well-designed review has the potential to make a significant contribution to 
the Commission priorities, in particular the European Green Deal, Vision Zero and for making 
Europe fit for the digital age. 
 
For a successful deployment update, the Volkswagen Group encourages the Commission to 
consider the following points. 
 
C-ITS 
C-ITS services based on communication between vehicles and between vehicles and 
infrastructure can only contribute to improved traffic safety in case of substantial proliferation.  
This also means t hat future legislation has to  ensure interoperability and backward -
compatibility on application layer. Backward compatibility on lower layers will block progress 
and does not allow for exploring the full potential of technology evolution. 
Although the delegated regulation has not been ratified by the Council of the European Union, 
ongoing work by various institutions, especially with respect to the establishment of an EU trust 
domain in the form of the C-ITS Security and Certificate Policies  places Europe ahead of all 
other markets for C-ITS-based safety applications. However, there is still a need to accelerate 
deployments to maintain this position.   
The Volkswagen Group is committed to the goal of increasing road safety and traffic efficiency 
by means of C -ITS. This is why every  Volkswagen Golf 8 and every vehicle of  our recently 
introduced fully electric ID model family sold in Europe is equipped with ITS-G5 as standard – 
not as an option. This deployment is just the beginning, with more models and brands of the 
Volkswagen Group to follow. 
The vehicles follow the specifications and requirements of the aforementioned C-ITS Security 
and Certificate Policies.  In addition, our vehi cles offer ITS services based on backend 
infrastructure via cellular networks. However, technology continuously evolves and improves.

Consortia like the Car2Car Communication Consortium, 5GAA, and C-Roads have taken over 
the essential responsibility to pro gress basic ETSI specifications by releasing commonly 
accepted and followed standard profiles . A regular publication of an updated specification 
baseline through the EU Commission will help in establishing common grounds for generating 
continuity of service and interoperability.  
 
C-ITS Road Infrastructure & Cooperation amongst stakeholders 
Our deployment of ITS -G5 initially focuses on high -volume models of the compact class 
segment to ensure access to the technology and its benefits to a large customer base. 
However, ITS -G5 services based on vehicle -to-vehicle communications is only half of the 
story. The full benefit of the technology will be exploited when intelligent infrastructure 
components are also able to communicate with vehicles. We therefore strongly encourage the 
EU Commission to continue and expand its efforts in accelerating the deployment and 
equipment of C-ITS roadside stations in all EU member states. Currently, Austria and Germany 
are working on the deployment of ITS-G5 equipped roadworks warning trailers in 2020 / 2021.  
For the VW Golf 8 and ID models, infrastructure-based services such as the roadworks warning 
are already available but currently lack a sufficient number of communication partners on the 
infrastructure side.  
From our perspective, an EU -wide gradual equipment of newly purchased infrastructure 
components with ITS-G5 technology would be highly welcomed and encourages other vehicle 
manufacturers to also commence with their ITS -G5 deployment. As roadside infrastruc ture 
needs to be replaced or renewed on a regular basis, an EU directive or financial funding which 
would ensure the availability of intelligent roadside ITS-G5 stations would be highly welcomed 
and indicate commitment to a connected smart and safe mobility.  
In addition, the  announcement and presence of traffic control and improvement measures 
(e.g., route closures or roadwork locations ), as well as , digital HD maps should be made 
available via backend -based services, e.g., the National Access Points . Roadwork vehicles 
and equipment, as well as information about variable road signage and traffic sign bridges 
should be equipped with direct communication capabilities. 
   
C-ITS Interoperability 
It is expected that new and improved services as well as new communication technologies are 
added over time. To ensure interoperability between already deployed and future ITS services, 
the Volkswagen Group proposes to bind applications to dedicated technologies (e.g., direct 
communication via ITS-G5 or 5G-V2X, cellular and backend-based). Clear decision criteria for 
the selection of technologies ensure interoperability, high data quality, continuity of services, 
and innovation, while at the same time respecting incumbent deployed services.  
However, when not  binding certain applications to existing or upcoming fully backward 
compatible technologies, the issue of interoperability cannot be addressed . A clear 
 
 
 
 
   
 
commitment to use specific technologies for selected use cases would thereby ensure 
innovation, as it allows and encourages for competition among technologies for new use-cases 
without hindering investments in the deployment of already existing use-cases. 
 
Our remarks from above address the two key problem drivers “lack of interoperability and 
continuity of applications” as well as “lack of concentration and effective cooperation” as stated 
in your “Inception Impact Assessment” Document: For C-ITS safety applications, we focus on 
technologies providing high availability and reliability as well as low -latency communications 
such as ITS-G5 and 5G-V2X for future applications. A framework (e.g., of regulatory nature, 
binding guidelines, financial funding, or a mix of  these measures), that binds applications to 
current and upcoming fully backward compatible technologies  would provide a basis for 
interoperability and continuity of service . Such framework would also need to incorporate a 
transition path that would allow the existence of multiple technologies offering the same 
services for the time of a defined transition pe riod in case new communication technologies 
prevail in the future. 
 
C-ITS Spectrum 
The unsolved issue about technology usage within the 5.9  GHz frequency will block 
investments in the future. As long as there is no clear guideline on how (i.e., based on which 
technology) infrastructure components such as traffic lights should make data available, public 
authorities and private investments will be kept back, to lower the risk of choosing the wrong 
technology.  We propose a binding mechanism in accordance to above for road infrastructure 
manufacturers and providers. 
 
A key step in the availability of spectrum for C-ITS has been achieved with the adoption of the 
ECC Decision (08)01 and the corresponding EC Implementing Decision  2020/1426, just 
adopted and published. We consider that having 40 MHz of spectrum available, clearly favors 
the further roll-out of safety related applications, beyond the day-1 services already offered by 
our vehicles as stated above. Today, one channel is in use, but we expect other cha nnels to 
start being used for complex interactions (talking to infrastructure sensors and between cars) 
and cooperative maneuvers, including platooning. Work to this extend is ongoing in ETSI STF 
MCO and in the Car2Car  Communication Consortium. For these s ervices to exist and be 
deployed in mass, again, interoperability is of key importance, as well as preserving 
compatibility to already deployed services. It is key in this aspect to keep the spectrum 
technology neutral and to mandate ETSI to work on backwa rd compatible solutions, thereby 
guaranteeing a clear technology evolution path, as well as service continuity, particularly for 
day-1 services, over the lifetimes of vehicles and fleets. Such an approach equally ensures fair 
access to spectrum, as well as enabling vehicle manufacturers and road infrastructure eco -
systems to plan products and application launches in a cooperative and interoperable fashion.  
 
 
 
 
   
 
Relating to technology evolution, we note that both, the evolution of the ITS-G5 standard, i.e., 
802.11bd as well as 5G -V2X, show promising aspects for enabling advanced driving use 
cases. Pending perfecting and testing of these technologies, a regulatory reflection could touch 
upon allowing these technologies to access the non-safety part of the ITS Spectrum, e.g., 5855 
MHz – 5875 MHz. We support such discussions to be undertaken in the framework of ETSI 
TC ITS and CEPT WG FM, however , we respectfully recommend that the current regime for 
the safety part of the ITS Band (5875 MHz – 5915 MHz) remains untouched and allocated to 
safety applications. 
 
C-ITS Security & Privacy 
Security, Privacy and Trust are key topics for connected services. A central coordination of a 
European Trust Domain helps to ensure progress and enables improvements for the future. 
Nevertheless, the process needs to consider day-1 services and existing deployments 
differently than day -2 services or new developments and must accommodate ongoing 
deployments.  
Volkswagen is fully committed to the principals and goals of the GDPR and produces compliant 
products. However, there is one major issue with GDPR requirements and compliantly 
deployed C-ITS safety applications: without a separate legal basis for, e.g., C-ITS safety and 
efficiency applications, following GDPR article 6 1(c), such services have to be “off by default”. 
This is a major roadblock for high availability of C-ITS traffic safety and efficiency applications 
as our experience shows.  
We therefore propose to create a list of approved C -ITS safety and efficiency applications as 
well as according privacy protection mechanisms to be in place , to allow to turn such service 
“on by default” and “off by choice”. By solving this issue one way or another, we expect higher 
rates of enabled especially safety critical applications as well a s organizations like EU NCAP 
and Green NCAP to accelerate the availability of C -ITS by considering V2X within the NCAP 
rating by 2024.  
 
In general , Volkswagen does not support the idea of security certification schemes like 
Common Criteria for vehicle par ts, as they may be a potential driver of regulation 
fragmentation. Volkswagen does support the risk based approach of UNECE regulation R155 
and ISO21434, but we see limited additional benefit by general usage of certification schemes 
and supports the concerns raised in ISO TC22/SC32/WG11 “Contribution to the second Study 
Period (SP) of ISO/IEC JTC1/SC27/WG3 (WG3) on Evaluation Criteria for Connected Vehicle 
Information Security based on ISO/IEC 15408”. 
Volkswagen nevertheless acknowledges that for C-ITS services there is no commonly agreed, 
available alternative to Common Criteria or ENISA’s EUCC for the time being. 
 
 
 
 
 
   
 
C-ITS Compliance 
We welcome any framework that  ensures interoperability and continuity of products and 
services. As the lack of interoperability is one of the identified key problem drivers, there is a 
need to establish frameworks for self - or third party certification not only of security aspects, 
but of functional aspects as well. Such framework would enable as well misbehavior detection 
(MBD): without a proper framework to ensure that only secure and functional compliant devices 
are enrolled in the EU trust domain, the important MBD approach of detecting wrongful data in 
the C-ITS network would not have a baseline. The lack of such framework can only be tolerated 
at the moment, as the front-runners are still able to ensure interoperability of functional aspects 
and the respective security measures in bilateral interactions by their own efforts. This concept 
is not sustainable and will fail, once C-ITS deployments are accelerated across the EU. 
 
European eCall 
The current situation with a regulation in place that requires vehicle manufacturers to 
implement the EU eCall using 2G/3G technology, while at the same time leaving open the 
availability of respective mobile networks, is unsustainable: The mobile network operators 
(MNOs) across Europe have set dates for when they will switch off their 2G and 3G networks 
rendering the EU eCall non -functional. A solution to this issue must address at least the 
following two points at the same time: 
1. The European eCall needs rather sooner than later an updated regulation that allows 
vehicle types to implement Next Generation eCall (NG eCall) standards to proof 
compliance at type approval. An updated regulation should also regulate the usage of 
FRAND terms for standard essential IP in NG eCall standards. 
2. A solution for the ever-increasing fleet of legacy vehicles is required: a legacy network 
must be operated in each EU member state for the lifetime of these compliant vehicles. 
The second point should be address in a similar way for future generations of mobile network 
technologies and future eCall standards. 
 
Vehicle Data Sharing 
Any regulatory effort in the area of vehicle data sharing should aim at creating a level playing 
field between all stakeholders of the data economy. I ndustry-specific regulations would 
counteract the emerging convergent developments in the market. 
It is fundamental for security and safety reasons that in -vehicle data, when mandated, is 
exported by Ex/Ve ISO standards ISO 20077 -1 and ISO 20077 -2 through a vehicle 
manufacturer backend.  
On the specific issue relating to the availability and sharing of data supporting ITS services, 
Volkswagen calls on the Commission to take into account the activities of DG CNCT on the 
establishment of common and sectoral d ata spaces. By creating common rules standards, 
architectures and incentives to share data voluntarily, data availability will increase – also for 
 
 
 
 
   
 
ITS services. Rather than acting prematurely, we would like to suggest letting these initiatives 
take up speed first. 
 
SRTI & RTTI 
To ensure proper continuity for the deployment and operational use of Intelligent Transport 
Systems, the European legislator has established a differentiation between the safety-related 
traffic information and real -time traffic information. It is important  to highlight that any future 
revision should be aligned and preclude overlap of real-time traffic information processing with 
the processing of safety -related traffic information rules as set in Commission Delegated 
Regulation (EU) No 886/2013. The Data Task Force was established with the objective of 
engaging the ITS Stakeholders to solve common problems and has resulted in the creating of 
Multi Party Agreement. However, a lack of clarity on regulatory obligations for ITS stakeholders 
could result in the critical mass not being reached and can hinder the effectiveness of the 
implementation. 
 
National Access Points 
All EU member states should ensure speedy introduction of N ational Access Points and 
address key issues like standardized data formats or binding minimum  Service Level 
Agreements (SLAs) . An integration in strategic efforts like the “ Common Data Spaces 
Framework“ leading to pan -European coordination and supervisory  mechanism and the 
creation of a Mobility Data Space. 
 
 
Conclusion 
In order to ensure further successful deployments of ITS services on EU roads, we would like 
to ask the Commission to consider kindly the above-mentioned aspects. We remain available 
for any further technical questions.

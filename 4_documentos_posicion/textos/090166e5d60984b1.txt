Position Paper 
Revision of the End of Life Vehicles Directive 
Public Feedback Consultation on the Inception Impact Assessment – Ares (2020)5755999 
Publication date: 19 November 2020 
The European Steel Association (EUROFER) welcomes the review of the Directive 2000/53/EC 
on End-of-Life Vehicles (ELVs). The Directive has contributed effectively to the improvement of 
the management of waste generated by discarded vehicles (ELVs) and to  the establishment of 
ELVs recycling. However, the review of the Directive is actually desirable because the political 
landscape and the EU’s main goals have changed substantially compared to 2000.  
The launch of the EU Green Deal and the objective of tran sforming the EU into a resource 
efficient and circular economy mean the current Directive is not completely fit for these and 
some changes and improvements are necessary. 
The release of the Inception Impact Assessment (IIA) of the ELVs Directive covers mos t of the 
aspects that need to be fine-tuned or modified and most of the problems to be tackled.  
However, EUROFER has mixed views about the effectiveness of some  of the proposed policy 
options, and has concrete propos als to help  achieve the objective of th e IIA . Moreover, it is 
important to recall that the implementation of the solutions is the key passage in the revision of 
an EU directive. Every modification has to be accurately evaluated to ensure that it will work 
effectively towards desired goals and p revent or reduc e trade-offs to the greatest possible 
extent.  
Vehicle design is presently confronted with the need to reduce its Greenhouse Gases (GHGs) 
emissions and with the need to ensure the  high-quality recycling of ELVs. If the trade -offs 
between these two trends are not reduced, finding an optimum, the review of the Directive will 
not lead to the attainment of EU goals or could even end up working against them. The revision 
process of the ELVs Directive needs to have the following key objectives: 
1. to transform the ELVD into a Circularity enabler , promoting and ensuring design for 
recyclability of new vehicles and their parts;  
2. to measure the recycling target over the entire vehicle , rather than fixing specific per 
material targets; this will help keeping flexibility in vehicle design and reaching an 
optimum without big trade-offs between climate and circularity objectives;

EUROFER’s comments on the policy options  and objectives of the IIA and the extensive 
description of previo us key objectives  are reported below . They have been drafted to 
contribute positively to the revision of this important piece of EU legislation and to support the 
ambition to make the EU truly circular and resource efficient. 
Improve the implementation and enforcement of the ELV Directive 
1. The issue of ‘missing vehicles’ in the EU represents the leakage of valuable resources for 
the industries and it does not allow  for the closing of the loop of the material s used for 
making such vehicles.  These missing vehicles represent almost 35% of all ELVs annually 
generated. EUROFER supports the proposed action of releasing guidance documents 
and drafting legal acts. However, these actions should be substantiated taking into 
account the following measures: 
a. the member states (MSs) should monitor more systematically exports of ELVs 
and should reasonably ensure that ELVs are recycled at standards/con ditions 
equivalent to EU ones (l inkage between Waste Shipment Regulation (WSR) and 
ELVs Directive); 
b. the cer tificate of destruction (CoD) should b e mandatory for every 
scrapped/shredded ELV and this certificate should have the same format and be 
electronic for all MSs; 
c. the de -registration/registration and temporary de -registration procedures, and 
their associated information should be harmonised across all MSs. 
2. The IIA seeks high quality recycling of ELVs and to create an EU level playing field across 
Member States. EUROFER supports this. Thus, first of all it is essential to have an aligned 
method of measuring r e-use, recycling and recovering  of ELVs and their parts and 
material fractions . It is essential not only to correctly assess the performance of the 
Directive but also to check whether the EU will be on track relative to its  circular and 
green deal goals. The following actions in this respect are proposed: 
d. consistency of collected data is a fundamental requirement; for instance, the 
calculation of the tonnages of the vehic les fleet registered in each MS should be

A targeted modification of the ELV Directive 
3. The release of new EU Regulations and Directives and the updated/modernisation of 
existing ones oblige the adaptation of the ELVs Directive . However, this adaptation 
should not distract from the final goal of this revision. 
4. The introduction of specific targets for reuse h as been proposed , with more ambitious 
targets for recycling per material. Although EUROFER supports having ambitious target s 
and supports higher levels of the waste hierarchy, at the same time it cannot support 
the proposal of the EU Commission  of splitting the targets per material . In the following 
points there is the explanation in detail: 
g. the recycling target of ELVs should  be measured on the entire vehicle and not 
per material; switching from measuring the recycling performance of the vehicle 
to the measurement of the recycling performance per material will put at serious 
risk the effectiveness of the ELV. This will become even more the case because of 
the trend towards automotive electrification. 
An important share of the total weight of Battery Electric Vehicles (BEVs) is their 
batteries, and they also have a tendency to have greater plastic content than 
current models. This composition makes the recycling performance of BEVs 
significantly lower than more prevalent  Internal Combustion Engine (ICE) 
vehicles, due to  the recycling yields of batteries (much lower than 100% ; e.g. 
recycling yield at 50% for Li -Ion- and at 65% for  Pb- batteries) and low plastic 
recyclability. Figure 1 shows how the different type of power train and the 
different recycling yields combined with the BoMs of different vehicle types can 
heavily and negatively affect vehicle circularity  (in orange it is reported ELV 
recycling rate; in blue the real recycling rate).

In the past , setting-up recovery and recycling targets for the entire vehicle has 
proven to be a very effective option while splitting this target into different 
material (or part) specific targets will block any trend of optimisation of the 
vehicle design and recyclab ility. Recovery and recycling targets measured on the 
entire vehicle will ensure more flexibility during the design phase and help in 
reducing trade-offs between EU policies on ELV s that focus on different aspects 
(i.e. CO2 emission of vehicles; recovery and recycling of ELVs). 
h. The parts of the vehicle collected during the de -pollution steps and not recycled 
yet have not to be reported in the ELV recycling rate unless having factorised in 
the recycling yields (e.g. batteries), if any; 
i. Information about the recycling performance per material or per parts of the ELV 
could be reported as additional information, necessary to assess the different 
factors contributing to the circularity of vehicles. This would help the automotive 
industries in improving the design of vehicles. 
5. It is important to create conditions that facilitate the depollution and treatment 
operations of ELVs. The operators , and more in general waste treatment companies , 
need to receive the information necessary to disassemble the largest number o f 
valuable parts before the shredding process. 
6. ELVs Directive needs to be updated in order to harmonise its text . Annex II in particular  
requires work to align it with other EU legislation de aling with hazardous substances.

safely and efficiently , even  if it involves with hazardous substances. The restriction in

7. EUROFER supports the establishment of minimum criteria for the inspection of ATFs. 
The ELVs treatment and depollution  step needs to be much more regulated .  This will 
happen through the switch of most part of (if  not all) facilities into ATFs. The process is 
already ongoing because the upcoming entering into force of the EU Waste Treatment 
BREF will impose environmental and quality requirements on waste treatment and 
shredding installations. Therefore, ATFs and th eir management systems can contribute 
to a higher quality of ELVs recycling via the following measures: 
j. ATFs reporting requirements on the materials and parts collected and stored 
before the shredding process of the ELV; 
k. ATFs reporting requirements about t he output of the shredding processes in 
terms of segregation of different fractions sent then to different material 
recycling value chains (e.g. steel, plastic, non-ferrous metals…) 
l. ATFs obligation to issue CoD for each destroyed ELV; 
m. the de -pollution and treatment operations of an ATFs, before the shredding 
process, need to be more specific on the list of parts to be remov ed during the 
depollution phase and before the shredding process; 
n. in order to improve the quality of the materials coming out from the ELV 
shredding process, it is advisable to require the installation of Post -Shredding 
Technologies (PST) for enhancing a better segregation of different materials; 
o. the shredding campaigns on ELVs at ATFs should be carried out using normal 
operating conditions of the shredding equipment; moreover, the ELVs shredding 
operations should be carried out, to the extent possible, without mixing ELVs 
with other EoL products, for avoiding cross-contamination. 
Most of the measures on ATFs mentioned above are already included in the BAT 2 of the 
Implementing Decision 2018/1147/EU and they will help in creating  a win-win synergy. 
ATFs will become a crucial step for ensuring the true circularity of ELVs in the EU; they 
will help in  closing data gaps and ensuring the necessa ry conditions for the next high-
quality recycling steps.  
In addition, the proposed measures will help ATFs in increasing revenues from the 
highest quality fractions and  in collecting more valuable materials out of steel for 
instance. Moreover, the reporting requirements will be instrumental if policy makers are 
to monitor vehicle design trends , and for the  automotive industry to improve its 
circularity and resource efficiency.

An overhaul of the ELV Directive 
8. EUROFER looks at the extension of the Directive scope to cover other EU fleets , such as 
trucks and motorbikes , positively. Putting these two streams into the scope can help in 
improving EoL management and potentially contribute to more recycling in the EU. 
9. ELVs Directive had positive contributions to waste management and to the EU recycling 
value chain of ELVs. However, it is clear that it has not succeeded in improving vehicle 
design, i. e. design for higher quality recycling/circular design . Thus, EUROFER fully 
supports the introduction of product requirements focusing on making vehicle design 
more circular. 
Electrification trend s, light -weighting and more stringent emission limits, focused only 
on mitigating climate change, can negatively influence car recyclability and r esource 
efficiency. Moreover, the trade -off between the very high CO2 intensity of light -
materials and their extremely low, if not zero, recyclability should not be 
underestimated. Therefore, ELV should work as an instrument to reduce trade-offs 
between li ght-weighting/electrification and recycling and re -balancing the design 
process towards an optimum outcome. 
10. The proposal to set up the requirement for the mandatory use of recycled plastic in new 
cars could have a positive outcome as long as it is applied only to plastic. The content of 
‘recyclates’ in materials and products strongly depends on the dynamic (or existence) of  
a market for secondary raw materials and on the properties of the specif ic material 
under consideration. 
This requirement for plastic makes sense as an instrument to increase the demand for 
recycled plastic and to support the improvement of its quality. On the contrary for 
metals, and for steel in particular, the situation is reversed  because the demand for 
ferrous scrap always outpaces the supply and metals preserve their inherent properties 
after the recycling processes (if recycling value chain works properly ), while this is not 
yet the case for plastics. 
However, the recyclability should also be ensured f or parts containing ‘recyclates’. 
Otherwise, after the first recycling cycle the material has to be incinerated or landfilled. 
Ensuring recyclability is a more powerful instrument for creating market s for secondary 
raw materials and ensuring the high-quality recycling of ELVs. 
11. The IIA proposes to use Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) schemes for ELVs in 
order to increase the responsibility on car manufacturers. However, it is worth stressing 
that the first scope of an EPR is to ensure the functioning and the effectiveness of the 
recycling value chain of the involved products. 
Thus, the responsibilities should be shared along the value chain among all the actors 
involved. Moreover, the EPR schemes work imposing a fee on the product when placed 
on the mar ket. This fee needs to be eco -modulated in the sense that OEMs and car-

FERVER, the European Federation of Glass Recyclers, is an International Non -Profit Organization, created in 
2004 under Belgian law by private companies active in the collection and recycling of glass. 
 
The federation currently gathers 40 members from 14 EU -countries and 4 non -EU-countries, active in the 
recycling of glass waste - both flat glass and packaging glass - into high quality cullet - a key and valuable raw 
material used by  the glass manufacturi ng industry to produce new products. Th ose members are jointly 
responsible for the recycling of approximately 70% of the total packaging glass waste collected in the 
European Union. 
 
The objective of this document  is to give FERVER’s position on the roadmap for the revision o f the Directive 
2000/53/EC on end-of-life vehicles. 
 
Context of glass in vehicles 
Knowing that glass represents 3 % of ve hicles, this means that e very car contains around 25 kg of glass . As 
EU generates around 12 million ELVs every year, this represents 300kt of glass waste. Just as an example, in 
the Netherlands, glass remains in the wrecks and ends up in the residual mineral fraction. As a result, 4.000 
tonnes of glass are lost every year.  
 
Position of the glass recycling sector on the roadmap 
First of all, we are aware of the problem of illegal exports of end-of-life vehicles and we are in favour of this 
as a major point in the revision of the ELVs Directive. It is important to ensure that ELVs are collected and 
treated mostly inside the EU. 
 
However, in the European ELVs Directive, there are currently no obligations regarding glass recycling. That is 
why an obligation specific to the recycling of glass from ELVs should also be a major point of the revision for 
our glass recycling sector.  
 
Article 7 point 2 (b) of the EL Vs Directive1 only encourages Member States to take the necessary measures 
for the reuse, recycling, or recovery of components. It promotes to achieve an objective of 95% for recovery 
and 85% for recycling by 2015 . Knowing that glass represents 3% of vehic les, there is a gap for glass loss. 
Moreover, the point 4 of annex I is also only about promoting min imum requirements for the treatment of 
end-of-life vehicles.

Why is it important to add a mandatory objective specific to recycling of glass from ELVs? 
First of all, the roadmap specifies objectives to increase a level playing field for high quality recycling . This 
means that car glass should be collected separately at source and before shredding to be recycled at a high-
quality level. If this is collected separately,  it c an be processed sustainably. T his mean that glass can be 
recycled, saves energy, raw materials and then reduces CO2 emissions. Recovery of glass does not generate 
such energy  and material  savings. Moreover, the EU legis lations plan to a dd recycled cont ent objectives. 
That is why collecting glass separately is important to meet those future objectives. 
 
Secondly, the ro admap also specifies objecti ve of waste prevention by including targets on eco-design of 
cars to facilitate reuse, repair, remanufacturing, or recycling. This point is also important as EU aims to force 
producers to ensure their cars manufactured can be easily dismantled. 
 
Finally, it would be a great idea to impose recycling rate specific to every m aterial from ELVs. The Directive 
on packaging and packaging waste, f or example, sets up recycling tar gets per material. By 2025, glass 
packaging waste has to reach 70% recycling  and 75% by 2030. The ELVs Directive should be in line with the  
European objectives

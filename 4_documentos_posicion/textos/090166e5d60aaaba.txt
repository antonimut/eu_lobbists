 
 
5GAA feedback on the roadmap for revision of intelligent transport systems EU rules  
 
 
5G Automotive Association (5GAA) welcomes the initiative of the European Commission to review the 
EU rules on intelligent transport systems (ITS) with the objective to accelerate the deployment and 
use of these systems. With the rapid uptake of 5G connectivity, internet of things (IoT) services and 
artificial intelligence -based functionalities that will re -shape our economies in the coming years, 
establishing a well -defined ITS strateg y is an essential  condition for a chieving a clean, modern and 
competitive EU transport sector.  
 
General remarks 
 
5GAA advocates for establishing a harmonised, technology-neutral regulatory framework, stimulating 
innovation and market-based technology choices related to C-ITS. Particular attention should be given 
to the coordinated deployment of cross -border ITS services to maintain the geographical continuity 
of such services throughout the EU. Such an enabling environment will be conducive to C -ITS rapid 
deployment by vehicle manufacturers and road authorities, delivering upon the EU road safety and 
climate neutrality goals. 
 
5G-V2X technology will enable fully connected and automated mobility beginning today with LTE- V2X 
technology. Vehicles and infrastructure can exchange relevant information via LTE-V2X either through 
direct communication mode, which does not require network coverage,  or mobile network 
communications mode, creating a real-time connection between all road users. The 5G revolution will 
reinforce those interactions and spark a whole new set of services, dramatically changing the mobility 
landscape. 
 
An essential share of socio-economic benefits can be derived from use cases that are enabled and/or 
supported by Vehicle-to-Network (V2N) communications along with Vehicle-to-Vehicle (V2V), Vehicle-
to-Infrastructure (V2I) and Vehicle-to-Pedestrian (V2P) connectivity. In addition to safety gains 
(reducing road accidents including vulnerable road users), these will produce more efficient transport, 
predictable travel time with minimal traffic congestion and will improve environmental footprint.  
 
Objectives of the ITS Directive Revision  
 
5GAA acknowledges that the deployment of ITS infrastructure and services a mong the Member 
States remains restricted and varies among regions. 5GAA calls for an ITS regulatory framework that 
will allow for an acceleration of the deployment and achieving the full benefits offered by the ITS 
services to increase road safety and tra ffic efficiency. The three key objectives of the revision, as 
identified in the roadmap, require careful consideration. 
 
(1) Increase interoperability and cross-border continuity of ITS applications, systems, and services 
 
5GAA believes that in today's context of fast -pace innovation where different V2X technologies co -
exist, interoperability standards should focus on the service-level (as opposed to radio-level), and non-
discriminatory requirements between existing technologies. 
 
The revised ITS rules should focus on safety performance requirements and should not overregulate 
by requiring a specific technology implementation. Technical details such as standards, specifications, 
and communication system profiles, including th ose required to ensure interoperability, should be 
adopted by European standards developing organisations (SDOs).

By having interoperable, EU-wide solutions rather than fragmented ones, there is a strong potential 
for network effects  and efficiency gains. Individual ITS services can be more easily integrated into 
combined service offerings, making the ITS market broader and more widely available. Providing legal 
certainty for OEM deployments by securing a level -playing field among existing technologies an d a 
forward path for new technologies or services is a precondition to speeding  up large scale 
deployments and, hence, realising the full potential of connected and automated mobility.  Our 
understanding is that this will inevitably lead to the emergence of a genuine EU -wide ITS services 
market allowing European businesses to compete effectively in both US and Chinese markets, where 
C-V2X has gained considerable momentum1 2. 
 
(2)  Establish effective coordination and monitoring mechanisms between all ITS stakeholders 
 
The EU needs a coherent innovation and investment -friendly regulatory framework to secure 
efficient transition toward smart and sustainable mobility. As such, the role of all stakeholders, both 
public and private, should be duly taken into consideration. 
 
EU action is specifically needed to avoid a fragmentation into national regulatory frameworks, which 
would hamper the roll -out of ITS in Europe and jeopardise the expected benefits in terms of road 
safety, emission reduction or reducing traffic congestion.  Connected and automated transport 
requires significant cooperation, on top of investments , between the private and public sector, 
without prescribing a specific technology implementation , while at the same time ensuring 
interoperability. 
 
Investments in s mart infrastructure  can be ensured by stimulating synergies of mobile network 
infrastructure and intelligent road infrastructure (e.g. RSUs) along with cohesive funding mechanisms 
(e.g. Connecting Europe Facility). On this point, 5GAA would like to add that CEF Transport needs to 
ensure funding for C -V2X in order to maximise synergies with CEF Digital around 5G CAM corridors. 
Intelligent traffic monitoring, management and control on TEN -T and other important trans port 
corridors should be an EU-wide priority, as it will enhance road safety and improve both transportation 
system efficiency and sustainability. 5GAA believes that road authorities and public road operators 
should make available high-quality road and traffic data to improve its accessibility, exchange, re-use 
and update. 
 
The COVID-19 crisis has demonstrated the current and future benefits of transportation connectivity 
and automation for society. The transport sector has played a crucial role to support the delivery of 
essential goods and services in this critical period. The EU's preparation for a potential future similar 
crisis requires building a resilient transport system recognising the importance of technology and 
innovations. 
 
(3) Solve issues related to the availability and sharing of data which support ITS services. 
 
The EU regulatory framework should be conducive to European competitiveness and foster the 
development of innovative business models. As such, the participation and added value of different

stakeholders, both public and private, should be duly taken into consideration. The EU has successfully 
developed a highly competitive market in the real -time traffic information  (RTTI) domain, in 
conjunction with the emergence of world -leading European companies.  Deploying connected 
infrastructure and ensuring open access to mobility-related data will foster efficient transport mobility 
systems and will facilitate V2X technology roll -out. As such, the access to mobility-related data in a  
highly reliable and low latency manner is crucial. 
To ensure proper continuity for the deployment and operational use of intelligent transport systems, 
the European Commission has established  a differentiation between the safety -related traffic 
information and real-time traffic information. Any future revision of the regulation should preclude 
overlap between these two categories of data processing. Also, it is essential that existing and future 
RTTI services have a corresponding business model to enable a nd maintain investment decisions by 
service providers. Lack of business  models and stable regulatory decisions would hinder 
competitiveness and innovative capabilities in Europe.  
Vehicle manufacturers invest heavily in safety and data sharing models. From the envisaged regulatory 
framework, it is without question that  vehicle data sharing should not be mandated. The current 
provisions of the ITS D irective and its delegated act on R TTI facilitate data sharing with and through 
National Access Points (NAPs) but cannot and should not be read as a mandate for the private industry 
sector.  
Considering that business models are nascent and evolving, sharing such vehicle data should remain 
on a purely  voluntary basis and in the framework of commercial contracts between vehicle 
manufacturers and other actors of the value chain. Any mandatory requirements  would inevitably 
hamper the availability and the quality of services in Europe ; it would u ltimately be detrimental to 
customers' acceptance. 
Today, the main focus should be put on improving G2B data sharing, and notably regarding mobility-
related data. Beyond availability, data accessibility should not be overlooked in any legislative 
framework.  We support ongoing endeavours for a harmonised European ITS Data Infrastructure and 
we call for a clearer definition of what a NAP stands for in order to overcome the existing EU -wide 
fragmentation and facilitate the overall accessibility of data. A pur e data depository is by no means 
comparable to a fully-fledged platform with key technical capabilities. Developing common tools for 
data exchange (i.e. standards, formats) should be a priority to make sure the data is exploitable and 
effectively used by a ny stakeholder to improve the quality of ITS Services. Investments from road 
authorities are essential to ensure the continuity of ongoing standardi sation efforts, notably with 
regards to TN-ITS and DATEX II. Also, when talking about real-time services, the whole ecosystem must 
rethink how to best provide data. Instead of solely publishing data on some kind of storage repository, 
a push service should be considered, provided that data quality and security can be ensured, in order 
to facilitate data sharing and processing in real time. Considering upcoming regulatory challenges, the 
priority should be given to critical datasets, essential for providing services such as  CCAM (e.g. static, 
dynamic, variable speed limits, roadworks, traffic lights). For all the above reasons, 5GAA is supportive 
of the integration of NAPs in the upcoming Common European Dataspace for mobility, with the aim 
to increase the outreach and ensure a seamless provision of first in-class services across Europe.  
 
Revision timeline 
5GAA endorses a fair and transparent process for the revision of the  rules governing ITS in Europe, 
with the active involvement of all relevant ITS ecosystem stakeholders. 5GAA is looking forward to 
contributing to all stages of the public consultation, including the targeted consultations as part of the 
support study and the stakeholders' workshops planned as part of the revision procedure. We would 
welcome further clarity on how the European Commission intends to structure the exchanges 
 
 
between itself and the ITS stakeholder community ahead of the planned publication of the proposal, 
e.g. via the existing sub-groups of the  Expert group on cooperative, connected, automated and 
autonomous mobility (E03657)  or a Call for applications  to a newly dedicated sub -group under the 
Intelligent Transport Systems (E01941).

 
 
Contribution of the European Sea Ports Organisation to the Commission’s 
Roadmap on the revision of the Port State Control Directive  (2009/16) 
 
20 November 2020  
 
The European Sea Ports Organisation  (ESPO) takes notice of the Commission’s initiative to revise the 
Port State Control (PSC) Directive. Adopted in 2009 as part of the third Maritime Safety package, the 
PSC Directive provides for a maritime safety inspection regime based on the Paris Memorandum of 
Understanding (Paris MoU).    
European ports are committed to the highest possible standards in maritime safety. Port managing 
bodies play an important role in maritime safety and the prevention of pollution through sophisticated 
maritime traffic monitoring systems, tech nical-nautical assistance to ships and facilities to collect 
waste to avoid discharges at sea. 
In general, European ports consider the current PSC Directive a well-functioning EU regulation that has 
contributed to enhanced maritime safety and pollution prevention both at sea and in ports.  
While ESPO does not see the need at this moment to raise any specific points linked to the 
Commission’s roadmap, ESPO would like to remain involved in the possible review and the pathway 
towards this review,  including the imp act assessment, allowing its members to further assess  and 
potentially react on any concrete ideas or proposals that will be developed in preparation of this 
review.

The European Sea Ports Organisation (ESPO) represents the port authorities, port associations and port 
administrations of the seaports of 2 2 Member States of the European Union and Norway at political 
level. ESPO has also observer members in Iceland, the United Kingdom, Ukraine and Israel. ESPO is the 
principal interface between the European seaport authorities and the European institutions. In addition 
to representing the interests of European ports, ESPO is a knowledge network which brings together 
professionals from the port sector and national port organizations. ESPO was created in 1993.

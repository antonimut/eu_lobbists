The European Steel Association, EUROFER, welcomes the initiative taken by the EU Commission 
on the revision of lists of pollutants affecting surface and groundwater. The published Roadmap 
can be seen as an enabler for achieving necessary improvements on the Wa ter Framework 
Directive, as the Fitness Check found the EU water legislation to be broadly fit for purpose . 
Furthermore, it  
o stresses the aspects linked to the protection of environment and human health from the 
adverse effect of water pollution  
o associates the mandatory review of surface water priority substances (EQSD) and 
groundwater pollutants (GWD) with existing legislation in the area of Industrial Emissions 
Directive (IED) and EU Pollutant Release and Transfer Regulation (E-PRTR) with Chemicals 
Strategy for Sustainability, 2030 Biodiversity Strategy 
o will enhance consistency between water legislation and other legislations, including 
chemicals legislation for certain SVHC  
o links soil and water contamination 
Overall, it aims at increasing policy coherence and new/emerging pollutants. 
We agree on the main goals of the Roadmap, to revise the lists of pollutants in the light of the 
scientific development and recent technical work, particularly on emerging pollutants, and 
appreciate the opportunity given to industry to comment on this initiative. However, we are of 
the opinion that, to fulfil the policy objectives  and in the context of its zero -pollution ambition, 
the initiative needs to ensure a 100% scientific approach through:  
- the consideration of  the background concentrations of naturally occurring metals, when 
defining quality standards, e.g. EQS.  
- the use of an ecosystemic approach when it comes to evaluate effects of a contaminant in the 
aquatic environment, using up to date methodologies such as the evidence-based methodology 
(EBM), and integrate local environmental conditions.

EUROFER is committed and engaged in the important work of the revision of PS and PHS for the 
WFD and EQSD as well as groundwater pollutants for the GWD, being part of the CIS WG 
Chemicals and CIS Groundwater WG. In order to land in scientifically based and environmentally 
relevant substances, as well as correct limit values for the substances, it is important to include 
sufficient competence for the experts that are involved in this revision process  as well as 
sufficient time for the evaluation processes. 
We also  want to stress the importance, more than ever, of a close collaboration between all 
relevant actors (European Commission, Member States, stakeholders including industry). The 
current process of adapting a sound and harmonized  legislation to the European G reen Deal 
provides a tremendous opportunity for constructive partnerships, based on scientific excellence, 
and users´ experience. We welcome such an approach. 
In conclusion, EUROFER thinks that this initiative represents a key opportunity to achieve the 
objectives of a zero -pollution ambition in the framework of the Green Deal, and to introduce 
safer chemicals, materials and products in Europe, also supported by other initiatives running in 
parallel. However, only with  the above described conditions and a tackled approach to specific 
sectors to be put in place by the authorities and controlling bodies, the European steel industry 
will be fully able to achieve the objectives of using safer chemicals in an economically sustainable 
manner.

Revision of lists of pollutants affecting surface and groundwaters 
Europe’s non-ferrous metals industries’ response to the Inception Impact Assessment 
We welcome the opportunity to provide feedback on the Inception Impact Assessment and would like to share some 
recommendations on the way forward. The Non-ferrous metals industry is committed to continually improve its production 
performance to prevent, control, reduce and , as far as p ossible, eliminate its pollution  to protect human health and the 
environment. We support the process regulated under the Water Framework Directive, Environmental Quality Standards 
Directive and Groundwater Directive.  Below you find our considerations regar ding the recently re-started process of the 
revision and update of the Priority Substances list on which the following recommendations are based on: 
 
Key recommendations 
• Environmental policies need to be proportionate, based on scientific and factual evidence and efficiently implemented 
to support the industrial transition and global competitiveness of EU industry. 
• Integrate consideration of local environmental conditions , bioavailability, and natural backgrounds when identifying 
Priority Substances, and when defining and implementing EQS values. 
• Apply robust criteria  to utilise high quality data, and the most up -to-date scientific guidance and knowledge when 
assessing the risks posed by metals in water. 
• Improve data collection, better understand and assess emissions , and identify the main contribut ing sources  to 
pollution. 
• Allow in-depth exchange between experts before taking decisions.  
 
Revision of Priority Substances in surface waters list 
We recommend the Commission to encourage the use and implementation of the best available science when assessing 
risk of metals in surface waters. The scientific principles of bioavailability and natural background are well recognized, 
and they have been accepted by the EU Commission and by many Member States in the process of setting EQS values 
for several metals. In a continued effort to apply these same principles, we respectfully call on the Commission to finalize 
the last steps and proceed with the publication of the Technical Guidance Document on Implementin g Metals 
Environmental Quality Standards (EQS) . We believe that th is new Guidance  – when published – will greatly assist 
regulators in correctly assessing the risk posed by metals ; and therefore , more accurately evaluate the status of EU 
Waters. Concretely, it will allow Member States to correctly account for  bioavailability and natural background 
concentrations of metals in a compliance assessment.

Identification of new Priority Substances, and setting their EQS, must be done based on sound science . The relevant 
criterion is whether an EU -wide risk exists . It is important to consider that , according to the EQS Derivation Guidance,  
EQS values for River Basin Specific Pollutants may differ between Member States. This is normal, because the different 
EQS values primarily reflect the local environmental conditions which strongly differ in different river basins and Member 
States. Moreover, given the wide ranging implications, it is critical  to ensure  that the PS-setting and the EQS review 
process will adhere to the principle s of “better regulation” (proportionality, predictability, proper stakeholder consultation, 
transparency), making sure that all relevant data and scientific evidence are assessed carefully. Overall , the objective  
should be to ensure a transparent, consensus driven and robust consultative process  for the derivation and review of 
relevant EQSs , involving stakeholders . The review process of the Directive should allow enough time to enable 
stakeholders and Member States to provide constructive input via the relevant expert groups and consultations.  
Overall, to develop effective and proportionate policies, it is crucial to improve data collection, better understand and 
assess reported emissions and identify the main contributors or contribution sources to pollution.

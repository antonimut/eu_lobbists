The European Commission report Study on directors’ duties and sustainable corporate 
governance, and the Inception Impact Assessment 
 
 
Confederation of Finnish industries EK thanks for the opportunity to 
provide its comments to the abovementioned study. We share the 
target and the importance of enhancing human rights and trying to find 
solutions to fight climate change. However, we have some concerns 
regarding the proposals and findings presented in the study as 
described below in more detail. 
 
General points of the study 
 
Firstly, we would like to point out that quantitative analysis of the study 
is limited; the web survey is comprising of 62 stakeholders, interviews 
with 16 persons in 12 countries. According to our view a reliable result 
from an EU -wide study would have required a significantly larger 
quantity of respondents from different countries and representing 
different stakeholders. 
 
Secondly, we would like to point out that empirical facts underlying the 
the basic assumptions of the study have not been presented in the 
study. The business sector, when acting profitably and responsibly, is 
responsible for investment and job creation, thus generating growth, 
tax-revenue and well-being in the whole society. However, in the study 
the business sector is rather seen as irresponsible  short-term profit 
seeker without clearly stating why this has been set as the basic 
assumption.  
  
According to our view this has lead to problematic and harmful 
proposals that target very well established and developed company 
legislation and corporate governance regimes without a deeper 
analysis on the question whether real problems exit or not.

About ”short-termism” and other findings in the study 
 
The study rests on a concept of short-termism, which concept is 
described through the relation between company pay-outs and capital 
expenditure compared to gross and net revenue of the company. 
According to our view neither of these indicators, pay-outs or capital 
expenditure, can be seen as a proof of excessive short-termism in 
companies' behaviour.  
 
Moreover, the study argues that companies’ decision making would be 
based too much on taking into account the short-term interests of the 
shareholders. This view is justified by data according to which 
companies’ investments in fixed assets and R&D have decreased at 
the same time as the distribution of funds to shareholders has 
increased. This type of argument that is based on a single key figure 
comparison is too narrow to allow for such conclusion.  
  
The report is also stating that it would be preferred from a societal point 
of view to keep company dividends as low as possible. This suggestion 
totally collides with the functions of the modern market and the 
movement of the capital in the market – most of the dividends paid out 
to shareholders of listed companies are re-invested back in other 
companies in the market or they return to consumption. The pay-out is 
not disappearing from the market. 
  
To conclude, in our view the chosen indicators do not prove the 
existence of ”short-termism” in the company business behaviour. 
 
Ownership structure in the listed companies 
 
There are large and influential owners in most of the listed companies. 
These are for example large pension funds and other funds that have 
long-term strategy in their investment portfolio. Other examples of such 
owners are family-owners or establishers of the company. In Finland, 
for example, also the state is a large owner in some companies. We 
would like to emphasize that these are examples of types of owners 
whose interest is not to prioritize short term profit generation but to 
secure the best available ROE with a company that operates with long 
term goals and sound as well as sustainable business strategies. 
 
Finnish Corporate Governance model 
 
We do not agree with the statement that “directors’ duties and company’s 
interest are interpreted narrowly and tend to favour the short -term 
maximisation of shareholder value.” We are not aware of fact -based 
evidence to back up the statement that “growing pressures from 
investors with a sho rt-term horizon contribute to increasing the boards’ 
focus on short-term financial returns to shareholders at the expense of

long-term value creation.” If “companies lack a strategic perspective over 
sustainability and current practices fail to effectively identify and manage 
relevant sustainability risks and impacts”, as it stated in the report we 
would also see a lot more business failures.  
 
The Finnish corporate governance model is based on a clear division of 
responsibilities between the owners, the Board, and the management 
of the company. Strong position of the shareholders support the fact 
that the company’s Board of Directors and Managing Director take into 
account the company’s long-term interests in their decisions. According 
to research, responsible business is in the interests of companies and 
shareholders.  
  
The pressure to responsible business conduct already comes from 
both customers and investors and other stakeholders. However, 
corporate social responsibility can best be promoted only when the 
company’s business is profitable. Therefore, we would like to 
emphasize that only a profitable company is able to invest, employ 
personnel, and otherwise act sustainably and thus generate growth 
and well-being over time. The interests of stakeholders must be taken 
into account in decision-making, but the division of responsibilities and 
control relations in company legislation must be kept clear also in the 
future.   
 
Concluding remarks 
 
According to our view, the findings or proposals of the study are not 
applicable to be used as a basis for legislative proposals or policy 
changes. We are concerned that implementing the proposals in the 
study would, in fact, be disadvantageous to European companies and 
their global competitiveness as a whole.  
  
The big issues - climate change and human rights violations – need to 
be solved in global cooperation between societies, countries, and 
business. Changing the legislative elements and regimes that are 
forming the basis of the European limited liability company 
administration, would according to our view be an inefficient way and it 
would endanger a well-established and well-operating corporate 
governance model.

MTK’s feedback on the Roadmap “Protecting biodiversity: nature restoration tar-
gets under EU Biodiversity Strategy” (Inception Impact Assessment) 
 
 
MTK supports the main goal of the EU Biodiversity Strategy 2030 which aims at halting the loss of biodiver-
sity. MTK considers it important to solve biodiversity challenges also at the EU level and thus, MTK has wel-
comed the new Strategy. However, MTK highlights the need to better recognise all three pillars of sustain-
ability in a balanced way:  economic and social perspectives must also be considered when promoting envi-
ronmental sustainability. This must be considered also in the context  of nature restoration.  In addition, 
MTK reminds that the imple mentation of the Strategy must be in coherenc e with other EU policies and 
their objectives. Agriculture and forestry should not be treated narrowly from a biodiversity perspective 
only as they provide widescale well-being and play a crucially important rol e in a tra nsition towards sus-
tainable societies. The opportunities of responsible and sustainable agriculture and forestry to contribute 
to the achievement of the Sustainable Development Goals and the goals of the EU Green Deal must be bet-
ter acknowledged. 
 
Following t he statement included in the Council Conclusions on the EU Biodiversity Strategy 2030, MT K 
stresses that a thorough impact assessment must be provided for any legislative proposal that the Commis-
sion may put forward . Therefore, MTK supports th e Commission’s aim to analyse the possible impacts of 
different options related to the nature restoration targets and actions mentioned in the Strategy. 
 
According to the Roadmap document, intervention at the EU level is justified in view of the scale and trans-
boundary nature of biodiversity -related issues. Despi te MTK ack nowledges the value of coordination be-
tween the Member States, MTK is of the opinion that the EU Biodiversity Strategy must be a strategic tool 
and leave r oom for national de cisions. This applies also to the restoration section of the Strategy.  In its 
Conclusion, the Council mentioned the need to include an assessment of the subsidiarity and proportionali-
ty of the proposed measures  in the impact as sessment, and MTK supports th is remark. MTK finds it rel e-
vant to compare different policy options  and policy tools in the impact assessment  from ecological, eco-
nomic and social  viewpoints: instead of legally binding  instruments based on obligations, voluntary 
measures supported by incentives must be considered as an equally relevant option (e.g. an Action Plan or 
other non-legal policy tool). MTK reminds that comprehensive legislation related to e.g. environmental sus-
tainability of agriculture and forestry already exists both at EU and national level. 
 
MTK points out that due to the many specificities and heterogenous conditions existing across Europe, one-
size-fits-all approaches in nature restoration do not lead to optimal solutions. Flexibility is needed to allow 
the recognition of e very country’s precise needs, conditions and opportunities. Thus, more detailed plan-
ning and implementation of the Strategy in relation to restoration must take place at the level of the Mem-
ber States in order to ensure approp riate targeting, prioritisation and cost-effectiveness of actions as well 
as the fulfilment of the rights of landowners. Each Member State should have the possibility to choose the 
measures that are used to contribute to the achievement of the targets set in the Strategy. Restoration and

nature management actions can be done by using voluntary approaches instead of compulsive ones.  The 
Finnish Helmi Habitats Programme focusing on voluntary -based nature management and restoration  is an 
example of a win-win solution that combines a landowner-respective approach and ecological functionality. 
 
Related to the role of the Member States , MTK reminds that forest policy is the competence of Members 
States. Moreover, for those EU policies impacting forests and falling under shared competences, the princ i-
ple of subsidiarity applies. These principles must be respected also in the future and in all discussions relat-
ed to forest s in the EU. This premise has been highlighted by both the Parliament and by the Council in 
their recent views on the EU Forest Strat egy. Thus, in the context of e.g. definitions as well as protection 
and restoration measures , the role of national decision making through national forest policy must be 
acknowledged. 
 
When preparing an impact assessment, the general aim is usually to analyse what kind out outcomes are 
likely when certain policy measures are put in place. MTK reminds that the use of reliable scientific data as 
well as defining the concept of nature restoration are key s tarting points of a comprehensive analysis.  
When it comes to definitions, MTK points out that restoration is usually a series of actions or a process that 
leads to the final outcome. Depending on the c ase, restoration can mean either removing the im pacts of 
previous human activities or increasing the human intervention through e.g. nature management actions.  
Since the concept of nature restoration varies in practice and forest policy is a national competence, defini-
tions made by the Member States must be preferred instead of the ones made at the EU level. MTK points 
out that if any definitions, however, would be done at the EU level they must be commonly agreed upon 
after discussions. 
  
MTK highlights that respecting the rights of landowners and involving actors on the ground is a prerequisite 
for the acceptability of the EU Biodiversity Strategy and for its successful implementation. The outcomes of 
the Strategy will largely depend on the motivation, support and future involvement of landowners and oth-
er actors on the ground , and this is relevant in the context of nature restorat ion as well. In addition to ap-
propriate compensation measures, positive incentives, advisory servic es and voluntary bottom -up partici-
patory processes supporting the expansion of existing best practices are crucially important.  Therefore, re-
ferring to the C ouncil Conclusions on the Biodiversity Strategy  2030 MTK encourages the Commission to 
assess the need for compensations and financeability. With a reference to fun damental rights, MTK stress-
es the importance to analyse how the different policy tools would affect e.g. ownership rights. 
 
Fairness of the effort -sharing needs to be a central part of the imp lementation of the Strategy. Thus, also 
the restoration efforts needed to reach the objectives of the Strategy must be shared in a just way, and the 
fairness must be evaluated from several viewpoints: is the effort -sharing just between Member States, be-
tween different sectors (e.g. agriculture and forestry vs. others) and between different groups of people 
(e.g. landowners vs. others)? 
 
Based on and related to the aspects mentioned above, MTK finds it important that the impact assessment 
addresses, among ot her things, also the following questions: prioritisation (i.e. what s hould be r estored), 
active restoration vs. passive restoration, cost-effectiveness of different options, uncertainties in the resto-
ration process, time frame (e.g. the delay between a restoration action and the achievement of a measura-
ble result), possible conflicts and trade-offs between different environmental targets (e.g. biodiversity vs. 
climate), data gaps and administrative burden.

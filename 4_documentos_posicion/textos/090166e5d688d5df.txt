BIODIVERSITY 
 
Nature Restoration: A Cooperative Approach 
to Take Up the Challenge   
European g ypsum industry’s input for the future legislative 
initiative on EU Nature Restoration Targets 
 
DATE: 2 DECEMBER 2020 
 
 
The European Commission’s Biodiversity Strategy for 2030 has outlined important principles and 
announced major actions to address the challenge of biodiversity loss. Eurogypsum, the European 
federation of national associations of producers of gypsum products, has welcomed the European 
Commission’s ambitions on this subject and would like to submit some comments on the first 
elements provided in the “inception impact assessment” document relating to the “ EU nature 
restoration targets” initiative foreseen for the fourth quarter of 2021. 
 
We share the Commission’s assessment of the degraded state of biodiversity and ecosystems, and 
welcome the objective of restoring these degraded ecosystems.  
 
The principle of legally binding targets  based on the existing EU regula tory framework is worth 
exploring. However, a uniform approach throughout the EU would not be adequate, given the 
differences in the type and extent of ecosystem degradation across EU Member States. We believe 
that these differences should be properly considered in the definition of the targets, to make 
them most appropriate and useful for each Member State. In this regard,  national and regional 
authorities should be involved as much as possible  in the preparation of such targets . The 
governance and implementation mechanisms must also be designed in cooperation with relevant 
authorities and stakeholders, to ensure full support and cooperation in achieving the targets.  
 
Regarding the scope of act ion, we would recommend an unbiased focus on all degraded 
ecosystems, without discriminating against habitat types with low carbon storage , such as 
immature soils, which are  very often important habitats for endangered and European protected 
pioneer species, or nutrient-poor and xeric grasslands, which are particularly rich in species that 
benefit from such immature soils or depend on them.  
 
Considering the means of achieving the defined targets, we would like to stress that m any actions 
are already carried out by private operators, such as gypsum quarries, often in cooperation with 
scientific and environmental organisations, to protect, maintain, restore ecosystems, as well as 
support their development 1. Our industry is aware of the key role the sector, a s well as other 
extractive industries, can play in achieving the EU Biodiversity Strategy’s objectives.  
 
Therefore, we believe the extractive industries should be recognised as an important 
contributor and be given the possibility to concur to the restoration targets in Natura 2000 areas, 
not only through the implementation of existing rules on the environmental management of 
quarries, but also by encouraging and promoting further -reaching voluntary restoration and

Eurogypsum is a European federation of national associations of producers of gypsum products (i.e. plaster and 
plasterboard). It is one of the few fully integrated industries (from cradle to cradle) within the construction products field. 
The companies which mine gyp sum also process it and manufacture the value -added products and systems used 
extensively in construction and other industries. 
With a turnover of EUR 7 billion, the European gypsum and anhydrite industry operates some 160 factories and 154 
quarries and generates employment directly to 28,000 persons and indirectly for 300,000 persons. The gypsum industry 
provides jobs to 1,100,000 plasterers and plasterboard installers. It trains around 25,000 persons per year across Europe.

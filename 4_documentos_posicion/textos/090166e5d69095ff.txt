 
 
OP2B response on Inception Impact Assessment for  
“Protecting biodiversity: nature restoration targets under EU biodiversity strategy” 
OP2B members are committed to helping achieve the objectives set out in the EU Biodiversity Strategy to 
reverse biodiversity loss by 2050, in line with the ambitions set out in the Paris Agreement, and that will 
form the core of the post -2020 global biodiversity framework. As part of this commitment, we fully 
welcome the Commission’s ambitions to propose legally binding targets for nature restoration.  
Such targets are needed  as we are losing our biodiversity, the backbone of our human system,  at an 
unpresented and unsustainable rate. One of the key contributor to biodiversity loss is our global 
agriculture and food system, which within the past 50 years alone has brought an abundance of affordable 
consumption products to large swathes of the population. Thi s success however has come at a 
considerable cost as the food system alone is responsible for around 60% of global biodiversity loss, fueled 
by an increasingly overspecialized system, loss of both natural and cultivated biodiversity due to land use 
change, monoculture and intensive farming practices.  
OP2B members strongly believe that a  fundamental overhaul of our current global agricultural model 
towards regenerative practices is needed to restore biodiversity, guarantee the world’s growing 
population can be fed, and secure ongoing economic growth.  OP2B members  support regenerative 
agriculture as a key alternative to the conventional agriculture model that will restore soil health, increase 
biodiversity and contribute to any EU-set nature restoration targets. 
Regenerative agriculture is a nature-based solution  that aims to transition agriculture from being a 
primary source of environmental degradation globally, to a primary source of regeneration. Regenerative 
agriculture involves transforming agr icultural landscapes and practices to improve both the health of 
agricultural ecosystems, the functions and services they provide, and the quality of yields. It aims to 
restore soil health and fertility by increasing organic matter in the soil, improving water flows, increasing 
biodiversity and the agrobiodiversity of landscapes, and enhancing ecosystem services while respecting 
animal welfare.  
Scientific research has repeatedly shown that regenerative agricultural practices can contribute 
significantly to both soil health and climate change mitigation. Modelling exercises
  have shown that a 
shift to such practices would lead  to a reduction of agriculture greenhouse gas emissions of 20% at global 
level by 2030, and up to 40% in Europe by 2050 in line with the objectives set out in the Europe an Green 
Deal.

The transition to a more sustainable agriculture system is needed to achieve the goals set out in the 
European Green Deal, and needs to be supported by strong political ambitions at the EU level. The 
Commission’s proposal to adopt legal ly binding nature restoration targets at EU level can give a much 
needed push for this transition to happen. OP2B therefore calls for the following to be considered as 
essential aspects of the Commission’s impact assessment: 
• The need for clear, achievable targets that take into account efforts already underway to reduce 
biodiversity loss; 
• The need for a commonly -agreed clear methodology and monitoring framework to be applied 
across the EU, to guarantee a level playing field across the supply chain in Europe; 
• The need to acknowledge that nature-based solutions like regenerative agriculture are part of the 
way forward to restoring ecosystems and biodiversity; 
• The need of adequate financial support for the transition to more sustainable land- use practices 
like regenerative agriculture to ensure that any nature restoration targets are met.

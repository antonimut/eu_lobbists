 
 
We hereby state that the Agence de la biomédecine (ABM) fully supports the comments provided by 
the French Ministry of Health. The additional comments presented in this document aim to address 
in more detail specific issues we identified in our areas of expertise. 
  
Donation Principles and Protection 
We would like to reiterate the importance of volunt ary and unpaid donation principles for donors in 
all fields – blood, tissues, HSC and MAR. With the development of new therapies, such as CAR T cells 
and other cellular immunotherapies in the field of HSC, and scientific progress in general, it is vital not 
to lose sight of donor safety and their protection. To this end, we support using the CoE’s Guide for the 
implementation of the principle of prohibition of financial gain with respect to the human body and its 
parts from living or deceased donors  as a reference of values to uphold. 
In terms of protection in the field of MAR, we prop ose limiting donor’s age as well as the number of 
children born from the same donor and/or limiting number of families benefiting from the same donor 
in order to reduce the risks of consanguinity for t he next generation, especially when the donation is 
anonymous. In addition, a closer follow-up regarding genetic disease should be developed for donors 
and children born from donated eggs, sperm or embry os and a system of tracking serious adverse 
events should be put in place. 
Policy Options 
Agence de la biomédecine supports policy option 2 as well as the possibility of combining policy options 
2 and 3. Overall binding requirements would present  the same difficulties as the current system. 
However, laying down basic binding requirements in terms of minimal safety and quality of products 
would favour harmonisation of practices and facilitate their circulation between MS.  
The criteria for classification of substances whose requirements would be binding and the basis of the 
rationale behind the decision-making (e.g. publicat ions) should be established beforehand. Expert 
bodies should be composed in equal parts of both professionals and competent authorities and avoid 
any conflict of interest. 
Furthermore, data collection would be a useful indicator under the condition there is a consensus on 
the type and format of data expected to be provided . Establishing a system that would permit 
monitoring sufficiency levels could lead to better visibility of stock and product movements.   
Other Comments 
The notion of « patient benefit » requires further clarification in terms of how will it be defined an d 
whether it will be taken into account not only for novel products but also for routinely administered 
products.

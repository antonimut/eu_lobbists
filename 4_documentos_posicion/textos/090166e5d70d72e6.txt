SEOPAN comments on the Inception Impact Assessment (Ref. Ares (2020) 6977996 - 
20/11/2020): Revision of Regulation on Union Guidelines for the development of the trans-
European transport network.  
 
SEOPAN represents Spanish public works contractors and concessionaires leading the operation 
and management of all kind of transport infrastructure all over the World. From SEOPAN we 
would like to take the opportunity to express our views on the proposed Inception Impact 
Assessment document and provide some recommendations.  
 We strongly agree on the 3 main problems identified in the paper: i) the insufficient 
effectiveness of TEN-T to stimulate zero and low emission transport, ii) the lack of 
preparedness of the TEN-T for the digital transition in transport and iii) the insufficient 
resilience of the TEN-T infrastructure. 
 
 We also agree on most of the measures proposed to face these problems, however the text 
does not address funding mechanisms to implement those measures and reach the desired 
goals.  
 
 Important investments are still needed to offer a safe, reliable, multimodal and sustainable 
transportation system, specially in periurban and urban growing areas. 
 
 We believe that the source of funding should be sustainable in time, and as such it should 
be encouraged (so as to not burden future generations with debt repayments) and ideally 
come from road users that profit from the infrastructure and generate externalities. 
 
 Tolling/charging is not only a mechanism to finance the required investments on roads (to 
conclude, enlarge and make resilient the EU road network and adapt it to the digitalisation 
and green transformations) but also is a key mechanism to manage the traffic demand, 
boosting the transition to cleaner and safer vehicles and to place the right incentives to our 
mobility.  
 
 Provisions related to CO2 emission class with reduction charging and possibly free charging 
for electrical vehicle is not the appropriate measure to fix the correct prices or relief 
congestion. It will neither cover one relevant part of the costs allocated to these vehicles 
(the infrastructure costs), nor solve traffic congestion since it will not remove cars or truck 
from the road. Lower toll rates for electric vehicles would only make sense in urban 
environments and should be part of an integrated urban pricing policy including new 
elements easing sustainable accesses to cities: managed lanes, high occupancy vehicles 
lanes, dissuasive parking, carpooling, bus rapid transit, etc. 
 
 The private sector can act as the long-term liaison between administrations to dynamically 
ensure the pursuit of long-term goals through long term public private partnerships. A 
transparent mechanism where a private entity assume and manage most of the risks and 
has an economic incentive to ensure that infrastructure is resilient and can be transferred 
to future generations in the same or better condition, has already been tested with PPPs 
and should be encouraged, without losing the oversight and regulatory control that 
governments may require.

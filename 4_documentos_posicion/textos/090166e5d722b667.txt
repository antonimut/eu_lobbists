Inception Impact Assessment – TEN-T Regulation  
Completion of the network 
STRING stresses the strategic importance of the designated core network corridors. The TEN-T 
regulation is vital in promoting the free circulation of goods, services and citizens throughout the EU. 
The regulation  should therefore adhere to the strategic direction adopted in 2013  regarding the 
completion of  the core network by 2030 and focus on cross -border transport infrastructure. 
Eliminating bottlenecks should also remain a key objective in the revised regulation. 
Sustainable transport 
The technical requirements in the regulation regarding the rail networks are not sufficient in order to 
move freight and passenger traffic to sustainable means of transport such as rail. The regulation  
therefore needs to further facilitate the transition by strengthening and developing rail infrastructure. 
Measures to improve the rail competitiveness in comparison with road transport should also have high 
priority in the regulation and special importance should be given to sections where bottlenecks create 
an obstacle to move to more sustainable means of transport . Additionally, r equirements such as 
double track infrastructure should be included in the requirements for completion in bottleneck 
sections to increase competitiveness for rail transport . The railway infrastructure must also be 
integrated to other modes of transportation such as maritime transport. This also includes a greater 
focus on the Core Ports , as better integrated ports will lead to reduced congestion on the networks. 
Thus, to make rail more competitive the regulation must have a holistic focus on common standards, 
eliminating bottlenecks and increased intermodality.   
The regulation must also support the fundamental transformation of the road transport system and 
should to a greater extend include innovative solutions that are aligned with the priorities of the Green 
Deal. Furthermore, the regulation should include a technology neutrality approach by supporting all 
available technologies with market potential and ensure that the standards are the same across 
Europe. To allow for this transformation it is of greatest im portance that the EU focuses on an 
accelerated roll-out of charging stations of alternative fuels. A main concern for the development is 
the interoperability of alternative fuels in an international context; i.e. functioning payment systems . 
With regards t o transnational markets for alternative fuels, it is also important to make sure that 
alternative fuels are available in all relevant regional markets along the Core Network Corridors, 
otherwise it will be difficult to establish the fuels in international transport. 
Digitalisation 
The regulation needs to support the fundamental transformation of the transport system and better 
use the opportunities of digitalisation. It is the core network and the major nodes that are prerequisites 
for developing and implementing new digitalisation solutions, such as intermodality. The functioning 
of urban nodes as hubs for intermodal shifts, transfer traffic and a high density of different transport 
forms underlines the crucial importance of an efficient, digital, seamless, su stainable and efficient 
mobility. 
Resilience

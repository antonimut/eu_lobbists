CER’s response to the Inception Impact 
Assessment 
 
 
CER welcomes the opportunity to provide feedback to the Inception Impact Assessment 
expressing the intended revision of the Regulation on Union Guidelines for the 
development of the trans -European transport network (TEN -T). Transport infrastructure 
policy at EU level is essential for the coordination of important transport projects, the 
overall development of cross -border and regional infrastructure as well as cohesion and 
social inclusion. It is an important enabler for the European economic growth and 
provides opportunities for jobs. 
 
CER acknowledges that the decarbonisation objective s and other environment -related 
challenges as set out in the European Green Deal necessitate changes in TEN -T policy. 
Due to the high degree of electrification, railways, as the only green mode of transport, 
are already able to offer zero -carbon train operations (80% of the traction is electric)  in 
Europe thus making r ailways key to EU transport decarbonisation and maximising their 
contribution to the EU Green Deal . This is wh y railways are fully included in the EU 
Taxonomy as a green activity.  It is therefore important to include railways 
decarbonisation efforts for energy system integration and hydrogen  in the Commission’s 
revision for ensuring coverage of alternative fuels. In addition, further electrification and 
upgrades of the rail network (regional as well as main lines), must be ensured, as 
electrified railway transport is by far the most efficient form of e -mobility. Rail freight 
needs to be boosted notably in view of i ts resilience during the COVID -19 pandemic 
times. 
 
With regard to setting additional infrastructure quality requirements to further boost 
efficiency and sustainability of transport and mobility services towards the zero and low 
emission target, CER wishes to reiterate that the additional infrastructure requirements 
should be in line with the rail market needs and should not come as detrimental to the 
current existing ones. Some  of the infrastructure characteristics defined in the TEN -T 
Regulation for all transport modes have not been properly achieved. For example, the 
technical requirements of the core network for rail, such as full electrification , ERTMS 
and 740 m trains, are unlikely to be implemented by 2030 as foreseen.  Characteristics 
and requirements must remain realistic with regard to the deadlines for the completion 
of the network, the implementation capacities, socio -economic realities and financing 
capacities but also to be adapted to the needs of the market in order to promote 
interoperability for a modal shift to  rail. The concept of introducing new quality 
requirements and new monitoring technologies to ensure high structural infrastructure 
quality over the life -time of the infrastructure  requires more clarification, taking into 
account that in many cases maintenance activities carried out by infrastructure 
managers relate to the whole network, without distinction between TEN -T and non-TEN-
T. 
 
That being said, in order to achieve the objective of further modal shift and with regard 
to the climate neutrality objective set out in the European Green Deal,  the harmonized 
implementation of the TEN -T parameters is crucial for rail.  It is important to c reate a 
common understanding of the infrastructure criteria for the TEN -T Core Network f or 
smooth international rail traffic . This means, that the existing criteria should not be

softened, but clarified  and interpret ed.  A harmonised loading gauge, where it is 
economically viable and technically possible, is important to promote the interoper ability 
of rail according to the  specific needs of the market. These provisions should be in line 
with TSI INF requirements.  However, future amendments to the regulation should not 
significantly change the path to achieve the parameters required by the cur rent 
regulation by 2030, bearing in mind that  there are  ongoing and planned major 
investment works related to the core network in several Member States.  
 
CER strongly welcomes the intention of the European Commission to enhance synergies 
between rail freight (RFCs) and core network corridors (CNCs) without prejudicing their 
different roles and competences. Provisions should be included in the revised TEN -T 
Regulation,  improving the cooperation between them in order to allow the identification 
of the investments priorities of the CNC work plans according to the market needs and to 
reduce gaps and bottlenecks in the rail network.  In this context, it is import ant to 
improve the rail infrastructure between the Balkans and the Mediterranean ports, and 
with Central Europe, while relieving congested ports for seamless rail freight transport 
within Europe. 
 
It is essential to boost digitalisation, automation and oth er innovative solutions in TEN-T 
policy to ensure readiness for future developments and strengthen the TEN -T. The focus 
on soft components of the infrastructure rather than the traditional physical will help to 
achieve the objectives of the TEN -T policy, i ncrease capacity and better interconnect 
intermodal hubs and transport nodes. Digitalization is the backbone of the 
transformation of railway operations and the top priority for making rail more efficient 
and competitive. On the other hand, maintaining, re newing and upgrading the existing 
physical rail infrastructure will play a major role in multimodal, efficient, accessible and 
safe traffic across Europe. The latter will also assist largely in e nhancing resilience of the 
network infrastructure, notably in  relation to climate adaptation, security and civil 
protection needs. It should be underlined that both, traditional, physical approach and 
digitalisation scenario are of the same importance and one is closely correlated with 
another. Moreover, the implementation of cross-border infrastructure projects should be 
speeded up so that they generate the added value intended by transport policies.  This 
also includes important access routes, without which it is not possible to achieve the 
intended added value of projects. In addition, there should be corridor approach adopted 
when it comes to the construction of railway infrastructure .  Finally, t here are synergies 
also with the new regulation on rail passenger rights, as a strengthened rail network will 
allow smoo th passenger operations without unnecessary delays or cancellation, and 
widens the options for passenger connections. The Inception IA is however not very clear 
on this and speaks very general of passenger rights.  
   
CER agrees with the alignment with oth er on-going legislative initiatives and deems it 
necessary to ensure synergies, notably with the EU Directive on Alternative Fuel 
infrastructure and the Regulation on Rail Freight Corridors and the EU Directive on the 
Intelligent Transport Systems (ITS) and its interfaces with other modes of transport. It is 
however essential to link the revision to existing EU legislation in particular the 
Commission Proposal for a Regulation of the European Parliament and the Council on 
streamlining measures for advancing the realisation of the trans -European transport 
network. 
 
EU’s future infrastructure policies should go hand in hand with a smarter approach on 
pricing that is based on the ‘user -pays’ and the ‘polluter -pays’ principles. This will be 
required to fully internalise environmental externalities. 2021 will be a crucia l year in

revising EU’s climate and energy legislation. The revisions of the EU Emissions Trading 
System and the Energy Taxation Directives should lead to a robust carbon pricing in 
transport to guarantee the greenhouse gas emissions reductions required by  the EU 
Green Deal and at the same time eliminate the distortion of competition among modes.  
CER supports the realization of a high speed network connecting all capitals and major 
cities in order to achieve the doubling of passengers by 2030 and tripling b y 2050 in 
accordance with EUSSMS. Thereby a balance should be  also found with working towards 
achieving 30% of the rail freight modal share by 2030. 
 
Finally, sufficient funding  is necessary to complete the TEN -T network, including the 
finalisation of majo r on-going TEN-T projects, and to support the digital transformation 
of rail operations, notably ERTMS on board and on track.  
 
CER and its Members look forward to working closely with the European Commission and 
provide its input to the revision of the TEN-T Regulation.      
 
 
 
About CER 
The Community of European Railway and Infrastructure Companies (CER) brings together railway 
undertakings, their national associations as well as infrastructure managers and vehicle leasing companies. The 
membership is made up of long -established bodies, n ew entrants and both private and public enterprises, 
representing 71% of the rail network length, 76% of the rail freight business and about 92% of rail passenger 
operations in EU, EFTA and EU accession countries. CER represents the interests of its member s towards EU 
policy makers and transport stakeholders, advocating rail as the backbone of a competitive and sustainable 
transport system in Europe. For more information, visit www.cer.be or follow @CER_railways on Twitter.

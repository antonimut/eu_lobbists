ETSC input to TEN-T Revision Inception Impact Assessment  
December 2020 
‘The TEN-T guidelines need to be further strengthened to prioritise 
upgrading road infrastructure to meet safety requirements.’ 
https://etsc.eu/wp-content/uploads/ETSC-Position-on-Infrastructure-Directive-2008.96.pdf 
 
1. Supports identification under Problem C in the Inception Impact of ‘Insufficient  resilience  of  
the  TEN-T infrastructure.. in new safety…risks’;  
 
When building new TEN-T network (both core and comprehensive network) it needs to 
comply with minimum safety standards in line with new requirements of the RISM Directive 
2019/1936 and the new EU KPI on infrastructure safety.  
 
2. Supports finding of positive societal benefits from changes for public health avoiding 
accidents and increasing active travel cited in the inception Impact Assessment.  
 
3. Calls for inclusion to protect also Vulnerable Road Users (VRUs) especially in the ‘urban 
nodes’. As cited in the Inception Impact Assessment under 1b: ‘strengthening urban  and  
transport  nodes (including  multi-modal  terminals and  transfer facilities to active mobility  
solutions)’. ETSC supports specifically channelling funds for urban mobility also to support 
increasing the safety of pedestrians and cyclists. 
 
4. TEN-T Guidelines could be strengthened ‘strongly encourage’ compliance with the RISM 
Directive 2019/1936 in ‘urban nodes’. 
 
5. Promote the construction of safe cycling infrastructure, by including the EuroVelo cycle 
network as part of the TEN-T and earmark CEF funds for its continued realisation. 
 
6. ETSC supports the finding of the inception report under Problem 2 on the ‘lack of 
preparation of the TEN-T for digital transition’. ETSC  would  recommend  starting  with 
preparing  certified  sections  of  roads  which  meet  minimum  performance  standards for 
automated vehicles. They will need to comply with infrastructure performance covering 
visibility, state of repair of traffic signs, signals and road markings. Some elements may be 
covered by upcoming ‘common specifications’ due for adoption in 2022 under RISM.  
 
7. Motorway TEN-T infrastructure  may  also  have  to  be  adapted  as  well  to  allow  for  the 
requirements of automated traffic. For example, there may be the need for arrangements to 
enable drivers to re-engage in the driving task before leaving the motorway. 
 
8. A fail-safe/fault tolerant architecture is furthermore required to guarantee as nearly as is 
practicable that automated vehicles operate in a safe state in any event or under adverse 
conditions.  This will require investments for  upgrades  and  maintenance.  This will also 
lead to improvements in safety for conventional vehicles. 
 
9. Initiatives leading to deployment of C-ITS such as C-roads should be extended to the whole 
TEN-T network to maximise safety potential and benefits to all road users.  
 
10. Within the TEN-T Guidance recognise the need to support public transport as this is a safe 
mode.

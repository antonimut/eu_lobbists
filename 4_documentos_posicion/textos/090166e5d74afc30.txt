 
 
 
 
FEPORT Response to the Roadmap regarding the revision of 
the TEN-T Guidelines  
 
 
 
1. Introduction 
 
FEPORT, the European organization representing 12 25 private port companies and terminals  
welcomes the possibility to provide feedback regarding the roadmap on the revision of the TEN-
T guidelines . FEPORT supports any effort towards the completion of the TEN -T core and 
comprehensive networks and agrees it  is necessary to align the TEN -T guidelines to the EU´s 
increased environmental and digital ambitions. 
 
We subscribe to the TEN -T´s policy aim of promoting multimodal mobility and, in that context, 
consider that all actions aiming at improving seaports’ connections to rail freight , inland 
waterways and short sea shipping connections will certainly contribute to achieve the ambitions 
of the Green Deal and the recently published strategy for sustainable and smart mobility. Yet, 
investment policies should equally focus on the connectivity of core a nd non-core seaports, and 
should be based on demand. Moreover, it is crucial that EU -funded projects also benefit from 
private invesments. In this respect, FEPORT would like to remind that its members have invested 
56 billion euros during the last ten years.  
  
In line with the challenges adressed in the roadmap, this paper suggests to take into account the 
below considerations. 
 
 
2. Digitialization should be seen as a means to improve connectivity and reduce 
emisisons, but can only fully develop if clear rules of governance on data -sharing 
are adopted 
 
Private port companies and terminals indeed welcome the EU’s aim to better prepare the TEN-T 
for the digital transition in transport  but underline the importance to look beyond the physical 
infrastructure network and double down efforts to improve the ICT infrastructure along the TEN-
T core and comprehensive networks. 
 
All actors of the maritime logistics chain could benefit from these increased digitalization efforts. 
For example, interconnected systems allow port terminals to have better information about 
which mode of transport will collect the unloaded cargo/ container. This information enables

terminals to better manage the allocation  of slots  on their yard, allowing them to reduce the 
number of container moves and by extension emissions. 
 
Moreover, digitalization can contribute to improved communications and data-sharing between 
actors across the logistics chain which can help reduce emissions, congestion and companies´ 
operational costs. Both improved Business to Government (B2G) and Business to Business (B2B) 
communication have a tremendous potential of improving th e efficiency and environmental 
performance of supply chains. 
 
Aside from investments in ICT infrastructure, it is important to focus on clear rules of governance 
for data-sharing in order to establish trust between all actors and seize the full potential of B2B 
and B2G electronic information exchanges.  
 
In addition to the points addressed above, it should always be borne in mind that digitalization 
can be a means to reduce operational and administrative costs as well as emissions. Digitalization 
can be a tool to facilitate low-cost, clean and safe transport, but should not be viewed as a goal in 
itself. 
 
 
3. Remove local bottlenecks to improve intermodal connections and actively involve 
port terminals in the governance of the corridors 
 
Seaport terminals function as a connecting point between the main transport modes and 
therefore fully endorse the TEN-T policy aim of promoting multimodal mobility as mentioned in 
the roadmap and fully recognize its decarbonizing potential. 
 
When promoting multimodality, it is of paramount importance to improve the connections of 
seaports to inland waterways , rail freight corridors  and short sea shipping connections,  which 
will help increase the volumes moved by these transport modes in line with the ambitions of the 
Green Deal.  
 
It is indeed crucial to have a comprehensive strategy on the overall development of every 
corridor. However, for true intermodal connectivity, it is just as necessary to focus on the removal 
of local bottlenecks in view of ensuring the seamless transshipment of cargo from one mode onto 
the other.  
 
As port terminals experience the effects of local bottlenecks due to their close connection to 
hinterland infrastructure, they should be granted a significant role in the management of the 
various TEN-T corridors. 
 
To increase the modal share of rail freight, its position vis -à-vis passenger traffic should be re -
assessed. Rail Freight should be assigned equal importance when it comes to path allocation. In 
addition, more flexible systems for timetabling could be i nvestigated and the development and 
modernization of freight only corridors should be a priority.  
 
The Rail Freight Corridors Regulation and the TEN -T Regulation should be reviewed in a consi stent 
manner. It is appropriate that these two Regulations should be addressed together given their mutual 
importance to one another.  
 
The revision must also introduce clear rights for rail freight when operating on the corridors. For 
instance, legislation should guarantee that when a freight train is operating according to schedule, its 
operations will not be disturbed to accommodate other traffic.  
 
The European institutions must also address capacity management. The COVID -19 pandemic has 
shown that when rail freight has a sufficient and legally secured quantity and quality of infrastructure 
slots, it can provide an attractive and reliable service. Different options to address capacity 
management should be assessed during the impact assessment of the Rail Freight Corridors 
Regulation. 
All m ulti – and intermodal connection s should be improved through a combination of public 
funding, e.g. through CEF projects, as well as private investment. One way of stimulating private 
investments is to recognize the contribution to climate change mitigation of multimodal seaport 
terminals under the EU Taxonomy criteria while at the same time aligning the TEN-T guidelines 
to these criteria.  
 
Yet, when public money is invested in terminal capacity, this should at all times b e based on 
demand. Distortions of the Level Playing Field should be avoided, in particular between terminals 
operating in the same area, of which some receive public funding, while others do not.  
 
Finally, efforts to improve the connectivity of ports and terminals should not merely focus on the 
TEN-T core network, but should likewise address rail and inland waterways’ connections of non-
core seaports. 
 
 
4. Adopt a technology neutral approach and allow for transitional solutions when 
rolling out Alternative Fuels Infrastructure along the core TEN-T network  
 
Infrastructure for alternative fuels and energy solutions has a significant potential to reduce 
emissions of ships at sea as well as at berth. FEPORT therefore understands  the regulator’s 
intention to integrate the requirements of the Alternative Fuels Infrastructure Directive into the 
TEN-T Guidelines. 
 
However, when equipping the core TEN -T ports with green refuelling and recharging 
infrastructure, a technology neutral approach should be adhered to. The pathway to the 
decarbonization of shipping is still uncertain and will be based on a variety of fuel a nd energy 
solutions. Specific solutions should therefore not be prescribed in order to stimulate research and 
innovation and prevent stranded assets. 
 
At the same time, LNG should still be allowed as a transitional solution as in many cases it is the 
cleanest maritime fuel available. Moreover, as it was specifically prescribed by article 6(1) of the 
AFI Directive, investments have been made accordingly.  
 
Above all, it should in all cases be taken into account  that the installment of refueling 
infrastructure is a commercial decision which is based on demand . A demand-based approach 
should form the basis of any effort to increase the uptake of alternative fuels by shipping. As a 
means to prevent stranded investments, it could be considered to impose requirements on ships 
to use OPS infrastructure that is offered in ports when at berth. 
 
 
5. Concluding remarks 
 
FEPORT fully supports the revision of the TEN -T guidelines in view to adapt them to the EU’s 
increased green and digital ambitions. Investments into multimodal connections and rules of 
governance for data-sharing can be an efficient means  to realize such ambitions, together wit h 
the further development of green refueling and recharging infrastructure. However, public 
investments should always abide by a technology neutral approach and be backed by the private 
sector. A demand-based approach should remain the rule and the impacts of public investments 
on the Level Playing Field should not be overlooked.

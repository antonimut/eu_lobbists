About Wacker Chemie AG 
Wacker Chemie AG (WACKER) is a leading specialty chemical company active in the silicone, polymer, life 
sciences and hyperpure silicon markets, with a strong manufacturing base in Europe. The hyperpure silicon, 
for which WACKER’s business division POLYSILICON remains the only European manufacturer, is supplied 
to the photovoltaics and semiconductor industries. With an energy consumption of 9.3 TWh (thereof 4.8 TWh 
electricity) in 2019 and about one million tons CO2 regulated under the EU ETS, WACKER remains reliant on 
European carbon leakage protection mechanisms to uphold global competitiveness. 
 
Introduction: Supporting the reduction of F-gases with state-of-the-art technology 
 
Refrigeration systems with safety refrigerants [F-gases] are an integral part of the production process 
of polysilicon (hyperpure silicon), which is the basic material for solar panels and semi -conductors. 
These safety refrigerants have been carefully chosen to reduce the potential hazards in the systems, 
especially with regards to flammability and explosivity , while maintaining the necessary 
temperatures. In total, WACKER POLYSILICON operates 53 industrial refrigeration plants at two 
sites in Germany. 
 
The industrial plants are operated by our own qualified personnel, monitored around the clock and 
maintained according to latest standards  and in accordance with Regulation (EU) 517/2014 . 
WACKER POLYSILICON has over the last years made significant financial investments and 
technical improvements to re duce the environmentally harmful effects of F -gases. Besides the 
technical conversion of certain plants  to lower emitting refrigerants , these measures also  include 
improved state-of-the-art leakage detection systems with sensors for the purpose of detecting and 
eliminating leaks.  
 
As a result of these measures, WACKER  POLYSILICON has managed to reduce the leakage rate 
to low numbers at around 1 % of the hold-up volume. Only about 20 kilo tons of CO2 equivalents are 
emitted annually, which corresponds to less than 0.2 % of the about 15 million tons of CO2 equivalent 
of F-gases emitted in Germany in 2018, according to the German Federal Environment Agency.  
 
 
Review of F-gas Regulation (EU) 517/2014: Key issues at stake 
 
WACKER actively supports the reduction of F-gases emissions from its production via the various 
measures mentioned above. However, since entry into force of Regulation (EU) 517/2014, there has 
been no substantial technological progress that would allow for a cost-efficient emission reduction 
for the kind of industrial refrigeration plants operated by WACKER POLYSILICON. Site-specific 
technological barriers and safety issues remain in place that render the conversion to natural 
refrigerants unsafe or economically unfeasible. Depending on regulatory changes, investments 
could amount up to 100 – 200 € Mio., which corresponds up to 25 % of the turnover for the polysilicon 
division. This presents a disproportionate cost for the theoretical emission reduction of 20 kt of CO2 
equivalents and would significantly burden the global competitiveness of European polysilicon. 
 
Moreover, a revision of current GWP values could lead to stranded assets for WACKER, as 
conversion and retrofitting of plants have already taken place. For the purpose of investment 
certainty, any decision to change regulatory requirements must therefore exempt from its 
scope already implemented conversion plans and measures since 2015. Rather, a 
changeover to other refrigerants should be timed with the end of the service life of existing 
systems, which would maximize cost-efficiency and enable an update of safety concepts. 
 
To conclude, the changeover to technically feasible and safe (non-fluorinated) alternatives 
would entail disproportionate costs  for WACKER that would significantly harm the global 
competitiveness of polysilicon, without reaching any substantial emission reduction.

Regulatory and investment certainty: Avoid stranded assets and disproportionate 
economic costs 
- The industrially operated, large-scale refrigeration plants at WACKER POLYSILICON 
represent the state of the art and are extremely capital-intensive. The end of service life of the 
plants will only be reached in 25 to 30 years. The conversion to natural refrigerants, such as 
ethane or ammonia, is considered unsafe and/or technically very challenging due to site-
specific conditions relating to reactive risks with other substances. 
- Based on the maximum limit of 2500 GWP, WACKER POLYSILICON has set up a conversion 
plan for its refrigerant systems  for 2030. Moreover, significant investments have already been 
made in the conversion  of several plants in  order to comply  with regulatory requirements 
stipulated by the current F -gas Regulation. These measures  aim at replacing high-GWP 
refrigerants ( e.g. R507, GWP 3985)  with lower emitting refrigerants  below 2500 GWP (e.g. 
R410A, GWP 2088).  
- A change in the regulatory requirements could withdraw the legal and investment certainty, 
thereby jeopardize previous investments and introducing disproportionate economic costs: 
o Stranded assets [~2 Mio €] would occur for several plants that have already been 
converted to lower emitting refrigerants (e.g. R507 to R410A). Efficient and state-of-the-
art plants would have to be scrapped before the end of their service life and the 
conversion plans for remaining plants would have to be withdrawn, imposing economic 
costs and technical challenges for the industrial site. 
o Additional investments amounting up to three-digit million range (100-200€ Mio.) 
would have to be mobilized in a short period of time, if a changeover to technically 
feasible and safe (non-fluorinated) alternatives would become necessary. This roughly 
corresponds to 25 % of the turnover for WACKER POLYSILICON, which presents 
disproportionate economic cost for the theoretical annual emission reduction of 20 kt 
CO2 equivalents. 
- WACKER therefore urges the Commission to exempt already implemented conversion 
plans and measures since the entry into force of the regulation in 2015 from the scope 
of future changes to the regulatory requirements for GWP limits, as mentioned in Art. 
13(3) of the Regulation (EU) 517/2014.  
- For the purpose of regulatory and investment certainty, this must also include the 
continuation of the exemption from the ban on the use of refrigeration of products below 
- 50 °C until the end of the service life of existing installations (Article 13, § 3). 
- Innovation support could significantly help the promotion of technological solutions and 
facilitate the changeover of industrial refrigeration systems by overcoming the high 
economic costs. 
 
Maximizing safety: Time the changeover of refrigerants to the end of the service life 
- Changing to alternative, non-fluorinated refrigerants like ethane, propane or ammonia always 
entails an increase in the hazard potential due to the introduction of additional flammable and 
explosive or highly reactive substances. Depending on the hazard potential on-site, a 
changeover could cause for a conflict with requirements of national and European legal 
standards, in particular with the Seveso II Directive and the German requirements for emission 
control ("Störfallverordnung"). 
- Introducing and utilizing non-fluorinated refrigerants requires an entirely new and 
comprehensive process safety concept for the refrigeration system and the production process 
it serves. From a safety point of view, a changeover to other refrigerants therefore only makes 
sense at the end of the service life of the existing systems. 
- WACKER therefore urges the Commission to consider safety issues relating to a 
changeover to technically feasible and non-fluorinated alternatives. By timing the 
changeover of refrigerants to the end of the service life, it further avoids the 
unnecessary scrapping of efficient and low-emitting plants.

Safeguard competitiveness: Maintain efficient European polysilicon production 
- WACKER uses refrigeration systems with safety refrigerants [F-gases] as an integral part of the 
production process of polysilicon , which is the basic material for solar panels and semi -
conductors. As the only producer in Europe, WACKER operates in an environment characterized 
by extremely harsh international competition, where production costs must be kept at a level 
playing field with non-EU producers, mostly from China.  
- In the case of a changeover to technically feasible and safe (non-fluorinated) alternatives for the 
plants used for the production of polysilicon , it is estimated that investments amounting up to 
between 100-200€ Mio. would be required. As this roughly corresponds to 25 % of the turnover 
for WACKER’s POLYSILICON business division in 2019, it would present a disproportionate cost 
for the theoretical annual average emission reduction of 20 kt CO2 equivalents. 
- These costs would have to be added to the production costs and price of polysilicon over the 
time period of amortization ( about 10 years). As polysilicon is traded on global markets with 
extremely narrow price margins due to pressure coming from Chinese producers, any additional 
costs and price increases will significantly harm the competitiveness of European polysilicon. 
- A deteriorated competitiveness of WACKER’s polysilicon vis-à-vis Chinese producers would in 
return increase the risk of production and emission relocation to countries that do not impose 
corresponding reduction requirements or have less efficient technology , therefore risking an 
increase rather than decrease of the global emission burden. In fact, an additional 3 million tons 
of CO2 would be emitted annually, if European polysilicon was produced in China.    
- WACKER therefore urges the Commission to consider the negative effects on economic 
competitiveness and global emission burden. It is therefore in line with the objectives of 
the revision of the Regulation (EU) 517/2014 to ensure the continued operation of existing, 
safe plants with low to negligible emissions.

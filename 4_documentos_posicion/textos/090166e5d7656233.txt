Siemens Energy welcomes and supports the EU’s 
ambition to become climate neutral by 2050. 
Siemens Energy welcomes the opportunity to provide feed-
back on the review of the Fluorinated Greenhouse Gas Regu-
lation (F-Gas Regulation, 517/2014) regarding the phase -out 
of SF6 in high-voltage (HV) applications. 
 
Siemens Energy fully supports the European Union’s ambition 
to become the world’s first carbon-neutral continent by 2050. 
Internationally, we’re fully committed to supporting the Paris 
Agreement and the UN Sustainable Development Goals. For 
more than 150 years we have been working on the transfor-
mation of the energy world. Our ambition as Siemens Energy 
is to achieve climate-neutrality in our own operations by 2030 
and to cover 100% of our own electricity consumption from 
renewable sources by 2023 . We believe that the European 
Commission’s Green Deal needs to be not only a deal for car-
bon neutrality but also a deal for greater competitiveness and 
investments in environmentally friendly and safe technolo-
gies.  
 
Why phasing out SF6 is necessary and feasible 
A phase-out of SF6 in the HV electricity sector is possible. SF6-
free technologies are available and are being introduced step-
wise in the HV sector. To achieve carbon neutrality by 2050, 
the European Commission announced that it will align existing 
regulation with this overarching goal and promote the neces-
sary innovations. The F-Gas regulation is an important tool to 
bring Europe closer this goal. It therefore needs to be re-
viewed and aligned with the mission of the Green Deal as soon 
as possible.  
 
Six reasons why phas ing out SF6 in HV is necessary and 
should start as soon as possible:  
 
1. SF6 is the most harmful greenhouse gas : The global 
warming potential of SF6 is 23,500 times higher than the 
global warming potential of CO2. This means that one kg 
of SF6 leaking into the atmosphere has the same green-
house gas effect as 23,500 kg of CO2. In addition, SF6 stays 
in the atmosphere up to 3,200 years.  
  
2. Expanding the phase-out of SF6 to the electricity sector 
is feasible: This high greenhouse gas potential has let the 
EU in 2014 to the decision to ban SF 6 for most applica-
tions – except for the electricity sector due to lack of al-
ternatives. This lack has been filled with technologically 
and economically viable alternatives for the HV sector. 
The industry is ready to phase out SF 6 step by step and 
use SF 6-free alternatives in electrical equipment.  SF6-
free technologies are ready and are introduced stepwise 
in the HV sector. With more than 10 years of operational 
experience, they are in production and can become an 
industrial growth opportunity (Figure 1).  
Figure 1: Operational Experiences with Siemens Energy installa-
tions: More than 1400 units are ordered, and more than 300 units 
are successfully in operation. 
 
3. Phasing out SF6 is the best way to achieve climate neu-
trality: Limiting emissions by using state-of-art SF6 tech-
nology with lower quantities of gas and lower leakage 
rates isn’t enough. In mid-term this would lead to an in-
crease in SF6 emissions driven by electrification, the rapid 
development of decentralised energy systems and e-mo-
bility infrastructure.  
 
4. Phasing out SF6 drives jobs and technology leadership :   
Europe’s global success is based on its technology lead-
ership and strong sustainability credentials. SF6-free HV 
equipment is a huge opportunity for Europe to regain our 
innovative edge, expand our technology leadership, and 
keep jobs in Europe. To fully leverage our first-mover ad-
vantage and translate years of research and develop-
ment into j obs, industrial growth, and competitiveness, 
Europe will need to act quickly and boldly. 
 
5. EU leads in ambition but lags in action : Europe has set 
itself a clear and ambitious target to be the first carbon -
neutral continent by 2050. But the EU is still la cking be-
hind in phasing out SF6 where alternatives are available. 
South Korea is already working on a law to phase out SF6 
and California is in the final stages to do the same with

The phase-out timeline should be implemented as proposed.  
As described above , a stepwise phase -out of SF 6 in new HV 
equipment is necessary and feasible. The report  rightfully 
acknowledges that for voltage levels up to 145 kV SF6-free al-
ternatives are available, commercialised and in successful op-
eration. In 2023 there will be three years or more of opera-
tional experience for the full 145 kV portfolio. For the voltage 
levels up to 245 kV, instrument transformers are already in op-
eration and circuit-breaker and GIS will be fully available in 
2025. A phase-out can start after three years of operation in 
2028. For the voltage levels up to 420 kV, voltage transformers 
are also already in operation. 420 kV Circuit-breakers and GIS 
will be available in 2027 with the phase-out after three years 
of operation in 2030. 
 
Clean air GIS is a viable alternative when space is restricted. 
Clean air insulations without F -gases and greenhouse gases 
have a lower insulation capability. This can however be com-
pensated by higher pressure or innovative design features, 
such as low-power current and voltage transformers. These 
measures lead to comparable footprints like conventional SF6-
GIS. The substitution of GIS of the first generations is easily 
possible: the new Clean Air GIS bay width is reduced by 60% 
from 2,4 meter to 1 meter. 
 
Costs evaluations for carbon neutral switchgear should in-
clude CAPEX, OPEX and end -of-life costs, including costs for 
environment and society. The reasons for t he increased in-
vestment cost factor for alternative switchgear are: (1)  lower 
insulation capability of clean  air insulation without F -gases 
and GHG, which can be compensated by higher pressure and 
housing design modifications; (2) low er sourcing quantities; 
(3) low er production quantities. However, once the order, 
sourcing and production quantities ramp up, the CAPEX cost 
will decrease. Together with savings in operational costs  and 
end-of-life costs, the total cost of ownership and for society 
will decrease. Regulation can accelerate the shift to CO 2 neu-
tral transmission technologies by smart and innovative 
measures, considering the cost of SF6 and other F-gases emit-
ted during their life -time and especially at end -of-life, when 
man-made gases must be destroyed to prevent further cli-
mate impact.

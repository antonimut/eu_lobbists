Review of the requirements for packaging and 
other measures to prevent packaging waste 
Feedback on the Public Consultation 
 
21 December 2020 
 
MedTech Europe, the European trade association for the medical technology industry including diagnostics, 
medical devices and digital health, welcomes the opportunity to contribute to the public consultation regarding 
the review of the requirements for packaging and other measures to prevent packaging waste. 
 
Medical devices and in vitro  diagnostics save  and improve  people’s lives, support  in the diagnosis and 
treatment of diseases, and keep people in good health. The legislative framework that guarantees the safety 
and performance of medical devices has been recently overhauled by the adoption of Regulation (EU) 
2017/745 on Medical Devices (MDR) and Regulation (EU) 2017/746 on In vitro Diagnostic medical devices 
(IVDR). 
 
Overall, MedTech Europe supports the broad objective to review the essential requirements of the Directive 
94/62/EC on Packaging and Packaging Waste. This review aims to ensure the good functioning of the internal 
market through legal harmonisation on packaging. Simultaneously, it aims to tackle the negative impact that 
packaging as well as packaging waste could have on health and the environment.

13. What addi tional EU level measure(s), if any, would you recommend to meet any of the policy 
objectives set out above? 
The medical technology industry already strives towards the use of the least possible amount of packaging 
to reduce the carbon footprint in the industry’s supply chain. In addition, the medical technology industry 
believes it is important to enabling innovation in recycling capabilities to take advantage of newer and more 
advanced recycling technologies. 
 
1) Therefore, MedTech Europe recommend s some targeted measures in instances where options are 
limited and current provisions prevent the sector from eliminating packaging, such as: 
• The safeguard of shelf life, sterilisation and performance of healthcare products; 
• The delivery of child-resistant packaging solutions; 
• The preservation of the product integrity to avoid waste; and 
• The specific regulatory requirements for medical devices that hinder the use of: 
o post-consumer recycled (PCR) content in primary packaging materials; 
o reusable packaging for active implants; 
o etc. 
 
2) The differentiation among the types of packaging should also be considered. For e xample, some 
primary packaging interacts with instruments or devices and have a function or supports the function 
of an instrument, as described below: 
• Automatic opening and closing require an interface at the packaging for the instrument. 
• The piercing of some packaging needs to fulfil specific requirements. These sometimes require 
the use of specialised polymer composites that are not necessarily environmentally optimised. 
 
3) In addition, the medical technology industry supports the establishment of harmonised requirements 
between EU Member States on recycling systems and waste collection . We further support the 
distribution of responsibility among all actors in the value chain. Furthermore , MedTech Europe

believes that an official overview of regulated substances  and applicable products, thresholds, and 
exemptions should b e provided covering  all EU legislations regarding substances . This would 
simplify the legal obligation of the manufacturer to comply with all different requirements.  
 
4) Finally, in order to support the second objective  on reducing packaging waste , MedTech Europe 
strongly recommends that the European Commission enlarges the scope of the Regulation (EU) No 
207/2012 to instructions for use for all medical devices covered by the Regulation  (EU) 2017/745 
that are used by professional users  (i.e., healthcare professionals). The enlarged scope of this 
Regulation will help meet the policy objectives in the following ways: 
• The elimination of paper instructions for use (IFU) from each sales package. 
• The use of electronic IFU is likely to encourage manufacturers to use smaller sales package 
sizes, further reducing waste and allowing more storage space in the hospitals.  
 
Electronic IFU will facilitate compliance with the Directive 2008/98/EC on waste (Waste Framework 
Directive). Currently, manufacturers must provide paper IFU in all official EU languages for the many 
device categories not falling under Regulation (EU) No 207/2012. At the same time, manufacturers 
can face charges and penalties for not complying with national legislation that transposes the Waste 
Framework Directive . Wider use of an electronic format IFU would bring alignment among the 
different pieces of EU and national legislation s. Paper copies can still  be made available free of 
charge at any time when requested, as envisioned currently under Regulation (EU) No 207/2012. 
The e lectronic use IFU for professional users are already a common practice worldwide (USA, 
Canada, Brazil, Turkey, etc.) and seen to not pose any additional risks. 
 
14. Which are the most important reasons for you to have decided that you do not support specific

In general, MedTech Europe supports the aforementioned efforts. Equally, it is crucial to ensure that safety 
and product integrity are not compromised . To this end, MedTech Europe would like to note some  patient 
safety risks stemming from: 
• Poor traceability (use of commercially available and viable mechanically recycled content) ; 
• The difficulty of controlling the materials and processes (loss of functional equivalency of materials 
leading to extensive revalidations needed for sterile medical devices; 
• Reassessment of the risk profile of recycled content packaging materials; 
 
In addition to this, there are limitations subject to product requirements, for example:

Questions 16 - 18 - Impacts of Covid-19 
 
16. In your experience, has COVID-19 pandemic impacted the demand for packaging? Yes  
 
16.1 What have been the impacts on the demand for packaging? 
As far as the medical technology industry is concerned, there has been no significant increase in the amount 
of packaging per device. However, the large increase in the amount of Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) 
and medical devices, including in vitro diagnostic medical devices , purchased in the context of the current 
pandemic has resulted in the increase of the associated packaging.

18.1 What have been the impacts on the packaging supply chain and do you expect them to persist

The packaging production supply chains have not been significantly disrupted by the COVID -19 pandemic. 
However, they have faced challenges. For certain materials, it was challenging to keep up with the demand. 
For example, vaccines are normally filled in medical glass bottles and the COVID-19 pandemic has led to an 
increase in the demand  of these medical glass bottles . As a consequence , the delivery time has been

Final remarks 
The ongoing environmental and sustainability opportunities are beneficial for global industries, communities, 
and our planet. MedTech Europe strongly supports the protection of the environment and considers it also a 
human health issue. Our members support global efforts to minimi se the negative environmental impact 
caused by plastic products and packaging . Equally,  they have set goals to reduce their use without 
compromising product safety and quality.  However, the medical technology sector is also governed by 
regulations and it would be crucial to ensure that these packaging requirements are not in conflict with these 
regulations. 
When considering packaging waste, medical device packaging should be differentiated from other consumer 
package recycling such as cereal boxes, beverage bottles and detergent packaging. The medical device 
packaging often plays a crucial role in maintaining the safety, sterility and useability of the actual medical 
device. Hence, any efforts to reduce or minimise the packaging could result in an increased risk of damaged 
and/or defective products at the expense of an improved packaging waste profile. Equally, when a medical 
device packaging is required to form the device’s sterile barrier system, in particular, it should be considered 
as a component of the sterile medical device itself. These devices cannot be sold or used without packaging. 
Furthermore, s ome of our members have reported a lack of consistency , in the national framework, on 
whether certain products fall under the scope of the Directive 94/62/EC on Packaging and Packaging Waste. 
The interpretation of packaging definitions may even vary within Member States with regards to  producer 
responsibility and essential re quirements. This could lead to inconsistencies  in the approach by producers, 
regarding certain medical devices such as intravenous solution bags, syringes etc. Therefore, we would like 
to highlight the need for clearer and consistent packaging definitions. 
The legislative framework should prioritise actions targeting plastics that are known to cause environmental 
harm while ensuring full access to the plastics that have a key role in healthcare functioning. The legislator 
should further take into account  the highly regulated nature of our products  as it can often limit or prevent 
changes in packaging material. 
MedTech Europe is ready to fully engage with all relevant stakeholders in further discussions to develop and 
implement the reviewed essential requirements.

 
      Annex 1: GHG equivalent lifecycle emissions of different heating systems   Achieving carbon neutrality by 2050 as laid down in the European Green Deal is the overall target. EU legislation such as the F-gas Regulation should remain consistent with this target. Heating and cooling are responsible for 50% of EU final energy demand, and most of that heat is generated by burning fossil fuels. Decarbonising the heating and cooling sector therefore plays a key role in achieving carbon neutrality by 2050. In the European Commission’s impact assessments, the energy efficiency first principle, electrification of the heating sector and an increased share of renewables in heating and cooling are crucial to achieve climate neutrality. In its EU Strategy for Energy System Integration, the EC aims at a 40% electrification of heating in residential buildings and 65% electrification of heating in the service sector by 2030. Assuming that the “energy efficiency first” principle is applied, most of these buildings should be equipped with one or several heat pumps. This implies a quadrupling of the current amount of heat pumps by 2030.  The provisions of the F-gas Regulation should therefore ensure that the quadrupling of heat pumps by 2030 is not slowed down unnecessarily. If thresholds for heat pump technologies are artificially limiting the availability of renewable and efficient heating and cooling technologies such as heat pumps, more investors will invest in traditional heating systems using fossil fuel, creating a contradictory effect on the energy and climate targets since over its use phase, emissions from a heat pump, even containing refrigerants with a certain GWP, are much lower than emissions from any traditional heating system using fossil fuel (see graph below). A heat pump replacing a traditional heating system using fossil fuel saves at least 50% of CO2 emissions.

It is obvious that heat pump technology (even containing refrigerants with a certain GWP) reduces GHG equivalent emissions by more than half compared to a gas heater.

Availability of alternatives to fluorinated greenhouse gases in switchgear and 
related equipment, including medium-voltage secondary switchgear:  
Comments by German ZVEI electrical equipment manufacturers for 
applications > 52 kV on the EC’s report C (2020) 6635 
22. Dezember 2020 
ZVEI welcome the publication of the report "Assessing the availability of alternatives to 
fluorinated greenhouse gases in switchgear and related equipment” by the European 
Commission on 30 September 2020. 
ZVEI strongly support the Commission’s targets to strengthen all efforts for climate protection 
and to achieve climate neutrality for Europe by 2050. To this purpose, they appreciate the 
general view of the present report which correctly summarises the status of availability of 
SF6 free products. 
The report is a very good starting point for the debate on the necessary further development 
of the F-gas Regulation. It comprehensively outlines the different application fields of 
electrical equipment and gives a transparent overview of the state of the technological 
progress in the development of alternatives to SF6. The authors recognise that SF6-free 
products are available and successful in operation for some specific applications, as also 
shown in the T&D Europe report published in March 2020.1 They conclude that "policy 
intervention is likely to be needed to trigger a transition" to SF6-free products where 
alternatives are more costly. Moreover, the report provides orientation for the future 
legislative framework which brings planning security for the industry. 
Why this paper? 
Manufacturers for applications > 52 kV are willing to make their contributions to reduce the 
usage of SF6 equipment and SF6 emissions. 
Therefore, where alternatives to SF6 products are available (with operational experiences, 
from different manufacturers) and offer an overall benefit for climate protection while 
maintaining at least the same level of safety and protection of human health, they generally 
should be widely used as soon as possible. 
With this short paper, ZVEI want to present additional remarks to support the work on a 
review of the F-gas Regulation with regards to SF6 in electrical equipment and applications > 
52 kV. For a transition to alternative solutions, numerous steps for the development, testing 
and adaptation to specific requirements need to be considered. 
Manufacturers are committed to intensify their experience by pilot projects in a wider scale, 
because new technologies have to be tested over an adequate period of time. To allow a 
transition to SF6 free products, significant investments are required by the manufacturers to 
develop and industrialise SF6-free product portfolios and prepare for mass production. An 
adequate regulation shall support such a process.

Transition times 
In the following proposals, ‘transition time’ means the time span from the entry into force of a 
revised F-gas Regulation to the date as of which the process of development, piloting, 
industrialisation and homologation is completed. This would be the earliest starting point for 
any restrictive regulatory provision. ZVEI assume the revised F-gas Regulation to enter into 
force by end 2022. 
ZVEI propose the following approaches and the applicability of the respective provisions to 
newly installed equipment for high voltage applications: 
- In high voltage 72.5 kV, a transition time of 2-3 years from the date when a revised F-
gas Regulation enters into force shall be considered. 
- For high voltage up to and including 145 kV, a transition time of 2-5 years from the 
date when a revised F-gas Regulation enters into force shall be considered. 
- For voltage levels above 145 kV, the reported transition time of 5-10 years including 3 
years of piloting time is appropriate. 
New provisions should in principal only address new installations. Already installed SF6 
equipment should be kept in operation until their end-of-life or in case of abnormal increases 
in leakage rates occur.

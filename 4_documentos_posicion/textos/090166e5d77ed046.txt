ECSLA Position Paper  
On the review of the EU F-Gas Regulation 
 
 
Introduction 
We would like to thank the Commission for you for this opportunity to submit our comments to the 
public consultation on the review of the F-Gas Regulation. 
 
ECSLA fully supports the F-Gas Regulation and congratulates the Commission on the successful 
reduction of emissions achieved within its framework thus far: 
 
The current Regulation, adopted  in 2014, together with rules on air -conditioning in cars are 
expected to reduce emissions by two-thirds by 2030 compared to 2014.The expected cumulative 
emission savings are 1.5 Gigatonnes of CO2 -equivalent b y 2030 and 5 Gigatonnes by 2050, 
according to the Commission. 
 
Reduction of emissions, the successful phase down of high global warming potential refrigerants 
and the ongoing phase out of high GWP refrigerants evidently confirm that the measures are 
delivering on their intended results. 
 
This clearly p roofs that the F -Gas Regulation is working and delivering on the goals set in a 
successful manner. 
 
 
 
I. The European Cold Storage and Logistics Association  
 
The European Cold Storage and Logistics Association (ECSLA) is the voice of the European cold 
storage and temperature controlled logistics and transport operators . Food quality and safety, 
consumer protection and respect for the environment are the guiding principles for ECSLA 
members. 
 
ECSLA represents food business operators who deal in particular with foodstuff but also 
pharmaceutical products, including vaccines, that need to be stored, transported and distributed 
in temperature controlled conditions. Our members ´ daily business is to  take care of these high 
value products but also of frozen and fresh (perishable) food as their service is to provide the cold 
conditions in the appropriate temperature range in which the goods entrusted to them are stored 
and transported. 
 
Our national as sociation members represent third party logistics providers in their countries. As 
third party logistics companies they provide the temperature controlled storage capacity to safely 
freeze, store and distribute raw materials such as fruit and vegetable as well as meats and fish 
products, essential ingredients such as milk and dairy products and all finished frozen food like 
ice-cream, readymade dishes, etc. 
 
ECSLA member companies are a crucial link in the food supply chain connecting producers and 
processing companies with retailers and ultimately with the consumer. ECSLA member 
companies are as well crucial in the distribution of vaccines and other pharmaceutical items that 
require to be stored, transported and distributed under strict temperature controlled conditions.

II. Use of HFC refrigerants among ECSLA members  
 
The temperature controlled storage capacity of ECSLA members relies on different solutions in 
terms of refrigerants depending on the what needs to be cooled, the activity of the cold store, but 
also depending on the Member States’ specific circumstances and legal requirements.  
 
Among members in Germany and Spain, for example, more than 90 % of the temperature 
controlled storage capacity of ECSLA members are currently cooled with refrigeration equipment 
that is operated with ammonia.  
 
Whereas for other countries, like e.g. France, we would like to use this opportunity to remind the 
Commission that the use of alternative refrigerants to HFCs, such as ammonia or CO2, was not 
always feasible as late as mid- to late 2000s. Cold stores built in those years, or anticipating on  
their obligations regarding the use of ODS (ozone depleting substances) could not avoid using 
HFCs, and for negative temperatures this means using HFC-R404A.  
 
In this context ECSLA members appreciate the clarity of the obje ctives of the F -Gas Regulation 
and the clarity on the deadlines. This has enabled our members to include the objectives of the F-
Gas Regulation in their long -time investment and business plans. T he planning period to build 
and realise temperature controlle d warehouses takes a lot of time as does getting the relevant 
permits from the competent authorities as this is a long process. Building a cold store therefore 
takes several years and requires extensive planning. 
 
We can confirm, however, that investment into new installations – investment plans run for 20 
years and more - are ongoing and members invest in long term sustainable solutions based on 
ammonia and CO2. Members are very much aware of the provisions of the F-Gas regulation and 
have made plans accordingly.  
 
It is however of utmost importance that these long term investments can rely on guaranteed 
planning and legal security.  
 
 
III. HFC refrigerants development (Availability and Price)  
 
Since the F -Gas Regulation entered into force, we have witnessed a reduced amount of HFC 
refrigerants on the market and a steep increase in prices for HFC refrigerants (+700 %). The 
market is clearly reacting as planned to the F-Gas Regulation. 
 
This development inevitably means t hat members face increased difficulties in maintaining 
existing equipment and their capacity of producing energy efficient refrigeration to preserve the 
foodstuffs their clients have entrusted them could be jeopardized. 
 
 
IV. European Green Deal 
This Commission recently adopted the European Green Deal Communication and proposed a 
Climate Law with the objective to step up EU ambition and to reach climate neutrality by 2050.

According to the Commission, “early and ambitious action to avoid emi ssions from fluorinated 
greenhouse gases (F -gases) is key to reaching these objectives, because Fgases are used in 
equipment (e.g. refrigeration and air -conditioning) that will be emitting these highly warming 
gases for decades.” 
 
The ambition by the Commission to streamline the F-Gas Regulation with the goals of the Green 
Deal and the Kigali amendment is understandable and laudable and the right course of action in 
principle. Having said that, however, we would like to strongly insist  that the ambition and targets 
set in all legislation should be compatible with each other and should not contradict each other or 
cancel each other out.  
 
Considering the continued need for F -Gases for the safe and efficient functioning and 
maintenance of existing equipment, w e don´t consider an increase in the HFC phase -down 
ambition feasible nor do we support the prohibition of the use of F-gases where feasible. 
 
 
V. Kigali Amendment 
Alignment of the F-Gas Regulation with the Montreal Protocol is a desirable goal. But it has to be 
done after careful assessment and b y making sure that the  goals laid down in the F -Gas 
Regulation are not compromised. 
 
At this point, we don´t see the need to add new phase -down steps beyond 2030 or to create a 
separate HFC production phase-down. 
 
 
VI. Energy Efficiency and Circular Economy 
We would like to reiterate and underline the importance of energy efficiency in the context of the 
Regulation. F-Gases cannot and should not be seen as an isolated policy topic. Its full potential 
can only be realised wi th the application of an overarching/holistic approach that takes into 
account also other objectives. Namely energy efficiency, the targets of the Green Deal but also 
the targets of the Circular Economy. 
 
 
VII. Implementation, Enforcement and Harmonization 
ECSLA would like to underline that the most important aspects of the F -Gas Regulation, or any 
other legislation for this matter, are proper implementation and enforcement. Making sure that the 
F-Gas Regulation is properly implemented and enforced in a harmonised way across the whole of 
the EU should be made appropriately sure before any revision of the existing rules are 
considered. Only the strict and full enforcement of the existing rules can deliver the full potential 
of the F-Gas Regulation in terms of emissions reduction. 
 
 
VIII. COVID-19 
The COVID pandemic has highlighted the importance of the temperature controlled logistics 
sector. The companies who are active in this sector have indeed been doing essential work since 
the start of the COVID  pandemic and continue to do so ever since. ECSLA member companies 
have been doing essential work by storing, transporting and distributing food across Europe 
during lockdowns.  
 
Now these companies will be crucial in providing the temperature controlled transport, storag e 
and distribution for those COVID vaccines that have strict temperature requirements and need to 
be kept under specific temperature conditions.

IX. No strengthening of existing Regulation required 
 
We realize that with the current F -Gas Regulation applicable since 1 January 2015, some 
stakeholders are already calling for a strengthening of the existing Regulation. Other 
stakeholders are arguing that the introduction of a concrete date as of which the  use of HFC 
refrigerants would be forbidden would be a better solution than the one applied by the F -Gas 
Regulation.  
 
From our point of view, the step-by-step phase-down of HFC refrigerants introduced by the F-Gas 
Regulation is the only sensible and reali stic way in order to achieve a continuous reduction of 
HFC refrigerants by1 January 2030. 
 
The unavoidable price increase due to the reduction of available HFC refrigerants on the market 
constitutes an additional efficient tool to reach the objective of th e F-Gas Regulation of reducing 
the use of GHG.  
 
An acceleration of the current deadlines and/or further decrease of the t onnes of CO2 equivalent 
put on the market in the upcoming years  (until 2030) and beyond 2030  could jeopardize the 
capacity of our members of maintaining an efficient cold chain for lack of refrigerants. Also, an 
anticipation of investment plans is economically not feasible.  
 
Indeed, a stricter regulation concerning the use of HFC refrigerants or  a fixed phase out date 
would not improve the situation where we are faced with a decreasing amount of available HFC 
refrigerants while at the same time prices are increasing. 
 
Quite on the contrary, many end -users would be faced with the pressure to abrup tly implement 
alternative solutions which would completely overwhelm not only them (end-users) but also  
equipment installers in terms of personnel, technical knowledge, organisatorial capabilities and 
economic means. 
 
Indeed as a matter of fact, operators of temperature controlled warehouse facilities have planned 
their investments according to the provisions and the deadlines of the F -Gas Regulation and are 
counting on the legal certainty as provided in the Regulation.  
 
The current regulatory framework c ombined with the market forces are contributing to forcing 
operators away from F-Gases. Keeping to the current framework is more efficient than changing 
the rules in the middle of the game.  
 
On top of that reports from our member associations show that te mperature controlled logistics 
companies are faced with a lack of qualified and experienced technicians of the refrigeration and 
air conditioning contractor sector, equipment installers and technicians who are not properly 
familiar with bigger ammonia operated refrigeration systems. 
 
We therefore call on the Commission to adequately take this situation i nto account during the 
revision of the F-Gas Regulation. 
 
 
X. Conclusion  
Temperature controlled logistics providers must be able to rely on the timeframe laid down in the 
F-Gas Regulation in particular to be able to plan in an economically sound way and with the 
guarantee of legal certainty.

EFCTC: F-Gas Public Consultation – complete answers 
 
Are you involved in any of the following activities with respect to F-gases? 
EFCTC represents the manufacturers of HFCs and HFOs for use in refrigeration, air-conditioning and heat 
pump equipment, foam insulation, solvents, medical inhalers, laboratories, firefighting and feedstock 
and process uses. 
Which F-gas sector are you active in? 
EFCTC represents the manufacturers and importers of HFCs and HFOs. 
 
5. To what extent has the F-gas Regulation been coherent with other EU and international legislation? 
Montreal Protocol Kigali Amendment:  The F-gas Regulation pre-dates the Kigali Amendment and , 
therefore, more in-depth analysis is required to ensure comparability on a 1:1 basis.  
Paris Agreement: the Paris Agreement is based upon the submission of Nationally Determined 
Contributions by regions and countries. The EU NDC takes into account the F-gas Regulation in its 
objectives.  
Waste: Transport of recovered F-gases across borders for reclamation/destruction must be facilitated by 
the F-gas Regulation. The existing cross border waste shipment regulation are a barrier to effective end 
of life treatment of F-Gases. 
Customs Legislation: The F-gas Regulation must facilitate strong enforcement by Member States through 
linking directly with Customs legislation. This must apply particularly for T1 Transit. 
 
7. To what extent have the Regulation's requirements been effective regarding its objectives (see 
question 3 above)? 
Data from the European Environment Agency have demonstrated that F-Gas emissions have been 
decreasing since 2014, and it can, therefore, be concluded that the F-Gas Regulation’s provisions are 
effective.  
However, to improve the operation of the F-gas Regulation, it is imperative that there is a requirement 
for Member States to evaluation the log-books and hence data about leakage, recycling, recovery and 
reclaim – and ultimately emissions.

8. Have the following factors presented important challenges for implementing the F-gas Regulation? 
Illegal Imports: Have been recognised as a major issue,that is jeopardizing the goals of the F-Gas 
Regulation. A number of steps have been taken but it is essential that further actions are taken such as 
improved enforcement, harmonised and dissuasive penalties, a rapid introduction of the single Customs 
window and changes in the T1 Transit system to ensure the registration in the F-gas portal of all 
operators.  
Misuse of the Quota System: It is essential that quota system is enforced and that Customs Authorities 
stop and confiscate HFC imports without quotas as well as monitor companies that are importing for 
“re-export”. The system must not disadvantage those companies that have invested to build the 
infrastructure to safely handle HFCs and provide services such as recovery, recycling and reclaim. 
High Number of new market players: The New Entrant Reserve system is revised to ensure that all 
participants are legitimate companies and that they have the ability to handle safely HFCs.  
According to point 10 of the F-gas Regulation (EU) No 517/2014: 
The use of refrigerants with a very high global warming potential of 2 500 or more to service or maintain 
refrigeration equipment with a charge size of 40 tonnes of CO2 equivalent or more should be banned. 
(Exemption: GPW of max 2 500 with a charge size of <40 tonnes of CO2 equivalent) 
According to Art. 15(2) of the F-gas Regulation (EU) No 517/2014: 
This Article shall not apply to producers or importers of less than 100 tonnes of CO2 equivalent of 
hydrofluorocarbons per year. 
The threshold should be eliminated expect for laboratory uses. Furthermore, labelling should be applied 
as stated in Art. 12 of the Regulation. 
 
9. Have the following measures been effective in preventing illegal activities? 
Improvements are needed to better enforce the F-Gas Regulation, for example, more harmonised and 
more dissuasive penalties.  
Linked inspections on VAT and F-gas would be beneficial to restrict the use of illegal HFCs and the 
continued purchase and use of disposable containers with HFCs.  
The verification of data reporting by all New Entrants must be re-introduced and the threshold for 
imports of HFCs removed.

10. Has the F-gas Regulation been flexible enough to respond to: 
The F-gas Regulation has struggled to react to the increase in illegal imports of HFCs since 2017. This is 
primarily due to the uneven implementation and enforcement by Member States and the lack of 
European penalties and enforcement guidance.  
Legitimate companies that have invested in providing services to handle, recycle, recover and reclaim 
HFCs have been undermined by the large numbers of New Entrants which, in most cases, cannot provide 
such services.  
Ineffective enforcement of the F-gas Regulation by Member States has delayed the development and 
introduction of the lower-GWP alternatives to the high-GWP products. 
Internet sales (e-commerce) of chemical substances, including F-gases have been increasing significantly 
in the last few years. Often, F-gases sold on internet platforms and private websites do not comply with 
the European regulation, do not have quota and lack of hazard information. Non -compliant products 
(packaging, labelling, REACH etc.) placed on the market undermine the climate goals and the F-gas 
phase down process and pose a serious risk to end users. 
 
11. In what way has the F-gas Regulation impacted: 
Ineffective enforcement of the F-gas Regulation by Member States has negatively impacted the 
competitiveness of legitimate operators in the EU market.  
As a consequence, it has also delayed the development and introduction of the lower-GWP alternatives 
to the high-GWP products, which would have resulted in an improvement of the competitiveness in 
industry. 
EU based manufacturers of HFCs are disadvantaged compared to non-EU based suppliers  because the 
latter can supply their products to customers, who are F-gas Quota holders and who then use their own 
quota for the import, whereas the EU based manufacturers are forced to use their own quota for the 
sale (because this is considered as “placing on the market”) to their customers, even if these customers 
would like to use their own F-gas quota for the purchase.

The HFC phase-down is well-understood by the main market players although the vertical nature of the

The structure of the F-Gas Regulation is consistent as it leaves flexibility to the market to make adequate

18. Do you consider that the F-gas Regulation may lead to an increased accumulation of persistent 
chemicals in the environment? 
The purpose of the F-Gas Regulation is to mitigate emissions of HFCs through measures including an HFC 
quota system, selected use bans, containment, competence and training etc..  
HFCs and their alternatives such as HFOs and HCFOs do not fulfil the criteria for persistency according to 
of Annex XIII, paragraph 1.1.1 of REACH Regulation.  
The most commonly used HFCs and HFOs are (very) short-lived substances that do not accumulate in 
the environment.

19. Any other comments 
The F-Gas Regulation has achieved a reduction in F-gas emissions for quantities legitimately placed on 
the market since 2014.  
However, the impact of illegal trade quantities, since 2018 on the magnitude of the emission reductions 
is uncertain.   
As the F-gas Regulation has the objective that, by 2030, there will be a reduction by two-thirds (in terms 
of CO2 eq) compared to 2014 levels.  
Improved enforcement at Member State level is essential to ensure that the F-gas Regulation achieves 
this objective.  
Top priorities must be further harmonization, implementation and enforcement of the F-Gas Regulation 
across the EU linked with improvement in Customs procedure and market surveillance. 
Measures must be aligned with the Montreal Protocol to ensure that the competitiveness of EU 
companies is protected. New measures proposed during the revision must take into account the 
investments that have been made by market operators to meet the requirements under the current F-
gas regime which runs until 2030. Alterations in the current regime could potentially be disruptive and 
costly [with little environmental benefit].  
The Montreal Protocol references AR4 GWP values, as currently referenced in Annex I. Using different 
sets of GWP values for the F-Gas Regulation Annex I and Montreal Protocol reporting for supply 
(consumption) and production will lead to confusion.  Any changes to Annex I GWP values could require 
significant investment by industry. 
 
21. Do you see any other main objective for the revision, keeping in mind that a large number of 
changes may delay the negotiations and thus prevent quickly fixing urgent implementation issues? 
Please elaborate: 
EFCTC believes that the key focus of the review needs to be on ensuring and improving implementation 
and enforcement while aligning with the Montreal Protocol in case such alignment is necessary.  
In view of the European Green Deal, improvements of the F-Gas Regulation – if any – need to be 
towards ensuring that the F-gas Regulation is correctly enforced and that emissions of F-gases are 
minimised.

22. Do you think the original objectives of the F-gas Regulation (see question 3) and the proposed policy 
options (see information above) could be better achieved at EU Member State level? 
Harmonised implementation, enforcement and dissuasive penalties across the EU are essential to 
ensure a well-functioning single market, to reduce the risk of illegal trade (which, as experience has 
shown, can be triggered for example if certain member states install taxes on HFCs whereas their 
neighbours don’t) and to contribute significantly to regulatory certainty for all market actors.  
Furthermore, bans in individual Member States impact the competitiveness of EU companies and 
undermine the single market as well as the F-gas Regulation. 
 
23. How important are the following measures for improving implementation and enforcement? Any 
other relevant measure for improving enforcement, please specify: 
Improving and strengthening the T1 Transit process to ensure that operators must be registered in the 
F-gas portal. Mandating increased inspections at facilities in combination with analysis of logbooks to 
verify the use of legitimately purchased HFCs to ensure that the use of non-compliant products 
(packaging, labelling, REACH etc.) is prohibited. 
 
29. Do you expect any of the policy options to impact on: Where significant, please describe effect for 
the relevant option: 
Changes in the phase-down process or bans on the use of lower-GWP products could disrupt 
significantly the investments made by market operators that have planned through to 2030. This could 
particularly impact SMEs. Depending upon the measures chosen R&D and innovation could be affected.

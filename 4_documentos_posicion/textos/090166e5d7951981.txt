 
Input to EU F-Gas Regulation Evaluation Roadmap & Inception Impact Assessment  
(7/09/2020) 
AstraZeneca is a global, science -led, biopharmaceutical company that focuses on the discovery and 
development of prescription medicines for the treatment of disease in three therapy areas:  Oncology, 
Cardiovascular, Renal & Metabolism, and Respiratory & Immunology. It has been recognised for its 
efforts on sustainability, incl. carbon emissions, by the global environmental impact non-profit Carbon 
Disclosure Project (CDP); and is one of just three companies to achieve double ‘A’ listing for the past 
four consecutive years (1). In January 2020, AstraZeneca announced its commitment to be carbon 
neutral in its operations by 2025 and have a carbon negative value chain by 2030 (2). 
AstraZeneca welcomes the F-Gas Regulation Evaluation Roadmap & Inception Impact Assessment, 
and supports the European Commission’s climate ambition and promotion of climate -friendly 
technologies. 
Medicines delivered to the lung via an inhaler device are the backbone of treatment for common 
diseases such as asthma and chronic obstructive pulmonary dis ease ( COPD). Both are chronic 
conditions which take a significant toll on patients’ lives and on the healthcare systems that treat 
them.  176 million asthma attacks occur globally every year  (3). COPD worsens with time and 
exacerbations accelerate the decline (4). It will be the third leading cause of death worldwide by 2030 
(5). 
Asthma and COPD patients can be complex to treat and optimal management requires individualised 
care. Selection of pharmacotherapy includes a careful balance between efficacy, side effect profile, 
frequency of dosing and possible interactions with other medicines prescribed. Inhaler device 
selection is a critical consideration as patient need or preference for a specific device type will 
influence adherence to treatment (6) which in turn impacts clinical outcomes (7,8). No medicine can 
be universal , nor can a single inhaler device.  Attempts at widespread device switching have 
frequently led to patient harm  (9, 10). Treatment decisions are therefore best made by the doctor 
and patient together in line with clinical evidence. Treatment choice allows for the best medicine in 
the best device for the patient, which underpins outcomes improvement  e.g. symptom worsening, 
asthma attacks and COPD exacerbations. 
AstraZeneca has committed to develop a low global warming potential (GWP) propellant and will 
transition its pMDI medicines to near zero carbon from 2025 to ensure access to evidence -based 
guidelines-recommended treatment  (2). However, development must mee t the strict regulatory 
requirements for new pharmaceutical products regarding efficacy, safety and quality through 
extensive testing, meaning a more ambitious timeline is not possible. 
The full carbon cost of respiratory care will include both inhaler device use plus the impact of health 
care interventions. Thus, a patient whose treatment is optimised is more likely to have a lower carbon 
impact overall  (11) through reduced reliever pMDI use  and fewer unscheduled healthcare 
interventions.

AstraZeneca believes a more sustainable approach to respiratory care is achievable now based on 
choice and outcomes improvement with near zero carbon pMDI s bringing major reductions from 
2025.  Thus it proposes: 
FREEDOM OF CHOICE for inhaler prescribing to drive improvements in care 
HEALTHCARE-BASED ENVIRONMENTAL TARGETS that use evidence -based treatment guideline 
implementation to reduce carbon through outcomes improvement 
MAINTAINING THE CURRENT MEDICAL EXEMPTION ON THE F GAS REGULATION UNTIL AT LEAST 2030.  
pMDI propellants constitute <0.05% of anthropogenic carbon emissions (11). This minimal impact 
means it is appropriate to allow time for AstraZeneca and others time to complete the development 
and regulatory approval for low GWP pMDI devices, whilst preserving the right for doctors to tailor 
care for their respiratory patients 
DIRECT PATIENT & SCIENTIFIC SOCIETY REPRESENTATION on the targeted consultation so those most 
impacted by change are heard

The following feedback is related to the “Consultation Strategy Review of the requirements for packaging 
and other measures to prevent packaging waste” and will focus on biodegradable and compostable plastics 
(certified according to the standard EN 13432). We would highlight the following issues c overed by the 
consultation:  
1. Definition of “recyclability” or “recyclable”  
The definition of “recyclability” or “recyclable” packaging should be in line with article 3 of the Waste 
Framework Directive:  
‘recycling’ means any recovery operation by which wa ste materials are reprocessed into products, materials or substances 
whether for the original or other purposes. It includes the reprocessing of organic material but does not include energy 
recovery and the reprocessing into materials that are to be used as fuels or for backfilling operations 
Therefore, the WFD includes organic recycling in the overall definition. Compostable plastic packaging 
certified according standard EN 13432 is organically recyclable , in fact the Packaging and Packaging Waste 
Directive recognizes the contribution given by compostable plastics  packaging, providing that the organic 
recycling of this material is calculated towards the recycling targets on plastic packaging set by the PPWD.  
In conclusion, the  definition of recycling includes organic recycling, in line with the provisions already 
present in the Packaging Directive issued in 1994 , thus the definition of “recyclability” or “recyclable” shall 
encompass organic recycling.  
 
2. The Essential Requirements for biodegradable and compostable plastic packaging 
The suggested idea to fix by law what could be done with compostable plastics in the absence of health and 
safety reasons  represents a discrimination for these class of materials  and would potentially have a 
negative impact on both innovation and employment in Europe.  Regarding the current Essential 
Requirements, we would suggest rationalizing the two categories ( Packaging recoverable in the form of 
composting and Biodegradable packaging) by merging the  two requirements, which would help to avoid 
confusion between biodegradable and compostable.  
 
3. Revision of the standard EN 13432 “Requirements for packaging recoverable through composting and 
biodegradation” 
On the revision of the standard EN 13432, it should be duly noted that after 20 years and being confirmed 
in three systematic reviews (2005,2010, and 2015) , the standard has proved to be a valid test scheme with 
robust evaluation criteria for the organic recycling of compostable packaging. While we would welcome the 
idea of an update/revision of the EN 13432, we fail to grasp the sense generic statements – reported in the 
consultation – such as “ensure actual composting conditions are taken into account”. It goes without saying 
that when the techni cal standards are reviewed, they are subjected to analysis to verify their adequacy 
with respect to any changes that have occurred in the technologies concerned.  Thus, the revision process 
will include the analysis of the actual composting conditions , taking as a baseline the EU’s best practices in 
organic recycling processes that are capable of delivering high quality and mature compost.

Assomela response to European Commission Public Consultation for a ‘Review of the requirements for 
packaging and other measures to prevent packaging waste’ 
Packaging is an essential element of the Italian apple supply chain. Producers and traders use packaging to 
provide safe, high quality and fresh apples to consumers. Assomela supports the European Gree n Deal’s 
ambitions to increase the sustainability of packaging use through enhanced reusability and recyclability of 
packaging as well as the commitment to tackle over -packaging and waste generation, which is also 
reflected in the new Circular Economy Action Plan (CEAP 2.0).  
While much effort has been made by the Italian apple sector to address packaging use through innovations 
in packaging materials and design as well as reductions in over -packaging, there remains scope for change 
to furthe r boost the sustainability of the sector . Even if Assomela welcom es the review of the  Directive 
94/62/EC on Packaging and Packaging Waste and the requirements of packaging and other measures to 
prevent packaging waste, Assomela would like to underline the need for a full and detailed analysis of the 
impact of proposed changes to packaging legislation, which will have both direct and indirect implications 
for the apple sector.  
Functional packaging in the apple sector 
Packaging for apples is predominantly used by the sector for the following reasons:  
• Product containment 
 
• Extend product shelf life: Fresh apples crosses many stages in the supply chain before arriving at the 
home or in foodservice for consumption. Apples are delicate and perishable and during each stage of 
the supply chain  must be protected so that they arrive at the consumer in good condition  as per 
marketing standards. In this way packaging extends the shelf life of products by limiting minor damage 
to products that eventually may increase product perishability.  
 
• Prevent food  spoilage and waste : By extending the shelf life of products, protection provided by 
packaging prevents food waste by limiting product damage and maintaining freshness at each stage of 
the chain and in the home.  
 
• Prevent microbiological contamination  
 
• Product information display: Packaging offers the sector possibilities to communicate to both supply 
chain actors and consumers with obligatory and essential product information. Obligatory and 
essential information includes but is not limited to variety, colour, size, traceability, branding, country 
of origin, PLU codes, organic distinction from conventional products and fraud detection , EU quality 
schemes’ logos. 
  
• Consumption promotion 
 
• Consumption facilitation 
 
• Consumer confidence: Packaging gives confidence to consumers in guaranteeing b oth product safety 
and quality. Partic ularly during health crises, such as the current COVID -19 pandemic, packaging 
provides food safety and quality certainty to consumers, which facilitates continued consumption

Essential packaging & packaging waste policy aspects in the review of Directive 94/62/EC 
Assomela would like to highlight the most import ant aspects regarding waste prevention and increasing 
packaging reusability and recyclability that must be taken into consideration. 
EU harmonisation is essential : Without EU harmonisation diverge nt measures at Member State  and 
regional levels will disrupt the circulation of products on the internal market.  Assomela supports the use of 
EU standards where possible.  
 
Waste prevention must be supported by a harmonised EU waste management system  
 
Appropriate transition periods are necessary: For any new measures imposed at EU or Member State level 
appropriate transition periods must be included. These transition periods must be discussed with suppl y 
chain actors to guarantee feasibility to achieve targets.  
 
Unintended sustainability consequences, such as food waste, must be avoided : The review of Directive 
94/62/EC must not result in unintended consequences that are detrimental to the fresh fruit and vegetable 
sector’s efforts to enhance product sustainability. Packaging plays an important and in some cases vital role 
in food waste prevention and therefore the overall sustainability of the product.  
 
While the sector is continually innovating to facilitate the sale of unpackaged products, packaging continues 
to be often used as an essential tool for food waste prevention in the chain, especially for products with 
high perishability.  
Food contact materials remain a barrier to recyclabi lity and reusability : Ensuring food safety for the 
health of European consumers should remain a top priority. Innovation in this area must be facilitated, for 
instance through EU harmonisation of food contact material legislation.  
R&I funding must be provided : Specific funding for research, innovation and development for sustainable 
packaging solutions and infrastructure must be provided alongside other measures.  
 
Assomela remains concerned by some of the policy options outlined in the Inception Impact Assessment: 
Assomela does not support 1) any material-specific packaging waste reduction targets or waste generation 
limits, and 2) any consumption reduction targets/limits targeting specific packaging types or applications.  
 
Packaging product-specific concerns related to the review of Directive 94/62/EC 
 
In view of the review of Directive 94/62/EC Assomela would like to outline a number of concerns relating to 
packaging that is product-specific in the fresh fruit and vegetable sector.   
 
Fresh fruit and vegetable sticker packaging 
 
Assomela, that has followed the subje ct through Freshfel Europe , would like to remind the European 
Commission of the following aspects relating to apples sticker packaging  that must be taken into 
consideration:  
• Fresh fruit and vegetable stickers are an indispensable form of packaging in the European and 
global fresh produce sector. In particular, stickers play a vital role in the communica tion of 
obligatory and essential information for actors along the supply chain as well as for consumers that 
would otherwise necessitate further product pre -packaging. This includes variety, color, 
traceability, branding, country of origin, organic, quality schemes… 
• Assomela supports the European Commission’s vision to increase the sustainability of fruit stickers 
in light of the need to reduce packaging waste. As a form of highly minimal pre -packaging that has 
extensive marketing and communication benefits to the sector, retaining their use in Europe and 
globally is paramount. However, the recent adoption of varying Member State legislation  (e.g., in 
Belgium) highlights the need for EU harmonization of any proposed legislation for these widely 
used products to avoid disruption of the circulation of fresh fruit and vegetable products on the 
internal market.  
• As only a very small percentage of the fruit and vegetable sticker market (<2%) currently represents 
industrially certified compostable stickers (often for organic products dues to price differentiation)  
and in consideration that a certified home compostable construction that is food safe for fresh fruit 
and vegetable stickers is not available on the EU market or globally, regulation for compostable 
fruit and vegetable stickers must include an appropriate transition period.  
• In defining an appropriate transition period, it must be taken into account that sticker construction 
development can take a minimum of three years and further compost testing to meet standards 
must subsequently be completed (6 -10 months for industrially compostable sticker) after this 
period. Therefore, any transition period must be discussed with supply ch ain actors to guarantee 
feasibility to achieve targets. In consideration of a current lack of certified home compostable 
stickers on the market today, it could take 5 -10 years for sector transition to a switch to certified 
home compostable offerings globally. 
 
 
Fresh cut fruit and vegetable packaging 
Packaging for the fresh cut category in the fresh fruit and vegetable sector must be carefully considered if 
packaging requirements were to change under this review of Directive 94/62/EC  as f resh cut packaging 
requirements are highly specific, especially regarding oxygen permeability and strength requirements. The 
packaging material composition must allow the product to stand up on the shelves and protect the produce 
to maximise shelf life.  
 
Attention must be placed on waste reduction over consumption reduction

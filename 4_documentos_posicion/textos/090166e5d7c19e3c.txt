Re: Kaneka’s Response to Public Consultation to inform the review of the requirements for packaging 
and other measures to prevent packaging waste. 
 
Founded in 1949 and headquartered in Osaka and Tokyo, Kaneka Corporation produces chemical 
intermediates such as chemicals, functional plastics, foamed plastics and products,… for a variety of 
applications including packaging.  Kaneka employs more than 10,000 people worldwide and has been 
active in the EU since 1970 through Kaneka Belgium and has currently an extensive network within the 
EU through Kaneka Europe Holding. 
As a sustainable company, Kaneka is committed to addressing the environmental challenges of our planet, 
contributing to the circular economy and to a better quality of life for our societies. For this reason, we 
want to express our appreciation to the EU Commission for its openness and transparency. This public 
consultation is of great importance to industry to create legal certainty with a view to promote further 
research and innovation by reviewing the requirements for packaging and packaging waste; as a condition 
for the realization of the objectives as set within the “New circular economy action” and the “The 
European Green Deal” 
Within this later, it is stated that new technologies, sustainable solutions and disruptive innovation are 
critical to achieve the objectives of the European Green Deal.  Through innovative fermentation processes, 
Kaneka develops and produces natural polymer structures (such as Kaneka Biodegradable Polymer 
PHBHTM, a PHA (Polyhydroxyalkanoate)) suitable for advanced packaging applications, which are 
biobased, recyclable and biodegradable in open environment in case of leakage; as requested.   
In this respect, we would like to highlight some additional points, strengthening our position within the 
above referenced questionnaire related to review the essential requirements for packaging.    
 
Definition Recyclability: 
 
Within The Green Deal, it is mentioned that the Commission will develop requirements to ensure that all 
packaging in the EU market is reusable or recycleable in an economically viable manner by 2030”  
Organic recyclable packaging plays undeniable a role for packaging which are difficult to be recycled in 
a mechanical or chemical way (f.e. due to organic contamination,…) and which can contribute to the 
collection of bio-waste in respect to quantity and quality of the compost/digestate.   
The study on “Effectiveness of the Essential Requirements for Packaging and Packaging Waste and 
proposals for Reinforcement” as worked-out by Eunomia recognized the need for a proper definition for 
recyclable packaging.  Unfortunately, organic recycling is not well addressed within this discussion. For 
this reason, we propose to keep a proper definition for recycleable packaging including organic 
recycling, f.e.  in line with Art 3 (17) of the Waste Framework Directive, which is

Recycling means any recovery operation by which waste materials are reprocessed into products, 
materials or substances whether for the original or other purposes.  It includes the reprocessing of 
organic material but does not include energy recovery and the reprocessing into materials that are 
to be used as fuels or for backfilling operations 
 
 
Harmonized requirements for Biodegradable y and Compostable Packaging 
As expressed in the plastic strategy, the EU commission will propose harmonized rules for defining and 
labelling compostable and biodegradable plastics.  We strongly support this initiative by the Commission 
in order to ensure that consumers are provided with clear and correct information.   
Replacing conventional packaging one-to-one with organic-recyclable packaging is not always better and 
more sustainable and that is why we support as Kaneka the waste hierarchy Nevertheless, there are 
situations where organic recyclable packaging has a clear added value, both for the consumer, the 
collector, as well as the processing sector. The added value of organic recyclable packaging is most 
obvious in case at the end-of-life there is a clear link with bio-waste; such as products mixed-up with bio-
waste and/or a considerable likelihood of ending up in the bio-waste.  We clearly agree to set clear 
criteria for the use of compostable packaging however, a priory restricting the type of packaging 
that can be designed for composting would definitely hamper innovations. 
Within this discussion, we would clearly request the EU commission also to set-out harmonized rules 
including home-compostable packaging in addition to industrial compostable packaging.  On top of 
that, we would like to request the EU commission to realize an assessment of the scientific and 
technical progress concerning criteria or a standard for biodegradability in the marine environment 
applicable to single use products (which may or may not be packaging) as within the scope of the directive 
(EU) 2019/904 on the reduction of the impact of certain plastic products on the environment. 
 
 
Bioeconomy: 
Within the new Circular Economy Action Plan, the importance of the bioeconomy has been stressed.   In 
order to further stimulate these innovations and to realize a “low carbon economy”, we would 
recommend the EU Commission to promote the use of bio-based and renewable resources for the 
manufacturing of packaging which helps achieve Greenhouse Gas emission neutral society, 
equivalent to the promotion of recycled content.

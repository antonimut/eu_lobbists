RE:  Final Remarks on Public Consultation to Inform the Review of the Requirements for 
Packaging and Other Measures to Prevent Packaging Waste 
 
The Plant Based Products Council (PBPC) appreciates this opportunity to provide input to the European 
Commission in response to the 30 September 2020 Public Consultation to inform the review of the 
requirements for packaging and other measures to prevent packaging waste. 
 
PBPC represents businesses large and small in the United States and internationally, including the 
European Union, who are committed to guiding the evolving global economy toward more sustainable 
and responsible consumer products and packaging through greater use of plant-based materials 
(sometimes termed “bioproducts”). We aspire to deliver a future based on renewable goods, improving 
global resource efficiency to meet the challenges of the 21st century while also providing environmental 
benefits through reduced greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions and improved soil quality and water quality, 
along with improved recycling of food waste. Plant-based materials are derived from renewable sources, 
including agricultural commodities such as colza, maize, hemp, soy, and sugarcane and can serve as 
alternatives to traditional consumer materials, including plastics, that are often derived from fossil fuels. 
These plant-based materials can be used to manufacture the next generation of packaging, some of 
which is recyclable like bio-PET, and some which is compostable like PLA.  
 
PBPC recommends that in considering ways to enhance the Packaging and Packaging Waste Directive 
(PPWD) through implementation of the Circular Economy Action Plan (CEAP) and the European Green 
Deal more broadly, the European Commission should appropriately consider the role of plant-based 
packaging in meeting the PPWD objectives. Renewable plant-based packaging supports multiple 
objectives of the PPWD, including prevention of packaging waste and minimizing the environmental 
impact of packaging. These materials are an essential part of advancing a circular economy and part of 
the solution to the packaging pollution that plagues our planet’s environments. Across the plant-based 
packaging spectrum, from plastics to molded fiber, these materials offer a wide range of performance 
advantages and disposal options when compared to traditional packaging.  
 
1. Waste Prevention 
 
Many plant-based packaging materials, including molded fiber and plastic resins like polylactic acid 
(PLA), are compostable. Compostable plant-based packaging materials, when used in food-related 
applications, presents an opportunity to help divert both the packaging itself and accompanying food 
scraps to composting facilities. Such use helps prevent food contamination in the recycling stream and 
allows for broader diversion of food waste to compost. Challenges exist regarding the processing of 
compostable packaging, including confusion with non-compostable materials. However, when coupled 
with appropriate, consistent end-of-life labeling across all packaging, along with proper infrastructure,

2. Minimized Environmental Impacts 
 
According to the objectives of the PPWD, packaging must ensure greater environmental protection 
than just packaging waste prevention. All plant-based packaging can help support this objective.   
In terms of the environmental benefits rendered from compostable plant-based packaging, these 
materials can positively contribute to reduced waste and lower GHG emissions, as well as improved 
water quality and soil health. When considering the GHG emissions reduction potential of these 
materials, look to the beginning and end of the life of these products: compostable plant-based 
materials are produced from renewable feedstocks, and at the end of their useful life they are 
disposed of in a way that avoids landfill methane emissions due to the diversion of organic matter 
that accompanies this packaging. Soil health benefits can be derived from the composting of plant-
based materials, where these products contribute to the production of a powerful soil amendment. 
Lastly, compostable plant-based materials can contribute to improved water quality by keeping litter 
out of our oceans and helping prevent the runoff of nutrients into vulnerable bodies of water. 
 
In addition, some forms of plant-based packaging can be recycled, like molded fiber in paper recycling 
streams and bio-based PET in traditional PET recycling. In addition to fitting into existing recycling 
streams, recyclable plant-based packaging holds the benefit over petroleum-based packaging of being 
sourced from renewable materials, leading to reduced fossil fuel use and overall GHG emissions. 
 
In addition to supporting the general objectives of the PPWD, PBPC believes that compostable plant-
based packaging addresses the goals of the Circular Economy Action Plan (CEAP), particularly its 
goals of: making sustainable products the norm in the EU, focusing on the sectors that use the most 
resources and where the potential for circularity is high, and ensuring less waste. Regarding making 
sustainable products the norm in the EU, plant-based packaging is widely available and ready to 
assume a greater presence on the market at scale through efforts of the private sector or similarly 
through green public procurement. For example, the Ellen MacArthur Foundation has estimated 
that bio-based plastic resins that exist today have the potential to replace about 66% of major 
traditional plastics used in packaging.1 In focusing on sectors that have a high potential for 
circularity, plant-based plastics meet that criteria. Take for example the circular lifetime of 
compostable plant-based plastic: renewable plant-based feedstocks are converted into plastic 
pellets through advanced manufacturing technologies, which are then molded into consumer 
products and packaging, disposed of at the end of their useful life alongside any organic waste like 
food that was part of its packaging function, and sent to a composting facility to be turned into 
compost, a valuable soil amendment that helps grow the next generation of compostable plastic 
feedstock.  
 
In addition to supporting the implementation of the CEAP, the widespread adoption of compostable 
plant-based packaging also supports important elements of the broader European Green Deal from 
which the CEAP is derived. In its missions to achieve greater reductions in GHG emissions, plant-

In conclusion, PBPC recommends that the European Commission consider the numerous 
environmental benefits and circular economy merits inherent in plant-based packaging, particularly 
compostable plant-based materials, as it considers ways to enhance the PPWD. These materials can 
help Member States implement the European Green Deal and the CEAP according to the principles 
of each initiative.  
PBPC is eager to answer any questions that the Commission may have regarding our comments.  We are

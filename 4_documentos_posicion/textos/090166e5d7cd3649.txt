CERAME UNIE REPLY TO PPWD REVIEW CONSULTATION
 
Reference: Response to the Public Consultation on the Review of the Packaging and Packaging 
Waste Directive 
6 January 2021 
Cerame-Unie (the European Ceramic Industry Association) and its member association FEPF (the 
European Fede ration of Table - and Ornamental ware) would like to thank the European 
Commission for the opportunity to partake in the public consultation and set out the industry’s 
view in respect of the revision of the Packaging and Packaging Waste Directive (PPWD). 
In addition to the responses provided in the online questionnaire, Cerame -Unie would like to 
further elaborate the industry’s position in the sections below, in order to provide a dditional 
background with reference to ceramic packaging.  
According to the current PPWD, ceramic containers should adhere to a “Return, Collection and 
Recovery System”, although the nature of these products clearly distinguish them from other 
types of packaging waste. Ceramic packaging containers are placed in the market in negligible 
volumes and it is neither economically nor environmentally reasonable to set up a specific 
collection and treatment system for ceramics. 
In this regard, Cerame-Unie wishes to highlight that ceramic is a non-polluting material entailing 
no risk for natural sources like water , air and soil, nor for human health, animals and plants. 
Ceramic packages are designed and manufactured to be marketed indiscriminately as packaging, 
kitchenware or tableware. For instance, research by our member companies shows that ceramic 
food containers are long -lasting products that consumers are willing to keep (75 -95% of the 
cases) and reuse as kitchenware or tableware:  they generate no unwanted smell and can resist 
remarkable temperature changes, and they can be reused  multiple times simply by being 
washed and dried. Therefore, ceramic packaging containers are key products in the prevention 
of waste. 
The features of ceramic packages perfectly dovetails with the concept of reusable packaging laid 
out in the PPWD , as these items are conceived and marketed  to be  reused multiple times 
throughout their lifecycle. 
The current measures set out in the PPWD result in inefficiencies and shortcomings on multiple 
levels, which namely affect: 
(i) Packaging Waste Management System s, since they have to separate ceramic  
containers from other types of packaging;  
(ii) Producers of ceramic containers, who are being excluded from the market due to 
the high fees imposed to ceramics containers in some Member States resulting from 
a lack of a harmonised implementation in the EU based on  eco-modularity 
principles;

(iii) Ceramic containers fillers, who  are required to financially contribute to th e 
Packaging Waste Managemen t System without having a specific pick -up, 
transportation, storage and recycling system in place. The high fees for ceramic 
containers in some Member States are already having negative effects on the 
industry as container fillers are encouraged to shift t o cheaper but more 
contaminant packaging materials. 
That situation is particularly dramatic in France, where there has been a severe increase in fees 
in the last years on the grounds that there is no specific collecting/recycling channel for ceramic 
items, without taking into account the environmental harmlessness of such products  and the 
wide range of applications in which it can be recycled . This situation could potentially be 
replicated in other Member states as long as the lack of harmonisation at EU level remains and 
the eco-modularity principle is left out.  
This scenario is hindering the objectives set out in the  European Green Deal as the current 
legislation does not incentivise the use of packaging materials that are inert and reusable like 
ceramics, which are inherently conceived to prevent the creation of packaging waste. 
For all these reasons, Cerame-Unie advocates that the PPWD should be reviewed to exclude  
ceramic containers fillers from Packaging Integrated Waste Management System s as this 
product is mostly reused and does not generate packaging waste (if any, in a negligible way).  
Additionally, it is worth reiterating that ceramic packaging is entirely made of an inert material 
that causes no harm to nature. In this regard, ceramic containers waste should be treated as 
municipal and household domestic waste so as to be treated in Eco -parks to put an end to 
inefficient systems. 
In this sense, Cerame-Unie calls on the European Commission to amend the PPWD and exclude 
ceramic packaging waste from the Packaging Waste Management System and implement a 
harmonisation mechanism on the basis of the eco-modularity principle. 
In the alternative, Cerame-Unie calls on the European Commission to make use of Article 20 of 
the Packaging Directive by adopting a delegated act addressing more specifically the situation 
of ceramic containers (See Article 20 of the Packaging Directive:  “The Commission is empowered 
to adopt d elegated acts in accordance with Article 21a in order to supplement this Directive 
where necessary to deal with any difficulties encountered in applying the provisions of this 
Directive, in particular, to inert packaging materials placed on the market in very small quantities 
(i.e. approximately 0,1 % by weight) in the Union, primary packaging for medical devices and 
pharmaceutical products, small packaging and luxury packaging”). 
Considering the inert, pollutant -free, reusability, high recyclability and de pendence on locally 
available raw materials (clay), the EU legal framework should ensure that food and drinks 
containers opting for ceramic containers cannot be discriminated against through the imposition 
of disproportionately high recycling (green dot) fees as it is the case in France.

Amazon position on the revision of the EU Packaging and Packaging 
Waste Directive 
As a consumer-obsessed company that is committed to sustainability, we see it as part of our mission 
to optimize the overall customer experience by setting a high bar for the selection and use of our own 
outbound e-commerce packaging, and by collaborating with vendors and sellers worldwide to invent 
sustainable packaging that delights customers, eliminates waste, and ensures prod ucts arrive 
undamaged to our customers’ doorsteps. Amazon drives outbound packaging waste reduction by using 
machine learning to select the least wasteful type of packaging for each product we ship. We have used 
this tool to reduce the weight of our outbound packaging by more than 33% over the past 5 years , 
eliminating over 900,000 tons of packaging material. We share this knowledge with vendors and sellers 
through our  Frustration-Free Packaging (FFP) Program, which encourages them to package their 
products in easy-to-open packaging that is 100% recyclable and, where safe to do so, ships without any 
additional Amazon packaging.  
In addition to our work on  our outbound packaging,  we also promote sustainability in the product 
packaging of our own Amazon devices and private label products e.g. by eliminating plastic packaging 
materials whenever possible and encouraging the use of wood-based fiber packaging based on recycled 
or sourced from certified responsibly managed sources.  
Amazon welcomes the Commission’s intention to review the Packaging and Packaging Waste Directive 
to ensure a well -functioning and harmonized  application of rules throughout the European Single 
Market. While we look forward to contributing to the development of a revised Directive and 
subsequent secondary legislation, we would like to supplement our response to the public consultation 
by encouraging the Commission to consider the following points: 
1. Ensure harmonization of packaging rules across the single market to prevent market fragmentation 
and create a level playing-field, in line with Art. 114 TFEU 
The scoping study conducted ahead of the current review of the Directive found that essential 
requirements are not sufficiently implemented or precise. We have witnessed an uneven application 
of the PPWD rules, whereby some Member States do not fully implement the provisions of the PPWD 
while others go above and beyond PPWD requirements. Such divergence causes fragmentation of the 
European Single Market, creates an uneven playing -field for companies, and prevents the economies 
of scale that can accelerate R&D and investment in new packaging technologies, materials and 
practices.  
Amazon therefore encourages the Commission to ensure the integrity of the European Single Market 
through the review of the PPWD, as facilitated by its legal basis in Article 11 4 TFEU. We are pleased 
that the Commission recognizes the necessity of striving towards full harmonization of r ules on 
packaging and packaging waste and addressing the obstacles caused by diverging national measures. 
In the interest of the free movement of goods, we encourage the Commission to center its work on this 
broad objective . This can be achieved through the design of well-considered, clearly defined, and 
enforceable requirements and tools which must be applied uniformly in all Member States. The 
following paragraphs offer further considerations to this effect.

We note that the Commission  is looking into measures specifically targeting over -packaging in e-
commerce, including through potentially setting proportion or void space limits on outbound packaging 
used for e-commerce. Amazon’s Frustration Free Packaging (FFP) program has been an effective tool

This is not least the case for the primary product packaging itself, when outbound shipment packaging

ensure the right balance between cubic efficiency, weight and product protection. It is therefore crucial 
that any rules on  packaging proportions or design are not too generalized and thereby risk 
compromising product protection or penalizing companies that already employ stringent packaging 
design principles to optimize their product packaging for the relevant distribution environment. 
While we strive towards being able to ship products in their own  primary packaging (SIOC) , it is 
important to also consider that not all products sold through Amazon or other e -commerce 
marketplaces come in primary packaging that is optimized for e-commerce. Consequently, there is still 
a need to use secondary outbound packaging to ensure that the product arrives safely to customers. 
For these products, the introduction of requirements that are too strict on the proportion of sealed air 
in outbound packaging would create the need for customized packaging, which in turn would lead to 
waste generated on the manufacturing or fulfilment floor as packaging is cut to measure, rather than 
using a packaging suite that is nested together to reduce waste in manufacturing. 
Two e-commerce specific solutions that allowed Amazon to reduce our packaging weight per shipment 
by 33% in the last 5 years are the substitution of corrugated boxes by flexible packaging for items that 
are not fragile, and the shipment of multiple products in a single container (rigid or flexible). This was 
enabled by machine learning, which identifies the necessary protection for each product. We 
encourage the Commission to consider the need for system-level and innovation friendly measures for 
e-commerce to enable the continued development of such tools and methodologies by the e -
commerce sector.

3. Develop an EU-wide recyclability e-label to help customers improve recycling rates 
Improved recyclability of packaging only matters if consumers are aware of what can be recycled, how, 
and where. Amazon therefore supports the Commission’s intention to assess the feasibility of an EU -
wide label that facilitates the correct separation of packaging waste at source, as set out in the Circular 
Economy Action Plan. We already participate in voluntary schemes to promote common approaches 
across companies where they exist, such as the On Pack Recycling Label ( OPRL) in the UK. Since July 
2020, our distribution packaging is printed with  a QR code which points customers to a website 
outlining our packaging-based initiatives, as well as to Amazon Second Chance, where customers can 
find information on how to recycle their packaging where they are , alongside other circularity 
information and services.  
The establishment of an EU-wide label would enable companies selling across borders to communicate 
recyclability information to customers through harmonized pictograms which could become familiar to 
customers across the European Single Market . Furthermore, we would like to encourage the 
Commission to consider the benefits of e-labelling in this respect. Providing online information through 
a QR code or similar would enable more detailed information in several languages and with specific 
information on natio nal or local practices, all while minimizing the need for additional printing or 
stickers which risk contaminating the packaging waste stream.  
Finally, it is vital that any EU-wide label to this effect that is developed by the Commission is based on 
clear and enforceable definitions. Please refer to point 4 below.  
4. Develop clear and meaningful definitions of key concepts, including a restrictive understanding of 
‘biodegradable’ and ‘compostable’ packaging 
A key step towards a fully harmonized European Single Market for packaging and packaging waste is a 
set of clear  definitions of key concepts  governed by the Directive.  This would enable uniform 
transposition and implementation of the Directive across the Union and offer predictability that enables 
investments in material innovations.  Amazon supports the development of ambitious definitions of 
concepts such as recyclability, which will be key to enable a well -functioning market for secondary 
packaging materials in the European Union. 
Clear and harmonized definitions are especially needed for the commonly misunderstood and misused 
terms ‘compostable’ and ‘biodegradable’. A clear case is o xo-degradable plastics, which only imitate 
biodegradation and create microplastics in the process and should therefore be explicitly excluded from 
any definition of compostable or biodegradable packaging.  
Definitions of biodegradable or compostable packaging must also consider the real-world scenarios for 
disposal of the materials based on the information that is provided to end-users. For example, a plastic 
that is biodegradable under industrial composting conditions will not be composted without the 
necessary industrial composting infrastructure, which is currently limited to very few municipalities 
worldwide.  As availability of recyc ling sortation that prevent these biodegradable materials from 
contaminating the existing plastics recycling streams and industrial composting facilities become more 
widespread, and/or the environmental conditions required to compost a new material becomes  less 
constrained, composting may become an alternative end -of-life option for customers. Until then,  EU 
rules should make the limitations of such materials clear to citizens and companies through 
appropriately restrictive definitions of what is considered  ‘biodegradable’ or ‘compostable’ . For the 
same reason, the use of such materials should be discouraged except where recycling or reuse are not 
feasible options. 
5. Restrictions and requirements for primary product packaging must be developed in close cooperation 
with affected industry to avoid unintended consequences 
In addition to our efforts in outbound distribution packaging, we at Amazon are committed to 
sustainable practices in the packaging of our  Amazon and private brand products , and continuously 
strive towards increasing recyclability and recycled content in our primary packaging. For that reason,

we have learned that  that improving the sustainability of  primary product packaging is a challenging 
process riddled with trade-offs, and where solutions must often be tailored to the specific product.  
Increasing recycled content in paper, for example, can be achieved at the cost of reduced structural 
strength, which may require additional manufacturing processes and laminations, or increased material 
use and thereby higher weight, which increases greenhouse gas emissions in the manufacturing process 
and during transport.  
Food packaging is another challenging case, where requirements on food contact materials and the 
consideration of the safety of the consumer must always come first. Recycled content targets in food 
packaging must therefore follow the availability of food-grade recycled materials, which depends on an 
adequate collection and recycling infrastructure. 
While we are supportive of stronger EU legislation of primary product packaging, we would therefore 
encourage the Commission to adequately account for the specificities and needs of packaged products, 
and to account for lifecycle impacts on GHG emissions a nd resource use of packaging choices, when 
developing new requirements through the revised Directive. While overarching principles should be set 
in the Packaging and Packaging Waste Direct ive, Amazon encourage s the Commission to consider 
whether specific requirements should be designed through secondary legislation developed in close 
cooperation with industry experts. 
6. Develop a one-stop-shop for Extended Producer Responsibility  
Small and medium sized enterprises (SMEs) are the backbone of the European economy. Unleashing 
their full potential is key to preserving jobs and livelihoods, and for a swift recovery. Extended Producer 
Responsibility (EPR) rules place a substantial administrative burden on Euro pean SMEs in general and 
online sellers in particular. Today, an SME intending to sell a single mobile phone unit with its packaging 
in each of the 27 EU Member States is required to pay fees in excess of 140K Euro in administrative 
costs, while the actual recycling fees can be as low as 1 Eurocent per item. To fulfil these administrative 
requirements, up to 300 reports must be submitted every year, which equals almost 40 working days 
per year, to comply with rules on e-waste, batteries and packaging.   
EPR obligations originate from legislation from the 1990s and early ‘00s when e-commerce represented 
only a marginal sales channel. With the advent of digitalization and increased popularity of e -
commerce, compliance requirements must follow suit and offer  a convenient, alternative compliance 
option for merchants wanting to sell online . This will contribute to maintaining the financial 
sustainability of current EPR systems across Europe. It is important that funding for recycling schemes 
is secured, to fund collection and recycling of goods at the end of their useful life, while ensuring that 
companies, particularly SMEs, can continue to sell freely across the EU Single Market without incurring 
barriers to selling across European borders. 
We encourage the Commission to show ambition and propose harmonized measures in the spirit of a 
simplified compliance model, whereby EPR requirements, such as reporting and payment of fees should 
be possible via an EU one -stop-shop solution, instead of having to register and  report weights placed 
on the market in each Member State in accordance with national requirements. 
7. Requirements for reusable packaging must be based on careful lifecycle analysis, considering the 
GHG footprint of reverse logistics 
Reusable packaging and returnable pallets are interesting concepts which need further supportive 
evidence before mandatory requirements are considered. The circularity potential could be 
tremendous for certain applications and shorter delivery distances, but it is crucial that the complexities 
and additional greenhouse gas emissions caused by return logistics are properly understood before any 
mandatory requirements are introduced. Amazon looks forward to contributing to thorough impact 
assessments of such solutions.

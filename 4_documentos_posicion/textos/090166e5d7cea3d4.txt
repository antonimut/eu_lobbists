PCEP Response to PPWD Consultation 
The Polyolefin Circular Economy Platform (PCEP), brings together actors from all across the circular 
polyolefin value chain united in our common mission to drive the transition to a circular economy 
for polyolefins (polypropylene and polyethylene). Polyolefins are highly versatile and valuable 
materials, reflected in their use across all sectors of the economy.  
PCEP has three strategic goals to achieve our mission: designing out waste, keeping maximum 
products and material in use for as long as possible, and then recycling into high quality new raw 
materials. This includes working on design of our products, innovation in products and 
technologies, enhancing collection and sorting to ensure we get all our materials back at end of 
use and that significantly higher volumes are sent for recycling, mechanisms to improve the 
secondary materials market and drive quality of recycled polyolefins, as well as exploring new, and 
expanding existing, end markets for the uptake of recycled polyolefins.  
Our role in the transition to a carbon neutral circular economy for packaging is clear. Polyolefins 
represent 70% of Europe’s plastic packaging market and at the end of life that packaging 
represents the majority, 71%, of the collected post-consumer polyolefin waste.1 That collected 
material goes on to be transformed into over 80% of the post-consumer recycled polyolefins being 
used today in European products - across packaging, construction, automotive, electronics, 
agriculture and other sectors. In this respect, packaging is a lynchpin for making significant progress 
on the transition to a circular polyolefin economy, and a supportive policy framework that 
encourages innovation and incentivises investment is critical.  
We welcome this opportunity to input to the European Commission’s consultation on reviewing 
the requirements for packaging and other measures to prevent packaging waste. In a circular 
economy there should not be waste to prevent, all packaging should be feedstock for new 
products. 
Recyclable 
As PCEP we fully support that all packaging placed on the market should be recyclable in a cost 
effective manner. Indeed we are pleased to observe already the polyolefin market changing rapidly 
to make this reality. Recyclability, as is observed in the definition proposed in this consultation, is a 
function of material, collection and sorting infrastructure, and recycling technologies. PCEP 
supports a harmonised and actionable definition of recyclable that is applicable to all packaging

placed on the market. This will continue to drive packaging that is fully compatible with an existing 
or emerging recycling technology, and has the appropriate infrastructure to ensure it is actually 
recycled. The latter part, however, requires investment and an appropriate transition time for that 
investment to be translated into on the ground capacity. In this respect we ask the European 
Commission to consider carefully how the definition of recyclability in essential requirements and 
the implementation of the EPR fee modulation interact. We would like to see time to allow the full 
implementation of fee modulation, to generate the investment and deliver the infrastructure and 
recycling capacities needed. This will enable the scaling of proven existing and emerging recycling 
technologies for polyolefin packaging and encourage innovation throughout the system. We would 
not expect this scaling to take long with today’s market dynamics already enabling new capacities 
to come on line much faster than was expected only a few years ago.  
Another critical aspect to encourage investment in innovation is the clear recognition that all forms 
of recycling, in line with the EU Waste Framework Directive, will count towards both recycling rates 
and recycled content targets. This is required to encourage innovation and investment in all 
recycling technologies, physical and chemical. It will also require recognition for and establishment 
of a common approach to mass balance methodology for recycled content. At the same time, while 
infrastructures, capacities and innovations in sorting and recycling technologies expand, we need a 
policy framework that allows the shipment of collected polyolefin waste between member states 
to locations where recycling capacities exist. This is critical to create volumes of waste at a scale 
that makes recycling economically viable. 
PCEP believes that the circular economy is best helped by policies which focus first and foremost 
on quality of the recycled material, as this drives technology innovation and capacities to meet the 
needs of the markets using the recycled material. Quantity will follow. In this regard, we welcome a 
drive towards mono-material packaging by design but note that the 95% threshold proposed 
needs examination to ensure it is appropriate for different packaging types dependent on the 
functionality. Similarly, where materials are fully separable the percentage of each should not 
impact recyclability. We look forward to working with the Commission and all other stakeholders 
to further elaborate and establish these critical elements of the recyclable definition.  
As you see from our comments PCEP fully supports that all packaging should be designed for the 
circular economy, which requires simplification of materials, or fully separable materials, all of 
which must be compatible with an existing or emerging recycling technology. However we do not 
support reducing the number of polymers, or materials in general, available for packaging designers 
to choose from. This could limit innovation and would not reflect the rapidly evolving nature of 
what is possible to recycle.

Reusable 
PCEP welcomes and encourages the development of reusable packaging, except where the use of 
a circular single-use packaging item has a better environmental footprint. Many aspects of reusable 
and refillable packaging need to be further explored with all stakeholders to ensure a successful 
system development which brings overall environmental benefit. We need to establish a common 
understanding of the environmental footprint, logistics and infrastructure, liabilities, hygiene, 
consumer safety and behaviour, product integrity, product losses in transport and at home due to 
damage etc. for different packaging types, business models, and markets. Assessments of social 
and environmental impacts need to be scientifically based and holistic, considering both the pack 
and the product. 
Polyolefins are widely used in reusable packaging systems today, noting in particular uses in 
agriculture and food distribution within commercial value chains where these applications also 
have positive EFSA opinions for recycled content in this food contact packaging. PCEP is actively 
engaged in encouraging the shift to using reusable pallets made from recycled post-consumer 
polyolefins in transport packaging. We note that a critical aspect of the improved environmental 
footprint here is that such pallets are in an effective pooling system.  
Any reusable package should also be recyclable, otherwise we simply create a future waste 
management problem rather than driving a circular economy.   
Recycled Content 
PCEP is actively engaged in voluntarily increasing the use of recycled content across all 
polyolefin applications, through our own work and in platforms such as the Circular Plastics 
Alliance. Recycled content is at the heart of a circular economy, reducing reliance on virgin 
resources and saving CO2 emissions. We support recycled content obligations, which will 
help to balance the availability and usage of recycled polyolefins in the market. In general, 
PCEP is supportive of the ISO 14021 definition of recycled content, in conjunction with the 
EU Waste Framework Directive, to incentivise the recycling of all wastes back into product. 
A pre-requisite for a mandatory recycled content, is the existing functioning of the 
recycled material market to supply the qualities and quantities in a reliable manner, 
coupled with a supportive regulatory framework for use of recycled content in the 
application. In this respect a transition period to ensure the increase of infrastructure and 
recycling capacities for plastics is needed for mandatory targets to succeed.  PCEP 
welcomes the European Commission’s commitment to review the regulation for food

With regards to the choice between targets for only plastic packaging or for all packaging 
we believe the latter is the more appropriate measure. Targets for recycled content in 
plastic packaging only might drive material substitution rather than a carbon-neutral 
circular economy for packaging as a whole. 
Other comments 
In general we would like to note that we are broadly supportive of most measures suggested, 
however the cost-benefit analysis will be very dependent on the final definitions and a coherent 
look at how the different measures will interact with each other to drive circularity.  
For PCEP the first step in designing for a circular economy is to consider the necessity of the 
product. In a circular economy we produce only what we need and we then reuse the material as 
many times as possible. The reduction of unnecessary packaging and empty spaces is a logical 
application of this vision. However, the beneficial impact of such measures is highly dependent on 
definitions and the framework in which they are applied. Both aspects which need careful 
evaluation and discussion among all stakeholders. We believe limiting the definition of necessary to 
only protection and hygiene is too narrow. We do not today have a view on which metric would be 
best placed to reduce empty space and ensure overall environmental benefit.  
Similarly with measures to mandate loose produce PCEP would like to explore further for which 
packaging types and product categories this could be appropriate. We believe that to be effective 
this measure would need clear, harmonized, material neutral criteria applicable to all packaging 
types. We also need to look at the impact on the products’ environmental footprint to ensure that 
the savings are not offset by unintended consequences, such as from product losses or increased 
packaging elsewhere in the supply chain e.g. for protection in transport. 
At this time PCEP is unable to put forward a position on whether population or GDP based 
packaging waste reduction targets would be preferable, as we do not have the analysis available 
today to judge the differences in impact. We would note that once established there is no waste in 
a circular economy as it is all feedstock for new products, however during the transition a common 
approach is necessary to drive a single European market for secondary raw materials.  
PCEP believes that national advisory bodies could be helpful for businesses, particularly to 
understand the local infrastructure, systems and actors. However, we would encourage an EU level 
framework and a common approach that supports the economies of scale to drive circularity. PCEP 
notes that encouraging companies to generate waste prevention plans is helpful to drive circular

economy, however these should be harmonized at the EU level so that there is not a requirement 
on companies to produce potentially 27+ different plans fulfilling different criteria. 
We have not given an opinion on measures related to biodegradable or compostable materials, as 
these will not be made from polyolefins. As with all packaging however we support measures to 
provide clear definitions, consumer communications and collection into the appropriate waste 
stream for these materials. Such materials in the recyclable polyolefin stream would be a 
contaminant that needs to be clearly identifiable by sorting technologies and removed, to avoid 
negatively impacting the quality of the recycled polyolefin.  
Please note that for Chapter 2 of the consultation, we are of the view that it is not possible as an 
organization representing the whole polyolefin value chain to make a judgement on questions 
related to personal experience. Not least because this is very dependent on where in Europe you 
live. As such for these questions we have chosen the neutral response as a default reply.  
Polyolefin packaging is demonstrably highly recyclable and represents a significant share of all 
post-consumer plastic waste collected today. Through improvements in design, enhancements in 
collection and sorting, and increased recycling capacities, all supported by a future proof policy 
framework that encourages innovation and investment, we believe the circular economy for 
polyolefin packaging will grow rapidly. 
For all the items identified in the consultation PCEP stands ready and willing to have a thorough 
and open discussion with all stakeholders. Together we can advance the circular economy for 
packaging.

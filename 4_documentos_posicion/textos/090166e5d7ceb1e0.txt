 
 
Review of the Packaging and Packaging Waste Directive 
Club Bio-plastiques position paper 
 
Plastics which are both biobased and compostable  could play a significant role in reducing the impact on the 
environment and climate of the packaging sector. 
Waste management issues are not going to be solved by increasing mechanical recycling only. Our common willing to 
achieve a 100% rate of recycling implies to take complementar y solutions into account . In the former PPWD, 
Prevention and Reused were highlighted options, followed by Recycling in the meaning of both organic and mechanical 
recycling. Indeed, Biowaste collection targets will necessary lead to a large development of organic recycling facilities 
across EU. 
As they are fully organically recy clable, compostable plastics will be needed as introduced by the Italian example. 
Indeed, they help recover unavoidable waste which are not mechanically recyclable  for technical , economic and 
environmental reasons. Avoiding contamination of compost  by conv entional plastics , they improve biowaste 
collection and treatment to ensure high value compost which will improve soil fertility.  These facts led France to 
publish a law to exempt them of single -use plastic bags ban in 2015.  Recently, in its latest publications, the French 
Environment agency ADEME underlines that compostable plastics are recommended and efficient in order to develop 
biowaste collection.  
As a national association and in accordance with the European Bioplastics Association, we also advocate that EPR fees 
should cover the cost of recycling in industrial composting/AD facilities  in order to meet the separate collection of 
biowaste targets by 2024. This would help boost innovation and financial incentives in the modernisation and creation 
of new organic recycling infrastructures in the EU. 
Moreover, the use of sustainable, renewable resources and, consequently, the boosting of a circular European 
bioeconomy, is already strongly advocated by the Commission itself through its recent Bioeconomy Strategy and its 
Plastics Strategy. For consequence and as European Bioplastics association underlines, the benefits of using renewable 
resources for the manufacture of packaging should also be reflected in the Essential Requirements as well as in 
guidelines for the ecomodulation of EPR fees. 
Finally, the development of compostable plastics/packaging would make it possible to relocate the entire value chain 
(from agriculture to the production of high quality compost and the production and processing of resins) in Europe. 
 
Club Bioplastiques Policy recommendations 
1. Clarify the definition of “recyclability” or “recyclable”. The definition of recycling includes organic recycling, in line 
with the provisions already present in the Packaging Directive issued in 1994, thus the definition of “recyclability” or 
“recyclable” shall encompass organic recycling.  
2. Recognise the positive contribution made by compostable packaging in increasing separate organic waste collection, 
hence reducing the carbon footprint of landfilling, biowaste contamination and delivering high qualit y compost, by 
providing a rationalisation of the current Essential Requirements.  
 
 
Club Bio-plastiques is the national trade organisation for compostable and biobased plastics in France. The association 
gathers national agriculture associations, resin producers and converters, distributors, certification organism and 
consulting company. Club Bio-plastiques represents the entire value chain of compostable and biobased plastic s and 
promotes actively the development of biowaste collection.

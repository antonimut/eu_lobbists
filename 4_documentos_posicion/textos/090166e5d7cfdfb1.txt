L’Oréal response to the public consultation on the revision of the Packaging 
and Packaging Waste Directive 
 
 
For more than a century, L'Oréal, world leader in beauty, has focused its energy and competencies 
solely to one business: Beauty. L’Oréal has chosen to offer its expertise in the service of women and 
men worldwide, meeting the infinite diversity of their beauty desires. 
 
L’Oréal has been developing an innovative approach to packaging for a number of years. Already in 
2007, eco-design was integrated into our packaging policy. In 2013, Sharing Beauty with All, the first 
transformation programme of the Group, included a series of bold objectives for the sustainability of 
packaging. L’Oréal for the Future, our very new transformation programme to 2030, entails further 
commitments in this field. 
 
We welcome the opportunity to comment on packaging design requirements and other measures to 
prevent packaging waste. As we show throughout the response to the consultation and this paper, we 
support the Commission’s intention to make all packaging recyclable or reusable.  More broadly, we 
believe it is crucial to address the environmental impact of packaging in its entirety, as we have done 
over the years at L’Oréal.  
 
 
L’Oréal approach to the reduction of virgin plastics in packaging 
 
To meet consumer asks and our corporate responsibility duty, we embarked our suppliers in 2007 into 
a new eco-design policy  framework to create environment -friendly packaging  based on the 3Rs: 
Respecting safety, biodiversity and the environment; Reducing the footprint, volume and weight of 
packaging by only using necessary compone nts and optimal dimensions; and Using lower impact 
materials and promoting circularity. Along other initiatives, we have been at L’Oréal : 
 Eliminating PVC-based materials in finished products;  
 Reducing the weight and size of packaging, removing it entirely and promoting recharge 
systems; and 
 Increasing the use of recycled materials for plastics by 60% between 2018 and 2019.  
 
More substantially, we have been approaching our products from a life cycle perspective and have 
committed to improving the environmental profile of 100% of L’Oréal products by 2020 . 85% of 
products launched or renewed in 2019 had an improved social or environmental profile. For example, 
in 2019, Redken, one of the Group’s 36 brands, reformulated a series of iconic product lines leading 
to a new series of products with a packaging now composed of 100% post-consumer recycled (except 
for the colouring) and with 83% to 99% of ingredients of natural origin. 
 
Assessing the real benefits of packaging and products onto the environment ca n be undertaken only 
thanks to a multi-criteria life cycle analysis. T herefore, we developed the Sustainable Product 
Optimisation Tool (SPOT) to improve the eco-design of our products, both for packaging and formula, 
all along the life cycle of the product . Following the European Union’s Product Environmental

Designing packaging of an optimal size and weight with fewer resources and refillable formats

that luxury and sustainability go hand-in-hand. 
 
In the continuity of the 3R strategy, we have just adopted our new 6Rs packaging policy for Recycling, 
Reducing, Replac ing, Refi lling, Recharging and Reinvent ing. Our new policy reframes all the  
packaging levers to decrease the environmental footprint of packaging , being part of the e co-design 
approach. End of life is one of them, with the use of recycled content and recyclability at the end-of-
life. 
 
When it comes to the use of recycled content and recyclability at the end of life, we have found ways 
for recy cling unavoidable waste. For example, the L’Oréal Luxe sites in France have forged a 
partnership with a company called Cèdre , which dismantles the perfumes and transforms the 
recovered materials, particularly glass, into secondary raw materials. The recovered glass is then sent 
to one of our bottle suppliers, which incorporates it within its new bottles. 
 
When it comes to the end of life our packaging and when the use of mechanical recyc ling process is 
not enough ( e.g. quality, availability of feedstock), innovative recycling can provide a useful 
alternative. L’Oréal has created a consortium with Carbios to perfect the enzymatic bio recycling 
process for PET and entered in a partnership with PURECYCLE to produce recycled food-grade quality 
PP. To pursue our commitments towards giving up the use of virgin plastics for our packaging , we 
announced in June 2020 the creation of  a 50 -million-euro fund dedicated to finance inn ovative 
projects in the field of recycling and the management of plastic waste.  
 
As consumer behaviour plays a critical role, we have engaged our consumers in a recycling campaign 
called “Trions en beauté” . The campaign launched in France and Belgium explains how to sort each  
packaging of cosmetic products. We are also using globally the power of our brands to drive consumer 
changes. Khiehl’s, one of the brands of the Group, signed a global partnership singer John Legend 
around Kiehl’s sustainability p latform “ Kiehl’s Future Made Better ”, which connects the brand’s  
ongoing efforts in sustainability - through formulation, packaging, manufacturing and recycling - and 
community commitments. 
 
We strongly believe that criteria for choosing the right packaging go beyond protection of the formula 
and safety of our consumers. Ergonomics, performance, distribution, application and communication 
of the brand value matter very much when choosing the right packaging for the benefit of the formula, 
the consumers and t he environment. We are convinced that it is possible to improve the

Key policy signals for packaging sustainability 
 
We want to do more, and appropriate legislation is necessary to help us in this endeavour. Having a 
harmonized definition of recyclability, quality recycled content and unnecessary packaging will help 
us appropriately channel investments and R&D in long -term. If we are to invest into new packaging 
materials and eco-design, legislation should give the necessary signals without unduly restricting the 
way companies can achieve this.  
 
Need for a harmonised approach to packaging sustainability  
Currently, there are diverging signals between countries, especially with regard to single -use plastic 
packaging and products, the inclusion of recycled content and its alternatives. One of such signals is 
the prospect of policymakers at the national level banning some single-use plastic packaging while 
supporting higher take up of recycled plastic content or redesign for recycling. EU legislation can give 
the clarity necessary to steer investments into future-proof solutions at European level and create the 
preconditions for a well-functioning EU secondary raw materials market. This will allow companies to 
secure in an optimal way the required investments at the European level rather than adapting to the 
different set of requirements adopted by each Member State with regard to single -use products or 
packaging while providing companies with sufficient quantities of secondary raw materials. 
 
 
Need for a conducive definition of recyclability 
While we are committed to packaging redesign  for recyclability, benefits will not materialize if waste 
infrastructure does not evolve in parallel. The revision of the packaging design rules should capture 
the innovation element and ensure the definition of recyclability does not lead to a lock-in at the level 
of current waste management technologies and currently recyclable packaging formats. Separate 
collection, advanced sorting, optimized mechanical recycling and innovative recycling processes (e.g. 
chemical, enzymatic, solvolyse…) have a role to play in closing the loop, most importantly by providing 
us with a stream of food-grade recycled plastics.  
 
We urge the Commission to make available a sing le set of guidelines for  design to recycle to help 
companies with their design policy. 
 
Packaging policy which endorses life cycle thinking

remains the overarching goal of  harmonized legislation, whether this is through redesign, refill,

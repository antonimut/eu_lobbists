Introduction 
 
The EU Commission launched a consultation on the roadmap for the 
revision of the EU Ambient Air Quality Directives on 17.12.2020.  
 
The EU Air Quality Directives  define common methods for monitoring, 
assessing and providing information on air quality in the European Union 
and set air quality objectives to avoid, prevent or reduce harmful effects on 
human health and the environment as a whole. EU air quality standa rds 
have been set for 13 air pollutants: Sulfur dioxide, nitrogen dioxide and 
nitrogen oxide, particulate matter (PM10, PM2.5), ozone, benzene, lead, 
carbon monoxide, arsenic, cadmium, nickel and ben -zo(a)pyrene.  
 
As part of the European Green Deal, the EU  Commission plans to revise 
these standards to bring them more into line with the recommendations of 
the World Health Organization. The focus is on the EU Directive on 
ambient air quality and cleaner air for Europe (2008/50/EC) and the EU 
Directive on arse nic, cadmium, mercury, nickel and polycyclic aromatic 
hydrocarbons in ambient air (2004/107/EC). In addition, the EU legislation 
on clean air is to be improved overall, using the findings from last year's 
evaluation ("fitness check") of EU regulations in t his area. 
 
In BDI's view, a revision of the European ambient air quality 
directives is not necessary at present.  
 
 
1. Preserving Europe as a business location 
European industry is currently undergoing structural change and also has 
to cope with the effects of the pandemic. Corona and restrictions on 
business are leaving deep scars on employment and prosperity in the 
European member states.   
 
A forward-looking environmental policy must be committed to the goal of 
maintaining and expanding Europe as an attractive business location. 
Policymakers should therefore also act with a sense of proportion in 
environmental law and ensure that environme ntal law does not become an 
obstacle to investment for companies. A noticeable reduction in 
bureaucratic burdens and not the addition of additional disproportionate 
regulations is indispensable for economic recovery.

2. Revision of the European air quality directives is not required 
- Improvement of implementation in the member states  
A revision of the European  ambient air quality directives  is not necessary.  
 
In its recently published review of these directives, the Commission 
concludes that current legislation provides the necessary tools to meet 
existing challenges. The directives have effectively contributed to 
improving air quality and ac hieving high air quality standards. To the 
extent that they have not yet been fully achieved, this is mainly due to a 
lack of implementation or application of European law in the Member 
States, but not to weaknesses in the European requirements.  
 
 
3. Measures only based on sufficient scientific data 
A comprehensive scientific evaluation is required before air quality 
objectives are adjusted - also with a view to WHO recommendations. The 
scientific basis is currently not good enough (e.g. formation, sources, 
distribution) to derive limit values.  
  
 
4. Comprehensive impact assessment required 
Furthermore, an impact assessment should be conducted to examine the 
effects of lowering the current levels to WHO levels  not only in terms of 
health impacts and costs, but also in terms of their economic and social 
impacts and cost implications.  
 
5. Provide legal and planning security 
 
Legal certainty and planning certainty are indispensable for the economic 
activities of industry. The requirements in the European air quality 
directives must be regulated on a long -term basis, since the investment 
cycles of industry can also be very long.  
 
It should also b e taken into account that the national clean air programs 
with their partly very ambitious targets for 2030 (NEC -P Directive) have 
only just been implemented. This represents a major challenge for the 
member states and private actors. Renewed interventions  after only a few 
years should therefore be avoided.

About BDI 
 
 
The Federation of German Industries (BDI) communicates German 
industries’ interests to the political authorities concerned. It offers strong 
support for companies in global competition. BDI has access to a wide 
network within both Germany and Europe, to al l the important markets and 
to international organisations. BDI provides political flanking for the opening 
of international markets. It also offers information and guidance on all issues 
relevant to industries. BDI is the leading organisation of German in dustries 
and related service providers. It represents 36 sectoral organisations and more 
than 100,000 companies with approximately 8 million employees. 
Membership is voluntary. 15 federal representations advocate for companies’ 
interests on a regional level.

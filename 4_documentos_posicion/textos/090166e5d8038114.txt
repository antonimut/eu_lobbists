 
Feedback on the published inception IA on a Gender-based
 
and domestic violence initiative
 
 
Terre des Hommes Netherlands welcomes the EU initiative to provide uniform standards for
 
preventing gender-based violence, protecting victims and punishing offenders.
 
 
We take note of the three proposed policy options. Below is our feedback to the inception impact
 
assessment.
 
 
 
On the scope of the future legislative and non-legislative initiatives:
 
The inception IA refers to gender-based violence, including online gender-based violence, as the
 
scope of the initiatives. We recommend provide more details on the types of violence the
 
initiatives would tackle, at the minimum those should include the ones listed in the Istanbul
 
Convention, with a focus on those with cross-border dimensions, such as FGM, forced marriage
 
and online sexual violence, as well as reinforcing the prevention and protection of victims of
 
trafficking.
 
 
We recommend ensuring that the 
​
needs of child victims and witnesses of domestic violence
 
and (online) gender-based violence 
​
are fully considered and reflected in the EU initiatives.
 
 
 
According to INHOPE data, in 2019 INHOPE hotlines reports related to 320,672 illegal material
 
depicting child sexual abuse (CSAM), 91% of victims were girls and 92% of the images depicted
 
children under 13 years old. In addition,  the US companies made 16.9 millions of reports of alleged
 
CSAM, the EU being the largest host of CSAM globally. We know that the reported material is
 
only the tip of the iceberg of millions of images and videos circulating online. A total of 14 145
 
victims of sexual exploitation were reported to authorities in the EU Member States for the
 
2017-2018 period according to the latest data from the European Commission third report on
 
trafficking in human beings. Sexual exploitation being the prevalent form. Three quarters of
 
victims were women and girls, with children being a 
‘
considerable amount
’
 of those victims
 
according to Eurostat. Most child victims are girls. The data does not present an accurate picture
 
due to the large amount of unreported cases.
 
 
The data shows a gender element to the issue disproportionately affecting girls with more than
 
90% of perpetrators being men (see 
​
Wager et al, 2018
​
). This 
​
gender element
​
 should be front and
 
centre in any measure attempting to address the issue. We would also suggest that the European
 
Commission takes an 
​
intersectionality approach
​
 when drafting the future proposed legislative
 
and non-legislative measures.
 
 
On the legal basis of the future legislation:
 
We note the legal basis mentioned (Articles 82(2), 83(1), 84 and 157(3)). Yet, the policy options do
 
not explain how they would aim to harmonise laws or mention the adoption of new definitions of
 
criminal offences and sanctions.

For crimes that do not fall under the category of women and child sexual exploitation, the use of
 
Article 83(1) as legal can be challenging to use due to its unanimity requirement. We would
 
encourage the Commission to assess the use of alternative legal bases.
 
 
We suggest considering 
​
Article 114 TFEU (internal market)
​
 as a legal basis to tackle the online
 
forms of gender-based violence. We note how 
​
online terrorist content
​
 has been recently regulated
 
at the EU level through Article 114 TFEU and a regulation. Considering that online gender-based
 
violence, some of which include serious crimes such as online (child) sexual exploitation and
 
abuse, has more victims and occurs at a larger scale with important cross-border implications, we
 
believe a similar approach is required to tackle it.
 
 
On the policy options:
 
In our opinion, the proposed options 1 and 2 are not strongly enough formulated and do not align
 
sufficiently with the commitments made by the European Commission to tackle the issue of
 
gender-based violence and domestic violence across the EU. The policy options would benefit
 
from being 
​
further strengthened
​
.
 
1.
The first policy option
​
:
​
 We suggest separating the baseline scenario from the policy
 
options. 
​
The status quo should not be an option
​
. We would suggest moving all the
 
non-legislative together under this option, including those under the second policy option,
 
i.e. the proposed Recommendation on harmful practices. The Recommendation on
 
harmful practices should be based on the Istanbul Convention as well as the guidance and
 
recommendations of the Council of Europe Group of Experts on Action against Violence
 
against Women and Domestic Violence (GREVIO). Lastly, under this policy option, we
 
would recommend to also require Eurostat to publish data on the forms of violence
 
against women listed in the Istanbul Convention.
 
2.
The second policy option
​
:
​
 in addition to the adoption of legislation on minimum
 
standards for protection measures, we recommend that this policy option also aim to
 
tackle 
​
online 
​
gender-based violence, including against children. Under this policy option,
 
we would recommend to adopt common standards for 
​
data collection
​
 on the forms of
 
violence against women and child victims (by gender) listed in the Istanbul Convention
 
and require Eurostat to publish the data for all EU27 on an annual basis.
 
3.
The third policy option
​
: as it stands, this policy option is the most likely to reach
 
meaningfully the objectives of the policy initiative. 
​
We strongly recommend pursuing
 
and strengthening further this policy option
​
.
 
 
a.
Violence against children
​
: gender-based violence against children requires an
 
adapted response for both child victims and child perpetrators. The initiative
 
should also consider child peer to peer gender-based violence by identifying
 
legislative and non-legislative best practices on the issue with the view to adopt EU
 
wide measures.
 
b.
Online
​
 
​
violence
​
: it is crucial that the EU adopt strong measures to tackle online
 
gender-based violence, taking into account the specific vulnerability of children in
 
the online sphere. Considering the cross-border nature of the Internet, we believe
 
it is crucial that the EU steps up to 
​
harmonise the definitions
​
 of the online forms
 
of violence and set standards aiming at preventing and protecting victims, and
 
with specific measures aimed at child victims. The forms of online violence should
 
include sexual harassment, sexual exploitation, grooming, sexual abuse, stalking,

psychological violence as well as the use of the Internet to facilitate offline forms
 
of violence such as physical violence, forced marriage and FGM.
 
c.
Data collection
​
: as for the second policy option, we would recommend to adopt
 
common standards for data collection on the forms of violence against women
 
and child victims (by gender) listed in the Istanbul Convention and require
 
Eurostat to publish the data for all EU27 on an annual basis.

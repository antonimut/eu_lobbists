FEEDBACK 
Inception impact assessment - Ares(2020)7689281 
“Revision of the Ambient Air Quality Directives” 
 
WSM Wirtschaftsverband Stahl- und Metallverarbeitung e.V. (German Steel and 
Metal Processing  Industry Association), representing the German steel and 
metal processing industry towards politics and economy, would like to take this 
opportunity to comment on the Inception impact assessment  
Ares(2020)7689281 “Revision of the Ambient Air Quality Directives”. 
 
WSM is of the opinion that a revision of the Ambient Air Quality Directives 
(AAQ Directives) is not necessary at present. 
 
The Fitness Check1 of the AAQ Directives carried out by the EU Commission in 
2019 has shown in particular that the Directives  
• have guided the establishment of a representative high-quality monitoring 
of air quality, set clear air quality standards, and facilitated the exchange of 
reliable, objective, comparable information on air quality, including to a 
wider public and that they 
• significantly contributed to a downward trend in air pollution and reduced 
the number and magnitude of exceedances. 
 
Lack of implementation or application of European law in the Member 
States. 
However, the Fitness Check also showed that in most Member States, the qual-
ity of life of EU citizens remains hampered, as air quality standards, especially 
for particulate matter and nitrogen dioxide, are still not being met. The main prob-
lem is that the AAQ Direc tives have not ensured that sufficient action is taken 
throughout the EU to meet air quality standards and keep exceedances as short 
as possible. To the extent that they have not yet been fully achieved, this is 
mainly due to a lack of implementation or application of European law in the 
Member States, but not to weaknesses in the European requirements. 
 
Absolutely essential: Impact assessments. 
As requirements in the field of air policy are not only associated with health, 
social and environmental impacts, but also entail significant economic conse-
quences, impact assessments of possible new requirements (especially WHO 
recommendations) are indispensable as a basis for evaluating future efforts. It 
is important to describe the health, economic, social and environmental impacts, 
especially on SMEs and competitiveness. The principle of proportionality (cost-
benefit ratio) should play a decisive role in weighing up possible consequences. 
 
Furthermore, a comprehensive scientific evaluation is required before air quality 
objectives are adjusted - also with a view to WHO recommendations.

Only a coherent and consistent European air policy can achieve its goals. 
It is necessary to clearly define interfaces with other existing areas of law in the 
field of air policy. To avoid duplication of regulations and work, a coherent and 
consistent European air policy is necessary. 
In this context, it must also be taken into account that European industry is cur-
rently undergoing structural change and also has to cope with the effects of the 
pandemic. Corona and restrictions on business are leaving deep scars on em-
ployment and prosperity in the European member states. A forward-looking en-
vironmental policy must be committed to the goal of maintaining and expanding 
Europe as an attractive business location. 
 
Strengthening legal and planning certainty. 
Legal certainty and planning certainty are indispensable for the economic activ-
ities of industry. The requirements in the AAQ Directives must be regulated on 
a long-term basis, since the investment cycles of industry can also be very long.  
 
It should also be taken into account that the national clean air programs with 
their partly very ambitious targets for 2030 (NEC-P Directive) have only just been 
implemented. This represents a major challenge for the member states and pri-
vate actors. Renewed interventions after only a few years should therefore be 
avoided. 
 
 
Thank you for your considering our comments. 
 
 
 
Düsseldorf, 14th January 2021 
 
 
 
 
 
 
WSM Wirtschaftsverband Stahl- und Metallverarbeitung e.V.  (German Steel and Metal 
Processing Industry Association) 
WSM represents the economic policy interests of the steel and metal processing industry 
and is one of the largest business associations in Germany. The German steel and metal 
processing industry are approximately 5,000 mainly medium-sized industrial companies 
with a turnover of about 80 billion euros a year and an average number of employees of 
around 500,000. The companies employ on average 100 employees and are important 
customers of the steel producers. They process around 18 million tons of steel a year - 
around 40 percent of the German steel production. 
The industry is characterized by high specialization and intense compe tition. The com-
panies manufacture for the international markets of the automotive, electrical, construc-
tion and engineering industry and for the trade sector.  
WSM is a member of the Federation of German Industries e.V. (BDI). Political advocacy 
in Europe takes place through ORGALIM, the European association of the mechanical, 
electrical, electronic and metal articles industries. WSM comprises 14 member associa-
tions. More about our member associations at http://www.wsm-net.de/ueber-

 
Brussels, 14 January 2021 
 
EEB welcomes the publication of the European Commission’s roadmap on the inception impact assessment for 
the revision of the Ambient Air Quality Directives (AAQDs) and the opportunity to provide feedback to it. 
The roadmap includes some details on the Commission's understanding of the problem and possible solutions 
related to the revision of the AAQ Directives. Some of those are repeated in the list below, others had been 
reformulated and others are new – to fully reflect the picture that EEB thinks the Commission should have in 
mind when conducting the Impact Assessment:  
1. the alignment of the EU air quality standards with scientific knowledge including the latest 
recommendations of the World Health Organisation (WHO) and the work under the Long-Range 
Transboundary Air Pollution Convention; 
2. the need for improving implementation and enforcement, also through the introduction of provisions 
on sanctions and penalties for non-fulfilment of relevant obligations deriving from the Directives; 
3. the need to harmonise and improve access to information related to air pollution; 
4. the importance of strengthening provisions on air quality monitoring, modelling and plans without 
waiting for the AAQDs revision but making use of the EC’s implementing powers now; 
5. the importance of considering the air pollution’s impacts on public health, social equity, environment 
and fundamental rights; 
6. the need for strengthening supporting legislation and other initiatives which will reduce air pollution 
(such as the climate initiatives). 
Part B – Policy area 1 – a closer alignment of the EU air quality standards with scientific knowledge 
including the latest recommendations of the World Health Organisation (WHO): one key element which 
will define the level of ambition of the revised AAQD is the nature and the strictness of the forthcoming revised 
air quality standards. We welcome the fact that the European Commission has announced that it will include 
the revised new WHO air quality guidelines (expected to be published in the first half of 2021) in its Impact 
Assessment, i.e. to elaborate scenarios which will fully reflect the recommendations included therein. Moreover, 
the Commission should also consider scientific evidence on critical loads and levels for the protection of the 
environment as elaborated in the framework of the Long-Range Transport of Air Pollution Convention. This is 
especially important regarding ammonia - critical levels for ammonia concentrations must be included in the 
new AAQD. Air quality standards for ammonia (provided here 
https://unece.org/fileadmin/DAM/env/documents/2017/AIR/EMEP/Final__new_Chapter_3_v2__August_2017_.pd
f) are necessary to prevent damage to ecosystems, but as ammonia also is an important precursor of PM 2.5, a 
standard for ammonia concentration will also deliver benefits for human health. 
Scenarios considered by the Impact Assessment for the AAQD will have to integrate recent information 
provided by the Second Clean Air Outlook (published on 8 January 2021), in particular the role played by

complementary (National Emission Ceilings Directive) and source legislation, as well as expected co-benefits 
from climate policies. Also sector policies for e.g. energy, transport and agriculture should be accounted for. 
The combined effect of these legislations and policies will contribute significantly to the achievement of the 
updated WHO air quality guidelines, which most likely will be more stringent than the existing ones. The 
Commission’s Second Clean Air Outlook shows that the implementation of existing policies will already ensure 
that most areas of the EU will be below 10 ug/m3 concentration of PM2.5 by 2030, and many areas even below 
5 ug/m3. Given the growing evidence showing that there are no safe levels of air pollution, it will be 
fundamental for the Impact Assessment scenarios to consider this key fact and prospect the achievement of the 
air quality standards which are fully aligned with the updated WHO guidelines.  
1: The IA must consider full alignment with the forthcoming revised WHO air quality guidelines as well as 
scientific evidence produced under the LRTAP Convention on critical levels for ammonia to protect ecosystems 
and human health. 
As identified by the Fitness Check’s conclusions, air quality standards in the form of binding Limit Values have 
been and will continue to be a key driver for reducing air pollution concentrations. Other forms of standards, 
such as Exposure Reduction Targets or Target Values, play a complementary role (i.e. not an alternative one). 
We therefore urge that binding Limit Values must continue to be the primary legislative instrument in the 
revised AAQD. Target values can be useful as a temporary step for pollutants that are currently non-regulated 
(e.g. black carbon and ultrafine particles) - standards for these pollutants could initially be set as target values, 
but should eventually become limit values. 
2: The IA must elaborate scenarios where binding Limit Values play the primary role; options where these are 
complemented by other type of standards (such as Exposure Reduction Targets or Target Values for non-
regulated pollutants) should also be considered. 
3: The urgency to reduce health and environmental damage due to air pollution means that the IA must 
consider a very ambitious timeline for achieving the revised air quality standards (aligned with WHO 
guidelines).  
4: A process for automatic adjustment of air quality standards as soon as new scientific evidence is available 
should be part of the revised AAQD. 
Policy Area 2: improving the air quality legislative framework, including provisions on penalties and 
public information, in order to enhance effectiveness, efficiency and coherence: The role played by the 
revised AAQD will be complemented by policies to reduce pollution at source and reductions of emissions at 
national level. The Communication on the European Green Deal has announced several new actions which will 
also deliver on air pollution reduction (e.g. Smart and Sustainable Mobility Strategy, Renovation Wave and 
Zero-Pollution Action Plan, together with the Climate Law for a climate neutral EU). These initiatives will drive 
the development of more ambitious source policies which should be based on the latest technological and 
scientific developments. These three elements (air quality standards, reduction of national emissions and 
 
reduction of emissions at the source) are complementary: the AAQD will not be the only instrument to reduce 
air pollution, parallel paths exist and others will materialise in the coming years. This makes the need for an 
ambitious revised AAQD (aligned with the updated WHO air quality guidelines) an imperative choice, to make 
sure that the resulting picture is coherent, also with the Zero Pollution ambition.  
5: When assessing feasibility of compliance with the updated WHO recommendations, the scenarios defined by 
the Impact Assessment will have to account for all the policies and legislation (existing and soon to be adopted) 
which will contribute to reducing air pollution, including the support by financial instruments (for example the 
Next Generation EU Program) which will accompany these initiatives. It will also have to fully consider what has 
again been highlighted by the Second Clean Air Outlook, namely that the socio-economic benefits from 
reducing air pollution are much higher than the related costs, and that reduced air pollution contributes to an 
incremental GDP growth. This will make ambitious air quality standards (aligned with the latest WHO 
guidelines) key to ensure that the EU has a legislative framework which really promotes necessary change and 
reduce air pollution to the minimum, in line with the Zero Pollution ambition.   
EEB welcomes the purpose of merging Directives 2008/50/EC and 2004/107/EC into a single instrument and 
removing redundant provisions identified in the Fitness Check. 
A revised AAQD must include a specific provision covering Access to Justice. This provision will not take away 
the need for a horizontal instrument ensuring and harmonising access to justice in environmental matters at 
national level, but it will supply the needed legal certainty until the time a horizontal framework is established. 
6: The revised AAQD must include a provision establishing rules for ensuring access to justice at national level 
when the matter is related to air quality. 
Given the wide discrepancy in the national systems which results in lack of effective remedies in several 
countries, there is the need to revise provisions on penalties in the Ambient Air Quality Directives and introduce 
a more detailed provision than the current Article 30 of Directive 2008/50/EC. 
7: The revised AAQD must include a more detailed provision on penalties. 
Public information on air quality should be actively disseminated by public authorities, as requested by existing 
legislation. Updated and live data should be presented in an accessible and understandable way, using a 
harmonised system in all Member states, so to also allow comparability and the establishment of a common 
alerting system. 
8: The revised AAQD must define an air quality information system to present updated data to the general 
public in an accessible and harmonised way, which also allow comparability and as common alerting system. 
Policy area 3: strengthening of air quality monitoring, modelling and plans: the roadmap considers the 
revised AAQD as the instrument to update rules on monitoring, modelling and air quality plans. While it is 
 
important to include key changes in the revision of the AAQD, the Commission should immediately provide 
additional guidance on air quality monitoring, modelling and plans through the adoption of implementing acts 
under Article 28 of Directive 2008/50/EC; these provisions could then be reflected in the revised AAQD.  
 9: Key steps to be considered for strengthening of air quality monitoring, modelling and plans are:  
- Increase the minimum number of PM2.5 stations and set clearer requirements for the proportion 
between different types of monitoring stations; 
- Introduce definitions of different types of monitoring stations; 
- Require the installation of monitoring stations for ultra-fine particles, black carbon and ammonia; 
- Ensure continuity of measurements for all pollutants; 
- Strengthen the siting criteria and establishing a system to assess the functioning of the monitoring 
stations; 
- Provide clearer requirements for the content, publication and review of the documentation on network 
design and site locations; 
- Require a more regular use of models and indicative measurements to support information from fixed 
sampling points and introduce reference methods for modelling and indicative measurements; 
- Strengthen and clarify the requirements in Annex XV concerning minimum content of air quality plans 
and provide a checklist of pollution abatement measures to be considered during the preparation of 
air quality plans; 
- Set out clearer requirements concerning the process for the adoption and revision of air quality plans, 
including requirements on timeframes, public participation and provision of information on technical 
assessment and forecasting.

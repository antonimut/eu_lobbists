 
 
 
 
 
 
CATF feedback on the Revision of the Ambient Air 
Quality Directives 
 
The Clean Air Task Force (CATF), a climate and energy organization dedicated 
to decarbonizing the global energy system with staff in the US and Europe, 
welcomes the opportunity to provide feedback on the revision pr ocess of the 
Ambient Air Quality (AAQ) Directives. 
 
Air pollution has long been associ ated with detrimental impacts  not only on 
human health but on overall ecosystems. It is a major global environmental risk 
to human health, causing, in 2018, more than 400,000 premature deaths in 
Europe alone. CATF believes that the European Commission’s init iative to 
revise the AAQ Directives, especially the AQ Directive 2008/50/EC on ambient 
air quality and cleaner air for Europe (henceforth refer to as the AQ Directive), 
is a key step to ensure cleaner air, protecting citizens and ec osystems. This 
legislative framework has been instrumental in reducing air pollution in Europe 
but exceedances still occur and episodes of high pollution stil l have a large 
impact on the population. 
 
It is imperative though that these revisions are not made in a vacuum. Air 
pollutants and greenhouse gases s hare sources and the alignment  of climate 
mitigation strategies making consideration of the co-benefits a nd trade-offs 
from emission reductions essential. Revisions to the AAQ direct ive should 
consider the Commission’s plan to increase the ambition of the EU’s 2030 
climate goals to ensure carbon neutrality by mid-century and th e launch of an 
EU Action Plan Towards a Zero Pollution Ambition to address all  form s of 
pollution, to harmonize these efforts and to benefits of these programs. 
Additionally, scientific evidence and guidance developed, for e xample, under 
the Long-Range Transboundary Air Pollution Convention, the FAIR MODE 
initiative, and the recently provided by the Second Clean Air O utlook should 
be considered in this revision process. 
 
We would like to highlight sever al priorities that should be co nsidered in the 
upcoming impact assessment (IA) and proposed revision of the AAQ Directive: 
 
1. Update of Air Quality standards 
CATF welcomes the announcement of the long due update of AQ standards and 
the plan to follow WHO guidelines. Following the latest scienti fic evidence 
regarding the impact of air pollu tion on human health, the IA s hould consider 
more protective and binding air quality limit values for pollut ants already 
regulated, and consider the addition of new ones, namely, ultra fine particles, 
black carbon (BC), and Volatile Organic Compounds (VOCs). Furthermore, as

science evolves and new findings arise every year it is important that the AAQ 
Directive can be quickly updated to reflect new recommendations.  
 
2. Alignment of EU policies 
Improvement of air quality will not be achieved under the AQ Directive alone, 
but should be defined as a multilateral strategy starting from the sources. This 
revision should fully align with new EU climate policies aiming  at methane 
reductions which will also result in substantial reductions in VOCs and other 
air toxics, and other complementary existing EU Directives addr essing 
pollutant emissions (namely, the National Emissions Ceilings (NEC) Directive 
(2016/2284/EU), or similar sector-oriented regulations). 
 
3. Improved legal implementation and enforcement  
CATF commends the Commission for acknowledging the need to “amended 
provisions on sanctions and penalties to be established in national systems for 
non-fulfilment of relevant obligati ons deriving from the Directives.”  W e  
believe that the revision of the AAQ Directive should also add a provision to 
allow for citizen action to access justice, namely, allowing NG Os to challenge 
in court the noncompliance of standards, such as exceedances of limit values. 
 
4. Disseminating air quality conditions   
Growth in digitalization and technology offer possibilities to improve existing 
systems and develop new methods to disseminate live air pollution conditions, 
as requested by existing legislation, in accessible ways and ma king use of 
indexes that are clear to the general public and harmonized acr oss different 
countries. Furthermore, alert systems of exceedances should be improved to 
swiftly inform the population, especially the sensitive groups.  
 
5.  Transboundary impact  
Air pollution is not always a lo cally caused problem with many pollutants 
emitted in one location having negative impacts several kms awa y. High 
background ozone concentrations are often caused by precursors, namely NOx 
and VOCs (CH4 being one of these) emitted somewhere else. Effec tive 
reduction of air pollution will also require support for intern ational action by 
non-EU neighboring countries, and wider international reduction  of air 
pollution and its precursors. Methane is a critical component o f transboundary 
air pollution because it is a precursor of O3, and the second m ost important 
GHG contributing to climate chang e. Methane should be a priorit y of the 
European Commission that can be tackled with proper alignment o f air 
pollution legislation and climate mitigation instruments, such as the Methane 
Strategy recently adopted by the EU. We also recommend that the Commission 
should consider instruments to enforce action on Member States that are 
systematically negatively impact their neighboring countries. One possibility to 
assess such cases is by using sc ientifically recognized tools s uch as source-
apportionment studies, and/or chemical transport models that that offer source-
 
 
 
 
attribution or source-receptor capability to identify the sourc e of pollution 
exceedances.  
 
6. Strengthening of air quality monitoring, modelling and plans 
The success of the AAQ Directive is very much dependent on good  planning 
(on short- and long-term), that considers the implementation of  efficient and 
feasible measures and adequate c ontrol of air pollution levels.  CATF believes 
that key improvements to be taken into consideration are: 
 
 Monitoring: Provide guidelines on network design and site locations; 
increase the number of regulated monitoring stations representing 
different locations; introduce measurements for VOCs, including CH4, 
and collect more data on PM2.5, ultrafine particles and BC; ensure 
continuity of measurements; improve the QA/QC process and call for 
better data provided by the Member States; consider the inclusion of 
using small sensors for monitoring particular air pollution episodes; 
 Modelling:  provide clear guidance and requirements for the use of air 
quality models, working towards harmonization (e.g. on 
spatial/temporal resolutions suitable for assessment of Action Plans 
and air quality and population exposure), and recognize the key role of 
CH4 for O3 modeling and promote developments in this direction; 
 AQ plans: provide clear and updated guidelines, and define 
requirements for the adoption and revision of air quality plans, namely 
what aspects and different legislation can be addressed, guidance on 
technical assessment of suggested strategies and forecast of trends, 
implementation and execution timeframes and spatial scope, 
encouraging opportunities for public consultation provision of 
information on technical assessment and forecasting.

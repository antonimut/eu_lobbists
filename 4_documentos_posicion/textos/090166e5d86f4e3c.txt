   
 
 
Development of post-Euro 6/VI emission standards for cars, vans, lorries and buses 
 
Transport en Logistiek Nederland represents a significant number of Dutch companies in the logistics 
sector. Many are small and medium-sized family businesses, which operate throughout the European 
Union. The sector makes an important contribution to employment and economic activity in The 
Netherlands. With the current Euro VI norm for lorries, emissions have already decreased to a large 
extent. This has modernised the Dutch truck fleet significantly as most registered trucks in The 
Netherlands are Euro VI.  
 
The revision of the Euro 6/VI standards and a possible post-Euro VI standards poses the question to 
what extent a new Euro-standard will contribute to cleaner trucks. TLN doubts if a new standard will 
contribute significantly to cleaner vehicles when compared to other zero emission solutions. The 
current market developments show an increase in the number of zero emission trucks. This number 
will probably increase in the coming years since in the segment of trucks used for the short and 
middle long distances there will probably more types of ZE-trucks on the market. Also for the heavier 
types of trucks there seems to be a promising zero emission alternative to the combustion engine in 
the form of hydrogen.  
 
Taking the above into consideration, TLN has doubts about the impact of a new Euro-standard and 
would like to make a few remarks; 
- A new Euro-standard will make available even cleaner trucks, to which no objection can be 
made. As long as investments in the new Euro-standards do not undermine or slow down the 
development of zero emission solutions  
- There will be a need for (cleaner) combustion engines in the coming years but, given the 
developments as outlined above, this will be a decreasing market share. It is therefore 
important to have a good understanding whether investments and purchase costs justify the 
choice for a new Euro-standard 
- It will be most difficult for the heavier (or heaviest) types of trucks to switch to zero emission 
technologies. These truck types were also the last to switch to Euro-VI (for instance, in The 
Netherlands this happened only 3 years ago). A new Euro-standard would provide transport 
companies with cleaner trucks to continue their operations whilst in parallel waiting for the 
development of zero emission solutions for this segment so they can gradually phase out 
combustion engines 
- Experiences with Euro-VI requiring Ad Blue make it, in case of post-Euro VI regulations, most 
important to install robust technology that can’t be switched off easily in practise. This has 
turned out to be a vulnerability with Euro-VI trucks for which adequate enforcement is 
lacking

 
 
EUROPEAN COMMISSION PUBLIC CONSULTATION  
 
CLIMATE CHANGE – NEW RULES TO PREVENT METHANE LEAKAGE IN THE ENERGY SECTOR 
 
FEEDBACK PERIOD: 22 DECEMBER 2020 - 26 JANUARY 2021 
 
HSE GROUP’S CONTRIBUTION  
 
DATE: JANUARY 26, 2021 
 
 
HSE Group welcomes current efforts put forward by the European Commission and fully supports the 
objectives of the European Green Deal. The power sector is committed to the clean energy transition 
and we can expect additional emission reductions to be driven by the implementation of the climate 
and energy policy and intentions of the European power sector to invest in renewables and efficient 
low carbon sources.  
HSE Group supports the objectives of the European Green Deal and therefore the ambition ofcarbon 
neutrality of European economy by 2050 and more ambitious GHG emissions reductions by 2030, 
however, we believe that the proposed sector-specific measures should be guided by carefully 
examining in a broad and encompassing manner all the potential consequences, including those 
specifically relevant for individual member states and their energy mix.   
  
HSE Group is the largest electricity producer in Slovenia, our power plants produce 69% of electricity 
in Slovenia, of which 50% from lignite exploitered in modern underground coal mine, 100 % owned by 
HSE, with annual production about 3.5 million tons. 
 
It is estimated that in the EU, 53% of anthropogenic methane emissions come from the agricultural 
sector, 26% from the waste sector and 19% from the energy sector. We would also like to point out 
that the costs / measures for reduction of methane emissions in energy sector should be proportionate 
to the contribution, which is small in the case of energy, and especially underground lignite.  
 
Emissions of methane from lignite mining are of marginal importance from a climate point of view and 
can only be estimated on the cumulative level, due to measurement difficulties and consequently 
higher costs and lower accuracy. Satellite measurement has also so far proved to be too inaccurate 
and only detect larger emission sources. According to current estimates, 54% of methane emissions in 
the energy sector are fugitive emissions from the oil and gas sector, 34% fugitive emissions from the 
coal sector. We therefore believe that it is crucial for the energy sector to focus on fugitive emissions 
from oil and gas production, supply infrastructure and end-use, rather than coal.  
 
Below we provide you with our views on the management of methane emissions from lignite 
production.

1. Improving information related to the actions on compulsory measurement, 
reporting and verification (MRV) at company level for all energy related methane 
emissions building on the Oil and Gas Methane Partnership (OGMP 2.0) 
methodology 
 
The proposed MRV standards, based on the Oil and Gas Methane Partnership OGMP (2.0) 
methodology applicable to the oil and gas sector and the potential extension to coal by this legislative 
framework would in our case cause additional investment costs, the eligibility of which could be 
questionable and their amount disproportionate due to progressive phasing out of coal across the EU 
- in Slovenia the national strategy of coal phase-out is currently n preparation. Therefore HSE does not 
support any additional action in the field of MRV based on OGMP 2.0 that will cause any additional 
costs to the mining companies, which are in the process of abandoning the underground lignite 
exploitation, and are not connected to the safety and health of miners.  
 
We would like to draw attention to certain specifics in our country that relate to our underground 
lignite exploitation , due to which we already have a well-established regular monitoring of methane 
emissions, which is in our case essential for the establishment of adequate venting for the safety and 
health of miners. It is therefore essential to maintain control of methane emissions due to methane 
ventilation in underground coal mines. 
 
Methane is already measured continuously at cave sites and periodically at fan stations where the  air 
flows are large and methane concentration comparatively low, which impacts the measurement´s 
accuracy. Regarding measurement, we propose to maintain their implementation on an annual basis 
(as is currently the case) and the assessment of methane emissions in relation to coal production. 
 
2. Mitigation related to the action on eliminating routine venting and flaring in the 
energy sector covering the full supply chain, up to the point of production  
 
According to EU strategy to reduce methane emissions venting and routine flaring should be restricted 
to unavoidable circumstances, for example for safety reasons, and recorded for verification purposes 
It should be considered that methane emissions in underground mines are related to ventilation to 
ensure the safety of employees and therefore we should strive to exempt any safety measurements 
changes that serve to worker safety in the underground mines  from any additional financial 
obligations or regulation changes that may arise when the legislation is adopted.  
 
Due to the process of coal phase out, CMM (coal mine methane) emissions are constantly decreasing, 
compared to 1990, only a tiny fraction of past emission levels remains. Therefore, we suggest that 
more focus should be placed on abandoned mine methane (AMM) due to the declining trend of coal 
mining. The closure of coal mines requires smart approach with reallocating resources to reduce 
emissions and maximize the utilization of methane for other purposes (for heating and power 
generation). In this sense, of course, it is necessary to consider what is economically acceptable. 
Methane capture technologies are momentarily, in most cases expensive and unprofitable and will 
require additional governmental/EU support or taxation reduction to reach commercial availability in 
due time. 
 
 
 Overall, we consider that the initiative for new rules on methane leakage in the energy sector might 
be useful in the sense that more attention and possibilities of financing from EU funds and other 
sources outside the company will be focused on methane emissions reduction projects after the 
excavation is completed. Therefore, we propose and appeal to the Commission to provide funding 
 
 
resources for research projects focused on capturing and use of methane emissions - as a reference 
we would like to mention the project CoalTech2051, supported by the EU Research Fund for Coal 
and Steel (RFCS), which is focused on  AMM and use of methane for different purposes.

Proposal for a legislative act to reduce methane emissions in the oil, gas and 
coal sectors 
GAZ-SYSTEM position paper 
 
GAZ-SYSTEM S.A. as the Polish Gas Transmission System Operator is strongly committed to provide consumers with 
a secure, affordable and climate friendly energy supply. It is even of more importance as the energy and heating 
sectors in the CEE reg ion are dominated by solid fossil fuels . Thus, there is an urgent need to bring down CO 2 
emissions as well as to improve air quality as it struggles with one of the highest air pollution in Europe. GAZ-SYSTEM 
welcomes the  opportunity to express its views on the European Commission proposal for a legislative act to 
reduce methane emissions in the oil, gas and coal sector. GAZ -SYSTEM supports broad coverage of the energy 
sector by given regulation . Taking into consideration its main area of expertise  it refers herein mainly to the gas 
sector.  
GAZ-SYSTEM in previously submitted position paper in consultation preceding publication of the EU Strategy to 
reduce methane emissions  described its actions and initiatives taken in order to mitigate and limit methane 
emissions on its infrastructure . Therefore, herein we would like to highlight the fact that as for the moment there 
is no recognized and widely used methodology to calculate emissions with differentiation on each element of 
the gas infrastructure.  Such definition, as we understand will be built based on the outcome of the OGMP 2.0 
initiative. GAZ-SYSTEM fully supports such attempts and is of the opinion that its creation would be beneficial for 
the whole sector. However, there are some aspects that shall be taken  into consideration while working on this 
methodology such as the fact that gas infrastructure differs across Europe and the same measures should not 
be perceived for all of it. Until now, the European TSOs have been developing measures best adjusted to th e 
infrastructure they operate, w hich resulted in  the lack of harmonization among  them in this matter. Each 
company has developed its own approach to methane emissions mitigation, which has led them to use different 
equipment and methods to address leaks. Therefore, establishing a single approach will be difficult and may be 
resisted by some companies due to the higher costs associated with harmonization. Therefore, either the planned 
regulation should be flexible in a way allowing for adjustment to th e local circumstances or companies that will 
need to completely change their approach should receive additional support.  Another point to consider is  a 
situation where the modernisation costs of older parts of the gas infrastructure will not be fully recognised by the 
National Regulatory Authority due to its higher  value than calculated based on the other  parts of infrastructure 
(where such actions were easier and cheaper).  
With regard to the precise proposals of the European Commission voiced in the EU Strategy to reduce methane 
emissions as well as the given consultation paper GAZ-SYSTEM would like to highlight the following: 
• Recognition of the operating costs borne by the regulated entities – the costs of the actions taken by 
regulated entities such as TSOs in order to reduce methane emissions should be fully recognised by NRAs; 
GAZ-SYSTEM would like to also  highlight in this point that in order to achieve the lower level of emissions 
at net zero costs, the initial investments on the e .g. TSO side are required allowing for capturing of 
methane to be emitted otherwise. Such costs would also be included in the tariff base impacting the

consumers (that would probably recover the costs borne in the future when the income from ‘sa ved 
methane’ would be included in the tariff base). This in fact means that “net zero cost” is not the same as 
“no cost at all” – therefore measures created based on this assumptions should take into consideration 
its financial impact on consumers; 
• Scope of the legislation – GAZ-SYSTEM supports the proposal for the legislation to cover the whole supply 
chain of gas. One of the main principles of the Third Energy Package was to create competitive market 
for gas enabling diversification of routes and suppliers. If the methane emission obligation is not imposed 
on all gas suppliers to Europe, the ones which voluntarily decide to take actions to limit methane emissions 
will be in a worse situation as these supply routes will become less price competitive. Therefore, we are of 
the opinion that limiting the scope of regulation only to the infrastructure within EU will be 
counterproductive and may have negative impact on the methane emission reduction outside the EU; 
• Future of the OGMP 2.0. initiative – we believe that the best solution would be to combine option 1 (OGMP 
continues as a voluntary initiative ) for the transition period with setting option 3 ( Translating the OGMP 
framework into EU legislation applicable to the full supply chain ) as an ultimate goal (for example in the 
2025-2030 perspective); 
• Compulsory measurement, reporting and verification (MRV) – as mentioned above, providing the proper 
methodology for measurement and verification of methane emissions, such proposal shall be supported. 
Nonetheless, there are some aspects which in our opinion should be considered such as: there should be 
one international entity responsible for collecting data (an international methane emission observatory), 
multiplication of reporting obligations needs to be avoided; 
• LDAR projects – GAZ-SYSTEM supports the idea of improving of the LDAR programmes. However, we would 
like to underline that more ambitious LDAR projects are still under development as there are a number of 
technologies available on the market. We believe that LDAR programmes can significantly improve the 
level of emissions, however they are also requiring significant investments (in technology as well as 
employees trainings etc.). In our opinion, due to the differences among TSOs in: 
o equipping with methane detection devices, 
o age of the gas network in operation, 
o types of devices installed on the gas network, etc.,  
a possible future legislation for the LDAR program mes should be of a general nature. For example, the 
regulations should not: limit the methods allowed for performing LDAR inspections, set limit values for 
emissions from individual elements (leak definitions), indicate the frequency of LDAR inspections, etc. 
• Regulations related to restriction of venting and flaring – GAZ-SYSTEM supports the idea of limitation of the 
venting and flaring. However, in our view it might be impossible to completely forbid such actions without 
negatively impacting the system integrity and security. In case of emergency situation of the gas system, 
venting and/or flaring are sometimes the only technical option to ensure security of the system and quick 
recovery of the supply. Therefore, they should be allowed in exceptional circumstances. Of course, each 
time before deciding to use these methods all other available technical solutions should be considered 
as well as possibilities of maximum limitation of the methane emissions e .g. through using mobile 
compressor stations, decreasing  the pressure in a pipeline or using hot tapping and plugging  
technologies. 
On top of the above comments, GAZ -SYSTEM would like to once again stress the importance of following 
principles in creation of the future legislation: 
• Each market participant subject to the planned regulation, should be responsible only for emissions from 
its own scope of business, infrastructure or the infrastructure it is operating on.  
• In order to make the Strategy effective, it is crucial to provide a clear framework for each part of the 
value chain, taking into consideration its technical as well as regional specificities. This is especially

important with regard to the CEE region, where natural gas is indispensable to foster the energy transition 
(i.e. coal to gas switch is a quick win for the both emission reduction and consumers, gas supporting the 
uptake of intermittent RES). Thus, the potential savings in the emissions due to the transition towards low 
emission sources (e.g. natural gas) should b e also taken into consideration while assessing the climate 
value of the gas transmission systems. 
• The amount of methane emissions and targeted emission reductions should only be based on emissions 
released from gas infrastructure components under a direct  control of a given operator  and system 
failure being a sudden event that may be caused by many different (including external) factors should 
not be calculated within its scope.

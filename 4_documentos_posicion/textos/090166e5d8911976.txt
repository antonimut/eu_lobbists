 
Feedback on Inception Impact Assessment
 
Proposal for a Legislative Act to Reduce
 
Methane Emissions
 
Public Consultation
 
26 January 2021
 
 
We
welcome
the
decision
of
the
European
Commission
to
introduce
a
legislative
act
to
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
reduce
methane
emissions
in
the
energy
sector
[1],
and
would
like
to
highlight
the
need
to
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
cover
the
energy-intensive
petrochemical
sector
(from
an
energy
consumption
but
also
from
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
a feedstock point of view).
 
According
to
the
International
Energy
Agency,
petrochemicals
account
for
14%
and
8%
of
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
total
primary
demand
for
oil
and
gas,
respectively,
and
will
become
the
world’s
biggest
driver
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
of
oil
demand
–
ahead
of
trucks,
aviation
and
shipping
[2].
Given
that
oil
and
gas
production
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
and
processing
constitute
significant
contributors
to
methane
emissions—for
which
naphtha
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
and
natural
gas
liquids
contribute
as
co-products—venting
and
leakage
in
the
petrochemical
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
and
plastic
production
should
be
subject
to
the
measures
under
consideration,
including
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
measurement,
reporting
and
verification
(MRV),
leak
detection
and
repair
(LDAR)
and
the
ban
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
on
routine
venting
and
flaring,
among
others.
Addressing
methane
emissions
from
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
petrochemical
and
plastic
production
is
a
critical
first
step
toward
reducing
the
overall
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
climate
impact
from
plastics,
which
is
estimated
to
generate
56
gigatons
(Gt)
of
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
carbon-dioxide
equivalent
(CO​
2​
e)
emissions
by
2050,
corresponding
to
10-13%
of
the
global
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
carbon budget to stay within a 1.5° warming scenario.[3]
 
While
the
EU
has
taken
many
strides
to
reduce
plastic
use
through
the
adoption
of
the
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
European
Strategy
for
Plastics
in
a
Circular
Economy
and
accompanying
legislation,
more
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
must
be
done
to
ensure
that
the
plastics
that
are
used
do
not
contribute
needlessly
to
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
methane
emissions,
addressing
methane-intensive
petrochemicals
and
plastic
as
a
matter
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
of
priority.
Moreover,
plastics
degrade
into
methane
at
end-of-life
when
exposed
to
ambient
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
solar
radiation,
contributions
that
are
expected
to
increase
as
plastic
accumulates
in
the
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
environment, and for which measures should be advanced.[4]
 
 
For these reasons, we would like to see the following measures in the legislative act:
 
 
 
●
MRV and LDAR
 
The
legislative
act
should
include
petrochemicals
and
plastics
in
its
compulsory
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
measurement,
reporting
and
verification
(MRV)
regime
and
require
leakage
detection
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
and
repair
(LDAR)
at
all
sites
involved
in
petrochemical
and
plastic
production,
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
regardless
whether
used
as
energy
sources
and
feedstocks.
Moreover,
coverage
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
should
extend
across
the
oil
and
gas
supply
chain
including
imports—​
i.e.
upstream,
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
midstream,
downstream—and
should
include
biogas,
biomethane,
natural
gas
liquids
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
and naphtha.
 
 
 
 
Ref. Ares(2021)632071 - 26/01/2021
 
●
Ban on Routine Venting and Flaring
 
The
legislative
act
should
include
measures
to
ban
routine
venting
and
flaring
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
according
to
prevailing
and
emerging
best
practices
across
different
segments
of
the
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
supply
chain.
Moreover,
coverage
should
extend
across
the
oil
and
gas
supply
chain
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
including
imports—​
i.e.
upstream,
midstream,
downstream—and
should
include
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
biogas, biomethane, natural gas liquids and naphtha.
 
 
●
Mandatory Supply Chain Emissions Profile for Fossil Fuels entering EU market
 
The
EU
Commission
has
found
that
“​
most
of
the
fossil
fuels
consumed
in
the
EU
are
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
imported,
and
75-90%
of
the
methane
emissions
associated
with
these
fuels
are
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
emitted before reaching the EU’s borders​
”,[5] further noting that:
 
 
 
Methane
is
a
greenhouse
gas…
that
knows
no
borders.
When
emitted
in
one
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
country,
it
will
have
a
climate
and
air
quality
impact
in
others.
Moreover,
the
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
largest
share
of
energy-related
methane
emissions
associated
with
fossil
fuels
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
consumed
in
the
EU
are
emitted
outside
of
the
EU
borders,
making
international
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
action necessary.[6]
 
 
The
Commission
should
therefore
introduce
a
mandatory
supply
chain
emissions
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
profile
for
fossil
fuels
entering
the
EU
market.
This
should
include
fossil
gas
used
for
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
petrochemical
and
plastic
production.
The
development
of
an
emissions
profile
was
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
recently
proposed
by
the
International
Energy
Agency
(IEA)
in
its
report
​
Driving
Down
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Methane
Leaks
from
the
Oil
and
Gas
Industry:
A
Regulatory
Roadmap
and
Toolkit:
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Methane Leaks Regulatory Roadmap and Toolkit.​
[7]
 
 
●
Introduction of “Low-Leakage” Procurement Standards
 
In
addition
to
the
introduction
of
a
supply
chain
emissions
profile,
we
strongly
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
encourage
the
Commission
to
introduce
procurement
standards
aimed
at
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
demanding
a
“low-leakage”
supply
chain
as
a
basis
for
eligibility
to
bid
on,
or
as
a
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
performance
condition
in,
a
contract
as
proposed
by
the
IEA
in
its
report
​
Driving
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Down
Methane
Leaks
from
the
Oil
and
Gas
Industry:
A
Regulatory
Roadmap
and
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Toolkit: Methane Leaks Regulatory Roadmap and Toolkit.​
[8]
 
 
 
●
Mandatory Dual Global Warming Potential (GWP) Accounting for Methane
 
Methane
is
a
powerful
greenhouse
gas
that
has
a
very
significant
impact
on
global
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
warming,
particularly
over
the
next
almost
30
years
left
to
achieve
net
zero
in
2050.
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
However,
the
EU
and
its
Member
States
currently
use
only
the
100-year
timeframe
to
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
calculate
the
global
warming
potential
(GWP)
of
methane.
Moreover,
authorities
use
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
outdated
numbers
to
calculate
the
CO​
2​
-equivalents
of
methane,
which
leads
to
a
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
falsified
climate
impact
of
methane.
We
therefore
encourage
the
Commission
to
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
introduce
dual
GWP
accounting
for
methane
and
to
make
it
mandatory
for
all
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
European institutions to use the most accurate numbers as provided by the IPCC.[9]
 
Additionally,
we
ask
the
Commission
to
explore
options
to:
(i)
develop
detailed
phase-out
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
plans
for
fossil
fuel
infrastructure,
including
petrochemical
facilities;
and
(ii)
introduce
a
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Feedback on Inception Impact Assessment - ​
Methane Strategy - JAN 2021
 

 
moratorium
for
new
infrastructure,
including
petrochemical
facilities,
until
detailed
phase-out
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
plans are being delivered.

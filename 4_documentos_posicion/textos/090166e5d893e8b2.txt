The Austrian Federal Economic Chamber (WKÖ) welcomes the initiative of the European 
Commission (EC) to promote the reduction of methane emissions throughout the EU. In 
Austria, methane (CH4) is the second most relevant greenhouse gas with a share of 8.0 % in 
2017. At the same time, methane emissions were reduced by 36.3 % between 1990 and 
2017 (source: Umweltbundesamt: Klimaschutzbericht 2019).  
The EU accounts for approximately 5 % of global methane emissions. Since 1990, methane 
emissions have steadily decreased, with stagnating emissions in the energy sector and de-
creasing emissions in waste management and agriculture. According to the IPCC 5th Assess-
ment Report, the CO2 equivalent for methane over a 100-year time horizon is 28, meaning 
that within the first 100 years of release, one kilogram of methane contributes 28 times as 
much to the greenhouse effect compared to one kilogram of CO2. In terms of 20 years, the 
Global Warming Potential factor is 84 when converted to CO2. In this context, we take a 
positive view on the EC’s efforts to set up platforms and cooperation at the international 
level, which should lead to a reduction of methane emissions worldwide. 
In a nutshell: 
• It is crucial that the focus in the development of further legislation is on the main emit-
ters where relevant reductions can be achieved. So-called “big emitters” or “super 
emitters” should therefore be in the focus. 
• We strongly support the efforts to establish an international monitoring center for me-
thane emissions to detect and address global super-emitters. 
• We welcome the focus on improving the measurement, reporting and verification (MRV) 
systems prior to developing more rigorous policy measures. There is a need for an inter-
national MRV system that is clearly comparable while avoiding excessive technical and 
bureaucratic effort. 
• Biogas should play an important role, being beneficial for all. On the one hand methane 
emissions from waste and agriculture are minimized and on the other hand fossil gas can 
be replaced by renewable gas. 
• Further voluntary initiatives by the industry should be pursued. OGMP (Oil and Gas Me-
thane Partnership) will gain particular importance in the future.  
• Methane mitigation tools should, above all, be flexible. Additional burden and costs for 
consumers, especially energy-intensive industry, must be avoided. 
 
The energy industry is already contributing significantly to methane emission reduc-
tions 
It is a key strategic goal of the gas and petroleum industry to reduce emissions resulting 
from the exploration, production and processing of crude oil and natural gas. The European 
gas infrastructure is a highly complex construct with many different components. In Aus-
tria, strong effort was made to improve this infrastructure both in terms of safety and 
emission reduction. In addition to extensive renewals, such as replacing old pipelines with 
modern insulated steel or plastic pipes, work has also been and continues to be carried out 
on the further development of working procedures. An Austrian legislative act (GWG 2011, 
BGBl. I No. 107/2011-§ 7 (1) 53) determines the periodically inspections of pipelines and 
systems to detect even the smallest leaks in the parts-per-million range. Further, technical 
rules (continuously developed) provide detailed specifications for the detection and repair 
of leaks in this area for more than two decades. As a result, the Austrian gas infrastructure 
is now one of the most modern and densest in the world. This shows that the respective 
initial situation (condition of the infrastructure, maintenance program and working proce-
dures used) of individual member states must be considered in each case.

Principle aspects of the Austrian Federal Economic Chamber 
• The EU is responsible for about 5 % of global methane emissions. For this reason, we 
strongly support the efforts to establish an international monitoring center for methane 
emissions to detect and address global super-emitters. Many of our member companies 
compete on a global level and under no circumstances should EU member states be sub-
ject to further regulations that would weaken the competitiveness of our companies on 
the international market. In this context, we would once again like to emphasize that 
the economy needs security in planning and therefore a stable framework. 
• We welcome the initial focus on improving the measurement, reporting and verifica-
tion (MRV) systems prior to developing more rigorous policy measures.  
A well-structured, fit-for-purpose MRV system will be the centerpiece of more accurate 
detection and quantification of methane emissions throughout the gas value chain and 
will allow better evaluation regarding the results of existing reduction measures. Inno-
vation, development, improvement, and implementation of fit-for-purpose and properly 
targeted technologies and practices to improve MRV and mitigate emissions must be 
forced, consequently. 
It must also be considered that many industrial companies are in international competi-
tion: On the one hand, there is a need for an international MRV system that is clearly 
comparable. A common understanding or classification of the infrastructure compo-
nents with corresponding definitions must be established in advance. There are cur-
rently very significant differences and discrepancies between the EU member states in 
this respect. On the other hand, excessive technical and bureaucratic effort must be 
avoided. Our suggestion is to focus on cost-efficient emission reductions and synergies 
in the light of the economic potential in international competition as well as the pol-
luter-pays principle, and to focus on the emission sources already identified by the EU 
Commission. At the EU level, intensive work is being done to develop a methane emis-
sion glossary. The Austrian Federal Economic Chamber supports the activities of the EU 
associations (e.g. IOGP). 
• It is crucial that the focus in the development of further legislation is on the main 
emitters where relevant reductions can be achieved. The so-called “big emitters” or 
“super emitters” should therefore be in the focus. Hence, the first step is to analyze 
where the easiest and biggest improvements can be made and then start to implement 
measures step by step in accordance with industry best practices and the best available 
technologies. Only by knowing the major sources of potential methane emissions pre-
ventive measures can be implemented to avoid such emissions in new facilities.  
• It is also very important that no global measures are adopted, e.g. a general reduction 
of methane emissions by a fixed percentage without taking into account the existing 
conditions  
• Austria’s gas infrastructure is one of the most modern and efficient in the world. In our 
opinion, a general increase in the technical requirements should be well balanced. 
Technical feasibility and economic realizability must be considered. 
• Biogas can play an important role. We support the efforts made by the gas industry to 
gradually replace fossil gas with green gas (biogas and hydrogen). The production of bio-
gas can be beneficial for all, as on the one hand methane emissions from waste and ag-
riculture are minimized and on the other hand fossil gas can be replaced by renewable 
gas. For this purpose, several studies on feasibility have already been made on behalf of 
the Austrian gas industry. At the same time, the technical rules related to issues of

green gas feed-in, e.g. determination of the calorific value, are continuously being 
adapted.  
Furthermore, decarbonization potentials from the hydrogen content (H2) in methane 
(CH4), as well as the possibility of using biogas and the functionality of the methane grid 
as energy storage via power-to-X technologies are being examined. 
• A positive aspect is that further voluntary initiatives by the industry will be pursued. 
OGMP (Oil and Gas Methane Partnership) will gain particular importance in the future. 
The Austrian Federal Economic Chamber welcomes the use of this existing framework 
and will further develop legislative steps based on this initiative. 
• Methane mitigation tools should, above all, be flexible. This will allow the use of 
technologies that deliver the highest emission reductions at the lowest cost and in the 
shortest timeframe possible. 
• Additional burden and costs for consumers, especially energy-intensive industry, 
should be avoided. For example, gas consumers should generally be excluded from the 
assessment. In addition, the costs of methane use, including verification obligations, 
should be minimized in a sustainable and predictable way, especially for the larger con-
sumers. 
• In the light of the current COVID-19 pandemic and the resulting economic crisis, it is 
necessary to promote positive economic incentives and innovative technologies. We 
welcome an incentive system in the form of funding for research and development to 
further reduce methane emissions.

COMMENTS TO THE INCEPTION 
Anigas supports the development of a legislative proposal aimed to improve 
the availability and accuracy of information on the specific sources of 
methane emissions associated with energy consumed in the EU and to put 
in place EU level cost-effective and efficient regulation to reduce methane 
emissions across different segments of the energy supply chain. 
The management and reduction of methane emissions is among the gas 
industry’s priorities to contribute to the EU’s climate-neutrality objective and 
to ensure that gas plays its part to help achieve this goal.   
About improving information, Anigas supports compulsory measurement, 
reporting, and verification (MRV) for all energy -related methane emissions 
at company -level, building on the methodology of the existing global 
voluntary initiative called the Oil and Gas Methane Partnership, including 
upstream, midstream and downstream -OGMP 2.0 (policy option 2.b). The 
MRV framework should be extended as well to the coal sector. 
The commitment of the gas industry over the past years has certainly led to 
significant reductions in methane emissions and to methane emission 
values significantly lower than in the past. Security and safety of the grid are 
core of the operations: upstream and downstream companies have always 
been committed to limiting methane emissions in their networks performing 
leak detection and repair activities (LDAR), regular inspection of their grids 
and immediate intervention in case of incidents  in line with the operators’ 
public service mission and national technical regulation. 
A well-structured, fit for purpose MRV system of methane emissions is a 
crucial element in policy frameworks that aim to reduce methane emissions 
along the energy chains, including those beyond the EU’s borders.  
We believe that the exact understanding of the methane emissions from 
energy sector and across the energy chain will allow the effective 
development and monitoring of policies to mitigate the methane emissions.  
It should be taken into account that technology to detect and measure 
methane emissions is in development stage and available sets of data on 
emissions are not always consistent and homogeneous. 
Current data on methane emissions are typically reliant on so-called bottom-
up inventories which provide annual total emissions by source sector.  But 
inventory estimations are based on “old” emission factors  and may be 
inaccurate (they are based on a relatively small sample size)  and not 
consider technical innovations. 
The inventory approach uses a factor “emissions per mile”, which could lead 
to not accurate information about the presence of few large leaks 
constituting most of the total emissions or the distribution of methane 
emission across gas networks.

Anigas is promoting the development and implementation of the MRV 
framework devised by  OGMP to companies in the gas midstream and 
downstream via OGMP 2.0, considering it the best existing vehicle for 
improving MRV capability in the energy sector. We believe that it’s needed 
more clarity on DSOs’ responsibilities in methane emissions to cons ider 
whether their activities should be covered by sustainability aspects related 
to reducing methane emissions. This taking into account even the specific 
national nature and construct of DSOs, which may be a barrier for DSOs 
from engaging directly with global initiatives.  Company should be 
responsible for the methane emissions from the assets under their control. 
Anigas also welcomes the Commission’s intention to deliver a legislative 
proposal on an obligation to improve detection and repair of leaks on all gas 
infrastructure, as well as any other production, transport or use of gas, 
including as a feedstock.  LDAR obligations should be set according to 
prevailing and emerging best practices, including from industry, across 
different segments of the supply chain.  
We believe that a clear pathway and a supportive regulatory framework can 
help address the issue of methane emissions more comprehensively across 
Europe. A cost-effective regulation should focus on highest emission 
sources along the full supply chains of gas, oil and coal.
  
Anigas support the development of a legislative proposal s etting a 
harmonized MRV and LDAR framework across EU, as well as other 
methane regulatory requirements and incentives. Infrastructures operators 
should fulfil future obligations by reporting to their competent authorities, 
based on a common reporting template. Additional administrative and 
technical burdens (such as double reporting) on the companies should be 
avoided. 
 
Regarding regulated infrastructure s, cost efficiently incurred for MRV 
activities should be recognised and addressed within the scope of activity 
regulated by the National Regulatory Authorities (NRAs), in a homogeneous 
way across EU member states. Cost recovery should avoid any distortions 
and should be done via tariffs applied to exit/delivery points towards final 
consumers who are the final beneficiaries of decarbonisation investments. 
 
More specifically, in order to ensure effectiveness we endorse the 
Commission’s proposal to urge NRAs to include all investments on LDAR 
and methane reduction programs as allowed costs for regulated gas DSOs 
and other regulated infrastructure operators . We also believe that specific 
financing opportunities to cover the costs associated with the greater 
complexity of operations, deriving from the application of best practices, 
would complement and strengthen the fight against methane emissions.

Hence we propose the inclusion of the following instruments in the toolbox 
for pursing the desired reductions: 
 Establish and promote the application of a European MRV system to 
effectively detect and measure methane emissions, in line with the 
best global experiences such as OGMP 2.0; 
 Include methane emissions reduction within the scope of regulated 
activity, to ensure that all costs related to mitigation measures are 
included and incentivised accordingly by NRAs. 
Mitigation measures should embed flexibility to implement tools and 
available technologies: this would allow to achieve the highest emissions 
reduction at the lowest cost and in the shortest time.  
We believe that a supportive regulation will promot e innovation, 
development and implementation of new technologies and practi ces to 
reduce methane emissions: an increased development and injection of 
renewable and decarbonised gas in the distribution networks would ensure 
DSO a tool for reducing methane emissions in parallel to provide European 
end-users with the energy they require.

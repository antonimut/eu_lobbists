Proposal for a legislative act to reduce methane emissions in the oil, gas 
and coal sectors – ENGIE’s contribution 
 
 
ENGIE wants to underline first that , as stated in the “EU strategy to reduce methane emissions ” dated 
14.10.2020, if we consider only anthropogenic methane emissions at the EU level : 
• 53% of methane emissions is due to agriculture ; 
• 26% of methane emissions is due to waste ; 
• 19% of methane emissions is due to energy , oil and gas  representing then 10% only of EU 
anthropogenic methane emissions. 
As far as the 79% of methane emissions due to agriculture and waste are considered, the gas sector 
is one of the major means of mitigation. 
Indeed, the production of biomethane, in addition to generating additional revenue streams for farmers, 
acts on the two key aspects of climate change  : reducing  methane and carbon emissions and 
increasing carbon storage.  
GHG emissions reduction induced by the production of biomethane and especially the avoidance of 
methane emissions due to natural fermentation, in the atmosphere, shall adequately be taken into 
account. 
The biogas sector differs from the natural gas industry and it’s crucial to avoid the legislation becoming 
a barrier to implement biomethane plants. This is why ENGIE is hig hly committed to encouraging 
the development on a more industrial basis and will foster the adaptation of MRV+LDAR to biomethane 
plants, including cost-wise and operations-wise, in order to reach maturity and convergence in 5-10 years.  
 
As far as  the 10 % of methane emissions due to the oil and gas industry  are considered  ENGIE 
welcomes the ambition of the European Commission to address methane emissions generated by 
the gas industry. 
 
• For the gas mid-and downstream segments : 
 
In France : 
o Several decades long experience on Leak Detection And Repair  (LDAR) for safety 
have evolved to include methane emissions reduction efforts ; 
o The use of Gas Booster systems (mobile gas recompression systems) saves around 90% 
of methane emissions each year ; 
o Dedicated investment programs  such as the program to renew distribution network ’s 
pipelines have been launched. 
 
As a result, methane emissions of the main gas infrastructures in France have been reduced by 
15% since 2016. 
In November 2020, GRTgaz , GRDF, Storengy, Elengy and Teréga (French TSO not part of ENGIE’s 
group) have joined the Oil & Gas Methane Partnership (OGMP) 2.0. They target an overall 45% 
reduction in methane emissions from infrastructure between 2016 and 2025 .

Costs incurred by regulated operators for MRV,  LDAR, avoiding routine venting and flaring, and 
dedicated investment programs  shall be i ncorporated into the tariff  (IEA’s Methane Tracker 
indication of 40% emissions reduction at no net cost is the result of the comparison between costs and

• For the gas upstream sector : 
 
o ENGIE has launched concrete internal projects with its suppliers in order to address 
this issue but at this stage option 3 is not mature enough because foreign suppliers 
represent a too heterogeneous set ; 
o It is a constructive lever to use competition in the supply markets to pus h producers 
into voluntary approaches, on which a regulatory approach can be built up further . 
It is essential to support development of qualification frameworks that can help implement 
and test building blocks for a MSI. 
 
• For the entire gas supply chain : 
 
o ENGIE considers that the intra-EU supply chain is mature enoug h and is therefore 
in favor of option 2b (where coal should be added) as a first step, the ambition being 
to reach option 2c between 5 and 10 years ; 
o MRV (Measurement,  Reporting and Verification) is crucial as it is a guarantee of 
transparency and homogeneity  but it is worth mentioning that methane emissions 
measurement technologies have large measurement uncertainties ; 
o MRV and L DAR can ’t be seen independently and legislation shou ld be more goal 
oriented than prescriptive and allow flexibility to focus resources on activities that ensure 
the highest methane emissions reductions.

Gasunie Contribution to the European Commission Methane Emissions Roadmap 
 
Gasunie is an European energy infrastructure company. We transport natural gas and green gas with 
Gasunie Transport Services B.V. (GTS) in the Netherlands and Gasunie Deutschland in Germany. We offer 
a broad range of other energy services including LNG regasification, bunkering services and gas storage 
services. Gasunie is involved in numerous hydrogen, heat, biogas and CCUS projects in Germany and the 
Netherlands. For more information, go to www.gasunie.nl.  
 
Gasunie welcomes the work done so far on preventing methane emissions and the options proposed, and 
wants to work towards a manageable framework which will reduce methane emissions. The recent IEA 
report1 on methane emissions highlights the importance of reducing emissions of this greenhouse gas and 
to avoid a rebound in emissions as natural gas operations pick up again in 2021.  
 
In 2019 Gasunie signed the Methane Guiding Principles, which is a voluntary international partnership 
between companies in the gas supply chain. This agreement focuses on reducing methane emissions in the 
supply chain and demonstrates our commitment to reducing our emissions wherever this is possible. We 
have also signed up to the Oil and Gas Methane Partnership (OGMP) methodology, which provides a 
credible mechanism to systematically and responsibly address companies’ methane emissions, and to 
demonstrate this systematic approach and its results to stakeholders.  
 
Our methane emissions have decreased steadily over the years, both in the Netherlands and in Germany, 
from 7.8 ktonnes in 2015 to 4.6 ktonnes in 2019. In general, this reduction is due to the fact that we 
actively work to vent less natural gas and thanks to the leak detection and repair (LDAR) programme that 
we have carried out for several years. We aim to be a front-runner on reducing our network related 
methane emissions. Therefore we have set a target of a maximum of 50 ktonnes CO2 equivalents 
(approximately 2 ktonnes of methane) by 2030.2 
 
Regarding the options to improve the availability and accuracy of information, option 3 has the added 
benefit of including emissions in the entire natural gas value chain. This allows for setting-up a methane 
supply index since all the emissions are known in the value chain. We support the translation of the OGMP 
framework into EU legislation to be applicable to the full energy supply chains (from upstream to 
downstream, excluding utilisation). 
 
For the mitigation options we reiterate the importance of LDAR and routine venting and flaring mitigation 
with regards to the operational conditions (operational safety, security of supply).

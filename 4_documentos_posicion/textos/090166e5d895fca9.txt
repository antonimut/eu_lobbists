Eurogas is a European gas industry association representing 48 companies and associations engaged in gas wholesale, retail and distribution in Europe. 
Feedback on the Inception Impact Assessment on Methane legislation 
Eurogas would like to welcome the Inception Impact assessment pre-empting the drafting of upcoming 
methane related legislation. We welcome the focus, already expressed in the Methane Strategy, on 
Measurement, Reporting and Verification (MRV), Lea k Detection and Repair (LDAR) across the value 
chain, we support international initiatives and a more global approach on methane , while we note 
possible discussions related to targets and the methane supply index. 
 We believe that a clear pathway and a supportive regulatory framework can help address the issue of 
methane emissions more comprehensively across Europe . Eurogas and its members support a 
decarbonisation pathway towards 2050 which provides cost -effective solutions to reduce GHG 
emissions across sectors. We recall the Methane Strategy’s focus on waste, agriculture and energy -
related methane emissions and believe synergy between various sectors would be beneficial to reach 
the EU’s climate and energy objectives. 
As a signatory of the Methane Guiding Principles, an active participant to the development of the 
OGMP 2.0 reporting framework, and with members active in OGCI and the OGMP 2.0, Eurogas will 
happily provide valuable feedback in the next stages of the stakeholder consultation process. 
Measurement, reporting and verification and Leak Detection and Repair 
The IIA cor rectly underlines that  there is  no existing methane related legislation which is up for 
revision. We therefore note the proposal to explore new legislation  or to build on the Industrial 
emissions directive and the pollutant register with further discussion required internally on the latter 
options. We recall that the E ffort Sharing Regulation covers methane emissions today and could 
therefore be exploite d further to help address the issue , along with existing legislation which could 
serve to develop flanking policies such as renewable gas development that helps avoid methane 
emissions in sectors such as the agricultural sector and waste.   
We wish to unde rline that LDAR and MRV, as well as rules on venting and flaring, have historically 
existed to ensure safety can be guaranteed for end users and the energy system; this will remain the 
priority in the future. The sustainability aspect of GHG emission reduction need s to be further 
addressed and best practice rollout should be further accelerated by building on existing regulation 
and developing new EU legislation where necessary.  
The increased ambition to 5 5% GHG emission reduction by 2030 put forward by the Commissi on 
means that additional commitment is needed to address all greenhouse gases rather than just CO2. 
Higher commitments mean more investment requirements and to this end cost -efficient solutions 
should be pursued where ver feasible, as outlined by the IIA. Emphasis on cost -effectiveness across 
sectors would be welcome in the proposed way forward, with systematic consideration of positive 
externalities. The idea of pursuing all mitigation options outlined by the IEA, could help bridge this gap, 
though the “no-net cost” idea should be more thoroughly assessed and clarified. Ensuring more readily 
available and transparent data that cover the relevant parts of the value chain is important, while 
recognising divergence between regulated entities and market actors, in the way that methane 
emission reduction can be dealt with. A close dialogue with regulators will be beneficial, as outlined in 
ACER’s Bridge beyond 2025 and the Commission’ methane strategy.

With regards to the policy proposals outlined in the IIA, we believe that methane emission reduction 
should be handled in a holistic way along the entire value chain, taking stock of national and local 
conditions. At this stage we would welcome an impact assessment of the proposed policy optio ns in 
order to provide a more informed opinion. 
The way forward on LDAR, looking at best practices and allowing for flexible approaches across 
countries and the value chain, to cater for local conditions is welcome, it will help ensure decisive and 
tailored action across Europe. Regarding MRV, we believe the work of the OGMP 2.0 may serve as an 
adequate starting point to develop a robust, transparent reporting framework. 
International perspective 
The IIA rightfully notes that methane emissions as other GHG gases, knows no borders . EU as a net 
importer of energy, recognises the fact that between 75-90% of methane emissions occur outside EU 
borders, which reinforces the scope for a coordinated global approach. We wish to recall that the gas 
industry has increasingly come together over the past decade, to address the issue through voluntary 
initiatives such as the OGCI, the MGP and the OGMP  2.0, in addition to the existing regulation at 
national level related to safety but also increasingly, to sustainability.  As with LDAR and MRV related 
measures, the cost-benefit should be thoroughly assessed to ensure that most relevant measures are 
rolled out according to parts of the value chain and local conditions. A toolbox approach providing 
several regulatory options can help cater for this. 
Way forward 
While we believe in the importance of a coordinated approach, and while we note the Commission’s 
potential assessment of a target looking at imports or only certain parts of the value chain, we believe 
an EU target on  gas itself, would provide a better way of ensuring a cost -efficient outcome for GHG 
reduction on gas consumed in the EU. Indeed, by putting a target on renewable and decarbonised gas 
in 2030, facilitating both the development of biomethane  (Eurogas recommends a 11% target for all 
renewable gasses  including biomethane and re newable hydrogen ) which helps avoid methane 
emissions in the waste and agricultural sector, along with GHG emission reduction objective (Eurogas 
recommends a 20% GHG emission reduction target for gas consumed in the EU), can help adequately 
and decisively reduce GHG emissions, including methane, towards 2030 and beyond. Furthermore this 
approach would ensure the most cost -efficient solution across EU MS whilst taking local conditions 
into account.  
We wish to underline that Eurogas and its exp erts remain available for any further questions or 
clarification and that we look forward to providing feedback to the final report.

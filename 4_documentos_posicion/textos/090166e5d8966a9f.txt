MiQ, a not-for-profit partnership between the Rocky Mountain Institute and SYSTEMIQ, welcomes the European 
Commission’s initiative on a legislative act to reduce methane emissions, specifically in the energy sector. The EU’s 
dependence on natural gas imports underlines the urgent necessity to approach this topic on an international level. 
We see the new OGMP framework as a strong instrument to improve MRV practices, thus, to corroborate our 
understanding of where methane emissions are occurring and can be abated most efficiently, and to create a level 
playing field for actors across the supply chain. However, publicly reporting methane emission figures on a high level 
only, namely on country and company level will not deliver the granular information needed to ensure full 
transparency for the European energy supply portfolio. 
We therefore advocate the additional need for initially voluntary initiatives like MiQ, which are able to reinforce and 
complement regulatory action, and could provide a pathway for inclusion in future iterations of regulation based on 
lessons learnt. 
We propose a certification system (MiQ) which, adopted at scale, will create a differentiated market for natural gas 
for buyers and sellers. This system will create the pricing signals for the sector to invest in abatement technology 
and practices enabling the significant methane reductions necessary to support global climate targets. This 
approach has been proven highly efficient in the recent history of energy markets: the oil refinery industry invested 
globally in desulphurisation capacity on the basis of the pricing signals in forward oil markets due to the lowering of 
sulphur standards to 10 ppm (early 2000, reference to Directive 2009/30/EC) or more recently with the IMO 2020 
regulations for sulphur in marine shipping. 
One key advantage of a globally applicable certification system is that it enables all oil and gas producers to be 
assessed according to the same universal and independently audited standard. This means there is an international 
level playing field, in which operators’ gas production is awarded a grade based on the efficacy of their methane 
abatement mechanisms according to the same rules regardless of geographical location. This system will allow 
European utilities, the EU and its member states to compare gas imported from a variety of regions, including long 
pipeline systems or LNG imports, based on its methane emissions. Equally it will allow other gas consuming 
countries, for example Japan, South-Korea or the US to assess the methane emissions of their gas imports. At MiQ 
we see a strong demand in those countries for transparency about methane emissions. An independent standard 
will also enable climate-conscious producers, regulators, and buyers to credibly demonstrate methane abatement to 
consumers and investors.  
In a regulatory framework, there are many ways to integrate and promote such a certification framework. For 
example, a certain minimum certification grade (decreasing over time) could serve as the lowest acceptable bar for 
performance of production, or equally a certification could be the bar for entry into the internal EU gas market like 
any product standard. One can also consider linking methane certification and its grades to other schemes like ETS 
or taxation/incentivisation, based potentially upon the examples of Canada or Norway.  
Certification is compatible with existing and future regulatory frameworks, as well as international best practices. The 
inclusion of the initially voluntary methane emissions certification into a form of regulatory framework would further 
increase the price drivers of the differentiated gas market. In addition, a more granular certification system driven by 
market forces will increase global transparency and can easily provide the data needed for a Methane Supply Index. 
Differentiated natural gas will be imported at a limited number of entry points into the internal EU gas market and 
certification allows to closely monitor origin and methane emission performance of each batch of natural gas 
supplied to the EU.  
While the certification system will be rolled out over time and initially on a voluntary basis, it will increase 
transparency and provide credibility on day one, creating an opportunity for operators to differentiate themselves in 
the market relative to their peers. This would circumvent the current need for a comprehensive global emission 
inventory as prerequisite for well assessed regulatory action and would solve the by now infamous chicken-and-egg-
problem with a clear solution.

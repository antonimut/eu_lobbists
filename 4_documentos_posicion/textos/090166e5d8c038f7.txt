Further comments on question 56: 
 
We see no need for a definition as to which assets are to be considered liquid or not. Such 
an assessment can change over time and should therefore be made by the fund manager, 
taking into account the existin g circumstances (e. g. market situation and investors). This 
flexibility is necessary in order to be able to determine and manage the liquidity profile of the 
fund’s assets on an individual basis - and in particular taking into account the investor profile, 
e.g. considering likely future redemption quotas.  
 
We also see no need to further extend ESMA's powers of action, in particular to set out more 
specific provisions on ESMA's coordination role in market stress situations. The AIFMD 
already provides for a general coordination role for ESMA which is seen to be sufficient and 
moreover ESMA is equipped with significant and effective powers through the AIFMD to 
ensure effective implementation of the objectives associated with the AIFMD. 
 
GDV strongly believes that ESMA's primary responsibility is to ensure a convergent 
application of EU regulatory requirements. We therefore consider it important that ESMA 
continues its role in coordinating the achievement of a common understanding of AIFMD 
rules and concepts am ong NCAs and the industry. ESMA already fulfils this through its 
guidelines, interpretative communications or Q&As. We do not consider it necessary to 
extend the powers to act beyond this in order to ensure a consistent implementation of the 
AIFMD.  
 
In our opinion it is of utmost importance that National Supervisory Authorities (NCAs) remain 
responsible for the direct implementation of specific measures, such as the power to require 
the suspension of redemptions when necessary in the interest of unit-holders or the public.  
 
Further comments on question 101: 
 
The German insurance industry sees no need to merge the UCITS and AIFM regulatory 
frameworks into a single EU rulebook.  
 
As stated above, from view of the German insurance industry as the largest institutional 
investor in AIF in Germany, the AIFMD is a su ccessfully functioning regulatory framework for 
the AIF market. The regulatory architecture of the AIFMD is considered robust and effective 
and has proven its worth since its adoption and national implementation. 
 
The interaction between fund manager, depo sitary and professional investor works well. In 
particular, the regulation of fund managers under the AIFMD – which are now EU -wide

subject to ongoing regulation and supervision on a comparable level – has proven to be an 
effective measure to improve investor protection and to fulfill investors’ needs.  
 
The EU Commission itself in its June 2020 report to the European Parliament and the 
Council assessing the AIFMD came to the conclusion, that the AIFMD regulation serves to 
protect investor interests and improves investor confidence in financial markets. This is in our 
view a very important aspect, since long -term investors, like insurers first and foremost 
search and need legal certainty regarding the regulatory requirements for their fund 
investments. The revision of the well -functioning AIFMD should there fore not result in any 
unnecessary changes from the investor's point of view. However, this would possibly be the 
case in the event of a merger of the quite different regulations for UCITS and AIFM.  
 
The January 2019 KPMG report on the functioning of the AIFMD, prepared on behalf of the 
Commission, also confirms that the AIFMD has significantly contributed to the creation of a 
single market for AIFs by establishing a harmonized and stringent regulatory and supervisory 
framework for AIFMs. It also finds tha t most of the provisions of the AIFMD have efficiently 
and effectively achieved their intended objectives. The KPMG report identifies only a small 
number of areas that require further in-depth analysis. 
 
ESMA, in its letter on the AIFMD review of 19 August  2020, does propose greater 
harmonization of the two sets of rules, pointing out that the AIFMD provisions are in some 
respects more granular and specific than comparable provisions in the UCITS Directive. 
However, to our knowledge, a merger is not propose d and, in our view, would not be 
necessary to meet these requirements. 
 
All these findings and assessments on the AIFMD lead to the conclusion that a 
comprehensive amendment of the AIFMD is not necessary and that both regulations should 
not be merged into a single EU rulebook.

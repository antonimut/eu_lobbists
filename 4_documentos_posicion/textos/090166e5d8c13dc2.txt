EXECUTIVE SUMMARY 
In ACEA’s vie w, the three issues of concern illustrated by the Commission in both the Inception 
Impact Assessment and the current public consultation are generally valid and require EU action 
under the revision of the ITS Directive.  
Firstly, attention should be given to the  lack of interoperability and cross -border continuity of 
applications, systems and services. Whereas ITS services over the network (long-range) have long 
been successfully deployed and made interoperable across brands , the potential of short -range 
(V2I, V2V) has emerged as an efficient solution for offering across the board (vehicles and road-side 
infrastructure) interoperable safety services. Challenges related to the implementation of these 
technologies are currently being solved in European standard setting bodies.  Interoperability and 
cross-border continuity can be an issue in the area of ITS.  
Secondly, the lack of concertation a nd effective cooperation/monitoring among ITS  stakeholders 
needs to be addressed. Major problems derive from the different pace and governance levels in ITS 
with fragmented member states involvement,  ITS road -side infrastructure roll out and  EU 
coordination and synchronisation of national access points (NAPs). Measures should focus on the 
better coordination of infrastructure roll-out, integration of NAPs and an improved coordination of 
ITS services deployment. ACEA welcomes the public support action on the federation of the NAPs.  
Thirdly, as f ar as  the availability and sharing of data supporting the ITS services  is concerned, it 
should be left to the market players (data providers) to determine what data are best suited for 
given use cases and events. 
Alongside these aspects, additional unresolved issues have been indicated by ACEA, as priorities to 
be tackled within the future revision of the Directive. In order to further develop the EU ITS market 
and framework, we recommend the Commission to:  
• carry out a critical reflection concerning the non-adoption of the C-ITS delegated act: given 
the time and resources already spent on this issue, the policy makers are now well aware of 
the issues at stake. Our industry believes in ITS deployment and an accompanying legal 
framework with no mandates on technology choices; 
• pursue the principle of technology neutrality, whereby communication technology choices 
are left to the market as long as  interoperability, scalability and harmoni sation for ITS 
related services is ensured (e.g. impact of switch-off 2G/3G for eCall, C-ITS communication 
technologies); 
• synchronise EU work and priorities for the ITS Directive revision with the UN-ECE Road Map 
on ITS , which outlines 20 global actions to promote the de ployment of ITS  and which

revision is currently under discussion at WP.29 IWG on ITS1; 
• involve the automobile industry stakeholders in a timel ier and structured manner, e.g.  in 
the negotiation process of delegated acts, but also since  vehicles are a fundamental 
component of the multimodal connected and automated mobility ecosystem; 
• take a b alanced approach between regulatory and non -regulatory measures, where non-
regulatory action on safety and security in the form of consistent application, common 
interpretation, synchronisation between the Commission, Member States, and with the 
automotive industry is accompanied by a minimum level of standardisation in the field of (i) 
cybersecurity and (ii) security of C-ITS communication; 
• provide clarity in the f ield of data protection and privacy, as it is currently unclear how 
compliance with the GDPR and ePrivacy legislation can be ensured for data processing in 
the context of C-ITS; 
• review the currently (very) broad scope of the powers for delegated regulations (current 
powers relate to specifications necessary to ensure the compatibility, interoperability and 
continuity for the deployment and operational use of ITS for the priority actions); 
• take a holistic approach when revising this Directive and o ther legislation, e.g. revision of 
Regulation (EU) No 1315/2013 on Union guidelines for the development of the trans -
European transport (TEN-T) network, revision of Directive 2014/94/EU on the deployment 
of alternative fuel infrastructure (AFID), expected updates to the Type Approval framework 
and implementation of the General Safety Regulation for automated driving; 
• re-assess some of the definitions in the ITS directive  in a future proof fashion  (e.g. “ITS 
service provider” which is a circular def inition) and work towards a common definition for 
ITS, in line with Action 1 of the UN-ECE Road Map on ITS (as referred to above); 
• address the clear discontinuity between public and private transports at EU rather than at 
local/regional level 
 
In all these areas, we support that further EU action under the ITS directive is taken so to achieve 
societal sustainable development goals, ensure harmonisation and synchronisation at EU level with 
focus on interoperability & continuity, while allowing additional ser vices in cities tailored to local 
needs and policies. 
TEN-T is also a suitable framework for EU intervention, but it should be broadened. More stimulus 
should be given to accelerate the national ITS plans in a manner that ensures uniformity and 
coherence among the various Member States.  
We finally encourage Commission to define what "EU action" stands for, as currently it is unclear

whether it refers only to policy/regulatory aspects or if it includes also advanced R&I.  
In regard to the Priority Areas, the ITS Directive in its current form has specific relevance for vehicle 
manufacturers in the 4 original Priority Areas . ACEA shares Commission’s view that  also newer 
themes such as  connected and  automated mobility, mobility platforms such as MaaS, 
multimodality and the urgently needed advanced deployment of alternative fuels infrastructure 
(AFID) could benefit from further action under the ITS Directive.  For achieving future -oriented 
legislation, Commission may consider updating the 4 original Priority Area s with the three main 
themes identified (connected and automated mobility, mobility platforms, enhanced traffic

Overall, we support the policy options/measures put forward by the Commission in this public

Finally, as far as the impact of the revision is concerned , we believe that further EU actions would 
lead to an increased  collaboration, cooperation and harmonization between  ITS stakeholders in 
order to achieve interoperability and continuity of ITS services across the member  states. Support 
to road infrastructure deployments (and through this availability of high quality ITS  data) would 
boost the uptake of ITS implementation across member states, and other regions within its sphere 
of influence. Overall, a harmonised ITS deployment will result in a high societal impact on European 
roads in terms of safety, traffic efficiency, and comfort for drivers,  passengers, and users of public 
transport.

DIRECTIVE 
2.1 Main problems/issues identified by the commission (ref. 
to Question 11) 
ACEA recognises that the three main issues identified by the Commission in the Inception Impact 
Assessment2  following the ex-post evaluation of the directive are relevant and should be tackled in 
the future revision of the ITS Directive. In addition, a variety of other unresolved issues will deserve 
further attention in the revision . The following are the  main focus items that Commission should 
take into high consideration: 
Interoperability and cross-border continuity 
As also identified by the Commission in its Inception Impact Assessment, vehicle manufacturers 
consider the l ack of interoperability and cross -border continuity of applications, systems  and 
services as one major barrier to the full deployment of ITS across Europe.  
In regard to  problems related to interoperability,  the benefit of hybrid communication 
complementary to the information from on -board sensors should be considered in the review,  as 
short- and long-range communication complement each other and solve potential issues.  In cases 
where the long -range services do not require low latency to provide a benefit , this type of

connectivity should be encouraged. For low and mid-range latency services and functional safety, 
such as crash avoidance and other safety applications , the only option is to use direct 
communication due to requirements on high data quality and low latency. Currently there is no 
mechanism to define which application/service uses which communica tion technology, leading to 
potential interoperability problems, if different technologies are used for the same 
application/service across the group of stakeholders (OEMs, Infrastructure, Emergency Vehicles 
etc.). 
The lack of cross-border continuity is an issue particularly in the area of C-ITS road infrastructure: a 
concrete example is that some member states are deploying production level infrastructure, 
whereas other member states are still in pilot phases – which cannot be relied upon in a production 
vehicle.  
Concertation with industry and other stakeholders 
As also underlined in the paragraph below (“Governance”), ACEA calls for  a timely and early 
involvement of ACEA and its members in the set -up of possible future delegated acts. The Priority 
Areas in the ITS Directive seem still relevant but call for an intense dialogue with all industry actors 
including vehicle manufacturers, physical and digital infrastructure providers, National Access 
Points of Member States, etc. From our experience and ongoing discussions with respect to Spec C 
(SRTI), due consideration needs to be given to the situation of each Member State with its own 
internal systems and architectures.  
Vehicle manufacturers participate in several C -ITS related projects. Interoperability wil l be one of 
the items to focus on given the national EU-27 differences in the context of subsidiarity. We note 
however that the evaluation of the ITS Directive has considered such projects only to a very limited 
extent. The need for a harmonised and interoperable implementation of the various ITS platforms 
at national level requires the provision of EU funding to finance the implementation of the ITS 
Action plan.  
A harmonised ITS deployment will result in a high societal impact on European roads in terms o f 
safety, traffic efficiency, and comfort for drivers, passengers, and users of public transport. To that 
end, we support that further EU action is taken. Action only at national and local level and/or only 
non-legislative tools at EU level (i.e. without the ITS Directive) could lead to a slow and fragmented 
development of ITS, and hinder the achievement of sustainable, inclusive and multimodal mobility 
for passengers and freight. 
The varying  levels of Member States’ involvement as well as the lack of coordination between them 
are concerning aspects  that are preventing the development of a wider EU ITS market and 
framework, even more so as local initiatives have not proven beneficial for OEMs.  
Generally, to foster a harmonised accomplishment of t he objectives throughout Europe, ACEA 
believes that an action plan for the deployment of ITS should be envisaged and supported together

with National authorities. As for the national ITS action plans mandated under the current Directive, 
specific guidelines should be laid down on how to develop them in a manner that ensures uniformity 
and coherence among the various Member States.  
Whereas the setting up of NAPs is regarded as positive, they do not yet represent a sufficiently 
developed EU data backbone. A few leading Member States have finalised their implementation.  
The implementation in the remaining Member States has not been finalised yet, and/or their 
deployment differ s too much e.g. in terms of the type and the quality of available data, etc. 
Furthermore, the outcome of the work initiated for the creation of a federation of the National 
Access Points remains to be seen. This should be encouraged by the revision of the ITS Directive. 
Data availability and data sharing 
In regard to the availability and sharing of data supporting the ITS services, it should be left to the 
market players (data providers) to determine what data are best suited for given use case and event. 
Innovation between vehicle and infrastructure provider brands is paramount and the full potential 
of the data economy is being activated by all vehicle manufacturers. Data availability does not equal 
mandated data sharing. Also, vehicle manufacturers should be put on a level playing field with other 
relevant data providers in the private industry.  
 
2.2 Other unresolved issues identified by ACEA  (ref. to 
Question 12) 
Beside these three main problems, ACEA has identified additional unresolved issues that should be 
tackled within the revision of the ITS Directive. 
Safe and secure access to data 
A number of Priority Areas under the current ITS Directive imply data exchang es (V2X/X2V). Any 
risk of attack on or access to the vehicle’s data or its security electronics through external systems 
or software programmes , especially  when the vehicle manufacturer was not involved in the 
development, validation or implementation of t he solution must be avoided. It is the vehicle 
manufacturer’s responsibility that the vehicle operates in a safe and secure manner. Our members 
take this responsibility very seriously and have therefore devised a model which best ensures that a 
vehicle is not tampered with remotely. It appears crucial to us that the efforts put in by our members 
into ensuring the safety and security of drivers and passengers of connected vehicles is not negated 
by a regulation or soft law in the context of ITS. We refer to our position paper on access to data for 
third-party services and to the instructive materials. 
An open interoperable platform concept for third parties to access in-vehicle data is a severe risk to 
vehicle security and will endanger drivers’ and passengers’ safety. It will also negatively affect users’ 
 
 
 
ACEA Response to EC Public Consultation on ITS Revision – 29/01/2021  8 
 
privacy. The Commission should therefore avoid any reference to the creation of an open platform.  
Standardisation 
A balanced approach between regulatory and non-regulatory measures is advisable and should be 
pursued. Due account should be taken to the  competitiveness of our automotive industry . All 
vehicle manufacturers demonstrate this. Any idea for mandatory deployment of ITS systems should 
first be duly discussed with the industry and should be properly assessed in the context of jobs, 
growth, contribution to the economy and society as a whole. The vehicle industry is not a utility 
industry which has a different regulatory in -road and raison d’être . Mandatory/one -size-fits-all 
legislation should be avoided.  
The right framework to enable the uptake of ITS deployment is one that considers:  
• Further non-regulatory action on safety and security is essential in the form of consistent 
application, common interpretation, synchronisation between the Commission, Member 
States, and with the automotive industry (increasing road safety for all road users, linked to 
Priority Area III “ITS Road Safety and security applications”).  
• (Minimum level of) standardisation . We note an overall lack of application of existing and 
internationally recognised standards in the field of: 
(i) cyber-security: The Commission should not contradict international 
orientation as developed at UN -ECE level (UN Regulation on Cyber 
Security and Cyber Security Management System R155). The risk -
based approach from the UNECE regulation R155 and ISO21434 must 
be supported. The additional benefits of the general usage of 
certification schemes (e.g. ENISA) are very limited, as they may 
potentially lead to regulatory fragmentation.  
(ii) security of C -ITS communications:  there is a need for a commonly 
agreed basis for security requirements in the realm of C-ITS. We believe 
that the Common Criteria approach should only be used for the systems 
that is needed for and should not be used for vehicles. 
Data protection/privacy 
Compliance with General Data Protec tion Regulation (GDPR) is a major and critical issue. Vehicle 
manufacturers takes this seriously. It is currently unclear how compliance with the GDPR and 
ePrivacy legislation can be ensured for data processing in the context of C -ITS.  In its opinion 
03/2017, the European Data Protection Board (then called the Article 29 Working Party) noted that 
it was not clear which of the actors involved in C-ITS (software developers, vehicle manufacturers, 
road managers),  are data controllers who determine the means and purposes of the data 
processing that occurs.  It reviewed various possible grounds for lawful processing and concluded 
 
 
 
ACEA Response to EC Public Consultation on ITS Revision – 29/01/2021  9 
 
that “given the scope of C -ITS to improve road safety, foster transport efficiency and promote 
environmental sustainability, also through the implementation of this European wide interoperable 
system, the Article 29 Working Party finds that the long term legal basis for this type of processing 
is the enactment of an EU wide legal instrument (art.6(1)c of the GDPR)”. We believe the 
Commission should act upon this suggestion and provide the clarity that is required in this field. We 
also refer to ACEAs critical and detailed comments on the draft EDPB guidelines on connected 
vehicle services ( https://www.acea.be/publications/article/acea-comments-on-edpb-guidelines-1-
2020). 
Spectrum 
We stress the further existence of spectrum allocation uncertainties in the 5,9 GHz ITS safety band. 
On 27 November 2019, the automotive industry ACEA/CLEPA has issued a joint paper on spectrum 
needs for ITS applications  ( https://www.acea.be/publications/article/acea-clepa-paper-future-c-
its-spectrum-needs). The paper contains an overview of use cases requiring additional spectrum. It 
also underlines the necessity for interoperable solutions on C-ITS to guarantee safety. Our industry 
remains fully committed to work with ETSI to achieve technology coexistence in the ITS spectrum. 
Work in standardisation organisations is currently well underway with respect to the evolution of 
existing C -ITS solutions, ie IEEE 802.11bd an d 3GPP Release 16/17. Achieving interoperability  / 
coexistence in the ITS spectrum should not hinder technology evolution. 
The current ITS spectrum is not sufficient to achieve higher levels of safety and serve advanced 
levels of automated driving and advanced ITS use cases. Further needs have been recognised in the 
new EC Implementing Decision 2020/1426 of 7th October 2020, expanding the spectrum from 5875-
5905 MHz (30 MHz) to 5875 -5915 (40 MHz) and sharing possibilities between 5915 -5925 MHz (10 
MHz) with urban rail. However, studies carried out within the Car2Car Communication Consortium 
and in the 5GAA show that at least 50 MHz of direct communication spectrum in the 5.9 GHz band 
will be needed in the near future for the deployment of connected and automated driving (Car2Car 
Communications Consortium, White Paper on Road Safety and Spectrum N eeds in the 5.9GHz for 
C-ITS and Automation Applications, 20 February 2017 and 5GAA Study of spectrum needs for safety 
related intelligent transportation systems – day 1 and advanced use cases, 25th June 2020). 
Technology Neutrality 
As for the communication technologies, ACEA’s guiding principle is technology neutrality, pursuant 
to which technology choices should be left to the market as long as interoperability, scalability and 
harmonization for ITS related services is ensured. As mentioned before, it is essential to have a 
homogenous and interoperable implementation to facilitate the deployment of ITS services. With 
respect to the assessment of new candidate technologies and service profiles, ACEA strongly pleads 
for a transparent process for assigning assessment criteria for new services and technologies.

Involvement of automobile industry in the legislative process. The current Directive contains broad 
powers for the Commission to regulate various ITS aspects. Most of the topics are new and belong 
to the data economy. Therefore, we reiterate the need for a timely and early involvement of ACEA 
and its members in the se t-up of possible future delegated acts. The Priority Areas in the ITS 
Directive seem still relevant but call for an intense dialogue with all industry actors including vehicle 
manufacturers, physical and digital infrastructure providers, National Access Po ints of Member 
States, etc. 
Participation in the European ITS Committee (EIC).  The restricted accessibility of the EIC to 
Members States only has proven to be a limiting factor as the work carried out in the forum has 
implications for the industry. We understand the role of comitology but feel that vehicles as part of 
the equation (V2I/V2N/ and I2V/N2V) are left out of the negotiation in a formal way. Vehicles need 
roads and the other way around. Road authorities should be at the negotiating table with the vehicle 
manufacturers and the Commission to define effective regulatory measures. 
Role of the ITS Advisory Group. ACEA would like to see a more active call from DG MOVE onto the 
ITS Advisory Group which we believe has not been actively consulted up to now in this process as 
well as in the process of the C-ITS  draft delegated act and the update of the ITS Action plan of 2008.  
Commission’s delegated powers. The ITS Directive has a broad application sphere and contains a 
wide area for delegated regulatory instruments of the Commission and Member States (e.g. Priority 
Area 4 “Linking the vehicle with infrastructure” has not been activated with delegated acts yet and 
is particularly broad).  We therefore urge the Commission to review the currently (very) broad scope 
of the powers for delegated regulations (current powers relate to specifications necessary to ensure 
the compatibility, interoperability and continuity for the deployment and operational use of ITS for 
the priority actions). 
Holistic approach: link with other initiatives (AFID, type approval & GSR, TEN -T review, Common 
data spaces/data governance act, ePrivacy, etc.). To ensure coherent European legislation, we 
recommend the Commission to take a holistic approach when  revising the ITS Directive. Due 
consideration should be taken in respect to the planned revision of Regulation (EU) No 1315/2013 on 
Union guidelines for the development of the trans -European transport (TEN -T) network  and of 
Directive 2014/94/EU on the dep loyment of alternative fuel infrastructure (AFID) as well as the 
upcoming Communication on the European Strategy for a Sustainable and Smart Mobility. The  
revision  of  the  ITS  Directive  should also take into account the new approaches proposed in parallel 
by the Commission in the EU data strategy, the EU digital strategy, the  European mobility data 
space ecosystem,  the White Paper on AI and the new policy framework for automated vehicles 
(updates on the Type Approval Regulation and the delegated and implementing acts under the 
General Safety Regulation).

2.3 Priority areas and emerging themes identified by the 
Commission (ref. to Question 14 – 15) 
Priority Areas 
The ITS Directive in its current form has specific relevance for vehicle manufacturers in the 4 original 
Priority Areas: 
• Optimal use of road, traffic and travel data 
• Continuity of traffic and freight management ITS services 
• ITS road safety and security applications 
• Linking the vehicle with the transport infrastructure  
In regard to the latter "Linking the vehicle with the transport infrastructure", road operators should 
provide their data in a more open/standardized way so to enhance traffic management and multi -
modal transport services. 
Emerging themes since 2010 
Alongside these areas, we support the Commission’s view that a number of new emerging themes 
could benefit from further action under the ITS Directive . Hence, for achieving future -oriented 
legislation, Commission may consider updating the 4 original Priority Areas with the following three 
main themes: 
• Connected and automated mobility. In this area, further action should be focused on 
measures aimed at integrating advanced driver support information systems into vehicles 
and road infrastructure: in this regard, the current EU security & trust system is restricted to 
supporting “Day 1” / warning -only services. There is a need to adjust risk assessments and 
the resulting / corresponding security requirements. Focal point of EU action in this context 
should be a lso the further development of digital twins of physical infrastructure for 
automated driving.  
Automated driving regulations should not be tackled in the scope of this Directive. 
Nevertheless, priority should be given to the “connected” part in CCAM and to the resulting 
requirements for using connected technologies, as they are instrumental to the 
achievement of higher levels of automation. The ITS scope should therefore be extended to 
deal with this aspect. 
• Mobility platforms (e.g. Mobility as a Service - MaaS). ITS should embrace the new mobility 
services in a multimodal approach.  The Directive has not been effective in defining 
requirements for EU -wide multimodal travel information services.  The ITS action plan 
should indeed address issues linked to ITS business models, notably to widen the concept 
of public transport and public incentives to new mobility services, such as commuting

carpooling, car-sharing and MaaS. Further EC initiatives in this area should aim at collecting 
all possible ITS business models possible, assessing the impact of their diversity in the EU. 
Member States gathered in a WG with the industry could propose one or more business 
models and provide a cost -benefit analysis. Such an analysis should highlight any 
potentially hidden costs deriving from the compliance with requirements (e.g. in terms of 
respect to privacy, security requirements...) and follow a same methodology. One should 
also take into account the significant market power of utilities such as national railway 
companies which allows them to enter into MaaS solutions in an unbalanced way compared 
to the private sector.  
• Enhanced traffic management. Publicly accessible data from traffic management systems 
are invaluable for vehicle manufacturers and its service providers. Vehicle manufacturers 
are contributing to traffic and asset management on a one -to-one basis through 
contracts/public procurement. This market segment is a highly competitive one and does 
not require mandated approaches. 
Lastly, as regards to electro-mobility / Alternative Fuels Infrastructures , improving and expanding 
the deployment of alternative fuels infrastructure (AFID) is key and the revision of ITS Directive 
should be receptive of such infrastructure requirements, so to enable the uptake of electromobility 
and related technologies. Please refer to ACEA Position Paper on the Review of the AFID.

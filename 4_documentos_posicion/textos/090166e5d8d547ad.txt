EuroHeart: European Unified Registries On
Heart Care Evaluation and Randomized Trials
An ESC project to develop a new ITregistry system which will encompass multiple
features of cardiovascular medicine
In July 2019, the ESC Board agreed to co-ordinate and sponsor the
development of the European Unified Registries On Heart care
Evaluation And Randomized Trials (EuroHeart) for supporting assess-
ment and improvement of quality of cardiovascular care in Europe
based on continuous recording of individual patient data. EuroHeart
will offer a common IT- and dataset infrastructure, which will allow par-
ticipating countries to undertake continuous quality improvement, with
the added value of providing a platform for observational and random-
ized research and post-marketing surveillance of new devices and phar-
macotherapies. The national quality development programme,
infrastructure, and the database will belong to each participating coun-
try. The EuroHeart will have the potential to stepwise include most
ESC countries, which could either adopt the proposed EuroHeart com-
mon infrastructure or align already existing systems to the EuroHeart
data collection. The EuroHeart project will focus on common disease
areas: ACS-PCI, valve disease, heart failure, and atrial fibrillation. Initially,
EuroHeart will develop standardized datasets and quality indicators for
these diseases and devices. In parallel, the SWEDEHEART IT-platform
will be updated to be internationallyavailable and transferable, under a
license agreement, to participatingcountries. During the first 2 years,
the EuroHeart system will be implemented and evaluated in 2–4 coun-
tries. Following this pilot phase, the EuroHeart project may be rolled
out to other countries. EuroHeart will also develop a Data Science
Centre coordinating similar statistical analyses from participating coun-
tries. When implemented in several ESC member countries, EuroHeart
will have the potential to improve thestandards of cardiovascular care,
monitor the safety of new devices and medications, and provide an
important collaborative infrastructure for observational research and
within-registry randomized controlled trials.
Background and rationale
Major advances in cardiovascular medicine have been driven by inno-
vations in basic research in biological and technical sciences. The sub-
sequent testing in prospective randomized controlled trials (RCT) has
provided the evidence underpinning the introduction of new, more
effective, and safer treatments.
In 2009, the ESC launched the EuroObservational Research
Programme (EORP) which aimed to promote and monitor the imple-
mentation of new treatments as well as the adherence to the ESC Clinical
Practice Guidelines. The EORP provides important information on the
development of the treatment of cardiovascular disease in Europe but has
been criticized concerning data quality, representativeness and coverage
regarding common diseases. In 2018, it was agreed that there was a need
to further develop ESC-generated observational data and simultaneously
expand the scope of the registry programme to also include a quality
development programme, long-term monitoring of medical devices and
pragmatic RCTs and under instruction of the ESC Board, the principles
and pilot phase of European Unified Registries On Heart care Evaluation
And Randomized Trials (EuroHeart) project were developed in 2019.
This article presents a summary of the EuroHeart programme.
Improving the quality of observational
data
For rare cardiovascular diseases, EORP constitutes a unique pro-
gramme of observational research, providing greater understanding of
uncommon cardiac diseases and the effects of associated treatments.1–4
However, when applied to common cardiovascular diseases, EORP
has been criticized with regard to the quality, representativeness, and
coverage of the data at the hospital, country, and European level,
partly because the EORP programme records data from a limited
number of selected hospitals over short periods of time in each par-
ticipating country.
5–7 There was also the perception that such data
collection would be undertaken for the ESC with limited return to
the contributing centres and countries. In contrast, continuous indi-
vidual patient data collection in a large number of hospitals in each
country would provide reliable, representative, and generalizable
data, which would help the development and monitoring of quality of
care programmes at the hospital level both in each country and
across different countries, as well as provide an infrastructure for
observational research, long-term safety surveillance, and RCTs.
Such opportunities are aligned with the ESC’s mission to reduce the
burden of cardiovascular disease and address three out of the four
strategic trends identified in the strategic plan for 2016–20, that is,
technology revolution; globalization, and geographic reach; and the even
more stringent regulatory environment.

Providing a system for safety surveillance
of cardiovascular devices
Over the last decade, there has been an increase in the development
and use of new implantable devices for the treatment of cardiovascular
diseases. The level of evidence required to obtain regulatory approval
for new devices will increase substantially in the forthcoming years.
Furthermore, the new European Regulation on Medical Devices man-
dates that new devices will require post-marketing surveillance for
safety and efficacy. Ideally, this should be conducted independently by
an academic body or professional medical association, with compre-
hensive coverage. We believe that continuous national disease-
focused registries, coordinated by the ESC, would significantly contrib-
ute to post-marketing surveillance of devices, improving quality, being
independent, and with reduced costs.
16,17
Performing registry-based randomized
controlled trials
In recent years, there has been a considerable reductioni nt h ed e v e l o p -
ment of new drugs for cardiovascular diseases.18 This is partly due to
the perception that the price payers would be willing to pay for agents
that are expected to yield a moderate beneficial effect on a large number
of eligible patients would not bring sufficient financial gains to industry,
given the spiralling costs associatedwith bringing a new drug to the mar-
ket. In particular, the current complex regulatory environment has con-
tributed to increasing the cost of RCTs manyfold. The ESC believes that
access systems with continuous registration of high quality, harmonized
patient data at entry and over time, for different diseases across multiple
geographies, will allow for performing more pragmatic randomized trials
in the general patient populations while substantially reducing the cost
burden, as demonstrated by SWEDEHEART.
19–21
How to establish national continuous
quality registries
The key to success of national continuous quality registries is establish-
ing a network of hospitals and healthcare personnel with a culture
focused on continuous learning and quality development. To maintain
engagement and enthusiasm, the registries need to provide non-
selective, continuous monitoring and on-line feedback of patient char-
acteristics, processes of care, and health outcomes. Another important
component is the implementation of a reliable, user-friendly infor-
matics infrastructure to support data entry and immediate online feed-
back on performance including both system- and custom-designed
tabular and graphical comparisons over time, with other similar
centres and with guidelines. A third important option is the
opportunity to obtain long-term outcomes in all patients by merging
the data with public registries on vital status, causes of death, hospital
admissions, records from general practice, pharmaceutical prescrip-
tions, and eventually patient-related outcome measurements. A fourth
component is the access to an advanced epidemiology and statistical
data centre ensuring a scientifically robust assessment of the strength
of the associations between treatment strategies and health outcomes.
Finally, a fifth important part is dissemination of the results through
publication in major cardiovascular journals, followed by their inclusion
in national and international guidelines, which might lead to changes in
treatments and improved outcomes.
Accomplishments by continuous quality
registers
To date, the Swedish and UK continuous national cardiovascular regis-
tries have:
• Continuously monitored and supported improvements in the qual-
ity of cardiovascular care at individual centres and nationwide.9–12
• Conducted novel observational research based on long-term fol-
low-up of outcomes in all patient s; e.g., concerning temporal and
spatial variation in care and outcomes; associations between expo-
sure (environmental/pharmacotherapies) and cardiovascular out-
comes; international compari sons of care; development and
validation of prognostic models and indicators of quality; and bur-
den of disease in specific populations.
9–15
• Shown that implementation of new effective treatments is associ-
ated with a reduction in the mortality and morbidity of patients
with acute myocardial infarction.
10–15
• Enabled continuous safety surveillance of PCI and valve devices
including comparisons of outcomesof different devices and patients
managed with and without devices (Sweden).16,17

The SWEDEHEART IT -platform
Briefly, the Swedish national cardiology quality registries have established
an infrastructure with continuous and online recording of data and imme-
diate feedback concerning processes of care and outcomes on all
patients. The associated IT-platform generates both system- and custom-
designed user-friendly tabular and graphic reports on processes and
changes of care and outcomes for each user, whenever requested.5,6
The system includes information on all implanted devices in enough
detail to fulfil the regulatory requirements. Thereby the system can moni-
tor the technical performance of the implanted devices and provide safety
surveillance also concerning healthoutcomes with different devices.
5,6
Finally, the infrastructure also includes an application for prospective
RRCT, which can be integrated in different registries and may also be
combined with more conventional collection of follow-up data in so-
called hybrid trials (combining technologies both from RCT and RRCT).8,9
EUROHEART: a new ESC
initiative
EuroHeart is a new ESC initiative for supporting continuous quality of
care development based on individual patient data. The main aims are

to support continuous quality of care improvement and provide an
infrastructure for observational and randomized research and safety
surveillance of new devices and drugs. EuroHeart will be an ESC coor-
dinated programme, which will support the development of national/
local registry programmes using a common dataset and where the
national infrastructure and database will belong to the respective par-
ticipating country (Figure 1). EuroHeart will, as an option, provide an
IT- and dataset infrastructure to allow participating countries to under-
take continuous quality improvement, with the added value of being
able to perform collaborative observational and randomized research
and the post-marketing surveillance of new devices and pharmaco-
therapies. The system will address the common disease areas: ACS-
PCI, valve disease, heart failure, and atrial fibrillation and the devices
used in these conditions.
EuroHeart has the potential to include most ESC country members
through stepwise implementation. Given a harmonized methodology
for the collection of standardized data, EuroHeart will enable the pool-
ing and interrogation of data from participating countries. Moreover,
this approach has the potential to improve cost efficiency both at the
country level and at the European level. Notably, EuroHeart is com-
pletely aligned with the ESC mission to reduce the burden of cardio-
vascular disease. Acting as the project sponsor, the ESC will create and
support the EuroHeart organization, which will work with national car-
diac societies and/or other local stakeholders on the development and
deployment of an efficient and harmonized infrastructure for running
continuous quality registries.
EUROHEART development in practice
Presently around 20 countries have indicated an interest in developing
a similar infrastructure and participate in the EuroHeart project.
During the first 2 years, the plan is to perform a pilot phase by imple-
menting the international version of the EuroHeart system and IT-
platform in 2–4 countries (Figure 2). The selection of the countries will
be based on the availability of dedicated national champions working
with a network of hospitals willing to join and invest time and efforts in
the quality registry programme and with the potential to develop and
sustain an administrative and IT-infrastructure primarily based on their
own national resources. Participating countries will also need to have a

The pilot phase will investigate the possibility of promoting and facil-
itating the adoption of the continuous national registry model, as
developed by SwedeHeart, for data acquisition, quality development,
device surveillance, and RRCTs on major cardiovascular disease areas
across several of the ESC countries. After this pilot study, the
EuroHeart project may be rolled out to other countries.
Representatives from the participating and interested countries will be
involved from the very beginning in the development of EuroHeart
and be members of the different subcommittees. EuroHeart will be
organized as an ESC coordinated collaboration between national qual-
ity registries of cardiovascular diseases where all members are autono-
mous parts of the system controlling their own registry infrastructure,
their IT-platform, their databases and all national activities (Figure 1).
The executive committee coordinating the scheduled activities in the
pilot phase is presented inFigure 3.
T o establish EuroHeart the ESC will:
(1) Initiate, support, and co-ordinate the development of national con-
tinuous quality registry infrastructures.
(2) Provide a common dataset of standardized variables and quality indi-
cators for common cardiovascular diseases for use in the national
registries.
(3) Provide an optional IT-infrastructure for quality registries [e.g. the
QReg5 platform developed and maintained in Uppsala Clinical
Research Center (UCR), Sweden] providing tools for local quality
development, data management, local statistical analyses, device
monitoring, and registry-based clinical trials.
(4) Support the development and analyses of common datasets by pro-
viding tools for preparing national datasets of predefined variables
for use in collaborative analyses.
(5) Establish a data science centre (IT system and platform) that will co-
ordinate and support the statistical analyses and reporting of results
from common datasets based on initiatives from any of the partici-
pants in the collaborative group.
(6) Establish a support organization that ensures the sustainability of
EuroHeart over time.
Conclusion
The ESC decision to co-ordinate and sponsor the development of the
EuroHeart registry system is a change of paradigm on how to further
reduce the burden of cardiovascular disease with large geographic
reach in an era of IT-technology revolution but often unsurmountable
costs of clinical trials and an increasingly stringent regulatory environ-
ment. When implemented in several ESC member countries,
EuroHeart will have the potential to continuously improve the stand-
ards of cardiovascular care, monitor the safety of new devices and
medications, and provide an important collaborative infrastructure for
observational research and RCTs.

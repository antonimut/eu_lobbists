Amundi is the largest European asset manager by assets under management and amongst the top 
10 globally. It manages 1,662 billion euros1 of assets across six main investment hubs in Boston, 
Dublin, London, Milan, Paris and Tokyo. Amundi offers its clients in Europe, Asia-Pacific, the Middle 
East and the Americas a wealth of market expertise and a full range of capabilities across the active, 
passive and real assets investment universes. Clients also have access to a complete set of services 
and tools. Headquartered in Paris, Amundi was listed in November 2015. 
Amundi welcomes the opportunity to reply to this very important consultation on the review of the 
European Long-term Investment Funds (“ELTIF”) regulatory framework.  
As a matter of fact the ELTIF regulation has not been a success thus far as proved by the very small 
number of ELTIF launched. At this stage, let us mention that Amundi has registered four ELTIFs up to 
now, with two others in the pipe. 
Many of the reasons behind the low number of registered ELTIFs have been identified in the present 
consultation and we are keen to provide some proposals in this respect.  
In our view an in-depth review of the Regulation is needed. Indeed, ELTIF does not fill a crucial gap 
in the current European fund regulation landscape for allowing long-term savings to be invested in 
‘long-term’ assets. AMUNDI, in line with the Capital Market Union objectives would strongly support 
a review of ELTIF that would allow for increasing more long-term retail engagement, i.e. more long-
term savings oriented towards long-term investments. This would be beneficial for both the 
economy and of individual savers who, on one hand, would be granted with the long-term premium 
that they cannot access in many investment products, and on the other hand, would be less exposed 
to market fluctuations when investing in non-listed assets.  
In Amundi’s view in order to address the shortcomings of the current ELTIF regulation amendments 
should be made in two areas: 
A) A targeted review of some key features of the ELTIF regulation, and 
B) The introduction of a second ELTIF regime (i.e. an open-ended ELTIFs more appropriate for 
a number of retail investors). 
Regarding the targeted amendments, we believe that the key ones are: 
- Review the rules related to the eligible investment assets. Examples of amendments are: bringing 
to 50/50 the proportion between eligible assets under the Regulation and the assets referred to in 
Article 50(1) of Directive 2009/65/EC, raising the 500M€ cap for eligible listed companies to 2000M€, 
lowering the 10M€ threshold for individual real assets;

- Enlarging the Portfolio composition and diversification rules. Examples of amendments are: 
enlarging the types of eligible funds to any kind of AIFs and increasing the 20% cap, in order to offer 
investors exposure to a broader variety of assets; 
- Deleting the limit on encumbrances granted over assets provided under article 16(e) while 
maintaining a limit for leverage. The limit on encumbrances does not really increase the level of 
investors’ protection. On the other hand, a low threshold has the effect of increase the cost of 
funding since it is market standard that collateral to secure a financing normally exceeds the value 
of borrowed cash (now limited to 30% of ELTIFs’ AuM);  
- Introducing more flexibility in the distribution process through notably a review of the “entry 
ticket”. The 10k€/100k€/500k€ rule is quite burdensome and distributors are not comfortable with 
these complex rules that are difficult to monitor. A lower threshold will not only streamline the 
distribution process but will also grant retail investors the opportunity to have more diversification 
being able to invest in more ELTIFs;  
- Granting to ELTIF a favorable tax treatment throughout the Member States to the maximum extent 
possible; 
- Review the provision related to Conflict of interest and co-investment. Article 12 of the ELTIF 
regulation should be amended. It is normally market practice to ask managers to have “skin in the 
game” as it aligns the interest of the manager with that of the investors. 
With respect to the introduction of a second ELTIF regime aimed at promoting greater retail 
investment uptake we believe that it is needed since: 
-  A clear distinction would make sense between closed-ended ELTIFs, that would have their place in 
large diversified clients’ portfolios, and open-ended ELTIFs, more appropriate for a number of retail 
investors. 
- The creation of an open-ended ELTIF regime would allow the gathering a much larger number of 
investors with different time horizons. Instead of a limited subscription period, these ELTIFs would 
remain always open to new investors. Redemption should be possible at regular intervals and the 
frequency of redemption dates (monthly, quarterly or half-yearly) could be linked to the percentage 
of illiquid assets. In addition to this parameter, redemptions would be limited at each term (up to 3 
or 5% of AuM redeemable). 
- Open-ended ELTIFs could target large AuM, with possible “fund of funds” schemes that would make 
diversification easier to achieve, with access to the whole range of long-term assets: PE, listed 
Midcaps, infrastructures, real estates, high yield bonds. These ELTIFs should be authorised to invest 
in a larger variety of funds (any type of AIFs, closed-ended ELTIFs, or EuVECAs and EuSEFs).  
- A sufficient differentiation between the two different regime i.e. closed-ended versus open-ended 
ELTIFs would not only provide clarity for investors, but would also ease the management of these 
funds. In fact, the management of large open-ended funds differs significantly to the management 
of ELTIFs as defined in the present regulation.  
- This second regime would allow for a better use of abundant long-term savings across the EU, 
providing long-term premium to these savings and less exposure to financial markets’ fluctuations.

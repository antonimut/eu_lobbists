The future viability of Europe depends in part on whether it can succeed in finding specific 
European solutions to the challenges of digitalisation in competition with other regions of the 
world. The prospective European Health Data Space illustrates the significance of having a 
European approach to digitalisation, but also the need for a broad social debate on the oppor-
tunities and risks of utilising large amounts of data (Big Data) and analysing it using artificial 
intelligence systems.  
A European Data Space for the storage, transfer and collaborative use of health data harbours 
indisputable potential for  medical scientific research by offering the possibility to network 
expertise, but in particular by facilitating the gathering of an incomparably larger volume of 
data. This can help contribute to improved disease prevention and patient-oriented health 
care, as well as better treatment options . At the same time, a functioning Health Data Space 
requires that citizens have confidence that their health data will be handled securely and 
properly. They will only be prepared to consent to the use and transfer of their data if ade-
quate legal and technical security measures are in place.  
During Germany’s EU Council Presidency, the Council will discuss possible conclusions on a 
Code of Conduct for the use of personal data in a “European Health Data Space”. The German 
Medical Association  (Bundesärztekammer) fundamentally supports the use  of data for re-
search purposes to improve medical care, provided that the use of such data follows the high-
est data protection regulation and ethical standards. The German Medical Association would 
like to contribute its professional expertise to the discussion on a Code of Conduct. Every day, 
doctors are caught in the tension between the individual’s right to autonomy and the interests 
of medical scientific research. In order to safeguard physician -patient privilege and medical 
confidentiality, which constitute a fundamental pillar of the physician-patient relationship, 
guidelines must be drawn up for the use of personal data. From the perspective of the German 
Medical Association, the following requirements should therefore guide the development of a 
European Health Data Space. 
Establish the Health Data Space based on European data protection  
A common European approach must be taken in order to enhance the potential of the Health 
Data Space. It must be endowed with a secure and efficient digital infrastructure . This must 
be rapidly set up and expanded in accordance with the principle of interoperability and with 
the support of EU funding. There is also an urgent need for greater European cooperation in 
the event of threats to cybersecurity.  
We support the European Commission ’s idea of a common approach to health data sharing 
different from other regions of the world with purely market- or state-based data usage mod-
els. This can only succeed if the European Health Data Space is based on European data pro-
tection standards. Personal data must be protected through a binding legal framework which 
may be coupled with additional voluntary agreements. However, it must not lead to a reduc-
tion of the standards of protection contained in the European General Data Protection Regu-
lation (GDPR). Data may only be exchanged between institutions and stored only on servers 
that are subject t o European data protection law. The transmission or processing of data in 
third countries must be excluded under any circumstances.  
 
In a European Data Space that makes large amounts of data available for research projects , 
re-identification cannot always be prevented, even in the case of pseudonymised and anony-
mised data. Therefore, in addition to technical measures, organisational and legal measures 
must be taken to impede (e.g., intermediary advisory boards ) or prohibit t raceability. The

tasks of the European data protection officer could, for example, be expanded to include mon-
itoring the proper use of personal data in the Health Data Space.  
 
Develop innovative rules of consent 
If personal data are to be made available in a European Health Data Space for various research 
projects which are not known at the time of consent, innovative consent models must be de-
veloped. These must allow the data subject to retain control over his or her data by enabling 
him or her to choose between broad or explicit consent for a specific purpose on the basis of 
comprehensive and comprehensible information. A general objection to any future use must 
remain possible at all times. To this end, a supe rvisory body, for example, in the form of a 
trustee or ethics committee, is indispensable . This body could, in the context of the specific 
research project, assess the specific  risk of r e-identification and consider this in relation to 
the intended scientific use, or examine the suitability of the evaluation methodology and the 
appropriateness of the information provided to the research participants.  
 
Data may only be transmitted on a voluntary basis. From this it follows that a refusal to give 
consent must not lead to any disadvantages or discrimination. We reject commercial models 
for trading or selling health data . Consent for data donations must not be for sale. From the 
perspective of the German Medical Association, it is critically necessary to explicitly observe 
the rights of the patient as defined in  2016 by the World Medical Association in the Declara-
tion of Taipei using the example of health databases and  biobanks, and the resulting ethical 
and legal obligations of physicians to protect information.  
 
Ensure tested quality for health data 
The data used in the Health Data Space must be workable, i.e. of equally high quality and com-
parability. Methodological and scientific diligence, uniformly high data collection standards 
and verification by the participating health care institution are all prerequisites for obtaining 
higher quality data . The Code of Conduct must therefore ensure that only high -quality data 
are used in the Health Data Space. It must therefore contain a provision specifying the cases 
in which appropriately verified data must be used. To this end, it is essential that the source 
of the data is identified. 
The comparability of the data presupposes interoperability, not only in technical terms, but 
also in semantic terms. It is therefore necessary to have a common European strategy for the 
interoperability of all relevant health data . The uniform use of a systematic nomenclature in 
medicine throughout Europe would be conceivable here. Appropriate licences would have to 
be secured by the EU together with the Member States. 
 
Access to data geared toward the further development of patient care  
Data released for the purpose of exchange may only be acces sible to a group of institutions  
defined according to strict criteria, and under no circumstances to any commercial developer 
of an application . In the eyes of the German Medical Association, important criteria for the 
selection of authorised bodies and individuals include, for example, a focus on the objectives 
of medical scientific research. The decision about access rights must be made by a competent 
body determined by the legislator on the basis of clearly defined legal criteria.

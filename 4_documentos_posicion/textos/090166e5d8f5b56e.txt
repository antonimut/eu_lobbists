MaaS Alliance feedback to the questions by EC, October-November 2020

General feedback from the MaaS Alliance  
 
The MaaS Alliance agrees that EU has taken some significant steps towards more intelligent and multimodal 
mobility system, the current ITS Directive playing a key role in facilitating the access to multimodal data. The  
access to data is the first precondition for the development of MaaS services  but data availability alone does 
not enable an industry like MaaS developing . Today, t here remains a clear need for further action s on 
interoperability and APIs, ticketi ng, cooperation and fair competition . These challenges, also identified in 
the report Remaining Challenges For EU -wide Integrated Ticketing and Payment Systems , should be addressed 
in the upcoming review of the ITS Directive.  
 
The European Commission has set out ambitious goals for the European transport sector to deliver a  90% 
reduction in greenhouse gas emissions by 2050, while at the same time providing Europeans with more 
affordable, accessible, healthier and cleaner transport alternatives. Europe benefits fro m a reliable and high -
quality public transport system as the backbone of our sustainable mobility services. In the most efficient public 
transport systems the mass transit solutions are complemented by various shared and on -demand services. 
Thus the MaaS presents a powerful tool in reaching the decarbonisation goal , with a significant emission 
reduction potential while providing Europeans  with all-encompassing services to meet their mobility needs 
both locally and when travelling across the Union. This potential was also acknowledged by the EU Green Deal 
presented last year. In evaluations on the cost-benefit relation of different tools to reduce transport emissions, 
servitization has also been highlighted for its superior cost -effectiveness vis -a-vis ot her measures like i.e. 
renewing the vehicle fleet.  With an enabling push from the regulators we can make this European digital 
mobility innovation to deliver in wide -scale, across the whole European Union, making our transport system 
more sustainable (with reduced emissions) and efficient (by optimising resources and operations of transport 
system), but also more resilient.  
 
In the current development phase of Maas, the regulation is, first and foremost, a tool to build trust  among 
the industry stakeholders, setting a clear framework facilitating the development of the industry and creating 
a predictable environment for the investments both for public and private sector. The ticketing and IT-systems 
requires heavy investments often with a 10 -15 years  lifecycle and t hus th e public transport authorities, 
operators and industrial stakeholders need a foreseeable framework to secure financial viability of the 
investments that are having a direct impact to user experience in mobility . In addition, an important task of 
regulation, especially at EU level, is also to decrease the market fragmentation and support the development 
of scalable services beyond isolated city i slands. Although people mostly move and operate in the domain of 
one city or region, covering only one area is not enough to fulfil all their mobility needs. Seamless MaaS services 
support the accomplishment the European vision of a single European Transport Area and the free flow of 
people and goods across borders.  
In order to encourage much needed innovations and new services, the regulation should remain business model 
and technology agnostic and it should not select the winners or the losers  of the market . Instead, regulation 
should set a level -playing field and competitive and non -discriminatory market with open market access. By 
ensuring that MaaS -operators are not hindered by protectionism and market distortion, decision -makers 
support the Ma aS ecosystem and help it thrive . In this context it is suggested that regulators would evaluate 
and use the good benchmark set by PSD2 (Payment Services Directive) to facilitate trust -building within 
mobility systems and ecosystems.

At the MaaS Alliance our main focus is always on the user and how we can increase their freedom of choice in 
sustainable mobility. Regardless of what business models – publicly or privately led – are developed, the MaaS 
Alliance strongly encourage s an open  ecosystem approach  where the user would have several options to 
choose from. When it comes to this q uestion at hand, ticketing, the  open ecosystem approach implies that a 
non-discriminatory access to critical assets and essential services, such as public transport tickets, should be 
enabled for intermediate platforms, like MaaS . This is par ticularly important when the Transport Service 
Provider is in a dominant market position at local, regional or national level providing essential services, such 
as services to regional or national railway networks or public transport services with in a city or a region. At the 
EU-level this could be done by regulating an access to a gradually expanding minimal reference ticket offer , 
determing the ticket products and types of public transport and other modes that needs to be made accessible 
to MaaS operators. In this context, also the need for a framework for an equitable cost-sharing and supporting 
mechanisms have to be evaluated. While defining the timeline for the gradual requirements, it is important to 
bear in mind that this is a critical enabler for the development of the multimodal offerings . 
 
It is worth noticing that while in the emerging platform economy, it is typically the platform that benefits from 
a stronger bargaining position than the underlying service providers, in Mobility as a Service business the 
situation is often the opposite, due to the characteristics of the transport services market. In the  digital era, a 
digital platform can be a tool to add transparency and market dynamics and facilitate freedom of choice for 
users. Thus, when analysing the platforms in the context of the ITS Directive and the Digital Services Act, from 
a MaaS point of view one of the main questions is  how to regulate platforms so that they mitigate rather than 
amplify the monopolized characteristics of the transport service market itself.   
 
Link between MaaS and PSO1  
 
Ticket resales  is a prerequisite for MaaS to develop.  Multimodality and MaaS put ticket resales into a new 
context. In this context, ticket resale is no longer solely a tool to guarantee a high level of public service but an 
elemental cornerstone in a whole new market.  To drive MaaS development, it is essent ial to develop 
functioning markets for ticket resales on viable and reasonable commercial terms. An EU wide m inimal 
reference ticket offer  by PTOs towards the market , as introduced above, could be included in their PSO. We 
want to highlight the need to separate the different services and define more clearly the services that could 
be included in PSO and the services that are offered through market. To our knowledge, there are currently 
no contract defining MaaS services as pertaining to the PSO in Europe. We have, nonetheless, identified 
instances where public transport authorities are treating the operation and supply of MaaS services as if they 
were included in the PSO and creating MaaS offerings together with singular transport service providers - 
effectively restricting access to the market for other MaaS operators and transport service providers.  
 
The MaaS Alliance calls for effective separation of different roles of PTAs. PTAs can have different roles, but 
the roles have to be organised in a way that they  are not conflicting; for instance, it should be clear that PTA s 
cannot be “the referee” or governing body for the services at the same time than provid ing these services to 
the market t hemselves. While Regulation 1370/2007 allows PSO to cover a range of activities and duties that 
the competent authorities can carry out themselves or entrust to a third party, functioning as a MaaS 
aggregator and operator in a new m arket is beyond the scope of offering passenger transport services. As the 
MaaS market and the market for mobility services is deregulated and co mpetitive in many parts of the U nion, 
including MaaS services in the PSO would also be in conflict with the core European values of competition and 
a free market economy. To comply with Regulation 1370/2007 and general applicable competition law, any

To summarise, the MaaS Alliance suggests the following step s to be taken by the EC:   
1) Continued efforts to develop an open ecosystem, enhancing the access to static and dynamic data 
and establishment of non -discriminatory access  to ticket resale, validation and control 
functionalities with fair commercial terms, that is gradually extended to cover all ticket types2. In 
practise, we suggest this to be organised with a gradual requirements covering to all ticket products 
and types, starting with a minimal reference offer with defined additional phased requirements, 
similar to MMTIS approach.  
2) Conflict-avoiding r ules of effective separation of different roles of PTAs: they should not be the 
governing body at the same time than providing services to the market themselves , whether directly 
or through delegated public transport operation contracts  
3) Full enforcement of the competition rules with a credible market over sight at place to prevent 
abuse of domina nce that can disable and complicate the technical and commercial integration of 
services into a MaaS offering  
4) Further facilitation of data sharing with various data sharing models , especially data reciprocity , 
assessing also the costs of data sharing and ticketing access schemes to data providers and supporting 
if needed, especially the involvement of SMEs 
5) NAPs should be further developed keeping in mind that the data accessible via NAPs is serving needs 
of mul timodal ticketing and MaaS providers. All relevant data (static and dynamic) should be 
accessible and reusable in certain level of quality . Commercially sensitive data should be recognised, 
with appropriate safeguarding instruments at place  that enable to execute the contracts/business 
rules between entities. Regarding NAPs, one focus of the coordination in the EU level should be 
securing that minimum requirements are fulfilled.   
 
In addition, the MaaS Alliance sees mainly  risks in the following considered  measures and thus cannot 
support them:  
- Imposing an EU-wide ranking criteria for the service : this would reduce the level of innovativeness 
and personalisation of service experience and  decrease the availability of services in the area without 
frequent and well -serving mass transit options . Instead it might be useful to consider forcing the 
display of e.g. CO2 emissions to provide transparent information to the users so that they can make 
well-informed and conscious choices. In addition, local policy tools and incentive schemes supporting 
uptake of sustainable modes should be encouraged.

Topic 2: Governance framework for multimodal digital ticketing services  
 
Regardless of who will play a role of a MaaS operator, the public authorities  should have appropriate tools to 
govern development of multimodal mobility ecosystem and to ensure the compliance with the public policy 
goals with clear benefits for society, environment and users. The regulatory framework should remain neutral 
in supporting both pub licly and commercially -led MaaS operations (as well other potential models) and the 
establishment of open ecosystem s for the services . This implies that similar fair market access and 
operational conditions should be created for everyone , with an appropriate and up -to-date governance at 
place, preventing the establishment of private or public walled gardens blocking further evolvement of the 
services. This open ecosystem approach also supports the s calability of services beyond isolated city island s, 
facilitating the seamless services not only in cities but also for intercity and cross -border travellers.  
If, against of the above defined vision, there is just one MaaS platform in a locally or  nationally protected 
environment (closed system), the non-discriminatory rules imposed by EU regulation on access to services and 
integration need to be put in place. But if we build the system from the beginning to be open by facilitating the 
data sharing, access to market, fair commercial terms and access to integration then the competition rules and 
enforcement and oversight tools makes the obligations obsolete. The governance framework should remain 
proportionate and it should not e.g. standardise the user experience (for instance by imposing certain rank ing 
of services), as this would destroy any possibility for a more personalized approach, closer to individual needs. 
Instead it might be useful to consider forcing the display of certain information like GHG (e.g. CO2) emissions, 
similar to  nutritional info on food products, to provide transparent information to the users so that they can 
make well -informed and conscious choices based  on the information provided . Also, supporting and 
subsidising the demand of sustainable services w ould be a quicker and more efficient route.  Similarly, we are 
not in favour of regulating the content of the offerings or subscription as that means that the MaaS services can 
develop only in areas where regular public transport services are available.  
Also, as highlighted at the page 2, regarding the governance, t he MaaS Alliance calls for e ffective separation 
of different roles of PTAs, it should be clear that PTAS cannot be “the referee” or governing body at the same 
time than providing the services to the market themselves, or through a delegated service.

agreements. MaaS is not possible to develop without these contractual agreements and thus we do not

Should digital ticketing services be obliged to share data, (such as data related to travel demand / trip

Should digital ticketing services be obliged to integrate, in a non-discriminatory manner, all the candidate 
transport operator for the category of service they sell (in a relevant geographical scope)?  No. Obliging 
MaaS operators to integrate all services will restrict smaller players in the market as integrations require 
a lot of resources. Implementing an obligation would also most likely limit innovation and the possibility 
of tailoring services to customer needs. To ensure that a diverse group of services are available to 
customers, ensuring fair competition is key.  This is something the EC could support by issuing further 
guidelines on the application of competition policy in the mobility field.    
 
Should digital ticketing services be obliged to integrate all the services / lines of the operator for which they 
are selling the service ? NO. The Commission should move towards an open ecosystem where access to 
data, interoperability and fair market access are the foundational pillars. To ensure market 
development and avoid dominant walled gardens, open APIs are key.   The decision to integrate should, 
however, remain with MaaS operators.  
 
Should digital ticketing services be obliged to respect certain rules regarding ranking algorithms to require 
that the most sustainable services are displayed first? NO, not to be regulated at EU level 
 
Should digital ticketing services be obliged to always include active modes if relevant / available? NO, not 
to be regulated at EU level

NO. It’s too early and not possible at this point as granulated accessibility information isn’t included in

The MaaS Alliance Actions and Roadmap on Ticketing  
 
The MaaS Alliance has a strong focus on ticketing in 2020-2021. Working together with our member s and the 
Smart Ticketing Alliance, our aim is to initiate and foster efforts in creation of business and operation models, 
and guidelines and harmonised approach to support development of more integrated, seamless and scalable 
MaaS services, and encourage the customer usage and trust of smart ticketing within Mobility as a Service. The 
MaaS Alliance is keen on continuing its work to support the Commission and the other stakeholders , including 
the public transport and ticketing sectors,  towards the achievement of safer, greener and more inclusive 
transport system for Europe. 
 
Looking towards the future of the ticketing one of the fundamental question is whether the aim is to integrate 
existing ticketing networks or aim to be prepared for a EU -wide personal mobility value chain. Full 
interoperability of all existing ticketing networks is probably out of reach. Thus more recommended approach 
would be to thrive towards agreed principles and establishment of trust encompassing  GDPR compliant 
interoperability between ID, payment and mobility services and interoperability between accounts. This calls 
for agnostic and integrated forms of MaaS foundations and a rich div ersity of mobility communities and  
collaborations. By the end of 2020 we will e stablish a roadmap based on agreed fundaments, including list of  
legal/technical/commercial enablers that should be strengthened, indicating also the timing and order of the 
actions. The roadmap will consist of description of real -time interoperable minimum viable macro networks 
digital infrastructure and transforming existing micro networks from static data to dynamic and from siloed 
accounts to comprehensive mobility services.

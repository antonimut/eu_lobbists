What is at stake: increase modal shift to achieve our climate objectives  
Modal shift is a key element of any transport policy that aims to improve the sustainability of 
transport, contributing to a better use of existing capacity and to an increase of the efficiency of the 
transport system. Between 1996 and 2016, modal shift from pas senger cars to more 
sustainable transport modes has been slow . In 2016, passenger cars accounted for 71% of 
passenger transport measured in passenger.km, representing only a very thin decrease compared 
to their modal share in 1996 (73,2%). Over the same period, the modal share of rail and public 
transport has slowly evolved1. 
The transport sector is the only one that has not reduced its emissions since 1990 with an 18% 
increase since 1990. Out of the total emissions from transport, 23% concern urban areas. 
Most of these emissions come from private car use. Yet it is not in the city centres that car use 
is the highest but, in the peripheries, and in their links and connections with the city centres. A 
Europe-wide study reveals that the most frequent average journey of European citizens is 17 km. 
80% of this daily mobility is done by private car2.

The reality on the ground reminds us that we need physical services to get around. Digital technology 
is one of the components of the mobility system and without appropriate infrastructure, mass public 
transport services and a sustainable economic model, digital technology cannot give its full potential 
and added value to daily mobility. Transdev wishes to reaffirm the essential nature of access 
to data and the development of services that combine the general interest and services to 
citizens. This can be done thanks to MaaS solutions locally deployed and piloted by PTAs 
with public transport as a backbone . As there is no business model without public funding for 
daily mobility, PTA will be the core of any local MaaS solution.

Adapted local intelligent transport systems will require a proper share of mobility 
data with a clear governance framework of the reuse of the data 
Transdev believes that the availability of dynamic data listed in the Annex of Delegated Regulation 
2017/19263 is a core requirement for a coherent and efficient  development of MaaS solutions. Yet, 
it must be realized that there is a financing part that must be provided for the producers of the data. 
The production and updating of this dynamic data have a real cost.  Data availability may involve 
strong infrastructure and recurring costs  (that could be fixed by the local authority) . Public data 
should not systematically mean free of charge. Under the French mobility law adopted in December 
2019, dynamic and static data must be available on the French national access point.  Yet, in terms 
of technology, it’s not about more data. It’s about having consistent high -quality data with 
interoperable standards.  
Beyond the debate on the list of data available, the key issue will be the conditions surrounding the 
accessibility and reuse of these data. It is necessary to ensure a priori that their reuse is consistent 
with public policies : how can we understand that a service using public data sends road traffic in 
front of schools because the parallel street is congested? At a time when private services whose sole 
objective is to provide services to people, without measuring the impacts on the general interest 
(occupation of public space, security, etc.), a minimal and reasoned regulation of the reuse of public 
data is necessary. With its license agreement, the Lyon Metropolitan Area  in France has developed 
the most complete open data set in France since 2012, with ar ound a hundred re-users. With this 
type of license agreement, which identifies re-users, for sensitive data only (5% of data), 
a dialogue is initiated between PTA and re-users, allowing regulation only in cases of non-
compliance with public policies. It’s thanks to this framework that many non-open data producers 
have made their data available. 
Mobility data opening must remain a tool for public transport authorities to manage an 
adapted and coherent mobility policy on their territory . PTAs need to get qualitative data on 
the needs and uses of mobility on their territory. Local authorities lack of quantitative and qualitative 
element to apprehend mobility habits on their territory. That is linked to what Transdev defended in 
the Mobility Law in France, ac cess to data from connected vehicles and driving assistants (Google, 
TomTom, Waze, etc.) should be by right for public local authorities  and infrastructure manager in 
order to better understand mobility habits and for better traffic knowledge purposes. 
Governance issues: MaaS  solutions must remain a tool in the hands of public 
transport authorities to organize and pilot their local sustainable mobility policy  
As stated before, challenges of daily mobility do not lie in city centres but in larger urban areas that 
include the outskirts. That is why the MaaS Solutions must consider these logics and these issues at 
stake. Any MaaS governance framework should contribute to modal shift and guarantee the transport 
authority’s ability to organise mobility on its territory on equal and inclusive terms4.  
In a framework where public transport is heavily subsidized, investment in a MaaS project 
and to a lesser extent its long -term economic equilibrium seems  difficult without the 
financial support of the public authorities.  New mobility services operators and private actors 
are mainly designed to address city centres mobility issues and short distance trips for people who 
have a high value of time, i.e high incomes. These actors are suggesting that they can be more 
efficient and cheaper than public sector in the development and implementation of MaaS solutions. 
It is certainly true for specific market segment (business and tourists in large cities) but it is also not 
the case for daily mobility for all : the collapse of numero us private services (free bike services, e -
scooter services) reminds us the simple fact that private business model for all in cities is out of 
range for viable private BtoC business models, as demonstrated by the 2019 CERRE study5.

When it comes to daily mobility, MaaS is not a national and even less so a global dimension market. 
It is a multi -local market, and it will require public support for daily mobility Creating and 
implementing a sustainable urban mobility plan for cities and regions requires that the PTA retain s 
the ability to design a coherent fare structure acceptable for all public transport users and for the 
local taxpayer. Such fares should not be modified through an EU-wide ticketing system. In its effort 
to facilitate multimodal transport and improve the user experience, many PTAs across Europe 
currently already integrate different transport modes and issue combined tickets vi a different 
channels (apps, ticket offices, machines). The tariffs of mobility services and the categories of users 
(e.g., young, or senior, single user or families, etc.) will differ from one service to another and from 
one region to another.  Because of this experience and their central role as integrator, PTAs  (and 
sometimes PTO) should be able to either set up their own MaaS system, participate in cooperative 
MaaS systems, or regulate thi rd-party MaaS system(s) on their territory.  Nevertheless, standards 
(formatted data, APIs) can be organised to reduce deployment costs from one city to another. 
Keeping in mind that the MaaS is primarily a mobility service and that its success will depend on the 
existence of a visible and credible mobility offer as an alternative to the private car, the public 
transport authority must play its role as a strategist to define the hierarchy and 
complementarity of the different public and private modes, and the pricing rules 
guaranteeing the sustainability of the model . PTAs represent the right authority and 
commitment to bring together all urban mobility services as a trusted and independent third party 
pursuing general interest only. By setting the rules of the game (articulation of modes, use of public 
space, data governance, etc.), the PTA guarantees that the MaaS is built in the service of the general 
interest and avoids the uncontrolled development of offers and services.  
The Public Transport Authority has: 
➢ the legal and contractual authority to ensure that the operators under PSO Regulation adhere 
to the approach. 
➢ the role of trusted third party in which the different mobility actors can rely for data pooling  
The PSO Regulation r ecognises local competent authorities’ right to organise mobility 
flows on their territory as they see fit. This in turn leads them to define public service 
obligations (such as fare structure, regularity requirements, accessibility standards, 
selling points, capacity and quality requirements) that PTOs will carry out in return for 
financial compensation and/or exclusive rights on the territory. Should the selling of tickets 
be taken out of the hands of the PTA, this would weaken the underlying principle of the PSO regulation 
i.e., subsidiarity. The European framework should be designed in a way that it respects local 
differences and enables competent local authorities find the most appropriate mobility solutions for 
their territory. In order to contribute t o strengthening the social cohesion, it is important to offer 
affordable, shared, high quality mobility choices to all citizens. 
The French Mobility Law: a balanced framework for the development of local 
MaaS solutions 
The Mobility Orientation Act adopted in France in 2019 establishes a good balance in the 
implementation of local MaaS solutions, notably by avoiding the fears of certain players with regard 
to new entrants and the risk of disintermediation. 
Completeness of the mobility offer on a territory in local MaaS solutions 
Article 28 of this law states that all sales channels for all mobility services and tariffs are opened to 
all MaaS providers (public or private), with different safeguard measures in order to suppo rt public 
policy objectives. MaaS solutions should not encourage any monopoly but aggregate all the mobility 
solutions on a territory to highlight the mobility offer and increase modal shift.  
 
 
 
 
Some obligations for the MaaS provider 
Reciprocity principle between all the actors is key to the implementation of coherent and efficient 
MaaS solutions. Regarding all the data that PTOs are asked to share in the Open Data and ITS 
directive, they have also the right to expect reciprocity from MaaS service providers. PTOs need to 
receive data in order to improve and adapt their services, optimizing existing ones or creating some 
new ones. MaaS pro -vider shall transmit to the PTO or provider of the services it sells and, where 
appropriate, to the  competent local authority, all the data necessary for statistical information on 
journeys made, for the after-sales service of the fare products sold and for the fight against fraud, 
including customer identification data collected by the multimodal digit al service. Reciprocity is 
expected in terms of the quality and frequency of data sharing. 
One of the key elements provided by the French Law is that MaaS providers must sell tickets and 
mobility services at the price offered by the original service provid er or the PTA and not at a lower 
price.  
Moovizy 2, the most integrated MaaS solution ever deployed in a French 
metropolis with public transport as a backbone 
On September 21st in the city of Saint -Etienne (Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes region, France), Transdev, 
through its subsidiaries Transdev Saint Etienne and Cityway officially launched Moovizy 2, a MaaS 
solution covering the whole Saint-Etienne metropolitan area, i.e. 53 municipalities and over 400 000 
people. 
Mobility-as-a-Service (MaaS) integrates passenger information, as well as the booking, payment and 
account management of all the different transport modes available throughout a territory. And all of 
this within a single mobile application: 
- 5 modes of transport that can be purchased through this application: public transport (bus 
and tramway), bike-sharing, taxi, car-sharing and car-pooling; 
- real-time passenger information available for every transport mode in  the area; real -time 
vehicle position available for buses and taxis; 
- real-time intermodal route search and information on vehicle accessibility for passengers 
with reduced mobility; 
- a crowdsourcing module providing information on the occupancy rate of vehicles, to comply 
with social distancing measures; 
- a single invoice at the end of the month, whether for immediate payment or post -payment 
(end of month) 
- The complete technical integration of our mobility partners gives local authorities real -time 
access to usage data. 
The base of this cooperation leans on the full governance owned by the PTA. The MaaS version of 
Moovizy was built since 2018, i.e. before the French Mobility Law was established in December 2019. 
The PTA in St -Etienne was the one in charge to c hoose the modes it wanted to plug in this MaaS 
solution regarding its environmental ambitions. 
One of the PTA leitmotiv is to encourage multimodality and developing active modes on its territory. 
Thanks to this full -integrated architecture proposed by Tran sdev, the PTA can monitor in real -time 
the data produced by the app and has the means to: 
- Understand the main tendencies; 
- Work with the MSP in order to develop and boost their offer; 
- Propose personal offer to citizen in order to change their mobility. 
- The data is owned by the PTA, who has a trusted third -party, can lead a MaaS project in a 
neutral way.

and shared mobility

mobility. But driving the development and the attractiveness of private autonomous vehicles could 
increase urban congestion by 20 to 30%. However, the development of auto nomous vehicles will 
only make sense if it is oriented towar ds shared mobility and if a utonomous mobility is integrated 
coherently within existing public transport systems.

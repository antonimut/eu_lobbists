The German Association of Private Health Insurers has 42 ordinary and 10 extraordinary 
members. Its member companies cover almost the entire private comprehensive and supple-
mentary health insurance market in Germany. 
 
The German Association of Private Health Insurers welcomes the European 
Commission’s plans for a European Health Data Space (EHDS). 
 
In addition to the following comments, we refer to the statement of the Ger-
man Insurance Association (GDV), which is fully supported.

The Association of Private Health Insurers welcomes the intended establishment of a Euro-
pean Health Data Space (EHDS). As part of the digitalisation of the health care system, we 
see the possibility of improving both the care structures and the medical care of patients. 
 
We would like to draw particular attention to the following aspects: 
 
1) Access to the European Health Data Space  
 
The present proposals to improve preparedness and response to health crises in the EU also 
pave the way for the participation of the European Medicines Agency (EMA) and the Euro-
pean Centre for Disease Prevention and Control (ECDC) in the future European Health Data 
Space infrastructure. The Commission foresees that Member States will grant data authori-
sations to allow research institutes, health institutions and public authorities to access the 
EHDS. The fundamental danger of data monopolies should be taken into account when de-
signing these access rights. 
 
In order to improve care for the insured and to be able to further expand prevention 
measures with a view to the digital infrastructure, private health insurers also need access to 
the European Health Data Space. In this way, the specific care structures can be maintained 
in the best possible way for the insured. In order to offer all contractually agreed benefits to 
their insured persons, private health insurers have to rely on being able to access relevant 
data. 
 
2) Data security and interoperability 
 
The European Health Data Space should enable access to health data within a trustworthy 
governance framework based on clear rules and should promote digital health services. The 
health insured should have control over their health data, confidence in transparent and le-
gally sound data processing and be able to share their data on a voluntary basis. As it is al-
ready the case with electronic patient records in some Member States, the Commission 
should develop a uniform data basis with quality criteria.  This requires technical and seman-
tic interoperability between the different infrastructures and IT systems.

5GAA Reply to public consultation on the revision of the Directive on 
intelligent transport systems 
Question 10. In your view, what is the EU-added value of the ITS Directive in comparison 
with what could be achieved at Member States national and/or regional level activities? 
We consider that the EU policy on C-ITS over the past years was counterproductive as it 
antagonised part of the ITS ecosystem instead of encouraging deployments regardless of the 
technology used. For other priority actions under the ITS Directive, we consider EU action was 
“somewhat successful”. 
The EU needs a coherent innovation and investment-friendly regulatory framework to encourage 
and stimulate an efficient deployment and transition toward smarter and more su stainable 
mobility, powered by ITS. EU action is specifically needed to avoid a detrimental fragmentation 
into national regulatory frameworks. This would hamper the cross-border roll-out of ITS in 
Europe and jeopardise the scale of expected benefits in terms of road safety, emission reduction 
or reducing traffic congestion. 
Although national and regional deployment initiatives and investments are essential, making 
connected and automated transport a reality across Europe requires EU -level harmonisation and 
significant cooperation between the private and public sector, and between the different 
industrial sectors involved. Enhancing such cooperation should however not prevent or interfere 
with business decisions and commercial agreements. 
In particular, stakeholders’ role, both public and private, remains critical and should be duly 
taken into consideration when crafting the EU framework, as they remain the final decision -
makers on ITS deployment, based on the perceived business case, customer, and societal 
benefits. 
For instance, requirements must strike the right balance between maintaining continuity of 
services and interoperability at system-level while reflecting the diversity of existing technology 
solutions, and their planned evolution. Technological neutrality principle should be ensured to 
foster the deployment of the next generation of ITS services according to the technological 
evolution driven by the market.  
Question 12. Do you have any comment on these problems or other potential problems 
that should be considered?  
By having interoperable, EU -wide solutions rather than fragmented ones, there is a strong 
potential for network effects and efficiency gains. Individual ITS s ervices can be more easily 
integrated into combined service offerings, making the ITS market broader and more widely 
available. Providing legal certainty for OEM deployments by securing a level -playing field among 
existing technology solutions and a forward path for new technologies or services is a precondition 
to speeding up large scale deployments and, hence, realising the full potential of connected and 
automated mobility. 
However, we must collectively acknowledge the fast pace of innovation, technology evolution and 
its impact on product cycles. Today already (and tomorrow even more so), different V2X 
technologies co -exist in parallel with other technologies e.g., sensor -based safety systems. 
Therefore, the revised ITS rules should focus on achieving sa fety at system -level and not

Question 15. Please elaborate on your answer to the previous question. Do you consider 
that any priority areas for ITS should be changed, removed or added? 
Current priority areas as defined by Article 2 of Directive 2010/40/EC seem to be still valid. We 
would need further precisions as regards the intended scope and related actions envisaged for 
new priority areas s uch as “Connected and automated mobility” or “Enhanced traffic 
management” but generally positive if the intent is to have dedicated priority areas addressing 
more specifically on future advanced ITS services enabled by technology evolution.  
The ITS Directive would benefit from leveraging the digital transformation going on cross private 
and public sectors at global scale, and thereby enable different mobility service domains to be 
deployed on common technologies and business models resulting in a shorter T ime-To-Effect on 
safety, efficiency and environmental goals. 
We note that the terminology “Connected and automated mobility” is very broad and overlap with 
existing priority areas as well as embracing other regulatory areas e.g., GSR. We are positive yet 
neutral as regards the inclusion of a dedicated priority area for Mobility platforms (e.g., Mobility 
as a Service - MaaS) and leave it to the appreciation of stakeholders directly involved in MaaS 
deployment. Overall, the need for any prescriptive requireme nts should be carefully assessed to 
avoid being counterproductive for ITS deployment.

Question 19. Do you have any comment on these impacts or other potential impacts (not 
mentioned above) of the possible actions? 
As stated in question 17, although we gene rally support EU action in the field of ITS to achieve 
societal objectives of a safer, greener, less congested, and more integrated transport system, we 
cannot prejudge at this stage on the likely impact of the envisaged measures without having clarity 
on what these will be.  It would be useful if the Directive includes a set of objective KPIs (e.g. 
decreased incidents for safety, reduced pollution levels for environment, increased throughput 
for traffic efficiency etc) in order to better assess impacts. 
We demonstrated in a recent study conducted by TNO that V2X can truly have a positive impact in 
terms of emission reduction, by reducing congestion and making traffic more efficient but also by 
powering eco-driving services. 
See: https://5gaa.org/news/environmental-benefits-of-c-v2x/ 
On the last statement on the “increased sharing of personal data”, we consider the belief that ITS 
services or devices must necessarily rely on personal data to be incorrect. Intelligent transport 
systems can be designed to avoid relying on “personal” data, and when impossible to avoid, ensure 
privacy by design.

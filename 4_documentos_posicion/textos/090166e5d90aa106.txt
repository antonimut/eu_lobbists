 
   
 
 
 
CNUE Contribution to the Inception Impact Assessment on cross-border digitalisation of judicial 
cooperation 
 
The Council of the Notariats of the European Union (CNUE) has taken due note of the publication 
of the Communication on Digitalisation of Justice in the European Union by the European 
Commission on 2 December 2020 and, more recently, of the Inception Impact Assessment on 
digitalisation of cross-border judicial cooperation.  
The CNUE is convinced that digitalisation can contribute to offer a better s ervice to the citizen, for 
example in the field of company law (incorporation of companies online, meetings by 
videoconference). Citizens and businesses should have a simple and effective access to justice and 
the Notaries of Europe support this approach.  
Notaries all over Europe are actively developing technologies to simplify the secure 
communication between the notaries, public registers and clients via electronic channels and to 
provide their clients with effective solutions for the archiving of their documents and for smooth 
and secure  access to documents. The cross -border context is having an increasing impact on 
notarial activity. 
The Notaries of Europe agree with the findings of the Inception Impact Assessment to the extent 
that digitalisation is key and that some issues still have to be tackled in order to ensure that cross -
border exchanges can be carried out safely and securely by digital means.   
The CNUE believes that it would be important to ensure recognition of a signature delivered by a 
notary in the issuing Member State in the receiving Member State. 
The further development of the electronic signature, its use by the notarial profession and the 
secure cross-border online data exchange between notaries constitutes one of the priorities of the 
Hungarian CNUE Presidency 2021. Work at CNUE level is underway. With the Bartolus platform, 
the European notariat has gained experience in the recognition of electronic signatures. 
CNUE believes that the creation of a new section on the e -justice portal de dicated to the 
digitalisation of cross-border judicial cooperation would facilitate the access to information for the 
EU citizens and law practitioners. Information such as the list of providers of the digital 
certification services for the qualified elect ronic signature and documents mandatory in the 
national legislation for each service provider in the field of certification (information concerning 
trust chain, timestamp) could facilitate the recognition of the electronic signature from the issuing 
member state to the receiving Member State.

Another important aspect of the future cross -border digitalisation of procedures is 
interoperability. According to the European notariat, interoperability should be the key approach, 
as it allows to respect national features and particularities. The CNUE welcomes the fact that the 
Inception Impact Assessment confirms the approach of interoperability between national systems 
rather than building complex European systems in governance and maintenance.  
The digital world  is constantly evolving. An overly strict regulatory framework can hinder 
innovation or problem solving. It is important that Member States and the legal professions can 
continue to build the most useful tools for their specific needs. 
As stressed in the E uropean Commission’s communication on Digitalisation of Justice in the 
European Union, access to information is also of utmost importance. The CNUE is in favour of 
continuing the interconnection of the registers of wills and European Certificates of Succes sion as 
set up by the ENRWA (www.arert.eu) which is of real interest to European citizens. 
The CNUE is currently assessing how blockchain technology could facilitate an interconnection of 
national registers of powers of attorney. Powers of attorney constit ute the type of notarial 
instrument which are the most used in cross-border relations.  
Finally, the further development of videoconferencing solutions and secure and reliable e -
identification procedures for both legal professional and the their  clients a re of utmost 
importance when it comes to the further promotion and the fostering of digitalisation tools in 
cross-border proceedings. The notary, in the context of his task of verifying the identity of the 
person appearing before him, must be able to check the identity documents of all EU citizens.  
The CNUE will follow closely the revision of the e-IDAS regulation in this respect.  
The CNUE requests that notaries should, in all cases where a strong identity check is required, 
have access to all the means a vailable to carry it out. This is in order to combat identity theft 
effectively. 
The notariat requests that notaries have access to the data contained in the chips in identity cards 
issued by the Member States of the European Union in accordance with the c riteria set out in  
Regulation EU 2019/1157.  
 
In addition, each citizen should be granted access, through its national legislation, to a digital 
certificate for the electronic qualified signature. This approach would lead to an increase of the 
security level in authentication procedures based on videoconference. 
 
Being involved in e -Codex since its beginning in 2010, the CNUE will continue to closely monitor 
the development of the e-Codex platform and assess the opportunities offered by e -Codex for the 
notarial profession.  
 
 
   
 
 
The European notariat cautiously welcomes the creation of multilingual online forms to enable 
remote procedures to be carried out. It calls for feasibility studies to be carried out, for legal 
professionals to be consulted beforehand,, for the advisory dimension to be preserved and for 
citizens to be able to have all the necessary safeguards so that procedures cannot be initiated 
against the will of the parties. 
 
At any time, is has to be guaranteed, that legal certainty and the quality of preventive legality 
control within the justice system as well as the reliability of public registers (such as business 
registers, land registers) will not be impaired by an enhanced digitalization of cross -border 
cooperation.  
Trust has to be transported to the digital world. The question of control of the technical solution is 
also crucial.  
As public officials, notaries manage sensitive data in their daily activity, data protection, 
confidentiality and high securities standards in the digital world mus t be guaranteed in the same 
way than in the “physical world”.  
The notariat as profession close to the citizens insists also on the importance not to exclude 
citizens who do not have Internet access or cannot use technologies for various reasons and make 
sure that these people will also  continue to benefit from an efficient access to justice. This aspect 
would also have to be made clear in a new horizontal instrument, if it was to be proposed.  
In this regard, the Commission should take into account the percentage of the EU population living 
in remote or rural areas, where access to internet or new technologies may not be easily achieved 
and where the drafting of documents on paper is necessary. Therefore, the EC should ensure that 
the standards set up in th e digitalisation process can be met by Member States, in order to allow 
all EU citizens to have equal access to justice.  
 
Finally, the CNUE intends to contribute actively to the legislation process and will submit a 
contribution to the public open consultation on digitalisation of cross-border judicial cooperation 
which is announced for Q1/2021.

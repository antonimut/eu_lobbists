Introduction 
AIJN is the representative association of the fruit juice industry in the EU since 1962. It defends 
the interests of the juice industry including the entire va lue chain and promotes the sector by 
engaging with the EU Institutions and other relevant stakeholders.  
 
AIJN is hereby providing preliminary comments to the European Commission Roadmap on 
revision of EU marketing standards , and in particu lar the Council Direc tive 2001/112/EC 
relating to fruit juices and certain similar products intended for human consumption (amended 
by Council Directive 2009/106/EC and Council Directive 2012/12/EU).  
 
1. AIJN is in favor of maintaining the current Council Directive 2001/112/EC 
The EU producers of fruit juices and nectars are strongly in favor of maintaining the Council 
Directive 2001/112/EC.  We find that the Directive is very much ‘fit for purpose’ as it guarantees 
the level-playing field for the sector and sets clear and strict product definitions for fruit juices 
produced within the EU. 
This Directive brought several benefits to the market, most importantly: 
➢ Creation of the a level playing field for all EU operators 
➢ Enhanced authenticity and quality requirements for fruit juices 
➢ Removal of barriers to intra-EU trade 
The industry, therefore, finds this Directive very relevant to the sector, bringing substantial 
added value to the manufacture of high-quality EU food products.

2. AIJN sees no need for revision of the Council Directive 2001/112/EC  
This Directive was amended in August 2009 in order to be adapted to technical progress taking 
account of developments in relevant international standards, in particular, the Codex Standard 
for fruit juices and nectars (Codex Stan 247-2005) and the AIJN Code of Practice for evaluation 
of quality and authenticity of juices. To bring provisions of the Directive even closer to the 
Codex Standard, but also to the current practices in Europe, another revision took place in a 
form of the Directive 2012/12/EU of the European Parliament and of the Council. AIJN 
supported all these revisions , and sees no further need for revision of essential elements of 
the Directive.  
 
3. AIJN calls for harmonized interpretation of a particular labelling provision 
concerning fruit juices  
When it comes to the implementation of the Directive, there is a particular aspect of 
interpretation of some provisions in connection with the Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011 on the 
provision of food information to consumers, that poses challenges in some Member States for 
which we would request an interpretative, explanatory guidance note providing a harmonized 
implementation of the provision.   
This interpretation issue was acknowledged in the European Commission 2019 report on 
Evaluation of Marketing Standards , carried out by  Agra CEAS Consulting , Areté, and 
Directorate-General for Agriculture and Rural Development, that assessed the effectiveness, 
efficiency, relevance, coherence and EU added value of marketing standards.1  
Essentially, the 2012 amendment of the Fruit Juice Directive introduced the provision that the 
addition of sugars to fruit juices was not (longer) allowed. Th is has been requested by the 
industry itself in order to have the common practice of not adding sugar to fruit juice turned into 
a legal requirement in the Directive. As a response to rising consumer awareness for healthy 
nutrition and specifically the role of (added) sugar therein, the juice industry felt at the time that 
the inclusion of the legal prohibition to add sugar to fruit juices in the Directive would reassure 
consumers that 100% fruit juice is a minimally processed product containing only naturally 
occurring sugars as well as vitamins, minerals, and phytonutrients, all of which come straight 
from the fruit from which it is squeezed.

Article 7.1.c of the Regulation 1169/2011 stipulates namely that “Food information shall not be 
misleading, particularly by suggesting that the food possesses special characteristics when in 
fact all similar foods possess such characteristics, in particular by specifically emphasizing the 
presence or absence of certain ingredients and/or nutrients ”. Applied to fruit juices this 
particular provision could be interpreted in a way that, putting on a fruit juice  pack the “no 
added sugar, in line with the legislation” statement  or similar , could be misleading to 
consumers since legally no fruit juice can contain added sugar. 
AIJN is of the opi nion that the guidance note providing a harmonized interpretation of the 
provision of art 7.1 c of Regulation 1169/2011, should specify that the scope of the term “all 
similar foods” should refer to the scope of the directive 2001/112/EC, meaning “ fruit juices and 
certain similar products intended for human consumption” for which, besides fruit juices, all 
other products under this scope could have added sugars. Hence the reference of “no added 
sugars in line with the legislation” would correctly indicate the true nature of fruit juices  
compared to all other products under the same scope of the directive. 
Recent surveys conducted in a  number of EU countries demonstrate a substantial lack of 
understanding by consumers as well as by nutritionists of fruit juice composition.2 This lack of 
awareness of the existing fruit juice legislation combined with the impossibility for juice bottlers 
to label that no sugars can be added has eroded consumer confidence in juices. The fact that 
competing beverages, such as juice containing drinks, are allowed to use the claim “with no 
added sugar”, adds to the confusion and results in unfair competition.  
 
There is clearly a need to better inform consumers about the absence of added sugars 
in fruit juices, and this problem is best solved at European level, assuring continuous 
harmonized application of this important Directive across the EU.

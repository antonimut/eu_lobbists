Driving times and rest periods in the private bus and passenger transport sector are regulated by EU Regulation 561/2006, which also regulates the subject matter in freight transport.  However, the two sectors differ substantially, mainly due to the peculiarity of passenger transport, which requires greater flexibility in the organization of work.  We therefore welcome the initiative of the European Commission and, in order to meet the organizational needs of businesses in the sector, we call for changes to Reg. UE 561/2006, introducing elements of greater flexibility in the organization of work in the bus transport sector which, as already said, has substantial differences with respect to freight transport.  Detailed proposals:  1) With regard to breaks (art.7 Reg. UE 561/2006) in the 4h30' period, we ask that they be at driver’s discretion (according to the needs of the transported clients) and very elastic. For example: 15 min. + 30 min. / or 30 min. + 15 min./ or 15 min. + 15 min. + 15 min.  2) On the driving period 4h30' + 4h30' + 1 hour (art. 6 Reg. UE 561/2006) we ask that the additional driving hour, over 9 hours, can be carried out with a break of 15 minutes and not of 45 minutes.  3) Provide for the reduction of rest from 9 to 8 hours just in case of daily urban tourist services.  4) With regard to tours at national level, we ask that the 12-day derogation, already provided for international tours, be applied. This allows the regular weekly rest period to be postponed after 12 24-hour of working periods (“12-day rule” - Article 8, paragraph 6a of EU Reg. 561/2006 has been amended by Article 29 of EU Reg. 1073/2009 by entering a derogation for drivers engaged in single international journeys, who can postpone their weekly rest period until the end of the twelfth day).  5) On driving at night – We ask to allow driving at night (from midnight to 7 am) for four consecutive hours without limiting the total hours of work shift to 10. Essentially, we ask for keeping the possibility of work shift at 13 hours.  6) In order to improve the environmental impact of vehicles, provide substantial incentives to encourage the renewal of the fleet, sometimes with targeted (electric) investments planned in certain areas (historic centers, natural parks).

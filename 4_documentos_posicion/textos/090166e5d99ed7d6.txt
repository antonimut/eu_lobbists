 
 
   
 
IRU feedback –European Commission inception impact assessment on  
the need for specific driving and rest time rules for drivers in occasional 
transport of passengers by bus or coach 
 
IRU, the voice of the commercial road transport sect or, welcomes the opportunity to  participate in the 
initiative of the European Commission (EC) inception impact assessment specifically relating to the 
“need for specific driving and rest time rules for drivers in occasional transport of passengers”. 
 
The social provisions under the Mobility Package 1 1 currently make no distinction between the driving 
and rest time in the case of drivers for goods transport and for passenger transport.  
 
We would like to state from the very beginning that the passenger component of coach travel creates a 
distinct operational and s ervice environment, including own driving patterns, different from the freight 
sector. The table below  provides comparative examples of key differences between the transport of 
passenger and goods. 
 
Transport of goods  Occasional coach/bus services 
Fundamental requirement: timely point to point 
delivery (from point A to point B) during the year.  
High seasonality and trips (e.g., summer holiday 
trips, ski holiday trips) and variation of time and 
length of trips (e.g., weekend trips, weekday trips, 
night trips, day-long trips, or week-long trips). 
 
A typical coach journey incorporates a number of 
places for embarking and dise mbarking, for 
stopping and for resting. These types of journeys 
are normally conducted as touristic, study or 
other types of group trips. At the beginning and at 
the end of these trips, the distances covered 
without stopping are usually longer.  
Drivers o f goods can have a constant driving 
pattern as they are not confronted with 
customised routes and personal demands of 
passengers. For example, goods may remain 
loaded on a lorry or in a parking area over night, 
even where there are less than 10 km left to the 
destination.  
In occasional passenger transport, times for 
departure and arrival, for visits and meals are set 
from the beginning. If there are delays or changes 
in the route or visits, passengers require detailed 
information or compensation. Weather can affect 
the plan, and one destination can be replaced by 
another to adapt . Passengers have needs that 
goods can never have (for example toilet visits, 
rests and requests to take photos). Flexibility is 
extremely important in this type of people’s 
service. 
Dissatisfied passengers can increase the stress 
level for drivers. Refusing the flexibility required 
by passengers because the driver has to comply 
with strict rules can have a negative effect on the 
work of the drivers and on their attempts to 
remain focused. In these types of situations, 
regulations that are intended to promote safety in 
road traffic are instead counter - productive, and 
increase the pressure and stress on the driver.

The ratio between hours driven and the daily 
spread-over shows a clear dominance of hours 
driven.  This is because, in the transport of goods, 
typically the driver spends the majority of his 
working time driving.  
The ratio between hours driven and the dai ly 
spread-over is lower in the passenger sector than 
in the freight segment because the number of 
hours actually spent on driving is limited in 
comparison with the duty time  (e.g., time spent 
waiting for tourists to visit museums etc).  
 
The absence of se parate rules for occasional transport of passenger by bus or coach will harmfully 
impact drivers’ working conditions, road safety (reduced risk of road accidents, reduced driver fatigue 
and improved health), the costs and profit margins of the undertakings  (namely small and medium 
companies), and the industry’s competitiveness compared to other sectors of the economy as the quality 
services are not offered to passengers.  
  
Due to the fundamental difference between the work of passenger transport drivers and goods transport 
drivers, we foresee the need for specific derogations for drivers in occasional transport of passengers 
by bus and coach . This should take into account the specificity and driving patterns of the passenger 
transport business, at the same time guaranteeing road safety and the well-being of drivers.  
 
IRU welcomes the initiative of the EC and happy to engage further to develop necessary rules that are 
suitable to drivers in occasional transport of passengers by bus and coach.

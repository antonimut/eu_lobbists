Additional information submitted by the Instituto Internacional de 
Derecho y Medio Ambiente (IIDMA-Spain) for the purpose of the IED 
review public consultation process 
 
According to the findings of the IED evaluation undertaken in 2020, public participation in 
permitting procedures has been identified as one of the key areas where further revision and 
improvement is needed. In particular, it is essential to widen the scope of public participation 
under the IED  (article 24)  to all permitting proc edures, including those aimed for the 
reconsideration or update of the permit conditions at industrial installations. 
In this regard, in order to reinforce public participation obligations within the IED, it is highly 
advisable to take into consideration  the Findings and Recommendations with regard to 
communication ACCC/C/2014/121 concerning compliance by the Euro pean Union , 
adopted by the Aarhus Convention Compliance Committee  (ACCC) on 30 March 2020  
(available at https://unece.org/sites/default/files/2020-12/ece.mp_.pp_.c.1.2020.8.e.pdf).  
In this case the ACCC has found the EU not compliant with Article 6(10) obligations on public 
participation under the IED  by failing to provide  any possibility for public participation in 
relation to reconsiderations and updates under article 21 (3), (4), (5)(b) and (5)(c) of the IED. 
Consequently, the ACCC has recommended the EU to “put in a place a legally binding 
framework to ensure that, when  a public authority in a Member State of the Party concerned 
reconsiders or updates permit conditions pursuant to national laws implementing article 21(3), 
(4), and (5)(b) and (c) of the IED, or the corresponding provisions of any legislation that 
supersedes the IED, the provisions of article 6(2) -(9) will be applied, mutatis mutandis and 
where appropriate, bearing in mind the objectives of the Convention”.  
Based on the above and taking into account that the EU is a Party to the Aarhus Convention, it 
is crucial that the IED revision process takes fully into consideration the findings of the ACCC 
in the referred case in order to align the IED provisions with Article 6 of the Aarhus Convention 
by reviewing and amending the content of article 24 of the Directive.

TURBODEN COMPANY PROFILE 
 
Turboden, a Mitsubishi Heavy Industries group company, is an Italian firm and a global leader in the  design, 
manufacture and maintenance of Organic Rankine Cycle (ORC) systems, highly suitable for distributed genera-
tion.  
With more than 400 plants in operation worldwide, Turboden has a proven experience in renewables (biomass, 
geothermal, waste-to-energy, concentrated solar power) and energy efficiency (waste heat recovery in industrial 
processes, oil and gas and power generation, combined heat and power and steam). Today Turboden broaden 
its solutions portfolio with gas expanders and large heat pumps to contribute to the worldwide efforts to miti-
gate global warming by creating reliable and clean energy systems. 
 
 
REGULATORY PROPOSAL 
 
Turboden S.p.A. welcomes the publication of  combined evaluation roadmap/inception impact assessment -
Hydrogen and Gas markets Decarbonisation Package, which represents an improvement of this sector in the 
European Energy System.  
Aiming at avoiding the distortive effects of uncoordinated, fragmented policy initiatives (such as Energy Effi-
ciency Directive, Renewable Energy Directive, TEN -E regulation and Strategy for Energy System Integration) 
and in order to facilitate the production of renewable gases, here below are listed proposals for the revision 
of Gas Directive and Regulation: 
 
a) consent transportation and distribution companies to produce/sell electricity when it derives from en-
ergy efficiency projects within their facilities (according to the EED 2012/27/EU and amended 2018/2002 
- art. 15 par. 4); 
b) include sustainability criteria in order to decarbonized gas network (according to Proposal for TEN-E reg-
ulation); 
c) Include as mandatory the energy efficiency projects in gas distribution tenders (e.g. waste heat recovery 
from gas compressor station or  industrial processes and gas expanders), according to Energy Efficiency 
first principle.

TECHNOLOGY OVERVIEW 
 
 
1. Waste Heat Recovery from gas compressor station 
 
Turboden ORC technology, thanks to the use of specific organic working fluids, permits an efficient exploitation 
of exhaust gas streams, such as small-to-medium size gas turbines in gas compressor stations. Depending on 
the characteristics of each specific project, i.e. number of gas turbines available for heat recovery, possible lay-
out constrains, water availability on site etc., the ORC solution is designed to best fit with such characteristics.  
Power, produced by the plant, can be exported to the grid or used for internal consumption, especially electrical 
motor-driven compressors.

2. Waste Heat Recovery from energy intensive industries processes 
Turboden ORC units can produce electricity by recovering heat from industrial processes and in combined 
cycles with reciprocating engines and gas turbines. The power of Turboden turbo generators in this applica-
tion generally ranges up to 20 MW electric per single shaft. 
In heat recovery applications, Turboden ORC units can be a valuable way to convert residual, low-grade heat 
into useful power in production processes, such as cement, glass, steel, other ferrous and non- ferrous metals.

3. Gas explander 
 
In view of the important role of the Natural Gas (NG) distribution network and its high potential in terms of 
efficiency improvement, Turboden recently developed the Gas Expander (GEX).  GEX is a solution to enhance 
the energy efficiency of a natural gas network infrastructure, producing electricity by taking advantage of the 
reduction of gas pressure from the delivery level to the one required by users (residential as well as industrial). 
The basic concept of a GEX is to use the energy that would otherwise be lost through throttling valves: the 
pressure drop becomes the “driving force” to produce electrical power.  
In order to avoid natural gas freezing issues at the end of the expansion, the GEX requires a thermal input, 
before expansion. This thermal input can be provided exploiting waste heat available (e.g. in gas intensive in-
dustries where waste heat is most likely present) or burning a small amount of natural gas, for example in case 
of GEX applied to city gates where waste heat is not available. It shall be noted that even in cases where some 
natural gas consumption is required, GEX is capable to produce electric power in order of 150 kWe -2MWe 
with an efficiency, respect to the thermal input, of 65+%.  Thus, GEX efficiency can be compared to the effi-
ciency of a combined cycle power plant (CCPP) of more than one GW of power; therefore, the GEX efficiency 
represents an outstanding potential being a small-scale energy production system rather than a large power 
plant.

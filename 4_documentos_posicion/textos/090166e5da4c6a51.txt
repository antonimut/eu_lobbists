     Hydrogen and Gas markets Decarbonisation Package 
 
 
The CFE Energies, a french union federation of employees in the power and gas sectors, 
welcomes the European initiative for a public consultation on Hydrogen and Gas markets 
Decarbonisation Package. This reflects the European Union (EU) willingness to be the driving 
force of the international community in the fight against climate change. From the CFE Energies 
point of view, the EU has to play a key role in promoting and putting in place policy frameworks 
and legislative measures that will contribute towards the greenhouse gas emissions (GHG) 
reduction and reaching carbon neutrality by 2050. 
 
CFE Energies is convinced that clean hydrogen is a key driver for achieving deep decarbonisation 
in an affordable and effective manner through an integrated sectoral approach using both the 
electricity, gas and heat infrastructures, considering clean means carbon free and guided by low 
carbon technological neutrality. As a clean energy carrier, hydrogen represents an efficient 
solution for many areas such as transport, heating and energy-intensive industries as well.  
 
While gaseous fuels will still represent 20 % of EU total energy consumption in 2050 from 22 % 
today, the gas infrastructures will be transporting decreasing quantities of natural gas, making 
way for increasing quantities of renewable and decarbonised gases. Hence, CFE Energies 
believes that clean hydrogen and gas integration provide an opportunity to reduce the carbon 
footprint and ease the energy transition towards a decarbonisation through the emergence of 
European industrial players providing then beneficial impacts on the economic and social stakes. 
We should keep in mind that gas infrastructures are offering a key role to promote hydrogen 
technologies through conversion of gas grids to hydrogen and renewable gas (biogas) as it can 
be achieved via central and decentralised actions. This combination (hydrogen and biogas) 
provide an efficient storage and flexibility solution to the European energy system. In that 
respect, CFE Energies recommends to work on legal and administrative barriers which hinder 
the injection of hydrogen into the gas grid. In that regard, a full assessment is essential to define 
the pricing principles covering connection fees/charges and remuneration for hydrogen 
supplied/injected and the acceptable safety and operational threshold of end-user appliances. 
 
Even if the production costs of renewable energies have significantly reduced, the level of 
investments in hydrogen infrastructure (electrolyser, storage, etc.) and the cost of electricity are 
such that renewable hydrogen production is not yet competitive. CFE Energies believes that 
some measures have to be implemented to accelerate the deployment of power to gas, to 
decarbonise the gas grid sector and facilitate hydrogen penetration in energy extensive industry. 
Since the hydrogen infrastructures are depreciated over a long-term period, mechanisms such 
as Feed-in-Tariffs should be investigated through call for biding in order to enable the take-off 
of renewable/low-carbon hydrogen and for promoting visibility, securing pay out time of any 
industrial investments while taking into account the challenges of competitiveness and 
purchasing power of end users. CFE Energies believes that Feed-in-Tariffs can be adjusted as 
long as cost of hydrogen infrastructures production decreases and CO2 prices evolve.  
 
CFE Energies would like to stress that European gas infrastructure is well spread and placed to 
play the role of hydrogen and gas market decarbonisation. Today, with 2,2 million kilometers of 
gas pipelines and 1,200 TWh of underground storage capacity, these infrastructures represent 
a valuable and essential asset. As the production of renewable gas in a decentralised way is 
forecasted with the coupling between electricity, hydrogen and gas networks, CFE Energies 
believes that it might require gas network operators to better master gas quality across the 
network, to ensure interoperability, while supporting a wide integration of renewable gas in the 
network. The gas TSOs and respectively ENTSOG are better qualified to manage the

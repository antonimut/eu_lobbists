OMV position  
on the  
EC Roadmap 
Decarbonisation Package revision on EU rules on market access  
Status: 08.03.2021 
► OMV is committed to the objective set by  the Paris Agreement. The very ambitious climate targets discussed 
on European level in the context of the European Green Deal require speeding up the decision-making process 
by the policy makers. 
► As the energy transition lacks appropriate gas -regulation, we welcome the planned decarbonisation package. 
It shall support the energy transition at least cost with focus on the role of investors, infrastructure and markets 
in ensuring secure, affordable and sustainable energy supply in the EU.  
► The EU should create a framework that provides certainty to investors in order to create momentum for the 
decarbonisation of the gas system. This framework should include cross-sectorial incentives for renewable, 
decarbonised and low-carbon gas supply and demand. 
► EU decision makers should bear in mind the strategic role of gas infrastructure for security of supply, system 
resilience and the ability to support the system-wide energy transition in the most cost-effective way. 
► We support the objective to include hydrogen issues into the gas legislative framework.  
► In order to safeguard the achievement of the well-functioning gas market, rules governing the future hydrogen 
markets shall be included in a joint legislative framework with the aim to even increase  the functionality of this 
market and the resilience of the system. 
► It is necessary to enable infrastructure-operators to develop and operate gas infrastructure for hydrogen and 
CO2 matching the decarbonisation of the gas sector. Infrastructure-operators involvement should not result in 
crowding-out of market initiatives and shall be in compliance with the key unbundling principles. 
► Hydrogen will be a fundamental element of the future European energy mix. Therefore, as soon as third party 
access is a relevant issue, it should be regulated in each of its phases.   
► It would be important to specify Europe -wide minimum requirements and consequently the readiness of the 
grid for a percentage of hydrogen (not just an upper limit value).  
► As already mentioned, energy transition lacks appropriate gas -regulation. Hence, the existing regulation shall 
be adapted in order to clarify the situation of pure H2 network or P2G facilities not connected to the gas grid.  
► It is necessary to e stablish a credible certification scheme to value life cycle GHG intensity reduction of 
renewable and low-carbon gases. We ask for a European terminology for renewable and low-carbon gases in 
order to be able to develop a robust market. 
► Any willing and competent market participant, including infrastructure-operators, should be allowed to operate 
P2G facilities, as long as compliance with the basic rules of non-discrimination is ensured.  
► We see the need for a variety of low-carbon and renewable hydrogen, including hydrogen produced  from 
natural gas to support the energy transition . The regulatory framework should have the flexibility to deal with 
the various stages of the development in the hydrogen market. Private initiatives shall be mobilized. We expect 
fair legal treatment of existing businesses and new regulated activities. 
► A further important topic is gas quality. It is necessary to avoid undue gas quality restrictions at system entry 
points. It is important to maintain the capacity of the transportation system to blend and co -mingle gas of 
different sources, and allow indigenous gas production to enter the system as well as renewable and low -
carbon gases. 
► It should be taken into account that transmission, storage and flexibility in the gas sector is highly cost-efficient. 
Coordinated infrastructure planning can capture synergies by linking the gas and electricity sectors in order to 
achieve a low-emission and competitive European economy.

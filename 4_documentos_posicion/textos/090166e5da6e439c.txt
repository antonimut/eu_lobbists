Introduction 
EEX welcomes the Commission´s initial impact assessment / roadmap for the hydrogen and gas 
markets decarbonization package . Scaling up markets for clean hydrogen affects many dimensions 
that require coordinated European answers and framework conditions. We  are convinced energy 
trading markets are enablers of a hydrogen economy helping to provide a broad access to the energy 
carrier to diverse actors from all sectors. Also, t rading markets for decarbonized gases offer 
producers an income option beyond subsid ies and should play a significant role in the gas package. 
In our response to DG ENER´s consultation on the IIA/roadmap, we line out why we believe so  and 
what we consider necessary as a regulatory framework.  
 
Commodity markets are based on non-discriminatory grid access 
Non-discriminatory access to networks  at transmission and distribution level is an important 
cornerstone of competitive energy markets  and will also be essential for the creation of traded 
hydrogen markets and of traded markets for decarbonized gases in general. Though transportation in 
pipelines is the preferred option from an efficiency point of view, also alternative transport vectors 
such as trains and trucks are to be considered  as they may help to integrate smaller prod uction 
facilities and connect clusters. It is important to ensure harmoni zed European requirements for 
network access on a non-discriminatory basis.  
In the case of a hydrogen network, EEX supports early EU-wide regulatory standards to ensure third-
party a ccess for new pipelines and a predictable pathway for the transition of existing hydrogen 
pipelines. Non-discriminatory third -party access to the network,  storage and LNG -facilities, 
transparent and non-discriminatory tariffs must be ensured . The principle  of unbundling must be 
respected. If dynamic regulatory approaches, or sandboxes, are considered for some aspects, close 
monitoring by national regulatory authorities and ACER is key to guarantee a level playing field for all 
actors.  
 
Blending hydrogen into natural gas networks  will help to make sure the natural gas grid is used 
efficiently, but should be based on established principles and rules applicable to network operators 
and users  and decided in cooperation with consumers . For this purpose, standardization of gas / 
hydrogen qualities and stable blending ratios are essential to protect sensitive systems and 
appliances, while recognizing technical limitations and regional differences in blending capabilities. 
To enable interoperability and EU wide trad ing these should be harmonized as far as reasonably 
possible. 
 
European network planning and development for hydrogen pipelines  should take place in a 
coordinated manner in order to benefit from the integration between electricity, natural gas plus other 
forms of methane and hydrogen markets and networks. EU rules for hydrogen network planning are 
necessary to facilitate this , including potential alternative transport vectors . A mandatory integration

The role of markets for hydrogen and decarbonized gases 
Energy trading markets for decarbonized gases and hydrogen provide transparency on prices and 
volumes, they offer a market-based income-option for producers and integrate different actors from 
different sectors.  
Based on a supporting regulatory framework building upon Europe´s unique experience in organizing 
liberalized energy markets, transparent hydrogen trading markets can attract international hydrogen 
trading interest. Europe is to become the international hub for clean hydrogen trading, and by 
that, strengthening the role of the Euro in global commodity markets. 
To support the market development of hydrogen, EEX has initiated the EEX Hydrogen Working 
Group. The group´s aim is to gather intelligence for product development for early trading markets 
supporting the scale up process, but also for mature hydrogen markets. The group consists of around 
50 companies from hydrogen production, consumption, banks, regulators and associations. 
EEX believes the following prerequisites need to be met to enable trading markets for decarbonized 
gases to unfold: 
- It is important that any support schemes provide incentives for market participation of 
both supply and demand. This is necessary for trustworthy and representative market price 
signals and  reduces the risk of long -lasting dependency on subsidy schemes. While 
competitive allocation mechanisms (‘competition for the market’ as referred to in the 
roadmap) are importan t to allocate any support, clear timetables for the phase -out of 
subsidies are necessary. 
 
- The trading of Guarantees of Origin (GOs), reflecting the climate value of gases, should 
take place on dedicated markets  separately from the commodity trading. Estab lishing 
European standards for decarbonized gases and hydrogen quality and a European GO 
system are important factors to develop a traded market that can take into account the 
different carbon footprint of the gases. 
 
- Long-term bilateral contracts may lead  to market foreclosure, as has been learned from 
experience with gas and power market liberalization. Therefore, long -term contracts should 
only cover a part of the production and consumption respectively of two contract parties. This 
will incentivize market parties to actively participate in the markets and will help to build up 
liquidity on traded markets for decarbonized gases. 
 
- Harmonized standards and metrics allow for cross -border trading of decarbonized 
gases, covering comparable grid standards, harmonized balancing regimes and market

About EEX 
EEX is the leading energy exchange in Europe, which develops, operates  and connects secure, 
liquid and transparent markets for energy and related products. As part of EEX Group, a group of 
companies serving international commodity markets, EEX offers contracts on Power, Natural Gas 
and Emission Allowances as well as Freight and Agricultural Products. EEX also provides registry 
services for White Certificates, Capacity Certificates and Guarantees of Origin (GO) on behalf of the 
French State, as well as Auctions for Guarantees of Origin. EEX Group provides further services to 
GO-registries via Grexel. EEX is the largest electricity exchange, the second largest CO 2 exchange 
and third largest gas exchange in the world.

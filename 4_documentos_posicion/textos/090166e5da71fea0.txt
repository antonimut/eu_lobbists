Key priorities for the gas decarbonisation package 
Eurogas is committed to achieving the objectives of the Paris Agreement. We support the EU’s 
aim to reach climate neutrality by 2050 and the intermediary target of reducing GHG emissions 
by at least 55% by 2030. The speed with which change must happen to m eet these objectives 
requires policy makers to take decisive action and acknowledge the role of the gas sector in 
achieving these goals.  
Eurogas welcomes the Gas Package IIA as an important step in the development of a suitable and 
forward-looking policy  framework for gaseous fuels that delivers an energy mix that is 
environmentally, socially, and economically sustainable. 
Meeting the EU’s ambitious climate and energy targets in the most cost-effective way requires a 
multi-vectored approach across all sectors. It needs to build on an integrated energy system, that 
delivers energy efficiency improvements, growing shares of renewables and the progressive, cost-
effective decarbonisation of the electricity and gas sector using all available technologies and with 
targeted support.  
The scenarios used by the Climate Target Plan IA and the Eurogas study by DNV GL confirm the 
role that the gas sector will have to play in the EU’s decarbonisation efforts, as both foresee a 
strong role for gas in final energy consu mption in 2050. Gas consumption will be gradually 
decarbonised through growing shares of renewable, decarbonised and low -carbon gases as well 
as use of CCUS. 
The revision must consider the role of natural gas as an enabler of the transition. Switching from 
coal and oil to gas can deliver significant emissions reductions. SSLNG for instance can support the 
reduction of CO2 and other pollutants in the transport sector. 
A modern regulatory framework that is fit for purpose to support the transition to a cost efficient 
and carbon -neutral energy system, should be built on three pillars focusing on the role of 
investors, infrastructure, and markets. It should confirm the key regulatory principles of 
unbundling, third-party access, transparency, non -discrimination and monitoring of the market. 
It should allow a gradual approach ensuring a level playing field for the technologies to be selected 
in the most effective ways. 
Investors: EU policies should create a stable policy and regulatory framework that provides 
direction and predictability to investors to create momentum for the decarbonisation of the gas 
system. This should include cross -sectorial incentives for renewable, decarbonised and low -
carbon gas supply and demand. This will need to be consistent with other policy instruments such 
as the RED, ETD, the EU ETS, ESR, EEAG as well as the Sustainable Finance framework. 
Infrastructure: EU policies should leverage the strategic role of gas infrastructure for security of 
supply, system resilience and ability to s upport the system -wide energy transition in the most 
cost-effective way. Energy system integration and a move to a more decentralised energy system 
requires that adequate consideration is given to distribution infrastructure and connected end -
users. The decarbonisation of the energy system cannot be tackled by any single energy vector, 
as this would be uneconomical and lead to higher costs along the value chain. Both electrons, heat 
and molecules will be needed to achieve carbon -neutrality while guaranteein g secure and 
affordable supply of energy.

Markets: effective market design should ensure a well -functioning, competitive and integrated 
internal energy market that creates value for customers and security of supply as well as 
promoting the integration and trading of renewable, decarbonised and low -carbon gases. The 
dual challenge for policy makers will be to set a framework for the transitioning to an increasing 
share of renewable, decarbonised and low -carbon gases while ensuring the continued stability 
and interoperability of the internal energy market to safeguard the vital role of gas in supporting 
a competitive EU industry and providing affordable energy to consumer.

Eurogas feedback on specific points raised in the Inception Impact Assessment 
1. We welcome the explicit recognition that reforms should enable fair competition between 
electrification, energy efficiency, and renewable and low -carbon gases like hydrogen , 
synthetic methane and bio -methane, as well as technologies such as P2G facilities (e.g. 
electrolyser or pyrolysis) or CCUS technologies. Rather than simply picking winners and losers 
ex-ante, reforms should be properly impact assessed with a particular attention to cost -
effectiveness across the entire value chain.  Eurogas notes in this regard that: 
a. Targeted support is wa rranted to help commercially immature technologies. 
Renewable, decarbonised and low -carbon gases are at the early stage and an 
important cost reduction can be achieved through scale -up, with the right level of

public support, as was the case in kick -starting the renewable electricity sector. 
Ensuring that customers can choose among the most efficient and cost -effective 
technology that suits their specific requirements. This is in line with Eurogas 
recommendation to introduce gas sector specific decarbonisation targets as outlined 
in point 4 below.  
b. The benefits of renewable, decarbonised and low-carbon gas for the system may be 
much higher than a Levelized Cost of Energy (LCOE) comparison may suggest, as such 
an approach underestimates the integration costs of intermittent energy sources and 
does not consider   positive externalities, such as: 
i. the ability of gaseous fuels to provide seasonal flexibility, storability and 
security of supply, 
ii. The ability of anaerobic digestion and pyrogasification to help reduce 
emissions from hard to abate sectors such as the waste and agricultural 
sector, 
iii. The ability of gaseous fuels and their infrastructure to facilitate the 
integration of renewable energy, especially in regions with strong seasonable 
variation of renewable electricity generation. 
2. We welcome the clear commitment to competitive markets to drive decarbonisation 
technologies. Eurogas agrees that the regulatory framework should allow for increasi ng 
liquidity, competition, and consumer protection to accompany the deployment of 
decarbonised and low-carbon gases. 
3. We share the view that parts of the regulatory framework for gas markets need to be aligned 
with the recently adopted Clean Energy Package. We outline however that the assessment 
about which parts of the regulatory framework to mirror should consider the specific 
circumstances of the gas market as outlined in Eurogas assessment on ‘mirroring’.  
4. We fully support t he view that an efficient and sustainable development of renewable, 
decarbonised and low-carbon gases may require, among others, regulatory incentives for 
production and/or consumption. The decarbonisation of the gas sector must start without 
delay through targeted action. The EU cannot afford to wait until appropriate carbon pricing 
signals are in place and start deliver adequate investment signals to end-users. The scenarios 
of the 2030 Climate Target Plan Impact Assessment project 2030 ETS carbon prices ranging 
for 32 to 65 EUR, far from the level required to substitute natural gas, which start on average 
at 180 EUR for domestic low-carbon hydrogen and 205 EUR for domestic renewable hydrogen 
(energy switching costs, modification of equipment not included - Cost-effective 
decarbonisation study, Florence School of Regulation 2020 ). With regard to the forthcoming 
Impact Assessment, Eurogas would welcome however that it properly addresses the question 
of priority sectors identified ex-ante by the European Commission for hydrogen consumption. 
The IIA notes that:  
“The areas where decarbonised gaseous fuels are expected to come into play include today’s 
energy-intensive industry (e.g., refineries, fertilisers, steel making) and certain heavy duty

With regard to the problems the initiative aims to tackle, Eurogas regrets that the question

existing EU decarbonisation efforts, such as the Renewable Energy Directive, the Energy

Regulation.  
The forthcoming Impact Assessment should therefore also assess a less prescriptive approach 
to promote the production and demand for renewable, decarbonised and low-carbon gases. 
This should therefore include the option of the introduction of a binding 2030 EU-level target 
to lower the greenhouse gas intensity of gas consumed an d increase the demand for 
renewable, decarbonised and low-carbon gas in Europe, as proposed jointly by Eurogas and 
the European Biogas Association: 
a. EU level greenhouse gas intensity reduction target . Eurogas calls for a binding EU 
target on gas demand for the reduction of the GHG intensity of gas. By 2030 the GHG 
intensity of the gaseous energy [i] consumed [ii] in the EU shall be reduced by at least 
20% compared to 2018 levels, through the use of renewable and decarbonized gases. 
To be eligible, each renew able and decarbonized gas typology production pathway 
must comply with appropriate sustainability and emission reduction criteria in line 
with RED II and future European legislation. Eligible gas typologies account for the 
2030 EU binding target for the reduction of the GHG intensity of gas in proportion to 
its greenhouse gas emissions savings (Life Cycle, including negative emissions). In 
order to avoid a disproportionate administrative burden, a list of greenhouse gas 
emissions savings (Life Cycle) should  be laid down for common gas typologies 
production pathways and that list should be updated and expanded when further 
reliable data are available. Producers shall always be entitled to demonstrate that the 
level of greenhouse gas emissions savings of their production process are higher than 
those that were assumed when calculating the default values.  
[i] includes liquid methane and hydrogen 
[ii] includes gas consumption as feedstock for industrial processes

supports the introduction in European legislation (e.g. revision of the Renewable

least 11% in terms of energy content of gas consumed [i].  Renewable gases 
contributing to the “EU level renewable target” account at the same time also for the 
“EU level greenhouse gas intensity reduction target” in line with the eligibility criteria 
set out for “EU level greenhouse gas intensity reduction target”. 
[i] includes liquid methane and hydrogen 
c. Design of the greenhouse gas intensity reduction target and renewable gas target .

5. We welcome the Commission’s intention to ensure that the Renewable Energy Directive 
contributes to the further development and promotion of renewable, decarbonised and low 
carbon gases. This should cover all gases, including hydrogen, hydrogen derivatives, such as 
synthetic methane as well as biomethane.  However, as noted above, promoting the 
production and demand for renewable, decarbonised and low -carbon gases should also be 
explicitly addressed in the revision of the gas regulatory framework. In this respect a common 
terminology via clear, accurate and science-bases definition of renewable, decarbonised, and 
low-carbon gases is necessary , building on existing proposals by the gas industry presented 
during the 33rd Madrid Forum. Overall, the framework should aim at reducing of greenhouse 
gases intensity of gas consumed in Europe, while ensuring a steady growth of renewable 
gases, decarbonised and low -carbon gases along the lines of the Eurogas proposal set out 
above in point 4.

detailed input. Eurogas has already provided detailed feedback and invites the Commission 
to take it into account. Eurogas also takes note of the series of White Papers published by 
ACER and CEER in 2021 and considers that they provide a basis for further discussion on some 
of the recommendations outlined therein. Eurogas specifically notes:

7. Eurogas supports the notion outlined in the IIA that coal and oil to gas switching in heating 
and power should not lead to sunk investments and lock-in effects. Eurogas underlines that 
the role of coal and oil to gas switching in meeting EU climate targets will be essential not only 
in power generation, but also and in particular in the heating sector, and carries the advantage 
of providing quick wins and decisive reductions in emissions. This will also ensure network 
stability, which will play an essential role to support the switch to a renewable energy system. 
Eurogas therefore regrets that the European Commission seems to already ha ve selected 
specific priority end-use sectors for renewable, decarbonised and low -carbon gases, such as 
industry and transport. Eurogas believes that the objective of avoiding sunk investments and 
lock-ins can only realistically be achieved if a holistic a ssessment is carried out that includes 
the role of renewable, decarbonised and low -carbon gases in the heating sector. The 
assessment should consider the role of: 
a. The heating sector and the local distribution grid to provide early access for 
distributed hy drogen production and thereby preventing the chicken and egg 
problem of leapfrogging to hydrogen applications when supply might not initially be 
triggered by demand and vice versa. 
b. Embedded generation at the low and mid voltage electricity grid through fue l cells 
and micro -CHP to support increasing electrification of residential heating and 
passenger transport.

8. With regard to infrastruct ure, Eurogas regrets the sole focus on pure hydrogen networks 
and a limited selection of sectors in industry and mobility.  
a. Eurogas agrees with most of the areas identified under point “1) Hydrogen 
infrastructure and hydrogen markets”, in particular: the r elevance of infrastructure 
(existing and new where needed) to integrate renewable, decarbonised and low -
carbon gases; the need to clarify the development, ownership and operation of assets 
related to renewable, decarbonised and low-carbon gas development; as well as the 
need for early regulatory intervention to provide investor certainty and to ensure that 
undue cross-subsidisation among network users is avoided when setting tariffs. The 
development of infrastructure linked to hydrogen may involve different  
configurations: dedicated networks, (partial) repurposing of existing natural gas 
infrastructures, adaptation of existing natural gas networks to accommodate 
blending. The gas package reform should consider adequate solutions when setting 
the tariffs in o rder to allow a fair definition of the RAB and allocation of costs to 
network users. We invite the Commission to consider ‘Eurogas 2019 
Recommendations for the Decarbonisation Package – Enabling the role of gas in a 
competitive and carbon-neutral EU’, referenced above.  
b. Eurogas regrets, however, in line with the comments made in point 4 and 9, the 
absence of a more holistic approach to the forthcoming Impact Assessment when it 
comes to the role of blending and residential heating. The problem statement 
identifies that the production of renewable and low -carbon gases will be more 
decentralised. Yet it fails to acknowledge that, particularly initially, this will require 
blending of renewable and low-carbon gases with methane in existing infrastructure. 
This in turn entails a particularly important role of gas transmission and distribution 
infrastructure. Moreover, since all residential heating customers are connected to the 
distribution level, not the transmission level, it is essential that the Impact 
Assessment considers the heating sector; the role of blending; the role of early 
hydrogen sinks and its role in avoiding carbon lock -ins and sunk investments as a 
result of coal and oil to gas switching. It is important to note, in addition, that a large 
share of industrial users are also connected at the DSO level, making the local level 
and the question of blending and gas quality key in addressing their decarbonisation 
pathways as well.

Commission that an alignment with the Clean Energy Package rules should be assessed. 
Eurogas has provided an initia l assessment on specific elements of the Electricity Market

a need to adopt stricter requirements for price regulation in gas, an alignment of consumer 
protection rights and the right to switch suppliers) . Eurogas supports alignment with regard 
to transparent, understandable, and accurate information for the customers. However, an 
over prescriptive approach on how companies communicate with their customers should be

SEDIGAS response to  European Commission’s consultation on 
the revision of EU rules on gas market access 
1. To meet the objectives and policy options identified, the introduction of binding renewable or 
decarbonisation gas targets in the market should be properly assessed, based on the 
corresponding environmental and cost benefit analysis, as well as any potential economic impact 
on industry and final consumers.  In case a binding target for renewable or low-emissions gases is 
set, it should be accompanied by incentive schemes to producers, mirroring the mechanisms used 
with renewable electricity, allowing producers to face their liabilities in supplying demand the 
pursued objectives. 
2. Early regulatory intervention  is fundamental to dully manage costs and create regulatory 
predictability for investors. Additionally, predictability for existing natural gas network users should 
be prioritized. 
3. A coherent regulatory approach across EU Member States and Third countries  should be 
implemented. 
4. The hydrogen market and infrastructure will likely grow and depart connected and from the existing 
gas system to a great extent. The fragmentation of legal instruments differentiating between  
(renewable, natural, or decarbonised) gas and hydrogen is not necessary nor desired . The 
existence of isolated or non -connected hydrogen systems is not suffici ent to require separate 
legislation. 
5. The Impact Assessment should address specifically the relevance of blending as a market-creating 
tool. In transport sector, it is aggravated by the lack of a basic and interconnected charging grid. 
Blending and injectin g hydrogen into the gas grids will certainly be a necessary driver of 
large-scale projects to accommodate the production. It will be especially suitable for gas uses 
robust to variations on gas quality and where transport and distribution grids are largely compatible, 
provided that the cost -benefit analysis of the blending is positive in the particular situation. The 
necessity of dedicated hydrogen infrastructure is rather reduced in the next decade. 
6. The regulatory framework should be ready to accommodate new legal figures paying attention to 
the evolution of the market needs. The definition of hydrogen network operators should be included 
and combined natural gas and hydrogen network operators should be allowed to realize efficiency 
gains and economies of scope. 
7. Coordinated network planning should be promoted, across market areas, energy carriers, and 
transmission and distribution levels. The optimization of existing energy infrastructure -reducing 
the risk for stranded assets and the risk for overinvestments- should be promoted. This should be 
considered, for instance, for the conversion of gas infrastructure to hydrogen, or for new electricity 
and gas investments, especially at transmission level. 
8. The digitisation of the gas sector should be encouraged: roll-out of smart meters and smart 
grids.

conditions of economic sustainability should apply for the maintenance of gas networks (e.g. for

10. Non-discriminatory third-party access to hydrogen networks (and large-scale storage) should 
be guaranteed for all market operators. 
11. Renewable gas should be encouraged to enter the system and the gas market, through 
existing gas infrastructure and platforms. It will enable producers and users to access to a larger 
market; it reduces dependence on captive producers/consumers; avoids the emergence of 
fragmented markets; and it brings emission reductions in all sectors of consumption.  
12. To raise the interest of consumers the system of guarantees of origin of gas should mirror 
the GO electricity system and comply with the RED2 to foster the interest of consumers. As 
in the electricity sector, the gas system will cover a mixture of gases produced from different 
sources. Once that renewable energy is in the energy system, the sole function of GOs is showing 
to a final customer that a given share or quantity of energy w as produced from RES. As in the 
electricity sector, GOs won’t be valid for target compliance . Likewise, its market use should be 
decoupled and won’t be precluded based on physical limits (e.g. blending limits, isolated/connected 
systems) similar to the electricity GO system (e.g. island production). 
13. The regulatory framework should be aligned with the Renewable Energy Directive, the Waste and 
Landfill Directive or the Methane strategy and other EU policy instruments.

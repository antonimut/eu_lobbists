Feedback on 
 
Gas networks -  
revision of EU rules on market access 
 
 
March 10, 2021 
 
 
 
The Austrian Federal Economic Chamber is the legal representative of the entire Austrian business 
community and represents all Austrian companies – some 540,000 businesses drawn from the areas 
of Crafts and Trade, Industry, Commerce, Banking and Insurance, Information and Consultancy, 
Tourism and Leisure, Transportation and Communication. 85 % of our members are SME with less 
than ten employees.

The Austrian Federal Economic Chamber (WKÖ) welcomes the initiative of the European 
Commission to review the legislative framework in order to design competitive 
decarbonised gas markets as a means to facilitate the gas sector’s contribution to the 
overall energy system decarbonisation. While achieving the goals of the European Green 
Deal following main principles must be ensured: 
• Guarantee security of supply at the highest possible reliability.  
• Promoting renewable and low-carbon energy in the most cost-effective way. 
• Simultaneously using and upgrading the existing energy infrastructure as well as 
expanding the infrastructure when justified need is given.  
• Renewable and low-carbon energy must be available at competitive prices. Avoid 
any further significant burden for the economy.  
An integrated energy system across all sectors is the key for meeting the EU’s climate and 
energy targets in a cost-effective way. Economy-wide energy efficiency improvements, a 
growing share of renewables and low-carbon energy and a progressive, cost-effective 
decarbonisation of the electricity and gas sector using all available technologies shall be 
delivered.  
There is a challenge to set a framework for the transition to an increasing share of 
renewable, decarbonised and low-carbon gases, while ensuring the following:  
• continuing stability and interoperability of the internal energy market to safeguard 
the vital role of gaseous energy carriers in supporting a competitive European 
industry; 
• providing affordable energy to consumers, while at the same time maintaining a 
high level of security of supply. 
Gas must be seen as enabler for the energy transition. In the context of an increasing 
volatile renewable power generation, the existing gas infrastructure (pipelines and storage 
facilities) are the necessary backbone for the required energy supply security in Europe. 
Upgrading and repurposing the existing gas infrastructure to climate-friendly gas 
compatibility will play a key role in a well-functioning future energy system. It is crucial to 
bring renewable energy from the supply peaks in summer season into the high-demand 
winter season with lower renewable energy production. A precise analysis of the producer 
and consumer centres is required in order to be able to make the best possible decisions on 
the further expansion of the energy infrastructure. When developing and repurposing gas 
networks, all other energy-producing sectors must be considered together and the TYNDP 
must be adapted accordingly. In this context the TEN-E Regulation can help to pursue a 
more effective approach. In this regard, the acceleration of approval processes and 
funding conditions for new technologies must be prioritized.  
However, a step-by-step approach must be pursued. In the light of a more decentralized 
production of climate-friendly gases, blending of those gases with methane in existing 
infrastructure will be required, at least in the midterm. Considering the approach of sector 
integration, the location of hydrogen production, but also the integration in the gas system 
must be evaluated. The role of blending must be carefully assessed together with the 
infrastructure on distribution and transmission level. In the short and mid-term blending 
will play a key role: for a full climate-friendly energy mix there will be neither enough 
climate-friendly gas nor the required infrastructure available, regardless if is repurposed,

retrofitted or even newly built. This concerns all sectors, from households up to industry. 
In addition, it is important to note that a large share of industrial users are also connected 
at the DSO level. For them and their decarbonisation pathways the local level and the 
question of blending and gas quality are key as well. 
In order to cost-efficiently transform our gas system into a climate-neutral one, existing 
and new technologies and infrastructure must be best used together in an economically 
and ecologically way. This requires mechanisms that enable uniform competitive 
conditions for all energy sources and carriers by taking external factors (above all 
greenhouse gases, but also e.g. nitrogen oxides and air quality) into account. The 
availability of affordable renewable and low-carbon energy will play a decisive role in this 
process.  
In the interest of a gradual transition and a technology-neutral approach, we call for 
acknowledgement of the role of all climate-friendly gases, renewable, decarbonized and 
low-carbon gases. This will help to rapidly reduce CO2 emissions from existing gas 
production, while renewable gas is not yet available in sufficient quantities. 
No sector must be excluded for climate-friendly gas a priori. We agree with the 
European Commission that in certain areas gaseous fuels will remain present in the EUs 
energy system by 2050. However, it is regrettable, that the European Commission 
apparently already started to select specific priority end-use sectors for climate-friendly 
gases, such as industry and transport. To avoid sunk investments and lock-ins a holistic 
assessment of the market must be carried out. This assessment must consider the optimal 
balance between demand and supply of climate-friendly gases. On our way towards 
climate neutrality, especially residential heating consumers can rely on a stable gas 
infrastructure on distribution level. 
Regulatory framework has to set incentives for climate-friendly gases. To strengthen 
the role of climate-friendly gases and to increase their competitiveness a stable regulatory 
framework and an effective market design are needed, while ensuring security of supply. 
The integration and trading of climate-friendly gases must be promoted. Barriers have to 
be removed while ensuring the key regulatory principles of unbundling, third-party access, 
transparency, non-discrimination and monitoring of the market. Market integration of 
green gases must be promoted with a fair and cost-effective support scheme that 
guarantees investment security for suppliers, at the same time taking international 
competitiveness of the industrial users into account. 
We welcome the Commission’s intention to ensure that the Renewable Energy Directive 
contributes to the further development and promotion of renewable, decarbonised, and 
low carbon gases. However, promoting the production and demand for such climate-
friendly gases should also be explicitly addressed in the revision of the gas regulatory 
framework. A future framework for climate-friendly gases at EU-level should be embedded 
within the principles underpinning the Internal Energy Market for gas and be supplemented 
with additional elements that reflect the particularities of the gradual deployment of 
climate-friendly gases and the key role that gas infrastructure operators will play in it. 
These existing rules, together with transparent and published network tariffs have proven 
to be efficient in ensuring market access to the infrastructure on non-discriminatory basis 
for all market users over the last decade.

Investors and project promotors need a stable, predictable, and comprehensible EU 
policy framework. Thus, a cross-sectorial guidance and streamlined legislation is 
necessary, covering consistency in the RED, EED, ETD, EU ETS and EEAG. As financing of 
projects is essential the Sustainable Finance framework, covering especially the 
Taxonomy, must be considered. When revising the existing gas legislation, we call for an 
integration of the hydrogen framework. A separate framework, duplication and any 
legislative conflicts should be avoided.  
Finally, a lack of uniform EU-wide classification of renewable, low-carbon and 
decarbonized gases makes the appropriate design of guarantees of origin for the 
functioning of the pan-European energy market currently impossible. In adopting the 
relevant legislation, clear definitions for climate-friendly gases must be developed to 
ensure the functioning of the EU-gas market.

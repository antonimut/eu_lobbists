PGNiG POSITION AS REGARDS THE REVISON OF EU RULES ON MARKET ACCESS TO GAS NETWORKS 
 
PGNiG welcomes the opportunity to comment on the  Commission’s Inception Impact Assessment as 
regards the revision of EU rules on market access to gas networks. 
The planned revision of EU should include only targeted changes in the regulatory framework in order 
not to undermine the benefits of the Third Energy Package . The rules in p lace since 2009, with 
amendments and complementing them network codes, proved to serve well the development of the 
energy market and the regulatory regime supporting competition on the market should not be 
changed. Their full implementation is the key acti on in terms of guaranteeing the fully functioning 
internal market for natural gas.  In particular, current rules governing LNG terminals serve the  
development of this market and provide necessary  stability for bringing  diversification of supply 
sources. The targeted revision of rules should aim at the main goals identified in the IIA, i.e. creation 
of the market for renewable and low-carbon gases (bio-methane, hydrogen) in order to contribute to 
increased EU climate ambition of 55% greenhouse gas emissions reduction by 2030. Still, the revision 
should be fully in line with the pillars of EU energy policy, i.e. security, competitiveness, sustainability 
and achieve a proper balance between them. 
The revision of rules should take into account specificities of d ifferent markets on their way towards 
climate neutrality, namely decreasing high dependency on coal, economic importance of energy-
intensive industry (e.g. refineries, fertilisers, steel making) and recent costs incurred for infrastructure 
development including the EU’s PCIs. In some Member States natural gas will play an important role 
as a transitional fuel longer than in other Member States as natural gas delivers immediate emission 
reductions and is the only economically and technically feasible way to meet increased targets of GHG 
emission reduction. The use of natural gas will allow a swift switch away from coal. Moreover, it will 
not lead to lock-in as natural gas and gas infrastructure serve scaling up of hydrogen and bio-methane 
production and enable increased deployment of renewable sources of energy  by bringing backup 
capacity. Therefore, we sha re Commission expectation that b iogas, bio -methane, renewable and 
decarbonized hydrogen will contribute to energy mix in 2050. Moreover, we also sha re the 
Commission analysis that abated natural gas can also contribute to carbon neutral energy mix. 
 
HIGHLIGHTS OF THE POSITION 
 Better integration of markets and infrastructure should encourage developing gas storage 
serving natural gas as well as renewable and low-carbon gases.   
 The rules introduced by the Third Energy Package should be applied to the hydrogen market.   
 Technology neutral approach to hydrogen is necessary for market uptake and should be 
applied both in supporting of infrastructure development and in designing market rules. 
 Cooperation with third countries should be based on following similar market and 
sustainability standards. In particular, imports of renewable and low-carbon hydrogen to the 
EU should follow the market rules of the Third Energy Package.

 Regulatory changes, including for  LNG terminals, should be limited only to issues directly 
connected with renewable and low -carbon gases and the third party access (TPA) 
exemptions should be applied restrictively in order not to disrupt competition. 
 To guarantee that customers would not be affected by disruptions caused by changing gas 
quality, there should be clear technical criteria for bio-methane and hydrogen based on the 
NRA decision. 
 
1. The role of gas storage in better integration of markets and infrastructure 
 PGNiG supports in general the idea of better integrating markets and infrastructure as 
serving increasing penetration of variable renewable energy sources. In particular natural 
gas storage facilities with the utilization of power -to-X technologies provide the cost -
efficient and available option for storing renewable electricity. These facilities can also 
serve as buffer storages for peak sources used for balancing the national power system. In 
this regard a significant role of gas storage should be recognized and EU policy, including 
EU financial resources, should encourage its development and retrofitting.  
 
2. Hydrogen infrastructure and hydrogen markets 
 In order to avoid monopolies in hydrogen production as well as abuses of dominant 
position resulting from natural monopolies in  transport of hydrogen , we share the 
Commission’s assessment that an early regulation would be instrumental in this regard. 
The future regulatory regime should provide for competition in the hydrogen market. For 
this reason, the rules introduced by the Third Energy Package, in particular third party 
access, unbundling, transparent and cost -reflective tariffs should be applied to the 
hydrogen market design. Therefore, the division of roles between market participants and 
infrastructure operators should as a general rule allow to focus the activity of TSOs and 
DSOs predominantly on operating transmission and distribution network.   
 Considering that the hydrogen market is in the early development stage, the main effort 
should be put on the uptake of hydrogen as fuel and market development. Therefore the 
principle of technology neutrality needs to be applied. 
 To face the challenge of creating a hydrogen commodity market, hydrogen infrastructure 
needs to be developed as well as natural gas infrastructure needs to be retrofitted for 
transporting blends (taking into account the technical feasibility, safety and sustainability). 
There should be available support under the TEN -E regulation and following financial 
support under Connecting Europe Facility. Principle of technological neutrality should be 
applied for that purpose.   
 The technology neutral approach needs to be applied as well in order to bring the highest 
volumes of hydrogen to the market, ensuring a swift and efficient (including cost-efficient) 
market kick-off. Therefore, all hydrogen  should be allowed to contribute to the market 
development.

 Cooperation with third countries should be based on following similar market and 
sustainability standards. In particular, imports of renewable and low -carbon hydrogen to 
the EU should follow the market rules of the Third Energy Package. This should be 
applicable in particular to the import of low-carbon hydrogen produced from natural gas, 
in order to avoid transferring the abuses of dominant position in the natural gas markets 
to the hydrogen market s. Therefore, s imilarly to the gas market, the implementation of 
rules to the importing pipelines should guarantee security of supply and serve functioning 
of the internal market without its fragmentation by privileged regulatory regimes arising 
through exemptions or derogations.   
 
3. Access of renewable and low-carbon gases to the infrastructure and the market 
 The future regulatory framework should allow injections of renewable gases  to the grid 
as soon as they meet respective technical standards. This is in particular applicable to bio-
methane, which meets the technical cri teria and requires only grid adjustments, that 
would serve accommodation of the decentralized production. In order to make the best 
use of the existing national circumstances, the technical standards should be approved by 
the respective NRAs. 
 As part of the transition to renewable gases, amelioration of the network infrastructure to 
reduce gas losses should be provided. The above is important for both hydrogen and bio-
methane, as it is currently impossible to build a dedicated network for these gases.  
 Supporting decentralization of production requires recognizing the role of distribution 
networks in developing the market and guaranteeing security of supply. For these reasons, 
there should be support for cooperation between DSOs and TSOs in operating gas 
networks, in particular by creating physical reverse flows from a distribution to 
transmission networks and other necessary infrastructure. Financial resources under the 
synergy of TEN-E and TEN-T regulations and other means of the EU budget should be made 
available.  
 Holistic infrastructure planning  should be ensured . Coordinated infrastructure planning 
can build on synergies by linking the gas and electricity sectors in order  to achieve a low-
emission and competi tive European economy. Constructing and retrofitting of a gas 
network as part of the energy transformation becomes even more important in terms of 
maintaining coherence of DSO and TSO development plans. 
 Regulatory chan ges as regards LNG terminals should be limited only to issues directly 
connected with renewable and low -carbon gases. The current regulatory regime should 
remain unchanged as the functioning of the market should not be undermined, in 
particular as regards already made booking and contracted gas supplies , which serve the 
purposes of diversification of supplies, therefore contributing to security and competition 
in the internal market.

 The third party access (TPA) exemptions should be applied restrictively i n order not to 
disrupt the competition between LNG terminals and, consequently, the competition 
between suppliers. 
 Constructing and retrofit ting of a gas network in order to decarbonize the gas sector 
requires an assessment based on the Cost -Benefit Analysis (CBA). The CBA methodology 
should take into account : developing both gas and electricity sectors (due to the sectors 
coupling) and environmental and social factors.  
 
4. Consumer rights and transparency 
 It is essential to guarantee that customers would not be affected by disruptions caused 
by changing gas quality. For this purpose, there should be clear technical criteria for 
biomethane and hydrogen before injecting into the grid determined by the NRAs, which 
guarantees the that the technical criteria remain appropriate for the state of 
development of production, processing, transmission, storage, distribution  
infrastructure and development of gas, energy, heating and cooling appliances.

Anigas welcomes the IIA on a “Hydrogen and Gas Decarbonisation Package”: 
the revision of the Directive 2009/73 and the Regulation 715/2009 is required 
for supporting the achievement of the climate neutrality by 2050 and 
developing a suitable and forward-looking framework for the changing gas 
landscape with an increased role for renewable and low carbon gases. 
The challenging target of 55% reduction in GHG emissions by 2030 requires 
interventions to enable a rapid and cost -effective decarbonisation of the 
energy system. The speed with which changes must happen requires policy 
makers and Member States to take decisive actions , outlining a credible and 
sustainable pathway, from an economic, social and environmental point of 
view, as well as for the flexibility, security and resilience of the energy system. 
The reform of the current gas legislative and regulatory framework should 
leverage the strategic role of gas infrastructures for security of supply, system 
resilience and ability to support the system-wide energy transition in the most 
cost-effective way,
 enabling gas and gaseous fuels to play their role in the 
current and future energy mix (20% of the total EU energy consumption in 
2050). 
Meeting the EU’s ambitious climate and energy targets in the most cost -
effective way requires a multi-vectored approach across all sectors to achieve 
carbon neutrality. It needs to build on an integrated energy system, that 
delivers energy efficiency improvements, growing shares of renewables and 
the progressive, cost-effective decarbonisation of the electricity and gas sector 
using all available technologies and with targeted support.  
All options listed in the Inception Impact Assessment should be explored to 
assess a complementary and synergic development and integration of 
hydrogen and renewable and low -carbon gases in the existing gas 
infrastructures and markets, enabling gas to fully express its decarbonisation 
potential. 
It is important to pursue a gradual, flexible, open and technology neutral 
approach to the Hydrogen Regulation: the envisaged measures – ranging from 
pure dedicated hydrogen pipeline/other logistic solutions  for decarbonisation 
of hard to abate sectors to blending in the existing gas distribution networks – 
should be assessed in terms of effectiveness, practicability and opportunity , 
based on the actual situation of each Member State and its National Energy 
and Climate Plan. 
Anigas considers important that the reform of the current gas regulatory 
framework: 
- is coherent and consistent with the entire European legislative framework 
aimed at supporting and promoting the energy transition set by the Green 
Deal, namely through the coordinated interventions on different texts

EED, TEN-E…). The Hydrogen and Gas Decarbonis ation Package shall 
be coherent with the Taxonomy: the classification criterion of sustainable

therefore include all network expansions and adaptations compatible with

synergic and complementary way the integration of hydrogen and 
renewable and low -carbon gas es in the existi ng energy system and ii) 
identifies the environmentally sustainable solutions to be implement ed in 
the short time. Effective measures should be assessed based on the actual 
situation of each Member State (energy mix, available assets, concrete 
potentiality in the short, medium and long term) and on the rapidity/ timing 
with which the various energy carriers can actively contribute to the 
reduction of emissions;

development; considering how hydrogen and renewable and low -carbon

o to define European gas quality parameters, providing a path of

- pays close attention to the allocation of the costs linked to any possible 
development of networks for H2 transport in order to ensure gas tariff 
sustainability for network users, eventually providing fiscal coverage; 
- addresses the issues already affecting the current market structure 
through the im plementation of a revision of the gas transportation tariff 
system. A (not exhaustive) example  of those issues  is the fact that 
transport infrastructures are remunerated on the basis of the capacity 
booking they receive, and therefore of their actual use, causing the risk of 
having excessively high transportation tariffs (with significant implications 
on market spreads) and, ultimately, potential decommissioning for those 
pipelines facing low demand but that are nonetheless necessary both for 
the EU Security of Supply and for preserving an effective competition on 
the market. This risk is even more relevant, due to the progressive 
expiration of the old long-term supply contracts with their associated 
capacity bookings. The transmission tariffs may need to evolve in the light 
of major future changes such as decarbonisation, smart sector integration, 
gas demand scenario and transportation capacity. According to ACER, 
most long-term contracts for gas transportation in Europe will expire in the 
next ten years, and the market tr end is to book shorter -term capacity 
products to pursue as much flexibility as possible while avoiding lock -in 
effects. In the light of these challenges, the transmission tariffs system 
needs to be rethought and should evolve in order to be sustainable and  
predictable for traders and TSOs.  
The early development of hydrogen and renewable and low -carbon gases 
should avoid distortions of the natural gas markets and tariffs  and be 
coordinated with the effective situation and production possibilities of each 
Member States.

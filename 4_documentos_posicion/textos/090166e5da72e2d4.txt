 
 
 
EDISON’S CONTRIBUTION TO THE HYDROGEN AND GAS MARKETS 
DECARBONISATION PACKAGE INITIATIVE 
 
Edison welcomes the opportunity to share some preliminary comments on the Hydrogen and Gas Markets 
Decarbonisation Package aimed at revising Directive 2009/73/EC on the internal market in natural gas  and 
the Regulation (EC) No 715/2009 on the EU gas transmission networks . The adaptation of the relevant 
legislation for the EU gas market should aim in our view at viable decarbonization pathway for the EU g as 
system while ensuring and preserving market competition and security of supply. 
As a leading energy company with more than 130 years of history and an ambitious and forward -looking 
strategy centred around innovation and sustainability, Edison welcomes the new EU climate ambitions of the 
Green Deal proposals, being aware that a profound rethinking of some of the main features of the European 
economy will be required, while preserving the social aspects and energy affordability.  
The role of gas in enabling and supporting the transition to climate neutrality is clear in our view, especially 
given the difficulty of achieving full electrification in certain areas and the need to accompany and support 
increasing shares of renewable energy with flexible and performing capacity. Especially in some sectors, such 
as heavy, long distance and maritime transport, the use of gaseous fuels, including LNG, will drastically reduce 
CO2 emissions and local pollutants, while renewable and low-carbon gases already covered by the existing 
legislation can certainly increase their role in supporting the overall decarbonization of the gas sector thanks 
to additional provisions in this direction. 
Likewise, we believe that the new EU gas package should define a legal and regulatory framework that caters 
for the emergence of a hydrogen market in Europe grounded on a shared understanding of its technological, 
industrial and economic context. We encourage a gradual approach for the EU strategy for hydrogen that 
ensures level playing field and competition as a compass  and envisages a "preparatory phase" that focuses 
on research, pilot projects and regulatory sandboxes. This should be done in our view in a market framework 
that maintains both for the financial and sectoral legislation a solid and healthy business case for companies 
to invest also into the gas value chain and infrastructure, as to deliver on policy objectives in a cost-effective 
manner. Moreover, c onsidering hydro gen production costs, availability and decarbonization potential, it 
should be first considered to foster green hydrogen production at  or near the consumption sites. Blending 
H2 with fossil gas could be considered in some specific cases, as a possibility t o decarbonize the import for 
example.   
In particular for investment in infrastructure s, we wish to recall that the framework provided at European 
level for new initiatives, namely under article 36 of Directive 73/2009/EC, formerly article 22 of Directive 
53/2003/EC, is still a valid success story with lessons learnt that should be kept in mind when approaching 
new investments, including in hydrogen infrastructure . While we regret to read in the Inception Impact 
Assessment that “private and unregulated nature of hydrogen infrastructure would also imply no guarantee 
for non-discriminatory access, transparency and competitive market outcome”, we wish to recall that under 
an EU exempted framework, and without any bias towards private investors, the conditions granted for the 
infrastructure development have always been defined within a robust regulatory framework scrutinized and 
approved by the European Commission and the national regulators and geared towards full transparency of 
the tariff models, non-discriminatory and competitive access to infrastructures, while the investment costs 
can be dealt with in a way that can ensure greater benefits for users and consumers. 
Therefore, the new hydrogen and gas markets decarbonisation package should keep the existing principles 
of unbundling, third party access and transparency as a starting point in order to preserve the market

functioning and to avoid cross subsidies between regulated and market -based activities. Only in case of 
insufficient market developments or market failures, we believe that regulated operators – such as DSOs and 
TSOs – should be given, temporarily and under specific conditions , the opportunity to carry out those 
innovative activities. In any case it will be important to avoid cross -subsidization between gas and H2 
networks: RAB should be kept separated. 
To conclude, we wish to recall that in light of the different characteristics of Member States in terms of energy 
mixes and decarbonisation pathways, the future Hydrogen and Gas Markets Decarbonisation Package should 
take into consideration the objectives identified by EU governments through their National Energy and 
Climate Plans, aiming at creating a more resilient system endowed with a rich portfolio of technologies and 
energy options across the Internal Market.

CONFEDERATION OF INDUSTRY OF THE CZECH REPUBLIC RESPONSE TO 
PUBLIC CONSULTATION - INCEPTION IMPACT ASSESSMENT ON 
HYDROGEN AND GAS MARKETS DECARBONISATION PACKAGE 
Confederation of Industry of the Czech Republic (SP CR) welcomes the opportunity to comment on the 
Inception Impact Assessment on Hydrogen and Gas markets Decarbonisation Package.  
SP CR believes he amendment of the Gas Directive and Regulation should achieve the following objectives: 
maximize synergies between gases (methane) and hydrogen (in particular , repurposing or retrofitting gas 
infrastructure opportunities), maintain security of supply, ramp-up of a hydrogen market, contribute to the 
EU decarbonisation goals while keeping an integrated gas market and secure affordable energy transition 
and encourage investment in renewable, decarbonised and low carbon gases. 
Many of the below mentioned topics are interconnected and influence each other. However, the aim of the 
SP CR was to bring attention to these topics and particular issues as they are in our opinion crucial for 
decarbonisation of gas sector but also of the whole energy system of the EU. 
Nevertheless, it is also suggested that the revised gas r egulation and directive should not seek to cover all 
the required technical details and these should, instead, be dealt with in Network Codes (as for the 
regulation, the detailed rules that apply to hydrogen should be tailored to each stage of development of the 
hydrogen market). 
1. Application of the Internal Energy Market (IEM) rules to the future hydrogen market 
The IEM, and notably the rules in relation to natural gas, have served EU citizens and industry well by 
establishing a liquid, competitive and secu re gas market across the EU. Its provisions should serve as the 
foundations for the emerging hydrogen network and market. Applying similar principles from the Gas 
Directive, such as those concerning unbundling and non -discriminatory Third Party Access (TPA ), clearly 
makes sense because the future hydrogen network is anticipated to share the same basic attributes as the 
gas network, i.e. a natural monopoly, used by multiple types of producers and consumers, with the purpose 
of transporting a commodity, and t hereby facilitating its trade in the most efficient manner. Incorporating 
the rules for hydrogen in the gas legislation would be the most efficient way of ensuring regulatory alignment 
between these two closely related energy vectors. Furthermore, includin g the relevant regulatory 
arrangements for the hydrogen market and hydrogen infrastructure within the gas legislation will support 
the system integration between gas and hydrogen - in particular regarding the repurposing of gas 
infrastructure (to transport hydrogen only) and the retrofitting of gas infrastructure (to transport hydrogen 
blended with natural gas/methane).

2. Hydrogen injected into the gas network (blending) 
Blending of hydrogen into natural gas and biomethane should be recognized in gas regulati on and directive 
as one of legitimate ways how to realise the decarbonisation of the gas infrastructure, thus contributing to 
the overall decarbonisation of the EU economy. In cases where hydrogen production does not in itself 
economically fully justify dedicated hydrogen infrastructure (e.g. in the initial stages of market development 
when both production and demand are expected not to be that high), blending of hydrogen may be an 
attractive solution, at least for a transitional period. Blending of natural gas and hydrogen will be instrumental 
in scaling up hydrogen production capacities and facilitating transport of hydrogen when volumes are not 
sufficient for dedicated hydrogen systems. 
The role of blending will vary between regions and may be more suitable for some consumption sectors and 
less for others. Pipelines and operational system can be retrofitted to accommodate hydrogen blended in 
the natural gas flows. The costs involved in such adaptations need assessment and should be able to be fully 
recovered.  
When blending hydrogen in the gas network, some specific issues may arise which would need dedicated 
provisions. 
3. Role of gas regulated entities 
The EU gas/hydrogen legislation should explicitly recognise that existing gas TSOs/DSOs/SSOs should be 
permitted to own and operate hydrogen infrastructure. Authorising gas TSOs/DSOs/SSOs to carry out 
activities related to the development, integration and operation of hydrogen infrastructure would facilitate 
an efficient way forward due to the experience of  TSOs/DSOs/SSOs in planning, financing, constructing, 
operating and maintaining gas infrastructure. Furthermore, since parts of the existing gas grids (owned and 
operated by the gas TSOs/DSOs/SSOs) will be retrofitted and repurposed to transport and store hydrogen, 
this would also be more easily facilitated by allowing TSOs/DSOs/SSOs to own and operate both gas and 
hydrogen networks/storages. It should also result in a more efficient operation of the future hydrogen 
network and enable existing, well-proven arrangements concerning safety and security of supply cooperation 
to be more easily mirrored to the hydrogen market. 
The infrastructure operators should facilitate the development and operation of the technical and 
commercial framework (e.g. via network codes) and ensure transparent, fair and non-discriminatory access 
to the infrastructure. 
4. Repurposing/retrofitting of gas pipelines 
The majority of the future hydrogen network will be comprised of repurposed gas pipelines. The regulatory 
framework for the repurposing and retrofitting of existing gas infrastructure to enable the integration of new 
clean gases, including hydrogen must be sufficiently outlined. Repurposed / retrofitted infrastructure means 
physical upgrade of existing natural gas infrastructure for use of pure hydrogen or of admixture of methane 
with hydrogen at a pre-defined level.

To ensure the cost -effective and timely development of the hydrogen network, it will need to be designed 
and constructed in advance to meet the longer -term forecast of  hydrogen demand in terms of 
sizing/dimensioning, location and timing. 
Moreover, it should be allowed that the repurposed/retrofitted infrastructure may transport methane for a 
pre-defined period until there will be a sufficient demand/supply for hydrogen/ blends. The repurposing of 
gas infrastructure for hydrogen/blends must take place in advance so it is available when demand and supply 
emerge. First, the repurposing/retrofitting will help to avoid sunk costs of gas infrastructure as it guarantees 
utilization in decarbonized future. Second, the readiness of infrastructure might be crucial for any business 
entity intending to decide whether to invest into hydrogen technologies, which will then help a hydrogen 
market development. 
Also, a repurposing clause and a link with TEN-E should be established to say that repurposing costs should 
be recognised by the NRAs and that the switch from natural gas to hydrogen should be planned in a 
transparent way, with inclusion of all relevant stakeholders in infrastructure planning (both on European and 
national level). Similarly, costs of retrofitting the network to accommodate hydrogen/gas blends should also 
be appropriately recognised by the NRAs. 
5. Regulatory sandbox  
In our vision the developed new gases market should have similar pillars to the ones present on the natural 
gas market such as independent unbundled infrastructure operators, non-discrimination, third party access 
and tariff transparency. However, until the market is secure and competitive there should be a lot of flexible 
solutions created to enable its ramp -up, such as e.g. regulatory sandbox. A regulatory sandbox framework 
approach at EU level would promote innovation, scaling up, system adaptation and optimisation for 
technologies at an early stage of development. This can play an important role in encouraging R&D activities 
and innovative projects. 
Such an approach can provide a boost to test and roll-out the required new technologies, prove their benefits 
in practice, and evaluate their contributions to th e energy decarbonisation pathways. It would allow R&D 
activities and innovative projects to be handled under more flexible terms regarding general rules such as 
state aid, funding access criteria, cost socialisation via regulated assets and based on specif ic regulatory 
oversight. It is important to make sure that Member States introduce and apply the regulatory sandboxes in 
a harmonized way consistent with the functioning of the internal EU gas market . In any case, regulatory 
sandboxes shall respect unbundling rules and third party access  and should rather deal with other aspects 
needed for new technology uptake.  Therefore, the revised gas legislation should allow for the 
implementation of such arrangements. 
6. Incentives for production of renewable, decarbonised and low carbon gases 
The introduction of incentives for renewable, decarbonised and low -carbon gases production will be 
essential to realise the EU's decarbonisation goal. Incentives must be introduced to unlock the full potential 
these technologies can have in decarbonizing the energy market and to ensure a level playing field between 
the existing technologies.

Possible decentralization of production of low carbon and renewable gases must be addressed. 
Decentralization of production will lead to decentralization of injection which may cause swing in gas quality. 
Thus, decentralization will require tighter coordination among old and new market actors (such as 
infrastructure operators and producers of new types of gases) and stronger management of the overall gas 
system. 
One of the key issues in this regard is the creation of regulatory and legislative framework for blending of 
hydrogen and methane (natural gas). 
7. Sector integration and coordinated infrastructure planning 
Gas regulation and direct ive should create a framework for system integration between gas , heat  and 
electricity sectors. It is expected that particular energy systems will be gradually interconnected and thus 
interdependent. Establishment of regulatory framework should lead to inc reasing liquidity, competition, 
consumer protection  and contribute to the decarbonisation goal with the lowest possible costs for final 
consumers. 
The sector integration will require coordinated approach to infrastructure planning between gas , heat and 
electricity sectors. We expect that such a harmonized planning will lead to inclusion of relevant projects from 
all energy sectors and thus lead to an affordable and effective creation of a well -integrated energy system. 
However, network and market planning w ould reflect all infrastructure projects including natural gas, 
renewable, decarbonised and low carbon gases projects. 
Moreover, the transparent and non-discriminatory processes that are currently in place for planning at the 
national level for gas infrastructure should also be applied for repurposing, retrofitting and/or building new 
hydrogen infrastructure. The European gas legislation should give some guidelines/principles in this regard.    
8. Hydrogen production pathways  
Building on only one and at the same time more expensive technology option – renewable/green hydrogen 
– will result in a slower displacement of hydrogen than including clean hydrogen produced from natural gas 
with carbon abatement or using low-carbon electricity, as well as slower replacement of fossil fuels by clean 
hydrogen in industry and transport. Relying on renewable/green hydrogen alone will therefore most likely 
lead to:   
 a more expensive EU energy transition and  
 a slower overall greenhouse gas reduction. 
Therefore, mid-term hydrogen demand should be stimulated and secured with decarbonised and low carbon  
hydrogen. Production of such hydrogen is essential to achieve rapid development and growth of hydrogen 
technologies on the market. This will allow variou s sectors of the economy to be decarbonised, as such 
hydrogen is currently the most economical and environmentally -friendly way to produce hydrogen in 
contrast to RES hydrogen production. Without including all kinds of hydrogen, hydrogen technologies would 
not be able to achieve necessary economies of scale.

9. Role of renewable, decarbonised and low-carbon gases in different sectors 
With regard to the forthcoming Impact Assessment, SP CR would welcome that European Commission 
properly addresses the question of priority sectors identified ex -ante by the European Commission for 
hydrogen consumption. The IIA notes that: 
"The areas where decarbonised gaseous fuels are expected to come into play include today's energy-intensive 
industry (e.g., refineries, fertilis ers, steel making) and certain heavy duty transport sectors (maritime 
transport, aviation, long distance heavy vehicles). The objective of promoting renewable and low -carbon 
gases is to decarbonise these sectors, increase flexibility of the power system an d, if needed, allow to store 
and produce again electricity." 
SP CR supports the notion that coal to gas switching in heating and power sectors should not lead to sunk 
investments and lock-in effects. SP CR underlines that the role of coal to gas switching in meeting EU climate 
targets will be essential not only in power generation, but also and in particular in the heating sector, and 
carries the advantage of providing quick wins and decisive reductions in emissions. SP CR believes that the 
objective of avoiding sunk investments and lock-ins can only realistically be achieved if a holistic assessment 
is carried out that includes the role of renewable, decarbonised and low-carbon gases in the heating sector. 
10. Gas quality management 
The question of gas quality  must be addressed in the gas regulation and in the directive. Any disruptions 
caused by possible changes in gas quality that might affect consumers must be prevented. New roles and 
services inherently linked to gas grid management (such as gas quality management and conversion services) 
will need to be established to complement and support the development and operation of increasingly 
integrated gas and hydrogen pipeline systems. 
11. CO2 infrastructure 
Revised EU gas legislation should be sufficiently flexible to allow possible application of rules to infrastructure 
for CO2 transport, if it emerges in the future. CO2 transport infrastructure is inter alia being considered in the 
EC’s draft revised TEN-E regulation from 15 December 2020.

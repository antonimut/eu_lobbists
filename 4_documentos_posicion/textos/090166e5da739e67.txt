AIM OF THE INITIATIVE 
 
Gasgrid Finland 1 welcomes the initiative of the European Commission to future -proof the  Gas Market  Directive and 
Regulation, as well as  the transmission networks towards the deployment of hydrogen , biogas/biomethane and other 
clean gases. Whilst maintaining the existing e nergy infrastructures to play their  essential role in the energy transition. 
Furthermore, we appreciate the European Commission for giving the public an opportunity to provide feedback on this 
initiative. 
 
The overall objective of th e initiative is to ensure a suitable market framework to support the achievement of the EU 
climate ambition of 55% greenhouse gas emissions reduction by 2030 and net-zero emissions target by 2050. The revision 
of the Gas Directive and Regulation aims to translate the strategies and plans under the European Green Deal, notably 
the Energy System Integration Strategy and the Hydrogen Strategy, into legal measures. More precisely, aims at modifying 
existing EU legislation and creating a new framework for an internal hydrogen market.  
 
According to the initiative, the  key is to achieve a cost -efficient clean hydrogen economy  by addressing the following 
problem areas: 
 
1) Hydrogen infrastructure and hydrogen markets 
2) Access of renewable and low-carbon gases to the infrastructure and the market 
3) Consumer rights, competition and transparency 
4) Lack of integrated energy markets, in particular through network planning 
 
 
STATEMENT 
 
When considering a ‘perfectly functional’ framework, Gasgrid Finland would like to underline the following standpoints, 
that are strongly present in the company’s strategy for the upcoming decades. Gasgrid Finland builds its operation on the 
objectives of contributing to the EU decarbonization goals ; maximizing synergies between gas and hydrogen by future-
proof gas infrastructure  and regulation , supporting an integrated and competitive  gas market and encouraging 
investment in renewable and low carbon gases  (e.g. through guarantees of origin) . Our vision for year 2035 is: “ Gases 
enable carbon neutral society – We provide platform for it”.

2. Application of the Internal Energy Market (IEM) rules to the future hydrogen market 
 
Regulatory changes take place and markets evolve. Gasgrid Finland recognizes that the legislative frameworks, that were 
put into force over a decade ago, are not fully compatible with the way in which EU gas markets need to be regulated in 
the future. However, Gasgrid Finland agrees with the notion, that the current provisions in relation to natural gas should 
serve as the foundation for the emerging hydrogen network and market, as these are anticipated to share the same basic 
attributes as the gas network. Therefore , it seems rational to apply principles from the current regulation,  such a s 
unbundling and non-discriminatory TPA, to hydrogen as well. On the other hand, the established framework should be 
flexible and innovative enough, so that it can be tailored to the specific stage of development of the hydrogen market (at 
least for a transitional period).  
 
In addition, relevant consideration should be given to industry practice. Today we have seen private companies in closed 
private industrial network settings to utilize current regulatory frameworks in their bilateral contracts voluntarily. This is 
probably because both parties need to invest less in developing the contractual practices and to have mutually 
independent legal framework s – EU directives and national laws and regulat ions – forming the basis for future 
development. Consequently, this is bringing efficiencies, stability and clarity for all parties involved. 
 
As hydrogen will most likely develop from local clusters advancing to potentially regional, national and international 
infrastructures and markets, developing regulatory principles originating from the current IEM structures would most 
likely be beneficial over the decades. The focus should be on the core parts of the value chain wit h natural monopoly 
characteristics that enable sector integration and the goals of IEM.

3. The role of the TSO 
 
The question of recognizing gas TSOs right to carry out activities related to the development, integration and operation 
of hydrogen infrastructure, including the core hydrogen network, is of focal nature. Undoubtedly, the TSOs have essential 
experience in planning, financing, constructing, operating and maintaining gas infrastructure.  Furthermore, TSOs are a 
key factor in cross-border and pan-European networks as they can bring their local expertise into play. Creation of supply 
and demand to facilitate the new market for hydrogen needs enablement from the TSOs through market entries and by 
creating integrated systems of energy production and consumption.  
 
TSOs should be allowed to participate in the development of hydrogen infrastructures and markets. For example, based 
on the feedback received from large users of hydrogen today , they want stable and gradual change. They want to have 
current gas infrastructure to co -exists together with either captive clean hydrogen production and/or pure hyd rogen 
infrastructure. They also see potential for extra value in blending excess hydrogen to the current gas infrastructure or, 
alternatively, in injecting it to the pure hydrogen network. Over time, and depending on the location, current gas 
infrastructures will be replaced by pure hydrogen networks.  
 
Gasgrid Finland sees that the evolution of the current regulation would enable both development of the hydrogen 
markets and bringing benefits to customers by utilizing all capabilities and flexibilities from existing infrastructures.  With 
a right re -design of current regulation, we can provide security for customers e.g.  from cross -subsidization or from 
double-counting regulatory asset bases when infrastructure is repurposed. Moreover, the right regulatory r e-design 
would enable synergies between current and new gas infrastructures including reinvesting returns from current assets 
to new infrastructures.    
 
 
4. Further reflections 
 
Gaseous fuels will continue to provide an important share of the energy mix also by 2050, requiring the decarbonization 
of the gas sector via a forward-looking design for competitive decarbonized gas markets. These necessary changes have 
prompted a need to rethink certain key elements of traditional gas market dynamics. The basic conclusion reach is that 
there is no simple answer but that various considerations speak in favor of establishing a new system of dynamic and 
targeted regulation, that is build upon the current gas market rules. This situation has also acted as a catalyst for a wave 
of encouraging R&D activities and innovative projects  to promote system adaption.  Gasgrid Finland supports and finds 
the opportunities arising from these changes welcome.

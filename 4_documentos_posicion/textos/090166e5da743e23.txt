Gas for Climate’s feedback 
Gas for Climate welcomes the European Commission’s vision described in the combined roadmap 
and inception assessment on the Hydrogen and Gas Markets Decarbonisation Packag e, and would 
like to emphasise the importance of the topic and give brief feedback on the described problem areas 
and objectives. Targeted specific feedback will be submitted during the public consultation covering 
the wide range of issues on the Gas Directive & Regulation and policy options. Gas for Climate would 
be glad to contribute to the impact assessment in the stakeholder workshop mentioned.  
The need for a review of the legislative framework  
Renewable and low-carbon gases transported via gas 
infrastructure will play an important role in the 2050 
energy mix in order to realise the EU’s climate targets 
in a cost -efficient manner. T he current Gas Directive 
and Regulation are designed for the organisation and 
functioning of the present natural gas market, 
therefore a review of the legislative framework is 
needed to design a competitive  European 
decarbonised gas market that will allow for an efficient 
and sustainable development of renewable and low -
carbon gases and that will facilitate the injection, 
transport and storage of biomethane and green and 
blue hydrogen through gas infrastructure. 
Infrastructure operators  are well positioned and 
should be enabled to contribute to the overall energy 
system decarbonisation.  
Gas for Climate endorses the problem areas and 
specific objectives the EC’s initiative aims to tackle . 
Specifically focussing on enabling a cost -effective 
hydrogen infrastructure and developing local and 
decentralised production of renewable and low 
carbon gasses . Gas for Climate recognises the 
importance of a holistic and inclusive infrastructure 
planning, taking into account the end -user, and 
ensuring a coherent approach across all Member 
States. Gas for Climate would like to raise the following 
suggestions and points of feedback: 
An important role for renewable and low-carbon 
gases in heating 
The Commission states that renewable and low 
carbon gas would by 2050 be used in industry , heavy 
transport and the electricity sector. Gas for Climate 
emphasises that gas can play a valuable role in 
decarbonising the heating of buildings  as well. Our 
study ‘Gas for Climate. The optimal role for gas in a net-
zero emissions energy system’ (2019)  showed that by 
2050 most new build buildings will be heated by all -
electric heat pumps or through district heating, yet it 
is cost -effective to decarbonise the 100 mi llion 
buildings with existing gas connections today through 
heating solutions, using a combination of electricity 
and renewable gas. Future gas volumes used in those 
buildings would be lower compared to today, yet with 
very high societal cost benefits, compared to solutions 
that do not consider gas.

Creating an initial regulatory framework for hydrogen 
For hydrogen to develop to its full potential, a clear perspective towards developing a liquid and well-
functioning European hydrogen market over time is needed. The recently adopted EC hydroge n 
strategy highlights the need for an enabling regulatory framework for a liquid hydrogen market and 
infrastructure, however it does not answer the question on how this new market should be developed 
and regulated. Gas for Climate underlines the view of the EC as described in the roadmap; the current 
Gas Directive and Regulation needs to be amended in order to enable the emergence of renewable 
and low -carbon gases combined with  different pr oduction technologies, and allow for increased 
energy system integration.  
To further enable a scale-up of hydrogen, blending is a suitable intermediate option , in particular in 
regions of Europe where existing gas infrastructure may not become available  in the short term for 
dedicated hydrogen transport. Furthermore, in order to decarbonise specifically the industry sector 
quickly there is a need for a dedicated hydrogen infrastructure already before 2030. In order to enable 
this European hydrogen backbone and market, and to ki ckstart developments and investments, a 
foundation needs to be laid out by the European Commission as a first step. This foundation should 
in the short-term first of all provide a clear vision and direction on how the hydrogen market and the 
associated regulation will develop, combined with an initial hydrogen regulatory framework, setting 
the first steps towards this future-state. European-wide oversight is a must, as a solely national view 
will not be sufficient to develop a functioning hydrogen market and network across Member States.  
To reach the EU’s ambitious climate targets and the desired scale -up of hydrogen , quick action is 
needed, both in terms of support schemes and in providing regulatory clarity. The creation of an initial 
hydrogen framework will provide regulatory clarity to the hydrogen supply chain and would facilitate 
investor security. It would allow frontrunner Member States to align their national regulation with the 
future EU regulation. Harmonised EU regulation will enable future compatibility in hydrogen cross-
border trade and economies of scale.  
What should this initial regulatory framework at least cover? 
An initial European regulatory framework should aim at enabling the creation of a dedicated 
hydrogen infrastructure, facilitating the creat ion of a harmonised yet flexible hydrogen market. To 
this end, regulatory features which will ensure alignment and coordination between Member States 
need to be established, setting the trajectory towards a functioning internal hydrogen market.  
According to Gas for Climate the initial EU hydrogen regulation framework should cover at least the 
following elements: 
1. Ensuring coherence between the hydrogen regulation and the EU’s overall direction for the 
Energy Transition  
2. Aligning definitions and set ting technical standards for European -wide standards for 
hydrogen transport as well as for blending, to enable hydrogen grid injection  
3. Enabling cross border trade  
4. Recognising the role of the infrastructure operator in the development of the renewable and 
low-carbon gas market  
5. Clarity on progressive regulation on third -party access (TPA) and a level of unbundling, and 
the financing of dedicated hydrogen infrastructure 
6. Integrated infrastructure planning  
7. Setting of regulatory exemptions for the initial phase (incl. hydrogen valleys and private 
networks) 
The upcoming Gas for Climate policy paper on Hydrogen regulation will provide further details.

Who we are 
Gas for Climate was initiated in 2017 to analyse and create awareness about the role of renewable and 
low carbon gas in the future European energy system in full compliance with the Paris Agreement 
target to limit global temperature increase to well below 2 degrees Celsius. To this end, the entire 
economy has to become (net) zero carbon by mid-century. 
Gas for Climate is a group of eleven leading European gas transport companies (DESFA, Enagás, 
Energinet, Fluxys, Gasunie, GRTgaz, ONTRAS, OGE, Snam, Swedegas , and Teréga) and two 
biomethane industry associations (Consorzio Italiano Biogas and European Biogas Association). Gas 
for Climate is dedicated to contribute to the overall energy system decarbonisation and is convinced 
that renewable and low carbon gas u sed through existing gas infrastructure will help to deliver this 
at the lowest possible societal costs and maximum benefits for the European economy.

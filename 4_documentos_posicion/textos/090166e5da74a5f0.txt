Preliminary SNAM inputs to the  
EC initiative on hydrogen and gas markets decarbonization package  
SNAM welcomes the EC initiative for a revised Gas Package enabling the integration of low-carbon 
and renewable gases, including H2. An integrated gas system plays a crucial role for EU 
competitiveness and security of supply. In this perspective,  we would like to provide the following 
considerations. 
- Key role of gases in the future energy mix  
SNAM shares the EC view that gases, both natural and low-carbon gas in the transition phase and 
renewable molecules in the medium-long term, will play a key role in the future energy mix, 
guaranteeing both security of supply and affordable energy sources for the EU economy (around 
20% of the total EU energy consumption in 2050 - according to the Climate Target Plan Impact 
Assessment - of which 2/3 green gases and 1/3 natural gas with CCS/U). 
- Key role of EU gas network in the future energy system 
In this context, the EU gas network will continue to be an essential facility for the future energy 
system, ensuring the integration of new green sources and preserving at the same time the 
integrity of the EU energy market. Gas infrastructure will continue to move massive amounts of 
energy in forms of H2 and other green gases and as such will be the back-bone of the future energy 
system. 
- Need to move towards a more integrated and circular systems  
Sector integration is a major opportunity to address the climate change challenges and delivering 
energy transition in the most effective and efficient way. Integration across systems requires to 
move from separated energy value chain to a more integrated perspective, encompassing all 
sectors (primarily gas and electricity as well as heating, transport, agriculture, waste etc.). In this 
context, gas infrastructure, accelerating the scale up of low carbon and new green molecules, will 
enable and support also circular economy developments. 
- TSOs contribution essential for the energy transition 
Gas TSOs can provide an essential contribution to the achievement of the Green Deal goals also 
through coordinated and integrated infrastructure planning among the different sectors, 
ensuring timely and cost-effective developments also with a long-term perspective in terms of 
assets sizing, location and deployment. Integrated and coordinated planning of gas infrastructure, 
both at EU and national levels, would be beneficial for the internal energy market.  
- New sector governance allowing a more active TSO role 
To promote the development of green gases (in particular H2) an evolution of the sectorial 
governance should be pursued in order to allow TSOs Group Companies to contribute to the 
required developments by investing along the green gas value chain. To this extent, the current 
unbundling models adopted across MSs should be further developed by ensuring a level playing 
field among different separation model currently in place (ISO/ITO/OU). 
- Dynamic regulation and sand-boxes 
A flexible and open regulatory framework, in particular for the H2 sector, will be required to 
promote an acceleration of the deployment of this vector essential for energy transition. Specific 
regulatory measures can be assessed and introduced at any point in time or defined upfront with 
a dynamic regulatory approach according to the sector development stages. A regulatory sandbox 
approach could be first step within a dynamic regulation to encourage projects enabling H2 scaling

introduction of harmonised and consistent regulatory provisions among MSs, supporting the

- Consideration of all options for H2 sector development 
To let the H2 sector starting, all options favouring H2 developments should be considered. From 
the production side, low-carbon (blue) H2 can be an essential component together with renewable 
(green) H2 for the sector taking off. As well, blending solutions will play an important role for the 
integration of H2 into the energy system until dedicated H2 demand and supply develop and/or 
until this represents a suitable solution to fulfil the customers energy needs. 
- Completion of the EU internal gas market  
An integrated gas system plays a crucial role for EU competitiveness and security of supply. In this 
respect, the Package reform should consider the need for additional measures which are still 
required for the completion of the internal gas market. Indeed, solutions should be found to 
achieve a structural price alignment among the different markets, as spreads are experienced to 
be in some cases still relevant.

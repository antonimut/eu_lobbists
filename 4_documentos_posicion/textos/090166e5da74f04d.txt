1 General observations 
 
EnBW Energie Baden-Württemberg AG shares the vision of a climate neutral energy system and supports 
the objective of EU-climate neutrality. Renewable and decarbonized gases like biomethane and hydrogen 
will have to play a key role to reach this objective. For this reason, EnBW is engaged in the EU Clean Hydro-
gen Alliance, working on projects along the gas value chain. 
We think that the revisions of Directive 2009/73/EC and Regulation (EC) No 715 /2009 have the potential to 
enable the needed swift transition towards a climate-neutral gas sector and energy system. As regards hy-
drogen, today the high costs of the technology are the main barrier and therefore need to be reduced as 
quickly as possible by achieving economies of scale. EnBW welcomes the European approach for an EU hy-
drogen strategy and thus its implementation in the upcoming revision of the gas markets package.  
 
2 Main recommendations 
 
In our view, the following elements are important: 
 
• Broad application of hydrogen in distribution grids needed: As 60% of industry (registering power 
measurement as well as standard load profile) are connected to the distribution grid, 80% gas sales 
occur at distribution grid level. As the German Monitoring  report points out, large emission reduc-
tions could be achieved by blending of 20-25% H2 as of 2030. 
• Fast inclusion of hydrogen in the current regulatory EU framework on the premise that H2 will re-
place gradually natural gas in all applications /sectors: In order to achieve the Green Deal targets, 
uptake and scale-up in renewable and decarbonized gases need to take place in a very fast manner. 
The existing regulatory framework for natural gas is an established and proven framework that can 
achieve this task.  It is familiar to all market participants and offers a strong degree of long -term 
predictability and reliability. In addition, it prevents possible fragmentation by the Member States.  
• Adoption of existing unbundling rules for hydrogen:  Since the framework  for natural gas has been 
successful in creating well -functioning markets, the production, transport, trading and sales of hy-
drogen should be organised in a similar way. Third party access and network access rules are part 
of this. 
• Need for uniform European certificate market: The climate characteristics of all climate -neutral 
gases should be certified, i.e. regardless of whether it is chemically H2 or CH4 or a gas mixture. This 
will create a much larger market, which can thus be more liquid and have a greater impact.

• Level playing field between power and gas: As analysed in the Commissions Impact Assessments on 
the “2050 long -term strategy” as well as for the higher2030 targets, the power and gas systems 
should work together symbiotically towards decarbonisation. 
• H2 for flexible and climate neutral power generation:  The goal to reach climate neutrality in power 
generation by 2040 requires an H2 pipeline connection to gas-fired power plants by 2035 at the latest. 
• Open approach for the heat sector: While further electrification of heat may provide a suitable solu-
tion for some European member states, others rely more on the existing gas grids. Depending on the 
region or local conditions, the otherwise needed strong extension and reinforcement of the electricity 
grid and renovation of buildings, urban infrastructure and industrial patterns can make the use and 
adaptation of the existing gas grid more suitable as a quicker, more cost -efficient decarbonisation 
option. 
• Technological neutrality and market mechanisms concerning blending:  Using gas-hydrogen blends 
in the short and medium term achieves a larger GHG reduction at a lower systemic cost than by using 
only new dedicated infrastructure to deliver hydrogen.  
• Development of European H2-Backbone and infrastructure for imports: Estimations for the German 
market point out, that in 2050 around 80% of H2 will need to be imported.  
• A comprehensive network development plan and holistic view for electricity, gas and H2 : The inclu-
sion of H2 in the existing network planning procedures is essential. 
 
3 A more detailed statement 
 
1. Should the hydrogen related elements of the initiative may either be addressed  by a 
revision of the above instruments or by a new legal instrument? 
 
• Assuming that natural gas will be gradually replaced by H2 and renewable/decarbonized gases  
in all applications/sectors , including the power and heat sector , EnBW believes that H2 should 
be integrated into the existing gas market regulation together with renewable/low-carbon gases 
including biomethane. This is mainly supported by the needed speed of implementation  (blue-
print of the existing gas regulation is available). The existing regulatory framework for natural 
gas is an established and proven framework  that is familiar to all market participants and pro-
vides a certain degree of long-term predictability and reliability for all stakeholders (society, reg-
ulators, investors). 
• Due to the monopoly position of a future public hydrogen network, it is generally necessary to 
include hydrogen in the network regulation. Considering the special challenges of rapidly devel-
oping a liquid market, expanding the existing regulation for natural gas, is the quickest and most 
effective way. 
• It is important to establish a regulatory framework at EU level, which prevent from market frag-
mentation due to differing implementation at Member State level. If each country decides on its 
own H2 regulation, there will not be a strong and fast emergence of a European hydrogen econ-
omy.

• Regional island networks/clusters in which H2 is produced and consumed would hinder a com-
prehensive transformation , as these structures are difficult to  be integrated later on into a Eu-

• The development of a European hydrogen network must happen quickly to meet the climate tar-
gets in the given ambitious time frame. Climate neutrality in power generation by 2040 requires 
an H2 pipeline connection to gas-fired power plants by 2035 at the latest. 
 
 
2. How to avoid that demand for natural gas is locked-in? 
 
• EnBW is convinced that  a forward-looking expansion of the existing gas infrastructure will not 
result in a natural gas lock-in effect, as applications can be converted from natural gas to climate 
neutral gases. This applies to new gas-fired power plants as well as to heat generation technol-
ogies for buildings, commerce and industry. 
• Hydrogen robustness is already being considered in investment decisions today. This contributes 
to prevent a natural gas lock-in effect already today. 
• Legal requirements for minimum tolerances for H2 in end -devices would strongly prevent nat-
ural gas lock-in. 
 
 
3. Is ensuring more competition in the market e.g. through third party access and network 
access rules and guarantees for neutral hydrogen network operations a feasible way? 
 
• EnBW recommends that  the existing unbundling rules for production, transport, trading and 
sales should also apply for a hydrogen system. 
 
 
4. How to foster the emergence of liquid hydrogen markets and their integration  in other 
energy markets? 
 
• EnBW would recommend to focus on assuring a climate-neutral production of hydrogen. There-
fore, technology neutrality should prevail with regard to green, blue and turquoise hydrogen, as 
well as consideration of biogenic hydrogen from biogas. This means that on the commodity mar-
ket, no distinction should be made between the different production variants, and on the certi fi-
cate market, in principal, a distinction should only be made according to the GHG footprint for 
the proof of the green property. 
• For the rapid and successful scale-up of a liquid H2 market, it is particularly important not only 
to establish rapidly a European H2 production , but also a European H2 backbone network, as 
well as import structures also from non-EU countries. A major impetus would be the creation of 
an efficient international market for climate -neutral gases. This requires a predictable growth 
in hydrogen demand on the demand side. 
• In the field of green hydrogen, a liquid market cannot be achieved without globally installed 
large-scale electrolysers in the 500-1000 MW size class. 
• A fundamental distinction should be made between a commodity market for hydrogen and a 
market for the "climate property"  (origin, CO2 footprint, sustainability criteria). The need for a 
separation already stems from the need that spot prices on the commodity market should reflect

5. How to achieve a cost-effective development, including through repurposing of existing 
natural gas infrastructure of dedicated hydrogen networks? 
 
• The energy transition should be ecologically, economically, and socially fair. Pushing the heat 
demand largely into the electricity sector could strongly increase the price of ele ctricity for end 
customers due to the subsequent need for a considerable expansion of electricity grids (DC and 
three-phase grids) and the allocation  of these costs  to electricity grid charges. Depending on 
regional or local conditions, the use and adaptation of the existing gas grid to H2 would be more 
economical.  
• It should be borne in mind that the development of a European hydrogen network (and its repur-
posing) must take place quickly to meet the climate targets. In particular, climate neutrality in 
power generation  from 2040 requires an H2 pipeline connection to gas -fired power plants by 
2035 at the latest. 
• Our analyses demonstrate that the conversion of today's natural gas networks to hydrogen  is 
feasible with limited costs , as in large parts the existing infrastructure can be used, which is at 
least in Germany already largely "hydrogen-ready" today. 
• Despite the high complexity of electricity and gas network planning, it seems desirable to inter-
link the electricity network development plan as closely as possible with gas network expansion 
planning and the hydrogen strategy. In our view, the primary goal should be to harmonize the 
framework conditions for electricity and gas network expansion as far as possible.  
• We assume that hydrogen will mainly be transported by pipeline . Hydrogen can be transported 
very well by pipeline. Although hydrogen has a lower energy density than natural gas, it flows 
through a pipeline at almost three times the speed of methane (natural gas) due to its good flow 
properties. Overall, a pipeline fully loaded with hydrogen can transport about 80% of the energy 
volume of natural gas. 
• A key factor in determining the advantages of using pipelines is whether existing infrastructure 
can be used. Pipeline rea ssignment can reduce hydrogen delivery cost by at least 60%  
(https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0360319920307023 ). 
• From the current perspective , the existing transport infrastructure for natural gas can largely 
continue to be used for the transport of hydrogen. Relatively minor adjustments are required for 
this (e.g., different compressors and different measuring equipment are necessary ). 
• In addition to the rededication of pipeline sections, new construction of H2 pipelines will also be 
necessary in some places to create a continuous H2 network.

7. Why and how is facilitating access of bio- and synthetic-methane to the infrastructure 
(pipelines, storages and LNG terminals) and the market necessary? 
 
• Biomethane production remains too low to replace natural gas demand. Therefore, H2 is needed, 
which can fill the natural gas demand through infrastructure rededication. Biomethane also has 
a role to play, but more at a local/regional level. It should be considered that with the conversion 
to hydrogen, the methane pipeline network may become less dense. 
• We expect that Biomethane will be increasingly used in industry for high temperature production 
as well as for material use. 
 
 
8. Concerning sectors, in what areas should decarbonised gaseous fuels be expected to 
come into play? 
 
• EnBW strongly advocates for an open approach, i. e. H2 use should be possible in all sectors  - 
industry and mobility, as well as heat and power generation - via methanation probably only in 
individual areas due to higher costs. A premature exclusion for the use of hydrogen in a specific 
sector should also be avoided for reasons of market efficiency. 
 
9. What are the important reasons for the use of renewable and low -carbon gases in the 
heating sector? 
 
• We assume that the future structure of the heating market will be characterized by a heat mix of 
different technologies, with hydrogen being the predominant element in the gas distribution net-
work. It would be economically inefficient not to use the well-developed infrastructure of the gas 
distribution network (and possibly to dismantle it at high cost) and at the same time to multiply 
the number of power lines (in the case of a purely electricity-based heat supply). 
• Renewable and low -carbon gases in the  heating sector have the advantage of providing quick 
wins and decisive emissions reductions. This will also ensure network stability, which will play 
an essential role to support the switch to a renewable energy system. 
• An all-electric approach is neither realistic nor economically feasible. For example, a demand of 
at least 26 GWh/h gas capacity (the network development plan 2020 -2030 even envisages an ex-
pansion to 36 GWh/h) for heat supply in Baden -Württemberg would also have to be covered by 
electricity.

10. Why is an increase of flexibility of the power system needed? How to store and produce 
again electricity? 
 
• The conversion of hydrogen into electricity via gas turbines will be the technical standard in the 
next few decades. H2 admixtures could be used to achieve GHG reduction effects at an early 
stage. 
• This would also ensure network stability, which will play an essential role to support the switch 
to a renewable energy system 
 
 
11. Why and how should hydrogen blending be among the solutions? 
 
• The blending of H2 in the natural gas grid in combination with, an incentive instrument on the demand 
side are decisive drivers for a rapid ramp-up of the hydrogen market. 
• Regulatory standards for H2 blending  and for the market ramp -up of climate-neutral gases should 
therefore be created at the European level, differentiated according to transmission and distribution 
networks, so that harmonized H2 blending becomes possible very quickly throughout the EU. As long 
as we do not have a comprehensive H2 infrastructure, the blending of H2 and other climate -neutral 
gases is an efficient way to decarbonize all sectors. 
• Using gas-hydrogen blends in the short and medium term achieves a larger GHG reduction at a lower 
systemic cost than by using only new dedicated infrastructure to deliver hydrogen  to specific cus-
tomers. 
• It allows for building-up hydrogen produc tion capacity and developing economies of scale that will 
foster a positive business case for the transition to a hydrogen system.  
• As for sector coupling between electricity and gas, it enhances system -wide resilience by possibly 
integrating surplus interm ittent renewable electricity,  reducing power network congestion, and 
providing short-term flexibility and energy storage. 
• Injecting hydrogen into the gas distribution grids would provide renewable and low-carbon energy to 
consumers already connected to the gas networks. 
• Regarding production of thermal energy, hydrogen preserves its value when mixed with natural gas. 
 
Therefore, we would like to encourage the Commission to expand the scope of its Impact Assessment to 
also address the relevance of blending as a market creating tool.

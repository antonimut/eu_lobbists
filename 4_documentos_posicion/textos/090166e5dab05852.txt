 
Trade Disputes-Mechanism to deter and counteract coercive action 
by non-EU countries 
 
Cerame-Unie’s reply to the Public Consultation on a proposal for a regulation establishing an 
instrument to deter and counteract coercive actions by third countries 
 
16 March 2021, Brussels 
 
Cerame-Unie, the ‘European Ceramic Industry Association’, welcomes the opportunity to partake in 
the public consultation and provide feedback on the Inception Impact Assessment published on 17 
February 2021 by the European Commission.  
 
The EU ceramic industry supports the establishment of a mechanism to reach against coercive actions 
by third countries and look forward to contributing to the upcoming consultation to equip the EU with 
a new instrument to counteract unilateral retaliation against EU operators.  
  
With 1/3 of production exported outside of the European Union, the European ceramic industry is well 
positioned to understand the impact of coercive practices in global trade. The ceramic sector has been 
particularly exposed to these practices by non-EU countries in recent years, as retaliation has become 
increasingly common for a wide range of industries.  
 
In the context of the WTO Boeing-Airbus trade disputes, for instance, ceramic products were originally 
part of the list of tariff lines subject t o additional duties published by the US Trade Representative in 
2019; after several revisions by the US administration, ceramic products have been exempted from 
the retaliation and currently appear in the list of “products under consideration”. Nevertheless, some 
ceramic sectors have been indirectly affected as input in the supply chain of other goods subject to 
higher tariff lines (i.e. aircrafts).     
 
Similarly, the U.S. administration threatened to impose additional ad valorem duties of 25% on ceramic 
tableware originating from France in retaliation to the Digital Service Tax adopted by the French 
administration in 2019.  Despite French tableware has been eventually excluded in July 2020 from the 
final tariff backlash, this has revealed once again the high exposure of the ceramic industry to coercive 
behaviours and trade retaliation. 
 
Furthermore, an effective policy tool to address these practices will prove important in view of the 
upcoming initiative on a Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM). Trad e retaliation is a key 
challenge to implementing an EU CBAM as some trading partners have already threatened reaction 
against potential carbon border measures. In an increasingly politicised economic environment, it will 
be crucial to deter other countries  from retaliating and to attenuate the negative effects of these 
coercions with effective and prompt policy measures, able to counteract to the responsible trade 
partners.  
 
Cerame-Unie looks forward to participating in the upcoming public consultation and to set out its 
position on this important initiative.

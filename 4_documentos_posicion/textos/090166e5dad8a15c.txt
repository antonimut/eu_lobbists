 
Build Europe 
Feedback on Energy efficiency – Revision of the Energy Performance of Buildings Directive 
Deadline: 22 March 2021 
 
Build Europe and its members consider that the EPBD has been successful in achieving its 
goals of increasing the energy performance of new buildings. However, this did not achieve 
cost-optimal solutions for European citizens at the same time , as the increase in the energy 
performance of buildings has also raised construction costs and, consequently, the price of 
new dwellings. 
 
Our Members support t he European C ommission's commitment to wards ambitious  
environmental goals, but Build Europe believes that the European Commission should refrain 
from developing new stringent energy requirements on the construction of new dwellings, as 
this would only worsen the current housing affordability crisis by increasing the price of 
buildings. In its communication to the European Parliament, the Council, the European 
Economic and Social Committee and the Committee of the Regions titled “A Renovation Wave 
for Europe ”, the European Commission recognises the fact that “ new buildings today 
consume half of the energy compared to similar new buildings 20 years ago ”. The current 
energy requirements for new buildings already provide EU citizens with sustainable housing 
stocks.  
 
It is important for the European Commission to acknowledge that t he building sector has 
contributed the most to absolute reductions in emissions in the sectors covered by the Effort 
Sharing legislation since 1990. Today we have the technical capacity of constructing nearly 
zero, zero and even energy positive buildings. It is however an economic challenge to make 
such buildings cost -effective and p rofitable for the buyers . As the European Commission 
focuses on “encouraging investment in eco -friendly solutions”, Build Europe expects that EU 
directives will create incentives for the construction of new build and for operations of 
demolition and rebuild, since these would contribute significantly to the reduction of the EU’s 
overall emissions without causing an increase in the total cost of housing for first-time buyers 
and tenants.  
 
There is no doubt that additional regulations would end up worsening the affordability crisis 
in a very difficult time, when European citizens need more than ever a home where they can 
live, work, and study. These regulations  would also create additional burdens for all the 
companies and workers in the construction sector. We believe that the European Commission 
should create new employment opportunities rather than creating difficulties for European 
entrepreneurs in order to tackle the harsh economic consequences of the Covid-19 recession. 
 
While the European Commission seems ready to provide funds for the renovation of existing 
buildings, we believe it would be crucial to also guarantee funding for the construction of new 
buildings, which could ensure 1) a smaller environmental impact, 2) an environment more in 
line with the current needs of the citizens’ in terms of liveability, space and comfort, and 3) a 
better energy performance.  
 
Therefore, in relation to the objectives and policy options  on energy efficiency listed by the 
European Commission, Build Europe opposes  the option of amending the EPBD to increase 
the ambition towards building decarbonisation. Developers and constructors need funds and 
support to keep providing new sustainable and affordable buildings to the European citizens,

especially the ones affected by the Covid-19 crisis, and creating further obstacles would only 
further aggravate the difficulties faced by millions of households in the EU to access decent 
and affordable housing . Bui ld Europe urges the European Commission to consider the 
importance that housing will play in the lives of our fellow citizens in the coming years, as 
families are expected to spend more time in their homes , which have become safe spaces 
dedicated to work and study. 
 
We would also like to remind the Commission that the Energy Performance of Buildings 
Directive was already reviewed in 2018 and that companies need time to adapt to the most 
recent requirements. We believe that providing companies with a reasonable amount of time 
to efficiently adopt legislative measures is particularly important now, when many enterprises 
and businesses have been damaged by the restrictions due to the Covid crisis and its 
consequences. The next review should therefore take place – as expected - in 2025/2026, so 
to leave sufficient time to SMEs and companies. Therefore, Build Europe is not favourable to 
the opening of a public consultation on this issue in 2021. 
 
For the aforementioned reasons, Build Europe also believes that the EPBD should remain as 
it is, without any modification until the review envisaged for 2026 by Article 19 EPBD  
2018/844.

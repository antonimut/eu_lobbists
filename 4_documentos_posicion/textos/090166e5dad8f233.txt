 Feedback to EPBD Inception Impact Assessment 
 
BPIE welcomes the opportunity to give feedback on the EPBD Inception Impact Assessment  (IIA) in view 
of the revision planned in Q4 2021. The Commission has decided to open the EPBD for revision earlier than 
its planned review (by 1st January 2026 according to Article 19) to align legislation with the ambition of the 
Renovation Wave Strategy. While we welcome the revision process to start earlier than scheduled, we call 
on the Commission to undertake a comprehensive, rather than targeted, revision of the Directive , and 
this, for two reasons. 
 
The first reason is that we need to align the EPBD with the ambition of the updated 2030 climate target 
and more importantly, with the climate neutrality objective by 2050. The Commission states in its IIA that 
this revision should aim at delivering on the Renovation Wave objective of doubling the renovation ra te 
and fostering deep renovation. Our research (read here: https://www.bpie.eu/wp-
content/uploads/2020/12/On-the-way-to-a-climate-neutral-Europe-_Final.pdf) shows that th is is not 
enough to contribute to a 2030 climate target of at least 55% GHG emissions reduction. According to BPIE’s 
analysis, the EPBD revision should aim at reaching an annual 3%  deep renovation rate by 2030 . 
Moreover, our recent analysis of a selection of LTRS (read here: https://www.bpie.eu/wp-
content/uploads/2021/03/BPIE_LTRS-_10-1.pdf) shows that while some of them comply with the legal 
text of the EPBD Article 2A, none of them are in line with a full decarbonisation of the building stock by 
2050. This reinforces the need to make the EPBD not only “fit for 55” (2030) but also aligned with climate 
neutrality by 2050 (‘fit for 2050’). 
 
The second reason for a comprehensive revision rather than targeted review of the Directive, is to make 
sure that all provisions, whether existing, or newly introduced, will be coherent and build on each other 
to deliver on the targets and goals described above. Considering the challenge to deliver the commitment 
of the Paris Agreement , disregarding the need to address the overall architecture o f the EPBD  would 
inevitably lead to a sub-optimal result. Consequently, we invite the Commission to go beyond options 2 & 
3 currently outlined in the IIA.  
 
Some EPBD provisions, which are not included in the document, need to be re-opened as well, be it for 
aligning them with the climate ambition or for enabling other (new) provisions to work on a good basis. In 
that regard, we can mention inter alia:  
- the cost-optimal methodology used to set minimum energy performance requirements , introduced 
into EU legislation a long time ago and now out of sync with current climate ambition . The 
methodology should now factor in quantified multiple benefits of deep renovation; 
- the major renovation definition needs to be addressed with the introduction of the ‘deep renovation 
standard’ and MEPS; 
- the national LTRS, whose ambition and requirements should be updated in line with climate neutrality 
by 2050. 
It is also unclear from the IIA how the Commission plans to exactly address the decarbonisation of H&C, 
as phasing out fossil fuels in existing buildings is not mentioned.

In any case, the EPBD revision  should never be only about legislation (option 3) or only about non -
regulatory measures (option 2), but options 2 & 3 should go hand in hand  so as to tackle the “barriers” 
listed by the Commission in the document. Technical Assistance, one-stop-shops and financing enable the 
implementation of legislation, while stringent legislation ensures take -up of financing , one -stop-shops, 
and Technical Assistance. In that sense, option 2 should no longer be considered as ‘enabling framework’ 
or weaker alternative, but as ‘essential framework’ alongside measures under option 3. 
 
While BPIE recommends a comprehensive revision of the EPBD, there are some key principles that we 
would like to share in order to ensure an optimal design of proposed policy measures (under option 3):  
- Policies shall respect and operationalize t he Energy Efficiency First principle , while preparing for 
Whole Life Carbon (WLC) considerations. This means that the design of policy options should enable 
carbon performance as a (future) complement to energy performance, while keeping a balanced focus 
between the different outputs (energy savings, carbon emissions reduction, Indoor Environmental 
Quality, comfort, and affordability). Preparing for WLC considerations would be relevant for the design 
of MEPS, EPCs / BRPs / Digital Building Logbooks, but also cost-optimal methodologies or the definition 
of an updated standard for new buildings;   
- Policies shall be designed having in mind the potential impact of other possible linked legislative 
provisions (such as the introduction of carbon pricing for heating, or a minimum share of RES  supply 
in or on buildings ), and policies shall also identify and enable synergies while avoid contradictions 
between them (for example, the link between the introduction of MEPS and the reform of EPCs should 
be carefully assessed); 
- Policies shall be impactful (aligned with climate neutrality by 2050 ), while workable (enforced and 
implemented by the national to the local level); 
- Policies should lead to the transformation of the whole building stock, and this transformation shall 
start before 2030. We cannot afford to only address in the next decade a selected number of segments 
of the building stock or only focus on the very worst performing buildings. 
 
Finally, when it comes to the methodology used for the Impact Assessment, BPIE would like to underline 
that over-relying on past Impact Assessments and models used for other legislative revisions, as foreseen 
in this IIA, entails the risks of: 
- using outdated assumptions and data,  
- overlooking specific characteristics of the buildings sector compared to other sectors,  
- ignoring technological and business model developments in support of positive energy buildings and 
districts or in support of serial/industrial-scale renovations 
- dismissing the multiple benefits of transforming the building sector.  
All this might lead to policy options being put forward in the upcoming EPBD proposal which are sub-
optimal and not aligned with the EU’s 2030 and 2050 objectives.

All in all, a light and targeted revision of the EPBD would (1) not put us on a climate neutral compatible 
path, and (2) entail a high risk of creating  / sustaining  inconsistencies, leading to non- or bad  
implementation of new and existing provisions. Thus, BPIE calls for a comprehensive and ambitious 
revision of the EPBD. We remain at the disposal of the Commission over the coming months and during 
the consultation process, to further discuss in more detail ideas and suggestions on specific policy options 
outlined in the Commission document.

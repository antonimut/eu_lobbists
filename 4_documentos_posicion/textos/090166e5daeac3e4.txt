 
Feedback on European Commission’s Inception Impact Assessment on the Energy Performance 
of Buildings Directive 
The VELUX Group welcomes the opportunity to comment on the European Commission’s Inception Impact Assessment 
on the Revision of the Energy Performance of Buildings Directive (EPBD). 
A new paradigm – Lifetime CO2 reductions in buildings 
The first version of the EPBD was published almost 20 years ago (Directive 2002/91/EC). It provided Member States with 
a methodology to set up an energy calculation of their building stock but also set out clear requirements for Nearly Zero 
Energy Buildings (nZEB) for new buildings . This nZEB ambition has led to a reduction of  CO2 emissions from the use 
phase, representing now only ~25% of the total CO2 emissions from the building stock. The remaining ~75% of the CO2 
emissions come from the sourcing and manufacturing of materials, transport and construction work, maintenance as 
well as end of life and reuse processes1. 
In order to meet the 1.50C target in the Paris Agreement, future EU legislation on buildings should reflect CO2 reductions 
throughout the full lifetime of buildings and not only the use phase, which for new buildings is no  longer the area with 
the highest CO2 emissions . The development of a lifetime perspective on CO2 emissions from buildings is already 
reflected in legislative proposals in several Member States like France, The Netherlands and Denmark and addressed in 
the European Commission’s development of Level(s) as well as in the Basis Work Requirement 7 in the upcoming revised 
Construction Product Regulation. It is therefore important that the revised EPBD includes a methodology on how 
Member States shall integrate lifetime CO2 emissions from buildings with clear  targets for both new build and 
renovations by 2025 and 2030. 
Deliver energy savings in existing buildings to reduce CO2 emissions 
We agree with the Commission’s assessment that existing legislation will not suffice and that a revision of the EPBD is 
necessary to deliver on the Renovation Wave and European Green Deal objectives , i.e. achieve 55% net emissions 
reduction by 2030; become climate neutral by 2050 and at least double annual renovation rates . Indeed, a recent BPIE 
report on National Long -term Renovation Strategies clearly showed that Member States’ current  renovation plans are 
not ambitious enough.  We therefore fully support Option 3 as described in the Inception Impact Assessment . 
Particularly given that energy renovation of buildings has been identi fied as a key European flagship for national 
recovery plans, we should make sure to invest in measures which lead to energy efficient and sustainable buildings.  
Make the European building stock healthy for all Europeans 
We see the upcoming revision as an opportunity to secure the introduction or the update of a coherent set of measures 
which will enable the green transition  of Europe’s building stock while at the same time creating healthier living 
conditions for all Europeans. The long-term performance of our buildings will heavily rely on their resilience and ability 
to match the residents’ needs and expectations.  We therefore very much welcome the preliminary assessment by the 
Commission on the expected economic, social and environmental impact of a strengthened EPBD, particularly the 
reference to the wider benefits of energy renovations, “ making buildings healthier and im proving wellbeing and 
productivity”. We call on the European Commission to conduct a comprehensive impact assessment, looking into all 
parameters (especially health and indoor climate) to ensure future efforts lead to sustainable buildings with an optimal 
indoor climate. Only focusing on the energy performance of buildings would overlook the needs of the occupants and 
would also generate a risk of counter-performance on the envisaged energy savings, as energy performance of a building 
is intrinsically linked to the occupants' behavior . When indoor climate is ignored, renovations can induce unexpected 
energy demands such as cooling (when summer comfort has not been considered) or lightin g (when daylight has not 
been considered) . These aspects are also important in the context of incentivising people to conduct deep energy 
renovation projects: A Navigant study (2019), commissioned by the European Commission, found that the most relevant 
aspects of energy renovation for consumers are not the energy  savings themselves, but the cost savings and making 
their home more comfortable and healthier.

Utilise passive solutions to reduce CO2 emissions where possible 
Buildings should be designed for people to live, work, play  and being educated within . In order to create the best 
conditions, indoor temperature, comfort, air quality and light have to be managed through different systems. Buildings 
should be designed so the risk of using too much energy to manage the above parameters is minimised. We therefore 
recommend that the upcoming revision specifically focuses on methodologies that support passive systems to develop 
a good indoor climate. 
Windows and other transparent areas have the ability to allow the intake of free solar gains through a building’s 
envelope. This characteristic – known as Energy Balance – should be factored in by  Member States as the appropriate 
product criterion in national regulations on building modernisation. Energy Balance and the benefits of free solar gains 
are currently only accounted in energy calculations for new buildings and major renovations (as stated in Annex I 4. (a)), 
but are disregarded for minimum product requirements which exclusively focus on heat losses (i.e. “Uw” for windows). 
This situation leads to underestimating the energy benefits of transparent products in the building envelope. 
Specific comments on the Inception Impact Assessment 
The Inception Impact Assessment represents a good starting point . Based on our above comments, key components of 
a strengthened EPBD are: 
• A strong focus on minimum requirements for Indoor Environmental Quality (IEQ)  by addressing health and 
comfort (Daylight, Air Quality, Summer Comfort…) in nZEB definitions to secure the long-term resilience of the 
European building stock and align design criteria with the needs of end-users: 
o Daylight should be a significant source of illumination for all spaces  as it is strongly favored by 
building occupants and has a significant energy saving potential. The European Standard, EN 
17037:2018 Daylight in Buildings, defines metrics to ensure adequate daylighting conditions and 
reduced energy consumption, by applying the principles of the European EPP Standards, EN 15193-
1, which specify methods for evaluating energy performance of lighting systems, including linkage 
with building automation control systems (BACS), and other indoor environmental parameters, 
supported by EN 16798-1. They are critical to support the health and wellbeing of occupants. 
• A methodology and  targets for Member States on factoring in lifetime CO2 emissions  (embodied carbon)  
from buildings, as suggested in the Inception Impact Assessment; 
• The phased introduction of mandatory Minimum Energy Performance Standards (MEPS) for all building types 
to secure that ambitious national renovation objectives are met; 
• An improved framework for Energy Performance Certificates, especially the inclusion of additional 
information, to ensure that key renovation triggers are harnessed, and that essential information on the wider 
benefits of energy renovations, e.g. health and comfort is available for all Europeans; 
• A stronger link of Article 2A on the Long-term Renovation Strategy for all building segments with Article 5 of 
the Energy Efficiency Directive  to ensure the renovation of all public buildings is fully integrated into  future 
national plans and not only the quantity but also the quality of future energy renovations is addressed . 
To conclude, we are currently working on the 2021 edition of the Healthy Homes Barometer, our annual report which 
takes the pulse of Europe’s building stock. This year’s edition will contain economic modelling of the costs and benefits 
of a healthy EU building stock by 2050 and focus on the role of buildings in creating sustainable communities. We hope 
our research can provide helpful data for the upcoming work on the EPBD and EED revision. 
We look forward to supporting the European Commission throughout the revision process.

Solar Heat Europe contribution 
to the open consultation on EPBD impact assessment 
 
Solar Heat Europe strongly supports the idea that buildings decarbonisation will be key for the 
achievement of Europe’s 2030 and 2050 climate and energy objectives. In this content, the revision of the 
EPBD is necessary to ensure an effective renovation strategy and a smooth implementation of the Fit for 
55 Package. 
The amendment of the directive (opt.3) should be complemented with reinforced non -regulatory policy 
instruments and measures providing technical assistance (opt.2), to address key issues in the context of 
building decarbonisation like the lack of awareness on energy solutions and incentives, and the absence 
of highly skilled professionals. 
Two thirds of heating and cooling (H&C) in buildings comes from fossil fuels showing that strong actions 
are needed. Solar Heat Europe advocates for a comprehensive Renovation Wave, with a strategy focused 
on an optimal combination of energy efficiency a nd initiatives to promote the modernisation of heating 
and cooling systems switching to locally produced renewable heat (RES-H).  
To ensure that this switch happens in a timely and affordable way, the replacement of old and inefficient 
systems with renewable ones should be staged and planned in advance. When heating appliances break 
down unexpectedly, consumers will replace the old ones with similar products often powered with fossil 
fuels. Considering that H&C appliances have an average lifetime of 20 years, emergency replacement must 
be avoided to achieve carbon neutrality by 2050. Consumers aware of the conditions of their current 
systems and the renewable alternatives available, would more easily plan this change and move away 
from fossil-based heating. For this, the planned replacement of H&C systems must be further promoted 
and supported in the revised EPBD. 
The objective for the EU building stock is to be highly efficient and decarbonised by 2050, thus the links 
between the EPBD and REDII must be stren gthened with the further development of the Long -term 
Renovation Strategies (LTRSs). The LTRSs shall design building renovation strategies to increase energy 
savings and decarbonise heating and cooling by implementing RES -H systems on -site or nearby the 
buildings. These measures will also improve transparency and planning at local level. In addition, stronger 
efficiency measures will also facilitate the integration of low temperature RES-H, like solar thermal.  
To achieve a higher share of RES -heat sources is crucial to have solar-friendly building regulations, 
including an obligation for solar thermal collectors for heat generation and PV for power consumed in the 
building. Building renovation strategies and new codes shall promote, besides EE, the use of a vailable 
space for solar thermal energy. This can be facilitated with regard to façades or rooftops, meaning that 
buildings must be designed to have suitable space for solar heat collector and sufficient indoor space for 
the technical equipment. For instan ce, chimney outlets and other elements should be on the perimeter 
of the north side of the roof. The integration of solar thermal solutions shall also be an important element 
of the New European Bauhaus.

In this context, art.7 must be revised to prevent th e support to fossil -fuel based systems only, rather 
promoting the use of RES-heat or hybrid solutions, to ensure a gradual change towards a 100% renewable 
future. 
Finally, despite the EED, Energy Labelling Directive (ELD) and EPBD being separate regulations, they should 
become more interconnected. As in the EED, the ELD is applying the energy savings concept (even though 
on product label), whilst the EPBD is looking into the same principles at system and buildings levels. The 
lack of a strong connection amo ng these directives is a limitation and a gap that must be bridged to 
reinforce their connections. In particular, the EED can be an enabler for exploring further synergies 
between ELD and EPBD.

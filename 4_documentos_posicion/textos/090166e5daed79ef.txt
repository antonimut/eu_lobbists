Feedback on 
 
Energy efficiency – Revision of the  
Energy Performance of Buildings Directive 
 
 
March 22, 2021 
 
 
 
The Austrian Federal Economic Chamber is the legal representative of the entire Austrian business 
community and represents all Austrian companies – some 540,000 businesses drawn from the areas 
of Crafts and Trade, Industry, Commerce, Banking and Insurance, Information and Consultancy, 
Tourism and Leisure, Transportation and Communication. 85 % of our members are SME  with less 
than ten employees.

The Austrian Federal Economic Chamber (WKÖ) welcomes the initiative to revise the 
Energy Performance of Buildings Directive. It is a necessary step towards contributing to 
achieving the goals of the European Green Deal. Investment in buildings can provide a 
boost to businesses. In the light of the COVID-19 pandemic, this will be a necessary driver 
for the economy and central to the EU’s green recovery. In view of increased 
unemployment rates, it is essential to put a focus on the know-how and competences of 
the workforce in the renovation industry. Future-oriented job profiles that meet upskilling 
requirements must be developed. 
 
SMEs are backbone for renovation wave 
It is important that the demands of small and medium-sized businesses (SMEs), which form 
the backbone of the Austrian economy, are appropriately reflected by the adapted 
directive. However, overarching administrative burden and obligations must be avoided. 
We are in favor of a practical and future-proof revision of the energy certificate e.g., 
based on regular inspections of heating systems in existing buildings by authorized service 
providers. By contrast, we reject the introduction of a Building Renovation Passport. This 
would be an additional burden for SMEs as well as jeopardize trade and business secrets. 
Instead, we prefer digital product information on a voluntary basis.  
It must be ensured that companies producing high-value raw materials or components for 
constructions1 are still be located in the EU. With stricter climate policy requirements, 
comprehensive and improved carbon leakage protection must be guaranteed. 
 
National circumstances must be considered 
WKÖ generally agrees with the plan to increase the renovation rate. Only with a 
respectively high rate the climate targets in the building sector can be achieved. However, 
we take a critical view of a mandatory, general European renovation rate or non-flexible 
EU-wide minimum requirements. Geographical, meteorological, and cultural differences in 
Europe, as well as the different levels of renovation, make such targets unrealistic and 
must be considered. 
Member States are already obliged to set national targets thorough the Governance 
Regulation. In this regard, the national CO2 reduction target is the determining factor 
anyway. It is important that there are no mandatory targets for building owners, since this 
could make access to subsidies more difficult on the one hand, and for social reasons on 
the other hand.  
 
Choice of materials and technologies must be individually assessed 
Building targets, including those for renovation, should be applied in a technology-neutral 
manner. That means, that no material or technology should be favored unilaterally and in 
a way that distorts competition. Instead of a preference for a particular material or 
technology, we propose combining the advantages of all technologies to enable a climate-
neutral building sector in 2050. This is not only beneficial for the economy and the 
environment, but also helps to preserve the regional building tradition. Especially the

Achieving goals through incentives 
The Commission wants to create incentives and a framework to promote the renovation of 
buildings. We welcome these plans, but at the same time we would like to point out that 
these incentives and conditions must be designed in a technology-neutral way. 
In addition to funding instruments in the narrower sense, tax measures that provide an 
incentive for households and businesses to implement additional energy efficiency and 
climate mitigation measures in buildings could be a powerful instrument. It would be 
advisable to predefine tax incentives that comply with EU law and that a Member State 
can use at any time without the need for extensive notification procedures.  
In general, businesses need planning security. Therefore, incentives need to be predictable 
as well as stable and should not be decided on a year-by-year basis.  
 
Renovation wave offers benefits for all stakeholders 
It is important that both tenants, and owners benefit from refurbishment measures. For 
this purpose, the legal parameters must be defined. This is the only way to generate the 
owners’ interest in improvements. Otherwise, there is a risk that owners will only have to 
cover the costs without benefiting from the improvements. Legal barriers that make 
renovation more difficult must be removed. 
 
Awareness raising as a key to success 
As a complementary step, campaigns to raise awareness and provide information are 
urgently needed. Currently the benefits or renovation, lack of trust in the energy savings 
that renovation will achieve, burdensome procedures are some of the biggest obstacles 
that hinder renovation. Thus, raising awareness and better communication of the broader 
benefits of sustainable buildings (especially in terms of cost savings) is as well necessary in 
terms of decarbonization, sustainability for generations and society. 
 
Stable, predictable, and comprehensible EU policy framework needed 
The building sector has to be considered as a holistic energy system. Renovations must not 
be seen only as thermal renovation in the narrow sense. We welcome the fact that energy-
related renovation is the focus of EU measures in the strategy. Only in this way the 
broadest possible spectrum can be addressed (e.g. intelligent control systems in 
lighting/smart lighting solutions or smart measurement and control systems for heating, 
cooling and ventilation systems make a significant contribution to saving electricity while 
at the same time reducing CO2 emissions). Selective individual measures (e.g. only 
promoting insulation) will not lead to the intended result. At most, they will fulfill 
individual interests and miss the overall goal. 
The aim must be to preserve the building substance and bring it closer to the state of the 
art, as well as to integrate it into the energy and anergy networks and thus use the 
buildings as energy hubs and storage facilities. A holistic approach is crucial in this sense.

Conflicting goals must be identified in advance and the goals must be prioritized among 
each other or brought to a balance.  
As part of a holistic approach, regulatory interactions and existing conflicting objectives 
must be addressed. The European Green Deal provides a good framework strategy in which 
initiatives must be developed in a well-coordinated manner. Thus, a cross-sectorial 
guidance and streamlined legislation is necessary, covering consistency between legal acts2 
and synergies with other European Green Deal initiative3. As financing is also essential the 
Sustainable Finance framework, covering especially the Taxonomy Regulation, must be 
considered too. 
Buildings are an essential element for the energy system integration. Especially in the field 
of renewable energies and sector integration, we have to think outside the box and focus 
not only on renewable electricity but also on climate-friendly gas and climate-friendly 
heating/cooling.  
 
Appropriate instruments must be developed to promote deep renovation, 
whereby we see a need for action especially in the non-regulatory area, 
covering reinforced non-regulatory policy instruments and additional 
guidance and support measures, such as technical assistance, information 
campaigns, training, project financing etc.

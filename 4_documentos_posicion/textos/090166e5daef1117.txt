Inception Impact Assessment of the Revision of the Energy Performance of 
Buildings Directive  
The Views of the Enel Group 
 
Enel warmly welcomes the  Commission Inception Impact Assessment to review the Directive 
2010/31/EU and amending Directive 2018/844/EU on Energy Performance of Buildings, as the “Energy 
Efficiency First” principle constitutes one of the main pillars of the EU’s fight against climate change  
and a successful and sustainable energy transition . The decarbonization of the buildings sector is crucial 
to deliver on the EU’s 2030 and 2050 climate and energy objectives, given that buildings are responsible for 
40% of total energy consumption and 36% of energy-related greenhouse gas emissions in the EU.  
Enel supports the Commission’s proposal of an  increased EU decarbonization ambition by 2030  to 
reduce GHG emissions at least 50% and towards 55% compared to 1990 levels in a responsible way,  
and to reach  climate neutrality in the EU by 2050.  Enel has set itself the obj ective of reaching the 
decarbonization of its energy mix by 2050.  Upscaling climate action through the energy transition will deliver 
substantial benefits in terms of economic and resource efficiency, reduction of global and local pollution, 
resilience to economic, technological and environmental change . In this context, e nergy efficiency is one of 
the most cost-effective ways of addressing the EU’s strategic policy objectives of carbon neutrality by 2050, 
ensuring security of supply and enhancing competitiveness. Energy efficiency has become a structural trend, 
accelerated by ambitious policy targets, which place the "energy efficiency first” principle in the center of the 
energy policy in Europe.  
The EU should more than double the renovation rate of buildings by 2030 and foster deep renovation 
to achieve the EU higher climate a mbition, which could bring at the time  significant social , 
environmental and economic benefits. EU policies should target at reaching a deep energy renovation rate 
of at least 3% by 2025 to achieve the decarbonization of the buildings sector1, exceeding thus by far the actual 
renovation rate of  1%, and avoid delayed action due to the current COVID -19. From an environmental and 
health perspective, deeper renovations are needed to improve air quality as particularly in urban areas air 
pollution – for a large part coming from buildings – still is critical. Furthermore, investments in energy efficiency 
projects in buildings create the highest rate of jobs compared to other s ectors. Moreover, the construction 
sector in Europe is dominated by small and medium – sized enterprises with less than 10 employees. This is 
specifically relevant within the current COVID – 19 context, as renovation projects might be leveraged as part 
of the recovery strategy. Additionally, the energy performance of buildings has a direct impact on energy 
poverty, which affects the most vulnerable, since better performing buildings significantly lower the electricity 
bills. Energy efficiency projects in buildings therefore are a cornerstone for a just transition towards a climate -
resilient economy.  
Deep renovations should go along with the phase-out of fossil fuel energy sourcing and lean on clean, 
electrified renewable technologies.  Electrification through mature electric technologies such as solar panels 
and heat pumps offers important opportunities for decarbonizing the building sector. In order to aggressively 
decarbonize the building sector, by 2050 its electrification must more than double to approximately 72%2. This 
can be done through efficient electric technologies in heating, where heat pumps are 4 to 5 times more efficient 
than their gas boiler equivalent. Heat pumps are  an increasingly proven technology that  has been maturing 
over the years. Among the main advantages of heat pumps is the fact that their functioning principle allows 
them to use less energy than the heat they provide  and that the extra energy they recover from the ambient 
air contributes to the renewable energy contribution produced by buildings .  Today’s technology allows them

Shifting to electricity as the most efficient energy carrier plays an important role in reaching the energy 
efficiency targets thanks to the multiple benefits it brings, by means of technologies spanning across 
sectors. Increasing energy efficiency does not mean only energy savings. The trend towards an overall more 
efficient use of energy has to result in a shift in the way energy is used, including the choice between the most 
appropriate energy carrier and sources. In s uch context electricity is part of the solution. Electrification can 
bring forward a set of benefits that go beyond increased efficiency such as CO2 emission reduction, improved 
urban air quality and security of supply.   
The Commission has to align the EPBD revision with the increased climate ambition for transport, 
considering the  increasing EV stock, by providing the necessary deployment of charging 
infrastructure, both in residential as in non-residential buildings. Based upon the EC increased ambition, 
it is estimated that in 2030 EVs will represent 67% of new private vehicles sales, and in 2050 EVs will represent 
almost 80% of private vehicles, rapidly growing from 64 mln EVs in 2030 to surpassing 167 mln EVs in 20504. 
Thereto, the minimum charging infrastructure requirements set in the Article 8 of the directive should be 
reviewed and any barrier that could hamper its adoption, such as the limiting provisions in Article 8.6 should 
be removed. The Commission should explicitly ensure the “right to plug” principle, in order to guarantee for 
owners and tenants smooth and quick approval procedures to install recharging points in existing multi-tenant 
residential and nonresidential buildings . The Commission should also introduce specific requirements for 
charging points in some buildings (power of at least 7kW for public buildings, ability to react to dynamic pricing) 
so as to avoid the installation of obsolete equipment and excessive charging time in public buildings, and 
unlock demand response features.  
The revision of the EPBD should take a more holistic approach towards buildings’ renovation, 
enhancing synergies between other pieces of EU legislation  and policy areas,  such as the Energy 
Efficiency Directive, the Renewable Energy Directive, the Sustainable and Smart Mobility Strategy and 
the Circular Economy Plan. Member States not only should step up the renovation rate of national building 
stocks in their National Long  Term Renovation Strategies , but  also evaluate the wider benefits of energy 
efficiency measures and highlight synergies with other policy areas such as health, environment, and 
infrastructure. Wider benefits only can be obtained by deep renovations, including minimum energy 
performance requirements supported by clean technologies, digitalization and innovation.   
Enel supports the phased introduction of mandatory minimum energy performance standards (MEPS) 
for different types and particularly for worst performing buildings.  MEPS, as part of an overall 
comprehensive renovation policy framework, can help overcome actual barriers that have hindered deep 
renovation. They can enhance the effectiveness of existing renovation programs and give a positive signal to 
the market, driving demand and steer the rate and depth of renovation. They should, however, be accompanied 
by economic and non - economic measures in order to actually unlock the potential efficiency improvements 
of buildings. 
Enel supports the reinforcement of the smart readi ness indicator for buildings  in the EPBD revision , 
as it should foster the deployment of smart technologies, enabling the increase of the charging 
infrastructure for electric vehicles, on-site renewables and demand response. To achieve the EU energy

Policy enablers in driving building electrification are needed to ease efficient renovation of buildin gs 
and promote fuel switching.  A forward-looking policy framework in the building sector should point at: (1) 
well targeted renovation objectives for the building stock, (2) updated building codes requiring electrification 
technologies and energy management systems to be present in new and renovated buildings, (3) simplification 
of administrative procedures for building renovation, ( 4) mandatory energy efficiency audits and inspection 
schemes linked to energy performance certificates and building renovation passports, ( 5) promotion of 
interoperability among assets, assuring compliance to relevant technical standards (6) tax reductions and 
deductions for building renovation, ( 7) funding schemes and innovative financing models for paying back the 
upfront investment, (8) training and enhancing skills of artisan and building sector companies in order to lower 
cost and increase quality of work and (9) the revision of the energy taxation considering the climate impact of 
different fuels, removing undue taxes and levies from electricity tariffs and transfer them to general taxation, 
where necessary.

Housing Europe contribution to the consultation on the Policy Roadmap for the revision of the 
Energy Performance of Buildings Directive (EPBD) 
Draft version March 2021 
 
1. The ambition of the Social, Cooperative and Public Housing Providers:  
The leaders of the 27 member states of the European Union (EU) agreed on Friday 11 December to 
reduce their greenhouse gas emissions by “at least 55%” by 2030, by compared to the 1990 l evel, 
against a previous target of 40%. The decision aims to achieve carbon neutrality by 2050. This increased 
target will inevitably have consequences on the policies that the EU and member states will put in 
place in particular in the most emitting secto rs such as transport, buildings (in particular heating 
systems).  
In this context Housing Europe has presented the ambition of the sector to renovate 4 million homes 
by 2030. The ambition will only be achievable if the right policies are in place. It is therefore essential 
that the forthcoming legislative changes aimed at reaching the new C02 target take into account the 
challenges and specific nature of the building ecosystem and provide solutions to increase 
decarbonization 
Social, cooperative and public housing providers are already frontrunners in the fair energy transition: 
the average energy performance of their homes is b etter than in any other segment of the housing 
market and they cater for most of the low -income families thus ensuring comfort at an affordable 
price. Fundamentally, social, cooperative and public housing providers’ main objective for the 
renovation wave and the fair energy transition is to create and maintain sustainable communities with 
a high level of quality of life and an affordable cost of living.  
The renovation wave will be an important milestone for the EU push towards a carbon neutral Europe 
by 2050. It will also be a complex piece of policy -making, since energy performance of homes cannot 
be regulated in the same way as energy efficiency of appliances or cars. The observation of the results 
of measures on energy efficiency in buildings show that t here is no single route towards 
decarbonization of the building stock (in particular there is no correlation between one specific set of 
policies in one country and the average performance of the building stock1).  
 
2. Housing Europe general view on energy policy legislative changes:  
Social, cooperative and public housing providers need visibility and stability of the legal framework in 
order to plan for the decarbonisation of their stock. Private homeowners need a clear framework as 
well that focus on the expected outcome in terms of CO2 reduction and energy performance. Regular 
change of the energy legislation can create a confusion as to which standards need to be applied. A 
focus on the final outcome (i. e. Decarbonization of the building stock by 2050) and incentivizing area-

Housing Europe therefore  does not consider that the current legislative framework made of the EED,

3. Preliminary considerations about the revision of the Energy Performance of Buildings Directive: 
As mentioned before, we consider that the current provisions of the Energy Performance of Buildings 
Directive are sufficient to lea d to an increase of the energy performance of buildings. The evidence 
about the decreasing energy consumption per m2 and on the CO2 emissions per m2 in the residential 
building sector in the EU confirms this2.  
However we would like to put forward some preliminary comments about elements invoked under 
policy option 3. 
On the Minimum energy performance standards  
The European Commission inception impact assessment states : “The phased introduction of 
mandatory minimum energy performance standards for different types of buildings (public and private, 
non-residential and residential) will be a central part of the EPBD revision. Different options for the 
type, scope, timeline and phasing in of such standards and the level of flexibility for Member States will 
be assessed. When phasing in such standards, one option could be to start with stricter requirements 
for specific types of buildings, such as public buildings or office buildings, and to extend progressively 
the requirements to other buildings. The conditions under which energy performance standards should 
apply to residential buildings, for example change of ownership or rental, are also subject to 
assessment. In this context, the need for accompanying support policies to ensure affordability of 
housing will also be examined”

• Requirements related to obligations are traditionally part of national policy mix to promote

• Different approaches exist that we can summarize as follows:  
- The French approach : getting rid of the worst performing housing units through obligation 
to renovate F and G apartments by 2028  
- The Dutch approach : improving the average energy performance of the stock by bringing the 
entire social housing stock to average B by 2021 
- The German approach: retrofitting obligations and minimum standards for individual 
measures (for instance obligations to isolate roofs for any renovation programmes) 
• There is no evidence yet that one approach is better than the other in terms of reduction of 
energy consumption and CO2 emissions3 
 
• The European Commission has not clarified what added value a EU provision similar to 
minimum energy performance standard would bring to national policy mixes compared to 
existing EPBD provisions  
• In general, there are several conditions under which MEPS would be relevant for national 
policy mix:  
1. They should not undermine national existing schemes and create regulatory confusion  
2. They should be applied with the right timing, i.e leave sufficient time for member states to 
implement the measures and for the renovation and construction sector to strengthen 
innovation and skills 
3. They should be accompanied by adequate (public) funding in order to avoid the increase of 
rents that would make it unaffordable for most of the current residents (bearing in mind that 
there is also a clear need for new affordable housing units)  
4. They should allow a flexible approach: consideration of a cluster of buildings, consideration 
of the decarbonization of heating systems, consideration of the CO2 emissions as a “control” 
indicator

Supporting Member states in set ting up databases and data -driven policies  and support housing 
companies in their decision-making process by promoting reliable data and digital tools would certainly 
be helpful.  
The fair energy transition in the social, cooperative and public housing sector requires a very good 
knowledge of (i) the state of the building stock – including the heating systems - at any point in time 
and (ii) the impact of different types of programmes in terms of real energy consumption, CO2 
emissions and costs. With those information, housing providers can calibrate their activ ities, ie. 
deciding which buildings to renovate and to which level of energy efficiency. Only since the last few 
years, those information have been made available in a systematic way. 
An essential tool remain the Energy Performance Certificates databases. An energy performance 
certificatee describes the building or dwelling (surface, orientation, walls, windows, materials, etc.), as 
well as its heating, domestic hot water production, cooling and ventilation equipment. It indicates, 
depending on the case, ei ther the amount of energy actually consumed (on the basis of invoices) or 
the estimated energy consumption for a standardized use of the building or the dwelling 
On the integration of resource efficiency considerations and circular economy principles:  
The European Commission states:” Addressing resource efficiency and circularity principles in order to 
reduce whole lifecycle emissions, digitalisation, climate resilience and health and environmental 
standards also requires consideration” 
The consideration of the carbon content of the energy in the building and of the construction material 
is increasingly a criterion used by providers of social, cooperative and public housing providers. 
However this require a long process of change within the construction and renovation sector in order 
to provide the adequate technical solutions. The role of the Construction Products Regulation as well 
as the Green Public Procurement in the promotion of technical solutions adapted to the carbon 
neutrality needs to be taken into account.

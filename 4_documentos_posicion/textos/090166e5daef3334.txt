Liquid Gas Europe feedback to the roadmap on the revision of the 
Energy Performance of Buildings Directive 
Liquid Gas Europe is a European association composed of national Liquefied Petroleum Gas ( LPG) 
associations, the main European LPG suppliers, distributors and equipment manufacturers. With the 
support of its working groups of industry experts, Liquid Gas Europe is actively involved in concrete 
initiatives and programmes to ensure the sustainable, s afe and efficient development of LPG and 
bioLPG in Europe. 
Liquid Gas Europe wishes to share the LPG industry’s input on the preparation of  the Energy 
Performance of Buildings  (EPBD) review. We believe that the upcoming Commission’s impact 
assessment should focus on identifying cost-effective and easily accessible measures to improve the 
energy performance of buildings. 
In rural areas, LPG condensing boilers, on their own, and hybrid systems represent inherently high 
energy-efficient solutions to keep warm homes that are not connected to the gas grid. They also offer  
a long-term, cost-effective pathway to decarbonisation through the gradual introduction of bioLPG  
into the energy mix. This renewable drop -in substitute reduces carbon footprint by up to 80% 
compared to conventional LPG . This means that over time carbon and air pollutant emissions from 
heat generation will increasingly reduce, improving the emission profile of a building, without having 
to resort to expensive future retrofitting of the heating system.  It can be estimated that t here are 
already over 2,000,000 LPG boilers1 in the EU that can lock-into lower CO2 emissions cost-effectively. 
To both reduce energy demand off the gas grid and increase the market share of renewable gases in 
the rural energy mix, w e invite the European Commission to evaluate the following aspects of the 
Energy Performance of Buildings Directive  improving the energy efficiency and the emission footprint 
of buildings:  
1. Incentives to invest in the replacement of their inefficient heating systems with cleaner 
alternatives such as new LPG condensing boilers and hybrid systems 
2. Take into account that existing condensing LPG  boilers can drastically reduce emission 
footprint of buildings by switching to bioLPG, a 100% sustainable drop in substitute 
3. Adopt an objective approach when assessing the impact on air quality 
 
1. Incentives to invest in the replacement of inefficient heating systems 
Considering non-condensing boilers still represent over 50% of all heating appliances installed in 
the EU, switching them to LPG or natural gas condensing boilers represent an immediate low -
hanging fruit in improving the energy performance of buildings. 
In rural areas, there is a greater role for LPG as a gaseous fuel for gas-powered heating combined 
with solar thermal systems or hybrid systems (an electrically driven heat pump with an LPG boiler).

Hybrid solutions would be the m ost efficient in remote areas characterised by severe climactic 
conditions; with LPG as a second energy source, warm water can be produced efficiently even 
with low temperatures outside. The hybridisation of heating systems can lead to a sharp drop in 
the share of heating oil and solid fuels and an improvement of the overall energy performance of 
buildings across Europe.    
Member States should be encouraged to offer incentives in the form of subsidies , scrappage 
schemes or interest-free loans for the purchase of new gas boilers. This supports homeowners in 
replacing their inefficient heating systems. Such supporting schemes additionally promote the 
awareness of the costs and benefits of these choices. 
Recommendations 
The energy performance of buildings can be drastically improved by replacing non -condensing 
boilers to condensing gas/LPG boilers. At the same time, hybrid technology combining the 
environmental benefits and reliability of LPG with renewable energy is on the rise. 
• Given the ongoing developments in the hybrid heating systems, it would be advisable for the 
Commission to introduce the information sharing and learning opportunities for small and 
medium-size enterprises. This could take the form of national platforms for best practice 
sharing among installers. 
• Contributing to the Renovation Wave initiative, the Commission should encourage Member 
States to offer rural homeowners incentives to invest in the replacement of their inefficient 
heating systems with cleaner alternatives like new LPG condensing boilers and hybrid 
installations, where appropriate. 
2. Take into account the potential of drop-in fuels to decarbonise residential heating systems 
LPG condensing boilers, on their own, also offer a future-proof, cost -effective pathway to 
decarbonisation through the gradual introduction of bioLPG into the energy mix. This renewable 
drop-in substitute reduces carbon footprint by up to 80% compared to c onventional LPG. This 
means that over time carbon and air pollutant emissions from heat generation will increasingly 
reduce, improving the emission profile of a building, without having to resort to expensive future 
retrofitting of the heating system. It can be estimated that there are already over 2,000,000 LPG 
boilers2 in the EU that can lock-into lower CO2 emissions cost-effectively. 
The Commission can increase the energy performance of buildings by supporting the switch from 
LPG to renewable bioLPG. It can do so by providing a clear definition of bioLPG and recognising it 
as a renewable energy source for the calculation of the energy performance of buildings.

Recommendations 
The increased use of renewable bioLPG can drastically improve the carbon fo otprint and energy 
performance of buildings.   
• To support the increased use of bioLPG as sustainable heating technology for buildings the 
Commission should provide member states with a clear definition of bioLPG. 
• BioLPG should be recognised as a renewable energy source for the calculation of the energy 
performance of buildings and minimum share of renewable energy. 
 
3. An objective and technology-neutral approach when assessing the impact on air quality 
The inception impact assessment mentions that “ through the reduction of energy consumption 
and a switch away from the use of fossil fuels in buildings, air pollution is expected to be reduced.” 
We would like to stress that a switch away from fossil fuels in b uildings does not automatically 
lead to a reduction of air pollution but that this depends on the chosen alternative technology. 
The PM emissions of (bio)LPG are between 96% and 99% lower than renewable biomass, 
depending on the age of the boiler and mater ial used (wood logs, pellets, …). W hen combined 
with an LPG -boiler, hybrid systems have a negligible impact on air quality when compared to 
conventional fuels or biomass. 
When aiming to improve air quality via the revision of the EPBD it is advisable to adopt a 
technology-neutral approach which measures the individual impact of supported heating systems 
objectively. This is ever more important in light of the vast scientific evidence of the air pollution 
issues related to the use of biomass space heaters.   
 
Recommendations 
The switch away from fossil fuels in buildings does not automatically lead to a reduction of air 
pollution but depends on the alternatives which are supported.  
• The Commission should adopt a technology-neutral and objective approach when assessing 
the impact of the revision of the EPBD on air quality 
• Member states should be made aware about the negative impact of biomass boilers on the 
air pollution of buildings

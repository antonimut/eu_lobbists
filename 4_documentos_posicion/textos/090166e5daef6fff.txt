 
 
 
EDISON’S CONTRIBUTION TO THE REVIEW OF THE ENERGY PERFORMANCE OF BUILDINGS DIRECTIVE 
2010/31/EU 
 
Edison welcomes the opportunity to share some preliminary considerations on the review of the Energy 
Performance of Buildings Directive 2010/31/EU, to assess the important contribution to the European 
objectives it is pursuing. 
Edison continues with conviction in strengthening a solid growth path in the industrial energy efficiency, 
construction and energy and environmental services sector s. In light of the European objectives already 
included in the Italian National Energy and Climate Plan, we intend to actively develop and promote new 
consumption models through distributed generation and initiatives developed on a public -private 
partnership model; the renovation of the real estate portfolio, especially for the Public Administration; 
further enhancement of the role of the dedicated European legislation in achieving energy performance 
targets. We absolutely agree with the European Commission that the existing legislation will not be sufficient 
to drive Europe in filling the present gap in energy efficiency ambitions, especially in the building sector 
responsible on a European basis for the emission of 36% of climate-altering gases.  Thus, we have welcomed 
the decision taken to put energy efficiency at the first place in the EU's economic recovery policies, the 
publication of the Renovation wave and the related initiatives to revise the Energy Efficiency Directive (EED) 
and, indeed, the Energy Performance of Buildings Directive (EPBD). 
We agree with the principles and objectives expressed in the roadmap published by the Commission for the 
revision of the EPBD, and we expect that relevant interventions, as described in the policy option 3 of the 
roadmap itself, will be put in place to achieve goals identified. In this context, however, we believe it is fair 
to point out that some Member States, such as Italy, have already taken important steps towards adapting  
their legislation and implementing targeted initiatives necessary to fill the present gap in European energy 
efficiency ambitions. 
In order to design an effective regulatory approach, we believe that the following actions should be 
considered by the legislator: 
• harmonizing overlapping measures that may emerge in the revision of the RED II and the EED 
initiatives, thus coordinating in a more effective way all the existing policies; 
• encouraging dialogue and cooperation between the public and private sectors by strengthening 
public-private partnerships (PPPs), defining appropriate mechanisms to support and stimulate 
additional investments, identifying measures that can ensure simplified procedures and adequate 
implementation times; 
• taking into account the specific characteristic s of individual Member States and the current 
conditions of the building stock, ensuring an adequate level of flexibility in achieving a set common 
goal, when planning the introduction of mandatory minimum energy performance standards for 
different types of buildings (public and private, non-residential and residential); 
• making the adaption of the EPBD to the new climate ambitions in the field of sustainable mobility an 
opportunity to expand the tools and support measures dedicated to involving the entire public and 
private building stock, in all its forms: residential, commercial and tertiary; 
• reinforcing the integration of renewable sources and electric vehicles charging infrastructures in the 
renovation of buildings and, therefore, in the assessment of their performance.

However, we strongly believe that any legislative instrument will not be fully effective unless, first of all, a 
campaign to increases awareness is designed and implemented in order to inform all European citizens, 
public administrations, micro, small and medium enterprises about benefits and savings of energy efficiency 
measures, and how to access such measures , thus ensuring greater public awareness on the issues of 
efficiency, self-consumption and distributed generation. As of today, awareness represents the precondition 
that European institutions and Member States should meet to allow their citizens to become more engaged 
in achieving a higher ambition of energy efficiency.

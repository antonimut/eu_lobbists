 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Inception Impact Assessment 
 
Revision of the Energy Performance of Buildings 
Directive 2010/31/EU 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
OBSERVATIONS CONFINDUSTRIA 
 
22 MARZO 2021 
Ref. Ares(2021)2033758 - 22/03/2021
 
 
 
1 
 
• Noting the increase in the EU's ambition for 2030 and 2050 in terms of reducing 
emissions of climate-changing gases and energy consumed, a revision of this Directive 
is considered essential to have greater involvement of the construction sector, to 
which a significant share of CO2 emissions is linked. 
 
• In fact, the construction sector accounts for about 40% of final energy consumpti on 
and 36% of Europe's greenhouse gas emissions; of these about 80% are attributable 
to the satisfaction of heating, cooling and production of domestic hot water in 
buildings. 
 
• We therefore agree with the principles and objectives set out in the RoadMap, 
published by the Commission, for the revision of the EPBD and expect relevant action 
to be taken, as described in strategic option 3 of the roadmap itself. In this context, 
however, we believe it is right to point out that some Member States, such as Italy, 
have already taken important steps towards adapting legislation and implementing the 
initiatives necessary to fill the current gap in European energy efficiency first 
ambitions. 
 
• The EPBD is an essential instrument in the context of the national long-term strategy 
for the restructuring of the national real estate stock, and it must be an opportunity to 
act in coordination with other European directives – the Energy Efficiency Directive 
and the Renewable Energy Directive – in order to further promote the use o f 
technologies using renewable sources. 
 
• It is considered essential to evolve from a "static" vision of interventions to improve 
energy efficiency in the built environment referring to two timely and contextual 
evaluations at the beginning of the project (E PA ex ante and ex post building) to a 
"continuous dynamic" on effective measures of indicators that demonstrate the 
maintenance over time of the expected energy and environmental benefits.  
 
 
 
2 
• The reduction of thermal  consumption, electrification, the availability of biofuels 
(biomethane, hydrogen, etc.) should be the guidelines for the redevelopment 
interventions of the short and medium term. The adoption of the so -called "Building 
Passport" would trace the path of energy efficie ncy interventions to be followed. For 
the building certification framework, it is hoped that in the "Building Renovation 
Passport" the various existing certifications (EPA, Energy Diagnosis, etc.) can 
converge. The BRP should include information from indiv idual building components, 
including "link to up-to-date" for their upgrade.  
 
• Of course, if we recognize the contribution that  can be made to the profound 
restructuring of energy efficiency, we can also see the difficulties in implementing 
them. When it is not possible, however, it is essential not to neglect "specific" 
renovations, such as the replacement of fixtures, installation of hot /cold systems, 
devices etc…  that are still a fundamental support for energy efficiency in the 
construction sector. 
 
 
• At present, nothing or little is said about smart building and digitization. The only 
aspects currently mentioned in the legislative texts are relating to the use of self -
regulating devices whose installation is subject to their availability, their technical 
feasibility and economic convenience, in fact cancelling their forecast. These tools are 
instead necessary for the balancing of the  building that guarantees housing comfort 
and real energy efficiency. 
 
• To define an effective regulatory approach, we consider it important: 
• harmonizing regulatory measures by reducing the risk of overlap that may 
arise in the revision of the RED II and EED initiatives; 
• foster dialogue and cooperation between the public and private sectors by 
strengthening the public -private partnership (PPP ), establishing appropriate 
mechanisms to support and stimulate further investment, identifying measures 
that can ensure simplified procedures and appropriate implementation times; 
 
 
 
3 
• when planning the introduction of mandatory minimum energy performance 
standards for different types of buildings (public and private, non -residential 
and residential), take into account the specific characteristics of individual 
Member States and current building conditions, ensuring an adequate level of 
flexibility in achieving a common target,  
• to exploit the adaptation of EPBD to new climate ambitions in the field of 
sustainable mobility; 
•strengthen the integration of renewable sources and electric vehicle charging 
infrastructure into the renovation of buildings.

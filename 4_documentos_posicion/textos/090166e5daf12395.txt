Executive Summary 
 
The European Cyclists’ Federation welcomes the revision of the Energy Performance 
of Buildings Directive (EPBD) (EU) 2018/844 as part of the ‘Renovation Wave’ and ‘Fit 
for 55 Package’. 
 
The last EPBD revision in 20181 established the link between the building and mobility 
sector b y providing requirements for charging infrastructure of electr ic veh icles. 
While ECF supports this, we regret that Art 8. effectively applied to electric cars only. 
 
However, since electric bicycles are 1) far more energy efficient than electric cars 
and 2) sell in much greater numbers, it is only logical and would form coherent policy 
to extend the e -mobility scope of t his directive to electric bicycles and electric 
cargo bikes and include recharging infrastructure for these types of vehicles too in 
all residential and non-residential buildings, both new as well as in those undergoing 
major renovation. 
 
The wording of recital 28 of the current directive evokes the importance of including 
recharging infrastructure for electric bicycles too but falls short in making this a legal 
requirement. This shortcoming needs to be fixed by this revision. 
 
“When applying the requirements for electromobility infrastructure provided for in 
the amendments to Directive 2010/31/EU, as set out in this Directive, Member States 
should consider the need for holistic and coherent urban planning as well as the 
promotion of alternative, safe and sustainable modes of transport and their 
supporting infrastructure, for example through dedicated parking infrastructure for 
electric bicycles and for the vehicles of people of reduced mobility.”

3. ECF policy recommendations 
 
3.1 Recharging infrastructure for electric bicycles and e-cargo bikes 
 
A dedicated number of E-charging points for e-bikes and e-cargo bikes should be 
provided in the close proximity of easily accessible and secure bicycle parking spots. 
The e-charging point ideally should be placed in a secured locker. Since a standard 
230V household power plug is sufficient to charge an e-bike, such a locker can also 
be used to charge other electric appliances, such as mobile phones, laptops, and, 
if locker space permits, other micro-mobility devices, such as e-scooters. 
 
Scope: All new residential and non-residential buildings and those undergoing major 
renovation 
 
Residential buildings: 
 
• At least one e-charging point and bicycle parking spot per apartment; 
• At least one e-charging point and cargo bicycle parking spot per 10 apartments; 
• The e-charging point should be in close proximity to the bicycle parking spot.  
 
Non-residential buildings: 
 
• At least one e-charging point and bicycle parking spot per 10 employees; 
• At least one e-charging point and cargo bicycle parking spot per 100 
employees; 
• The e-charging point should be in close proximity to the bicycle parking spot. 
 
3.2  Wider mobility and social considerations: Parking norms for bicycles and cars 
 
Art. 8.8 of the EPBD Directive evokes the need to consider the wider mobility context 
beyond the e -charging infrastructure: “Member States shall consider the need for 
coherent policies for buildings, soft and green mobility and urban planning.”

This ‘consideration’ is not a legal requirement and is not fit for purpose. ECF proposes 
two changes: 
 
 
 
 
3.2.1 Eliminate minimum car parking requirements 
 
In numerous EU Member States  relevant housing laws mandate minimum car 
parking requirements in new residential and non-residential developments, hereby 
artificially increasing car ownership and car use.9 The ITF therefore now recommends 
that such minimum off-street car parking requirements are to be eliminated.10 
 
The ITF also argues that due to minimum car parking requirements, more car parking 
is provided than customers demand which in turn drives up constructions costs. One 
underground car parking spot can easily cost 25,000 Euro and more, and if not sold 
or rented, these costs are distributed among other  buyers/ tenan ts. With future 
stricter energy performance standards further increasing initial  construction and 
renovation costs, rem oving minimum car park ing norms for cars would be an 
efficient social measure to improve affordability of housing. 
 
3.2.2 Establish minimum bicycle parking requirements 
 
The bicycle is not only the most-energy efficient of all transport modes, it is also a n 
affordable means of transportation. Some 20 million bicycles have been sold 
annually over the last years, and the total market is set to increase to 30 million units 
by 2030. 
 
Bicycle parking costs are also much cheaper than car parking costs as total space 
consumption per unit (i.e. the parking spot + access lanes) is up to 20 times lower.11 
Many, but by far not every, housin g code in EU Member States  requires minimum 
bicycle parking  in new devel opments. If they exist, they typically mandate 1 - 2 
bicycle parking spots per apartment or more. Malmö mandates 2.5 bicycle parking 
spots per apartment; for an apartment in Hamburg with more than 125 sqm, at least 
5 bicycle parking spots have to be provided. 12 To stimulate bicycle use across  the 
EU, it is essential that for all new residential and non -residential buildings and those 
that under go major renovation,  minimum bicycle parking requirements are 
introduced.

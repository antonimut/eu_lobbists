 
CATF POSITION: INDUSTRIAL EMISSIONS – EU RULES 
UPDATED 
CATF is an international non-profit organisation that works to safeguard against 
the worst impacts of climate change by catalysing the rapid global development 
and deployment of low-carbon energy and other climate-protecting technologies 
through research and analysis, public advocacy leadership, and partnership with 
the private sector.  
CATF strongly supports the EU’s ambition to become climate neutral by 2050 
and welcomes the revision of th e Industrial Emissions Directive  (IED) and the 
European Pollutant Release and Transfer Register  (E-PRTR) to ensure 
compatibility with climate  neutrality and to improve their implementation and 
effectiveness.  
INDUSTRIAL EMISSIONS DIRECTIVE (IED) 
The IED aims to create an integrated approach to prevent  the environmental 
impacts of industrial activities and to set the best available standards. However, 
it fails to address greenhouse gas emissions and therefore falls short to “achieve 
a high level of protection of the environment taken as a whole ”. Currently, the 
regulation of GHG emissions is left to the EU Emission Trading System (ETS) 
Directive. However, since more than 90% of industrial GHG emissions are 
covered by free emissions allowances, the ETS does not incentivise the 
decarbonisation of industries . Moreover, the ETS and IED have different 
approaches that could be complementary since the first one provides emissions 
benchmarks and a price for carbon while the second one includes performance-
based energy efficiency standards.

The prohibition in article 9(1) of the IED prevents the inclusion of limits on GHG 
emissions in IED operating permits. This provision restricts the options available 
to Member States to tackle GHG emission reductions of industrial installations.  
The provision in article 9(2), leavi ng requirements on the energy efficiency 
performance or fuel choice optional , is inconsistent with the IED objectives to 
use energy efficiently. Consequently, the IED fails to support the use of current 
technologies that allow for better energy efficiency as well as reductions in both 
air pollution and GHG emissions.  
To remedy this major shortcoming and make the Directive fit for climate 
neutrality, GHG emissions  need to be included within the scope of th e IED by 
amending article 9(1) and 9(2) . Decarbonisation should be included in the 
revised framework and the BREF reviews, with binding GHG performance 
standards based on utilisation of inherently clean technologies, pollutant 
emissions removal technologies,  and/or carbon capture and binding energy 
efficiency standards based on best existing solutions. 
Furthermore, including GHG in the IED and developing a regular updating 
mechanism of the BREFs which ensures the addition of clean technologies such 
as electric boilers and pollutant removal technologies such as carbon capture 
and storage, and deployment of low-carbon fuels such as hydrogen, will support 
the industry transition towards climate neutrality. 
The revision of the IED should also address the derogation from article 15( 4) 
allowing competent authority to set less strict emission limit values  in some 
specific cases. The assessment of these exceptions should be transparent and 
objective criteria and methods should be set up.  
Moreover, the Commission should ensure a better focus on methane during the 
reviews of Best Available Techniques (BAT) Reference Documents (BREF) and 
make the identification of techniques for reducing methane emissions a key point 
in BREF reviews for the Methane Strategy to deliver on its objectives. In addition, 
methane BAT-associated emission levels (BAT -AELs) should also be included 
in BAT conclusions and the IED should consider limits for methane emissions.

EUROPEAN POLLUTANT RELEASE AND TRANSFER REGISTER (E-PRTR) 
The E -PRTR is a database that has all the potential to contribute to better 
transparency of emissions from European facilities. Such detailed information 
on industrial activities is key for  EU citizens  and civil -society groups to be 
properly informed about the environmental impact of industrial installation s. 
Moreover, it also provides detailed information on emission values to research 
activities that are crucial to understand  the trends of air pollution and climate 
change, and assess the effectiveness of mitigation measures and implemented 
policies.  
Nevertheless, we believe that the current dataset should be reviewed to improve 
the level and detail of information made available. Consistent and harmoni sed 
data format together with continuous emission levels time series at a high 
granular level will support not only assessment of pollution levels in Europe but 
also provide a better overview of the contribution of each facility to total 
emissions. Additionally, information should be available on the facility attributes, 
such as on the control types for common pollutants, the fuel types and quantity 
used, and the type of installations of the facilities. For large industrial 
installations, the emissions information should be provided on a process unit 
level.  
Furthermore, considering that large agro-industries are also responsible for high 
ammonia levels, as well as the number one source of methane emissions in the 
EU, we believe that an improved reporting from activities in this sector is 
necessary, namely emissions resulting from rearing of cattle. 
More information should also be provided to assess the environmental impact of 
industrial installations,  such as air quality data around the installations or 
information on compliance with permit conditions and on the conformity with the 
EU BAT -C. The E -PRTR should also provide links to Continuous Emissions 
Monitoring (CEMs) data. 
The E-PRTR should in addition provide clear information on the compliance with 
permit conditions and how the industrial activity performs against the EU Best 
Available Techniques conclusions  (BAT-C) standards to tackle their negative 
impact.

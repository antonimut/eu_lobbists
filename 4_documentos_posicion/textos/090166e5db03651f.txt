Explanatory note 1 on the EU Action Plan “ IED-EPRTR 
Revision 2020” open public consultation 
 
 
In the spirit of continuous improvement of existing environmental 
acquis, Cefic supports the overall strategic direction of a  combined 
review of the IED and E -PRTR . Whilst we notice  that  both legislations 
have proven to be effective in reporting (E-PRTR) and reducing (IED) 
pollutants emissions , we also recognise science and technologies have 
evolved since IED and E -PRTR inception. To preserv e efficiency, we 
recommend the adoption of a pragmatic, data-driven and science-based 
approach to revising both legislations . The outcomes of the refit exercise 
should follow a strict application of Better Regulations Principles.  
 
The c hemical industry is committed to seeking continuous improvement through innovation to wards 
reducing the environmental and health impacts and contributing to a more sustainable Europe. The long-
lasting commitment of the EU chemical manufacturing industry to implement the highest operational 
excellence standards and procedures to protect the people and en vironment such as through the 
implementation of Cefic’s Responsible Care® management framework underscores this commitment 
 
With this paper Cefic would like to provide additional comments to the questions in the questionnaire of 
the Open Public Consultation and an explanation of our answers in selected instances.

The Chemical industry provides the European Society with indispensable goods that go into many

Question 3: The role of large (agro)industrial plants post 2030 should be important. 
 
Since COVID hit Europe, the chemicals industry has demonstrated resilience, adaptability, 
innovative approach to solving unprecedented issues and competitiveness. We foresee the role 
of the chemicals industry becoming even more prominent in the post-COVID recovery times when 
Europe will be in even stronger need for innovation and longer-term commitment to sustainable 
growth under the framework of the European Green Deal.  
 
In the recovery context, we call on the European Institutions to apply the strict Better Regulation 
Principles. While the IED provides the right framework for continuous improvement, we caution 
that overlapping regulation shall be avoided.

Question 6: Access to permitting information : we see public access to information on the permitting 
process as important. The public concerned already has the right to access information and submit 
views in the permitting process, including the right to appeal the permitting decision. These procedural 
rights are already provided for under a range of environmental directives.  We also recall that trained 
and highly educated experts/public servants of the respective local authorities represent the interest of 
the broader public in this process.   
The current participative process already includes a broad public participation to the permitting 
process. In this case, we refer to the thorough environmental impact assessments  that are 
mandatory steps for development projects of integrated chemical installations for the production 
of certain chemicals. The Environmental Impact Assessment Directive requires consultation of the 
public concerned prior to adoption of the permitting decision.  The Seveso Directive establishes 
additional public consultation requirements for development projects relating to modification of 
existing establishments or planning of new establishments. While filing for an operating permit,

Question 8: In general, we strongly agree that the existing IED, supplemented by horizontal legislation 
sufficiently controls environmental impacts.  
 
The evaluation of the IED carried out by DG ENV in 2019/2020 (see staff working document) has 
shown the IED is broadly serving its purpose of reducing industrial emissions and shows tangible 
results (reduction of emissions for virtually all major key environmental issues taken into 
consideration). See also EEA report shown at the IED workshop in Brussels, May 2019.  
 
On the proposed inclusion of GHG emissions under IED extended to ETS -regulated sites: We like 
to note that the proposed GHG inclusion in the IED scope would introduce a major change in the 
functioning principles of EU ETS.  
 
Cefic supports an “one emission -one policy instrument” approach to regulating the GHG 
emissions. We see the EU emissions trading system (EU ETS) as the cornerstone of the EU's policy 
to combat climate change and its key tool for reducing greenhouse gas emissions cost-effectively 
whilst providing a long term stable legislative framework , enabling investments visibility and 
promoting innovative low -carbon technologies. While we agree with the need to ensure 
consistency be tween the IED and climate legislation(s) and welcome discussions to identify 
different options, we want to keep the current IED art9(1) exemption for ETS -regulated 
units.  Cefic remains at the Commission disposal for participating to the debate and feed 
stakeholders with more background. 
With regards to ‘soil contamination’ we think additional clarifications on this matter in the IED 
would be most helpful to support the intended benefit of the baseline report. We would like to 
highlight that Article 21 of IED states that the actual possibility for soil and groundwater 
contamination needs to be assessed before deciding if a baseline report has to be prepared or 
not. In practice, we believe the risk for soil and groundwater contamination can be reasonably 
excluded for cases where appropriate technical measures are in place. Moreover, as described in 
Article 16, such a contamination risk can also be excluded on the basis on a systematic appraisal 
of the risk of contaminations.  
Overall experience of our industry with the implementation of the baseline report shows that 
there is a high uncertainty on when a possibility of soil and groundwater contamination can 
reasonably be ruled out. Under a Better Regulation Principle , we think it is crucial vital that

Question 9: With respect to the existing sectors covered by the IED we moderately disagree with the 
notion that they still have a significant impact.  
 
As the EEA has shown, industrial pollution has significantly been reduced. Concerning the question 
on thresholds: Except for the BREF on LVOC (Large Volume Organic Chemicals) there are no 
thresholds for the chemic al processes as it is the case for almost all other BREFs. We hence 
suggest to implement thresholds for chemical sector as well, leading to a relief of administrative 
burden for Competent Authorities and the smallest chemical installations falling under th e e.g. 
CWW, WGC, LVIC, OFC, SIC, POL and other BREFs (e.g. EFS, ICS)  without damaging the overall 
impact on environment . As e.g. the WGC BREF has shown, the relevant emissions are in fact 
covered when a threshold is applied and the part of emissions ‘lost’  (means: not monitored or 
regulated) is comparably low.  
As to the questions in part 9: we do not comment on BREFs/activities not related to the 
production of chemicals.

We strongly support a pragmatic, technology and data-driven, multi-stakeholders IED-BREF 
Sevilla platform as a very solid approach to regulating pollutants limits values. Since inception, 
our industry has long contributed to improving the “Sevilla process” . Lately, Cefic has been 
providing the largest ever data -source to make the Wastes Gas for Chemicals (WGC) BREF the 
engine for enhanced emissions reductions. 
 
We also believe that the BREF-BAT development has its own limits in terms on number of “ Key 
Environmental Indicators” (KEI) that could be processed  in a reasonabl e timeframe. As a 
reminder, the WGC has taken five years to accomplish through the final conclusions with 30-some 
KEIs to process .  Also, we would like to remind that the Staff Working Document of the IED 
Evaluation, e.g. the report of EEA quoted during t he IED workshop in May 2019 shows that the 
IED and other sister legislation have factually led to (significantly) reducing emissions to the 
environment 
 
While considering circularity and carbon neutrality along with an efficient use of scarce 
resources , we think that the Sevilla Platform could help in addressing some of these societal 
concerns while regulating KEIs under the BAT -BREF drafting process . However, we foresee 
complexity in defining properly concepts like circularity and resources efficiency in “ S.M.A.R.T. “ 
emissions limits values for the industry and national Authorities to use in their operating permits.  
 
In this regard, we would caution the Commission to keep the Sevilla platform manageable by 
avoiding to over-load it with too many new and difficult to frame KEIs to handle at the same time. 
Over-burdening the Sevilla Platform will unavoidably slow down the BREF process, simply due to 
the sheer increase of complexity.    
 
Question 23: Future impacts of extended IED 
 
These impacts must be assessed during a thorough new IED revision impact assessment . In our 
opinion, we think that the main future impacts of the Directive refit on chemicals operating units 
could range from : 
• an increased legislative burden at the permits level ( National Autorities) stemming from 
new KEIs and emissions limits or even BAT Conclusions opened to breakthrough ( yet not 
existing) technologies  
• the potential impact on increasing further the European operating costs (OPEX) that are 
already the highest compared to the rest of the world ( source : Cumulative Cost 
Assessment report-2013- DG ENVI)  
• the complex and new trade-offs that would be stemming from reducing further emissions 
or increasing circularity of processes. Fo r instance, recycling process waters would 
demand more energy thus would lead to more CO2 emissions from indirect energy 
sources .  
• This latter aspect comes along with the need for enhanced evaluation of cost-benefit and 
assessment of cross-media effects as an even further reduction of emissions will 
inevitably be outweighed by the additional efforts.

FORTUM’S RESPONSE TO THE PUBLIC CONSULTATION ON NATURE 
RESTORATION TARGETS UNDER EU BIODIVERSITY STRATEGY 
 
Fortum supports the EU Biodiversity Strategy and its aim to protect and restore 
species and habitats. Biodiversity loss and the degradation of ecosystems are a severe 
global concern that has to be tackled. The EU Nature Restoration Plan including the 
upcoming EU nature restoration targets is a key tool to restore damaged ecosystems 
and ensure their sustainable management. Biodiversity restoration can also 
significantly contribute to increasing soil carbon storage and sequestration leading to 
removal of carbon dioxide from the atmosphere. 
Fortum’s views in this consultation response are largely based on the impact of the 
restoration targets on hydropower. The Biodiversity Strategy largely builds upon the 
Habitats Directive and the Natura 2000 network. In addition, the EU Water 
Framework Directive with its classification system of waterbodies sets strict norms 
for waterbodies. These existing regulations already have far-reaching objectives for 
the restoration and maintenance of ecosystems and biodiversity. It is highly important 
to avoid new overlapping legislation and to align any new legislation with the existing 
one. 
In practice, we have already encountered challenges between hydropower production 
and the existing biodiversity regulation in regulated rivers. On top of this, binding 
national restoration targets would put additional pressure on hydropower that is 
contributing to climate change mitigation. In our opinion, the restoration targets 
should primarily focus on improving the implementation and mutual alignment of the 
current directives. 
We suggest the following issues to be considered in the development of the EU nature 
restoration targets: 
• Fortum supports the binding EU-level restoration targets provided that they 
can be set taking into account and supporting sustainable human use, 
especially such related to mitigating the climate change. Cost-effectiveness is 
crucial when planning restoration actions, in order to achieve the most in 
combatting biodiversity loss with the resources available.  
• Biodiversity restoration and climate change mitigation targets support one 
other and can be reached simultaneously. Planning of the targets to restore 
degraded ecosystems and making proposals for legislation shall take into 
account the interlinkages with climate change mitigation and adaptation. It has 
to be considered that in some cases there is a trade-off between biodiversity 
and climate change measures and goals. Restoration targets and measures 
should avoid negative impacts on CO2-free energy production, such as 
hydropower (production as well flexibility and storage), wind power, solar 
power and bioenergy. This is especially important in order not to counteract 
the aim to focus restorations on those areas with the highest potential to

• In the implementation of the EU-level target, member states should have 
discretion to choose their national contributions to the overall target. Nature 
restoration measures should remain under the member state’s national 
competence. Legally binding national targets to restore degraded ecosystems 
may risk the degree of freedom to evaluate “the right measures in the right 
location”. Differences in cost-effectiveness of measures between member 
states and the principle of ecological compensation should be taken into 
account when designing the optimal distribution of restoration efforts across 
EU. For example, if free flowing rivers can be more easily restored in one 
member state without disturbance to crucial economic activities such as 
hydropower production, the action should be taken there. In case that a 
member state needs to do disproportionate amount of measures compensation 
should be provided. 
• Existing regulatory frameworks, in particular the Water Framework Directive 
should be utilized in order to avoid creating new administration and extra 
burden to authorities and stakeholders. The interlinkages of restoration targets 
with water legislation (e.g. Water Framework Directive) need to be 
considered. Here an important aspect is that hydropower production offers 
flood protection thus reducing risks for natural disasters. In addition, the 
geographical and hydrological differences in ecosystems and energy 
production, e.g. hydropower production in different parts of Europe as well as 
cost-effectiveness of the restoration actions have to be taken into account in 
planning of the restoration targets. The EU Biodiversity Strategy suggests 
several important ecosystems, habitats or species to be considered for 
restoration, such as free-flowing rivers. As stated in the biodiversity strategy, 
the target for free flowing rivers can be reached by focusing on rivers where 
there is no hydropower production and other barriers and obsolete dams. 
Hydropower dams make up less than 3 % of all river barriers in Europe.

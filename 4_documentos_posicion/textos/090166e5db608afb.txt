UNIFE Position Paper on the revision of the TEN-T guidelines 
 
 
 
Introduction: the strategic importance of the European rail supply industry  
 
With the 400 000 jobs they provide, their €47 billion of combined annual sales and the environmental 
excellence of the products they manufacture and sell all over the world, European train-builders and 
rail equipment suppliers have been a key contributor to the competitiveness and sustainability of 
Europe’s industry. As such , the European ra il supply industry plays an essential role for both 
achieving the European Green Deal objectives and contributing to our continent’s economic 
recovery – the two main political priorities of the EU. 
 
 
1. Rail and modal shift at the heart of the TEN-T  
 
The guidelines for the development of the trans -European network were established by Regulation 
(EU) No 1315/2013. The importance of this policy is recognized and supported by UNIFE because its 
sets a legally binding obligation to develop and complete Core and Comprehensive TEN-T Networks 
which are key drivers for connecting regions all over Europe, thereby contributing to building a 
European Single Market and reinforcing economic, social and territorial cohesion in a sustainable way. 
The strategic dimension of TE N-T also lies on the fact that it sets standards and requirements to be 
met along the whole network, and they include smart and innovative components to facilitate efficient 
infrastructure use and high-quality services. 
 
In its recently published Sustainable Smart Mobility Strategy  (SSMS)1, the European Commission 
reaffirms its determination to “complete the TEN-T network on time” with major transport corridors 
to be completed by 2030. To this end, the role of rail is particularly highlighted as “more cross-border 
projects will be needed to integrate all Member States into the European rail system of the future, in 
turn establishing smooth interconnections for cross -border rail travel across Europe”. Therefore, 
modernising and adapting the TEN-T guidelines will be a key enabler for this endeavour. In particular, 
in order to achieve the objectives of doubling rail freight traffic and tripling high speed rail traffic by 
2050, as established in the SSMS. 
 
In this sense, UNIFE welcomes the revision of the TEN-T guidelines. The TEN-T network is the flagship 
EU p olicy on transport infrastructure which aims to facilitate transport across Europe and reduce 
regional, economic and social disparities by developing interconnected infrastructure for all transport 
modes. As EU Commissioner for Transport Vălean informed th e European Parliament in June 2020 2, 
the TEN-T revision takes place “in response to the huge transformation affecting transport” confirming 
“its vital role in enabling an efficient and decarbonised transport system” as part of the Green Deal 
objectives.

2. The role of rail to boost the contribution of the TEN-T to zero-emission transport 
 
The Green Deal sets the pathway to make Europe a climate -neutral continent by 2050. No climate 
neutrality goal can be fulfilled without the decarbonisation of the transport sector, which accounts for 
nearly ¼ of  overall emissions in the EU . Rail stands as the exception – reducing steadily its carbon 
footprint while improving its energy efficiency  – and therefore aims at being part of the solution in 
the EU aspiration to connect all regions in the most climate-friendly manner. 
 
Rail is already the most carbon-efficient motorised way of transport. In this sense, rail accounts for 
less than 0.4% of GHG emissions from transport although it carries 17.9% of inland freight and 8,4% 
of passengers in Europe 7. Each moving train corresponds to several dozen cars and trucks and this 
helps avoid GHG, dust (PM) and NOx emissions. The environmental benefits of promoting rail in terms 
of GHG emissions are also analysed by the European Environmental Agency. The following graph s 
show the GHG Emissions by transport modes – EU 28 in million tonnes CO2 equivalent8.

Rail’s success in reducing GHG emissions relies on, among other things, the electrification of 
infrastructure. As stated in the 2017 European Commission Report on the Electrification of the 
Transport System9, “Rail in Europe is mostly electrified and therefore a key to decarbonising transport. 
Especially in cities, rail almost exclusively runs on electricity already today. Regarding main lines, 60% 
of the European rail network is already electrified and 80% of traffic is running on these lines. There 
are no technical obstacles to further electrification, but the cost for upgrading and electrifying the 
existing rail infrastructure and the expected carbon reduction need to be considered on a case by case 
basis, with EU funding support where necessary”. The construction or upgrading of electrified railway 
lines shall remain the key focus on the Core network. Nevertheless, as far as the Comprehensive 
network and nodes such as ports are concerned, the cost for electrification may lead to direct EU 
funding towards  alternative technologies instead  to enable zero emission operation . Finally, in 
connection with the possible inclusion of rail in the Alternative Fuels Infrastructure Directive, it may 
be relevant to consider what are the best alternatives to decarbonise the rail network which is not 
part of the Core or Comprehensive network. As mentioned in the new Sustainable and Smart Mobility 
Strategy “wherever rail electrification is not viable, the use of hydrogen should be increased”10. UNIFE 
also highlights the importance of batteries to be considered also as a sound alternative.  
 
With regards to energy efficiency the rail sector is also constantly improving to reduce its energy 
consumption11. This is done for example by enabling automatic train operation so that trains always 
optimise their energy consumption, and by developing energy efficient rolling stock. This is not only 
limited here to LED lightening or more efficient cooling and heating systems, but also done by 
decreasing the weight by seat (e.g. by using double decker vehicles). The overall objective is to reduce 
the specific energy consumption and CO2 emissions.

Key message:  
In line with the new EC Mobility Strategy in which the Commissions requests investments to “be 
geared towards upgrading the necessary TEN -T infrastructure to enable the shift towards more

3. The role of rail to boost the contribution of the TEN-T to digital transport 
 
 ERTMS 
 
The TEN-T policy plays a fundamental role for boosting the deployment of ERTMS on the European 
network.  
 
ERTMS is the unique train signalling system (made in Europe), aiming at replacing more than 20 
different national, incompatible signalling systems in Europe. Its key objective is interoperability: 
this means that any train equipped with ERTMS may run on any line, as long as the trackside 
equipment is also fitted with ERTMS. 
 
ERTMS has many benefits for the operators as well as the passengers:  
- Increased capacity of the currently existing infrastructure of up to 40% as it enables trains 
to run at closer distance 
- Higher speeds - maximum speed up to 500 km/h 
- Higher reliability rates – important for passengers and freight transport  
- Improved safety for passengers. 
 
We especially call here on the need to speed up the deployment of ERTMS as both a major enabler 
of a truly interoperable TEN-T and as the backbone of the future Digital Railway. In this sense, as 
stated in the new EC Mobility Strategy 12, UNIFE is pleased to see that the Commission wants 
ERTMS to be “the heart of a digital rail system” by making ERTMS roll out an important priority in 
order to “create a truly smart transport system”. 
 
 5G & FRMCS 
 
There are several areas where fifth-generation connectivity can enable significant progress for rail 
transport operations, among those we pinpoint real-time train’s load rate, traffic forecast, video-
surveillance, automatic train operations and signalling13. UNIFE acknowledges that 5G technology 
will contribute to the development of  the successor of  GSM-R (railways telecommunication 
system), namely FRMCS – the Future Railway Mobile Communication System.

 Mobility as a Service (MaaS) for urban nodes 
 
The current TEN-T guidelines recognise and formalise the role of urban nodes in these transport 
networks as important hubs that facilitate the flow of people and goods. In addition, they are also 
connected to freight and passenger transport, being the major centres for production and 
consumption. It is  of utmost importance to ensure that cities and metropolitan regions  can 
provide smart, efficient, and sustainable transport and thereby play their role as efficient urban 
nodes in the TEN-T network.  This should even be translated into funding opportunities for clean 
urban mobility as the Commission states in its new EC Mobility Strategy that “financial support 
should also reflect the importance of urban mobility for the overall functioning of the TEN-T”. 
 
Mobility as a Service (MaaS) can play a key role in this respect.  MaaS approach builds on the 
integration of all transport ation modes through collaboration arounds standards for data 
exchange. The completion of the ‘’physical’’ infrastructure around the nodes is paramount to the 
development of MaaS ‘’digital’’ services and the deployment of connectivity layers across modes. 
Rail has the potential to become the backbone of Maas, providing green, high-capacity, safe and 
reliable urban & suburban transport. But, in order to fully succeed with MaaS, it is essential to be 
able to provide a user-friendly, convenient and seamless passenger experience across the whole 
journey, including the important first and last miles.  In this regard, Research & Innovation (R&I) 
plays a key role. Shift2Rail Joint Undertaking’s Innovation Programme 4 ‘’IT Solutions for attractive 
railway services’’ has been contributing to building a digital ecosystem for MaaS, encompassing 
all available transport modes. 
 
 Cybersecurity 
 
Cybersecurity represents one of the fastest -developing digital topics. As cyber -threats are 
redesigning the security environments of every indus try and economic sector, building a solid, 
holistic cybersecurity strategy has become the essential step to successfully harness the benefits 
of digitalisation. The European rail supply industry has recognised that the protection against 
cyber-threats is a  vital element of maintaining a safe and reliable railway, – with its complex 
interdependences and legacy infrastructure. As a matter of fact, ensuring the integrity of rail 
systems and maintaining operational continuity standards is an objective which is shared by the 
whole rail sector. The TEN-T revision should incorporate the key elements of the forthcoming EU 
‘’Cybersecurity strategy’’, thus contributing to strengthening EU digital sovereignty. This will be 
essential to sheltering European rail networks from external, third countries’ technological 
developments and threats.

4. Smart mobility: the TEN-T as a showcase to deploy EU-funded innovations 
 
In the context of the Green Deal, and its climate -neutrality ambition to be fulfilled by 2050, R&I will 
be essential to create a more sustainable, more efficient, more competitive transport paradigm with 
rail – the cleanest and safest mode – as its backbone. 
 
The European rail sector’s innovation efforts have found continuity, stability and long -term vi sion 
through the Shift2Rail Joint Undertaking established under Horizon 2020. With rail suppliers, 
operators, infrastructure managers, research centres and academia working together across Member 
States, all innovation ecosystems in the sector have been effectively boosted. 
 
In its EC Mobility Strategy, the Commission announces that it will “drive the deployment of innovative 
and sustainable technologies in transport” and mentions the importance of “tomorrow’s deployment, 
through instruments like the Connec ting Europe Facility (CEF), the Cohesion Fund, the European 
Regional Development Fund or InvestEU”.  
 
Therefore, UNIFE calls on the deployment of innovative solutions developed under the Shift2Rail JU 
and its successor  to be done at European scale on the TEN -T network. The support of  the afore 
mentioned EU funding & financing instruments will be key in this respect.  
 
 
Key message: 
In order to leverage the potential of R&I in the TEN-T network, UNIFE calls the Commission to reflect 
and showcase the activities of the Shift2Rail JU and its successor  programme in the further 
deployment of the network. Thus, increasing synergies between EU  funded programmes such as 
Horizon Europe and the Connecting Europe Facility should be promoted in the revision of the TEN-
T guidelines. 
 
 
 
5. Final considerations  
 
The TEN -T policy is of fundamental importance as it supports and symbolises connectivity and  
accessibility for all regions of the Union. UNIFE would like to conclude here by highlighting a few points 
which are key for ensuring the success of the TEN-T policy implementation in the future:  
 
 
 
 
 
8 
 
• Funding  
An ambitious TEN-T policy with sustainability and modal shift at its core, back up with massive 
EU funds, will be key to boost investment on the rail market and avoid a severe post-Covid 19 
market contraction. Therefore, UNIFE calls on significant financial envelopes to be dedicated 
to TEN-T implementation at EU, national and regional level and reminds of the importance of 
the Connecting Europe Facility (CEF) and Cohesion Policy Funds for boosting  and rail 
investments. UNIFE calls on EU funding and financing instruments to prioritise zero emissions 
transport modes. UNIFE also shares the European Parliament’s TRAN Committee views when 
calling on the European Commission to “strengthen synergies between the relevant EU 
instruments beyond the CEF and, furthermore, to optimise the use of blending tools in order 
to attract private investors and leverage public funding”14 . 
 
• Timeline and targets 
In its Report on the Evaluation of the TEN-T guidelines 2013–202015, adopted on 16 July 2020, 
the European Economic and Social Committee shares “doubt th at the Core Network can be 
completed by 2030, but considers this target important to push Member States to work 
harder and believes that several major cross -border projects might be completed by that 
date”. UNIFE also believes that it is essential to keep the 2030 and 2050 completion targets, 
respectively for the Core and the Comprehensive Networks. UNIFE also calls on the 
Commission to include a monitoring mechanism. 
 
• Regulatory stability 
Regulatory stability can be supported (as a principle, not necessarily with specific requests) as 
an enabler to achieve the 2030 & 2050 deadlines. UNIFE calls on the European Commission to 
carefully consider the economic impact of further changes in the Technical Specifications for 
Interoperability (TSIs) and plan for transition rules in line with the asset life in the industry. 
 
• Public Procurement 
The TEN-T policy should be used as an opportunity for the EU to incentivise Member States to 
swiftly implement the 2014 EU public procurement framework. More particularly, UNIFE calls 
on the European Commission and Member States to ensure that the TEN -T policy promotes 
the “Most Economically Advantageous Tender” (MEAT) principle in rail procurements. Indeed, 
through the choice that contractors can make to favour more qual itative, social and 
environmental criteria (including life -cycle cost approach), public procurement is an 
instrumental driver for deploying smart, sustainable and innovative technologies on the TEN-
T network. And because contractors can already decide to reject bids if more than 50% of the 
value is added outside the EU (Article 85 of Directive 2014/25/EU), public procurement can 
also be used as a lever to maintain industrial jobs within Europe.

UNIFE asks the European Commission to be particularly vigilant on the proper enforcement of

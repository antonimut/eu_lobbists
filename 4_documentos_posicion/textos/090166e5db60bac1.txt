 
To date, the efforts to restore ecosystems in the EU have been insufficient. In 2011, a key voluntary 
target of the EU 2020 Biodiversity Strategy was to restore at least 15% of degraded ecosystems by 
2020. This voluntary target has not been met, and restoration plans were only developed by a 
couple of Member States. Some progress has been reported, in particular in areas where legal 
obligations exist in the Birdsand Habitats Directives, the Water Framework Directive, the   Floods 
Directive and the Marine Strategy Framework Directive, but there are still challenges that hinder 
restoration progress. For instance, there is no requirement for Member States to have biodiversity 
restoration plans. There are not always: clear or binding targets; specified timelines;  clear 
definitions of what is meant by restoration or the sustainable use of ecosystems. There is also no 
requirement to comprehensively map, monitor or assess the condition of ecosystems, the benefits 
they provide to society and restoration efforts. The EU Mapping and Assessment of Ecosystems and 
their Services initiative has made methodological progress in this respect, but there are still 
significant data gaps. 
Moreover, existing legislation covers only some of the EU's ecosystems. For example, the Birds  and 
Habitats Directives cover many natural and semi-natural parts of ecosystems but not cropland, 
intensively used grassland, forest plantations and urban ecosystems. Soil health and soil biodiversity 
are not explicitly covered by EU legislation. Thus, there is no EU legislation to explicitly address a 
number of ecosystems and habitats in need of restoration or species whose decline needs to be 
reversed. Furthermore, in most cases (with the exception of the prioritised action frameworks) 
there are no clear links between restoration needs and EU funding instruments. 
This consultation is part of the impact assessment that will underpin the Commission’s proposal for 
binding EU restoration targets. We would like to have your views on the main elements and the 
approach to devising the Commission’s proposal for binding res toration targets. Further targeted 
consultations and public workshops will be carried out to obtain stakeholder views on the 
conditions in which these targets should be implemented, as well as on the potential social, 
economic and wider environmental impacts that need to be taken into account. For more 
information, see  https://ec.europa.eu/environment/eu-naturerestoration-targets_en.

ECN prioritised ecosystems in relation to circular economy 
Ecosystem Remark 
Urban ecosystems, such as urban 
green areas and brownfields 
Maintaining and restoration of urban green areas and 
brownfields are important in relation to environment and 
health aspects. Impacts on air quality and climate are well 
known. In a circular economy park and garden waste should be 
collected separately and be treated in composting plants to high 
quality composts, which can be locally used for urban 
gardening, landscaping purposes (restoration of street 
greenings etc.). As a win-win solution using locally the potential 
of urban park and garden waste will save primary resources 
(fertilisers, soil substrates and growing media from primary 
sources (peatlands), energy (reduced transport costs, lower 
energy consumption for the composting process in comparison 
to mineral fertiliser production) and will finally save cost. Green 
public procurement using compost from bio-waste and green 
waste should be promoted.  
Inland wetlands, such as marshes, 
peatlands, wet grasslands, fluvial 
forests, riparian areas and 
floodplains 
Maintaining and restoration of peatlands are important in 
relation to biodiversity and saving GHG emissions. Peatlands are 
non-renewable resources, which stores carbon and contributes 
to biodiversity.  
Destroying peatlands leads to GHG emissions. Energy use of 
peat (burning of peat) should be forbidden. 
Using alternative materials for replacing peat in growing media 
should be promoted: Today, 80 to 90 % of growing media on 
the European market are sold as peat-based growing media, 
which can be replaced with a significant percentage of 
alternative organic materials from recycled processes or with 
other natural plant materials (e.g. compost, wood fibres, bark). 
Peat can be replaced with a significant percentage of quality 
compost (≥ 50 %) in potting soils and substrates for hobby 
gardening purposes. Depending on the characteristics of the 
compost (salt and nutrient content) up to 40 % of quality 
compost can be used in professional growing media. 
Soil ecosystems (across urban, 
agricultural, forest and other 
terrestrial ecosystems) 
Soils are essential ecosystems which deliver valuable services 
such as the provision of food, energy and raw materials, carbon 
sequestration, water purification and infiltration, nutrient 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
regulation, pest control and recreation. Healthy soils provide 
these functions simultaneously, therefore, soil is crucial for 
fighting climate change, protecting human health, safeguarding 
biodiversity and ensuring food security. Life in soils plays a key 
role to keep our soils fertile and healthy.  
We recommend to set up a comprehensive EU Soil Directive 
which includes 
▪ a European- wide monitoring system on soil health 
based on measurable indicators,   
▪ proposals for sustainable soil management systems  
These sustainable soil management systems should be effective 
against  
▪ desertification and the loss of soil organic matter, 
▪ erosion from wind and water, and  
▪ the loss on soil biodiversity. 
These sustainable soil management systems should maintain 
and /or improve 
▪ soil fertility,  
▪ water infiltration, and  
▪ soil biodiversity. 
As organic matter plays a key role in soil health and fertility, we 
call on the Commission to encourage Member States to develop 
eco-schemes in their National Strategic Plan for Agriculture that 
reward the farmers when they use quality compost to maintain 
and restore soil quality on their land. Carbon farming systems 
should include the use of quality recycled organic materials, like 
compost and digestate.

EAZA’s feedback to Commission implementing regulation amending Implementing Regulation (EU) 
No 2016/1141 to update the list of invasive alien species of Union concern - Ares(2019)1565888 
 
Created in 1992, the European Association of Zoos and Aquaria (EAZA) is the world's largest regional zoo and 
aquarium association, bringing together 400+ Member institutions across Europe, in the Middle East and 
beyond. We facilitate our Members’ collaboration towards the goals of conservation, education and research. 
Our Members receive over 140 million visits annually and we aim to ensure that all our visitors leave with a 
strong understanding of biodiversity and the threats to it wherever they originate. 
Within our commitment to protecting biodiversity, we have a specific responsibility for the conservation of 
species native to the EAZA region (please see EAZA Conservation Standards). In addition to carrying out our 
own conservation activities, EAZA also supports legislation that protects native European wildlife. In this 
context we have been closely following the establishment and implementation of the EU IAS Regulation and 
the updates of the IAS list. EAZA, and individual experts from the EAZA community, have been actively 
contributing to the collection of scientific evidence to inform the listing decisions. 
Against this background, we wish to express our surprise and concern about the non-inclusion of the 
American mink (Neovison vison) in the upcoming update of the EU IAS list.  
We wish to recall the arguments which we have previously shared with the European Commission:  
• The American mink is a species that has a massive impact on the European environment, if not the single 
most invasive mammal species in Europe. The risk assessment presented by Portugal contains strong 
scientific evidence to justify an immediate listing. Non-inclusion of this species threatens to undermine the 
entire IAS Regulation and its positive impacts.  
• In our opinion, prevention is a more environmentally-desirable and cost-effective approach to IAS than 
eradication of populations already established by feral individuals. This includes prevention of escapes 
from mink fur farms that have been a common pathway of introduction of the American mink across the 
EU.  
• We realise that the mink fur farming sector may consider the listing of the American mink to cause 
a disproportionate burden to their industry. However, Article 9 of the Regulation opens the option for 
farms to continue operating throughout the EU, if they can guarantee an adequately strict biosecurity 
regime.  
• Indeed, such an option is not offered to zoos although they have been recognized to present a low risk in 
relation to IAS. Whilst EAZA maintains its position that zoos should be offered a similar exemption, our 
focus at this time is on protecting native biodiversity against the threats posed by the invasive American 
mink. 
• The non-listing of the American mink makes it impossible to control feral populations of the species in an 
effective and coordinated way across the EU and to avoid further expansion of the species to areas in 
which it is not yet present. 
• We realise that listing the American mink would result in additional costs. However, any cost incurred 
would be modest and outweighed by the environmental benefits of eradicating the species. Besides, 
Art. 19 of the Regulation clearly stipulates that any action taken by Member States must be proportional to 
their own situation and based on a cost-benefit analysis.

Rejecting the inclusion of American mink on the IAS list is a missed opportunity to spare the European economy 
millions of Euros in LIFE projects to control American mink in order to protect native species. It also effectively 
means that the EU is deviating from its 2020 Biodiversity Strategy and Aichi targets.  
We are concerned that this decision will compromise the public confidence in the EU’s commitment to tackling 
invasive alien species and protecting European biodiversity for future generations.  
We strongly urge the European Commission and the Member States to revisit the listing of the American mink 
in the future.

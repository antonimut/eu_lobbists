 
 
 
2 April 2021, Brussels 
 
Statement on legally binding nature restoration 
targets  
The European Commission has opened a public consultation on the development of binding EU nature 
restoration targets for 2030.  In this context, t he Confederation of European Paper Industries (Cepi) 
would like to address the importance of the overall aim of the EU Biodiversity Strategy for 2030 : 
Europe’s biodiversity should be put  on the path to recovery for the benefit of people, the plan et and 
climate and encourage global action so that by 2050 all of the world’s ecosystems are restored, resilient 
and adequately protected.  It would be of utmost importance that the targets enable increasing the 
amount of resilient and actively managed forests in Europe. Care should be taken to avoid any negative 
impacts on the availability of sustainably sourced home-grown forest-based raw materials for the further 
development of the circular bioeconomy in Europe.    
Consequently, the Biodiversity Strategy for 2030 outlines an EU nature restoration plan in which a key 
element is a commitment by the Commission to propose, in 2021, binding EU nature restoration targets. 
The aim is to restore degraded ecosystems, in particular those with t he most potential to capture and 
store carbon (carbon-rich ecosystems) and to prevent and reduce impact of natural disasters. Cepi is 
of views that - as it comes to forests - scope of the policy should be limited in areas degraded by 
natural disturbances. These areas should be restored as a part of sustainable forest 
management to enhance resilience of these ecosystems in changing climatic conditions.  
Cepi supports of setting a generic EU target for nature restoration to enhance health and resilience of 
ecosystems. However, setting up a target for nature restoration is challenging as nature restoration is  
a nature management tool rather than an objective itself. Therefore, as it comes to forests, it is 
important to focus on restoration in protected areas . In case of m anaged forest land, nature 
restoration should be limited to areas degraded by  natural disturbances. The aim should be in 
facilitating the recovery of resilient forest ecosystems. A specific restoration target for forest 
would lead to unintended consequences as regards positive developments in forest resources 
in terms of quantity a nd long -term quality. Therefore, setting up of such target should be 
avoided.       
Due to the diversity of forest resources and their ownership structure  as well as different conditions 
across member states , it is crucial to take into account local specif icities and geo -climatic conditions. 
Voluntary tools such as forest certification play also an important role in demonstrating responsible 
sourcing from forests.  European pulp and paper industry has a strategic interest in keeping healthy 
and growing fore sts in Europe. It uses wood that has been legally harvested and comes from 
sustainably managed forests: 90% of the wood used by the industry comes from the EU. 74% of the 
wood is third-party certified as coming from responsibly managed forests.  
In order to embed forest ecosystems in the upcoming legislative framework, it would be essential to 
develop the approach under the auspices of the upcoming EU forest strategy and in particular 
benefitting the expertise of the Standing Forestry Committee (SFC). Active and sustainable forest 
management is a prerequisite for the further development of the circular bioeconomy in Europe. Cepi 
is committed to continue sustainable sourcing in Europe, based on the EU and national forest and 
nature legislations in place. In addition, responsible sourcing can be demonstrated via voluntary tools,

such as FSC and PEFC certification schemes. To summarize, the EU restoration initiative should take 
advantage of the expertise of  SFC in these complex matters to ensure enabling po licy framework for 
healthy forests and viable and innovative forest -based sector offering  employment for 3.5 million 
workers. One in five manufacturing companies in the EU is in forest -based industries. The economic 
value of wood encourages forest owners t o manage their forests with a long -term, sustainable 
perspective.

Onderwerp : Review of the application of the EU Regulation on Invasive Alien Species, 
 
 
Dear Sir, Madam,  
 
LTO Nederland, the Dutch Organisation for horticulture and agriculture, w elcomes the 
opportunity to contribute about the application of the EU Regulation on Invasive Alien Species 
(IAS). In our country, production and trade of nurserystock, perenials, garden plants and 
potted plants are a major and important part of our horticultural production.   
 
We kindly ask you to consider our remarks about this regulation: 
 
1. To combat IAS requires a close cooperation with private operators involved in 
production and/or trade of plants and animals. When plant and animal species are 
listed under the Regulation, the operators’ property may be banned and operators 
may hence suffer economic losses. Although justified in view of the general interest to 
stop IAS entry and spread, this is not fair in cases where banned IAS have been 
legally cultivated and traded for decades or more. We therefor suggest to add to the 
Regulation: 
-  A fair and sufficiently long transitional period. 
-  Financial compensation in cases where listed IAS are not invasive at all in a 
Member State given its climatic conditions.  
The Charter on Fundamental Rights of the EU requires that property cannot be 
confiscated without financial compensation. This notion is lacking in the Regulation 
on IAS. As a minimum, in analogy with the EU plant and animal health legislation 
(Regulation 652/2014), a clause needs to be inserted in the IAS Regulation permitting 
Member States to provide financial compensation for banned or destroyed species 
that have been legally possessed, cultivated and/or traded for more than 10 years , 
and EU co-financing in such cases. 
 
2. In the implementation at EU level, we think a stronger focus is required on 
prevention (import), rather than the control of already present and often widespread 
IAS species. The latter should be listed only exceptionally, for example when human 
health is at stake. In cases of already widespread species, the EU should refrain from 
listing, as it does for plant and animal health.  
 
3. When species are widely present in some Member States and absent in others, a 
regionalization policy is required. It is not proportionate to list species at Union level 
in such cases. The Regulation needs to be amended in this respect. 
4. Pests of plants and animals should not be covered under the IAS Regulation 
but fully left to the plant health and animal health regimes. The overlap is confusing 
and undesirable.

5. Decisions about changes to the list of Union IAS should be made for separate 
invasive species. Currently decisions are made in Comitology about large packages 
of candidate IAS. From a technical perspective, some candidate IAS are more 
justified than others while finetuning is not possible because the package is 
negotiated as a whole.

Preamble 
Uniper, a responsible and reliable operator of hydropower plants in Germany and Sweden, contributes significant 
resources to help achieve the targets of the Water Framework Directive (WFD). We see ourselves as part of the 
solution and commit to live up to society`s expectation to provide secure, renewable, environmentally and climate 
friendly power. This position paper aims to develop a broader perspective on how trust-based collaboration, 
unbiased viewpoints and ambitious, yet realistic measures produce sustainable results in terms of ecological 
performance. Finally, we advocate for the recognition of all efforts, big and small, while allowing nature to benefit 
from man-made improvement measures over time. At Uniper, we see the WFD as an opportunity to foster the 
relationship with our stakeholders and the public.
General considerations  
As a large hydropower operator and referring to the 
experiences we have gained in the context of the 
WFD in recent years, we emphasize the following 
general considerations: 
 
First, every technical solution has positive and 
negative impacts. 
 
Second, each situation requires individual measures 
that must be related to a cost-benefit analysis in 
order to achieve the best result; i.e. the highest 
contribution to a Good Ecological Status (GES) / 
Good Ecological Potential (GEP) within the given 
framework per Euro spent. 
 
Third, prioritization of ecological values and the 
benefits of hydropower in a defined scope (national, 
regional and local) is essential. This includes: 
 
• the morphology of rivers, connectivity and the 
availability of habitats; 
• the importance of renewable electricity production 
from hydropower; 
• the importance of hydropeaking in terms of 
balancing and regulating capacity. 
 
Fourth, the implementation of any measure should 
serve to improve the maturity of solutions and 
develop a toolbox of approved measures. 
Our view on the WFD fitness check: 
We support the prolongation of the WFD. 
Uniper endorses the goals of the WFD to reach the 
GES/GEP of European surface waters. The status of 
water bodies has improved noticeably since the 
WFD became effective. Nevertheless, achieving the 
targets by the end of the next management period 
challenging. Therefore, Uniper clearly supports a 
prolongation of the WFD beyond the year 2027. 
 
We promote a system of step by step 
improvements. 
The system of fixed time schedules does not reflect 
the temporal needs of ecosystems to unfold their full 
potential. Instead, a step-by-step approach of 
continuous improvements should be installed, 
resulting in visible successes. 
 
We require a realistic view of ecological 
potentials of heavily modified water bodies. 
Heavily modified waterbodies constitute a category 
under the WFD, with individual goals that differ from 
those of natural water bodies. The reference 
conditions should thus be evaluated realistically and 
improvement potentials set at challenging yet 
achievable levels. Ecological measures should 
furthermore always consider site-specific conditions 
to avoid a detrimental “copy-paste” approach of 
general measures

Unique rivers 
To implement the WFD via effective step-by-step improvement measures, we propose to distinguish three different 
reference scenarios (1-3) and possible measures to mitigate negative impacts (A-D). 
1. Natural river sectors without direct hydropower usage 
Natural river-ecosystems without relevant human influences rarely occur in European cultural landscapes. Where 
they do exist, they depict areas of the highest ecological quality and are of regional and trans-regional importance.  
River Lech (Germany), Litzauer Schleife 
 
Advantages: Disadvantages: 
• The ecosystem, nearly untouched by men 
possesses healthy, stable and highly adapted 
populations of fish and other river organisms. 
• The river independently shapes its 
morphology via high water events and 
sediment transport. It creates side arms, 
widenings and river banks, which bear 
precious habitats. 
• Unfortified rivers can result in severe flooding 
events, rendering them incompatible with 
urban areas, settlements and industry 
complexes.  
• The use of these river stretches for power 
production, touristic infrastructure, navigation 
or industrial purposes however, is almost 
impossible under current legislation.

Diverse opportunities 
The detrimental effects of hydropower plants under the second scenario can be tackled with various measures. 
Uniper offers tailor-made solutions to create diverse opportunities – individual for unique sites with specific needs. 
 
A. Technical solutions allow fish to migrate upstream beyond hydropower plants and weirs. 
 
Possible measures: 
• Construction of technical fish passes (basin type 
fish pass, rough channel, fish lifts, fish sluices 
etc.) 
• Construction of nature-like bypasses 
• Integration of existing parts of old riverbeds where 
applicable 
 
Limiting factors: 
• The realisation at high heads is currently not 
reasonably achievable. 
• Each construction is unique, tailor-made for a 
specific site. 
 
Uniper examples: 
 
B. Anadromous and catadromous fish are protected from entering turbines and where feasible solutions are 
installed to allow selective downstream migration. 
 
Possible measures: 
• Construction of downstream migration facilities 
• Mechanical barriers to hinder fish from entering 
turbine entrances (fish guides) 
• Regulated weir management during main 
migration times 
• Catch & carry methods 
 
Limiting factors: 
• No state of the art technology is available for 
downstream migration facilities and guiding 
structures for large scale hydropower plants. 
 
Uniper examples:
  
In river Faxälven (Northern Sweden), a 
tributary to river Ångermanälven, Uniper opens 
two low-head dams for fish-migration by 
constructing two nature-like fish-ways. 
Together with another ongoing project where 
Uniper is the largest owner this connects 
approximately 150 km of a highly regulated 
river. 
With the construction of the fifth fish pass on 
the Bavarian Danube between Donauwörth 
and Regensburg, upstream connectivity will 
soon be realized over a total length of 130 km. 
Inclined trash racks used to guide migrating 
fish downstream past small and medium size 
power plants were introduced in Sweden by 
Uniper for the first time at river Ätran ten years 
ago. Today this method has been pointed out 
as “Best available technique” for downstream 
guidance. Since the first implementation 
Uniper installed more inclined trash racks to 
support the continuous improvement of design 
criteria and ecological performance. 
Eel protection on the rivers Main and Regnitz 
(Germany) was realized by a combination of 
different protection measures – tailor-made for 
the individual local situations. Uniper combines 
catch & carry measures, the MIGROMAT® 
survey system, as well as zigzag tubes and 
the Bottom Gallery® as potential downstream 
paths for eels. In close cooperation with local 
fisheries and research institutes, Uniper 
continues to develop new solutions.

At river Isar the issue of sediment transport is 
widely managed through flushing of pebble 
banks during flood periods. Therefore, some 
weirs are equipped with pebble slots or gravel 
flushing channels. During floods the gravel is 
moved by consistent flushing for several days. 
If this is not sufficient to move the sediments 
adequately, the transport is done mechanically 
in some cases. 
Where necessary and useful, gravel is added 
manually to provide spawning ground. At 
hydropower plant Dessau at river Lech this is 
done regularly to support the natural river 
stretch downstream of the power plant.  
To reduce the effects of hydropeaking on a 
free-flowing river stretch on river Lech the 
influence of the hydropower operation on the 
risk of stranding of river organisms was 
analysed. As a result, the maximum change in 
flowrates was reduced to 20 m³/s per hour to 
improve the situation for juvenile fish. The flow 
pattern is controlled by the central control 
room of Uniper Hydro Germany. 
At the power plant Icking the old river part of 
river Isar (Germany) was revitalised with a 
high ecological flow (increased from 4 m³/s to 
15 m³/s) and an accompanying side arm of 
1.4 km length was introduced (Auebach) and 
enriched with precious habitats. 
Hydraulic models combined with field-studies 
have been used to design ecological flows in 
two N2000-rivers in Sweden (river Emån and 
Mörrumsån). By testing different scenarios 
both ecological aspects and impact on 
renewable energy was estimated and used as 
an input in the decision-process.

Political framework 
Knowing about the condition of rivers and the efforts which have already been made to improve the ecological 
status, Uniper is dedicated to play a part in improving the current situation. To focus on significant measures that 
add real value to our ecosystems, the WFD must build the right framework. Therefore, we propose the following 
policy recommendations within the fitness check of the WFD.
 
 
Consider more flexible timeframes for ecosystems to respond to 
improvement measures. 
Although the final management period has not yet begun, it becomes obvious 
that
 more time will be needed to reach the WFD targets. The difficulty to 
define and implement adequate measures, along with the uncertainty of the 
effects of certain measures and the ecological needs of river systems to 
develop lead to this recommendation to prolong the WFD beyond the year 
2027. Moreover, the strict timeline makes it difficult for authorities and project 
owners to find an effective and efficient ecological solution in unique 
situations because they put pressure to act in the short term. Uniper is 
dedicated to support the improvement process in the long-term allowing new 
ecosystems to develop. 
 
Create room for acknowledgement of individual milestone 
achievements towards the ultimate WFD objectives. 
Uniper promotes a system of continuous stepwise ecological improvements, 
by implementing measures, evaluating their success and – based on the 
learnings – defining further improvements. The development of new technical 
solutions requires time and practical experience to result in mature 
standards. Every incremental improvement should be considered a success, 
independent of the question, if the final goal has been achieved or not. By 
making success visible and transparent, the constructive public dialogue 
about further optimizations is enriched, if not enabled in the first place. Uniper 
sees this process as a permanent challenge for all involved parties and is 
committed to actively support it. 
 
Allow member states to set targets considering the broad variety of 
societal interests. 
The different political goals of the EU, expressed in various legislative and 
non-legislative documents (EU Floods Directive, EU Renewable Energy 
Directive, Paris Climate Agreement), need to be aligned with the WFD and 
then reflected in national, regional and local guidelines. The important 
contribution of hydropower towards the EU’s climate goals must be 
considered when implementing the WFD. Uniper strives to maximize the 
advantages and to mitigate the pressures through apt measures and focusing 
on the overall value. 
 
Uniper therefore supports the prolongation of the WFD and a case-sensitive implementation, which can be 
improved within the existing framework of directives.

EUsalt - The European Salt Producers’ Association 1 - welcomes the EU 2030 Biodiversity strategy and its 
targets, which aim to halt biodiversity loss and safeguard ecosystems. EUsalt stresses the need to 
recognise Solar Sea Saltworks as unique coastal ecosystems which strengthen the sustainable 
development of the European economy, tourism and coastal landscapes and help to improve resilience 
to climate change.  
Key Environmental Value of Sea Saltworks for 
Biodiversity Preservation 
 
The COVID -19 pandemic has highlighted the 
extent of the nega tive impact climate change 
and biodiversity loss have on our economies and 
wellbeing, and the urgent need to safeguard 
biodiversity in view of the rising risks the world 
faces in the next decade.  
 
Sea Saltworks produce salt by evaporating 
seawater using only the incident solar radiation 
assisted by the wind action. They are uniquely 
constructed saline wetlands where regular and 
hypersaline environments coexist, fostering key 
shelters for wildlife.  Sea salt production is an 
ecosystem generator. The wetlands originating 
from saltpans are reservoirs which gather 
significant bacterial communities, similar to 
natural saline ecosystems, which are home to a 
wide range of bird and fish species, some of 
them end angered and protected. Saltworks 
make a crucial contribution to biodiversity 
conservation. More than 100 bird species have 
been observed in every Sea Saltworks around 
Mediterranean Sea, many of which are 
protected by European and International 
conventions. Key species such as Avocets, 
Flamingos, Himantopus, Black -necked Grebes, 
Kentish Plovers, depend directly on the existence 
and functioning of Saltworks.  
 
Sea saltworks can represent an important capital 
to develop and improve biodiversity. Its activity 
depends on biodiversity and vice-versa. They are 
an illustrative example demonstrating that 
development and environmental protection can 
go hand in hand.  
 
The EU should recognise the importance and uniqueness of Sea saltworks in safeguarding wetland areas 
and protecting biodiversity in the Mediterranean and globally. They should be recogni sed by the 
European Commission and its Member States as crucial and unique ecosystems where human 
intervention helps maintain and safeguard biodiversity and wildlife.

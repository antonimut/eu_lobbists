Intesa Sanpaolo Group, one of the top banking group s in Europe, welcomes the opportunity to 
provide feed-back to the European Commission on its Inception Impact Assessment (IIA) on Instant 
Payments (IP). 
 
Intesa Sanpaolo agrees that a lack of well-functioning instant credit transfer solution that could fully 
rely on European payments infrastructure obliges most of the European PSPs  to be dependent on 
global players, being them cards or more and more Big Techs for EU cross-border card payments.  
 
Also, Intesa Sanpaolo shares the European Commission concerns on the still low level of adherence 
to the SCT Inst scheme by EU PSPs . Specifically, on the adherence issue, we believe that there is a 
need to urgently act and take most effective measures to make IP the “new normal” across the EU. 
We believe that the most effective measure to make IPs the new normal across the EU would be an 
EU legislation making Payment Service Providers’ adherence to SCT Inst. Scheme mandatory within 
a very short timeline. The mandatory adherence to the scheme should be limited to the reachability. 
The choice whether to offer or not an Instant Payment should be, of course, left to each PSP. 
 
Furthermore, the widespread uptake of IPs in the EU is an essential element, in our opinion, to deliver 
a successful pan-European payment solution, along with the following other conditions that should 
be satisfied: reliance on already existing EU payments infrastructures ; leveraging on existing 
domestic solutions, and VAS to be added to the new scheme such as, for example, digital identity.  
 
Furthermore, we would like to share our preliminary comments on some measures the Commission is 
considering under the legislative options:  
 
1. Effective incentives for PSPs to offer instant credit transfers in euro, which could be 
constructed in a manner similar to that adopted in the past in the SEPA Regulation for SEPA 
Credit Transfers and Direct Debits: We agree that the best legislative option to make the SCT 
Inst. Scheme mandatory would be to construct it in a manner similar to that adopted in the 
past in the SEPA Regulation for SEPA Credit Transfers and Direct Debits. Also, consistency and 
coordination with other legislative measures should be ensured, such as the PSD2 (and its RTS) 
as it contains some provisions related to IP; 
 
2. Tailored fraud prevention measures :  Frauds referred to instant payments is a crucial issue 
because of the increasing importance of this method of payment. However, this 
phenomenon is critical for digital payments in general. Considering the need of fostering 
digital operational resilience at European level, it is fundamental to strengthen the protection 
of citizens in an increasingly digital economy with a more effective operational response at 
EU level to the phenomenon of online frauds.  
 
In order to prevent frauds, there are, for examples, substantial obstacles when sharing cyber 
threat information, especially when these involve personally identifiable information (PII), due 
to the number of requirements imposed by both national and EU regulators. It is therefore 
important to define cases of exception when information sharing can lead to high benefits, 
for example in the anti -fraud management, in compliance with competition rules, in which 
the difficulties related to the presence of data associated to physical people  may been 
countered.  We believe that these aspects should be addressed in the DORA proposal (Art. 
40 on information sharing).  
 
In order to combat frauds , we call for more involvement by the public sector in the fight 
against frauds, for example by making available instruments both for the customers and the 
private sector to act promptly against cyber criminals  trying to gain credentials or to

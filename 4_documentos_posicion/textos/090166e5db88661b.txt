Response to Commission Inception 
Impact Assessment on Instant Payments 
from Trustly Group AB 
 
Trustly Group AB (“Trustly”) is a Swedish licensed payment institution providing 
payment initiation services throughout the EU and UK.  
Trustly welcomes the opportunity to provide input on the Commission Inception 
Impact Assessment on Instant Payments (the “Impact Assessment”). We are in 
favour of the Commission’s objective to enhance the payments infrastructure in 
the EU by increasing the possibilities to make instant payments, which would be to 
the benefit of both consumers and business in the EU. 
For many years, outgoing transfers have been debited more or less instantly, 
whilst incoming transfers are credited only with one day delay, which is particularly 
disadvantageous to consumers and SMEs. SCT Inst will bring this to an end and it 
will also give payers an additional payment instrument, which will be less costly 
than the handling of cash, and more available and possibly cheaper than cards 
A well-functioning instant credit transfer solution in the EU, would have the 
advantage of enhancing competition between providers of instant payments 
reducing the reliance on e.g. card schemes. The reliance on other payment 
methods than card payments can only be reduced once if the lower non-execution 
risk of an SCT initiation is reduced. That can be achieved if SEPA Instant Credit 
Transfers are implemented across all banks. 
As Trustly’s business model entails making credit transfers from consumers’ bank 
accounts we have indeed experienced several of the problems indicated by the 
Commission in the Impact Assessment. In particular lack of support of SEPA 
Instant Credit Transfers and high fees associated with such transfers constitute 
obstacles towards utilising the possibilities that come with SEPA Instant Credit 
Transfers.  
Trustly believes that the Commission, when choosing between the available 
options, should carefully consider what option is likely to most quickly achieve the 
most significant increase in use of SEPA Instant Credit Transfers. Although the

Rather, we believe that dedicated measures in line with the non-legislative option 
is likely to quicker have a material impact by increasing the availability of SEPA 
Instant Credit Transfers. Such measures could be coupled with a limited legislative 
effort to reduce the possibility of account servicing payment service providers to 
charge consumers fees for SEPA Instant Credit Transfers. Currently, consumer 
fees are common for instant payments more than non-instant payments. I.e. there 
are consumer fees to a large extent specifically targeted for the payment being 
instant. Moreover, the legislative option could be focused on addressing fees for 
instant payments specifically, eliminating differences in fees between instant and 
non-instant payments.

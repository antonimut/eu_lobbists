The aviation industry in Europe is facing a growth in demand of 5,4% average 
per year.
▪ While passenger numbers reach unprecedented levels, current modes of 
transportation are reaching capacity limits struggling to cope with the increased 
demand for transportation. 
▪ However, this is only going to intensify looking at forecasts for future growth 
scenarios. In 2040, 160 million passengers, equivalent to 1.3 million flights, 
would be unable to fly.
Rail has been unable to solve the problem without the need for large 
investments and new infrastructure. 
▪ Greenpeace and Royal Haskoning DHV studied the potential to substitute 
short-haul flights to International rail from Schiphol. 
▪ The proposed network consists of 31 destinations with 176,000 flight 
movements per year. 
▪ The share of rail will increase from 27% (current) to 63%.
▪ A new network of 3,310 kilometers new HSR infrastructure is required. This 
network would require an international investment of €78 billion (€25 million 
per km). 
▪ The German government and DB approved an investment program of about € 
86 billion to upgrade the existing rail network and perform maintenance work.
▪ This does not account for the necessary expansion of the network in order to 
handle a doubling of passenger numbers until 2030 as it is the aim of DB. 
Hardt studied on the impact of substitution of short/medium-haul flights 
within Europe to support decarbonization goals towards 2050.
▪ A 10.000 km network in Europe connecting the 11 countries with the highest 
number of aviation passenger-kilometers, 37 European cities, and 39 out of the 
top 50 airports.
▪ Aligns and builds on the strategy of the European Commission to strengthen 
and expand the Trans-European Transport Network (TEN-T).
Hyperloop is projected to decongest the aviation sector by substituting 252-
303 million passengers in 2040 (between 1,7-2,0 million aircraft movements 
annually).
- With two scenarios of low and high growth for aviation and rail in 2040, it is 
expected that aviation will grow towards 297-357 million passengers.
- Hyperloop would be able to capture 85% of the market share of aviation.
- With a cost of €30 million per kilometer, the total network of 9.588 km is 
estimated to cost €288 Billion. 
- Hyperloop would be able to reduce CO2 emissions between 12-26 million 
tonnes of CO2 per annum, a reduction of 71-79% compared to aviation.
- The hyperloop network is projected to run a positive operating margin on the 
full network, but no financially profitable isolated links when substituting only 
aviation and international rail.

accommodate the growing number of passengers while

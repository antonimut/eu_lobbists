Abstract 
 
The emergency created by the spread of the COVID-19 pandemic has strongly 
highlighted the strategic role of freight and passenger transport and logistics as an 
irreplaceable driver, in order to support domestic supplies and to give new impetus to 
the demand for foreign trade, in particular to reboot recovery and economic 
development. 
In this context, it is therefore important to stress that, while one of the national priorities 
in the field of transport is to ensure the implementation and improvement of the National 
Transport System (SNIT), it is imperative to develop and strengthen these 
infrastructures in connection with the Trans-European Transport Networks (TEN-T), 
which guarantee links both between the different areas of the country and abroad, 
improving their territorial, economic and social cohesion. 
In order to give a contribution to this consultation, Confindustria presents below a brief 
reflection on some profiles of interest for companies of our System directly and indirectly 
involved in the fields of transport, sustainable mobility and infrastructure. 
Confindustria is the main association representing manufacturing and service companies 
in Italy, with a voluntary membership of more than 150,000 companies of all sizes.

1. Introduction 
With a view to greater integration and development of the internal market, national 
implementation of TEN-T is essential to allow people and goods move faster and safer and 
to promote sustainable and digital solutions, necessary to achieve European objectives 
mainly based on energy and climate aspects. 
Among the existing corridors, four of them cross Italian territory: the Mediterranean Corridor, 
the Rhine-Alps Corridor, the Baltic-Adriatic Corridor and the Scandinavian-Mediterranean 
Corridor. The efficiency of rail and road connections and the completion of “last mile” 
connections to ports and airports require a major effort, particularly in terms of economic 
equipment for their construction. For this reason, the completion of TEN-T has been included 
among the main actions of Mission 3 of the National Recovery and Resilience Plan (PNRR), 
also in order to improve the endowment and service capacity of the Southern transport 
system, and it is also considered  the priority of national infrastructure policy, systematically 
confirmed in the Infrastructure Annexes to the Economic and Financial Documents (DEF), 
with particular regard to the implementation of missing links and enhancement of existing 
ones, with a view to more effective long-term planning. 
2. Integrations of the current structure 
Among the main objectives of TEN-T policy, the extension of interoperable networks, which 
make it possible to facilitate cross-border links and the multimodal integration of transport 
infrastructures and reference telematics applications, is the strategic issue of completing 
sections of the core network and of the comprehensive network, with a view to improving 
the efficiency of the use of resources, the integration of passenger and freight transport and 
logistical coordination, in particular on cross-border routes, multimodal connections and 
connection points. 
In this context, Confindustria stresses the need to achieve some “missing links”, both rail 
and road, which would allow the strengthening of transport and logistics links and, at the 
same time, greater national economic growth. 
I.e., in current conformation of the Baltic-Adriatic corridor, the Adriatic-Ionic macro region is 
completely devoid in some sections of the Adriatic strip. In this case, the absence of 
connection could not only compromise the competitiveness of any of the areas bordering 
the Adriatic, but it could also hinder the development of the entire macro-region, due to the 
difficult crossing of passengers and goods of this territory and increase its marginality and 
peripherality compared with the main economic, national and European markets and poles. 
In this context, we must also frame the aim, expressed in the Infrastructure Annex to the 
DEF 2020, to strengthen national competitiveness in Europe, through the consolidation of

horizontal maritime links between the Adriatic-Ionic region and the Balkan area, as well as 
the construction of new railway and road infrastructures. For the same purpose, it is 
essential to extend the TEN-T to the transverse crossings between the Adriatic and 
Tyrrhenian stripes, also in order to connect the Inland Areas (Aree Interne) of the Peninsula. 
Confindustria, in order to support better territorial and socio-economic cohesion of the entire 
national territory, therefore suggests a new configuration of TEN-T that can include new 
sections, to ensure the continuity of the Corridors through the creation of missing links and 
the improvement of the degree of interoperability between the networks, without neglecting 
the importance of finding appropriate solutions for cross-border sections, logistical and 
urban hubs and existing bottlenecks. 
In particular, it is important to promote the most complete modal interconnection (train, road 
and local transport) of port and airport services as the main “gateways” to the TEN-T 
Corridors, which constitute real “Mobility hubs” for the flows of people and goods of the 
territory. The evolution into “Smart Mobility hubs” – consistent with objectives of the Green-
Deal underlying the revision of Reg. EU 1315/2013 – should be more strongly supported by 
the CEF, to promote projects aimed at the green/digital transition. In this sense, it would be 
desirable to access to the CEF interventions to support sustainability of green projects 
serving transport infrastructures, such as energy and renewable (i.e., solar/wind) 
infrastructures and production/storage of green fuels (hydrogen/sustainable fuel) or 
realization of electric charging points at modal interchange hubs and along road networks. 
3. Governance mechanisms 
In the context described above, Confindustria focuses on TEN-T governance instruments, 
highlighting the relationship between specific pre-funding, financing and pricing systems and 
transport policies as a whole, in particular in the infrastructure management phase. The 
main attention is on consequences of the lack of governance – without neglecting the effects 
of economic policy and of transport impacts – linked to Corridor management and to solution 
of possible crises in hubs and transit lines. 
In the context described above, it is important to highlight some cases involving national 
transport infrastructures framed in the context of the TEN-T, such as the crisis generated by 
the blockade of the Rastatt railway junction, the Brenner corridor at the Italian and German 
border with Austria, and the succession of cross-border blockades in various parts of the 
Union as a result of the health emergency. The difficulty of managing the crises generated 
by these events recalls the need of a deep reflection on a European governance intervention 
related to the crossing of the Alpine arc in order to avoid blocking transits and ensure the 
management of alternative rail/road routes.

Currently, the European Rail Corridors are organized into autonomous legal entities 
(European Economic Interest Groups or International Associations) and have their own 
operational governance, in which the different infrastructure managers are represented, with 
which they define optimal ways of harmonizing processes and tools for the management of 
international railway capacity and planned traffic interruptions, as well as coordinating the 
management of cross-border traffic and emergencies. There is a lack of similar governance 
for Motorway Corridors, to allow interventions at European level of coordination between the 
infrastructure management of the different countries and to solve any critical issues on 
transits. 
Even more important is the lack of a level of “European corridor governance”, i.e. concerning 
the integrated functionality of the different modes of transport, in particular road and rail. 
At the moment, the most emblematic case is on the Scandinavian-Mediterranean Corridor, 
which has seen its operation in the crucial section of the Brenner called into question on 
several occasions in recent years, by measures taken by Austrian local authorities to restrict 
(in a selective and discriminatory way) motorway transits, in the presence of a limited and 
very poor-quality rail alternative (pending the completion of the new rail tunnel included in 
the TEN-T projects).1 
The current pandemic has also highlighted the critical nature of the lack of European 
governance of corridors, with numerous cases of national/regional unilateral blocking or 
restrictions of border crossings, with impacts and repercussions along the networks and 
subsequent border crossings, without prior notification and without any European prior 
screening to safeguard the free movement of goods and avoid potential distortions of 
competition that are not justified and proportional to other interests to be protected. 
The optimum use of European Corridors must therefore be a primary objective to be pursued 
and guaranteed at European level, which must be included in the TEN-T regulation, through 
the establishment of European governance structures and procedures concerning the 
control and management of Corridors of the relevant road and rail modes (e.i. Brenner).

4. Structure of calls for tenders and financing 
Confindustria is focusing the possible proposal to change the rules on the mechanism of 
calls for tenders governing the financing of assistance for the development of the TEN-T.  
Criteria adopted in the 2014-2020 cycle of “thematic concentration” or “specialization” of 
annual calls creates many problems on the timing and programming of the project and 
technical-administrative activity of the proposals. 
In order to finance projects with greater flexibility and to avoid potential discrimination, the 
European Commission should therefore ensure that Member States can participate more 
freely with proposals not limited to certain categories of intervention set out in the calls for 
proposals. 
Finally, the financing of national corridor sections should also be excluded from the 
constraints of the Stability Pact, depending on the European interest in completing the TEN-
T. 
5. Development of combined and intermodal transport 
The TEN-T aim to create a European transport area based on a multi-level and multimodal 
articulation integrated between road, rail and port and airport solutions, urban and territorial, 
with to the aim to create intermodal terminals to be completed and made fully interoperable 
by 2050. 
In this context, Confindustria supports the European priority of improving the degree of 
interoperability and modal shift between networks, in particular cross-border networks, 
ensuring the necessary links between the different modes of transport and removing existing 
bottlenecks at major urban hubs. 
Due to the centrality attributed to rail mode for the sustainability of transport, it is therefore 
important to promote more combined and intermodal transport within TEN-T, which already 
accounts for half of the EU's rail freight traffic and is expected to increase significantly. 
For this purpose, new regulation should help to define TEN-T technical parameters for rail 
infrastructure, taking into account combined transport’s point of view, guide the 
redevelopment of existing transport and the construction of new modal transfer terminals. 
Interoperability represents another profile related to the development of combined transport 
(e.i., through the digitization and homogenization of information flows) between the transport 
service operators of each corridor, including ports, airports and interports, with the aim to 
facilitate the establishment of an overall intermodal transport system.

6. Integration of transport, energy and digital TENs 
In view of the major European challenges of the digital energy transition, the development 
of TENs should take place on greater synergy between TEN-T (transport), TEN-E (energy) 
and TEN-D (digital), in order to promote zero/low emission, digitised, quality and safe 
mobility, especially for to logistic (ports, airports, interports) and urban/metropolitan hubs. 
In particular, sustainable mobility requires strong coordination between energy and transport 
networks, from major logistics hubs (ports, airports, interports) and urban networks to road 
and motorway networks, for the supply of vehicles with alternative sources with low 
environmental impact (electricity, biofuels, etc.) and to encourage the transition to the 
production and use of zero- or low-emission vehicles.

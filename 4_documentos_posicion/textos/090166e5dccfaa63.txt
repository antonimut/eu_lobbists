E.ON’s recommendations for the TEN-T revision  
 
As part of its initiative to ramp-up the production and deployment of sustainable and carbon neutral 
transport fuels in the European Green Deal, the European Commission will review the Regulation (EU) 
1315/2013 on guidelines for the development of the Trans-European Transport (TEN-T) Network. 
E.ON is Europe’s largest privately-owned energy company operating Europe-wide. We are one of the 
largest network provides in Europe and offer services in 15 countries to more than 50 million 
customers. We are focused on delivering intelligent networks and innovative customer solutions. E.ON 
possess a very broad and deep added value in the e-mobility sector. We act as a provider of car power 
contracts, as an operator of charging infrastructure in the public, semi-public and private environment, 
as well as a solution partner for integrated e-mobility solutions for private and business customers. 
E.ON has made advanced contributions to the charging infrastructure network development: We 
manage a Europe-wide charging infrastructure network and operate more than 12,000 public charging 
points in 11 countries. 
 
Standardisation is key  
Given the EU’s objective to promote cross-border interoperable mobility among its member states, 
regarding e-mobility the vastly different ability to recharge an EV when driving through Europe must 
be addressed. The EU must ensure that cars and trucks can move easily along Europe, even when the 
number of electrified vehicles is rising.  
When it comes to charging infrastructure, standardization is key. Standards play a key role in the 
development and deployment of technology in society, providing an indispensable basis for widespread 
market penetration and customer convenience. Agreed standards tend to encourage innovation, boost 
productivity and shape market structure in a way that enhances economic efficiency, reducing or 
eliminating technical barriers that can create market distortions. 
For plug-in vehicles to become a success, both hardware (connector and cables) and communication 
software standards are a prerequisite to the establishment of a secure investment climate for the 
required infrastructure. Common standards will generate cost benefits and help to create economies of 
scale for both electricity companies and the automobile industry. They will also help to avoid the risk of 
stranded assets resulting from the deployment of interim proprietary solutions and foster the sharing 
of development costs. 
CCS uses PLC (Power Line Communications) to communicate with vehicles, the same as the power 
grids. This technology should therefore be supported and part of the criteria when it comes to funding 
programs such as CEF Transport.   
 
EVs and grids, a symbiotic but risky relationship 
The penetration of EVs into the power grids is increasing in Europe. With an increase in penetration, an 
optimal management for charging is becoming an important factor for customers as well as grid 
operators. EVs can work in a symbiotic relationship with power grids since EVs can provide valuable 
services to the system. They have the ability to be used for peak shaving, frequency regulation and 
voltage control. With their flexible nature, they also have the ability to support further integration of 
renewables into the energy system.  But this comes with at a price. Cyberthreats raise various 
concerns, such as public safety hazards to vehicle operators and those in the immediate vicinity as well 
as disruptions to electricity grid operations. Electrification and smart mobility must therefor be 
considered in light of the persisting cyber threat, taking into account the increasing interdependency of 
the various components and actors of the EU interconnected system. The revision of the TEN-T must 
therefor incorporate key elements of the forthcoming new EU Cybersecurity Strategy.

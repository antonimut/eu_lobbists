UIP Position Paper on the revision of Regulation (EU) No 
1315/2013 on the TEN-T guidelines 
With this position paper, the International Union of Wagon Keepers (UIP) welcomes the revision of the TEN-T 
Guidelines but would like to draw to the attention of the European Commission some important aspects that 
require careful consideration in the revision of the regulation.  
Same technical parameters than the core-network and more targeted implementation of 
the TEN-T parameters on busy rail freight sections  
A speedy and coordinated implementation of the technical parameters on the whole TEN-T transport network 
is essential for rail freight where many interoperability obstacles remain. The distinction between the core and 
comprehensive network in the regulation and the different deadlines  for their completion are hampering rail 
freight transportation . In practice,  as trains do not sta rt or end only on the core network, the core and 
comprehensive networks shall belong together as artery and veins . Since logistic, supply chain and market 
demand alter freight flows, we consider as crucial  for the European Commission to gather data on the actual 
important rail and road freight flows within the EU,  taking into account industrial areas and sidings which may 
not be covered yet by the comprehensive nor the core network. The related relevant rail infrastructure as well 
as any important alternative routes  or sections shall be updated with the same technical parameters as the 
core corridors and the comprehensive network . This would  ensure an efficient train ride  for both freight and 
passengers and allow for the definition of appropriate contingency measures to avoid another Rastatt incident. 
In addition, such information would serve to  shift traffic from road to rail  and hence get us closer to the 
objectives of the European Green Deal . An analysis of the current bottlenecks on relevant rail infrastructure 
sections and their resolutions in a tar geted manner would also be needed. Given the time and budgetary 
constraints, UIP considers as imperative to prioritise the upgrade of bottlenecks and busy rail sections with  
early deadlines and the appropriate political pressure. Besides a quick upgrade of infrastructure parameters, 
one should also consider the possibility of doubling or increasing the number of lines on busy rail sections if 
justified by rail freight traffic demand. 
Multimodal transhipment facilities to replace the restrictive view on terminals 
To reach the objective of a sustainable transport system, multimodal transhipment facilities, covering not only 
the movements of Intermodal Loading Units (containers, swap-bodies, trailers) but also the movement of goods 
and wagons  (hubs, marshalling yards, public and private sidings)  should be covered in the new TEN-T 
requirements. Important infrastructure parameters relating to transhipment facilities shall include the possibility 
to operate 740 m trains, sufficient sidings length (for transhipments and repair as well as maintenance and 
storage of freight wagons) and double or multiple tracks (for marshalling, crossing and passing of other trains). 
A clear categorisation of existing transhipment facilities by type and capacity  would facilitate the ir upgrading 
while providing a tool for deciding about the necessity of having new  builts based on market demands from a

Improve status reporting about implementation of TEN-T parameters on core and 
comprehensive network 
In today’s TEN -T report , UIP regrets t he low level of information provided as the reports does  not provide 
relevant data on which part of the TEN -T core and comprehensive infrastructure are or would be ready to 
accommodate 740 m long trains, or are equipped with ERTMS or able to handle rail freight traffic  with 
appropriate tunnel profile and bridge clearance (for examp le for  HGVs measuring 4m  at their top corners ). 
The TEN-T reporting should be improved in that respect if it intends to serve rail freight  interests and help 
railway undertakings to decide on appropriate routes for developing new offers. 
Alignment and cooperation between RFC and TEN-T core network need to serve rail 
freight interests 
When it comes to aligning the Rail Freight Corridors (RFC) and the TEN -T core network, rail freight interests 
need to be saved and the natural freight flows guarded. We therefore call on the European Commission to 
ensure that relevant RFC sections which might not belong to the TEN -T core corridors are upgraded with the 
same technical parameters. 
The coordination between Rail Freight Corridors (RFC) and TEN -T should be significantly improved by 
including the relevant TEN-T coordinator(s) in the RFC Executive Boards and giving them a clear mandate to 
also defend rail freight interests vis-à-vis the Member States. The RFC should focus on the operational aspects 
of rail freight traffic including the traffic management side, whereas the TEN-T should focus on the standards, 
quality, and investments in existing or new rail infrastructure. 
Strengthen nature of TEN-T investment plans in one document 
With the alignment of the RFC and the TEN-T network , UIP would welcome the introduction of a single 
investment plan which is mandatory in nature . Furthermore, the possibilities for Member States to derogate 
from the TEN -T parameters shall be further restricted1 as to avoid a  European patchwork which will further 
hamper rail freight operations  und undermine the competitiveness of the rail sector . Since the RFCs also 
convene platform meetings where railway undertakings (RAG) and the end-customers (shippers, operators,  
..) can express their views (TAG), RFC should also have a say in the investment plans and priorities relating 
to the TEN-T network, in particular in order to address market concerns, needs and priorities.

Recommendations 
 
Based on the above, UIP strongly recommends the European Commission to: 
■ Consider the core and comprehensive TEN-T network as the arteries and veins of the Single European 
Transport Area 
■ Broaden the scope of terminals to include all relevant transhipment facilities  when looking at interfaces 
between the modes and multimodality 
■ Improve the reporting in scope and time on the implemented TEN-T parameters  
■ Align RFC and TEN-T and strengthen cooperation on defining investments priorities

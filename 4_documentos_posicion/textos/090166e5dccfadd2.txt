Port of Rotterdam position paper on the TEN-T revision - May 2021 
Excellent infrastructure to and from maritime ports and port -industrial complexes is crucial for the 
competitiveness of both existing and new markets and hence European prosperity and employment. 
The Port of Rotterdam (hereafter: “the Port”) welcomes the opportunity to contribute to the revision of 
the Trans-European Transport Network (TEN -T) Regulation (2013/1315) and the own -initiative report 
by the European Parliament. The revision of the TEN -T guidelines must be used as an opportunity to 
update the TEN-T framework. In order to meet evolving environmental, economic and societal needs, 
the completion of the TEN-T Core Network by 2030 must be an absolute priority.  
The port is committed to making transport corridors and hinterland connections more sustainable and 
efficient by increasing operational efficiency, delivering the infrastructure for the deployme nt of 
alternative fuels and encouraging a modal shift to rail and inland waterway transport. It is of great 
importance for the Port and interdependent and interconnected distribution and industrial clusters to be 
able to count on a robust, reliable, effici ent and sustainable transport system with strong connections 
to the hinterland in order to remain a “global hub” and stay competitive with respect to emerging 
markets, new trade routes and an ever -changing global playing field. In particular, the COVID -19 
crisis has demonstrated the importance of reliable uninterrupted supply chains. It is this 
reliability or crisis resilience that must be central to the review.  To this end, the port would like 
to propose recommendations with regard to the legal status of the directive, the accuracy of the 
compliance standards and adaptation of  those standards  to actual and future market 
conditions.  
 Legal status TEN-T Core Network  
The TEN-T policy framework serves as a powerful instrument to monitor the status and progress of the 
international transport corridors and projects related to meeting the objectives . During the COVID-19 
crisis, the core networks have proven to continue to function well with regard to maintaining the 
flow of essential cross -border freight transport.  This property advocates protecting the status of 
core networks. The TEN-T policy framework should therefore be strengthened in order to further 
support projects along the corridors and coordinate investments at Member State level. Although 
the TEN-T Regulation prescribes obligations with regard to compliance to Core Network requirements, 
it does not grant special protective status to supported projects located on the Core corridors. This leads 
to the situation where investments in projects can be cancelled out by local or regional conflicting 
interests, e.g. housing construction in the vicinity of rail freight routes. The Port recommends that the 
TEN-T revision should give  projects on the TEN-T corridors a special legal status on the basis 
of which they enjoy protection in the national and local p olicy context. Additionally, the revision 
should also prevent national regulatory add -ons that hinder both an equal European level playing field 
and the market accessibility and movement on the core network. Additional national regulations 
regarding cross -border rail freight transport with regard to vibrations, noise and the transport of 
hazardous substances are examples of this.  
Futureproof Inland waterways   
Inland waterways are responsible for 45% of the Port of Rotterdam’s hinterland transport , more than 
150mln ton per year. As this figure indicates, this is a unique transport mode with a high intrinsic energy 
efficiency compared to other modes of transport. Inland waterways therefore offer a huge potential to 
contribute to the objectives as se t out in the European Green Deal and stimulate the modal shift if the 
necessary parameters are met.  The current scenarios depicted in the corridor work plans of the future 
goods flows however paint a prospect in which inland waterway transport is no longe r growing. This is 
worrying as it does not correspond to the Green Deal objectives. At the same time the modal shift 
potential is not being met because of lack of awareness of SME shippers about how to use IWT (mental

shift) and the handling of inland vesels in the ports. Cooperation of barge operators and inland terminals 
on certain corridors of TEN-T network can improve the attractiveness of container transport to and from 
dedicated deepsea terminals in the ports. The development of multimodal nodes and services with IWT 
and rail, together with the roll-out of clean energy hubs along the main arteries, should improve the core

Inland navigation on the Rhine is under pressure due to the increasing frequency of low and 
high water levels due to climate change.  The use of additional ships, planning costs, storage 
costs and even delivery problems - and the scarcity of certain products and raw materials - are 
all factors that have increased transport costs  and have also led to significant production 
losses, making inland shipping a less attractive transport mode for shippers.   
Inland waterway operators and their clients are depending on reliable infrastructure. The optimal 
use of inland shipping in view of the expected growth of the container market requires not only 
compliance with the TEN -T requirements, but also a smart maintenance policy for bridges, locks and 
inland shipping terminals. The upcoming TEN -T revision should therefore focus on future -proof 
inland navigation by stimulating the adjustment of depth levels, the clearance levels for bridges 
and river management on European inland navigation.   
Currently there are no clear definitions of capacity bott lenecks defined for IWT, while at the same time 
the minimum standards for waterways and bridge heights are relatively low for the entire TEN -
T network.  Although this is understandable given the large degree of variation between European 
waterways, it is recommended to develop higher standards and requirements  than the current 
ones in a differentiated way for heavily navigated routes and waterways.  Inland waterway 
infrastructure needs performance upgrades to guarantee continuity and reliability towards its users and 
clients and to be able to compete with road transport . Furthermore, It is important that the TEN -T 
frameworks are in line with and support the low water measures of various Member States. This also 
applies to measures concerning the sustainability of the inland shipping fleet.  
Robust Rail freight transport  
Over 250 international rail connections run to and from Rotterdam. The transit times are short: 
cargo arrives at the German border within three hours and at other European destinations within 24 
hours. Current developments in central and eastern Europe make it possible to link Asian supply chains 
to manufacturing industry, distribution hubs, industrial clusters in Central and Western Europe, to Great 
Britain and Ireland in a robust and sustainable manner via Short Sea (ferry)connections.  Rotterdam's 
ambition is to increase the market share of railways in the modal distribution of the Port to 20% in 
2030.  A more ambitious approach is needed to make cross -border freight transport not only 
competitive, but also robust and reliable. It is vital for the TEN-T freight corridors to focus on improving 
the interconnection between the Belgian, Dutch and German networks.    
Sufficient competitive rail capacity benefits from better assurance of the quality and flexibility 
of freight train paths in the planning.  Not only does the intensification of (international) passenger 
transport give rise to this, also the diversion of trains from the dedicated and principal rail freight Betuwe 
Route to the mixed track, especially the so-called Brabantroute, should be taken into account because 
of construction work as is the co ming years  the case on the construction work on the Third Track  
between Emmerich and Oberhausen . The Rastatt incident (no Karlsruhe -Basel trains for 7 weeks, 
damage of € 2 billion) has highlighted how important it is that the rail system is robust and contingent 
across borders and can cope with emergencies.  We therefore argue for more high quality diversion 
routes. This also means that the earlier mentioned Brabantroute should be upgraded from diversion 
route to the core network of the Rhine-Alpine Corridor. 
Rail freight Corridors  
The Rail Freight Corridors (RFC) currently do not sufficiently focus on the predictability of investments 
and the coordination of investments between Member States, nor on the conditions for a stronger 
market orientation. There should be a better overview and supervision of the financial plans of 
the Member States. This also applies to the mutual coordination of maintenance schedules. This 
could be achieved by giving the governance structure of the RFCs more overarching mandate.  
740m train length  
High availability of the rail infrastructure is important in order to create a competitive rail product for 
ports. A performance indicator of an availability of the infrastructure  (excluding planned 
maintenance) of, for example, 99.5% would therefore be desirable. On top of that, the use of 740-meter 
trains, when carefully implemented, can make a significant contribution to the cost -effectiveness of the 
rail freight product. We therefore also call for capacity expansion on rail freight corridors by extendin g 
the length of used freight trains to 740 meters. This requires rail yards that are suitable for 740m meters. 
It should be noted that the rail connections in the ports do not always have enough space for this due 
to a lack of space. Current compliance standards unfortunately do not provide an accurate picture of 
the actual state of affairs and require amendment before the Rhine Alpine and North Sea Baltic rail 
freight corridors meet the required requirements. For example, despite promises and in contrast to 
the compliance view in the corridor work plans , there are still length restrictions on the Dutch-
German border sections and not all yards on the primary routes are sufficient.  Additionally it is 
important that diversion routes are also suitable for 740 meters, because otherwise the situation 
arises in which rail freight operators allow reliability of shorter trains to prevail over the 
possibility of using cost-efficient 740 meters trains. The overtaking tracks and sidings at the railway 
yard at Venlo are not yet sufficient on the important Brabant  (diversion) route. With regard to the 
Northsea Baltic corridor, the route via Oldenzaal / Bad Bentheim is not suitable for 740-meter trains. 
Inclusion of current market realities and future demands  
The above observations show that one of the weaknesses of the current T EN-T Regulation is the lack 
of participation of market parties and (their data and market realities) in the current corridor setting. 
Important information about current  and future market demands and therefore transport needs and 
considerations therefore are not structurally inc luded in the corridor work plans. For example, with 
regard to freight transport by inland shipping, due to future expected change of fossil fuel based 
cargo, the volume of containers is becoming more important, which at the same time means 
that the market and thus transport flows and the use of transport modalities are becoming more 
dynamic. It is therefore recommended to make the market studies process coordinated by designated 
consultants more transparent. 
With regard to rail freight transport, this means that not only the use of 740-meter trains (as mentioned 
above) depends on a more complex reality experienced by market parties based on reliability estimates, 
but that the actual elaboration and interpretat ion of ERTMS may also be different. With regard to the 
latter, the port would like to point out that currently insufficient attention is paid to 
interoperability problems that exist behind the current existing patchwork of security 
systems. In addition, if  we want to realistically anticipate the discussion on increasing 
international rail passenger transport, in order to promote the modal shift to the railways and to 
reduce road congestion and air traffic, the port advocates a higher priority for freight tr ains in 
capacity planning combined with the reinforcement of existing railway infrastructure.  
The Potential of Short Sea shipping  
European ports can serve as enablers of re - and near-shoring of production, and as mentioned above 
as enablers of new and more diversified supply chains and as locations for strategic reserves.  Short 
Sea shipping can support this development as flexible so lutions for the necessary new transport 
connections and as a resilient mode in times of border crossing restrictions. Facilitating Short Sea 
Shipping connections will therefore be instrumental for completing the TEN -T network and 
enhancing Europe’s connectivity in a sustainable way. In this regard, short sea shipping should 
be included alongside rail and inland waterway transport as a sustainable transport mode to 
reach modal shift objectives. This applies not only with regard to emissions, but also with regard to 
reducing road congestion.   
Currently, many shippers and forwarders  are not yet familiar with the possibilities and advantages of 
short sea shipping and the sector lacks marketing tools. Tools to provide insight into the benefits for 
market parties must be supported.  Motorways of the Sea (MoS) can develop into an important 
supporting instrument in this regard and should therefore receive a proper status in the revision. 
The strengthening of MoS links with third countries neighbouring the EU will become increasingly 
important in the framework of near-shoring and now that the UK has withdrawn from the EU. 
Smart Truck Technology 
Truck technology , and thus freight transport by road  is undergoing 3 simultaneous revolutions: 
connectivity, autonomy, and a shift towards electric and alternative-fuel propulsion. We estimate 
that all 3 revolutions, when successful, might significantly influence both legislation and practise in a 
radical way in favour of road use in the near future.  Ultimately c lean autonomous -trucks, driven 
overnight, may well be more efficient, cheaper and more sustainable  than their counterparts in the 
current situation. To make this possible, it is important to have supportive legal frameworks that 
anticipates local and cross-border barriers. In that sense we recommend investments in, and wider 
distribution of smart road infrastructures (control towers, and smart traffic lights, V2X) to facilitate the 
transition toward safe autonomous driving. We also urge for a more progressive legislation towards the 
testing and deployment of autonomous vehicles (especially trucks).   
Multimodality  
The presence of modern well -equipped inland terminals is essential in the relationship to multimodal 
transport. Various stakeholder working group evaluations that focused on the resilience and reliability 
of logistics chains in response to recent calamitie s on rail and inland waterways have shown that the 
extent to which inland terminals are able to replace between calamity modalities is crucial for 
the degree of robustness of the corridor supply chain. This requires increasing the trimodal 
infrastructure in a general sense. This could include specific requirements such as terminal capacity, 
740m compatible track, etc. which could lead to designation of important nodes.  
Alternative Fuels Infrastructure  
The deployment of alternative fuels and uptake of renewable energies that support the development of 
a clean and sustainable society and transport sector will contribute to the realisation of a carbon-neutral 
Europe by 2050. The TEN -T guidelines should recognise the changing role that ports can play as 
strategic energy nodes, particularly in terms of facilitating and accelerating the deployment of alternative 
fuels infrastructure. The port would like to use the link of Alternative fuel infrastructure directive 
revision (AFID) with TEN -T to argue in favor o f a holistic supply chain approach to alternative 
fuels. This means an inclusive approach that includes safety aspects, production, 
interoperability as well as the supply and demand side.  
TEN-E Synergies  
Industrial clusters like Rotterdam can play a cent ral role in the coordination and planning of smart 
integrated energy networks, especially with regard to hydrogen and CO2 . A general overarching 
barrier is (the lack of) the availability of energy infrastructure that strengthens the synergy 
between the clusters. Pipelines are used to connect industries in the port, to ensure the connection 
among industry clusters and users, even beyond the port area.  For example when it comes to open 
access (CO2 and hydrogen) infrastructure to the Ruhr area in Germany as well as Chemelot in the 
south-east of the Netherlands. Companies in those industrial clusters want to become sustainable. In 
the first stage in the short term, a good CO2 pipeline (discharge) is of great importance, but in the 
second phase they must also have access to renewable energy and raw materials.  
Pipelines can play a crucial role in the transport of certain commodities (such as chemicals and fuels). 
They are vital for the implementation of certain decarbonisation technologies (such as carbon capture 
and storage). Pipelines should thus not only be considered as an energy infrastructure but also 
as a sustainable mode of transport and should be part of the transport infrastructure network. 
The EU could urge Member States to ensure the spatial aspect of pipelines in the ir Member 
States from a NorthWest European perspective. Additionally, having in mind the use of hydrogen as 
a fuel for mobility purposes, synergies with the Trans -European Networks for Transport (TEN -T) 
should be established where possible. With both the HyTruck and Rh2ine consortium and the which 
are currently being formed, various parties (including production companies) want to make heavy road 
and inland waterway transport on the corridors between Rotterdam, Antwerp and Duisburg more 
sustainable, by developing the hydrogen supply (filling stations) as well as the delivery of hydrogen 
trucks and vessels . This enables synergies on delivery routes where transport and hydrogen 
pipelines run parallel to each other. 
Extension of the TEN-T to third countries  
With regard to the TEN-T policy in relation to neighbouring third countries, it is important that they find 
the best possible connection with the TEN -T corridors. Both in terms of interoperability standards, 
physical infrastructure and coordination of cross -border investments. The Port supports cooperation 
such  as the EU-China Connectivity platform. These partnerships can boost the development of the rail 
network in Eastern Europe. The EU institutions should insist that the European regulatory and technical 
framework is promoted. Specifically, EU policy makers should ensure that any new rail corridor 
terminal is well connected with existing rail freight corridors.  Here connections with ports are of 
key value, as ports can form a link in the transport chain on train connections from China through Short 
Sea Ro-Ro connections to the United Kingdom and Ireland.   
While these partnerships can generate synergies and better transport(infrastructure)connections, the 
Port points to the importance of cooperation in an open and transparent manner with regard to 
procurement conditions and sustainability standards. In other words, the exercise should not 
undermine the TEN-T network, its priorities and features, which should remain the cornerstone 
of Europe’s Transport Infrastructure policy . Th is policy should not be determined by outside 
players.  
Financing  
European financing programs are ideally suited to scale up demonstration projects to the level at which 
they generate international impact on Ten-T corridors. The transition from the pilot phase to deployment 
for energy transition and  sustainable transport solutions i s the added value that CEF (as a TEN -T 
supporting program) must focus on in comparison with existing (national) financing instruments. In the 
current situation, CEF calls handle only one aspect per call. This means that combined projects , 
involving both the charging, energy infrastructure and the associated vessel will not develop, while those 
projects that would already be implemented do receive support. The Port therefore advocates for 
integrated financial instruments that include both mobile transport equipment and transport 
infrastructure on one corridor at the same time.  In particular when it comes to bridging the 
additional costs in the start -up phase of new sustainable means of transport.  In addition, it is  
important that there are European measures and financing options that are in line with the development 
of (announced) low-water measures in certain corridors intensively used by inland waterways.

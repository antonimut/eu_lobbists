 
Trans-European transport network (TEN-T) - revised guidelines 
An opportunity to increase modal shift and achieve 
the 55% reduction in GHG emissions 
The Trans-European Network for Transport (TEN-T) stands as a key European initiative to tackle the 
challenges posed by current environmental and recovery challenges. The transport sector is at the 
heart of the transitions and crises to which the European Union must respond. Environmentally, the 
transport sector accounts for 30% of GHG emissions in Europe and is the only sector that has not 
reduced its emissions since 1990 with an 18% increase since 1990. Mobility means freedom of 
movement, which is one foun dation of the European project but also a major lever to meet the 
challenges of equity and social inclusion. On the other hand, worker mobility is key to our continent's 
economic dynamism and must be a clear concern of the European Union. The social and ec onomic 
cost of urban congestion in Europe is estimated at 270 billion euros per year.  
Review of the Regulation on Union Guidelines for the development of the trans -European transport 
network stands as a true opportunity to make adaptations to the reality of today’s mobility and to 
enshrine the ambitions of the Green Deal into concrete projects for greener and smarter mobility in 
Europe.  
Modal shift: an essential tool for reducing emissions of transport 
sector in the European Union 
Modal shift is a key ele ment of any transport policy that aims to improve the sustainability of 
transport, contributing to a better use of existing capacity and to an increase of the efficiency of the 
transport system. Between 1996 and 2016, modal shift from passenger cars to mor e 
sustainable transport modes has been slow. In 2016, passenger cars accounted for 71% 
of passenger transport measured in passenger.km , representing only a very thin decrease 
compared to their modal share in 1996 (73,2%). Over the same period, the modal share of rail and 
public transport has slowly evolved1.  
Achieving the objective of a 55% reduction in emissions by 2030 implies reducing either 
the distances travelled or unit emissions or increasing the modal shift so that more people 
can be transported by vehicle. This will not be possible with the sole switch to electric vehicles . 
If we manage to reduce unit emissions by 20% by 2030, which seems optimistic given the  current 
market share of the electric vehicle, modal shift will have to be re sponsible for around 56% 
of the emissions reduction: this step is very important if we want to be ready in time for 2030.  
At a time where climate issues and impacts are becoming more pressing every day, the period ahead 
will require a vigorous orientation  of the European Union’s actions for the transport sector towards 
modal shift. COVID-19 crisis is undermining the essential tool for addressing this challenge : public 
transport. Beyond the heavy financial losses for the sector2, the crisis already suggests new dynamics 
and transformations that will impact mobility:  increased use of private cars, escape from urban 
centers, an increase in distances travelled and therefore in emissions. Reviewing the main European 
tool to develop strategic sustainable mobility  solutions seems crucial to address these trends. The 
future orientations of the TEN -T should clearly promote effective modal shift towards sustainable 
shared mobility solutions in its strategic hubs to ensure a coherent framework for European actors. 
This revision is an essential opportunity to avoid the simple back to “business as usual" 
and really address the issue of the ecological transition of mobilities and reverse the 
emissions curve in the transport sector.

Urban nodes are key to tackle modal shift challenge and address 
transport decarbonization   
74% of the EU population is living in cities3. The increase of urban population and of the economic 
role of these areas have had a significant impact on urban mobility and its share of emissions. Out 
of the total emissions from transport, 23% concern urban areas 4. Yet, a recent report from the  
European Court of Auditor has shown that in the last budgetary period, the amount of funding for 
urban nodes represented 1% of the total of the total funding of the Connecting Europe Facility 
programme. In order to address modal shift in a coherent manner, urban nodes should represent at 
least 20% of the CEF grants.   
Most of the mobility emissions in urban areas come from private car use. Yet car use emissions are 
very low in city centers and are mainly focused in peripheries and in their links with the city centers. 
It is therefore in these areas that the main challenges related to the decarbonization of mobility in 
Europe are polarized. Thus, urban nodes are key areas to ensure that citizens have sustainable 
solutions to acc ess jobs in cities in order to curb GHG emissions, ensure air quality and preserve 
public spaces in city centers which are part of our common goods. 
Focus should be on facilitating multimodal access to, and connectivity between, urban nodes, 
together with the efficient connection of cities to airports in order to integrate long - and short -
distance transport.    
Need for a comprehensive development of urban nodes  
Urban areas are where modal shift is reachable. There are many measures that potentially contribute 
to modal shift in urban areas, including the provision of infrastructure for alternative modes, the 
implementation of shared mobility and intelligent transport systems, vehicle access restrictions and 
the integration of ticketing, payment and information for public transport. 
To ensure their sound and coherent development, a more explicit definition of urban nodes  is 
required to allow them to be more effective in European transport corridors, regional 
development, and inclusive ecological transition. A clear definition of urban nodes which would 
include a comprehensive set of infrastructures allowing the promotion of clean and smart mobility 
would ensure the bolstering of urban nodes towards the objectives set by the TEN -T, i.e. improving 
the use of infrastructure and reducing the environmental impact of transport. This definition should 
include the periphery of identified urban nodes as these areas are particularly affected by the 
challenges related to the decarbonization of mobility as wel l as that of access to effective mobility 
solutions.  
For instance, t he EU -OECD recently proposed a definition of a functional urban area (FUA) 
encompassing the economic and functional extent of cities based on daily people’s movements 5. A 
certain threshol d of population could be determined to include more urban nodes in the TEN -T 
Regulation (for example, all FUA of more than 300 000 inhabitants). 
In keeping with the ambitions of the Green Deal, development of urban nodes should ensure the 
modal shift towards shared mobility services.

To encourage a modal shift from private cars, it is crucial for the alternative modes to be competitive 
in terms of time, convenience, reliability and price. Fast and reliable public transpo rt like bus rapid 
transit systems, tramways, with priority lanes and separated tracks, can be attractive alternative to 
conventional cars. The introduction of shared spaces designed for pedestrians and cyclists, alongside 
measures to reduce the dominance of cars, can also be interesting options.  
Innovation should remain a core component of the TEN-T and should be a lever to increase the modal 
shift. It is also important to combine these measures in a multimodal perspective to facilitate the use 
of public transport at interchanges facilities (train stations or airports where feasible) allowing users 
to travel seamlessly between transport modes and plan efficiently their journey. The potential for 
social, environmental and economic benefits of multimodal mobility via, continuous access to travel 
information, planning and mobility services, as well as ticketing integration and payment, could be 
significant. It is crucial to facilitate the use of public transport at multimodal hubs allowing users to 
travel seamlessly between transport modes and plan efficiently their journey.  
Furthermore, the TEN -T should focus on supporting shared innovative mobility solutions, such as 
shared autonomous services, to ensure a fair access to innovative solutions for citizens as well as 
ensuring a strong modal shift.  
2. Accelerate the energy transition of public transport in urban nodes 
The review of the guidelines on the TEN -T should keep the overarching objective of developing a 
low-carbon transport system. Public transport is playing a key role contributing to reduce congestion 
and GHG emissions thanks to modal shift to lower emissions modes. Beyond the modal shift, public 
transport operators must contribute to developing the sustainable forms of mobility of tomorrow with 
alternative energies that will make it pos sible to absorb and distribute increasing flows, while 
reducing their carbon footprint.  
Hence, the TEN-T should be at the forefront of supporting the development of zero and low-emission 
public transport solutions for European citizens, including for inst ance alternative fuels like biogas, 
with the comprehensive development of alternative fuel refueling infrastructures for public transport 
fleets.

PORT OF GALWAY POSITION ON THE TEN-T REVISION 
The Port of Galway (“the Port”) is located in the largest city of the Northwestern region of 
Ireland and has the ambition to expand its business and redevelop the inner port area, 
thereby stimulating commercial activities and the development of the inner-city lands. The 
Port has the ambition to be included in the Trans European Transport Network (“TEN-T”) 
comprehensive network and form a strategic link in the North Sea Mediterranean Corridor  
(“NSMED”). In addition, its inclusion in the comprehensive network will have a significant 
impact on its expansion plans.  
The Port welcomes the opportunity to contribute to the revision of the TEN -T Regulation 
(2013/1315). The revision of the TEN-T guidelines must be used as an opportunity to update 
the TEN -T framework and subsequent requirements for maritime ports and to allow 
additional beneficiaries to be included in TEN -T to close the missing links and to meet 
evolving environmental, economic and societal n eeds. The Port believes it has two unique 
propositions which would contribute to the TEN-T objectives. 
1. Importance of connectivity, accessibility and economic development 
The Port is a strategic node which facilitates  the connectivity and accessibility of remote 
and peripheral regions in Northwestern Ireland. It is the last port of call for the peripheral 
Northwestern region, serving more than 1,000 km of coastline. In doing so, the Port connects 
remote areas to the economic and industrial growth areas on the mainland , thereby 
stimulating their economic potential. In addition, the North & Western region in Ireland, in 
which Galway is located, is the only NUTS2 region on Europe’s Atlantic coast without a 
maritime seaport in TEN -T and one of t he very few NUTS 2 regions in Europ e without a 
connection to the maritime TEN-T network. The current criteria for maritime ports to be 
included on the comprehensive TEN -T network, as defined in Art. 20 of the TEN -T 
Regulation, do not capture the geographical realities in peripheral regions of the EU , 
including the West of Ireland, and should therefore be updated. 
As a result of the COVID-19 outbreak, we have seen disrupted trade flows across the globe. 
The global pandemic demonstrated the critical  role of port infrastructure and well -
functioning port operations in ensuring the supply of goods and materials across Europe. This 
is especially relevant for Irish ports due to their strategic location and becomes even more 
relevant in view of Brexit. As such, the TEN-T policy should reflect the importance of 
maritime ports in the comprehensive network , and not only the core network , in the 
recovery of the European economy, as ports are the backbone of the wider supply chain. 
Furthermore, as also highlighted by the parliamentary committee for Transport & Tourism 
(TRAN) in its own-initiative report on TEN-T, Brexit has a major impact on the accessibility, 
connectivity and economic cohesion between Ireland and continental Europe. Irish ports will 
have to accommodate a shift of trade flows to maritime routes . Inefficiencies in ports can 
have considerable knock -on effects on the economy and wider supply chain . The 
peripherality of Ireland should not become an obstacle and should not lead to a lack of 
competitiveness. The TEN -T policy should  therefore acknowledge the growing 
importance of Irish ports and ensure the effective connection of Ireland to continental

2. Enable the uptake of renewable energies 
The deployment of alternative fuels and uptake of renewable energies that support the 
development of clean and sustainable transport will contribute to the realisation of a carbon-
neutral Europe by 2050. According to the Irish Climate Action Plan 2019 (“CAP2019”), Ireland 
is strategically located to take advantage of at least 70 GW of ocean energy opportunity 
(wind, wave and tidal) within 100 km of the Irish coast and become a major contributor to 
the EU’s electricity generation. In the CAP 2019, the Irish government outlined a target to 
increase electricity generated from renewable sources to 70% by 2030.  The Northwestern 
region in Ireland has been identified by the Irish government as a key region to stimulate 
the development of renewable energies  and exploit a potential of at least 30 GW of 
offshore floating wind power in the Atlantic.  
It is clear, based on the recently published EU Offshore Renewable Energy Strategy as well 
as research commissioned to date, that ports will play a vital role in the deployment of 
floating wind installations off Europe’s Atlantic coast. The Port is well situated to facilitate 
the deployment and service of floating wind off Ireland’s Atlantic coast. In doing so, it would 
not only serve domestic energy needs, but also export this new source of energy to 
Continental Europe.  In addition to the offshore opportunity, the Irish government has 
identified that the deployment of onshore wind should increase from approximately 3.8 GW 
to 8.2 GW over the next 10 years. The Port has commissioned research which suggests that 
approximately 50% of the proposed new deployment could be routed through the Port. 
The TEN-T policy plays a crucial role in providing adequate infrastructure that boosts access 
to renewable energies  and to enable  ports to capitali se on the onshore  and offshore 
opportunities. In line with the EU’s Offshore Renewable Energy Strategy, the TEN -T 
guidelines should stimulate the expansion  of ports that are well situated to serve the 
deployment of floating offshore wind  and recognise ports as strategic energy nodes in 
decarbonising the European economy. 
In conclusion, the Port is an engine of Ireland’s future economic prosperity , a driver o f 
regional growth and a significant enabler of the European Green Deal. The Port believes the 
TEN-T guidelines should recognise the role of ports as strategic partners in responding to 
today’s key challenges and meet evolving societal, environmental and economic needs such 
as the decarbonisation of transport and the transition to a carbon-neutral continent by 2050.

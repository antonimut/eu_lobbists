Question 2.a: 
Changes in traffic flows and the necessity to include peripheral regions will bring the need for 
necessary adjustments to the network. We believe that further congruent alignments between 
CNCs and RFCs are needed wherever possible, taking into account that different routings in 
RFCs are unavoidable. 
In addition, important urban nodes, terminals and routes are missing in the CNC and they must 
be included and adjusted in the network. Rail is still missing a significant number of important 
last mile infrastructure and multimodal connections for freight and passengers. This is 
especially the case for rail freight where the origin and destination are rarely found on the core 
network. The TEN -T Regulation needs to address these issues by ensuring sufficient 
intermodal hubs and access points to conventional rail freight on corridors to allow long -
distance transport via the most efficient mode. 
The network design has to take account of changing transport flows within the EU, but also to 
transport flows with third countries like China. The amount of goods transported between 
Europe and Asia, especially linked to the belt and road initiative is increasing. The 
Mediterranean ports are the gateway of the Maritime Silk Road to Europe. However, the rail 
connection to the Balkans is insufficient, and makes rail transport within Europe difficult.  
Especially to ports such as Piraeus. Extending the TEN-T Core Network to the Balkans (adding 
RFC 10 with its Pyhrn -Tauern routes) helps to make rail freight transport in the Balkan area 
more reliable. It also forms the basis for co -funding projects through the Connecting Europe 
Facility (CEF), which is a viable alternative to infrastruc ture investments from third countries 
that do not comply with EU standards. Relieving congestion in ports is also important, as the 
amount of transported goods increases. Making the Villach-Fürnitz terminal (AT) a Core Node 
in the TEN-T Core Network promotes its position as a hinterland port (dry port). Thanks to its 
strategic location on the Baltic -Adriatic axis, it has the potential to become a logistics hub in 
the region and help to relieve the port of Trieste. 
 
Question 3.a: 
• High-speed rail plays a crucial role in the transition towards sustainable transport by 
offering a viable replacement for short and medium haul flights and long car journeys 
on a wide number of ori gin-destination pairs. The European high -speed rail network 
must be made more coherent and interlinked if the EU is to realize the EUSSMS targets 
of doubling and tripling the high speed network by 2030 and 2050 respectively. The 
creation of a European high -speed network that is interoperable, linking European 
capitals and major cities, connecting urban nodes and airports and supporting the 
development of an international passenger service market must be one of the priorities 
in the TEN-T policy.

• Wishes concerning the closer link between RFCs and CNCs have been heard. The 
parallel revision of TEN-T and RFCs Regulation is welcomed. In this regard, we would 
like to point out that provisions should be included in the revised TEN -T Regulation, 
enabling the RFC s to provide input into the development of the CNC work plan, 
including their investment needs for all core lines and core terminals designated to a 
RFC. Similarly, the views and investment needs of the passenger market should have 
an influence too. That being said, the provisions concerning the relation between RFCs 
and CNCs should not undermine the CNCs’ main responsibility for drafting the 
investment plans, and should in no way infringe on the roles and competencies of the 
RFCs. In addition to ensuring the input of market participants in investment decisions, 
projects on a corridor should be embedded in a cross-border plan or corridor approach 
rather than being considered and evaluated individually. This is because the full impact 
of infrastructure projec ts becomes particularly evident in a cross -border and more 
network-oriented context. 
• The decarbonization objectives and other environment-related challenges as set out in 
the European Green Deal necessitate changes in TEN-T policy. Due to the high degree 
of electrification, railways, as the only green mode of transport, are already able to offer 
zero-carbon train operations (80% of the traction is electric) in Europe thus making 
railways key to EU transport decarbonization and maximizing their contributio n to the 
EU Green Deal. It is therefore important to include railways decarbonization efforts for 
energy system integration and hydrogen in the Commission’s revision for ensuring 
coverage of alternative fuels. In addition, further electrification and upgrades of the rail 
network (regional as well as main lines), must be ensured, as electrified railway 
transport is by far the most efficient form of e-mobility. Rail freight needs to be boosted 
notably in view of its resilience during the COVID-19 pandemic times. 
• What is missing in the above statements in the questionnaire is the technical 
requirements for rail. It is important to create a common understanding of the 
infrastructure criteria for the TEN -T Core Network for smooth international rail traffic. 
The existing criteria should not be softened, but clarified and interpreted. 
 
Question 4.a: 
A common understanding of the criteria for the Core Network laid down in Regulation 
1315/2013 is crucial for rail interoperability across Europe. Existing TEN -T Core Network 
criteria should under no circumstances be softened, if the quality of rail transpo rt is to be 
maintained. On the contrary, they should be interpreted and clarified. Examples include: 
"enabling trains of 740 meters in length" (does this mean 5 trains of 740 meters per day, or 1 
train of this length at night?), or the definition of 100km/ h (under what conditions would it be 
acceptable to temporarily go below this speed?).

Question 5.a: 
We agree that the role of the European Coordinators need to be strengthened, however their 
roles and responsibilities must be clearly defined and their work transparent and non -
discriminatory. 
The implementation of cross-border infrastructure projects by member states must be sped up 
so that they generate their intended added value. This also includes important access routes, 
without which it is not possible to achieve the intended added value of projects. Cross-border 
approval procedures further accelerate project implementation. In this context, the EC should 
provide more incentives in Regulation 1315/2013 so that member states finish projects on time, 
make use of thei r tools to speed up implementation, or get stronger tools by revising 
1315/2013. Although the TEN -T Regulation addresses challenges such as bottlenecks and 
missing links, the implementation of new projects is often problematic in practice. 
 
Question 7.b: 
• Security/public order: as a first step, we need an EU infrastructure policy that defines 
and develops strategic infrastructures and protects them from external control. 
However, the screening mechanism for foreign direct investment created at the EU 
level should be applied with caution to ensure that Europe remains open for FDI.  
• Public order: Consistent application of quality criteria (not only price) in public 
procurement for infrastructure projects in order to prevent dumping offers from 
companies from EU third cou ntries. Art. 85 of the EU Directive 2014/25 should be 
consistently implemented in all EU states. 
 
Focus area 4: 
A combination of the aforementioned focus areas will help the TEN -T Policy achieve its 
objectives to decarbonizing transport, close infrastruct ure gaps, remove bottlenecks and 
technical barriers, as well as to strengthen social, economic and territorial cohesion in the EU 
and make transport more resilient. While focus on the traditional physical infrastructure will 
help indeed completing the CNCs and the Comprehensive Network, a balance must be made 
with also the soft components of the infrastructure. The focus on soft components of the 
infrastructure will also help to achieve the objectives of the TEN -T policy, increase capacity 
and better interc onnect intermodal hubs and transport nodes. Both traditional, physical 
approach and digitalization scenario are of the same importance and one is closely correlated 
with another. The focus area 2 must be also combined with 1 and 3, because it is crucial 
nowadays to enhance resilience of the network infrastructure, notably in relation to climate 
adaptation, security and civil protection needs.

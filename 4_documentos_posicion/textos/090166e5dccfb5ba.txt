SNCF reaction to the TEN-T public consultation 
 
SNCF welcomes the opportunity to share its view on the revision of the TEN-T regulation.  
The TEN-T network is a key pillar of the modernization of the European rail network. We believe it will 
represent one of the pillars of the Green Deal, in particular with its objective of reducing CO2 emissions 
in the transport sector by 90% by 2050.  It w ill also be the key to achieve the ambitions of the 
“Sustainable and smart mobility strategy” and its targets to double high speed rail traffic by 2030 and 
the increase by 50% of rail freight traffic by 2030 and by 100% in 2050.  
Regarding the questionnaire, there are several topics will would like to clarify:  
   
The network requirements and the deployment targets are sufficient  
The implementation of the current requirements for the core and comprehensive networks and their 
deadlines are already an important milestone to reach today. The effort should focus on the 
completion of the network  with sufficient funding to help the sector to reach the targets as soon as 
possible.  
It is important to secure and establish  a long -term vision of the necessary investments and the 
technical requirements to be put in place  in the network . Consistency must be the key word in the 
implementation of the obligations imposed under the TEN -T regulation. These obligations will allow 
each of the stakeholders to organize themselves and to be assured of the relevance of their 
investments in the TEN-T. The TEN-T regulation should require national investment and funding plans 
in order to boost the implementation of the n ecessary works in the network and its necessary 
investments.  
As we can see for the deployment of ERTMS, t he budget allocated is already insufficient to cover all 
the needs up to 2030 (for the core network) and 2050 (for the comprehensive network).  Moving 
forward the completion of the implementation of the comprehensive network for ERTMS in 2040 
seems a little bit too ambitious as the level of the next CEF remains as it is. We believe that the budget 
allocated to ERTMS in the next CEF and the co-financing rates should be much larger if the objectives 
set by Europe must be met.  
Moreover, the implementation process for these kinds of proje cts requires a long period of time 
(between 10 and 15 years). Shortening the implementation deadline for the comprehensive network 
will only constrict into a shorter period all the works necessary for the implementation of the TEN -T 
network. The risk is to  create a potential bottleneck of works, and difficulties to manage the 
infrastructure.  
Similarly, there is no need to add additional requirements for the network. A loading gauge parameter 
cannot not be an additional requirement on the TEN-T network to be imposed on the whole network. 
As we already stated, the level of funding is already insufficient and adding a new requirement will 
divert a part of the current funding in this new requirement.  We believe that the regulation can 
accompany the introduction of a specific loading gauge on part of the TEN-T when necessary. An extra 
and optional loading gauge criterion, where it is economically viable and relevant, and also technically 
possible, could accompany the development of certain rail traffic.

A better coherence between RFC & TEN-T is needed 
We believe that the CNCs and the RFCs should be c omplementarity and their roles should be clearly 
defined in the future.  There is a need to adjust the roles and obligations of each regulation in addition 
to the adjustment of the network. This should be done without unintended consequences in terms of 
investment. 
The link between the TEN-T and the RFC regulations should be better reflected in the future revision  
of both texts . Cooperation needs to be improved b y defining clear roles and competences. Core 
network corridors have not achieved the objective of synchronizing investments along the core 
network. The governance structures of the core network corridors  (for instance the role of CNCs 
coordinators, the CNCs fora …) are not always aligned with the obligations of the RFC regulation. It is 
necessary, for a better efficiency, to avoid duplication of governance structures and to give clear and 
distinct competences. Core network corridors should oversee investmen t-related issues and leave 
capacity and path issues to the RFCs. The RFCs can help in the identification of investment priorities 
according to market needs. The revision of the TEN -T Regulation should contain provisions indicating 
that RFCs can indicate their needs to the CNC Workplan. 
The role of the CNC Coordinators also needs to be strengthened with focus on RFCs. The Coordinators 
could play a n important role in coordinating investments in the corridors and especially on cross -
border sections. The link with the “customer side” of the RFCs will also help the CNC’s coordinator to 
better coordinate the works on the corridors.   
In the end, in order to achieve the targets of the TEN -T regulation, Member States should be made 
more responsible for the completi on of the TEN -T network, with the objective of ensuring the 
completion of projects of strategic importance. The CNC Coordinators could help the Member States 
in this regard. Indeed, they could help the Member States to design implementation plans or funding 
plans to boost the completion of the TEN-T network in 2030 and 2050.

3rd EU Driving Licence Directive 
road map consultation 
may 2021 
 
 
The European Commission has set itself the objective of improving road safety and the free movement 
of persons in the European Union with the Third EU Driving Licence Directive. The Commission 
launched a road map consultation  in May 2021  to inform about the initiative and to give stakeholders 
the opportunity to provide feedback . In January 2021 , the bdo submitted a position paper (attached) 
with the following demands: 
 
- Increasing the passenger capacity for the D1 licence from the current maximum of 16 to a 
maximum of 22 passengers. 
- The driving licence classes should adequately reflect the vehicle classes available on the 
market. For this purpose, the vehicle classifications may have to be adjusted.  
- Adjustment of the minimum age for the D and DE driving licence to allow trainees to accompany 
the main driver on cross-border tourist journeys and to drive in the domestic section of the route. 
- The acquisition of driving licence class C should also include class D1 and the acquisition of 
driving licence class D should also include class C1. 
 
In addition to its position paper, the bdo  has the following comments on the objectives, analyses and 
intentions of the European Commission based on the background paper of the road map consultation: 
 
- Risk awareness and safe driving can only be ensured through good training and experience. 
Trainees must therefore be given the opportunity to drive buses accompanied at an early age. 
Therefore, the following adjustments are necessary: 
o the minimum age for the D and DE driving licence as well as for motor vehicle 
mechatronics technicians specialising in  commercial vehicles in Europe must be 
lowered to 18 years. 
o Adjustment of the vehicle classes for accompanied driving of young drivers, for example 
with gradual access to higher vehicle classes. 
- Uniform EU-wide standards for the recognition and transcription of driving licences to make it 
easier for professional drivers to make use of the free movement of persons and reduce 
obstacles and disadvantages.  Minimum standards must ensure that drivers do  not suffer any 
disadvantages in their driving qualifications when they move. 
- New vehicle technologies, e.g. autonomous driving, need to be incorporated in training and 
further education. 
 
 
bdo, may 2021 (PO)

 position paper      
 
 
 Bundesverband Deutscher Omnibusunternehmer e.V. 
 
of the German Bus and Coach Operators Association (bdo) on the 
revision of the 3rd EU Driving Licence Directive 
 
The German Bus and Coach Operators Association is the head organisation of the private German bus 
industry. It represents at national and international level the trade policy and professional interests of 
around 3,000 bus operators who are involved in local public transport, bus tourism and long -distance 
bus services and who have joined forces under the umbrella of the bdo.   
 
The challenge to society as a whole of the transport turnaround also poses great challenges for the 
private bus industry in Germany. As a vital component of multimodal transport systems, the already very 
good mobility offer "bus" must be further optimised and expanded so that more people can be convinced 
of this alternative to motorised private transport. 
The forms of transport, also within the "bus" module, will change in the coming years and decades. For 
example, hardly anyone can predict today which vessel sizes will prevail in the future - especially in 
sparsely populated rural areas. Private bus companies must be able to react flexibly to future 
developments. 
At the same time, the search for professional drivers already represents a "Herculean task" for the 
industry. 
The costs of obtaining a driving licence for professional drivers are very high in Germany. This 
circumstance is a further obstacle to the recruitment of newcomers to the profession, since small and 
medium-sized enterprises must of course also make economic calculations at this point.  
Against this background, the industry cannot afford that a certain driving licence category, namely the 
D1 driving licence - the "small bus driving licence" - is in danger of "running out" due to the lack of 
corresponding vehicles available on the market. 
We are of the opinion that important legal framework conditions for the bus industry must be designed 
and, if necessary, updated in su ch a way that they also correspond to entrepreneurial reality. We 
therefore welcome the fact that the Federal Ministry of Transport and Digital Infrastructure would like to 
discuss possible changes with stakeholders on the occasion of the evaluation of the  3rd EU Driving 
Licence Directive 2006/EC/126 EC announced by the EU Commission and thank for the inclusion oft 
the Bundesverband Deutscher Omnibusunternehmer e.V.. 
  
Initial situation 
Among other things, Directive 2006/126/EC raised the minimum age for the D and DE bus driving licence 
to 24 years of age and also regulated the D1 driving licence. 
The private bus industry in Germany is already suffering from a clearly perceptible shortage of drivers. 
This trend will intensify massively in the coming years. This makes it particularly necessary to make the 
legal regulations on driving licences practical in order to attract new professional drivers to the labour 
market and to be able to use them sensibly in our member companies, most of which are medium-sized 
and family-run.

For some time now, the private bus industry has seen a need for action regarding the regulations for the 
D1 driving licence. 
We also see a need for change in the minimum age for the D or DE driving licence.  
In detail: 
 
D1 driving licence 
 
The directive stipulates that the D1 licence may carry a maximum of 16 passengers, not including the 
driver. With this specification for the maximum number of passengers, however, the D1 driving licence 
is in danger of becoming practically meaningless. This  is because in this so -called "sprinter class" the 
trend has been towards higher passenger numbers, often 19. This means that vehicles in this class with 
16 passengers are hardly offered on the vehicle market any more. 
One of the reasons for this is that the current regulations for driving licence class D1 do not correspond 
to the type approval of the vehicles according to Art. 3 No. 2 and 3 of Regulation (EU) No. 661/2009, 
which sets the limit at 22 passengers. 
In this respect, we suggest increasing the number of passenger places from the current maximum of 16 
to a maximum of 22 for the D1 driving licence. 
 
Negative effects on road safety are not apparent. As far as we know, there are no conspicuous features 
in the accident statistics that would justify concerns.  
In the basically identical vehicles, only one or two additional rows of seats will be installed.  
 
In the future - especially against the background of the currently much -discussed change in transport 
and mobility - smaller passenger transport  vehicles will also gain in importance, especially to ensure 
mobility in rural areas with a changing age structure of the population.  
Also against the background of the acute shortage of drivers, it seems necessary to be able to transport 
up to 22 passengers with the "small" bus driver's licence. 
 
In our opinion, the structuring of the driving licence classes should adequately reflect the available 
vehicle classes. 
 
 
Minimum age D and DE driving licence 
 
With regard to the regulations on the minimum age for D and DE driving licences, tourist -oriented bus 
companies are faced with the problem that trainee drivers can only be employed after they have 
completed the basic qualification according to § 4 para. 1 BKrFQG or after they have reached the age 
of 21 (cf. § 10 para. 1 no. 9 FeV). 
As a result, there is hardly any incentive for coach companies to employ trainees.  
 
Trainees could be allowed to start cross -border tourist journeys in such a way that they "acc ompany" 
the "main driver" with an inserted driver card abroad as part of a double driver crew (here they cannot 
drive due to the lack of entry of the code number 95), in order to then take over the wheel again on the 
domestic section of the route.

Minimum age for training as a motor vehicle mechatronics technician specialising in commercial 
vehicles (bus) 
 
If a class C driving licence is to be acquired as part of the training to become a motor vehicle 
mechatronics technician with a focus on commercial vehicle technology, because the acquisition of this 
driving licence appears to be sensible in order to gain full professional capacity to act, empty runs with 
buses for the purpose of a driving test are not permitted until the age of 21. 
The acquisition of dr iving licence class C in the context of training as a motor vehicle mechatronics 
technician (specialising in commercial vehicle technology) is also not uncommon among bus 
companies.

According to this, the minimum age for driving class C vehicles in Germany is 18 years and for class D

Thus, it is not possible to technically inspect buses on the road before reaching the minimum age of 21

obtained a class C driving licence may already carry out such journeys with a truck.  
 
 
Breaking the strict separation of C and D driving licences 
 
EU regulations separate the driving licence categories C and D from each other. 
 
Since there is no apparent reason why a class D driver's licence holder, with the authorisation to drive 
articulated or double-decker buses, should not also be able to drive a 5t snow removal vehicle in front 
of the depot, we suggest that  the acquisition of the driving licence class C should also include D1 and 
the D driving licence at the same time C1.

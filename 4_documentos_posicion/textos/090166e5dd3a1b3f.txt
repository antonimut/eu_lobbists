With a rapidly ageing workforce, a 
lack of young drivers is one of the 
main challenges facing the European 
road transport industry in both the 
freight and passenger sectors, in 
part due to minimum age rules for 
professional drivers.
The different regulations across 
Europe create restrictions and reduce 
opportunities for young people to 
enter the profession, at a time when 
the youth unemployment rate is 
over 16% in the EU and is set to 
increase due to COVID-19 (corona-
generation). The upcoming revision of 
the EU directive on driving licences is 
an opportunity to resolve this issue.

No gap between school and 
the wheel: allow young people 
to start their driver training as 
soon as they finish school.
The profession is not on 
young people’s radar: only 
2% of drivers are under 
30 years old.6 
Safety has nothing 
to do with age
• A comparison of the average 
accident rate of professional 
drivers shows that the age of 
entry into the profession does not 
influence safety performance.
• In fact, the accident rate is higher 
in countries where the minimum 
age is 21 than in those where the 
minimum age is 18.1
• Almost all accidents involving 
young drivers are caused by 
non-professional drivers, as 
shown in the graph below.
Combat youth unemployment
• In Europe, 23% of truck driver 
positions and 19% of bus and 
coach driver positions remain 
unfilled.2 Facilitating young 
people’s access to the profession 
could help to meet this demand 
and reduce youth unemployment 
in the EU, which is currently 
at 16.4%3 and is expected to 
increase to 17% in 2021.4 (This 
figure does not take into account 
the impact of COVID-19).
• The road transport industry 
can be a major job engine: the 
European Commission estimates 
that increasing the modal share 
of coach transport in the EU by 
one percentage point would 
create 85,000 new jobs by 2030.5 
Maintain service offering
• A succession planning disaster: 
ensure continuity for an ageing 
profession. In 2019, the average 
age of European bus drivers was 
52 and the average age of truck 
drivers was 46.

Harmonising the minimum age of professional drivers in Europe
The demand for workers in the road transport sector will 
continue to grow in the coming years, creating even more 
jobs. Harmonising the minimum age for professional drivers 
will enable young people to access these opportunities 
and diversify the talent pool of professional drivers. 
Support the European economy and youth employment 
by supporting harmonisation of the minimum age.
How can public authorities 
support young drivers’ 
entry to the profession? 
• Safety is a matter of training, 
not age: establish national funds 
to finance driver training. The 
average cost of initial driver 
training is around EUR 3,700. 
• Communicate the benefits: 
professional driving offers very 
competitive conditions compared 
to other professions with similar 
qualification requirements. 
• Catch talent early: run 
awareness-raising campaigns 
for students that target school 
curriculums and career 
programmes. Present the 
career opportunities available 
in the road transport industry 
and the opportunities to gain 
new digital skills as vehicles 
are increasingly automated.

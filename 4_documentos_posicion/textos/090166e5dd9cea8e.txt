Feedback on the EC Inception Impact Assessment on Driving Licences 
 
The Irish Road Haulage Association welcomes the future revision of the directive as it is necessary to adapt 
the EU legislation on driving licences to the challenges of the 2020s including to improve road safety; and to 
facilitate the free movement of persons in the European Union.  
Issues of particular concern for the road haulage sector are both youth unemployment and driver shortages. 
In the EU-27 for 2019, there were 2.8 million unemployed persons aged between 15 and 24. This number 
is expected to rise following the COVID-19 health crisis. Meanwhile all sectors of road transport are affected 
by a shortage of new driver recruits  and with a high average age for active drivers as Driver crisis looms 
large. 
In October 2020, the International Road Union (IRU) published a position paper on key barriers to professional 
driver training and employment. The IRHA strongly supports the paper and inspired part of it. The main actions 
recommended by the IRU are: 
▪ Reduce the minimum age for trained professional road freight transport drivers to 17 years old. 
▪ Adopt an EU list of third countries meeting EU professional driver training standards.  
▪ Create a genuine D1 driving licence allowing drivers to drive passenger transport vehicles of 21+1 
seats (currently 16+1). 
▪ Allow road transport companies to access official databases covering information on temporary or 
definitive withdrawal of professional driving licences.  
▪ Allow 17-year-olds to start professional truck driver training, so that they can take t he exam 
and begin driving as soon as they turn 18. 
▪ Adapt the EU driver categories A and AM, to include in these categories electric cargo bikes and 
small electric delivery vans, which are increasingly used in green urban delivery.  
The IRHA welcomes many of the policy options mentioned in the Inception Impact Assessment and would like 
to insist on : 
▪ The importance of lower age limits considering the ageing of the driving workforce in the road haulage 
sector. The IRHA recommends allowing learner permit and lessons to be done at 17 with test after the 
18th birthday to mirror pilot licences; 
▪ The reclassification of Driver Licences to inc lude electric “Cargo” Bikes and urban electric vans  with 
lower age limits in order to employ under 18-year-olds who left school early (which is the case for 85% 
of the sector’s workforce). See the 2 tables below:

▪ Lifelong training especially regarding the digital and environmental transitions which will affect the 
road haulage sector: to this end the early training and recruitment of young people to the practice of 
eco-driving and of new driving technologies will be crucial. This will contribu te towards meeting the 
goals of Smart and Sustainable Mobility Strategy. 
▪ The involvement of insurers in the revision of the Directive. We may indeed end up in a situation where 
the industry and even the Commission and Member States can agree  to reduce age limits for the 
different types of driving licences but the insurers would still make it impossible . To avoid this barrier, 
it is important that insurance brokers would take into account: 
o The impact of automation and digitalisation coupled to the safety features found on all 
modern commercial vehicles and their impact on safety records ; 
o The actual safety record of young truck drivers and not some prejudice against them  that 
would lead to expensive and unjustified premiums; 
o The training that drivers are currently receiving. Once a driver has completed the mandatory 
official training and passed all the exams , insurers should not refuse a coverage but offer a 
coverage at an affordable price; 
o The work placement element in terms of the calibre of the Company taking young drivers on. 
Let the cost be less of a reflection of the person's age but more a reflection of for whom they 
are to work for. This puts the focus on training and calibre, to determi ne a premium, with any 
reputable company unlikely to risk their history on an unsuitable candidate; 
o The additional option of inviting those already insured in the industry to voluntarily pay a 1%  
levy to help mitigate the risk for a new policy of insuring the 18 to 24 year olds. This gets 
everyone involved in helping the insurance industry open the door to a new risk category with 
NO history or data to base any commercial formula on. This will also mak e for a transport 
community approach in conjunction with the insurance body, thereby giving the new blood 
an elevated position in the industry as such a collective effort has been made to give them this 
opportunity. See below a chart demonstrating that trained you ng professional drivers have

▪ The rationalisation of the procedure to obtain a driving licence and a Certificate of Professional 
Competence and exchange them among Member States . Accordingly with the digital transition 
promoted by the European Commission, we would like to see further digitalisation of training 
procedure and renewal of licences and CPC on the operational and administrative levels. 
▪ The clarification by the Commission towards EU Member States  that under the EU Driver Training 
Directive, initial and periodic training and exams/tests can be held in any EU language , and most 
specifically in English with any CPC module done in any member state to be acceptable to all member 
states.  
Through these measures the Commission would: 
▪ Give unemployed young people access to driving, with initial access to 4 wheels being electric  
including 16 years old doing urban deliveries with an electric "Cargo" Bike or urban electric Van.  
▪ Allow those, who, for social or academic reasons  leave school early, to pursue gainful employment 
giving them purpose, status and dignity. This can but have positive social consequences. 
▪ Help alleviate the pending driver shortage as these young drivers may well decide to remain as drivers 
and can now have a Rigid Truck license and possibly an artic driving license, on becoming 18 years of 
age. 
▪ Lay the foundation for the new retail and urban delivery model to be pursued, while giving an 
opportunity for our youth to contribute in a positive manner to this change.  
 
Moreover, the IRHA would like to insist on a swift adoption of the revision of the Directive on driving 
licences as fifteen years have passed since the last update and the issues at stake are pressing. The IRHA 
would also like to wish the best of luck to the European Commission regarding the thousands of contributions 
submitted during the public consultation!

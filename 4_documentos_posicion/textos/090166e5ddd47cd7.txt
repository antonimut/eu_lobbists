EMPA contribution to the public consultation on the revision of EU legislation 
on hazard classification, labelling and packaging of chemicals  
(4 May 2021 – 1 June 2021) 
The European Melamine Producers Association  (EMPA) welcomes the opportunity provided by the 
European Commission to comment on the revision of the CLP Regulation, expected for Q2 2022. 
The publication of the Chemicals Strategy for Sustainability marked a milestone in the European 
chemicals policy framework, including the proposal to revise the CLP Regulation. EMPA supports the 
overall objective of the European Commission to ensure a high level of protection of human health 
and of the environment. As EMPA, we consider safety of consumers and workers a n essential 
prerequisite for the use and production of the product we represent, melamine. We acknowledge the 
efforts from the European Commission to fill the gaps of incomplete information about hazards to 
human health and the environment.  
To cr eate a level playing field at both the European and global level, we recommend remain ing 
consistent with the existing regulatory framework throughout the revision exercise. 
We are concerned that measures taken at the EU level on classification and labellin g of chemicals 
could lead to a challenging legislative scenario for both manufacturers and consumers, in case they 
are not agreed in parallel under the umbrella of the United Nations’ global standard (GHS). 
We support the European Chemical Industry Council’s (Cefic) position that the European Commission’s 
proposal to include a separate GHS/CLP classification for endocrine disruptors  (ED), PMT/vPvM and 
PBT/vPvB substances is unnecessary, would result in unclear communication to the public  and in 
double classification of some substances. In the case of PMT/vPvM and ED, risk assessment could be 
conducted via a dedicated Annex under REACH, in line with the current process for PBT. This would 
ensure simplification of the process and avoid d ivergence from t he GHS global standards , which is 
expected to affect hazard communication for exported EU -manufactured chemicals  and to cause 
undesired consequences to the value chain.  
As highlighted in the Inception Impact Assessment, “additional hazard identification, notification and 
the possible associated relabelling will increase costs  compared to the baseline scenario ”, having a 
significant impact especially on SMEs. We ask that these challenges are taken into account, as well as 
the feasibility of substitution of hazardous chemicals with available alternatives on the market. 
Finally, EMPA is in favour of strengthening enforcement measures to ensure  a level playing field 
between European and non -European manufacturers. This issue becomes even more  relevant in 
relation to online sales, which have been increasing in the last year also due to the Covid-19 pandemic. 
EMPA remains at your disposal for any further information.

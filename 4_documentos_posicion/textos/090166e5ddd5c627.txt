MCEC – Microbial Control Executive Council 
CLP Revision Inception Impact Assessment (IIA) 
MCEC contribution to feedback mechanism (04 May 2021 - 01 June 2021) 
 
The Microbial Control Executive Council (MCEC) welcomes the opportunity provided by the European 
Commission to submit views about the forthcoming revision of EU legislation on hazard classification, 
labelling and packaging of chemicals (CLP, (EC) No 1272/2008). 
MCEC company members are committed to the betterment of public health and wellbeing through 
sustainable microbial control technologies. Microbial control active ingredients are the building blocks 
of a wide range of antimicrobial technologies, that are applied by thousands of companies, many being 
European SMEs. These products provide essential benefits to living environments , hospitals, food 
processes and industrial workplaces, covering a wide range of applications from  disinfectants to 
metalworking fluids. Microbial control technologies ensure the sustainability and durability of a wide 
range of goods and treated articles and processes such as paints on wall surfaces or ship hulls, plastics 
or wood for industrial and consumer use, and industrial cooling circuits.  
Although biocidal substances and products are regulated under the Biocidal Products Regulation (BPR, 
Regulation (EU) 528/2012) , they fall under the classification, labelling and packaging requirements 
established under the CLP Regulation, as per many other chemicals in the EU. Therefore, the outlined 
options by the European Commission for the forthcoming revision of CLP are likely to have an impact 
on the microbial control industry. 
Among the policy options outlined by the European Commission in the IIA, MCEC h as taken note of 
the objective to introduce new hazard classes and corresponding criteria  for the classification of 
chemicals in CLP. Specific references are made to endocrine disrupting chemicals. Back in 2017, the 
European Union adopted scientific criter ia for the identification of endocrine disruptors  (EDs) in the 
BPR and the Plant Protection Products Regulation (PPPR). These criteria have now been implemented 
in the respective legislations and work is ongoing on their application. Although it is underst ood the 
expected new criteria under CLP need to be applicable for all relevant legislation and beyond the BPR 
& PPPR, MCEC urges the Commission to ensure they will remain consistent with those already adopted 
for biocides. As per any industry, the biocidal sector needs regulatory predictability: if not aligned with 
the existing regulatory framework , a new hazard class for EDs under CLP could lead to a mismatch 
with the BPR criteria, and a potential need for a revision, despite the adoption only a few years ago. 
This would create substantial regulatory uncertainty for the currently ongoing approval of active 
substances and product authorisation under the BPR. 
MCEC would appreciate if the Commission could analyse the possible impact on biocides following the 
introduction of new hazard classes under CLP under the impact assessment to be performed for the 
reopening of CLP; in that regard, the Commission unit responsible for biocides in DG SANTE, should 
be involved in this process and in support to the leading units in DG GROW and DG ENV.  
On the increased number of hazard classes under CLP, MCEC is concerned this could lead to a 
mechanical effect of increased classified biocidal active substances, making their approval even more 
difficult than it is already the case today. Furthermore, it remains unclear if the changes in CLP will 
require a re -assessment of the already approved and marketed biocidal products. This should be 
evaluated and clarified as early as possible to the relevant stakeholders of the biocidal sector.

Overall, MCEC would appreciate if the European Commission could clarify what are the anticipated 
consequences for biocides following the CLP revision, as part of t he CSS impl ementation. These 
questions should be in our view addressed under the forthcoming impact assessment. This could help 
avoiding regrettable consequences for the microbial control industry that has  been proving his 
essential contribution to societal safety, not least during the Covid-19 crisis.  
Finally, we welcome the policy option to introduce specific rules for online sales, considering the 
increasing number of non -compliant products (including detergents and other  products containing 
microbial active subst ances) being imported from third-countries and sold in the Single Market via 
online platforms, as a consequence of the restrictions to movements during the Covid-19 pandemic. 
We hope our input  will be  considered constructive by the European Commission. For a more 
overarching chemicals industry position on this policy initiative, please refer to the submission from 
Cefic. As far as microbial control technologies are concerned, MCEC remains at your disposal for any 
further information, and in the context of the follow-up consultation.  
 
Disclaimer 
While contributing to this consultation we remain very aware we are all still experiencing 
unprecedented times, with industry, governments and institutions around the world taking major 
actions to address the Covid-19 crisis, and introducing large -scale policies which will have significant 
impacts for years to come. We will continue to support Europe’s Member State governments and 
institutions in their efforts to overcome the socio-economic impacts of the crisis. 
When investing for the future, industry, governments and institutions will have to ensure investments 
align with the policy targets of a climate neutral Europe by 2050. All this also means that the 
attractiveness of Europe as a re -investment destination, and re-shoring industry back to Europe, will 
depend more than ever on a favourable policy framework that manages ever -growing differences 
between the world’s regions. We look to the European Commission to undertake the appropriate 
assessments and to include these wider considerations in the future framework that will be developed.

About us 
The Microbial Control Executive Council or MCEC, is an initiative of the world’s leading companies 
developing and supplying microbial control technology and solutions . It was established in 2012  in 
order to promote the safe and effective use of microbial control technologies. MCEC has six members: 
BASF, IFF, ICL, Lanxess, Lonza and Troy.  
In creating MCEC, we envisioned creating a reference poin t for stakeholders and the public alike to 
understand the benefits of microbial control technologies and the steps being taken to ensure the use 
of more sustainable biocides across all applications. 
To learn more, follow us on Twitter, visit our website and our LinkedIn page. For more information, 
do not hesitate to contact us at info@microbial-control.com. 
MCEC is a sector group of the European Chemical Industry Council (Cefic).

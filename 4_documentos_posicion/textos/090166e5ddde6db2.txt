Subject: Revision of EU legislation on hazard classification, labelling and packaging of chemicals 
 
Plastics Recyclers Europe ( PRE) welcomes the opportunity to give a feedback to the Inception Impact 
assessment on the Revision of EU legislation on hazard classification, labelling and packaging of chemicals. 
As an industry association representing plastic recyclers, we are supportive to update the CLP Regulation 
given more than a decade of scientific and technological progress.  
Over this period, we have seen growing societal concerns over endocrine disruptors and other hazardous 
properties of substances. PRE believes that clear criteria for the identification of such substances might 
be beneficial. Such criteria should enable a clear differentiation of what is, but also very importantly what 
is not an endocrine disruptor. We question, however, whether it could be more appropriate to have this 
discussion within the UN GHS framework. PRE would like to point out that criteria to be adopted by the 
EU legal framework should preferably not differ from criteria that might be adopted within the UN GHS. 
Would the opposite occur, the risk is of a permanent divergence between the classification of substances 
under EU legislation and the global system , and/or trigger the need to amend the CLP Regulation in the 
short future.  
Automatic links with secondary legislation represent another point PRE would like to underline with the 
present note. The harmonized classification process is solely about the intrinsic properties and nothing 
but the intrinsic properties of a substance: socio-economic considerations are out of scope of classification 
discussions. This can be appreciated from a societal point of view as the CLP Regulation then triggers 
communication of hazard information down the supply chain allowing actors to take precautionary 
measures to protect human health and the environment.  
We, however, notice a trend whereby secondary legislation with references to hazard classes would 
trigger automatic restrictions following the classification procedure that would have a socio -economic 
impact. Without a proper procedure to a llow for such impacts to be assessed, there is a risk that socio -
economic consequences would pollute discussion on the classification, which are meant to be, once again, 
about the intrinsic properties of the substance only. A s such, we would caution agains t such automatic 
links or potentially incorporating text into the CLP Regulation addressing them.  
PRE remains available to clarify these concerns more extensively should this be desired.  
 
About 
Plastics Recyclers Europe (PRE) is an organization representing the voice of the European plastics recyclers who 
reprocess plastic waste into high-quality material destined for the production of new articles. Recyclers are 
important facilitators of the circularity of plastics and the transition towards the circular economy.  
Plastics recycling in Europe is a rapidly growing sector representing €3bn in turnover, 6.6 mt of installed recycling 
capacity, 500 companies and 18.000 employees.

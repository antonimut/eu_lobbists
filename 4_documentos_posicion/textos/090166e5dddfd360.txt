1. Definitions  
Below is a set of definitions as intended in this document: 
> Public/Private Service Provider is whoever instance that delivers over the internet a 
dematerialized service or tangible good(s). 
> Private Attributes Provider is whoever instance that delivers credentials that can later be 
presented by a user as claims; claims can be of several kind: attributes, assertions, 
attestations. Private Attributes Providers can be academics, corporate, financial sector, 
associations, etc. A Private Attribute Provider may act as a Private Service Provider and 
vice versa. 
> Public Attributes Provider is an instance part of a National eID scheme.  
 
2. Aim of the document  
The aim of this document is to describe how the OSIA APIs could act as an interconnection between 
the user, Public Attributes Providers (national eID schemes) and Private Service/Attributes Providers 
for the latter to verify or access the user’s ID information.  
OSIA APIs may act as a limited verification system for private sec tor seconding the existing eIDAS 
framework. OSIA APIs flexibility would facilitate the adherence of private stakeholders to the current 
scheme.  
3. eIDAS framework – a short summary 
Whenever a European citizen wants to access a Public Service Provider remotely, the connection is 
subject to several redirections via the eIDAS nodes - a gateway to interconnect cross -border the 
relevant Public Attributes Provider (national eID scheme) for the verification of the user’s identity.  
The principle is that each user has his identity verified by his Public Attributes Provider (national eID 
scheme) and the confirmation of the verification is received by the Public Service Provider.  
In practice, the Public Service Provider  systematically redirects the user’s request via t he eIDAS 
nodes towards the adequate national eID scheme to receive in return the confirmation of the user’s 
ID alongside the set of attributes defined by the implementing Act 2015/1501.  
The implementing Act 2015/1501 describes a limited set of attributes representing “pivotal 
attributes”. Attributes can be classified in three layers, pivotal being one of them: 
> Pivotal (name, first name, birth place, date of birth etc.) 
> Biological or Physical representation (DNA, fingerprints, iris etc.) 
> Social footprints (connections to internet, social network information etc.)

4. OSIA potential complementarity to eIDAS 
Beyond Public Service Providers, there is a need to extend the verification of the user’s ID to Private 
Service Providers and to Private Attributes Providers. The former is essential for the verification of 
the user’s ID before providing a service. The lat ter is essential for Private Attributes Providers to 
provision user's personal device(s) with attributes on demand only after a thorough ID verification.   
In this context, further features could be set up:  
> Depending on the nature of the service, users may need to share with the Private Service 
Provider/Private Attributes Provider more or less than the defined pivotal set of attributes and be 
allowed to disclose selectively their attributes (user consent, data minimization) 
> Unlike for Public Services Providers, Private Service Providers/Private Attributes Provider would 
need to make use of stringent privacy features like consent management and to grant 
anonymous access to services  
> Private Service Providers/Private Attributes Provider would need to assess a variety of claims 
(depending on the use case requirements) such as assertions (e.g. "I'm over 21-year old"), 
attributes ("my name is John Smith"), attestations ("I'm graduate of xyz University"). Private 
Attribute Providers would need to deliver such attributes on demand.  
> Even with digital proof, the Private Service Provider/Private Attributes Provider may need to 
check data beyond the very attributes as for instance their associated terms of usage, the 
attributes validity, the user’s legitimacy (his identification token is not revoked), the fact that the 
attributes are truly recorded in the civil registry, the fact that the user is still alive and standing 
for a unique natural person.  
> The Private Service Providers/Private Attributes Provider that access the user’s ID data need to 
be registered so to create a chain of trust and avoid fraudulent private access providers from 
phishing users and collecting and diverting their attributes for unauthorized purposes.  
The OSIA APIs could act as an interconnection between:  
> User, Public Attributes Providers (national eID schemes) and Private Service/Attributes 
Providers for the latter to verify or access the user’s ID information.

A National eID scheme offering restricted and filtered access to identification information would allow 
for either Private Service Providers or Private Attributes Providers to verify claims (pivotal attributes) 
received from users. While a claim may be comprised of pivotal attributes alongside other attributes 
related to user's activities inherent to their third attribute layer (social footprint), a Private Service 
Provider can check against OSIA API services only the validity of the pivotal attributes through the 
National eID scheme. Likewise, before delivering some specific attestations, Private Attribute 
Providers (e.g. a corporate or financial institution or an academic attestation) may need to make sure 
that the pivotal attributes featured by this attestation are reliable/valid at the moment such attestation 
is issued.   
 
However, envisioning a notification of all Private Service Providers/Private Attribute Providers to 
become full part of eIDAS scheme per se is likely to stumble against a process complexity that may 
not be attractive. Business -wise, as a general statement and by experience, enforcing a Private 
Service Provider/Private Attribute Provider to abide by regulatory rules without a clear return on 
investment would not be a promising business model. Instead, a direct registration of Private Service 
Providers and Private Attribute Providers with a Registrar under EC control to ensure a total trust 
down the certification chain can be a more convenient process allowing for both user confidence in 
the registered Service Providers and Private Attribute Providers, and for a proper filtering of National 
eID scheme service through OSIA API.  
 
The access to OSIA API services shall not necessarily be interpreted as a limited access to National 
eID registries but rather as an access to a static and updated replica of a dedicated part of such 
registries e.g. OSIA API could in some implementations act as a service interface to a blockchain.

In conclusion, OSIA API s may act as a limited verification system beside the existing eIDAS 
framework. OSIA APIs would second the eIDAS framework for private sector and facilitate 
adherence of private stakeholders by sparing them the constraints of a notification scheme, and 
sparing the Commission the need to amend the regulation to fit the numerous expectations of private 
sector and their users in terms of privacy, anonymity, growing diversity of exchanged claims, liability, 
etc.   
 
4.1. Benefits 
The approach of leveraging OSIA APIs to allow private sector to verify users’ ID as a parallel 
scheme to eIDAS brings the following benefits:  
> The National eID scheme will not be impacted if the OSIA APIs are implemented, OSIA APIs 
would just be another layer on top of the existing solution 
> OSIA APIs are an open standard therefore any member state would have the possibility to 
select the IT integrator of its choice, the part of the API to implement, the kind of information it 
accepts to deliver, etc.  
> OSIA APIs would be something working alongside the existing eIDAS framework so that the 
existing framework does not need to be broken down and reviewed 
 Moreover, as the two systems can work together side by side, the maintenance and 
update of the APIs can be tailored without impacting government-based services 
> Due to the large panel of accesses and services, exposing indirectly some part of the national 
registry information to Private Service Providers/Private Attributes Providers through OSIA APIs 
would increase eIDAS scheme performance and appeal 
> OSIA APIs could be conditional to some requirements among which a registration to make sure 
that the information/request comes from the appropriate party 
> OSIA APIs can be subject to filtering and could check the user consent with user authorization 
tokens, including terms of usage (for Private Service Providers or Private Attributes Providers 
liability on data collection limitation and use)  
> OSIA APIs could implement an automatic notification of the user upon demand for each 
attribute checking event 
> Traceability through OSIA could be both private (anonymous in that the purpose of the 
verification remains hidden) and at the same time allowing the tracking back of a user who 
would have endorsed a false attestation

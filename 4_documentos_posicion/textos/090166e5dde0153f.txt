Revision of the Union legislation on blood, tissues and cells – Position paper  
Cryos International Sperm & Egg Bank would like to thank the Commission for the opportunity to bring 
forward our views in the current consultation. We fully support the revision of the current EU regulation on 
safety and quality of human tissues and cells (Dir. 2004/23/EC).  
Cryos is part of an alliance with the other Danish Sperm Banks. Each of us sends a consultation contribution 
and we look forward to a revision of BTC legislation with focus on strengthening the security, quality and 
supply of tissues and cells. 
As industry professionals, we call for clearer and more standardized rules across the EU member states to 
increase the safety and security of the many persons involved in or affected by the Directive. 
Today, the lack of common EU standards and requirements and inconsistencies in national regulation is 
creating a very differentiated landscape across Europe. This is a problem for the protection and treatment 
of donors, recipients, and donor children. It is also hampering the cross-border exchange of tissues and 
cells. Some examples are divergent approaches towards blood testing frequency, multiple national donor 
registers with different requirements for donor documentation and different requirements for egg- and 
sperm donors in the same Member States. 
We agree on the five defined challenges in the current legislation, and we believe that the current 
legislation should be updated to allow for more flexible alignment to scientific and technological 
developments.  
Policy Option 2 
In our opinion, the challenges are best met with policy option 2. This policy ensures the possibility of rapid 
updating of safety and quality requirements defined by European Expert Bodies.  
Today, different national requirements and interpretations of EU guidelines are challenging, for example 
within requirements of laboratory test and the timing hereof for sperm donors. If option 1 is chosen, it 
might lead to even greater diversity in safety and quality requirements, which is why we as an industry 
recommend option 2 to strengthen patient safety as much as possible, and to optimize the conditions for 
easing distribution of human tissues and cells between tissue establishments in different EU Member 
States.   
In our opinion option 2 is more likely to secure equalities in the safety of patients and in the quality of 
treatments the best. 
Initially, we would like to point out a few areas that we believe should be included in revision of the rules, 
and we look forward to furthering involvement and dialogue on the update of the BTC legislation:  
Equal and sufficient access to donated gametes 
Much greater focus should be placed on the security of supply of tissues and cells, for clinical- or self-
treatment. The EU should define a right for all women and couples to have access to donated gametes, 
regardless of sexuality or marital status, and the transfer of gametes between member states should be 
further supported and eased e.g., by aligning national requirements and interpretations of EU guidelines.

A more unified approach to donor documentation/ consent forms etc. should be ensured to avoid donor 
shortages. 
 
Common requirements for testing and screening of all gamete donors 
We suggest standardizing the tests of egg and sperm donors and the release methods hereof securing that 
all gamete donors are tested and released according to the highest standards ensuring patient safety.  
Today egg donors are being subjected to less stringent tests regimes than those posed for sperm donors 
within the same Member States. This fails to mitigate the potential risk of affected children being born 
from donated eggs to the same degree as donated sperm. The Sperm Bank Alliance recommends that there 
should be the same test and screening requirements for all gamete donors.  
Donor Registry 
We suggest ensuring further protection of donor conceived children and women in fertility treatment by 
establishing a common Donor Register in EU. A common EU register for sperm and egg donors, wherein all 
donors are given a unique ID donor number, will establish a much better insurance that donors do not 
donate their gametes in several different places or countries.  
A common register will make it easier to ensure traceability if a sperm/egg bank obtains knowledge of a 
hereditary disease from a donor or a donor child. Accordingly, a common register could help increase 
patient safety in the EU as well as data security and accuracy in tissue centers. 
Today, there are national differences and uncertainties between countries in regards of having a Donor 
Register and the requirements for accessing the information therein.  
With a high level of patient safety and several highly specialized and experienced tissue centers, an EU 
Donor register can advantageously be placed in and administrated from Denmark.

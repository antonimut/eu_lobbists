 
 
Automotive suppliers’ submission on the Revision of the EU legislation on hazard classification, 
labelling and packaging of chemicals 
The Automotive Industry welcomes the opportunity to provide feedback on the inception impact 
assessment. 
Making the economy and society more sustainable. This overall ambition of the "Green Deal" is fully 
supported by the European Automo tive Industry, committed to a safe working environment to its 
employees, as well as safe articles to its customers. We suppor t to protect human health and the 
environment from the risks posed by chemicals while at the same time to increase the 
competitiveness of EU industry.  
In this context, we wish to point out that chemicals are one of the most essential building blocks to 
achieve the Green Deal-targets. 
When producing complex products such as vehicles or their components, automotive suppliers are at 
the far end of the regulatory chain, supporting all the conformity requirements initially related to 
products provided by those at the other end of a highly international and complex supply chain, the 
chemical industry. Through a close cooperation across this international value chain, the automotive 
industry has invested a considerable number of capacities into the efficient implementation of these 
complex requirements resulting in tools and processes which are still unique in the world. 
 
In responding to the Inception Impact Assessment for CLP, the automotive industry limited its scope 
to those Policy Options that are likely to directly affect automotive aftermarket chemical products. 
 
Policy Option CLEPA Comments 
Introduce new hazard classes (such as 
endocrine disruptors) and corresponding 
criteria. 
We do not support the introduction of new 
hazard classes that are referenced in the 
REACH regulation but are not defined in UN-
GHS because we fear that the global 
harmonisation approach is disregarded. 
Introduce an obligation to provide 
information of some hazards on the label for 
products currently outside the scope of CLP. 
We do not support the introduction of new 
labelling obligations that are not defined in 
UN-GHS because we fear that the global 
harmonisation approach is disregarded.

Policy Option CLEPA Comments 
Clarify the obligations to classify mixtures and 
some complex substances. 
We support the obligation to classify mixtures 
and some complex substances to the extent 
that this is done consistently with the GHS 
classification rules. In particular, care should 
be taken in classifying mixtures with regard to 
CMR properties and biodegradation and 
bioaccumulation potential, since these hazard 
classes are intrinsic to the respective 
substances. 
Introduce specific rules for online sales. No comments offered. 
Introduce the possibility to submit proposals 
for and set harmonised environmental and 
safety values for some substances. 
No comments offered. 
Require importers and downstream users to 
submit information on substances classified 
for physical effects or health hazards to 
poison centres and clarify obligations for 
distributors to submit such information, 
through an only representative or other 
means. 
While we support the clarification of poison 
centres obligations for distributors, we would 
prefer those obligations to be limited to 
substances or mixtures classified for health 
hazards only.  We would welcome 
simplification of the notification obligations for 
products placed on the market in multiple 
Member States. 
Introduce a mandate for Commission to 
request ECHA to develop new harmonised 
classification and labelling (‘CLH’) dossiers. 
No comments offered. 
Allow multilingual fold-out labels. We strongly support changing CLP to allow 
multilingual fold-out labels, as the current 
requirements introduce unnecessary burdens 
for Member State-specific stock management 
and/or relabelling for chemical products 
placed on the market in multiple Member 
States. 
Introduce tailored labelling rules where there 
is not enough space on packaging. 
Where there is not enough space on 
packaging, we strongly support the 
introduction of multiple labelling options that 
allow the labeller to select the most effective 
of the options tailored to the specific product 
size, shape and intended use. Such options 
should remain in line with the GHS rules, so 
that any changes to EU rules for labelling 
should continue to permit labelling according 
to GHS rules if the labeller so chooses.

ANFFE'S OBSERVATIONS ON THE REVISION OF THE CLP REGULATION 
 
Although CLP has proven to be a harmonized way of identifying and communicating 
hazards and an effective tool to ensure the safe use of chemicals, the Commission has 
deemed it necessary to review it. The Spanish National Association  of Fertilizer 
Manufacturers (ANFFE) would like to transmit to the European Commission its 
observations on the initial impact assessment of the revision of the CLP Regulation: 
- With regard to the obligation to provide information on the label on the hazar ds of 
products that currently do not fall within the scope of CLP, we consider that if these 
products already have specific labelling requirements set out in a European legislation 
complementary to the CLP Regulation, should remain subject only to those pr ovisions, 
to avoid confusion with CLP labelling obligations. 
- For mixtures and complex substances that have more than one constituent substance 
(More than One Constituent Substances MOCS), the MOCS classification should be 
based on the data in the REACH d ossier and preference should be given to test data. 
Since no substance is totally pure, in practice a lot of substances could be classified as 
MOCS. It should not be necessary to generate more data at the constituent level if 
information on MOCS exists. It is  not feasible to isolate and test all individual 
components that are present above 0.1%. If this were required, animal testing, time 
and cost of data generation and evaluation would increase, complicating it to the point 
of making it unfeasible. The result s of the tests in the MOCS allow taking into account 
the interactions between all its components that mimic complex exposure. This 
approach is more efficient and realistic than dividing MOCS into individual 
components. 
- With regard to a mandate of the Commission to request ECHA to draw up new 
harmonized classification and labelling (CLH) dossiers, we question the 
appropriateness of this proposal. The additional resources that would be required 
should be taken into account and appropriately assessed in an impact assessment.

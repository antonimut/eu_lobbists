European Power Tool Association – EPTA  
Position paper on the “Sustainable Products Initiative” 
 
The European Power Tool Association represents the interest of European power tool 
manufacturers. Our 25 member companies represent approximately 70.000 employees in 
Europe (170.000 worldwide) and 90% of all corded and cordless power tools sold. Power tools 
are used by professionals, skilled tradesmen , and DIY consumers. The industry’s annual 
turnover is about 6 bil lion Euro. EPTA members are committed to the highest possible 
standards of quality and safety of their tools. Innovation and advanced technologies as well as 
customer-friendly applications are key to our companies’ economic growth, competitiveness, 
and the creation of jobs. 
We would like to take the opportunity to contribute to the discussion around the upcoming 
“Sustainable Products Initiative”. Our industry is committed to the highest possible standards 
for safety, circularity, and sustainability. The gui ding design principles and features for our 
products across the entire value chain are robustness, durability, repairability, recyclability , 
material quality, and energy efficiency . A power tool is designed to serve reliabl y and for as 
long as possible. The after-market services of our companies ensure that products are repaired 
when and wherever necessary. Also, it is important to highlight the global character of our 
supply chain and markets. The value chain of each product is rather complex and comprises 
numerous different suppliers, which is an important element to be taken into account when  
considering policy options t hat aim at  creating more transparency and information about 
products sold on the EU market.   
With regard to the Sustainable Products Initiative, we would like to bring the following aspects 
into the discussion: 
➢ Build on existing legal frameworks 
It is necessary to keep the legal framework as simple and clear as possible. The basic 
legal instrument for enhancing product sustainability is the Eco -Design Directive. 
Hence, creating a parallel legislation to Eco-Design with the same product groups in its 
scope could create difficulties concerning the implementation and adoption of 
additional measures. It is necessary  to build on experience to give European 
companies predictability and guidance. For instance, when it comes to materials and 
substances, the EU has already existing instruments and policies in place such as the 
RoHS Directive or REACH Regulation.

➢ Clear scope and definition of a Digital Product Passport 
For many years EPTA members have been  supporting the digitisation of product 
information for users (e.g. digital instruction manuals ). The current considerations 
regarding a Digital Product Passport and other relevant legislation such as Battery and 
Machinery Regulations are only partly going in this direction . I t is crucial that the 
collection, storage, and availability of data fulfil and are proportional to a clear objective. 
In this respect, contracts, intellectual property rights, and data protection must be 
considered. Scope, content, and quality of data collected, stored , and being made  
available for third parties have to be strictly pre-defined as well. EPTA companies have 
complex global supply chains, making full transparency and traceability  hard to 
maintain and at some point impossible.  
 
➢ Alignment between standardisation and sector specific legislation 
Every sector and product has its own requirements and standards. International and 
European standards play a crucial role for EPTA members. It will be important to align 
standardisation and legislation over sustainability criteria. As there is no one -size-fits 
all approach, European policy must be sector and – where necessary - even product 
specific. This can only be accomplished by using sound assessment procedures which 
are underpinned by clear evidence.  
 
➢ Complementary “green diplomacy” 
In addition to standardisation, it is important f or the EU  to carry out cohesive and 
complementary “green diplomacy”. Most of our companies are already following 
general principles (e.g. UN Sustainable Development Goals ) which serve as a good 
basis for orientation. However, these need better promotion an d stronger articulation 
in the European foreign policy. For example, it would be sensible if the EU used more 
of its political power  to influence and enhance the working and social conditions in 
African or Asian countries. Companies and other stakeholders are willing to support 
this, however they cannot act by themselves and need the political voice of the EU  to 
achieve sustainable supply and production chains. 
 
➢ Safety consideration and pragmatism over sustainability criteria 
European power tools are robust and durable  by design  as they are used by 
professionals (B2B) for applications in areas such as construction sites, the automotive 
sector, and by consumers in the DIY sector. When it comes to defining sector or product 
specific criteria, EPTA members suggest a pragmatic and safety-conscious approach 
when defining each criterion. In the case of a power tool repair , only professionals 
should be allowed to open and access the inside parts as the opening of the tool itself 
or of the battery could cause a serious explosion/fire or even an electric shock. Safety 
comes first and must be part of all reflections regarding repairabilit y. In this respect, 
EPTA proposes to use the existing repair networks of power tool manufacturers that 
are qualified and certified. Furthermore, all potential benefits concerning repairability, 
refurbishment and so on are rather limited in their application for our products and need 
to make sense from an economical as well as an environmental perspective.

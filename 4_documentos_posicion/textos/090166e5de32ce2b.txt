June	2021	Additional	input	to	the	questionnaire	on	the	Sustainable	Products	Policy	Initiative	MWE	welcomes	the	Commission's	legislative	proposal	for	a	new	Sustainable	Products	Policy	aiming	at	making	products	in	accordance	to	the	Circular	Economy	Action	Plan	2.0	and	the	European	Green	Deal.	MWE	especially	welcomes	the	extension	of	the	scope	under	the	new	Ecodesign	Directive,	which	will	also	include	non-energy	related	products,	such	as	textiles	and	furniture.	We	believe	that	this	is	the	right	way	to	move	forward	and	we	would	like	to	invite	the	Commission	to	take	into	consideration	MWE’s	answers	to	the	public	consultation	on	the	questionnaire.	Waste	hierarchy	must	be	on	the	top	of	the	decision-making	process	while	shaping	the	initiatives	foreseen	by	the	 roadmap	 of	 the	 Sustainable	 Products	 Policy.	 For	 this	 reason,	 we	 suggest	 the	 European	 Commission	 to	integrate	the	following	points	into	the	initiative	(extension	of	answer	to	question	1.C):	• Ban	on	the	destruction	of	unsold	and	return	goods	(either	physically	or	online).	The	Sustainable	Product	Initiative	must	clearly	set	out	that	no	product	is	sustainable	if	it	ends	up	into	landfill	or	incineration.	This	prohibition	 would	 incentivise	 producers	 to	 take	 more	 responsibility	 on	 these	 unsold	 products	 and	 may	discourage	fast	fashion	and	overproduction	trends.		• Sanctions	to	discourage	planned	obsolescence.	Products	should	be	designed	based	on	a	‘waste-prevention’	principle.	Producers	should	facilitate	repair	solutions	by	providing	services,	manuals	and	making	spare	parts	easily	available.	The	Sustainable	Product	Policy	should	encourage	and	support	the	creation	of	repair	centres	and	boost	education	and	training	to	fit	these	new	jobs.	• Criteria	of	Extended	Producer	Responsibility	(EPR)	modulated	fees	should	cover	minimum	requirements	of	ecodesign	and	embrace	the	waste	hierarchy.	If	Extended	Producer	Responsibility	(EPR)	is	implemented	in	a	EU	Member	State	for	a	product	category,	this	scheme	should	embrace	waste	hierarchy,	cover	minimum	requirements	of	ecodesign	and	be	designed	in	collaboration	with	all	the	players	involved	in	the	value	chain	and	 decision-making	 process	 (including	 municipalities).	We	 recommend	 to	take	 into	 consideration	 MWE	Position	Paper	on	EPR,	published	in	2013	in	the	context	of	the	revision	of	the	Waste	Framework	Directive,	for	further	details	on	this	point	(link). • Policy	 strategies	 to	 boost	 reusable	 options	by	 pricing	 incentives	 for	 the	 consumers,	 communication	strategies	targeted	to	consumers	for	them	to	trust	reusable	options	and	funding	support	to	reuse	activities.		• Products	as	a	service.	The	potential	of	this	type	of	activities	should	be	included	in	the	Sustainable	Product	Policy.	Incentives	for	producers	and	consumers	should	be	developed.	 	• Policy	strategies	to	boost	the	uptake	of	secondary	raw	materials	should	be	also	developed.

• Ecodesign	 criteria	 should	 include	 restriction	 of	hazardous	substances	for	 which	 there	 are	 available	substitutes.	In	particular,	substances	which	are	toxic	for	human	health	and	environment,	and	substances	that	 technically	 or	 legally	 make	 recycling	 difficult	 or	 impossible.	 Funding	 should	 address	 research	 and	innovation	to	find	appropriate	alternatives	to	these	substances.	 	Additionally,	 we	 would	 like	 to	 provide	 policy-makers	 with	 better	 reasoning	 on	 MWE’s	 answer	 to	several	questions:	1.A(b),	products	such	as	electronics	become	obsolete	quickly	because	of	technological	innovations.	We	agree	that	technological	innovations	should	not	make	electronics	obsolete.	1.A(d),	many	 products	 are	 designed	 for	 shorter	 term	 use	 due	 to	 changing	 fashion	 trends.	 We	 believe	 that	products	should	be	designed	to	have	the	longest	lifespan	as	possible	regardless	the	fashion	trends.	1.A(k),	the	quality	of	second	hand	goods	cannot	be	guaranteed	or	is	difficult	to	assess.	Although	we	agreed	on	the	statement,	we	believe	that	second	hand	goods	can	be	actually	guaranteed	if	they	have	been	repaired	in	a	repair	 &	 reuse	 centre	 and	 this	 centre	can	 guarantee	the	 repaired	 item.	 At	 national	 level	 it	 should	 be	 made	possible	for	repair	&	reuse	centres	to	certify	their	goods	and	give	a	guarantee	period	depending	on	the	country	and	on	the	product	category.	Further	to	that,	we	believe	that	the	wording	of	the	question	2.A(g)	is	incomplete	and	thus	it	should	have	written	as	follows:	Require	producers/importers	to	offer	product	guarantees,	which	could	include	"commitment	to	free	repair	 as	 first	 remedy"	within	 the	 guarantee	 period	in	 case	 of	 failures	 and	 a	 "commitment	 to	 upgrade	 the	product	 periodically”.	As	 far	 as	 guarantees	 for	 electric	 and	 electronic	 goods	 are	 concerned,	 we	 believe	 that	these	products	in	the	EU	should	have	a	guarantee	period	of	a	minimum	3	years.	Looking	forward	to	discussing	these	and	further	points,			MWE	Secretariat

 
 
 
POSITIONS OF ÖBB – AUSTRIAN FEDERAL RAILWAYS 
Hydrogen (H2) will play a crucial role in meeting European CO2 emission reduction targets. 
H2 is not only a catalyst for sector integration (e.g. power to mobility) but also enables CO 2-
neutral intermodal transport. Indeed, it is an essential element in the transition towards a low 
carbon economy.  
Hydrogen can also secure economic prosperity in the European Union. Creating strategic 
value chains for H2 and renewables within the E uropean Union would stimulate economic 
growth, create jobs, and increase the EU’s competitiveness in an international technology 
race while reducing dependencies on transcontinental value chains.  
In order to reap the benefits from hydrogen, the EU has to step up its efforts along entire H2 
value chains. This includes R&D, equipment manufacturing and H 2 application. The focus 
should be on public transport and rail, as feasibility studies have demonstrated high 
potential for the application of H2 in these areas. By opening these sectors to hydrogen, 
they can kick-start Europe’s H 2 economy: as partner s in R&D, buyer s of equipment, 
consumers of hydrogen, as well as a means of sustainable transport. First, however , 
adequate and supportive political conditions on EU level are required. 
 
1. Speed-up the roll-out of hydrogen technology  
Many applications of hydrogen technology (e.g. electrolysis for generating green hydrogen 
or hydrogen- powered vehicles) are not yet economically competitive compared to 
conventional fossil -based technologies. Hence, any su ccessful integration of  hydrogen 
technology into the railway and public transport sector requires a consistent funding strategy. 
This funding strategy has to be applied until a competitive market integration is achieved.  It 
should cover the entire innovati on process including all Technology Readiness Levels 
(TRLs), especially deployment projects.  Successful projects would be an incentive for 
European companies to scale up their production of hydrogen equipment and vehicles.  
The following measures are recommended to the European Commission: 
 Revise the Energy Tax Directive 2003/96/EC so that green hydrogen, i.e. hydrogen 
generated from renewable sources, is taxed favourably compared to fossil fuels. 
 Remove the existing cap on financial support for investment s in hydrogen 
vehicles set in the Guidelines on State Aid for Environmental Protection and 
Energy. The aim is to allow member states to provide financial  support for the entire 
additional costs of hydrogen vehicles. Furthermore, a framework for financial state aid 
for hydrogen production should be introduced in the same guidelines. 
 Guarantee that hydrogen technologies become eligible to receive financial 
support from programs like Horizon Europe , the European Innovation Funds, as 
well as through subsidies (or facilitation of subsidies, e.g. IPCEI rules). 
 Ensure consistent funding and support from future cooperative R&D programmes 
(e.g. Shift2Rail -II and FCH -JU-II) to reduce companies’ first mover risk.  If  their

2. Make multimodal transport the backbone of the European hydrogen economy: as 
reliable consumer and sustainable carrier of H
2 
As a reliable consumer of H2, public transport by train and bus will ensure a baseline level of 
utilization of H2 infrastructure. Hence, the reliability that comes with long term planning in this 
sector contributes to the successful roll-out of H2.   
However, t he successful rollout of H2 for public transport and rail  requires strategically 
planned H2 supply chains, as well as the coordinated deployment of H2 infrastructure (e.g. 
transport and fuelling infrastructure  within the strategic framework of the Directive on 
deployment of alternative fuels 2014/94/EU) and of demonstrator projects across the EU. 
Rail can also be used to transport H 2 itself. Actively positioning rail as a mode for hydrogen 
transport between waterway and road makes H2 supply chains more sustainable. In addition, 
rail acts as a back -up-system in case of roadblock s or low water periods. Apart from this, 
large time gaps resulting from complex approval and construction processes for pipelines 
can only be bridged by rail. 
The following measures are recommended to the European Commission: 
 Establish a master plan for the deployment of H 2 infrastructure (e.g. fuelling and 
transport infrastructure) across the EU.  This provides a common framework for 
member states and leads to a coordinated and strategic roll -out of H2 infrastructure at 
national and regional levels and ensures technical interoperability and accessibility for 
consumers through harmonization of technical standards . This master plan should 
incentivise the creation of  demand-driven “hydrogen hotspots” , which are situated 
strategically near switching stations, loading terminals or  areas with bus garages, non-
electrified railway tracks and highways.  H2 fuelling stations should be located where 
supply is needed, and strategically placed at intermodal nodes between rail and road. In 
the latter case, the rollout of H 2 in the rail secto r would be accelerated and intermodal 
nodes would be strengthened. 
 Give preferential treatment in public funding programs to projects that aim at 
achieving an embedded, fully integrated, sustainable hydrogen supply chain 
(starting from the generation of renewable energy, electrolysis, compression, transport, 
storage management to final consumption). E.g. projects that supply public transport and 
rail freight with hydrogen from filling stations that are directly integrated with electrolysis, 
H2 storage and renewable power generation. 
 Make the integration of rail as a long distance transport mode in H2 supply chains 
between production and application projects a precondition for EU cofunding of 
hydrogen transport infrastructure projects. 
 
3. Get a “Made in Europe” Fuel Cell off the ground  
ÖBB has had successful test runs of hydrogen powered buses and railcars. We also engage 
in projects that convert diesel-powered railway vehicles. In our experience, some European 
companies are able to offer railcars with hydrogen propulsion. However,  a sufficient market 
for shunters  and large scale refurbishment of diesel trains  does not exist yet.  Similarly, 
European bus manufacturers do not seem to keep up with the targets set by the EU -Clean

Vehicles Directive. Current production capacities are significantly lagging behind the potential

European vehicle industry obtained a fuel cell of “domestic” origin. 
The following measures are recommended to the European Commission: 
 Make additional efforts in the area of the “Fuel Cells and Hydrogen Joint 
Undertaking”-program to accelerate the production of components . Especially for 
hydrogen applications in the railway and public transport sector.  
 Introduce a strategy that increases fuel cell production rates and harmonizes the 
standards applicable to trains and buses at the EU level.  This should be done 
through unified technology standards, unified vehicle interfaces for electricity supply and 
unified data protocols to support the transformation to carbon-free mobility solutions.

EC Public Consultation on the Sustainable Products Initiative: 
Cosmetics Europe contribution 
 
 
Cosmetics Europe represents the cosmetics and personal care industry in Europe, from large multi-
nationals to SMEs. The vast majority of Europe’s 500 million consumers use cosmetic and personal 
care products in their daily life to protect their health, enhance their well-being and boost their self-
esteem. Ranging from antiperspirants, skin care products, fragrances, make -up and shampoos, to 
soaps, sunscreens and toothpastes, cosmetics and personal care products play an essential role in 
all stages of life, serving the needs and expectations of consumers. 
 
Cosmetics Europe welcomes the opportunity to engage with the European Commission on the 
Sustainable Products Initiative. We support the objective to make products fit for a climate-neutral, 
resource-efficient and circular economy, reduce waste and ensure that the performance of 
frontrunners in sustainability progressively becomes the norm. 
 
Complementary to our input provided through the public consultation questionnaire, we would like 
to further detail three key aspects of sustainable products  that are important for  our sector. Our 
comments are made from the perspective of cosmetic product formulations and also from the  
cosmetic product packaging perspective, as the latter plays a crucial role in ensuring the integrity, 
stability and safety of the formulations.  
 
As a general comment, s ufficient transition time s will be needed for companies , and in particular 
SMEs, to prepare adequately to comply with the various new requirements which may necessitate 
new internal infrastructures.  
 
Policy-related challenges to making products more sustainable 
 
Consistency between the various policy and regulatory initiatives, and a harmonised framework of 
rules at European level, are essential factors for meeting the European Commission’s objectives. 
These should be complemented by sectorial approaches that are best adapted to the variety of 
products on the EU market and their specificities. 
 
The cosmetics industry is investing significantly in the development of more sustainable pr oducts. 
It strives to reduce its environmental footprint, generate economic benefit through high value jobs 
and growth, enhance the social value of its products and support the communities in which its raw 
materials are sourced and its products are manufactured . Please see our Environmental 
Sustainability Report 2019 1 as well as  the section ‘ Environmental sustainability in action’ on our 
website2.

However, substantial challenges exist which hinder the industry in its efforts to increase the 
sustainability of its products. The key challenges are posed by the lack of a harmonized set of EU 
rules and the increasing emergence of diverging national rules  and initiatives; these increase the 
complexity of compliance and add to the cost of doing business for companies which operate across 
various EU Member States . Other important challenges are the availability of information in the 
various supply chains and consumer demand and acceptance of more sustainable products. 
 
Furthermore, diverging national rules hinder consumers from being truly empowered  given that 
information may not be consistent between the country where they live and the countries where 
they travel across the EU.   
 
In particular, with regard to packaging and packaging waste: 
o harmonisation of definitions and of rules for the collection, sorting and recycling of packaging 
waste is imperative; 
o adequate quality and supply of post-consumer-recycled plastic must be available in the EU , 
and specificities of the different product categories must be considered 3 to make recycled 
plastic more available and competitive; advanced recycling technologies need to be rapidly 
scaled to play an important role in the circular economy; the economics for these technologies 
should be further improved since they are currently not at an acceptable level. 
o in parallel, instruments encouraging the use of recycled materials, such as the modulation of 
fees for products placed on the market , also help to increase recycling and recycled content 
in plastic packaging. 
o harmonisation or rules for packaging -related labelling must be established to preserve the 
integrity of the Single Market and to ensure that consumers benefit from information that is 
consistent between the various Member States  and that allows them to make informed 
choices.  
 
Generally, improved access to finance  for the production of more sustainable products  and 
consumer education campaigns to enhance sustainable consumption would greatly support and 
encourage sustainable production and consumption patterns.  
 
Also, voluntary approaches such as (environmental) labelling can be effective tools to incentivise 
businesses to provide more sustainable products. However, they must follow a life cycle  based 
approach and take into account essential product characteristics and functions such as fitness for 
purpose and efficacy and performance.  
 
Responsibility for information: the digital product passport 
 
The cosmetics industry is subject to comprehensive regulatory requirements on product-related 
consumer information. Given the drive to redu ce packaging, the relatively small size of cosmetic 
products, and the increased use of digital means of communication  in consumers’ daily lives, it is 
clear that the digitalisation of information provides a range of opportunities both for consumers and 
for the industry.

Therefore, Cosmetics Europe supports the Commission’s objective to increase access to digital 
information. In this context, the development of ‘digital product passports’ (DPP) is a good way to 
provide companies and other key supply chain actors, consumers and market  surveillance 
authorities with access to information that is relevant for the sustainability of products along their 
respective value chains. However, it will need to be ensured that the information included in the 
DPP is relevant and reliable. 
 
The introduction of digital product passports to track the sustainability impacts of products should  
take into account relevant existing initiatives and databases developed at global, EU, Member State 
or sector level , as well as be thoroughly assessed against its effectiveness and feasibility , making 
sure not to duplicate work. 
 
Sufficient transition time will be needed for companies , and in particular SMEs,  to prepare 
adequately to comply with the digital product passport requirements which may necessitate new 
internal infrastructures.  
 
Provisions regarding unsold goods 
 
The cosmetics and personal care sector  is keen to avoid as much as possible the destruction of 
unsold products that are suitable for consumption and its potential negative impact on the 
environment. There are already many industry-led initiatives conducted to improve recycling, reuse 
and donation  of cosmetics and personal care products. These industry initiatives should be 
promoted and supported. 
 
Depending on the nature and characteristics of their unsold products, and to take into account the 
technical challenges associated with the recycling of  certain materials and the legal and business 
risks associated with the donation of certain products, manufacturers and distributors should be 
free to decide whether and how to recycle, reuse and/or donate their unsold products.  
 
In particular, donations may present the risk of supplying parallel markets for products operating 
through selective distribution channels. These parallel markets themselves create major risks of 
counterfeiting and not meeting cosmetic products’ safety standards. Therefore, manufa cturers 
should be free to donate their unsold products when they can do it in a safe and legal way for 
themselves, beneficiaries and distributors.

Annex to the response on the European Commission public consultation on 
the Sustainable Products legislative Initiative 
GROW International (Grow Int) welcomes the opportunity to provide feedback on the public 
consultation published by the European Commission  the Sustainable Products legislative 
Initiative which aims to establish overarching product sustainability principles . In order to 
explain further our position, we have developed the present document. 
Promoting a circular bioeconomy will only be achieved by  replacing fossil-based materials 
with sustainably produced bio -based materials  such as wood  which are renewable  and 
recyclable. Non-sustainable materials should be allowed ONLY when there is NO alternativ e 
solution in a sustainable/ renewable material . The overall sustainability of products is  
determined by the renewable origin of the material and proven sourcing from sustainable 
sources.  European Parliament supported this by encouraging the Commission in their last 
report on the new Circular Economy Action Plan1 “to explore different mechanisms, including 
market-based mechanisms, in order to incentivise the use of renewable raw materials, 
including wood and wood products , thereby offering climate benefits while promoting the 
most efficient use of wood and respecting the cascading use principle”.  
GROW Int would like to highlight the following topics and we hope these elements will be 
considered by the European Commission:  
First, it is crucial to adopt a lifecycle approach and promote the use of the Lifecycle Cycle 
Assessment (LCA) and/ or Product Environmental Footprint (PEF) to understand the whole 
environmental impact of packaging materials and adopt the most adequate policy measures.  
Second, develop a clear and realistic definition of recyclability  as well as  a compostability 
and biodegradability with the aim to have legal clarity updating the current definitions.  
Third, reusable packaging may not be the right solution for all materials. It will depend on 
the absolute environmental impact. 
Fourth, we think a mandatory percentage of recycled content should be imposed only when 
it makes sense and not for all materials. 
Fifth, wood packaging industries are all SMEs and it is important not to impose a 
disproportionate administrative burden on them.  
1. It is crucial to  adopt a  lifecycle approach and promote the use of LCA or Product 
Environmental Footprint (PEF) for packaging materials. The waste hierarchy is a useful 
tool for policy making but it should not the only criterium as it was conceived for non -
renewable materials such as plastics. This will mean that the absolute environmental 
impact should be considered including the impact of preparing the materials for reuse 
or recycling. The upstream phases of the renewable material and its transformation into

We believe, it is important to mention the promotion  of the use of recycled or renewable 
materials as included in French legislation3 and consider wood has always the potential to be 
used in energy recovery. Its natural destination is to be recycled or be a resource for energy 
recovery more that being reused. By using waste wood to manufacture sustainable products 
and to produce climate-friendly energy, 5.8 million tonnes of CO₂ are saved in Germany every 
year4. 
 
It is also important to consider the environmental impact of the process to make the  
packaging hygienic again : plastic packaging uses many natural resources such as water, 
chemical products and energy to dry them again. The lifecycle of wooden packaging is circular 
as it can be easily reused, recycled into other products, a nd it is biodegradable. This means 
wooden packaging is the ultimate example of circular thinking, having an essential role in 
contributing to a circular economy as it can be recycled. Natural resources used for producing 
carton (cellulose in water, use of bleach, contaminated wastewater) are far greater.  
 
2- We ask for the development of a harmonised, clear and realistic definition of recyclability 
at EU level to ensure legal clarity. Existing definitions in European standards and legislation 
are ambiguous or obsolete  and they should adapt it based on product type categories in  
order to consider the purpose, use , collection scheme s and lifespan of the product. In 
addition, it should maintain note A.3.15 from European standard EN 13430 on Requirements 
for packaging recoverable by material recycling. This note mentions that it  should be 
considered as recyclable materials those new materials which are in the process of 
establishing a recycling process as it is recognised their development may take a period of 
time. This will allow  the industry to set up recycling schemes  during the transition period 
before compliance.

Concerning a quantitative definition, the recyclability of a product cannot be determined by 
the actual recycling rate of the packaging at scale or in practice. These are usually estimations 
and they do not depend on packaging producers. They are responsible for paying EPR 
contributions at national level  but have no control over the final destination of the waste 
wood. 
Concerning a qualitative definition, it is being discussed if separate waste collection will be 
a requirement to consider if a product is recyclable. Wooden packaging is recyclable and it 
can be separately collected at household level in the packaging bag (co-mingling in the yellow/ 
blue bag). Once shredded or compacted, it is used to make chip board, coal bricks, 
composting, cattle bedding, sawdust or biomass for heating purposes. It c ould also be 
separately collected with the organic or garden waste in order to improve the quality of the 
compost as the wood fibers brings structure to it. It is a natural way of improving the quality 
of soils without using chemicals. Therefore, we also ask for a clear definition compostability 
and bio -degradability so wood waste could be collected with bio -waste. Wood does not 
need the use of any chemicals to biodegrade the resource: it becomes compost. After 
shredding the waste wood, it is only needed to recover the staples from wood crates using 
magnets which can then be sold as metal scrap.  
3- Reusable packaging may not be the most ade quate solution for all materials.  We 
support the adoption of policy measures that are evidence -based and take into account 
a lifecycle approach showing the absolute environmental impact of the adopted 
measure.  
 
On the recently published guidelines6on single-use plastics from the Commission it mentions 
what is considered as single use: “Whether a product is conceived, designed and placed on 
the market for reuse, can be assessed by considering the product’s expected functional life, 
i.e. whether it is intended and designed to be used several times before final disposal, 
without losing product functionality, physical capacity or q uality, and whether consumers 
typically conceive, perceive and use it as a reusable product. 
It also summarises the requirements included  on standard EN 13429:2004 on Packaging – 
Reuse: “The package can be emptied/unloaded without significant damage, and without risk 
to the integrity of the product, and health and safety ; The package can be reconditioned, 
cleaned, washed, repaired, whilst maintaining its ability to perform its intended fu nction. 
Lightwood packaging cannot currently be reused as it complies with  strict EU food hygiene 
standards and regulations  and reusing it will not guarantee the health and safety of the 
packaging and without losing its functionality, physical capacity or quality.

Promoting reuse targets makes sense only for materials from fossil fuels such as plastic but 
not for renewable materials like wood.   
According to the Directive 2018/852, one of the objectives is to protect, preserve and improve 
the quality of the environment by improving waste management, and ensure a prudent and 
efficient utilisation of natural resources. T hen it is very important to manage natural 
resources sustainably and efficiently for the next generations. The European wood packaging 
industry manages  its forests sustainably and is increasing tree planting 8. This directly 
contributes and has an impact on the reduction of total annual greenhouse gas emissions  
which is a target of the EU and a reduction of the EU's dependence on imported natural 
resources.  
Wood packaging can help the food industry to reduce the use of plastic, especially in the case 
of plastic in contact with food products. Wood packaging for food products comes from 
sustainable forest management and emits less CO ₂ in its lifecycle . Furthermore, they are 
easily recyclable and extend the shelf life of food and reduce food waste (antibacterial 9, 
maintains moisture and breathable which are essent ial properties of wood). Reused plastic 
materials do not always guarantee the non-contamination of food.

5- All Wood packaging companies are small and medium sized enterprises (SMEs) based 
in the European Union. It is composed of more than 2 20 companies that represent a 
business of more than 500 million euros.  If the administrative burden is increased 
substantially and specially during the coronavirus pandemic, it will be very difficult for 
them to comply. They should be consulted to avoid this situation and it shall be assessed

opportunity to boost the bio -economy, increase the value of forests and economic 
activities linked to the forestry sector and tackle rural exodus. 
About GROW International 
 
GROW International is the  European organisation of wooden packaging manufacturers (Group Recycling of 
Wood). It represents the interests of wood packaging with regards to the environment, quality , hygiene, food 
safety, etc. It is an organisation created as a result of cooperation between European organisations in the wood 
packaging sector in Spain, France, Germany and Italy. It represents more than 150 companies that manufacture 
800 million boxes for fruit and vegetables annually, and 400 million boxes of other types, representing a business 
of more than 500 million euros.

 
 
Brussels, 4 June 2021 
Making sustainable product the norm in the clothing and 
textile industry 
 
EURATEX, the European Apparel and Textile Confederation acknowledges and supports the 
Sustainable Products Initiative (SPI) published by the European Commission. We are pleased to see 
that the European Commission is moving forward on this policy. EURATEX also recognizes this initiative 
is necessary in achieving the Circular Economy Action Plan and the Green Deal targets as wel l as to 
implement the necessary circular economy aspects in the textile industry supply chain. The objective 
of this initiative should also encourage consumers  and public and private procurers to choose more 
sustainable and  circular products and encourage manufacturers to improve the sustainability and 
circularity of their products, where technically feasible1.  
It is an opportunity for a win -win situation for the environment and the European economy. It will 
help to achieve circularity, make products appropriate for a climate neutral future , as well as bridge 
the gap for common understanding of what is a sustainable product . The latter calls for the 
consideration of a strong level playing field with an appropriate level of market surveillance to 
accompany the new sustainability requirements ensured by the SPI. There is a need for cooperative 
approach to promote and offer incentives to choose  high quality and durab le products and reward 
low-impact manufacturing processes , as well as f avour products designed with recycled or 
biobased/biodegradable materials or designed for recycling2, recyclability3 or/and for longevity. 
The textile industry is very diverse  and complex. Its specificities must be taken into account with a 
strong recognition of its heterogeneity. With the upcoming EU Textile Strategy and Industrial strategy, 
ensuring policy coherence is a strong concerns for EURATEX. 
Furthermore, a “one-size-fits-all” approach is not possible. A product-specific perspective is essential, 
although general principles or guidelines might be developed to help the overarching objectives of the 
SPI’s measures. Nevertheless, it is important the new instruments developed under the SPI does not 
create legal uncertainty or additional burden for companies, especially for the SMEs.  
Similarly important as the role of consumers in the industry transition, B2B and publi c organisations 
can also have their role to play in incentivising sustainable products. The SPI should, therefore, take 
into account the public and private procurement.

SUSTAINABILITY PRINCIPLES 
Effective sustainability principles should provide guidanc e and clarity for the elaboration and the 
implementation of the SPI. However, we see some important conditions to be applied so the principles 
can be effective:  
• Product requirements must be considered on a product group4 specific basis.  
• Sustainability principles and requirements shall be material and technology neutral5 
• Repairability, reusability, reliability, serviceability,  recyclability should not be seen 
independently as they are all part of basic quality product requirements and depend on each 
other.  
• It should not hinder the development of new business models, new products and innovation. 
• The principles should be enforced by market surveillance authorities to ensure a level playing 
field. The same principles and requirements should apply to both EU products and imported 
products. To achieve this purpose  and ensure the effectiveness of this policy, a strong 
enforcement by market surveillance authorities will be required. 
• All stages of the products’ life cycle stages need to be considered as it provides an assessment 
of the overall products’ impact at the different stages. 
• It should ensure that ther e are sustainable materials6 available on the European market. SPI 
shall ensure an EU-wide functional and effective secondary raw materials market, as well as 
the necessary infrastructure to collect, sort and recycle materials at competitive price level  
and in adequate quantities and quality to be used by the industry . Moreover having a 
secondary raw materials market with competitive price is a must for the industry.  
• The European Union has already developed comprehensive set of rules which includes 
BREF/BAT, REACH, IED and others. It set some requirements, restrictions or guide lines on 
environmental protection, energy efficiency, chemicals. In the idea of safeguarding the level 
playing field, these rules should apply equally to all (for European manufactured goods and 
imported goods). In the meantime, as these rules are already implemented or being 
implemented, they must be taken into account in the elaboration of the SPI. We should 
concentrate more on an effective legislations without increasing the administrative burden 
and the overlapping of requirements.

Although important benefits can be drawn from implementing a digital product passport, it is 
important that the European Commission takes into consideration following aspects:  
• In order not to increase the red tape for companies, the digital product passport should take 
into account data already collected through existing legal frameworks, norms and standards 
such as labelling certification, product safety sheets and others. 
• As the DPP might include critical information. It should be clear at early stages who should 
have access to critical information. Considering the critical aspect of these data, on ly the 
public authorities/market surveillance authorities should be granted the access to consult 
these data.  
• The structure of the passport should reflect the need for different information between the 
different entities. A consumer do not need the same information as, for instance, recycler or 
manufacturer. According to whether the DPP is used as a tracking tool, surveillance tool or  as 
a communication tool, clear structure and information collected (i.e. which  information is 
needed according to the concrete purpose of the DPP) should be defined. 
• The DPP should be useable , manag eable and implementable for companie s. The process 
burden and costs should be proportional in relation to the benefits.  When defining the 
architecture of the DPP, the question of the resources, data protection and the administrative 
burden must be considered. Particularly SMEs might be challenged to provide the necessary 
information and it could increase rapidly the burden and the uncertainties. It could also be a 
very complex instrument to implement , especially for companies with limited resources , 
therefore a sufficient transition period should be defined when the passport will be adopted.  
• When designing a DPP,  it must be possible to answer questions about data origin, data quality 
and data processing right from the start. Ultimately, DPP can only be used effectively and 
efficiently if the stored data are trustworthy, correct and in a usable form. 
• Requirements for data should be open source and the data set should be composed in a way 
it facilitates flexibility and interoperability when it comes to existing systems and systems in 
development. These requirements should be developed in cooperation with the industry, 
especially SMEs. 
• To maximise the benefits of DPP and c onsidering, the European Commission is looking at 
existing initiatives, it is important to take into account the existing limitation of these systems. 
For example, information on SVHC substances, collected in the SCIP database, cannot be used 
for some of the originally intended purposes of the SCIP database. Information cannot be used 
in disposal and recycling, because the interface between the database and the application in 
the recycling industry has not been sufficiently considered. 
 
 
 
 
The information in the Digital product passport should  contained a basis of minim um product 
information completed by relevant and logical information adapted to the product, namely:  
➢ Exact product fibre composition 
➢ Active substances 
➢ Contact details of company placing a product on the market 
➢ Country of origin 
➢ Maintenance instruction 
➢ Information on use and EOL (e.g. recycling) 
➢ Product claims (e.g. recycled/organic content) 
 
EURATEX has been involved in the development of the UNECE global standards for enhancing 
transparency and traceability. The global standard for traceability might be interesting to consider 
when developing the DPP as it has benefi ciate from the collaboration of the industry. This standard 
could facilitate the data exchange along the value chains and the implementation of a level playing 
field.  However, the European Commission should note that what the UNECE is developing might be 
consider heavy for SMEs. Transparency and traceability should be open source, free or low cost and 
interoperable.  
 
ECODESIGN  
The SPI plans to extend the Ecodesign directive to non -energy related products. It is a suitable legal 
instrument that may guide further developments towards circularity in Europe, but also help with the 
definition of what is a sustainable product  by setting requirements from the design phase . For the 
textile industry, developing specific requirements can be quite complex as the industry is very diverse 
and implies different requirements  for the different of products . Widening the directive’s scope 
towards products that were explicitly not originally included, require definitions and requirements to 
be very clear and relevant. Such criteria should be as well developed in cooperation with the industry. 
The revision of the Ecodesign Directive should remain to its initial topic, in this case design and should 
not introduce any EPR or G PP requirements. On the contrary, t he EPR scheme mod elling or GPP 
criteria should be based on the framework conditions and principles defined in the Ecodesign 
directive. 
We considered that the requirements must be clear, proper and science based . Some technical 
challenges (e.g. discussion of chemical recycling processes, end of waste criteria , lack of standards ) 
might hamper the implementation of Ecodesign requirements. To move forward, support and 
research are needed. These challenges can also be overcome by sharing knowledge. The set of 
requirements in the Ecodesign for sustainable textile products should primarily focus on modularity, 
durability, longevity, recyclability, repairability and reusability. The requirements should also consider 
the nature of the product and should be applied proportionally to the product type.  
Another important aspect related to the Ecodesign is the product guarantee. In most members states, 
the national guarantee schemes are currently limited to 2 years.  However, the term of guarantee 
 
 
depends on expected lifecycle of a products and can amount to a much longer term. Longer warranty  
and better reliable lifetime calculation could expand the life cycle of a product and may as well 
encourage consumers in product assurance. Underlying  this logic, there is also the aim of having more 
quality product s on the market. The m ore quality the re will be, the longer they will last and the 
guarantee should logically expand.  
Establishing new circular economy business models need the involvement of the consumers and the 
industries. Without sustainable good quality products , new business models won’t work . However 
they also won´t work, if consumers are not willing to use them/won´t change their behaviour patterns 
to more sustainable business models. To support society and industry through this systemic transition, 
policy must incentivise sustainable behaviour for both. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
About EURATEX 
As the voice of the European textile and clothing industry, EURATEX works to achieve a favourable environment 
within the European Union for design, development, manufacture and marketing of textile and clothing 
products.  
 
The EU-27 textile and clothing industry, with around 160,000 companies employing 1.5 million workers, is an 
essential pillar of the local economy across many EU regions. With over € 61 billion of exports, the industry is a 
global player successfully commercializing high added value products on growing markets around the world.  
 
Working together with EU institutions and other European and international stakeholders, EURATEX focuses on 
clear priorities: an ambitious industrial policy, effective research, innovation and skills development, free and 
fair trade, and sustainable supply chains.

Key principles to guide the development of the Sustainable 
Product Policy Initiative 
 
This paper is part of PlasticsEurope’s response to the European Commission’s public consultation to inform 
the development of the Sustainable Product Policy Initiative. 
 
 
Key messages  
• PlasticsEurope supports the development of the Sustainable Product Initiative which ensures 
the placing on the EU market of more sustainable, circular and carbon neutral products, and 
calls for a sector-specific approach which is based on full life cycle assessment and embeds all 
aspects of sustainability, namely, environmental, social and economic 1. 
• We welcome the adoption of requirements to improve products’ reusability, repairability and 
recyclability and call for a multi -criteria approach where all requirements are balanced against 
the actual environmental impact of the final product in its specifi c application and are flexible 
enough to ensure development of innovative solutions. 
• We support a n enabling  framework that boosts the market of secondary raw material and 
support measures to incentivize products circularity such as Green Public Procurement  and 
Ecolabel when they favor the overall most sustainable products  and are grounded on science-
based requirements.  
• A harmonised and consistent legislative framework which considers existing product-specific 
and chemical legislation and protect the integr ity of the Single Market is key to ensure legal 
certainty and accelerate the transition to a carbon neutral and circular economy. 
 
Measures to make sustainable products the norm 
 
Expanding the scope of the Ecodesign Directive , while ensuring consistency with existing 
legislation 
 
PlasticsEurope supports the European Commission ’s intention to widen the scope of the Eco design 
Directive so as to include also non -energy related products. However, sustainability principles, if 
developed for all products, must take into consideration provisions already laid down in the existing 
product or sectoral  specific framework s which reg ulate for example, construction products or 
packaging, ensuring cohe rence among the legislations  and avoiding double -regulation. Certain 
drivers for sustainability, such as increased application of recycled content  in construction and packaging , 
already considered in the revision of the C onstruction Product Regulation and Packaging and Packaging 
Waste Directive should continue to be regulated in those contexts. 
 
 
Design for sustainability - Sustainability requirements for products 
 
PlasticsEurope welcomes the goal to foster overall products’ sustainability and believes that the revision of 
the Ecodesign Directive and the establishment of sectorial and general products’ requirements should 
consider several essential aspects:  
• Sustainability requirements , whether they are defined in the context of the Eco -design or in 
additional/complementary legislation, should be product -specific and take into consideration the

• Combination of sustai nability criteria such as increasing energy efficiency, durability, 
reusability, recyclability should always be balanced against the actual environmental impact of 
the required final product in its specific application.  Therefore, requirements should be developed 
for the product applications and not be material specific. Evaluation of functional requirements is  key 
before assessing the overall most sustainable solution. For example , a durable plastic in a long-lasting 
product will have life cycle benefits, while it may not have benefits in short-lived applications, depending 
on whether or not it facilitates reuse and recycling 2. 
• When we talk about products’ sustainability, we should always assess  the performance of the end -
product, balancing several aspects such as reduction of waste, efficient use of resources as well as  
carbon and environmental footprint. The assessment should also consider the benefits of the product 
in its use phase, including health and social impacts and take account of technological innovations and 
societal trade-offs.  
• Sustainability criteria for products containing plastics could be based on the following principles: design 
for sustainable sourcing, design for optimised resource use, design for environmentally sound and safe 
products, design for prolonged product use, design for recycling  and reusability 3. These principles 
should be all considered concurrently with a view to identify the most sustainable solution. 
• Sustainability requirements should  be technology ne utral and provide sufficient flexibility to allow 
the development of innovative solutions and new recycling technologies.  
• It is essential that sustainability requirements are designed in such a way to prevent substitution with 
materials that result in overall worse environmental performance and are harmful to human health. 
• To drive the uptake of sustainable technologies and products, clear communication to consumers about 
the product’s sustainable properties must be ensured. This communication shall be consistent with the 
EU Green Claims initiative and should be based on LCA considerations and a holistic evidence-based 
approach so as to prevent misleading and discriminatory information. 
 
- Prioritisation in product design 
PlasticsEurope welcomes measures which could improve reusability, a product’s life span, repairability and 
therefore reduce use of natural resources . However, we believe that measures on product design should 
be driven by the option which reflects the best overall environmental impact of the product and not be limited 
to a rigid hierarchy. Therefore, if a certain degree of prioritisation in designing requiremen ts is 
envisaged, this hierarchy should allow for flexibility and deviation from prioritisation when justified 
by compelling grounds such as for ex. by the life cycle assessment, product safety and functionality.

- Substances in products which could inhibit products recyclability and substances of concern 
PlasticsEurope acknowledges the need to reconcile the objectives of a n improved circularity with those of 
proper risk management of substances of very high concern. To ensure that recycled materials are safe for 
their intended use, it is essential that they comply with REACH5  regulation as well as other sector specific 
legislations (for ex. WEEE6, RoHS7, Food Contact8, toys9, etc.). Ensuring the safe use of products is one of 
our guiding principles. We believe that existing legislation such as REACH ad CLP10, should remain 
the instruments by which ensure safe handling and use of substances in product regardless of 
whether they are made from recycled or virgin material, especially when it comes to addressing 
substances of very high concern. Therefore, any potential gap  or further assessment should be 
addressed within this framework.  
 
Furthermore, considering the recent ly communicated European Commission’s intention to revise REACH 
and CLP, and to develop criteria  within the Safe and Sustainable by Design initiative, it is essential  that 
overall consistency in the  EU legislation on products and chemicals is maintained . We believe that the 
Sustainable Product Initiative should not be the means by which to regulate the presence of substances in 
products which could hinder  recyclability and eventually ban them.  Moreover, products must be designed

- Safe and sustainable by design criteria 
PlasticsEurope believes that it is essential to ensure consistency between the criteria developed under the 
Sustainable Product Initiative and those developed under the Safe and Sustainable by Design approach. 
The latter criteria are expected to be determined by the Euro pean Commission in 2022. Therefore, eco-
design criteria under the SPI should not be based on an approach which has not ye t been developed and 
which impact has not yet been assessed. Moreover, m ost of the products which could be covered by the 
Sustainable Products Initiative, have already their own legal framework. Therefore , it is essential that the 
new requirements developed under the SPI  as well as those emerged from the Chemical Strategy 
for Sustainability such as the SSbD approach, will ensure consistency with REACH Regulation and 
all the sectorial and vertical legislation ( e.g., WEEE, RoHS, Food Contact, etc.). SSbD criteria should 
also ensure that all dimensions of sustainability are considered, be science -based, and not hamper 
innovation.  
 
 
Digital Product Passport 
 
PlasticsEurope supports the objectives of the Digital Product Passport to improve consumers information 
and increase communication and transparency along the value chain  and in particular to waste 
management operators and recyclers so as to increase collec tion, sorting and recycling. The framework 
regulating the DPP should consider the following aspects: 
 
• The DPP should be developed in a way to avoid that the amount of data gathered would lead to 
excessive costs and to ensure consistency, compatibility and interoperability with existing or 
legally required databases  (e.g. ECHA SCIP database, International Material Data System (IMDS), 
the Global Automotive Declarable Substance List (GADSL) and standard IEC -ISO 82474) so as to 
prevent duplication and excessive administrative burdens.  
• Information disclosure should fit for purpose and be tailored to the specific needs  of the 
recipients. The level of detail should vary depending on whether information is directed to consumers, 
waste management operators, recycler s, or regulators. Information on materials and chemicals in 
products are for example not directly relevant to consumers. Communication on chemical composition 
should therefore be addressed to the value chain and waste management operators and only be limited 
to regulated or predefined lists of substances  with a view to balance the need to increase 
transparency and improve sorting and recycling with the protection of confidential business 
information and competitiveness between companies. 
• The legal framework supporting the development of the DPP should be flexible and proportionate to 
the specific features of each sector and could consider sectorial approaches  for priority waste 
streams. 
• We favour communication of recycled content  in products which should ref lect both mechanically 
and advanced recycled content. Certified/attributed recycled content from chemical recycling 
based on mass balance  should also be included. In addition, information on recycled content and 
carbon footprint must be based on harmonized  and standardized approaches  and methodologies. 
Furthermore, Member States should ensure strict controls on the accuracy of information on 
products from non-EU countries.

Incentives for circularity: r egulatory, market and reputational incentives to  encourage more 
sustainable production and consumption 
 
PlasticsEurope acknowledges that certain regulatory or economic incentives could be necessary to 
encourage the placing on the EU market of more sustainable, circular and resource efficien t 
products, if these incentives favour products with the best sustainability performance, are based on sound 
science and full life-cycle thinking.  
 
More harmonised EU rules on Green Public Procurement requirements could represent a promising option, 
when based on a sector -by-sector assessment and scientific knowledge. Furthermore, GPP requirements 
should be based on clear, measurable, and non -discriminatory criteria which are assessed holistically. 
Public authorities should favour those products which meet the best sustainability performance in terms of 
efficient use of resources, products safety requirements, carbon footprint, cost-benefit analyses as well as  
environmental and social impact. GPP could also be considered a promising tool to drive an increased 
uptake of recycled content in products , stimulating its market and working as a “pilot test” to understand 
possible barriers and challenges. 
 
The same principles should be applied to  possible additional incentives. For example,  PlasticsEurope 
welcomes the promotion of voluntary approaches, such as the use of EU Ecolabels, where the y prioritize 
best performing products while preventing any arbitrary discrimination against materials. 
 
 
Protection of the Internal market 
 
Product-design requirements and sustainability principles must be clearly defined and fully 
harmonised at EU level  so as to ensure a level playing field among all products placed on the EU 
market and preserve the integrity and well -functioning of the Internal Market . Market fragmentation 
would lead to legal uncertainty and unpredictability and  slow down the transition to a low -carbon, circular 
economy, even if motivated by national ambitions to be a “frontrunner” . The introduction of measures that 
might fragment the Internal Market, for example in their implementation, must therefore be avoided in order 
to provide the scale that industry needs to deploy effective solutions at the highest possible speed . To this 
end, the European Commission should maintain ownership in the implementation process, including 
monitoring and follow-up processes with Member States and any interpretative guidance must be foreseen 
and delivered well in advance of the deadline for Member States’ transposition.  
 
In addition, t o safeguard both competitiveness among the Member States and of the whole EU economy 
vis-a-vis third countries, the European Commission should consider the adoption of measures aimed at 
ensuring that the same sustainability requirements apply to products imported from outside the EU.

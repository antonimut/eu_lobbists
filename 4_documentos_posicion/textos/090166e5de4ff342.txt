Heating serves an essential human need. Heat, meaning indoor comfort and access to hot 
water, supports the health and well-being of people, whether they are at home or at their 
workplace. Putting people at the centre of their innovation, EHI members are leading the 
energy transition in the heating sector. EHI members provide affordable, smart and 
decentralized solutions to pave the way to climate neutral buildings in 2050. In doing so, they 
foster EU jobs, economic competitiveness and reduce energy system costs. 
 
The EHI recognises the necessity of complementing the decarbonisation objectives 
under the European Green Deal with efforts to improve the environmental 
sustainability of products, via legislation.  However, when it comes to the 
sustainability of products, there is no one -size-fits-all solution. Th erefore, the 
Sustainable Products Initiative absolutely requires a product -specific approach. In 
this spirit, the EHI reply to the public consultation on SPI is to be understood from the 
point of view of the heating industry. 
 
General comments and remarks 
Vertical product standards are absolutely needed in order to ensure accurate 
verification of material efficiency aspects per product group. Indeed, setting common 
environmental requirements for all products in a common Ecodesign framework  
Directive could  put  at  risk  the energy efficiency  first  principle that  is  essential  for 
Energy related products. 
 
There should be no overlap of the SPI with existing legislations. 
Over the past few years, the European Commission has proposed more and more 
product regulations. However, this becomes problematic when it leads to double or 
cascading requirements on a single product category. Namely, the current Ecodesign 
regulation already includes requirements on the availability of spare parts , and the 
presence of substances is regulated under the waste and chemical legislation (e.g. 
WEEE Directive, RoHS Regulation).

Adding horizontal information requirements on these in the SPI that overlap with the 
already existing requirements would create a double legislation, which would  make 
measurement, verification  and enforcement more complex.  Therefore, a ny 
requirement in the SPI  should avoid double regulation with regards to already 
existing product requirements.  
 
Requirements should only be introduced if they are enforceable. In the questionnaire 
on SPI, several propositions for information requirements would be very difficult to 
put in place for the  concerned industry and/or complex to be checked by market 
surveillance. Examples include the amount of recycled content in products, which is 
difficult to verify. For heating products, information concerning the amount of critical 
raw materials is rarely provided by non-EU suppliers. Therefore, it has to be assessed 
for each information requirement not only what its added value is, but also whether 
it is enforceable . On the other side , market surveillance needs to be improved to 
ensure compliance of products with the relevant requirements.   
 
 
 
On heating systems: 
 
Increasing the energy efficiency of heating products should be prioritised over 
prolonging the lifetime of the old and inefficient stock. LCA analysis made on heating 
products confirm that the use phase is the main cause of environmental impact; 
consequently we believe that increasing the energy efficiency of the heating systems 
should be prioritised over prolonging the lifetime of the old and inefficient stock . 
Indeed, 64% of the heating systems installed in Europe’s buildings are old and 
inefficient. They are on average 25 years old and, if they were labelled today, they 
would end up in class D or below. Replacing them with new, state-of-the art efficient 
and renewable-based technologies will be key to attain CO2-neutrality in the building 
sector. Therefore, the energy efficiency of heating system should be prioritized over 
prolonging the use of their embodied materials.  For this purpose, new circular 
economy aspects should not compromise the concept of best environmental 
performance over all life cycles applied in the assessment metric.  
 
Information on the repairability of products should only be made available to 
professionals: For products such as air conditioners, heat pumps, cogeneration space 
heaters and gas space heaters, specific tr aining and certification are required, 
pursuant to other EU legislation for environmental or safety related goals. As such, 
repair by the end -customer is and should not be applicable, as it would jeopardize

product and end-customer safety. Therefore, it has to be carefully assessed to whom 
the information in the digital product passport should be accessible: Information 
relevant for testing, disassembly, maintenance, repair or reassembly (e.g. test 
protocol, disassembly process and instructions, etc.) shoul d only be made available 
to professionals. 
 
A reparability score at EU level is not really applicable for heating systems. It can lead 
to a subjective assessment of different parameters which are then summed up into 
one score, which can be misleading for consumers. Additionally, several Ecodesign 
measures set requirements also on replacement products, concretely endangering 
their availability and the possibility to repair products. These requirements can make 
these parts incompatible with the initial products. As a result, a perfectly repairable 
product may need to be replaced entirely earlier than its expected average lifetime. 
This is detrimental to the environment and in full contradiction with the principle of 
resource efficiency.  However, if a reparability score were to be introduced, only 
parameters for which reliable, reproducible, and enforceable standards to measure 
them exist should be considered. This is essential not only to ensure a level -playing 
field between manufacturers, but also for market surveillance purposes. In addition, 
the scoring system and the parameters should be based on a thorough impact 
assessment considering the specificities of each product group.  Finally, it would be 
preferable to have it defined at EU level rather than having a different scoring system 
per Member State. 
 
Requirements on fees on the sustainability of products under Extended Produ cer 
Responsibility schemes are also needed towards recycling companies  Depending on 
the sector, it has to be assessed whether a fee is needed or an incentive for the 
consumer to bring back the product at EOL. This depends on whether the cost of 
recycling is higher/lower than the cost of waste.

 
Additional comments to question 2.B.1 
 
Point a 
The question is ambiguous. We interpreted as the requirement to identify the economic operator who 
provided the information that is present on the digital product passport . It is important to identify 
who uploads or amends certain information linked to the product.  
On the other hand, we agree to publish the list of suppliers at company level, but not to report on the 
supply chain at product level due to competition laws. 
Points b, c and e 
The questions do not offer the opportunity to comment separately on the list and quantities of 
materials and list and quantities of substances in the product. While we agree with collecting 
information on list and quantities of materials, we would like to express doubts on the substances part 
of the question.  
The SCIP database already collects information on the presence of substances of very high concern 
(SVHC) present in products in a concentration above 0.1% weight by weight (w/w)  marketed in the 
EU.  It is crucial to ensure interoperability with existing databases, among which the SCIP database or 
the Ask REACH app. Companies should not be required to enter information twice: there should be a 
system to link the digital product passport to information submitted through other portals/databases.  
Regarding other substances not under the scope of the SCIP database, current tests to detect their 
presence in textile products are based on identifying them against a specific list of substances. There 
is no test  that could tell all the substances present; the tests need to look for the substances to be 
detected. Due to the variety of chemical substances used in textile products, detecting all of them is 
complex, not scalable  and very burdensome . Furthermore, another test should be performed to 
detect the quantity of such substances in the product. We express serious doubts about the scalability 
of such requirements. 
 
Point d 
We would like to acknowledge some of the challenges  related to claims on recycled content . Some 
countries do not allow recycled content claims for all types of materials at this time (or at least make 
it very difficult (China) or have different definitions for "recycled" (Mexico)). This is difficult to manage 
from a global labelling perspective. 
 
Points f, g and j 
These information requirement s should be implemented onl y in the case that a CE marking is 
necessary. In that case, a link to website with the Declaration of conformity should be considered as 
sufficient.

Point h 
In relation to the expected lifespan of products, we would like to highlight that much depends on the 
purpose of the products and how they are used. 
 
Point i 
Information relevant for testing, disassembly, maintenance, repair or re-assembly should be collected 
at product type, not SKU level. Recycling companies, who would need to know how to disassembly a 
product, would know based on their general experience and expertise. 
 
Point l 
If companies are required to disclose environmental characteristics, they should be able to that 
through the digital product passport, not through on-product mandatory labelling. 
 
Point m 
Information on social matters should be collected  through the reporting requirements of the 
upcoming Sustainable Corporate Governance (due diligence) initiative . Including this information by 
product level does not contribute to better compliance but rather lead to more complexity. 
 
Points n and o 
Information on the origin of product components and material sources are not a top priority to enable 
circularity. Issues regarding sourcing compliance should be addressed through due diligence at 
company level. 
 
Point q 
Information on how the product should be recycled and/or handled at the end of life is important. 
Nevertheless, we want to make sure that the responsibility shall not rely only on brands/retailers to 
know how to recycle products as it is not their expertise but sorters-recyclers’ one.  
 
Additional comments to question 2.B.2 
As priorities, we selected a., b. and c. Nonetheless, we would like to highlight that e. is also as equally 
relevant as c.  
 We would like to highlight the following priorities: 
1) Data confidentiality and protection of trade secrets . Companies own the core product 
data from their design work, bill of material, supply chain management, product tests, 
etc. Only those data that are critical for any follow -up use of the prod uct, i.e. repair or 
 
recycling, should go on the digital product passport. Other data sets should be subject to 
voluntary disclosure. 
2) Interoperability. Companies shall not be asked to upload data that has already been 
uploaded on another databases/apps.  
3) Relevance of the data: companies should be required to upload only the information that 
is relevant to achieve meaningful objectives. Burden on companies shall be proportionate 
and not excessive. If a digital passport is to function as a one-stop source on article level 
for all sustainability related information across the value chain, technology standards are 
to be clarified. 
4) Support, especially for SMEs for m anaging the complexity of products and value chains 
and the quantity of data that is required. 
5) Clarity on who is responsible for updating the information on the database after the 
consumer phase.

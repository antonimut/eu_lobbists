POSITION PAPER 
Sustainable Products Initiative (SPI) 
EUROPEN position paper, accompanying answer to the Public 
Consultation   
EUROPEN – the European Organization for Packaging and the Environment - supports the overall objective of the 
European Commission’s Sustainable Products Initiative (SPI) to make products more durable, reusable, repairable, 
recyclable, and energy-efficient.  
The packaging value chain is constantly innovating to ensure th e uptake of sustainable packaging solutions, 
supporting the EU climate and circular economy goals . This is done working together with product manufacturers 
to identify new solutions aimed at advancing the sustainability of both the packaging and packaged products, while 
continuing to protect products and ensuring their safe delivery to consumers and businesses.  
EUROPEN believes that the following elements are critical for the Commission to consider in its work on the SPI:  
- Packaging sustainability should be addressed in the Packaging and Packaging Waste Directive 
o The Packaging and Packaging Waste Directive (PPWD) is the EU regulatory framework that 
determines the conditions for placing packaging on the EU market. These include sustainability 
criteria, which play a key role in enabling the uptake of climate neutral and circular packaging solutions 
in line with the Green Deal and CEAP 2.0 policy g oals. The ongoing review of the PPWD is focused 
on translating these goals into concrete regulatory measures.  
o To avoid policy and/or regulatory inconsistency,  ensure compliance with the EU Better Regulation 
agenda, and given the peculiar role of packaging  in relation to the packaged product, it is important 
to exclude packaging from t he scope of the SPI.  While t he objectives of the SPI are naturally 
interlinked with those of the Packaging and Packaging Waste Directive  (PPWD), because of the 
intrinsic relationship between packaging and the packaged product, sustainability measures specific 
to packaging need to continue to be governed by the PPWD. 
 
- Sustainable product legislation should safeguard packaging functionalities and prevent environmental 
trade-offs  
o It is important to recall packaging’s reasons to exist , which  are to  protect products’ integrity and 
consumers’ health and safety, to increase products’ shelf -life, to reduce produc t losses, to facilitate 
transport, efficient handling and distribution, to promote the packaged product and to provide 
information and convenience to consumers. Any implications of product specific legislation on 
packaging should be duly assessed to avoid risk of undermining packaging functionalities . The 
Commission services responsible for packaging legislation , as well as relevant stakeholders,  should 
be duly consulted with regards to the interlinks between product and packaging.  
o As packaging plays an im portant role to prevent product and resource waste, a thorough case-by-
case assessment of potential trade-offs between product and packaging sustainability is key to avoid 
or minimise overall negative impacts (environmental, economic and social impacts).

- Considerations on the Digital Product Passport  
o With regard to mandatory information to be submitted as part of the Digital Product Passport, a tailored 
approach is needed per product category, based on the relevant environmental characteristics of the 
different products addressed. EUROPEN understands that, in principle, different stakeholders would 
have access to the Digital Products Passports (consumers, policymakers, national authorities, value 
chain operators, etc.). If this is the case, particular attention should be given to the rights to access 
the products’ information, and the information made available should be tailored to each audience (to 
ensure for example that consumers only have a ccess to information they can understand and use) . 
The Digital Product s Passports should also avoid creating  any further burden (administrative, 
financial, etc.) on operators entering information in the database.  
o A Digital Product Passport  should not appl y to packaging but only to the product it contains. The 
dematerialisation of information currently placed on packaging could be a positive aspect of the Digital 
Product Passport, i.e. to reduce the space needed on packaging and for accompanying instructions 
that provide certain product-related information. A Digital Product Passport could for example include 
information on products’ collection and sorting requirements. We welcome  more transparent 
information sharing across the value chain.

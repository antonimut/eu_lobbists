 
 
 
 
Input to the Public Consultation on the Sustainable Products Initiative   
 
SSAB welcomes public consultation on the Sustainable Product Initiative and the EU's ambitions to reduce its 
greenhouse gas emissions by at least 55% by 2030 compared to 1990. As the updated Industrial Strategy and 
associated working document on clean steel shows, the European steel sector currently emits 221Mt or 5.7% of the 
EU's total greenhouse gas emissions, and therefore has a huge role to play. This puts the European steel industry at 
a crossroad: It needs to rethink its production process in order to stay competitive but also to support the EU’s 
climate neutrality ambition. The role of circular economy and sustainable product designs will play a significant part 
in this transition, and because of that, we need transparent and ambitious standards and definitions. As the clean 
steel document states, standards are crucial to create markets for clean technologies. Furthermore, definitions will 
help the steel sector create common rules for the calculation of life cycle environmental footprints, define classes 
of environmental performance and minimum sustainability requirements in public procurement. 
 
Unfortunately, what we miss in both the Industrial Strategy’s clean steel document and in the SPI consultation is a 
clear intention to create a robust and harmonized approach to define what true "green steel” is. Not having a clear 
and ambitious definition for ‘green steel’ can prevent the creation and the proper functioning of a market for it in 
Europe, and globally:  
 
 The lack of a proper definition risks that the use of term ‘green steel’ becomes unclear to customers. We should 
avoid a situation on the market where steel is labelled as “green” if it is produced with processes that are only 
slightly better in terms of reducing CO2 emissions when compared to traditional production methods. It risks 
favoring the status quo and could prevent investments in breakthrough technologies that can deliver substantial 
CO2 reductions (>90 %). 
 
 Furthermore, it can have a deterrent effect for consumers. ’Green steel’ will initially be more costly to produce 
than traditional steel. A proper definition of ‘green steel’ is needed to provide consumers with information on 
the lifecycle CO2 content of the steel they buy, and give more transparency on the price differences compared to 
traditional steel. Certainty and transparency will also stimulate demand for high quality steel produced according 
to ambitious environmental norms. 
 
SSAB therefore calls for an ambitious definition of ‘green steel’ that focuses on a harmonized approach. The 
reference value should focus on a cradle-to-cradle life cycle analysis, where the CO2 performance of the steel-
product and reference values are based on the PEF methodology (performance should not be determined by 
recycled content alone). The use of recycled scrap is of course important, but not the only solution. To meet global 
steel demand, Europe has a responsibility to provide the world with iron ore based steel made without CO2 
emissions. If we keep the scrap inside Europe, the rest of the world will continue blast furnace based production 
with higher emissions than we have in Europe   
 
For SSAB, ‘green steel’ is fossil-free steel. With that, we mean steel produced in a fossil-free value-chain from mine 
to steel, ie a value-chain without fossil CO2 emissions, using fossil-free electricity and no fossil fuels.

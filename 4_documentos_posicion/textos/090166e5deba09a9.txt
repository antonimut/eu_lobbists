Thesui generis right isn’t rootedinany international copyright treaty, and it is conceptually inconsistent with the main premises of thecopyright protection, such as the originality of thework.
Thesui generisrightprotectionisparticularlyunsuitablegiventhedevelopmentofthedigitaleconomywhich depends on the use of large quantities of data. Beside data generated in IoT, which werefrequentlymentionedintheImpact Assessment, for manycompanies,avitalsourceofdataistheWebitself. However, in light of the sui generis right, most web pages, including online platforms whichorganize content, fulﬁl the criteria for protection,preventing the use of this valuable data.
Inour opinion, out of thesuggestedpolicyoptions for ensuringfairness ofdataaccessanduseinB2Bsituations, the proposition to critically reviewthe Database Directive with regard to exceptions andlimitations (point (5), page 5 of the Impact Assessment)will indeed be most desirable.
The European legislator realised that thesui generis right of thedatabasemaker hamperedresearchandinnovationandreactedbyintroducingthetext anddataminingexceptioninArticle4of theDSMDirective. However, the beneﬁcial eﬀect of theexceptionis severelylimitedbytwofacts. Firstly, therightsholders areentitledtoreserve, as appropriate, therights tomakereproductionsandextractionsfor text and data mining. Secondly, text and data mining represents only one purpose out of themultitudeofwayscommercialenterprisesusethedatatoexpandtheirbusinessandgrowinnovation.Itfails to encompass less sophisticated uses such as simple data extraction which may or may not befollowed by data mining. We strongly recommend either providing an exception for data extractionwhich cannot belimitedbytherightsholders’ reservationor, inlinewithpoint (3) of suggestedpolicyoptions for ensuringfairness of dataaccess anduseinB2Bsituations,laydowntherighttoaccessanduse data whicharepubliclyavailableonwebpages. Thethirdpossibilityis toderogatethesui generisright protection regime altogether.

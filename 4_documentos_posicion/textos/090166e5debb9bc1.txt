Request by the German insurance industry on the revision of the Directive concerning 
the distance marketing of consumer financial services (Directive 2002/65/EC) 
Status quo:  
• According to Articles 3 and 4 of the Directive concerning the distance marketing of 
consumer financial services (hereinafter referred to as Distance Marketing Directive), 
potential customers shall be provided with a wide range of product and company-related 
information prior to the conclusion of an insurance contract . Additional information 
requirements are the result of  more recent European regulations (e.g.  IDD1, PRIIP s 
Regulation2, Solvency II Directive3), which have just been expanded by comprehensive 
disclosure requirements addressing sustainability issues . This huge amount of pre-
contractual information conflicts with their actual purpose, which is to provide customers 
with a clear and transparent product description and thus with a sound basis for decision-
making.  
 
Furthermore, European regulation on pre -contractual information requirements is not 
consistent, which is due to the fact that there are many overlaps . The respective 
provisions of the Distance Marketing Directive, in particular, have become obsolete as a 
result of more recent legislation  such as the Insurance Distribution Directive (IDD), 
amongst others.  
 
• Pursuant to Article  3(1)(3)(a) in conjunction with Article 6 of the Distance Marketing 
Directive, information on the existence of a right of withdrawal , in particular, shall be 
provided. The right of withdrawal allows the customer to reverse their decision within a 
period of 14 or 30 days respectively from the day of the conclusion of the contract. 
 
Based on case law of the ECJ4, the period for exercising the right of withdrawal shall not 
begin if the consumer had not been properly informed about that right . This shall also 
apply in case no information is provided to the consumer or in case the contractual terms 
and conditions and the information  are inaccurate5. The result is  a “perpetual right of 
withdrawal” which will not expire even after the contract has been fully performed. When 
drafting the Consumer Rights Directive 6, the European legislator added another 
provision on the right of withdrawal , stipulating that the withdrawal period shall expire 
after 12 months even if the consumer has not been provided with respective information 
on the right of withdrawal7. The Consumer Rights Directive, however, does not apply to 
insurance contracts. 
In Germany, this provision on the right of withdrawal is abused, which is being facilitated 
by a litigation industry.

• Pursuant to Article 5(1) of the Distance Marketing Directive, all the necessary information 
and contractual terms and conditions shall be communicated to the customer on paper 
or on another durable medium. Given the further development of digital marketing and 
distribution channels, this provision has become obsolete. 
  
Request: 
• Deletion of  the obsolete information requirements of the Distance Marketing 
Directive and a focus on standardised product information documents (such as 
IPID) would be welcome. Language, presentation and scope of these documents are 
tailored to the needs of customers when buying insurance products. They make it easier 
to compare various products and help customers to make an informed decision.  
 
• Furthermore, in order to restore legal certainty, a provision on limiting the period 
in which the right of withdrawal can be exercised should be included with regard 
to the right of withdrawal provided for in distance contracts . Article 10 of the 
Consumer Rights Directive may provide guidance in this context. It provides for an expiry 
of the right of withdrawal after 12 months even if the consumer subsequently argues that 
they have not received the respective information on the right of withdrawal. In addition, 
a transitional provision should be adopted which allows the Member States to terminate 
the right of withdrawal with respect to already existing contracts. Similar arrangements 
are also required in the Solvency II Directive which also provides for a right of withdrawal 
that is not subject to a general time limit.  
 
A mandatory pre-contractual reflection period for customers, in particular, is not required 
given t he possibility to withdraw from a contract after its conclusion . Legal certainty, 
however, would be further increased through concise model instructions on withdrawal 
inspired by Annex I of the Consumer Rights Directive. 
 
• In addition, the further development of digital marketing and distribution channels 
should be accommodated by a technology neutral design of the provisions . 
Communication of information on a distance contract on paper, above all, is outdated, in 
particular when contracts are concluded through digital means. The requirement to 
communicate information on paper, which has become obsolete, should therefore also 
be omitted in the IDD and the PRIIPs Regulation. Furthermore, it should be taken into 
account that in  the era of mobile computing, permanent availability of information will 
become more important than the place where the information is stored. 
 
In general, the German insurance industry supports repealing the Directive concerning 
the distance marketing of consumer financial services, which has become obsolete. At 
the same time, some provisions which will continue to be relevant for the insurance 
industry in the future and some updated provisions respectively should be adopted by 
the Insurance Distribution Directive (IDD) . Existing overlaps could be resolved and 
redundant provisions could be deleted in this context. Moreover, the IDD already includes 
respective provisions on the Insurance Product Information Docu ment (IPID). Consistent 
provisions would also accommodate consumers’ need for protection that exists irrespective of 
the means of distribution.

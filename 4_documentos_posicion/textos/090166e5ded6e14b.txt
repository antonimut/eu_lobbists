PNC response on the Instant Payment Consultation 
Payments are at the forefront of digital innovation in finance and will play a key role in Europe’s recovery from 
the COVID-19 pandemic – which the Commission highlight in the Retail Payment Strategy for the EU.  
 
For these reasons, PAN-Nordic Card Association (PNC) find it is crucial to have a well-functioning payments 
market, and a market in which all players compete in a fair environment. 
 
PNC supports greater choices for consumers and merchants without any bias for a specific technology solution. 
We believe that instant payments, card-based payments, electronic bank transfers or other innovative 
payment solutions (- also new not yet developed) should be chosen by the consumers and merchants 
depending on the specific payment situation – without the regulators promoting one solution over the other. 
Consumer and merchant choices should be the basis of developing new payment methods and policies, as 
preferences may differ across Member States and according to payment needs.   
 
PNC calls on the European Commission to promote greater choices for consumers and merchants without any 
bias for a specific technology solution. PNC believes that regulation should enable the development of new 
solutions as an offer – rather than promoting, or even require one specific solution. 
 
As one of many choses for the consumers, PNC do supports the uptake of instant payments as part of 
more solutions for the consumers to choose between. It is a payment method that will support a need for an 
immediate transaction from one account to the other: (i) consumer to consumer, (ii) consumer to business, (iii) 
business to business, (x)xx to xx. It should be available 24/7. 
 
For consumers it is of course crucial, that they are aware of the different attributes and features of each 
payment solution – that (i) an instant payment is irreversible and do not offer dispute rights, (ii) that payment 
cards do offer dispute rights (extensive security procedures, and grant additional protection upon non -delivery 
of products or services or when products are damaged during delivery) (iii) that a credit card comes with a 
credit limit to offer more freedom for consumer when buying new good s.  
 
For these reasons, cards are often preferred by consumers in mid- to high value payment transactions and for 
online purchases. Currently, instant payments lack in additional services offered by cards. Again, other 
solutions may offer other and/or new opportunities. 
 
PNC calls on the European Commission to facilitate regulatory steps to support a level playing field for ALL 
payment methods. This to enable the consumers and merchant to choose from a range of payment products 
that is in line with a specific payment situation – and enable the development of new innovative and secure 
payment solutions.  
 
To further support a level playing field PNC ask for an oversight system that covers all payment means and 
payment providers at an equal level. This will secure that all payments and payment systems are safe to use: 
Both as part of the European economic ecosystem, but also to the benefit of consumers and retailers.  
 
Based on this, PNC recommend that the European Commission pursues the baseline option (1) detailed in the 
recent inception impact assessment “Initiative on instant payments in the EU”. This will include monitoring the 
market evolution and assessing the effects of voluntary efforts. PNC believes this to be the most sensible path 
to support the growth of instant payments, while the payment market works to address the present need for 
potential new payment solutions.

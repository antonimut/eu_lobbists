APPLiA Position Paper on the use of 
green gases in gas -fired cooking 
appliances 
 
 
 
1.  Introduction and scope 
The home appliance sector, being one of the key actors at the end of the chain affected by the upcoming 
regulatory changes on this discussion, would like to express its views and to illustrate the potential 
challenges on the uptake of green gases for gas-fired household cooking appliances. 
 
This Position Paper should in all cases be understood as a product-specific statement, limiting its scope to 
only gas-fired cooking appliances for household use. The gas-fired segment in household appliances is 
particularly complex and the realities of each different product type might differ among each other. Gas-
fired cooking appliances however do present particularities that need a specific approach. This Position aims 
at illustrating those particularities, hoping that these will be considered by the European Commission in the 
different policy changes needed to decarbonize gas household supply for cooking purposes. 
 
 
2. Technological pathways to gas decarbonisation in European cooking patterns 
Cooking appliances will have a role to play in the decarbonization of cooking in residential homes. Given 
the diversity of spaces and home configurations across the European Union building stock, greening the 
energy supply for cooking cannot be made on the basis of one single technology or only via electrification. 
 
In this context, APPLiA believes that green gases have to play a complementary role to electrification to 
decarbonize gas-fired cooking appliances in the European Union. Green gases should not be understood 
only as green Hydrogen 1. Also biomethane and synthetic (e-)methane should be considered as green 
gases. 
 
In conclusion, there is a variety of technological pathways for gas decarbonisation  in particular for gas-
fired cooking appliances that can be applied to fit the variety of cases existing in the European building 
stock. Policies proposed in the European Union at this regard should not only acknowledge that diversity 
but take advantage of it to achieve a cost-effective climate transition.  
 
 
3. Gas decarbonization will need an European approach 
Gas decarbonization for cooking applications does not necessarily imply large technological changes. The 
gas infrastructure in most European countries is already suitable for supplying renewable gases like 
biomethane, which is already being injected directly into the gas grid, although still in small amounts.

Also, from the experimental work currently ongoing under the THyGA project and the discussions from the 
different roundtables in the Clean Hydrogen Alliance, there is increasing confidence that injections of green 
Hydrogen that stay below a 20% threshold will not imply significant changes either in the gas grid or in the 
gas-fired appliances already installed. The same applies if we refer to biomethane, as already mentioned, 
or synthetic methane. Nonetheless, this might not be the case with pure hydrogen, where gas-fired cooking 
appliances would need to be redesigned  in some of their critical parts (i.e. burners). The necessary 
investments by home appliance manufacturers will need to be secured to this purpose, and therefore a 
harmonised, European Union-wide approach to bring the sufficient certainty will be key.  
 
In short, gas-fired cooking appliances can be adapted to any kind of renewable gases, and therefore help 
to achieve a transition to a decarbonised gas supply. Nonetheless, sufficient certainty to manufacturers on 
which technological pathways will be brought forward should be provided for them to deliver the necessary 
investments to adapt their products. This product adaptation will largely differ depending on how the final 
mix of renewable gases will look like in each Member State. Different scenarios can be traced, as it has 
been done already by the roundtable on residential applications from the Clean Hydrogen Alliance. The 
different options range from focusing on one single renewable gas (i.e. hydrogen) or several ones, with the 
necessary changes to product depending on the final outcome. This is another reason why an EU-wide 
harmonised approach will be vital to achieve the necessary decarbonisation of gas supply. 
 
APPLiA Home Appliance Europe invites the European Commission and any relevant stakeholder to consider 
the points above when addressing the use of green gases for gas-fired cooking appliances.

Open Public Consultation on the Hydrogen and Gas Market Decarbonisation Package 
Status: 17.06.2021 
 
OMV acknowledges the possibility of commenting on the open publication on the Hydrogen and Gas 
Market Decarbonisation Package and adds as follows: 
 
Regulatory Framework: 
 
► OMV welcomes the revision of the Gas Directive and the Gas Regulation - where necessary - in 
alignment with upcoming legislative changes, notably such as the Smart Sector Integration Strategy, 
the Hydrogen Strategy, and the Clean E nergy Package (considering the upcoming revisions of the 
Renewable Energy Directive and the Energy Efficiency Directive). In addition, traditional natural gas 
markets are an important element to ensure Security of Energy Supply in the EU and the functioning 
of the overall energy system and will need to remain in place during the transition period and their 
well-functioning must not be undermined. 
 
► The presence of both renewable and low carbon gases enables emission reductions in the short and 
medium term, given that the rollout of generation/production of renewable gases has a longer -term 
perspective. The decarbonisation of ga ses will heavily rely on the continuous investments in 
renewable and low carbon sources in the power sector, which will also need to be addressed as part 
of the energy system integration efforts. 
 
► The regulatory framework should foster the development of commercially non-mature technologies, 
as the decarbonisation of gases will be a necessary step to achieve the decarbonisation of numerous 
industrial sectors. It should build, to the extent possible, on the successful blueprint of the existing 
European wholesale gas market, which has established well integrated markets and liquid trading 
hubs. 
 
Certificates of Origin: 
 
► To accelerate development of renewable and low carbon gases Guarantees of Origin need to be 
available to certify renewable and low -carbon gases, which can be traded virtually without physical 
delivery; such Guarantees of Origin shall also be available for off -grid renewable and low -carbon 
gases. Therefore, establishing European standards for decarbonised gases and hydrogen quality and 
a European gas GO system are important factors to develop a traded, liquid market that can take into 
account the different carbon footprints of the gases. 
 
Technology neutral approach: 
 
► We are convinced that a technology neutral approach enables an EU energy transition at least cost 
and with broad social acceptance. 
 
► Regulations need to be technology neutral as well as non-discriminatory between electrons and 
molecules.     
 
Blending / Deblending: 
 
► OMV is convinced that blending/deblending of hydrogen into the existing gas infrastructure should be 
considered as options for decarbonisation of the energy system. Blending will help to make sure the 
natural gas grid is used efficiently, but should be based on established principles and rules applicable 
to network operators and users. For this purpose, standardisation of gas / hydrogen qualities and

limitations and regional differences in blending capabilities. To enable interoperability and EU wide 
trading these should be harmonised as far as reasonably possible. 
 
Use of existing infrastructure / avoidance of stranded-assets: 
 
► We support the use of the already existing gas infrastructure for retrofitting and repurposing. The 
transition should aim on the integration of decarbonized gases into the existing gas grid (or  where 
more appropriate in developing pure hydrogen networks) and aim to achieve this integration or 
development at the lowest possible costs. 
 
► The contribution of hydrogen to the decarbonisation of the energy system can be achieved via 
blending with other renewable and low-carbon gases into the existing gas infrastructure. This provides 
a cost-efficient and fast first step. Blending leverages the benefits of the existing gas market (market 
liquidity, balancing market, security of supply) and the existing regulatory framework. 
 
► Blending does not prevent the direct use of pure hydrogen in other applications (industry, transport). 
Ideally, the market should direct hydrogen to where its value is highest.  
 
► While we acknowledge that blending requires careful consideration of any constraints, which may be 
different for different parts of the system (e.g. TSO, DSO, storage), we do not support the assertion 
that blending would be a less cost -efficient option. Blending should be considered as a good option 
complementary to a dedicated hydrogen infrastructure. 
 
► The reform should aim to foster cross-border or cross-market area cooperation – legally lead by the 
European Commission - in order to (further) develop an internal gas market but to give the Member 
States sufficient regulatory freedom to enable the ramp-up of a national hydrogen economy.  
 
► The regulation on transmission level should use well known and proven grid access on the basis of 
non-discrimination, unbundling, transparency, etc. 
 
► In order to foster a cross-border market for pure H2, it is of utmost importance to strengthen the cross-
border cooperation of operators in order to quickly connect large production sites enjoying 
geographically favorable conditions (e.g. areas of huge wind parks or other large scale production 
Entry points) with consumption sites in more remote and disadvantaged areas. 
 
► Introduction of virtual trading points (VTP) for hydrogen, similar to natural gas, for balancing and title 
transfer transactions. Experience from the natural gas market shows that using the VTPs as focal 
points for trading helps to stimulate and increase the liquidity of the traded markets. Just as on the 
natural gas market, energy exchanges and traders can use the VTPs to offer (standardised) contracts 
for trading hydrogen. The establishment of hubs and market area managers is important to efficiently 
handle network balancing and the processing of trading notifications to the VTP. 
 
► Any stranded assets for the future use of blended gas or pure hydrogen transport should be identified 
and avoided.  This is relevant for both, infrastructure users and logistics providers. Long-term 
commitments for the purpose of gas-supply and transport were made far in advance when the political 
situation was completely different from today.  Future regulatory framework shall encompass  these 
circumstances. 
 
Security of Energy Supply: 
 
► For continuous security of energy supply and resilience of the energy system in the European Union, 
the role of the existing and future gas infrastructure (incl. storage) needs to be appreciated.

We would like to add supplementary comments to certain questions of the consultation: 
 
► Question 1: 
► Gaseous fuels are enabler for the energy transition. In the context of an increasing volatile 
renewable power generation, the existing gas infrastructure (pipelines and storage facilities) 
are the necessary backbone for the required security of energy supply in Europe.  
► Both renewable and natural gas will be necessary to achieve the 2030 decarbonisation targets 
of the EU. These energy carriers will be needed to decarbonise sectors which are difficult to 
electrify and will provide flexibility in a high-renewables electricity system.  
► Gas infrastructure is crucial for the uptake of renewable and decarbonised gases, such as H2 
& bioCH4, contributing to decarbonise all sectors where molecules & electrons can be 
complementary (hard-to-electrify sectors), but also heating or transport.  
► Due to the increasing share of renewables in the energy mix and the seasonal nature of 
generation, gaseous fuels/ gas storage facilities will be of utmost importance for Europe's 
security of supply in the future. 
 
► Question 3: 
► Natural gas will play an important role be supporting the energy transition and continued use 
by consumers. New legal framework shall provide necessary tools and legal certainty for new 
technologies while ensuring system decarbonisation and sector coupling. 
► A regulatory framework has to set incentives for climate-friendly gases. To strengthen the role 
of climate-friendly gases and to increase their competitiveness a stable regulatory framework 
and an effective market design are needed, while ensuring security of supply (duplication of 
legulation should be avoided).  
► The main elements are: 
► A regulatory framework for hydrogen-only networks, building on the blueprint of natural 
gas (grid access, unbundling, Virtual Trading points (VTPs), market-based congestion 
management and balancing arrangements etc.). 
► A regulatory framework for blending of renewable and decarbonised gases, such as 
H2 & bioCH4, in the natural gas grid. 
► A common legal definition of climate - friendly (renewable, decarbonised and low -
carbon) gases. 
► Guarantees of Origin to certify renewable and low -carbon gases (RED II revision), 
which can be traded virtually without physical delivery. Such Guarantees of Origin shall 
also be available for off-grid renewable and low-carbon gases. 
► Barriers for climate - friendly gases have to be removed, while ensuring the key 
regulatory principles of unbundling, third -party access, transparency, non -
discrimination and monitoring of the market.  
► The reform should aim to foster cross-border or cross-market area cooperation in order 
to (further) develop an internal gas market but to give the Member States sufficient 
regulatory freedom to enable the ramp-up of a national hydrogen economy.  
► The benefits are: 
► Lower cost of decarbonising the energy system by avoiding inefficient support 
schemes. 
► Innovation stemming from competition in the markets.

transport/store renewable, decarbonised and low-carbon gases. Coordinated and cross sector

► Question 22: 
► We would like to note that energy efficiency programs generally reduce total energy demand 
for consumers but have a smaller effect (if any) on peak flow, and thereby on the size of the 
required infrastructure. 
► These plans should build on existing infrastructure. In addition, the market outlook and

to be transported far to end-user. 
► We understand the discussion on the avoidance of stranded assets being in direct connection 
with the Energy Efficiency First Principle, therefore we would appreciate also to acknowledge 
the possibly stranded costs also on the network users side due to the unexpected political 
decisions such a climate law etc. 
► Only a holistic market approach will enable an energy transition at least cost. 
 
► Question 28:

► Question 31:

should kick in.

H2 blending standard in current methane networks. This requires a big effort for network 
operators and consumers. Stable and clear gas quality parameter are how ever essential for 
all market parties.  
► To ensure a stable and tradeable gas quality, the right balance between the step -wise 
decarbonization of existing natural gas infrastructure and the establishment of pure hydrogen 
grids is necessary. 
► At the same time  an neutral technology approach is necessary as in the meantime all the 
existing technologies are in the startup situation.

► Lack of clarity about future infrastructure operator roles (TSO, DSO, SSOs) and the related 
question which activities can be done by these entities and if and to which extent H2 activities 
are subject to unbundling rules, if any.  
► Clear, consistent certification system (extension of the obligation to issue Guarantees of Origin 
for renewable and low -carbon hydrogen to establish an EU -wide credible documentation of 
the green value of renewable and low -carbon hydrogen) that allows for the EU wide and 
harmonized trading of such GOs.  
► The development of clusters of private hydrogen networks with no clarity about how these will

► Regulation for costs of retrofitting (blending/deblending) - adequate financing incentives &

► Question 32: 
► Today we do not have any EU -wide regulatory framework for hydrogen market and it is 
necessary to develop one as soon as possible. 
► We believe that a common EU wide standard for H2 production and certification (GoOs) are 
necessary to ensure trading of H2.  Imports from EU Member States and third countries must 
be recognized and labelled appropriately to ensure a level playing field 
► Harmonized blending rules on TSO level to ensure cross-border flow in case the neighboring 
TSO agree on blending are required. Integration of Natural Gas storages and H2 storages into 
infrastructure planning which is necessary for a cost -efficient and timely energy transition in 
Europe. There is a lack of clear, pragmatic, and realistic standards for the definition of 
renewable or low -carbon hydrogen including certificates or/and Guarantees of Origin from 
third countries accepted within the EU. 
 
► Question 34: 
► A common approach is needed in which an EU legislative framework outlines key regulatory 
principles, inter alia neutrality of network operation, conditions for third party access, cost 
reflective and market compatible network tariffs, treatment of private networks etc. 
► As market conditions may evolve differently in member states, flexibility is needed to decide 
when the implementation of network regulation should kick-in. Some of the more detailed rules 
could be developed stepwise, e.g. the creation of more detailed EU- wide technical rules could 
be left to later, or Member States shall be allowed to develop such rules earlier where needed.  
► To this end, market monitoring & analysis, based on agreed EU -wide indicators can help to 
keep track for any required regulatory action. Exemptions/derogations could help to have legal 
certainty in case this is needed for investment purposes. 
► If rules are developed stepwise, it still needs to be ensured that the most important principles 
(e.g. non-discriminatory grid access, non-discriminatory and transparent tariffs) are followed.   
 
► Question 40: 
► We believe that it is important to evaluate each pipeline on an individual basis. In case of direct 
lines we do not see any need for regulation. It could lead to  unnecessary administrative 
barriers and deceleration of the market development.

► Question 42: 
► We would appreciate if the focus for operators of P2G facilities does also include infrastructure 
provider such as Underground Storage Operators.  
 
► Question 44: 
► The development of a dedicated hydrogen network will require public support (grants or 
subsidies) to network operators and should not be subsidised by users of the natural gas 
network. 
► Existing gas infrastructure operators should be allowed to develop and operate a hydrogen 
network without the need to change their legal and organisational structure to facilitate 
retrofitting gas assets into hydrogen service and to capture operational synergies of 
transporting natural gas and hydrogen. 
► New horizontal unbundling rules might jeopardise the early development of a hydrogen 
infrastructure and consequently of a hydrogen market. 
► Cost allocation rules should ensure that the costs of developing a hydrogen network do not 
result in higher tariffs for users of the natural gas network. 
► When retrofitting existing assets has benefits for the users of the natural gas network and for 
the hydrogen network users, those benefits may be shared amongst all users. 
 
► Questions 59 – 62: 
► We prefer to leave these questions open as it is too early to provide an appropriate answer. 
We ask for a technology neutral approach, energy efficiency first principle and transition at 
least cost. 
 
► Question 63: 
► The market rules for access to a dedicated hydrogen infrastructure should focus on promoting 
investment and providing regulatory certainty for investors with the aim to accelerate the 
development of a liquid competitive hydrogen market. 
 
► Question 75: 
► The role of storage for use of renewable and low -carbon gases shall be promoted in the 
revised Gas Directive and Gas Regula tion. Furthermore, storages should be included in the 
development plans. 
 
► Question 78:  
► The more efficient coordination on gas quality between member states can be achieved when 
enhancing cross border coordination tools. The specific gas quality standards shall remain 
with the member states during the ramp -up phase. A fully harmonized application of gas 
quality standards across Europe can only apply in a mature market and where dedicated pure 
hydrogen networks are in place for cross-border trade. 
 
► Question 98: 
► Although we do not understand, what is meant by “energy-specific measures” we emphasize

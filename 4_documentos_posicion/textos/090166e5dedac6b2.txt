Additional comments on the Public Consultation on Hydrogen and Gas Market Decarbonization Package  
 
 
Eni welcomes the opportunity to express its view on the Hydrogen and Gas Market Decarbonization Package 
initiative and would like to provide some additional comments on the main issues raised by the Commission 
in the Public Consultation. 
 
 
On the role of renewable and low carbon gases 
 
 The gas industry will play a key role in the short, medium and long-term in all sectors, as a result of 
the increasing use of renewable and low carbon hydrogen, biomethane and synthetic methane, as 
well as natural gas + CCUS.  
 
 We believe that gas is an enabler for the decarbonisation objectives. In the short term, natural gas 
can contribute by replacing more carbon-intensive fuels in power generation (i.e. coal-to-gas switch). 
In the medium and long term, gas can further contribu te by reducing its carbon -intensity via the 
implementation of CCUS projects and by increasingly integrating with renewable and low -carbon 
gases (e.g. biomethane and “blue” hydrogen from natural gas with CCUS). 
 
 Low carbon hydrogen , produced by n atural gas with CCU S, is key to start developing a hydrogen 
market able to integrate renewable hydrogen as soon as possible. The take -off of r enewable 
hydrogen production will very much depend on cost position improvements and, above all, on the 
increased availability of renewable electricity not needed to decarbonize the power sector. 
 
 We see the need to revise the gas regulatory framework so as to allow the penetration of renewable 
and low carbon gases and the development of a hydrogen market  and to provide a stable and 
predictable environment and the correct signals to investors. Setting a level playing field for all 
technologies, avoiding distortions and taking an integrated view when looking at the development 
of infrastructure should be the main principles to adhere to in this process.  
 
 The new Hydrogen and Gas Package should set the rules for the market to be efficient, competitive 
and to develop in a technologically neutral way.  
 
On consumer choice 
 
 The Hydrogen and Gas Package should also ensure consumer protection and empowerment.  On this 
we believe that the  full empowerment of consumers  would be enhanced by the  removal of any 
regulated supply tariff.   
 
 In particular, it would be necessary to introduce standardized and effective tools to provide relevant 
information to final consumers. Guarantees of Origin should be the preferred one to inform them on 
which kind of energy product they are supplied with and on the related carbon footprint. 
 
To support energy efficiency a wider access for consumers and suppliers to con sumption data  
(historical + detailed data once smart meters are fully deployed) would be crucial.  This is important 
to involve final customers either in the flexibility market (demand side response) or for efficiency 
purposes (customers’ behavior, education) to foster energy savings.

On infrastructure planning 
 
 Eni supports the adoption of combined  infrastructure development plans for power, gas and 
hydrogen, as foreseen by the sector integration EU strategy, so as to achieve better coordination and 
improve efficiency. Plans should be developed based on a joint scenario. 
 
 We also agree that the alignment of national and EU plans in terms of frequency, timeframe and 
possibly scenarios should be evaluated.   
 
 National development plans should include information about where the installation of new 
electricity production, consum ption, storages or electrolysers would reduce network investment 
needs, as an information only, without legal consequences.  
 
 
On hydrogen infrastructure and market 
 
 Eni supports a gradual approach to the development of a regulatory framework for hydrogen. 
General principles (such as unbundling, TPA, tariff setting criteria) for hydrogen network regulation 
should be set at European level but regulation should be progressively introduced accordingly with 
the actual development of market and infrastructure.  
 
 Exemptions from general principles and regulation (once in place) should be possible  only under 
defined conditions, as it is currently envisaged for natural gas infrastructures. 
 
 When setting tariffs for hydrogen networks, in case of repurposing of existing gas infrastructures  and 
similarly in case of new -built “hydrogen ready” gas infrastructu res, in order to fairly allocate costs 
among present (gas) and future (hydrogen) network users, the regulatory framework should provide 
that TSOs and DSOs cannot charge the  corresponding (e.g.. the additional investments for having a 
“hydrogen-ready” network compared to a pure gas network) to current gas network users. 
 
 Also, with regard to the blending of hydrogen into the gas networks, the costs for the investments 
needed to accommodate the secure injection of hydrogen s hould be recovered from their 
beneficiaries.  
 
 When the level of contracted capacity is too low to effectively recover costs from hydrogen network 
users, other measures should be adopted to lower hydrogen network tariffs (e.g. financing support 
and other t ype of subsidies)  so as to  avoid cross-subsidization between gas network users and 
hydrogen network users and distortions and to increase transparency.  
 
 
On access of renewable and low carbon gases to the existing methane gas networks  
 
 The access to networks for renewable and low carbon gases should be ensured, taking into account 
their decentralized production, by enhancing coordination between TSOs and DSOs in the network 
development and operation, and namely in balancing. 
 
 The blending of hydrogen into the existing gas networks is a complementary cost -efficient option 
which can provide a contribution to the decarbonization of the energy system, particularly in the 
early stages of development of the hydrogen market.

On the need to tackle other increasing pressing issues in the gas market  
 
 The current reform should also address the risk of stranded gas infrastructures, to anticipate the 
potential impacts on European Security of Supply and on final prices.  
 
 Fostering the repurposing/retrofitting of existing gas infrastructures to integrate renewable and low-
carbon gases would contribute to partially address the stranded asset issue.  
 
 Also, a revision of the current gas transmission tariff system should be evaluated. Under the present 
tariffs framework, the expected decreasing network utilization rate  entails an increasing risk of 
having excessively high transmission tariffs. This risk is even more relevant due to the gradual expiry 
of the existing long-term capacity contracts and the parallel shift towards short-term bookings. If not 
properly managed, the above mentioned spiral effect on transmission tariffs would exacerbate the 
pancaking effect affecting intra UE cross-border trade, with impact on market prices convergence. In 
addition, it is important to prevent the risk of decommissioning for those pipelines facing low 
demand, but that are nonetheless necessary both for the EU Security of Supply and for preserving an 
effective competition on the market. 
  
 Solutions should be evaluated and carefully assessed in order to  properly deal with the problem , 
while still preserving the attractiveness of the European market for extra-EU producers and avoiding 
to unduly penalize the competitiveness of natural gas as an energy vector.   The approach outlined 
in the questionnaire (Q68) is pointing to a  possible long-term solution, but the specific details need 
to be fully assessed and calibrated in order to ensure that  the competit iveness of gas at end 
customers level and the attractiveness of the EU gas market  are not negatively impacted . While a 
structural reform is addressed and implemented , Member States should be granted the possibility 
of introducing short-term solutions reforms to address the problems that have already emerged at 
national and at cross-border levels.

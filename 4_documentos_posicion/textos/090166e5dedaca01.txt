Subject : Open Public Consultation on the Hydrogen and  Gas Market Decarbonisation Package  
 
Pursuant to the sections embedded into the Public Consultation’s Questionnaire , it is common 
ground that EU electricity and natural gas market rules have been developed in parallel over the last 
20 years, without any significant distinctions as regards the above markets’ regulatory oversight. In 
order to achieve more integrated and competitive EU electricity and natural gas markets, a further 
alignment of their governing rules is required. Given the recent revision of the Electricity Directive 
and Electri city Regulation adopted in 2019 [Directive (EU) 2019/944 on common rules for the 
internal market for electricity and Regulation (EU) 2019/943 on the internal market for electricity], 
which has brought discrepancies in the institutional set -up between the electricity and natural gas 
sectors, our company strongly embraces the need to revise the legislative framework for the natural 
gas sector, in order to align its provisions to those already adopted for electricity, and most 
importantly bring forward a more  flexible approach regarding the devel opment of natural gas 
markets and new natural gas infrastructure. 
Specifically, in relation to the regulatory framework concerning the licensing of direct lines, 
the existence of ambiguity as regards certain aspects of the authorization procedures, poses the risk 
of jeopardizing their development, by rendering them exposed to the imposition of 
disproportionate measures. In particular, setting the prior refusal of access in the national gas 
transmission system by its  operator as a precondition for the licensing of a direct line  may lead 
Member States to abusive interpretations, thereby resulting in non-objective, discriminatory and 
non-transparent licensing practices. Our company’s suggestion, as regards this issue l ies in the 
abolishment of the above precondition, in order to achieve fair and proportionate access to the 
natural gas market for all undertakings and accomplish a safe and stable supply through direct lines, 
under justified and proportionate costs, which result to an overall increased social welfare.  
Based on the above, with regard to the concept of direct lines , as established both in the 
Electricity and Gas Directives (Articles 7 and 38, respectively), ELPEDISON believes that the revised 
Gas Directive should adopt provisions to safeguard the above rationale and at least ac commodate 
the amendments introduced to the Directive 2009/72/EC on electricity, through the adoption of the 
Directive (EU) 2019/944.  The latter added, inter alia, the provision that Mem ber States shall take 
the measures necessary to enable all producers and electricity supply undertak ings established 
within their territory, to supply their own premises, subsidiaries, and customers through a direct 
line, without being subject to disproportionate administrative procedures or costs. The said addition

The mere fact that a direct line in relation to either electricity or gas con stitutes an 
independent system, complementary to the interconnected one, should be taken into consideration 
and be subject to a distinct reference in the future revised Gas Directive. To that end, our company 
is of the opinion that the absence of an explicit reference to the non-imposition of disproportionate 
administrative costs and procedures in the revised Gas Directive leaves a wide margin of 
appreciation in the Member States, which are free to block the development of direct lines in the 
absence of a  refusal to access the regulated network/system and at the same time impose 
disproportionate pro cedures and disproportionate costs to access the regulated networks, This 
practice might lead to unjustified burdens for the development of direct lines resulting to increased 
costs for consumers. 
The imposition of any kind of fees and restrictions for reasons of public interest should be 
regulated in a concrete, transparent and foreseeable manner, so that the holder of a license for a 
direct line or any other  new LNG/gas infrastructure may be fully aware of the overall financial 
obligations he shall be requested to meet, at least concerning the fees and relevant costs arising 
from the regulatory framework in force.   
Most notably, the regulated fees should re flect the spirit and intent of the Gas Directive, 
ensuring that their establishment is based on a non -discriminatory, reasonable and proportionate 
frame-work allowing new natural gas infrastructure (direct lines included) to be developed where 
the existing regulated network/system cannot cover future consumption needs in the most cost -
efficient manner. That being said, the Directive should aim to fac ilitate and promote the 
development of the above new infrastructure by enabling the Member States to take neces sary 
measures for their establishment ensuring their viability under the provision of Article 36 par.1e, by 
completing and clarifying the wording in a way that explicitly prohibits Member States from posing 
extra charges and costs to new infrastructure in  the cases when the new infrastructure does not 
deter the efficient functioning of the regulated network/system. Thus, the Directive’s purpose 
should focus on the harmonization of network access conditions, a principle utterly contradicted by 
the enforcement of disproportionate fees or charges and costs, especially in the cases when these 
are imposed ex-post and without the necessary transparency.  
In view of the above, ELPEDISON recommends that the imposition of any kind of fees and 
measures upon the revision of the Gas Directive should be regulated in the context of the Directive’s 
intent, as described above. In particular, specific criteria should be laid down with particular regard 
to the principle of competition enhancement, social welfare increase, p roportionality, as well as 
transparency and predictability. Such reform should prevent discrimination between market 
participants, through the imposition of a disproportionate burden on some of them, thus taking into 
account the spirit of solidarity that the Directive aims at.

AEGIS Europe’s position regarding the envisaged  
Instrument to deter and counteract coercive actions by third countries 
 
AEGIS Europe is an industry alliance that brings together more than 20 European manufacturing 
associations committed to manufacturing in the EU on a truly level playing field ensured through rules-
based, free and fair international trade. Our members accou nt for more than €500 billion in annual 
turnover, as well as for millions of jobs across the EU. 
1. Coercive practices must cover actions exercised against the interests of EU economic 
operators 
AEGIS Europe calls for the clarification that "coercive actions" include those that target EU economic 
operators, in addition to those actions directly aimed at government authorities.  Put another way, at 
least actions targeting major and/or otherwise strategic EU economic interests should be considered 
"coercive actions" directed at Member State and/or EU authorities. 
2. The scope of coercive actions that trigger countermeasures must be sufficiently broad 
The scope of the instrument should not limit actionable  economic coercion to general trade or 
investment restrictions against the EU or individual Member States, but must include for example: (i) 
targeted tariff or  investment measures against the EU, its Member States or EU operators; (ii) 
administrative delays in obtaining certifications/customs clearance  or other targeted non -tariff 
measures; (iii) pressuring one or more third countries to act against the interests of the EU, its Member 
States or EU operators; etc. The list of conduct constituting economic coercion should further include 
any measures taken in retaliation for EU government measures (such as, for example, EU trade defence 
investigations and/or measures or measures taken under the envisaged Foreign Subsidies instrument) 
or for actions of EU operators in support of EU government measures, and must in any event be 
expressly identified as non-exhaustive.  
3. Interaction with the Blocking Statute (Regulation 2271/96) 
The scope of the instrument should cover any coercive action that is not within the scope of the 
Blocking Statute.  Indeed, the Blocking Statute is a tool that deals with (only) one specific type of 
coercive government measure, one which sanctions certain countries and those operators based in 
other countries which do not respect those sanctions. 
4. Investigations must be able to be conducted swiftly, and countermeasures imposed swiftly, 
while allowing relevant stakeholders to provide their input 
Coercive behaviour is often designed to put maximum pressure immediately or within a relatively 
short period.  Accordingly, the Commission must be able to act ex officio and to impose 
countermeasures (including interim measures) quickly in order to be effective.  At the same time, 
there needs to be a recourse for Member States and private operators if the Commission does not 
take action in a timely manner.

5. Investigation powers of the Commission must be as broad as possible while protecting 
anonymity 
The proposed legislation must allow the Commission the power to require the cooperation of any 
operator having an EU interest and/or conducting economic activity on the EU market in its 
investigations, and to impose effective sanctions in cases of non-cooperation.  At the same time, the 
Commission must protect the anonymity of EU operators which cooperate in its investigations in 
order to minimise the risk of further coercive measures. 
6. Possible countermeasures must be as broad as possible while remaining proportionate 
The list of countermeasures possible under an anti-coercion tool must not be limited in advance.  The 
standard applied should be that generally set out for the enforcement of EU rules, that 
countermeasures must be effective, proportionate and dissuasive.

Re:  Consultation on the Hydrogen and Gas Market Decarbonisation Package 
Dear Commissioners: 
On behalf of Hexagon Composites ASA and its subsidiaries (“Hexagon”), I am writing to provide 
comments on the Consultation on the Hydrogen and Gas Market Decarbonisation Package 
(“Consultation”) to be used as the European Commission (“Commission”) prepares and reviews 
its revision of the Gas Directive and Gas Regulations.  We appreciate the Commission’s efforts 
to ensure that the gas market framework contributes to the achievement of the greenhouse gas 
(GHG) emission reduction targets set in the European Green Deal and the 2030 Climate Target 
Plan, as well as the measures proposed in the Energy System Integration and Hydrogen 
strategies.   
Hexagon, based in Norway, is a manufacturer of clean energy solutions, offering a wide range of 
mobility, industrial and consumer applications, including natural gas storage and delivery 
systems, Type 4 composite high-pressure cylinders for hydrogen, natural gas and propane, 
complete electric vehicle systems and battery packs for fuel cell electric and battery electric 
vehicles.  Hexagon has 23 global locations, over 1000 employees, and facilities in Norway, 
Germany, USA and Canada, including the world’s most important clean energy markets in 
Europe, Asia and North America.  Hexagon is supportive of the Commission’s goal of increasing 
the use of renewable and low carbon gases and encourages the acceleration of decarbonisation 
of the transportation sector.  Hexagon is clean fuel-agnostic and believes, as the Consultation 
states, that technologies should compete to achieve decarbonisation targets in a cost-efficient 
way.  In the context of this Consultation, Hexagon focuses its comments on renewable natural 
gas (RNG), as Hexagon believes this technology’s potential is underappreciated and thus 
handicapped from competing fairly.  Hexagon believes that clean air is a right, not a privilege, 
and respectfully offers the following comments for the Commission’s consideration. 
1. Renewable natural gas is essential to a low carbon future. 
Hexagon agrees with the Commission that immediate action is needed to clean and 
decarbonize the transportation sector.  RNG offers the lowest carbon intensity solution of any 
transportation fuel.  RNG, derived from organic waste, has a carbon-negative footprint.  Unlike 
other biofuels, when produced from landfills, wastewater and agricultural waste, RNG does not 
require large amounts of land to be deforested and does not displace food production.  Indeed,

The potential to produce RNG exists across the globe by taking naturally occurring methane gas 
created from organic waste, capturing it, then using it to fuel traditionally heavy-carbon 
transportation applications.  And RNG has other advantages.  The development of RNG 
facilities contributes to the economic strength of the European Union (EU) economy by 
supporting the agricultural industry.  Specifically, it provides producers with new revenue 
streams while addressing growing solid waste disposal concerns.  Further, natural gas 
infrastructure across Europe, which is already designed to support RNG, is well developed.  
RNG is the logical fuel to accelerate the decarbonisation of the EU transport sector.   
2. Renewable natural gas exists in sufficient volumes to support widespread 
adoption. 
RNG mitigates global warming by capturing methane for energy, thereby reducing harmful GHG 
emissions.  Concern is often raised, however, about the volume and availability of RNG.  These 
concerns are unfounded.  Recent studies show that we use only 5% of the potential RNG 
supply.  The untapped potential of RNG is due in large part to a lack of robust and supportive 
regulatory policies and legislation.   
Only 5% of the potential RNG supply is unlocked because of a weaker competitive position 
compared to fossil alternatives.  The extent and degree of producing RNG can easily multiply by 
over 100% if governments enact favorable regulations and policies to support its adoption. 
Indeed, there is no raw material supply constraint for an RNG uptick in the medium and heavy-
duty vehicle sectors in Europe.  Put another way, there is a 20x potential for RNG supply ramp-
up using sustainable sources.   
Hexagon has a strong track record of providing OEMs, dealers, and fleet operators best-in-class 
product development, manufacturing, service, and support.  In recent discussions with Hexagon, 
truck fleet owners, OEMs and distributors have indicated that a stable regulatory framework with 
long-term foresight (i.e., 10 years) is a key requirement to achieve full potential of RNG in 
transport.  If achieved, RNG can facilitate the biggest CO2 emission reduction in the medium 
and heavy-duty vehicle sector between 2020 and 2030.   
3. Renewable natural gas must be addressed separately in regulations and 
legislation to maximize the EU’s decarbonisation potential. 
It is vital that we transition away from fossil fuels in transportation.  In many circumstances, 
Hexagon recognizes the benefits of and supports the transition to zero-emission vehicles, 
particularly in the light duty sector.  However, Hexagon is clean fuel agnostic and promotes 
diversification among fleets.  There is no one size fits all clean fuel solution.  In particular, 
electrification of high fuel use transportation modes such as Class 8 heavy-duty trucks is not 
feasible.  Furthermore, medium- and heavy-duty vehicles contribute 25% of transport CO2 
emissions and are a CO2 hard-to-abate sector.  RNG offers the only economically viable, 
scalable solution for this sector and others.  Its use takes carbon out of the environment,

Where RNG has been recognised and encouraged as a separate category in regulation, 
including in certain states in the U.S., it has been successful in replacing carbon-emitting 
alternatives at scale in hard-to-abate sectors.  Hexagon’s experience with fleets and OEMs

We encourage the Commission to recognize the distinct, sustainable attributes of RNG to unlock 
this technology and support its use across the EU.  Thus, Hexagon encourages the Commission 
to amend the Gas Directive and Gas Regulations to separately recognize and incentivize the 
use of RNG as a carbon-negative fuel.  This will make a major contribution towards achieving a 
goal that is also central to Hexagon’s mission:  Clean air everywhere.

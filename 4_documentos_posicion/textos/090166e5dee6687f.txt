TELEFÓNICA COMMENTS ON EUROPEAN COMMISSION’s  
INCEPTION IMPACT ASSESSMENT FOR A DATA ACT 
 
 
June 2021 
 
 
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY  
Telefónica supports the overall objective of the cr eation of a European model 
fostering data sharing and re-use across sectors an d Member States. For that, any 
future legislation should focus on clarifying how d ata sharing, data pooling and data 
re-use can take place as all relevant actors (Publi c Administration, businesses and 
individuals) need to understand how data can be vol untarily shared whilst upholding 
rights at stake (personal data, IPRs, trade secrets…). 
 
Anonymization (in case of data originating from ind ividuals) should be one of the 
pillars of any data sharing, as it will ensure data sharing is done in full compliance with 
data protection rules. In case of sharing personal data, GDPR applies.  
 
Telefónica understands the need to promote greater data availability and reduce the 
existing technical, legal and economic barriers to data exchange. The success of a 
European data space will depend on creating an entire ecosystem that encourages 
the voluntary exchange of data , from the public and private sectors. It is not ab out 
imposing additional obligations, but about creating a sustainable ecosystem in which 
all parties win.

1.- B2G DATA SHARING 
Telefónica supports the overall objective of building a flexible framework for improved 
access to and use of data, making data sharing a re ality for B2G and B2B in the 
interest of EU citizens. 
 Currently, with some positive exceptions, little sustainable B2G 
data sharing is taking place. This has been clearly  manifested during COVID-19 
pandemic, where companies have been requested, in a crisis mode , to share data with 
Public Administrations. Telefónica has engaged acti vely with Public bodies at local, 
national and EU level to provide anonymised aggrega ted mobility data to help in the 
fight against the spread of covid-19 in full compliance with EU legislation.  
But this example cannot be extrapolated as the way ahead to ensure that 
sustainable B2G data sharing models generalise . Due to the urgency of the 
situation, companies have shared data pro-bono, but this is not sustainable in the long 
term, as companies have heavily invested in data an alytics and need to see their 
investment preserved. Moreover, the pro-bono aspect  of the initiative has prevented 
companies from adapting to the specific needs of th e European Commission and 
companies have shared data as they had it available . This has created a significant 
burden on the teams of the Joint Research Centre of  the European Commission to 
make all data interoperable and has led to a subopt imal solution because due to the 
needed common dominator NUTS 1 granularity in the data was lost. 
Instead of ad-hoc urgent responses for crisis situations, Public Administrations should 
understand that long term public policies based on data insights might be an 
important factor for reducing public expenditure an d freeing up budget for a 
sustainable data-sharing model, benefitting the pub lic sector (less budget), citizens 
(less taxes) and companies (fair compensation for c ollecting, anonymising, 
processing and analysing the data). If Public Admin istrations really wish to perform 
evidence-based policy making, they will need to rel y on best quality data driven 
services.

Telefónica agrees that we need to define at EU level the necessary basis to 
facilitate B2G data sharing . This basis should not be through imposing mandato ry 
B2G data sharing, but on the contrary working in order to incentivise voluntary data 
sharing schemes  through: 
- reduction of transaction costs and perceived risks for data suppliers 
- tax incentives 
- fostering standardisation of contractual provisions and interoperability of data 
formats 
- launching pilot projects that would allow businesses to give valuable feedback 
on established principles to assess their robustnes s in practice and 
consequently lead to market evidence as a basis for future considerations. 
 
For the EU to build a strong data economy and socie ty, at large, EU cannot rely on 
mandatory obligations for companies to share data w ithout a fair 
compensation . Where is then the incentive for companies to inve st in robust data 
business, if they face the threat that these investments will not be rewarded?  
In order to have data available, a flexible, future -proof regulatory privacy framework 
that preserve privacy and, at the same time, foster innovation, is crucial. Neither data 
economy nor data sharing will emerge if no data is available due to rigid and no future 
proof privacy rules.  Finding the right balance is of essence. 
In parallel with the Data Act, there is the Data Go vernance Act (DGA). The DGA sets 
the “conditions” (not obligations) for re-use of pu blic data by private companies. If 
DGA builds on the principle of “voluntary data shar ing”, for public data (which has 
been created with public investment), even more the same principle should underline 
any B2G data sharing in the future Data Act. 
If the future Data Act imposes B2G data sharing obl igations without fair 
compensation, these obligations -complemented with the provisions of the DGA- will 
trigger the sharing of the data (initially provided  by private companies to Public 
Administrations) to other businesses (G2B data sharing). Thus, any possible business 
model sustainable in the long term will disappear a s companies will not have any 
chances to see their investments rewarded by future  demand of their services, by 
public or private entities.  
Instead of maintaining voluntary schemes where pote ntial customers of data driven 
services negotiate a data sharing agreement under c ommercial conditions, 
companies will be imposed a requirement to offer th e same services to public and 
private entities.  
Despite the principle of data use limitation, by which governments are not allowed to 
share business data onwards if not defined in advan ce in an agreement, the risk

2.- B2B DATA SHARING 
B2B data sharing  and data pooling are nascent markets that will continue to grow as 
companies from different sectors are steadily movin g towards data-driven models, 
and clear business opportunities for sharing data between companies will continue to 
appear. Therefore, it is of utmost importance to preserve contractual freedom as a 
guiding principle , or otherwise innovation on big data will suffer dramatically. 
In addition, B2B voluntary data sharing should be extremely respectful of privacy and 
data protection rules in case individuals are the origin of the data (as opposed to 
data generated from machine sensors). During the pa st few years, Telefónica has 
heavily invested in an Anonymisation solution, building a Big Data platform to create 
anonymised, aggregated insights in a privacy-preserving way. 
  
Competition rules  need to be updated in order to facilitate those co operation 
agreements among competitors aiming at B2B data sharing and data pooling. Indeed, 
fostering such agreements among competitors will he lp companies offer innovative 
digital services, contribute to the development of AI and enable European Data 
spaces. In addition, B2B data sharing and data pool ing agreements will allow 
stakeholders to compete with non-European ones with in the digital world, as well as 
to resolve structural competition problems in digit al markets. Therefore, Telefónica 
welcomes the ongoing EC work towards the revision o f the Block Exemption 
Regulations for horizontal cooperation agreements a nd the Horizontal Guidelines. In 
this line, Telefónica is calling for a new safe harbour for data sharing and data pooling  
agreements through a new Block Exemption Regulation, providing stakeholders with 
full legal certainty to ensure that  such kind of agreements, under certain conditions, 
comply with Article 101.3. TFEU. Telefónica also be lieves data sharing and data 
pooling agreements should be considered pro-competi tive in the Horizontal 
Guidelines.

B2B data sharing and data pooling are aimed at laun ching innovative services and 
resolve market failures in digital markets. Taking into account that digital markets are 
very fast-moving and dynamic and companies are the most interested in accessing, 
re-using and sharing data, no mandatary provisions for a “fairness test” are necessary 
as they would only create hurdles for such cooperat ion. In the same vein, no 
mandatory provisions on data access and use for non -personal data are necessary. 
Imposing new rights and obligations to data access and use without fair 
compensation will not bring the right conditions to  incentivize companies to engage 
actively in more data sharing. On the contrary, com panies obliged to share data will 
stop investing in such data and those companies wit h a “right” to access other 
businesses’ data will obtain less quality data driven services.    
Mandatory access to data provisions are proposed wi thin the DMA  especially in 
situations where this would open-up secondary markets for complementary services. 
Indeed, the DMA already tackles the concern of abse nce of (or reduced access to) 
some key inputs in the digital economy, such as dat a contemplating for those 
companies that qualify as online gatekeepers an obligation to provide business users, 
or third parties authorised by a business user, fre e of charge, with effective, high-
quality, continuous and real-time access and use of  aggregated or non-aggregated 
data, that is provided for or generated in the cont ext of the use of the relevant core 
platform services by those business users and the e nd users engaging with the 
products or services provided by those business users.   Any obligation on data sharing 
should be confined to online gatekeepers that benefit from access to vast amounts of 
data that they collect while providing the core platform services as well as other digital 
services as recognized in the DMA.  
 
3.- DATA PORTABILITY   
Telefónica agrees that the right to data portabilit y as defined by Article 20 GDPR is a 
key element to provide the proper balance between t he Fundamental Right to data 
protection and innovation across the digital value chain. Telefónica’s data strategy 
aims to “unlock the value of peoples’ data for them  and for society as a whole” by 
providing users with transparent tools to easily ma nage their data. In this context, 
Telefónica is working on innovative ways to allow c ustomers to share their data with 
partners. Data portability is at the heart of any broader data sharing policy.  
However, it is of outmost importance to avoid any o verlaps between different set of 
rules, GDPR, DMA, DGA, Data Act. This can be very c ounterproductive as a company 
or an individual will have very different legal ins truments to understand how to 
exercise the right to data portability.

GDPR has established the basic principles of the right to data portability in the context 
of personal data. It is not a question of applying it mutatis mutandi  to non-personal 
data.  
Data connections are an essential differentiating factor in the market and companies 
will find a way to allow customers to port the pers onal data that is most relevant to 
them, in the format that makes more sense in light of the technological 
developments. However, technical standards for data  portability might be 
fundamentally different if we refer to health data or to electricity consumption.    
In addition, EU policy makers should consider the r isk that given the digital capacity 
of tech giants, they are likely to faster obtain be nefits from reusing data than any 
other EU industry. This might be the case of ported data based on the data portability 
right as well as in the larger B2B context. EU comp anies can become net donors of 
data which will ultimately benefit the same tech giants. 
 
4.- COMPETITIVE CLOUD MARKETS 
Telefónica believes that a true European single market for data will only see the light 
if European secure and sustainable infrastructures can support it. The growth of 
connected devices, evolving into smart connected sy stems, will generate an 
exponential increase in data management requirement s that a more traditional 
centralised cloud will not be able to manage. A key competitive advantage will be edge 
networks. There is an opportunity for telcos to sol ve that problem by developing the 
vision of the network as a platform, while overcomi ng challenges like industry 
fragmentation and data sovereignty. 
  
Cloud, as well as edge computing, will be a major e lement of the future connectivity 
architecture (5G and beyond). A new processing laye r is emerging, one that will 
redefine not only telecoms but also the IT sector i n the future. Edge computing is 
becoming an industry strategic point, where collective effort could be beneficial. Edge 
computing is a sound European alternative to the cu rrent of offer of the US 
hyperscalers. It will be of utmost importance to en able a distributed cloud-edge 
infrastructure, potentially by federating/ intercon necting operators’ resources and 
homogenising APIs, catalogues and tasks. 
Having said that, Telefónica questions which is the added value of a binding obligation 
for cloud service providers to offer data portabili ty. Such right is already enshrined in 
GDPR, and the DMA is also proposing portability and interoperability requirements on 
big platforms, including cloud service providers ac ting as gatekeepers, for their 
business users. Overlapping provisions will not pro vide more legal certainty, but on 
the contrary will bring confusion for companies and individuals.

5.- SAFEGUARDS FOR NON-PERSONAL DATA IN THE INTERNA TIONAL 
CONTEXT 
Basically, European Commission (EC) is considering to expand the requirements for 
the cross border transfer of personal data outside the EU to non-personal data. EC is 
concerned that non-personal data, which might be of high value for companies, might 
be misused if they are not protected under the same conditions as in the EU.  
 
While we agree with the basic principle that the protection of data shall travel with the 
data, which so far it has been very focused on personal data, it seems wrong to try to 
extend the rules applying to personal data to non-personal data.  
 
Rules on trade secrets, Intellectual Property Right s already protect non-personal 
data. Make a parallelism between rules that were cr eated having in mind the 
specificities of personal data (data portability, cross-border data transfers…) and rules 
applying to non-personal data might not be the right way to ensure better protection 
of EU industrial data.  
 
Main problem is not the lack of a protection mechanism for non-personal data (which 
is currently protected by trade secrets, by IPRs or  sui generis right for databases…), 
but the lack of enforcement of that system of prote ction. This will not be solved by 
prohibiting non-personal data from traveling outsid e the EU, but by reinforcing the 
enforcement of the protection mechanisms that are a lready currently available to 
European organisations. 
 
CONCLUDING REMARKS   
 
Voluntary Data Sharing  based on contractual freedom should be maintained as 
guiding principle. B2B data sharing is a nascent ma rket that will continue to grow as 
companies from all sectors are steadily moving towa rds data driven models. The 
same applies for B2G data sharing as Public Adminis trations will understand that 
using privately held data could reduce public expen diture and free up budget for a 
sustainable data sharing model, benefitting the pub lic sector (less budget), citizens 
(less taxes) and companies providing data (fair com pensation). This is the virtuous 
cercle that needs to be created and not only in tim es of crisis, but with the aim to 
develop better evidence-based public policies in the long term.  
 
What is necessary is to set the right environment t hat stimulates data sharing (such 
as tax incentives) while minimising the associated risk and providing legal certainty, 
commercial drive and transparency rights. Any regul atory action at this stage is 
premature as the market is still developing. Regula tory sandboxes for exploration or

training programmes for public administrations are more adequate approaches than 
imposing mandatory data sharing on private companies with regard to the data they 
generate. Otherwise, innovation on big data will su ffer dramatically as European 
companies will see their investment in data penalised. 
 
As a true believer of data sharing, Telefónica takes its responsibility as relevant player: 
our support from day 1 to COVID-19 spread models by  sharing anonymous mobility 
matrixes with Health Authorities, has been instrume ntal to correlate mobility 
patterns with pandemic evolution. Telefónica believes this is just the tip of a massive 
iceberg which is still to be capitalized. We cannot  be reactive to these emergencies 
but proactive in all the areas where wellbeing of o ur citizens can be impacted, either 
to make it to grow or to protect it from threats. I nvestments are needed in this area 
and need to be structural, considering that best ef fort is not an option if we want to 
capitalize this opportunity for European citizens, probably one of the biggest we have 
ever faced. 
 
Inclusiveness in reaping the benefits of digitalisa tion for Public Administration, 
businesses and citizens should be the guiding princ iple for a European approach to 
data while, at the same, framing a smart model that creates the ecosystem and legal 
certainty that the Data Strategy needs to thrive, without being perceived as a barrier 
that prevents innovation and progress, but, on the contrary, generating a friendly 
research and technological development environment committed to securing both 
innovation and individuals’ and businesses’ rights.

Contribution to the Data Act Roadmap 
 
To the attention of the European Commission DG CNECT G.1 (Data Policy and Innovation), 
 
Brussels, 24 June 2021 
 
News Media Europe  is the voice of the progressive news media industry in Europe, 
representing over 2,500 news brands in print, online, radio and TV. 
 
Our feedback will focus on the purpose of the Data Act from the point of view of news media 
companies and the review of the Directive 96/9/EC on the legal protection of databases.  
 
Interest at stake for news media companies 
 
The review of the Database Directive stands at the intersection between the European 
Commission’s Data Strategy  and Intellectual Property Action Plan . It is a relevant issue for 
the news media sector which relies on intellectual property as one of its main sources of 
revenues, but also as an extension of the news media sector’s digital industry producing and 
selling data collections  (e.g. specialized press, business publications 1). It is also relevant for 
press publishers proposing specific information to users through classified advertising online 
services for example regarding jobs, apartments , houses on sale and for rent. In these 
classified advertising online services, the advertisers pay for the service, which can in addition 
be remunerated through more advertising space.  
 
While “original” databases are protected by copyright2, the Database Directive also protects 
investments in “non-original” databases (e.g. compilations of press articles, collections of 
figures, listings of advertisements, etc) u nder a “sui generis” right 3. The sui generis right is 
important for press publishers to prevent for instance the free extraction of their databases 
by third parties, such as search engines4.  
 
As such, the sui generis right is the only protection to classified ads online services  as 
mentioned above (e.g. real estate sales or rental5, car rental6, etc) which do not reach a status 
of copyright protected works. These platforms r equire continuous investments by the 
publisher to technically develop the service and ensure that the collection of new data (e.g. 
information related to apartments, houses, jobs, cars, etc) is smooth and relevant to the user.

Therefore, the modification of the sui generis right could represent a risk to the protection of

Views on the objectives and methodology of the Data Act  
 
We understand that the Data Act aims at the broader distribution of rights for the increased 
use of data. In this regard, the European Commission has identified as a challenge to the data-
driven economy “the uncertainty about rights on the data” and “the costs of licensing” (p.8). 
To address this problem, the Roadmap proposes: 
• Clarification of the scope of application of the sui generis right to increase legal 
certainty with regards to machine-generated data; 
• Definition of a specific access regime to facilitate trading in and access to databases in 
a balanced manner; 
• Aligning the sui generis right exceptions to more recent EU copyright instruments. 
 
While we agree with the above-mentioned challenges – in particular how the sui generis right 
would interact with the newly adopted  DSM Directive’s  text and data mining exception  
(Articles 3 and 4) and press publisher right (Article 15) – we need to take full consideration of 
press publishers’ intellectual property rights ove r their databases. In this regard, we  are 
satisfied that the European Commission acknowledges potential risks on fundamental rights, 
including the impact on property (p.8). This impact deserves to be thoroughly analysed in the 
preparatory work of the regulation proposal. 
 
Preliminary suggestions 
 
• The r eview of the Database Directive must be conditional upon an effective 
enforcement of the press publisher right.  The adoption of the press publisher right  
represents an important step in recognizing publishers’ investments in press content 
(Recital 55 DSM Directive) and protect ing its re-use by online platforms (Article 15) . 
However, the late DSM Directive transposition across Europe currently deprives press 
publishers of a key legal basis to protect and monetise their press content online. In 
addition, the attempt from tech giants to circumvent the application of the 
neighbouring right - as illustrated by the French competition case opposing Google

• The review of the Database Directive  must take into account the recently adopted 
text and data mining exception in the DSM Directive.  Under the new exception, 
research organisations and cultural heritage institutions are allowed to reproduce and 
extract press publications strictly for scient ific purposes  (Article 3 .1), as well as 
commercial players unless press publishers expressly opt out (Article 4 .3). Press 
publishers are also allowed to protect the security and integrity of their databases 
where the works are hosted (Article 3 .3). It is important to keep the delicate balance 
struck in the DSM Directive in the reviewed Database Directive between on the one -
hand, supporting research and innovation, and on the other, protecting rightsholders’ 
investments in content and databases.  
 
• In line with  the European Commission’s objective of fostering data-driven 
innovation, the Data Act must bring a clear benefit to businesses, including  news 
media companies . News Media Europe has long been arguing in favour of more 
transparency and data sha ring from gatekeeping platforms in relation to press 
content. This reasoning was recently supported by the French Competition Authority8. 
We are convinced that better copyright management  relies on  enhanced data 
transparency, something clearly impeded by  large platforms’ self -preferencing 
practices. Aware that these considerations go well beyond the Database Directive, we 
would like to point out for the purpose of the Data Act that data sharing must be a 
two-way road: copyright exceptions must be accompanied by data-sharing obligations 
from large tech distributors in relation to the consumption of publishers’ own press  
content. Enhanced d ata collection is  indeed crucial for news media companies  for 
research, innovation and ultimately bringing better products to their audience.  
 
• Finally, the  European Commission should carefully consider interactions between 
the Data Act and the creation of a “media data space ” under the Media and 
Audiovisual Action Plan. While the Data Act is not media -specific, we understand it 
could influence the framework regulating the proposed media data space . Such 
framework should allow for data-based innovation in the news sector (e.g. pooling of 
content, audience data, content met a-data, data on users’ behaviours , etc ) while 
providing a safe and IP -compliant infrastructure. Business-to-business exchanges of

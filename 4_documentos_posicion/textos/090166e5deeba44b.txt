Contribution of GANVAM to the roadmap for the Data Act: 
Data Access & Connectivity 
 
As the longest established and most representative Spanish association of the automotive distribution 
sector, GANVAM -the Spanish Association of Motor Trade, Repair and Parts - welcomes this 
opportunity to participate in the roadmap for a regulation tha t will have great impacts on our 
members. 
 
We represent a key sector for the Spanish economy, with the creation of 347,000 direct jobs, €92,000 
million of turnover and 42,000 SMEs and 35 ,000 self -employed workers . The trends in Mobility, 
Automation, Digitalization and E-Mobility (MADE) are propelling disruptive change in the long term 
for this sector. For instance, the control of customer and vehicle data, the growing importance of the 
online channel, the remote diagnosis of breakdowns and the electric vehicle will have a decisive impact 
on the sector, and in particular on the way of relating to customers, on the distribution model 
developed by each brand and on the role that distributors, both official dealers and repairers as well 
as independent or multi-brand dealers and repairers, will play in it. 
 
Of all these trends , digitalization is the one with the greatest impact due to the risk of 
disintermediation through direct sales by vehicle manufacturers. Connectivity will lead to the creation 
of new business models, but also the entry of new operators.  By 2030, 38% of the vehicle fleet and 
100% of those less than 5 years old will be connected. Since May 2018, the eCall emergency call device 
has accelerated this process of connecting the vehicle with the world. The functionalities are extensive 
and will grow exponentially in a short time: transport services and traffic information, service stations 
and recharging points, parking, insurance policies based on driving habits, or the predictive sale of 
vehicles and the remote diagnosis of breakdowns (e.g.: engine error codes, oil or tire pressure, etc.) 
and even their remote repair.  
 
With an adequate regulatory framework , we can prevent th e negative risks and allow for the 
developments of innovations that benefit economic operators, consumers, and society. If we share 
connected vehicle data, it will favour the creation of a single, open and neutral environment on which 
the different solutions associated with connected, safe and sustainable mobility  will be designed, 
tested and deployed . Th ese solutions include defining the deployment of electric vehicle charging 
infrastructure; new proposals for multimodal mobility;  development of 5G infrastructure, boosting 
artificial intelligence and data analytics; or the emergence of new business models. 
 
Access to data by all stakeholders involved in the sector is essential if we want this digital transition 
to be fair. Official and independent distributors are investing heavily in developing services that meet 
customer expectations. However, vehicle manufacturers are limiting access to vehicle -generated 
data needed to innovate and provide services on a level playing field, leaving out multi-brand repairers 
and dealers of other brands. Access to data will determine a company's market position. For instance, 
in-vehicle sensor and telematics systems generate and transfer all vehicle metrics and conditions to 
the vehicle manufacturer, who combines them with personal data and can offer predictive

maintenance and remote diagnostics to the vehicle owner.  This means that the manufacturer can 
become also a services provider, effectively excluding dealers and repairers from the market. 
 
Hence, in our sector there is a n imbalance between these two groups because of an unfair access to 
data. In line with the inception impact assessment, the Data Act must promote fairness in data sharing 
between manufacturers and dealers/re pairers and facilitate the access to data by the latter. The 
manufacturers' proposal of the so -called "Extended Vehicle Conc ept" and the neutral server is 
unacceptable for the distribution sector. Under this scheme, manufacturers control access to 
information, they know in detail what their competitors request, with what intensity, for what purpose 
and at what prices.  
 
As argued, this model would risk free competition if manufacturers employed their servers to decide 
on access to data by denying it, delaying it, deciding on the quality or functionality of the data, or  
simply monitor ing competitors. This limited access to dat a also causes negative impacts on the 
consumers: 
1. First, by limiting competition, this practice slows down innovation, investment, and 
deployment of new tech nologies (for instance, electric vehicles or the development of new 
connectivity systems) that can contribute to sustainability or road safety goals. For instance, 
Mobility as a Service (MaaS) depends heavily on access to dynamic vehicle data. Data, 
resources, and functions must be fully accessible to all players in the mobility ecosystem under 
the same technical conditions,  
2. Second, consumers might see their choices limited on digital services . During the process of 
selling the vehicle, the manufacturer has a privileged position that it could use to exclude its 
dealers and independent third parties from the digital  services market. By accessing the 
vehicle's control screen, it could redirect the customer to its subsidiaries instead of the brand's 
official network, the official repairers of another brand or an independent repairer. 
3. Third, the choice of consumers is further limited need as they cannot decide which providers 
have access to their data. GANVAM supports the true portability of the data, through which 
users -who are needed for the generation of data- can choose who can access their data to 
provide a better service.  
 
A direct, immediate, unrestricted, real-time access that is not monitored by the manufacturer must 
be guaranteed. Therefore, we propose the Secure On-board Telematics Platform (S -OTP). Together 
with a large alliance of distribution organizations, drivers, companies, etc., we call for what the 
Regulation (UE) 2015/758 concerning type-approval requirements for the deployment of the eCall in-
vehicle system announced: an in-vehicle system based on an interoperable, standardized, secure and 
open platform, which can guarantee future in-vehicle services and applications. 
 
The distribution sector considers that the safety and privacy of the users is the priority and, contrary 
to what vehicle manufacturers say, the S-OTP can guarantee this security. The interoperable platform 
guarantees at least the same high level of safety, security, accountability, and data protection as the 
manufacturers themselves  while safeguarding true consumer choice, competition, innovation and 
road safety. A hacker can just as easily attack the manufacturer's closed system. In fact, open systems

are technically more secure than clos ed systems. Furthermore, even if the brands reject an open 
system for security and environment , they have already validated third parties (Apple or Android) 
access to vehicle systems. 
 
Additionally, security could be improved by restricting access to certified professionals authorized by 
the competent authority and by anonymizing data. The personal data and privacy of users must be 
protected, but the personal data of the connected vehicle can and must be anonymized so that it can 
be shared and used. Therefore, whereas non-personal data should be able to be used professionally , 
personal data from the connected vehicle should be anonymized and shared between companies 
and with the public authorities for the benefit of all stakeholders invo lved, including the consumer -
who is in control and wants greater connectivity.  
 
A regulatory framework that ensures this model of open and direct access to vehicle data is urgently 
needed. Manufacturers will not remove these barriers, as it i s still the case  with technical vehicle 
information. Therefore, it is necessary for the European Union to adopt a regulatory framework that 
establishes a Secure On-board Telematics Platform that: 
a) guarantees the security and protection of the data; 
b) provides direct, immediate, and unrestricted access to vehicle data; 
c) is not under the manufacturer's monitoring; and, 
d) works in real-time, or with the minimum possible latency that the technology allows. 
 
This framework must be coherent with the European data strategy and its objective of creating a fair 
model in which data flows freely within the EU. Individuals will have full control of their data and their 
consent will be required, while relevant economic operators will have access to the data contributing 
to economic growth and technological innova tion. Regarding th e fa irness of the model, a special 
attention m ust be put on SMEs.  Support programs must reach SMEs  and must promote their 
digitalization in the distribution, sales, and after-sales sector, so that they are not excluded from the 
data economy.

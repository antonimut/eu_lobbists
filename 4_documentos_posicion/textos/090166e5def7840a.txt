 
 
EFPIA Comments on Inception Impact Assessment for  
Data Act (including the review of the Directive 
96/9/EC on the legal protection of databases)  
 
EFPIA welcomes the chance to comment on the Inception Assessment for the Data Act. We note the 
objectives of the Act to: 
- Promote fair reliable and transparent B2G data sharing. 
- Enhance B2B data-sharing by addressing fairness, legal certainty and harmonisation 
- Improve competitiveness in the Cloud market through assessment of self-regulation and 
enhanced interoperability to facilitate data portability. 
- Introduction of essential standards for smart contracts 
- Measures to mitigate risks arising from foreign government access to non-personal data. 
 
General Points 
At several points, the Inception Assessment defines the scope of specific proposals in the Act as relating 
to non-personal data. EFPIA would like to note that there is no definitive definition of non-personal data 
and that many datasets include a mixture of personal and non-personal data. This underlines the need 
for consistency of approach, particularly in relation to the proposals for international data transfers, 
which are intended to parallel those being developed for personal data.  
Data-Sharing 
The pharmaceutical industry has extensive experience of sharing data with the public sector. Our 
members are required by legislation to provide extensive details of new medicines to the medicines 
regulator and to report adverse events after medicines are made available to the public. Through official 
policy, initiatives taken by the industry and the process of scientific publication, much of this data is also 
made available to other economic operators.  
EFPIA is a partner with the European Commission, in the Innovative Medicines Initiative. Within the 
individual projects of IMI, extensive data-sharing takes place between the public and private partners 
based on innovative contracting and structural arrangements, designed to advance the public interest 
while protecting proprietary interests and respecting personal data protection. IMI projects have been 
an important mechanism for bringing SMEs into participation in research. We recognize that there may 
be opportunities to integrate, harmonise and scale up data-sharing arrangements in this area, but also 
express our readiness to share what has already been achieved. 
EFPIA welcomes the opportunity to share this experience but would also like to emphasise that in the 
pharmaceutical sector, access to public sector data (such as data held in health systems) is critical to 
innovation and is presently extremely difficult, due to legal fragmentation and uncertainty. While this is 
outside the scope of the Data Act, EFPIA would like to emphasise the importance of private sector 
access to public sector data and shared public-private data spaces.

The above points suggests that the Commission should consider a number of different modalities of 
data-sharing, as suggested in the report of the High-Level Group. A “one size fits all” approach to 
incentivizing data sharing is unlikely to work. Willingness to share health data depends on a number of 
factors (type and nature of the data, protection level at the site of storage, the extent to which the data 
is needed to compete successfully in the marketplace and has more value if kept proprietary or 
alternatively the extent to which more value can be derived from pooling data with that of others to 
derive meaningful insights from it). It also relies on  
• maintaining a strong RDP (regulatory data protection) and trade secret right regime in Europe, 
which continue to recognize the importance of incentivizing the generation of valuable data of 
the type in scope.  
• Appropriate reward for the maintenance and curation of data assets 
• a technical solution and governance process e.g. in EHDS, which would allow companies to 
voluntarily and safely share certain commercially relevant confidential data (CCV data) in certain 
circumstances 
• an equitable system of rewards for sharing data. 
• Confidence in the adequacy of the data protection and cybersecurity procedures of data 
partners 
 
In the light of the extensive data-sharing that already takes place in the healthcare sector, EFPIA 
believes that the Commission should particularly focus on improving the conditions for advancing 
voluntary data-sharing, through dialogue with stakeholders in the sector. Any mandatory data-sharing 
obligations should be clearly based on an overriding and important public health interest and 
proportionate to the goals being pursued, with the possibility of requests being reviewed.  
 
EFPIA notes the emphasis that the Commission places on “providing a harmonisation of horizontal 
modalities for access to data, which could apply to data access rights established in specific sectoral 
rules”. Measures of this sort and high-level principles can facilitate access to data, but we would urge 
the Commission to consult fully with stakeholders to ensure the right balance between horizontal and 
sectoral guidance and regulation. 
 
Portability 
The Inception Assessment also suggests that the portability right under GDPR has lacked 
operationalization due to the absence of interoperability formats. The Commission anticipates benefits 
will flow from the creation of such formats. These benefits seem to have more to do with enhancing 
competition than with enhancing privacy. EFPIA agrees that it is important to examine the barriers to 
the wider sharing of data from devices, would like to re-state the importance of getting the privacy 
aspects rights to preserve public confidence and believes that, alongside promoting competition, the 
Commission should also consider the facilitation of scientific research as a goal in this area.

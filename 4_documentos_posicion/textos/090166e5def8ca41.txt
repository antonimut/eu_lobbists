Microsoft feedback on the European Commission’s Data Act Inception Impact Assessment (IIA) 
Microsoft welcomes the opportunity to respond to the Inception Impact Assessment (IIA) for the Data Act and 
its ambition to foster data sharing and data-driven innovation. Greater access to, and re-use of, data will play 
a key role in the EU’s economic future, given the potential for enhanced data access and data sharing to fuel 
economic growth across all sectors, especially within the context of economic recovery. Microsoft has already 
taken steps to promote data sharing, as reflected in our Open Data Campaign, along with principles to guide 
our data collaboration  efforts, based on the themes of openness, usability, empowerment, security, and 
privacy and we’ve gained several insights, published in a blog earlier this year. 
Improving B2G and B2B data sharing 
To encourage voluntary B2G data sharing, the Commission should consider a flexible voluntary framework to 
facilitate the access and use of data in a B2G context , while safeguarding privacy, security, and commercially 
sensitive information. Potentially introducing a right for governments to access privately held data for “public 
interest” purposes, absent robust safeguards,  could raise legitimate concerns around sensitive information. 
Any data access requests by public bodies should remain proportionate a nd limited to the minimum extent 
necessary for the performance of a specific , well-defined purpose. Moreover, if such mandates were to be 
introduced, requests should be directed to the data co ntroller, as data processors should not be forced to 
share customer data with public-sector authorities, particularly without customer knowledge or consent, nor 
risk violating their contractual obligations. 
Fostering trust in voluntary B2B data sharing through incentives and investments is more likely to stimulate 
innovation and economic growth than imposing mandatory requirements. The Commission should work with 
the private sector to support and encourage the use of privacy-enhancing technologies to promote trust in 
the data sharing ecosystem, while safeguarding privacy, security and commercially sensitive information. As 
stated in the IIA, the development and use of model data sharing agreements would help reduce the “frictions” 
that today deter organizations from sharing data. The Commission could consider endorsing the use of certain 
model agreements for re-use of the Commission’s own data, similar to its decision adopting certain Creative 
Commons copyright licenses for purposes of its document re -use policy.  Clearer guidance on legal 
compliance would be helpful as organizations may be reluctant to assume liability risks of using 3rd-party data. 
Such issues can be addressed by endorsing industry standards and model data governance frameworks. In 
addition, intellectual property and access laws should not be interpreted to unnecessarily restrict legitimate 
access to and use of data.  The Database Directive should be carefully assessed to understand its impact to 
the EU data sharing ecosystem and provide clarity on the scope of sui -generis right with a lens to promote 
greater data access and collaboration and the corresponding innovation and growth  the overall proposal 
aims to stimulate.   
Supporting data portability 
We underscore the importance of data portability and believe that the Commission should support ongoing 
industry efforts to develop voluntary standards for data taxonomy, data sharing, data quality, and descriptions 
of datasets, and should continue to foster REST APIs for data access.  To enhance efficiency, these should be 
developed through consensus-based, market-driven, fair, and transparent processes that build on existing 
standards from ISO /IEC and other leading international standards bodies . As modern ICT applications are 
built on a rich and constantly evolving set of resources, we  would advise against  mandating technical 
specifications for  data portability  which could stifle innovation, create fragmentation  and regulatory 
divergences in global markets  and complicate the ongoing work of the SWIPO process and the 
implementation of the CoC’s on cloud switching and data portability. Requiring all cloud service providers to 
use specific technologies or data formats could ultimately reduce choices for customers  and slow the 
development of more innovative offerings.

Potential restrictions on international data transfers of non-personal data 
The Data Act should contribute to enabling , rather than restricting,  the free flow of data, to facilitate cross-
border data sharing and leverage its collaborative benefits. Companies and researchers rely on international 
data flows to enhance the ir global competitiveness, develop pr oducts, and gain insights o n issues that are 
global in nature, such as the COVID-19 pandemic. Imposing additional restrictions on data flows would have 
a detrimental impact on Europe’s abilities to compete internationally and hamper its ability to create global 
champions of the data economy by creating new costs for local companies and directly impact European 
customers. The Commission should utilize long-standing international agreements and treaties regarding IP 
(such as TRI PS and the Berne Convention), which are currently enforced and have enabled effective  data 
collaborations with like -minded countries by providing adequate protection for data protected by IP and 
trade secrets. Proactive steps should be taken to ensure that  IP laws are not interpreted to unnecessarily 
restrict the cross-border flow of data. Microsoft recently announced new protections for our public sector and 
enterprise customers who need to move their data from the EU . These include a contractual commitment to 
challenge government requests for data , and we therefore support the option for CSPs to notify users of a 
request for access to data by foreign authorities.

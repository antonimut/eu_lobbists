Belron: Submission on Data Act Roadmap 
 
Belron® is the world’s leading vehicle glass repair, replacement, and recalibration service provider, operating in over 25 
countries in Europe (and 40 countries globally) with brands such as Carglass®, Autoglass®.  In 2020, Belron served 
approximately 15 million customers worldwide.  Over the last 5 years, Belron has seen a significant increase in the presence of 
forward-facing cameras (which are housed behind the windscreen) linked to ADAS-related safety systems, which require 
recalibration after a windscreen replacement.  Recalibration services provided by Belron require a connection to the vehicle 
diagnostics and access to critical safety-related data.  The increase of safety technology within vehicles correlates to an 
increase in data being generated and transmitted to vehicles and an important need to have bi-directional access to that data 
for Belron and other aftermarket providers in the automotive industry.   
 
In that context, Belron supports the work being done on the Data Act, and the drive to open-up data access on a business to 
business (B2B) basis.  Belron welcomes the approach outlined in the roadmap for the Data Act and considers the Data Act a 
unique opportunity to set in place key principles that will safeguard and enable access to data for all businesses.   Belron 
considers the initiative as a regulation of critical importance to ensure Europe can reap the benefits of the data economy. 
 
As a participant in the aftermarket automotive industry, Belron considers there is a real need to address issues arising from 
B2B data sharing, to ensure greater availability of data to businesses that provide services to equipment that they have not 
necessarily manufacturers (Aftermarket Providers).  As more and more products become connected via the Internet of Things 
(IOT) and produce increasing amounts of data, a holistic approach to access to data – both generally but particularly for 
Aftermarket Providers (across all industries) is critical.  Access to data is important to ensure and enable undistorted 
competition among all market players (i.e. between OEMs and Aftermarket Providers) which ultimately benefits consumers, 
and to truly allow for innovation, as access to data is a key facilitator in the ability to create new service offerings previously 
unthought of.  A key aspect that must be incorporated is the concept of bi-directionality – to enable Aftermarket Providers to 
utilise the data received and send data back, whether that be to offer services or to potentially undertake remote servicing – 
naturally in compliance with any GDPR and cybersecurity requirements.   
 
Belron is supportive of the Data Act as a piece of horizontal legislation that outlines key principles, but reiterates the need for 
the Data Act to be supported by complementary sector specific legislation.  Belron considers sector specific legislation to be 
urgently needed in the automotive sector as it concerns access to connected car data. 
 
We elaborate below on certain key points of the initiative: 
 
 Principle-based regulation: Belron believes B2B data sharing should be underpinned by principles that allow for a fair 
distribution of data across value chains. They should include: 
o Transparency: on the data that is being generated and made available. In the automotive sector this is crucial – 
for both vehicle manufacturers (VMs) and other independent service providers (ISPs) to ensure fair competition.

o Fair competition and non-discrimination: data access should be made available to both OEM and Aftermarket 
Providers on the same conditions, at the same time, and with the same ability to use the data – including subject 
to data privacy considerations – the opportunity to be able to connect with the owners of the equipment that 
generates the data.   From Belron’s perspective, all those competing to offer services in the aftermarket should be 
competing on a level playing field with OEMs. 
o Full protection of IP: what is done with the data should then be for the service provider to decide and full IP rights 
protected, therefore protecting and encouraging innovation. The clarification of the application of the Trade 
Secrets Directive should align with this fundamental principle. For Belron, these key principles lay the foundation 
of a framework on data access, but importantly, must be complemented in their technical requirements with 
sector specific legislation. 
Complementarity with sector specific legislation: Belron agrees with the approach outlined in the roadmap that while the 
Data Act could provide horizontal modalities for accessing data, sector specific legislation establishing data access rights as well 
as technical requirements is needed. For the automotive industry, this is an urgent need.  Specific rules on access to connected 
car data, through both a review of the existing Type Approval regulation and/or through the introduction of sector specific 
legislation, that sets out the specific conditions for mandating access to data is required. 
 
Whilst related to the Data Act, the two initiatives should be discussed separately and drafted with clearly defined scopes (and 
Belron appreciates sector specific legislation would be in alignment with the broad principles of the Data Act). The Data Act 
should not slow down the sector-specific initiative. 
 
Fairness in contracts: When data is made available on B2B basis through contractual arrangements, the Commission could 
issue sector-specific model contract terms in order to set fair conditions for the sharing of data.  Belron notes the success of 
such approaches in other areas and considers that use and access to data (for connected cars in particular) is an area where an 
approach would lead to significant B2B efficiencies.  Belron considers the subject of fees can be included, along with dispute 
settlement.  Belron would support an independent body established by the Commission to oversee this – as with sector and 
subject matter expertise, such a body would be able to efficiently resolve disputes, while at the same time indicate areas of the 
sector specific legislation that may be adjusted and/or updated, to account for ongoing technological change. 
 
Portability: Belron believes consumers should be informed and in control with respect to the data they generate/hold and be 
able to choose who to share it with and for what purposes. Belron would welcome attempts in the Data Act to facilitate a 
better implementation of Article 20 of the GDPR, including through technical specifications and requirements.  
 
Future-proof: The Data Act needs to ensure flexibility, and be principle based to be future-proof. As a horizontal piece of 
legislation, the Data Act should remain fit to accommodate new data-driven services as technology evolves. For the same 
reasons, it should also be technologically neutral.

About Belron

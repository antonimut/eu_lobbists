General comments: 
 
We are pleased to submit additional feedback and comments on the European Commission´s 
consultation “Public consultation on instant payments” as a further part of the EU Retail 
Payments Strategy. The following aspects are important in our view for further consideration 
in addition to our answer in the corresponding questionnaire. 
 
We welcome the political efforts to support all the current developments in a preferred 
market-driven way. Our member banks support the idea of instant payments and we were 
involved in the development of this new fast payment method “SCT Inst” from the beginning. 
Today the vast majority of our member banks are participants of the EPC “SCT Inst”-Scheme 
and offer appropriate payments to their customers. So instant payments have been well 
adopted by the market now for the first relevant use cases. New solutions and services have 
been implemented or now are under development (e. g. actual initiatives with several 
solutions for further use cases). The existing scheme for SEPA instant payments (SCT Inst) is 
adequate to efficiently and sufficiently cover the current market needs.  
 
Within this context, instant payments should not be understood as equivalent to the well-
established mass payment instruments, which are cost-efficient for customers and banks. We 
consider instant payments as an further developed enrichment of existing “traditional” / 
”standard” payment instruments (e.g. regular credit transfer and / or direct debit) with its 
different characteristics and relevant market needs. The processing and handling of instant 
payments requires an instant operation of all background processes (e. g. real-time booking, 
AML checks, et cetera) and special real-time control mechanism (e.g. regarding fraud and 
liquidity management). Therefore the implementation and handling of instant credit transfers 
is technically and operationally much more complex than the handling of normal SEPA Credit 
Transfers or other mass payment instruments. 
 
From a market perspective, instant payments address specific demands and should be 
considered as a payment instrument in its own right. The customers on the payer and payee 
side should have the choice.  
 
Considering the above mentioned well established mass payment instruments we do not see 
any need for further legal intervention in this area or regulation of fee levels, in particular it 
would be an unjustified intervention into the price building mechanisms of the market. 
 
 3 
In order to support the further market driven way and establishment of instant payment an 
extension of the exemption period of the SEPA regulation 260/2012 (article 4 para. 4), e. g. by 
additional two years would be useful.  
 
Especially to have the relevant cross-border reach it is important to ensure interoperability 
between the different infrastructure providers of CSM’s.  
 
In this context, a mandatory participation by all types of banks, especially smaller or highly 
specialised institutions, do not offer electronic payment accounts for customers continues to 
be inappropriate. Today our member banks with relevant customer structure are participants 
of the EPC “SCT Inst”-Scheme and offer appropriate payments to their customers. 
 
We would therefore request the European Commission to very carefully consider any further 
actions, if any, in this field. A general market-driven approach should be the preferred way 
forward. 
 
 
Additional topics in the corresponding questionnaire 
 
Credit transfers as a common payment instrument with a final charcater: 
 
In principal a credit transfer is legally final after its initiation by the payer and its 
execution by the payers bank. This fundamental feature and legal quality of credit 
transfers – standard or instant – should not be put into question, neither for business 
policy reasons nor legally. Due to efficiency reasons in payment processing, there shoud 
not be any connection between the payment transaction between payer and payee 
and the underlying business. Regarding consumer protection it should be considered 
that the finality of credit transfers constitutes a defining feature of the payment 
instrument “credit transfer”. It provides legal certainty for both sides. For the payer to 
finalize the payment and for the payee when providing services. It is therefore an 
important factor in the competitiveness of credit transfers. 
 
Special customer needs concerning a refund right after paying at PoI are already 
covered with today exisiting payment methods, e. g. direct debit payments. 
 
The payment solution at PoI itself – not the payment instrument – should cover the 
customer needs of refunds. Therefore, there should not be a general refund right, that 
is legally connected to the payment instrument “instant credit transfer” payment. It 
should be open to the market participants to define additional product features in the 
different payment solutions. 
 
IBAN Verification Services (e. g. “Confirmation of the Payee / CoP”): 
 
In our view, IBAN Verification Services cover only very specific and local scenarios 
just recently appearing in only a few countries. Within our banking group and in 
the German market in general, we did not identify a specific need for IBAN 
Verification Services. In consequence, we do not see a proven relevance and need 
to establish IBAN Verification Services on a European level. 
 
Batch processing for corporate customers: 
 
Our corporate customers can already use batch processing of credit transfers 
(e. g. bundled payment transactions) for standard and instant credit transfers.

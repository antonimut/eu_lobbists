Question 27. Where AIFMs use tri -party collateral managers’ services, which of the aspects 
should be explicitly regulated by the AIFMD? 
No additional rules are necessary, the current regulation is appropriate. 
Please explain why you think the obligation for the asset manager to provide the depositary with 
the contract it has concluded with the tri -party collateral should be explicitly regulated by the 
AIFMD. Please present benefits and disadvantages of this approach as well as potential costs of 
the change, where possible: 
Article 83 (g) of Commission Delegated Regulations 2018/1618 as amended states that the “AIFM or the 
AIF transmits all relevant information or ensures the depositary has access to all the information it needs 
to fulfil its duties”. This also applies to “other parties appointed by the AIF or the AIFM”. 
The way parties enter into a contrac tual relationship for tri -party collateral management services may 
vary from one firm to another, for example where a depositary is party to the tri-party collateral agreement, 
or where a depositary only acknowledges the agreement. It is generally accepted by market participants 
that the depositary should receive a copy of the collateral management agreement, which includes 
provisions related to the administration and the safekeeping of the collateral assets (e.g. eligibility, pledge 
versus transfer title) and be part of the discussions when such a program is implemented.

Please explain why you think the flow of information between the tri-party collateral manager and 
the depositary should be explicitly regulated by the AIFMD Please present benefits and

The entry into force of the 2018 Amendments to the AIFMD Delegated Regulation effective 1 April 2020 
introduced the obligation for the depositary to maintain its own set of books and records and perform 
reconciliation as often as necessary between th e internal accounts and registers of the depositary and 
those of a third party to whom the depositary has delegated the custody function.  
To ensure that depositaries are able to meet this requirement in a consistent manner across the industry, 
we believe it would be beneficial if EU authorities were to establish minimum requirements for the scope 
and transfer of information between depositories and tri -party collateral managers, in order to ensure 
depositories have the necessary information to appropriately maintain their own set of books and records. 
We believe such minimum requirements could be set out in level three measures or a Q&A paper. 
Please explain why you think the frequency at which the tri -party collateral manager should 
transmit the positions on a fund-by-fund basis to the depositary in order to enable it to record the 
movements in the financial instruments accounts opened in its books should be explicitly 
regulated by the AIFMD. Please present benefits and disadvantages of this approach as wel l as 
potential costs of the change, where possible: 
We believe the level three measures / Q&A paper should recognise that daily, end of day custody position 
reporting on a fund-by-fund basis by TCMs to Depositaries is sufficient. Based on pre -defined rules set 
by the AIFM and its counterparty, TCMs perform intra -day collateral optimisation and substitutions 
multiple times a day and therefore reporting, and reconciliations between the TCM custody account 
records and the depositary custody account records, ar e only feasible on an end -of-day basis. TCMs 
already provide custody position files at the end of each business day, and upon the request of the 
Depositary and AIFM, for oversight and reconciliation purposes.  
Further, as regards the scope of a Depositary’ s reconciliation obligation, we believe that the level three 
measures / Q&A paper should recognise that Depositaries may rely on the daily reconciliations performed 
by TCMs with respect to the financial instruments that the TCMs hold in custody within thei r own sub-
custody / CSD network, as well as the end of day custody position reports provided by the TCM, for the 
following reasons: 
1. TCMs are regulated entities with respect to the provision of their custody services and are appointed 
as sub-custodians of the Depositary under the “intermediary model”. 
 
2. TCMs already perform daily reconciliations, as required by law and regulation, between the 
transaction instructions and end of day collateral holdings for each AIF down through their sub -
custodian / CSD network. 
 
3. As a matter of efficient market practise, matched transaction instructions are provided directly to the 
TCM by the AIFM and its trading counterparty, and are not routed via the Depositary. 
 
4. Under existing AIFMD legislation, Depositaries are required to  undertake a significant level of due 
diligence when first appointing a TCM to act as sub -custodian, and to maintain a high level of 
oversight through the lifetime of the sub-custody relationship. 
 
Requiring a Depositary to perform a reconciliation of tran saction instructions as against the end of day 
custody positions held by TCMs, would result in a significant level of reconciliation duplication and 
increased cost for a Depositary with very little incremental benefit from an investor protection perspective.

Question 28. Are the AIFMD rules on the prime brokers clear? 
Yes. 
Question 28.1 Please explain your answer to question 28, providing concrete examples of 
ambiguities and where available suggesting improvements: 
We believe the AIFMD rules on prime brokers are clear and were the subject to discussion as part of the 
2018 books and records provisions. 
The entry into force of the 2018 Amendments to the AIFMD Delegated Regulation April 2020 introduced 
the obligation for the depositary to perform reconciliation as often as necessary between the internal 
accounts and registers of the depositary and those of a third party to whom the depositary has delegated 
the custody function.  
As highlighted in our response to Q26.1 for collateral management services, we believe the market would 
clearly benefit from a further level 3 market standards which would provide guidance on the day to day 
operational aspects - in particular, guidance with respect to the oversight and reconciliation obligations 
of depositaries with an acknowledgement that, where AIF assets are under the control of a third party, 
real-time reconciliation is not possible, and that end of day reconciliation will suffice.  
We would welcome working with the EU Commission on developing such level three measures. 
 
Question 50. Are the delegation rules sufficiently clear to prevent creation of letter-box entities in 
the EU? 
Yes. 
Question 50.1 Please explain your answer to question 50: 
The current Delegation Rules set out in the AIFMD, as further supplemented by the targeted provisions 
addressing the creation of letter-box entities (as laid out in Article 82 of Commission Delegated Regulation 
No 231/2013) are both clear and effective.  
The current rules provide national authorities with sufficient clarity to prevent the creation of letter -box 
entities in the EU, with no evidence of either market failure or increased risk to financial stability which 
would warrant changes. 
Further, Nationa l Competent Authorities, coordinated by ESMA have set targeted standards on 
substance for their own jurisdictions, including with robust requirements on oversight, control, risk 
management and governance. 
The delegation model that results ensures that portfolio management activities can be appropriately and 
safely delegated to wherever the expertise for the strategy adopted is based, ensuring the best service 
and outcome possible for EU27 investors.

Question 51. Are the delegation rules under the AIFMD/AIFMR appropriate to ensure effective risk 
management? 
Yes. 
Question 51.1 Please explain your answer to question 51, presenting benefits and disadvantages 
of the current rules and where available providing concrete examples substantiating your answer: 
We believe the risk management framework is effective, irrespective of whether the AIFM performs some 
of those risk management activities itself, or delegates risk management. In cases where the AIFM 
delegates risk management, it also remains responsible for the activity delegated and must perform 
necessary oversight of such delegate in accordance with delegation requirements.  
The current rules clearly and effectively require for an adequate level of risk management measures for 
AIFMs to implement. The identification, measurement, management and ongoing monitoring of all risks 
relevant to each AIF’s investment strategy proves to be robust and appropriate under the current model. 
As also described in our answer to Question 50, AIFMs are already required to establish and implement 
quantitative and qualitative risk limits for each of their AIFs, taking into account all relevant risks. As these 
limits must cover a broad range of risks - market risks, credit risks, liquidity risks, counterparty risks and 
operational risks – the current framework is comprehensive.  
Liquidity aspects are also well addressed under the current rules: accordingly, AIFMs already implement 
and maintain adequate limits for the liquidity or illiquidity of the AIFM consistent with its underlying 
obligations and redemption policy (considering the nature, scale and comp lexity of each AIF they 
manage).

Question 53. Should the AIFMD standards apply regardless of the loca tion of a third party, to 
which AIFM has delegated the collective portfolio management functions, in orde r to ensure 
investor protection and to prevent regulatory arbitrage? 
No. 
Question 53.1 Please explain your answer to question 53: 
As per Article 75 of the Delegated Regulation, the EU AIFM is wholly and entirely legally responsible for 
any delegated activity and remains accountable for ensuring compliance with the relevant prov isions of 
the AIFMD and its implementation measures, including that where portfolio management functions are 
delegated this is carried out as per the investment policy of the AIF. 
Accordingly, the current rules already require delegation to a delegate that is:  
 qualified and capable of undertaking the functions in question; 
 selected with all due care;  and  
 that the AIFM is in a position to monitor effectively.  
 
Further, there are many levels of protection built in to ensure that the EU investor is protected and there 
is no regulatory arbitrage. 
The AIFM is subject to the EU rules and it is their legal responsibility is to ensure that the entity that it 
delegates portfolio management to – regardless of it being outside of the EU – contractually meets the 
EU rules and regulations. The AIFM is already subject to legal requirements to establish strong controls, 
governance, and procedures for reviewing on an ongoing basis the services provided by the delegate.

Question 59. Should AIFMs be required to report to the releva nt supervisory authorities when 
they activate liquidity risk management tools? 
Yes. 
Question 59.1 Please explain your answer to question 59 , providing costs, benefits and  
disadvantages of the advocated approach: 
We think it is reasonable to report to NCA when liquidity management tools such as gates, suspension, 
and side pockets are utilised. Please note that we do not believe swing pricing should trigger such a 
reporting as this is a tool that can be used in normal market circumstances as well and therefore should 
not be considered a liquidity management tool.

Question 84. Are the current AIFMD rules permitting NCAs to cap the use of leverage appropriate? 
Yes 
Question 84.1 Please explain your answer to question 86, in t erms of the costs, benefits and 
disadvantages: 
We would not support a universal leverage cap and support the current framework where NCAs have 
discretion over applying leverage caps.  
This is particularly important for some strategies that can optically seem to be highly leveraged but the 
risk may not be, for example because the funds may have access to readily available capital from outside 
the vehicles. It is important that NCAs have the freedom to be able to assess these situations on a case 
by case basis.

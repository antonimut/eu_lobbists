 FruitVegetablesEUROPE's feedback: 
Agricultural products – revision of EU marketing standards 
FruitVegetablesEUROPE (EUCOFEL) is the European private sectoral Association representing the European Fruit and 
Vegetables producers and exporters at the EU level. FruitVegetablesEUROPE 's members are national and regional 
Federations and Associations (mainly PO and APO) and companies from the main European producing countries (France, 
Germany, Greece, Italy, Poland, Portugal, and Spain). FruitVegetablesEUROPE's direct members account for more than 
4,500 companies.  
EU marketing standards 
FruitVegetablesEUROPE welcomes the opportunity to comment on the inception impact assessment of EU marketing 
standards (contained in the Common Market Organisation  (CMO) regulation, the "breakfast directives" and CMO 
secondary legislation). 
We believe that EU marketing standards are essential to facilitate trade by setting common trading references , and they 
play an essential role in informing consumers on product characteristics and protecting them from fraud. Furthermore, the 
EU marketing standards assure  the well -functioning of the EU internal market, guaranteeing high -quality production, 
improving the whole fruit and vegetable sector's competitiveness, and protecting the consumer interests for the high-quality 
products. 
Overall, FruitVegetablesEUROPE agrees with the conclusions of the 'Evaluation of Marketing Standards' published last 
year. The EU marketing standards have played a positive role supplying the European market with standardised and high-
quality products. Additionally, EU marketing standar ds facilitate producers to sell their products in the EU market. 
Nevertheless, we would like to reiterate the negative effects for the sector of the simplification of marketing standards. The 
evaluation report does not conclude the effectiveness of the tr ansition from product -specific standards to the general 
marketing standards for the fruit and vegetable sector. For this reason, we call for the fruit and vegetable sector to be 
taken into account when exploring the need for introducing new marketing standards. 
Concerning the objectives and policy options suggested by the European Commission, we subscribe to the need to revise 
the current marketing standards to address better the current challenges that the sector is facing. Introduce marketing 
standards for all fruit and vegetable products will provide high -quality consumer information as well as improve the 
effectiveness and efficiency of EU agricultural marketing standards. 
1.  Limitations of EU marketing standards regarding provision of information to consumers 
There is already much information offered to consumers regarding the European fruit and vegetables marketed in the EU. 
However, already today, the consumer does not usually take advantage of all the information provided. Frequently, they 
do not even look at the origin of the product (.i.e. oranges from South Africa, Spain, or Italy). Another interesting example 
is the garlic case: "European consumers buy Chinese garlic without knowing it because they do not look at the labelling of 
origin". 
FruitVegetablesEUROPE considers that possible improvements in identifying the country of origin could be achieved (i.e. 
a more visual label to allow consumers to identify better the origin or any action looking at improving the identification of 
the origin). There are a significant communication and information challenge s to the consumer for both the European 
Commission and the European Fruit and Vegetables sector.

The harmonisation of marketing standards has excellent benefits for European farmers and consumers. It will allow to 
communicate the production method used to cultivate them. At the same time, it serves to highli ght the high European 
quality standards and differentiate them from imports from third countries.  
2. EU marketing standards in contributing to the economic conditions for production and 
marketing 
FruitVegetablesEUROPE considers that when more information has the standard, better for the producer and the 
consumer and to regulate the market. In that sense, EU marketing standards:  
• improve the quality of the product. 
• facilitate the product valorisation. 
• allow easier access to markets and to more remunerative markets, particularly to the fresh market, which is more 
valuable than the industrial one. 
• contribute to create a common language. 
• are essential to facilitate trade, intra and extra EU. 
 
3. Replacing specific EU marketing standards in the fresh fruit and vegetables sector with a general 
EU marketing 
The EU marketing standards had and have a direct impact on the European fruit and vegetables sector. The EU specific 
marketing standards allow considering each product's particularities, which helps both the producer and the trade, in the 
final benefit of the consumer.  
The decision to reduce the EU specific marketing standards for fruit and vegetables from 36 to 10 products (apples, citrus 
fruit, kiwifruit, lettuces, curled -leaved and broad -leaved endives , peaches and nectarines , pears, strawberries, sweet 
peppers, table grapes, tomatoes ) was completed against the general opinion of the sector (producer and traders) and 
have had a negative setback for all.  
FruitVegetablesEUROPE considers that replacing specific EU marketing standards in the fresh fruit and vegetables sector 
with a general EU marketing standard would affect product quality, effectiveness and efficiency of EU marketing standards 
enforcement. Furthermore, removing non-overlapping specific provisions or combining similar provisions would also affect 
product marketing and consumers. 
The disappearance of more  specific marketing standards would be extremely damaging for the sector. The  consumer 
would stop having useful information , which would facilitate the mixing of European products with imported products of 
lower quality, as we have seen in the past.  
The disappearance of the EU marketing standards would be a disaster of such magnitude that one cannot imagine its 
consequences for both the producer and the consumer. The bigger retailers would have to incorporate new requirements 
in their specifications to ensure the products' quality and compliance. 
For these reasons, replacing specific EU marketing standards in the fresh fruit and vegetables sector with a general EU 
marketing it is not an option for the European fruit and vegetables sector. 
FruitVegetablesEUROPE would completely oppose the disappearance of the EU specific marketing standards. Moreover, 
European consumers would be the main victims of this elimination. 
4. EU marketing standards in terms of generation of food waste 
FruitVegetablesEUROPE considers that eliminating marketing standards will not solve the food waste issue. Nowadays, 
category 2 products can already be commercialized , but consumers in supermarkets do not ask for it. Besides, there is 
the question of food waste in the consumer's home.

Placing products on the market that do not meet the standards would drop the prices because it would increase the offer 
of products at lower prices. The European producers would be the big losers.  
In FruitVegetablesEUROPE's opinion, food waste is a general society challenge. The solution passes among others to 
better inform and increase consumers' awareness  to make more informed choices  when buying food . The European 
distribution sector would also need to play a vital role in identifying causes of food waste and food losses and possible 
ways of preventing them. 
5. Coherence of EU marketing standards with the international marketing standards 
European standards are based on international standards, UNECE and Codex Alimentarius. The European Commission 
must be very vigilant to ensure that European marketing standards are not lowered by international pressure. In the 
UNECE and the Codex Alimentarius, there are many more countries that participate and their interests sometimes differ 
from those of the European producers. 
The European Commission and the National authorities must guarantee that imported products from third countries meet 
all European  standards, including EU marketing standards  and other statutory requirements for marketing fruit and 
vegetables in the EU market . In addition, reciprocity between EU products and third counties products should be 
safeguarded, which does not happen today for EU products. 
Conclusions 
FruitVegetablesEUROPE considers that the EU marketing standards work properly in general. Nevertheless, they must 
be modernised to contemplate changes and trends in both production and consumption. 
Finally, FruitVegetablesEUROPE calls the Commission: 
• to maintain the existing specific standards for the European fruit and vegetables,  
• to recover those that were eliminated, and 
• to consider new ones for new products that have appeared or are growing in importance.  
• to protect and promote the consumption of all European fruit and vegetable production, whether 
conventionally or organically produced. 
The European Fruit and Vegetables sector would be very pleased to discuss the importance of the European marketing 
standards with the European Commission at any time.

Dear Mrs Commissioner Kyriakides, 
 
Worldline welcomes the opportunity to contribute to your Public Consultation on the Digital heath data and 
services - the European Health Data Space.  
In introduction may we remind you that Worldline creates and operates digital services to promote the 
transformation of the world of healthcare by meeting the challenges of healthcare IT systems. As a health data 
host (HDS) and service provider with dual expertise in health and digital technology, Worldline offers secure 
and innovative services to healthcare professionals. 
We support health establishments, pharmaceutical laboratories, mutual insurance companies and health 
insurers, major health institutions, as well as start -ups and innovation projects on e -health topics such as 
patient pathways, e-prevention, telemedicine, shared imaging and data enhancement. 
For example, Worldline develops and hosts the following services in France: 
• CNAM - DMP: The national Shared Medical Record Platform, with a generalization to 65M records by 
2022. 
• SI-VIC : Information system for monitoring victims of attacks and exceptional health situations.  
With the arrival of Covid -19 in March 2020, SI -VIC has become the pivotal tool for reporting 
information on hospital care in the management of this crisis, thus participating directly  in 
understanding the epidemic and helping the authorities to make decisions. 
 
With nearly 20 years of experience in e-health and several certifications, we would like to share our vision and 
points of attention with you in addition to the answers to your questionnaire that we have posted online. 
 
Historically, the core business of our group Worldline is to operate secure digital transactions. We have 
become experts in this field and have transposed this know-how to the health sector, taking into account the 
PGSSI-S (security requirements applied to medical data in France) . Based on this historical culture of critical 
services, it is essential for us to point out the crucial role of high security requirements in health data 
management.

The increasing use of public or private cloud hosting makes this security aspect of health data paramount. The 
services hosted are not only critical but also represent very large volumes. Which makes the supervision of 
instances hosting health data very sensitive to c yber-attacks, hence the need for permanent and constant 
24/7/365 security controls. On this point, it seems important to us to recall the importance of certifications 
as a guarantee of the ability to operate such critical activities. 
 
 
As a pan -European pl ayer, we can only support the ambition of a single digital market, not only for the 
transactional services but also for the digital health services.  
The rationalization of medical services in addition to the development of telemedicine implies that health  
professionals will have to share examination results, medical imaging files and many other data. In this context, 
the future European Health Data Space will also have to be able to cross borders because Europeans move 
around and also sometimes health cond itions can force people to be isolated. The COVID pandemic we are 
experiencing is a vibrant example of this. 
It is the reason why standardi zation has become more than ever a key factor in  avoiding market 
fragmentation.  
The entry into application of the EU Digital COVID Certificate Regulation on 01 July 2021 is a perfect illustration 
of this standardization and cooperation in health sector over Europe. The data remain on the certificate and 
are not stored or retained when a certificate is verified in anothe r Member State. The EU Commission has 
supported the Member States to develop national software and apps to issue, store and verify the certificate.  
This use case has also demonstrated the crucial role of the customer journey. In Worldline for instance, we  
have a dedicated studio to improve the customer journey which is now a must have also for health services. 
Digitalisation affects all sectors of activity and citizens expect the same ease of use also on a health pathway. 
 
 
Finally, we can only agree with the EU Commission's direction to link artificial intelligence to the health of 
the future. Making the most of one's data has become essential: in the context of health, it contributes to the 
innovation of treatments on the one hand, but also to a better ma nagement of processes. To do this, the 
players must have access to a state-of-the-art data analysis platform, with large capacity storage on suitable 
media, and which interoperates with various data sources without forgetting compliance with regulations 
and with a maximum level of safety. 
Artificial intelligence associated with health implies a broad spectrum of requirements. To name but a few. 
• Data security and preparation (Management of authori zations and access requests, traceability, 
anonymization, pseudonymization, cleansing, sorting and enrichment of data  
• Data hub (Enrolment of data producers and consumers, Data collection via multiple and 
heterogeneous sources,  
• BI with Provision of medico -economic indicators (Calculation of BI indicators, rules engi ne, Alerts, 
dashboards, reports, data exports  
• Artificial Intelligence Big Data and AI for a predictive and preventive medicine (Provision of a data 
analysis and processing environment (Datalab), Development of perception, cognition and decision -
making algorithms by our data scientists

 
 
 
 
 
Response to European Health Data Space Consultation 
 
The ACHIEVE Coalition, which advocates for EU support to end hepatitis as a public health threat in 
Europe by 2030 in line with the WHO Global Strategy and Europe Plan and the UN SDG, welcomes the 
European Commission’s initiative to establish a European Health Data Space (EHDS).  
 
Good quality data concerning diagnosis and patients’ pathway during treatment and, for hepatitis C, 
cure, is scarce as far as viral hepatitis is concerned. This makes it difficult to measure progress in the 
fight against the disease and sometimes complicates the design of effective measures to drive forward 
elimination. Furthermore, good quality data is also needed to ensure a timely diagnosis of follow -on 
diseases such as liver cancer and cirrhosis. Viral hepatitis B and C are sti ll today highly stigmatised 
infections. There are many different risk groups affected by viral hepatitis B and C, including but not 
confined to vulnerable populations. As important as the generation of better data is to improve 
patients’ health outcomes, their data privacy and security must equally be ensured. 
 
The COVID pandemic has rightly drawn the public attention to the need for evidence -based health 
policy making and timely data access for research and developing such policies. At the same time, 
COVID-19 has forced us to take new avenues in treatment and care, as well as harm-reduction, which 
have proved successful also for the fight against viral hepatitis and should be continued post -
pandemic. Furthermore, innovations as regards portable testing tool s, fully integrated, automated 
sample-to-result molecular analysers and platforms, can be used to drive forward the detection of 
other diseases, namely hepatitis B and C, in both point of care and field setting. 
 
An effective EHDS that helps reduce health inequalities, must ensure that these data collected across 
the EU are of similarly high quality and support those Member States which find this difficult to 
achieve. Data collected must be interoperable and become easily comparable between and within 
Member States. Data must be collected for all chronic diseases, be they communicable or non -
communicable, so that healthcare professionals can make the right decisions to improve patients’ 
outcomes, also considering co -morbidities, and help researchers develop innovative treatments, 
therapy approaches and interventions. Social data is important, too, to identify social determinants of 
health. 
 
Artificial Intelligence (AI) offers a great potential to support healthcare professionals in taking the right 
decisions for their patients. For example, in chronic hepatitis B and C which is a very serious, and if 
not cured/ controlled, fatal disease, as well as very slow -burning, many people might not even recall 
being exposed to risk, or might have been diagnosed once without the appropriate follow up. AI could 
very much drive the search for these cases. 
 
At the same time, trust in AI from both patients and healthcare professionals is key, so the 
development and maintenance of high ethical standards is essential, as is in general the protection of 
privacy. 
 
A European Health Data Space must be easy to operate and understandable for all those who are 
working with it, including healthcare professionals, public and private researchers, but also patients. 
Investments in training programmes, on and off the job, to  improve digital and data literacy, for 
professionals, citizens and patients, including vulnerable groups are therefore of essential importance.

ACHIEVE is excited about the opportunities that the EHDS will offer and looks forward to the European 
Commission’s proposal. 
 
 
 
ACHIEVE (Associations Collaborating on Hepatitis to Immunise and Eliminate the Viruses in Europe) is 
a multi-stakeholder coalition advocating for EU support to end hepatitis as a public health threat in 
Europe in line with the WHO Global Sector Strategy and the UN SDGs. The Coalition brings together 
the following member organisations: European AIDS Treatment Group (EATG), European Liver Patients 
Association (ELPA), Liver Patients International, Viral Hepatitis Prevention Board, World Hepatitis 
Alliance, Hepatitis B and C Public Policy Association, Barcelona Institute for Global Health (ISGlobal), 
Correlation – European Harm Reduction Network. The European Association for the Study of the Liver 
(Observer) (EASL). The ACHIEVE Coalition is enabled by the support of AbbVie, Abbott, Cepheid and 
Gilead. More information is available on the website: https://achievehepatitiselimination.eu

EORTC position statement on 
the European Health Data Space (EHDS) 
prepared on July 2021 for the public consultation  
on “Digital health data and services – the European health data space” 
 
Prepared by EORTC Privacy team  
16th July 2021 
 
The purpose of the EHDS is to promote health -data exchange and support research in its new 
preventive strategies, as well as treatments, medic ines, medical devices, and outcomes1. The EORTC 
(European Organization for Research and Treatment of Cancer) very much welcomes the initiative to 
create a European Health Data Space (a “common health data space”), as there is an unmet need to 
facilitate he alth data sharing across borders and at international level. Such initiatives have  great 
potential to improve the health of European citizens. 
 
Need for clarity, consistency, and certainty at the level of legal landscape 
The European Commission’s proposal for the EHDS seems to have three main pillars : i) to facilitate 
(cross-border) sharing of health data for improving health care management (main scope) or for policy 
and research purposes (secondary scope); ii) to facilitate the free movement of digital health services; 
and iii) to ensure an ethical and sustainable use of artificial intelligence in health care sector. 
In EORTC’s view, this means that there is a need for a strong uniform framework that regulates the 
setting and the management of such common health data space. In order to have such sustainable, 
clear and robust legal framework, the EHDS will need to clarify the interplay between legal frameworks 
already applicable to health research, not necessary specific to this field, such as  the GDPR (EU 
General Data Protection regulation) , DGA  (Data Governance Act) 2, AI  (Artificial Intelligence 
regulation)3, CTR  (Clinical Trial Regulation  (EU) No 536/2014 )4 , (IV)MDR  (In-vitro Medical Device

4. Zoom on data governance for health sector?

6. Data subjects and consent  
What will be the information provided to the patient and the legal ground for processing their 
data in scope of this regulation and sharing of data? Will this aspect be left at the decision of 
the initial data collector, eg. data controller? 
In regards to the rights of the patients under EHDS: will their health data be governed by the 
GDPR and the EHDS? Or will the latter define  only the data subjects rights that will govern 
specifically the data shared in scope of the EHDS? What if the procedure for execution of data 
subjects rights under the GDPR will conflict with the one under the EHDS? E.g. rights that are 
considered as absolute under the GDPR will be limited under the EHDS? 
 
 
The interpretation of GDPR remains a challenge for data sharing across MSs 
Several papers and initiatives, including ours were raised in scope of reuse of health data, and the 
shortcomings created by different laws, sometimes incompatible and impractical with the health data 
research field, specifically linked to already applicable GDPR.10,11  
Prior and since its coming into force on 25 May 2018, the GDPR has been causing significant concerns 
among the scientific community 12, mainly related to the diverging national implementation 13, the 
uncertainty regarding th e correct compliance with its provisions and perceived barriers for longer 
storage and re-use of already collected data as well as for the sharing of data with other researchers. 
The overall picture is further made complex thr ough application of national h ighly heterogeneous 
legislation in relation to other matters, such as biobanks14, registries15 and genetic information16.

With this unstable legal landscape, stakeholders are very concerned that EHDS will not be able to build 
efficiently and its initial good propose would be weakened by loopholes during the implementation 
phase of the regulation.  
 
With this position paper, we re -enforce and publicly support  more recent and similar statements 
and concerns17,18,19,20 of organizations or stakeholders that strongly advocate for a harmonized, clear, 
sustainable, and patient -oriented health data space which will be able not only to facilitate a more 
well-organized process for the access and data sharing for research purposes, but will also help reduce 
a number of the above-mentioned shortcomings. 
 
 
Recommendations:  
EORTC together with other stakeholders are currently urging the relevant regulatory bodies for legal 
clarity, certainty and consistency when defining and implementing GDPR within EU and when issuing 
new legal initiatives such as EHDS, DGA which aim to unlock the power of health data.  
Having st rong  collaborations  between different regulatory and expert authorities which generate 
harmonized legal initiatives will lead to increase understanding and trust of the patient and research 
community. 
Without a solid basis  and consistency in the existing legal framework, the EHDS risks to complicate 
European health research even more.  
The EHDS should not overlap with other legal initiatives. It should rather it should be clarify the gaps 
and help stakeholders create a research environment that is both safe and efficient for the patients.  
The EHDS should consider: 
- The nature of the multidimensional and multidisciplinary of clinical and biological data which 
may greatly diverge in quality and cross validation, as well as the forms and the methods 
through which they are acquired (i.e. pre-analytical).  The frame offered by the EHDS may not 
reach its goals if the inherent data science challenges are not addressed first.

About EORTC 
EORTC is a unique pan-European non-profit clinical cancer research organisation established in 1962 
operating as an international association under the Belgium law. It develops, conducts, coordinates , 
and stimulates high -quality translational and clinical trial research  aiming to in crease survival and 
quality of life of cancer patients. This is achieved through the development of new drugs and other 
innovative approaches, the testing more effective therapeutic strategies, using currently approved 
drugs, surgery and/or radiotherapy in clinical trials conducted under the auspices of a vast network of 
clinical cancer researchers supported by 220 staff members based in Brussels. EORTC has the expertise 
to conduct large and complex trials incl. those for elderly and rare cancer patients. 
EORTC research network consists of over 5500 collaborators from all disciplines involved in cancer 
treatment and research in more than 930 university hospitals in 27 countries. Its research spans the 
entire spectrum: from translational and preclinical research to large, prospective, multi-centre, phase 
III clinical trials that evaluate new cancer therapies and treatment strategies, as well as patient quality 
of life.  
Currently, EORTC is conducting 203 studies, and thousands of patients (85% from within the  EU) 
participate in EORTC clinical trials.  25,000 patients continue to be followed up on a yearly basis and 
the EORTC clinical study database now contains outcome data for over 205,000 cancer patients.

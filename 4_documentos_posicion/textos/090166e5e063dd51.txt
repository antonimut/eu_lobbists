FOREWORD
Water and energy are deeply entwined. The water-energy nexus 
refers to the relationship between how much energy is needed for 
abstracting, moving, heating, cooling, storing, treating and disposing 
water and how much water is used for generation and transmission of 
energy. This nexus is expected to intensify in the coming years. 
So far, Member States have notified a limited set of water-related 
measures in the framework of Article 7 of the Energy Efficiency Directive 
(EED). The most frequently notified measure is the production of hot 
water by solar collectors or more efficient gas water heaters. However, 
these measures rather relate to heat generation than water production, 
distribution, use, and wastewater treatment. Raising awareness about 
the energy-water nexus can help:
•
 Member States prioritise efficient use of both water and energy
• the business community to bring to market technologies and   
solutions designed to deliver water and energy savings across   
industries, municipalities and buildings
• the EU to deliver the energy savings and emission reductions   
necessary to achieve the ambitious goal of climate neutrality   
by 2050. 
This paper showcases concrete examples of water and energy saving 
projects across sectors and European countries. The projects feature 
some of the most advanced environmental technologies currently 
available on the market. Technologies delivering environmental, 
economic and social benefits.
We hope that these successful projects will inspire policy and decision 
makers at all levels and especially those working on the energy saving 
obligations under Art. 7 of the Energy Efficiency Directive.
Monica Frassoni
President of the European Alliance to Save Energy

AN ENERGY INTENSIVE WASTEWATER 
TREATMENT PLANT TURNED INTO 
ENERGY PRODUCER
Since 2010, the Marselisborg wastewater treatment plant has transformed its focus beyond 
minimising energy consumed, to maximising net energy surplus, making it the world’s first 
climate-neutral water facility. Nowadays the facility has net production of both electricity and 
heat, supplying the district heating system in Denmark’s second-largest municipality, Aarhus. 
Marselisborg, Aarhus, Denmark.

A GREEN ROOF THAT SAVES ALMOST TWO 
OLYMPIC-SIZED SWIMMING POOLS OF 
STORMWATER EVERY YEAR
Urbanscape Green Roof technology, provided by Knauf Insulation, covered 8,300 m2 of roof 
surface of a commercial building in France. The system, installed in only 8 days, allows to obtain 
huge cost savings and environmental benefits thanks to its water retention capacity. In addition, 
it contributes to cool down the building during summer months, reduces heat island effect, 
improves urban biodiversity and help with the absorption of air pollution.
Champniers, France.
         CHALLENGE
 SOLUTION
    RESULTS
• He at island effect, cooling the building and reducing the temperature in 
urban areas.
• Low water-retention capacity, causing water runoff and surcharging of 
waste water treatment plants, with very high energy consumptions and 
pollution of local water sources.
• S toring of more than 4,800m3 of rainwater p/year — the equivalent of 
almost two Olympic-sized swimming pools of water. 
• Up to 2.9 MWh of energy are saved - considering the average energy 
consumption on a yearly base in Waste Water Treatment Plants.
• Saving on real rain water treatment energy cost.
• Urbanscape’s unique Performance Evaluation Tool (PET) was used to ensure 
the precise calculation of the roof’s water retention potential. 
• PET analyses complex local weather data, the area’s climate history and the 
dimensions of the installation to produce reliable final figures.

SMART & INNOVATIVE UTILITIES DESIGN 
FOR A DANONE PRODUCTION PLANT
Veolia has supported Danone with the design and construction of the Estia project which 
consists of a new green field project for an early nutrition production plant worth €240 million 
of investment. Estia, a flagship facility for Danone, doubles its legacy plant. Its utilities designed, 
built, and operated by Veolia delivers the production plant with the necessary utilities, using 
60% less water, 25% less energy and 50% less CO2 than the legacy plant.
Nutricia Cuijk also embraces the principles of the circular economy – 100% of the facility’s waste 
is recovered, including all packaging waste. The new facility employs directly around 500 people 
and indirectly supports an additional 2,000 jobs.
City of Haps (Cuijk), North Brabant, Netherlands.

RECYCLING WATER AT END-USER LEVEL 
WITH A SMART SHOWER
Orbital System water saving recirculating solution for showers saved up to 90% of water and 
80% of related energy in a hotel in Malmö, Sweden. The system collects the water that would 
normally go down the drain, analyses its quality, cleans, heats it and reuses it in the same shower 
cycle. The sensors in the systems analyse the quality of water up to 20 times per second and 
decide whether it is good to be sent back for re-use.
Malmö, Sweden
         CHALLENGE
 SOLUTION
    RESULTS
• W ater heating is the second-largest energy expense in the home, and 
some showers use twice as much energy and hot water than we need. 
• If  an average 10-minute shower consumes about 100 litres of water, the 
Orbital Shower uses only a fraction of this, saving up to 90% of water.
• 80% of related energy is saved.
• The smart system filters, purifies, reheats and then recirculates the water used 
during a shower.
• The purification is done by a micron filter and a UV light. 
• The system also collects data on water quality and usage that can be 
monitored by a mobile app.

IMPROVING THE EFFICIENCY OF 
COOLING WATER PROCESSES FOR 
YOGHURT PRODUCTION
The Milchhof Sterzing performed a renovation of its yoghurt production facility including 
a new ice water system in order to treat the daily milk volumes of around 470 dairy farmers. 
Milk and yoghurt production is an extremely energy intensive process due to all the cooling 
water required. The dairy wanted to reduce the energy consumption for cooling in the ice water 
system. 
Xylem was able to supply an intelligent frequency controlled pumping system to ensure optimal 
pump usage and energy management based on the fluctuating demand which led to a 70% 
reduction in energy costs for this system.
South Tyrol, Italy.
         CHALLENGE
 SOLUTION
    RESULTS
The intensive use of energy in the milk processing chain forces dairies and milk 
processors to use electrical energy more sparingly and efficiently. The dairy 
farm Sterzing in South Tyrol, took appropriate action and updated its yoghurt 
production facilities including an ice water plant as part of a comprehensive 
renovation. The purpose of the plant is to cool the milk when it is received 
and to cool it down again after the pasteurization and keep it chilled for 
further processing. The required amount of ice water for cooling the milk and 
milk products varies greatly depending upon the production quantity, the 
season, the daily demand and the difference between the actual and the set 
temperature. In the summer months there is a greater need for refrigeration 
for milk cooling. The provision of heat and cold during the various thermal 
processes today requires sophisticated energy management. 
•  70% r eduction in 
electrical energy
Xylem’s intelligent pumping system conveys the ice water from the ice water basin 
via a distributor into seven consumer circuits and ensures that the system operates 
as required and is energy-efficient. The pumps supply a total of 11 pickup stations. 
In order to ensure supply via the seven circuits, the pumps are regulated by the 
frequency converter based on the ice water pressure and additionally switched on 
or off as required. The number of operating hours of the three individual Lowara 
pumps regulates the pump control, so that uniform utilisation of all pumps is 
guaranteed.

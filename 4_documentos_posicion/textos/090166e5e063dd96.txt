 Risks associated with discharging urban waste water 
without appropriate treatment 
There are multiple issues with this question that make EurEau’s answer being of no 
opinion.  
There are several risks associated to discharging urban waste water without appropriate 
treatment. However, in Europe thanks to the implementation of the UWWTD, these risks 
are managed. The definition of appropriate treatment is an is sue to answer this 
question. If there is no treatment at all, there are several risks mentioned that should 
be ranked high  but it does not represent the reality of Europe ’s situation today. IT is 
therefore difficult to answer this question without having a better understanding of the 
objective of this question.  
Finally, r egarding the contamination of drinking water, the Drin king Water Directive 
(DWD) protect the health of consumers so even if waste water could contaminate the 
water resource, the drinking water itself will still be protected by the DWD requirements 
and treatment will be put in place to comply with these requirements. 
 Marginalized and vulnerable groups  
We support the ambition of the European Commission to realise the human right to  
sanitation and the implementation of Sustainable Development Goal 6. We are greatly  
attached to the definition of the human right to water by the UN according to which this 
right encompasses five dimensions: availability, accessibility, affordability, acceptability 
and safety.  
We share the ambition to increase access to sanitation in recreational places, cafes and 
restaurants already in place with positive effects in some Member States. However, any 
such scheme needs to consider where the re sponsibility for maintenance, hygiene and 
cost coverage of these installations will lie (as this will likely be outside  the remit of 
waste water services), and the most appropriate location for these  installations. Local 
authorities are best placed to decide on these aspects.

About EurEau 
EurEau is the voice of Europe’s water sector. We represent drinking water 
and waste water operators from 29 countries in Europe, from both the 
private and the public sectors.  
Our members are 32 national associations of water services. At EurEau, 
we bring national water professionals together to agree European water 
sector positions regarding the management of water quality, resource 
efficiency and access to water for Europe’s c itizens and businesses. The 
EurEau secretariat is based in Brussels.  
With a direct employment of around 476,000 people, the European water sector makes 
a significant contribution to the European economy.

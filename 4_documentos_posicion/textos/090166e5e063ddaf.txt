Subject: Open Public Consultation on the Revision of the Urban Waste 
Water Treatment Directive 
 
Plastics Recyclers Europe (PRE), the association representing the plastics recycling 
industry, understands that there is a growing concern related to microplastics in 
wastewater treatment. We would like to approach this topic in the present paper, by 
demonstrating how the industry ensures that wastewater leakages from recycling 
facilities is controlled and minimized. 
 
The lack of a definition for microplastics 
PRE would like to stress that the lack of a clear definition for microplastics has 
weakened enforceability among the different Member States (MS), especially where 
facilities are not certified and audited under standards using the same vocabulary.  
Hence, PRE suggests providing a clear set of definitions to create a level-playing field 
across the MS and the industry’s value-chain to comply with the same objectives.  
Despite the lack of a clear definition of microplastics in the EU legal framework, the 
understanding is that any plastic particles smaller than 5mm in all dimensions are to be 
considered microplastics. Given these premises, the output products of a plastic 
recycling operation, recycled plastic pellets, would fall within the scope of this definition.  
 
Certification of plastic recycling facilities to minimize wastewater contamination 
Since decades, the setting of a plastic recycling plant includes wastewater 
management to ensure particles are filtered before the release of water to the 
environment and preventing the release into the environment. 
To monitor and guarantee the quality of the wastewater management, audit schemes 
of certification bodies in Europe already include requirements covering this aspect, with 
the objective of ensuring the traceability of waste and addressing the quality of recycling 
plants.

EuCertPlast, a well-known auditing scheme for plastic recyclers, includes in its audit 
scheme 1 all specific requirements for the treatment of wastewater by a recycling 
facility, not limited to microplastics releases. For example, auditors are requested to 
check whether there is a capture and treatment of rainwater runoff from the site. 
Certification schemes similar to EuCertPlast are generally adopted by the industry to 
ensure the traceability of waste and increase the overall quality of the recycling steps, 
these schemes are nevertheless voluntary schemes, where compliance is not set by a 
legislatively mandated programme.  
PRE considers the adoption of measures to limit the release of wastewater 
contaminants, including microplastics, from sites performing plastics recycling an 
important element to be evaluated, monitored, and assessed.  
Given its experience within this field and its extended membership, PRE would like to be 
involved in the technical work to define the specific of measurement methods and 
definitions for microplastics releases from industrial settings.  
 
PRE remains available to further discuss this policy file with the relevant team at the EC. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
About Plastics Recyclers Europe 
Plastics Recyclers Europe (PRE) is an organization representing the voice of the European plastics 
recyclers who reprocess plastic waste into high quality material destined for production of new articles. 
Recyclers are important facilitators of the circula rity of plastics and the transition towards the circular 
economy. Plastics recycling in Europe is a rapidly growing sector representing €3bn in turnover.

remain free, in accordance with Union law, to recognise electronic identification means 
having lower identity assurance levels. 
 
(1b) The use of assurance level ‘very high’ should be reserved for public online services with

level ‘very high’ must in addition to the requirements of assurance level ‘high’ provide 
the acting public sector body with an official electronic picture and/or any biometrical data 
in order to enable it to perform an identification process which may include video 
conferences or any other online means that provide a real-time audio-visual connection. 
In order to meet these criteria the Member States may choose to grant access to the highly 
secure storage medium of their national identity cards as provided for by Article 3 
paragraph 5 Regulation (EU) 2019/1157.

Justification 
For a couple of public procedures such as the issue of national ID documents or the notarial 
authentication procedure, national law requires face-to-face interaction between the applicants 
and the public sector body in order to meet highest identification standards. These standards are 
necessary in particular to prevent identity fraud (e.g. by passing on an electronic identification 
plus PIN) and to ensure the accuracy and reliability of public registers (e.g. national business 
registers and land registers) which are endowed with public faith in many Member States.  
In order to allow that the Regulation can also be used in future for the aforementioned 
authentication procedure and other public procedures with the highest identification security 
requirements, the existing three assurance levels in the electronic Regulation should be 
supplemented by higher security measures, which add to the existing assurance level “high” a 
videoconferencing identification procedure. The latter must make sure that an official electronic 
photograph of the holder of the electronic identification is being transferred to the national 
public sector body. In fact, this is the only way to ensure that electronic identification 
procedures are of (at least) the same standard as the existing procedures using an identity card 
or passport in the physical world.  
This is demonstrated by the example of the online formation of a limited liability company. In 
this case, the existing electronic identification procedure and the aforementioned 
videoconferencing identification procedure must be combined in an online authentication 
procedure in order to ensure the most reliable identification of the parties involved. The party 
must first authenticate himself/herself – as required by the Digital Tools Directive (EU) 
2019/1151 – by an electronic identification means (electronic identification) with the assurance 
level "high" according to the Regulation (e.g. by way of an identity card with an electronic 
identification function or an EU residence permit or by a full digital ID level high, if applicable). 
The acting public sector body can see via his system that the party involved has a valid 
electronic identification and read out the necessary data. In doing so, the acting public sector 
body must also be able to receive the party’s official photograph which is sent to him via the 
national electronic identification system in order to compare it with his/her video image during 
the videoconference. This is precisely not possible in a conventional videoident procedure, as 
it only allows a comparison with the ID card held in the camera. The advantage of combining 
the two methods is that the weaknesses of the respective other method are mitigated. The 
weakness of the (mere) videoident procedure is that the authenticity of the ID card held in the 
camera cannot be reliably verified. The weakness of the (mere) electronic identification 
procedure is that it is impossible to detect the use of electronic identifications which are stolen 
or deliberately passed on for abusive purposes – as well as psychological pressure and legal 
incapacity. These weaknesses are significantly mitigated by the combination of the current 
electronic identification procedure with the aforementioned videoconferencing identification 
procedure, since both the electronic identification and the declaration of the party can be 
assigned to a specific person at the moment of their submission.  
The current assurance level “high” would not meet these identification requirements, which are 
necessary to prevent identity fraud and to ensure for example the accuracy of business registers 
and land registers. On the other hand, if the revised Regulation provided for the proposed higher 
security measures, Member States could use the Regulation in future also for  the 
aforementioned authentication procedures and other public law procedures with the highest 
identification requirements, which currently require the personal appearance of the applicant.

 
PEPSICO SUPPORTING DOCUMENT TO THE REVISION OF REVISION OF EU 
MARKETING STANDARDS FOR AGRICULTURAL PRODUCTS 
27 August 2021 
PepsiCo supports the work of the European Commission to ensure uptake and supply of more sustainable 
products across the European Union. PepsiCo appreciates the opportunity to provide input into this 
process. This note complements the answers provided by PepsiCo to the questionnaire. 
One horizontal approach to sustainability  
PepsiCo recognises the need for a level playing field and for high standards of sustainability throughout 
the European Union, both for a fair and competitive market, and for transparent communications to 
European consumers. Given the ambitious targets set by the European Green Deal generally, and the Farm 
to Fork strategy specifically, we believe harmonisation to be critical to secure a coherent approach to 
sustainability across all sectors and value chains. With this in m ind, PepsiCo believes that sustainability 
standards for agricultural product s should be defined in a horizontal approach under the upcoming 
Legislative Framework for Sustainable Food systems as opposed to via vertical policy streams.  
 
“Reduced sugar juices” characteristics to be defined  
Innovative sugar reduction technologies introduce the possibility to remove sugars that occur naturally in 
juices, to meet dietary policies as well as consumers’ needs and expectations. However, according to the 
current legislation for fruit juices, sugar-reduced juices cannot use the legal name: “juice”. We would like 
to see a legislative framework for fruit juices that enables innovation to create a new category of juices. 
Characteristics of this new category of  (sugar-reduced) juice and new authorized processes  and/or 
relevant additives should therefore be regulated in the EU marketing standard of juice, while allowing for 
flexibility to implement evolving sugar reduction technologies.  
This approach has been applied for dairy products such as milk, for which, different sales descriptions such 
as “whole milk”, “semi -skimmed milk” and “skimmed milk” are defined to designate drinking milk with 
respect to fat content. These terms have been defined separately from the general rules for “reduced fat” 
claim and are well understood by consumers. 
PepsiCo believes that a similar legislative framework for juices that would allow for  “reduced sugar fruit 
juice” should be defined in Annex I of the Fruit Juice Directive along with definitions for “fruit juice”, “fruit 
juice from concentrate” and “nectar” with re spect to sugar content . Inclusion in Annex I is the most 
appropriate way to take account of evolving technologies and consumer needs, while securing the ongoing 
authenticity, organoleptic quality and natural characteristics of juices whilst allowing for sugar reduction.  
 
Addition of new ingredients in juices  
PepsiCo supports the possibility to allow addition of “non-juice ingredients” in juices, provided the addition 
is labelled appropriately without misleading consumers, and provided the addition does not result in any 
modification of the characteristics of the juice as defined in the marketing standard.

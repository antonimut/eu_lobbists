Answer to the public consultation on the 
Data Act 
Fuelling voluntary data sharing 
 
 
Our recommendations 
 
The future Data Act should:  
 promote voluntary data sharing;  
 foresee voluntary EU-wide tools for Business-to-Business data sharing; and  
 ease cloud service switching.  
 
 
Our contribution to the Data Act consultation 
 
Data is at the core  of our growth. Used the right way, Data enable retailers of all sizes to 
tailor their services to customers’ needs , improve internal efficiency  and reduce their 
environmental footprint. We are embracing the potential of Data to make life simpler, more 
affordable, fairer, and more sustainable for the many people and society at large. That is 
why we are rethinking ethical and responsible business in the digital age. 
 
The COVID crisis has sped up our transformation, shifting our focus towards online and 
hybrid consumer exper iences, but has also emphasised the need for a fair and ethical 
European Data ecosystem. Technologies relying on Data such as Artificial Intelligence were 
– and still are – a key enabler of our transformation, helping us to improve our services, 
innovate and find new opportunities that will support employment and speed up recovery. 
Nevertheless, responses to the crisis c an bias competition and open the door to more 
intrusive practices. We need to stay true to our European values and create a framework 
that will put people first, and balance business interests to benefit people and planet.  
 
We welcome the commitment of the  European Commission to the Single Market for 
Data and Cloud Services. We have the following recommendations for the Data Act: 
 
 
 The Data Act should promote voluntary data sharing. 
 
Data is paramount to the Green and Digital Transition s. Technologies such as Artificial 
Intelligence and blockchain rely on Data to build innovative solutions that improve 
consumer experience. Similarly, Data and tech nology are absolutely essential for any

Data sharing should remain voluntary  to support data reuse while safeguarding the 
competitiveness of European businesses. As the Data Economy deepens, Data is becoming 
a source of competitive advantage for companies. Companies inf er strategic information 
from the data they collect and use this knowledge to position themselves on the market. 
Data policies are becoming features of their unique offerings and define their brand. 
Solutions such as licensing would ease data access while ensuring control and fair 
compensation for data collection, maintenance and use.  
 
Business-to-Government (B2G) data sharing for the public interest should be carefully 
scoped to protect the fundamental rights of citizens and businesses alike. Clarity on which 
type of data (data generated in the EU, data involving EU citizens, etc.)  would be subject to 
such a framework would be needed to secure the attractiveness of the European Union for 
innovation. Any measure supporting B2G data sharing should take place at EU -level to 
create a proportional framework for governments and businesses alike.  
 
 
 The Data Act should foresee voluntary EU-wide tools for B usiness-to-Business 
data sharing. 
 
Voluntary Business-to-Business (B2B) data sharing benefits European retailers of all sizes . 
Access to partners’ data sets is overseen through contracts that are negotiated on a case -
by-case basis. Securing easier access to data from suppliers and service providers (e.g., 
transporters, IT service providers), as well as within a franchise system, would create a level-
playing field for retailers and improve customer experience. Improving access to co -
generated data, such as the data the connected products we sell generate, will safeguard a 
level-playing field to the benefit of consumers. Smoother data sharing within groups of 
companies would improve the competitiveness and support company -wide innovation of 
the many European businesses that own data across their subsidiaries.  
 
Business-to-Business data sharing would benefit from EU-wide voluntary frameworks. 
Attention should be paid to creating a clear framework and the necessary safeguards to 
comply with the General Data Protection Regulation.  
• The use of model contract terms would enable businesses to share data irrespective 
of their size and ease cloud service switching.  
• Any fairness test should foresee fair, reasonable and non-discriminatory criteria to 
prevent excessive, unlawful, and uncompetitive data sharing.  
• Smart contracts have a lready proved usef ul as a  tool for data transfers.  Clear 
governance models and interoperable features will be key for the uptake of complex 
blockchain-based technologies.

Intellectual Property (IP) rights have a differentiated impact on Business -to-Business data 
sharing according to the data types at hand with IP rights playing a more important role for 
some data types and less so for others. A one -size-fits-all approach could have 
unintended consequences for IP owners and holders  should their intellectual property

 The Data Act should ease cloud service switching.  
 
As businesses rely more and more on cloud services, securing a level-playing supportive of 
European businesses is more important than ever. Features such as data but also s ystem 
and logic portability will enable business users to switch services more easily, supporting 
competitive and innovative cloud services.  
 
A right to cloud service portability for business users should rely on high-level future-
proof principles. Relying on technical specification that will become soon out of date would 
require extensive and costly overhead to maintain compliance. Such a right should include 
the ability to reposition data and logic to another service provider with minimal data 
transformation and cost. Tying portability rights to offering tiers would allow business users 
to decide whether portability is important for their busine ss while ensuring appropriate 
remuneration for cloud service providers.  
 
Portability in cloud services would o nly benefit from flexible and easy-to-implement 
standards that complement the framework set by the General Data Protection Regulation.  
• Standardisation efforts should be targeted and rely upon robust international 
standards. Greater complexity would limit the ability of European businesses to 
innovate and reinforce market power resulting from the ‘data advantage’ large 
operators benefit fr om. Cloud portability standards for Application Programming 
Interfaces (APIs) and data formats building upon w idely used standards such as 
Internet Engineering Task Force (IETF) Requests For Comments (RFCs) would 
support data innovation without generating high database reorganisation costs.  
• Switching and Porting (SWIPO) codes of conduct on data portability put an extensive 
emphasis on proprietary features of cloud platforms while non -proprietary 
Infrastructure-as-a-Service (IaaS) features are less developed. As a result, business 
users have no choice but to use locked -in features, undermining the portability of 
systems and data. While distributed computer standards such as Kubernetes are 
emerging, we would welcome  the development of serverless  and function 
standards.

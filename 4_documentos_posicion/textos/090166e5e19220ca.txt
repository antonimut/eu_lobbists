Dear European Commission,  We would like to thank you for the open and collaborative approach that characterized the large debate that precede the Proposal for a review of the Regulation (EU) No 910/2014 - eIDAS, and for the opportunity to continue to express our voice in a formal engagement process.  As InfoCert Group, we are strongly committed in putting our vast, multi-national and variegated know-how on Qualified Trust Services, digital identity schemas, self-sovereign identity and trust governance frameworks, available to the to the Commission, the Parliament and all the other stakeholders involved in the legislative and implementation process. In a world where digital interactions are the preferred way to conduct business, access public services, communicate with other people and entities, digital trust becomes a fundamental need to compete on the internal and international markets and, for every citizen, the ground of the right to be protected against fraud, impersonation, misuse and manipulation of data and information. In this landscape, Europe is playing the role of the beacon in creating regulations that put the citizen at the centre of every digital interaction and structuring a framework where digital trust is not just a matter of technology, but it involves the governance models, the procedures and the rules that regulate the digital world. In this strategy, GDPR and eIDAS Regulation represent the main pillars in securing digital interactions, creating a common playing field for the various actors in the digital single market and setting up the global standards that are influencing even other geographies. The Proposal for the revision of the eIDAS Regulation, published by the European Commission on 3rd of June 2021, introduces some important changes that will have a positive impact on users' experience of trust services, and accelerate the take-off of digital identity-related public and private services. European Digital Identity Wallet The first consideration is related to the future introduction of a European Digital Identity Wallet: we welcome this position and agree with the Commission’s view to enable citizens of all Member States to benefit from a digital identity that allows the management of their attributes, both qualified and not, as well as the validation and presentation of their identification data.  In our direct experience we can confirm how Digital Identity is a key enabler to speed up the digitalization of processes and transactions involving citizens, governments and businesses: in Italy InfoCert acts as an Identity Provider and as a Service Provider in the SPID (Sistema Pubblico per l’Identità Digitale) ecosystem, that represents the biggest notified digital identity schema in Europe in terms of transactions. In Germany the use of the electronic identity card is growing to get access to digital services but also to enable cloud-based signatures, and Authada is giving a strong contribution, especially in the private sector, while in Spain the digital certificates, issued among the others by Camerfirma, are mandatory to access public services, even for professionals and SMEs. In this landscape, the European Digital Identity Wallet represents an important step forward in the harmonization of the different identity schemas, and an enabler of new services. To make the Wallet a success, we believe it is important to point out some aspects of the Proposal that could be better clarified or enhanced.  In general, it is not clear if the Digital Identity Wallet can contain more digital identity credentials or if there will be only one identity anchor provided by the State. Such formulation could create a potential confusion between the technical medium, the Wallet, and the credentials contained therein: we agree that the security level of the underlying technology should be the highest possible keeping in mind the existing constraints (see our comments later), but we also think that the Wallet should be able to contain a wide variety of identity and attribute credentials, issued by different providers and with different Levels of Assurance. The Wallet itself is not an identity schema but just a technological enabler of a national identity schema (brand new or designed in synergy with the existing means, up to the decision of the Member States) and, eventually, of an entire ecosystem of identities and attributes: in such ecosystem, citizens should be free to choose any type of credentials, with different LoA according to the digital service they are accessing.  The related liability framework shall be modulated according to the credential’s LoA. For example, an identity with LoA high should be preferred in all those use case where the reversal of burden of proof and the absolute certainty of the identity is paramount. In other use cases with a lower level of need trust, using the highest level of identity can be disproportionate, therefore a Wallet that supports different types of credentials could better enable a broader variety of use cases and boost the success of the initiative. In that regard, we suggest to clarify in article 6a, point 4.(a).1, that the Wallet should be accessible with a standard interface to trust service providers issuing both qualified and non-qualified electronic attestations of attributes, but even to Identity Proofing Service Providers (IPSP, as defined by ETSI in the recently issued ETSI TS 119 461). Furthermore, recall the LoA frameworks, defining that the level high will in primis be applicable to the primary identity managed and issued by the Governments.  In general, it seems not clear the liability model under the derivation of the primary identity from the official national identity: we believe that a flexible approach, with the cooperation between the Governments and strongly audited players could benefit the overall system. A concrete example is Germany, where the law recognizes player like the Identifizierungsdienstanbieter that are strongly monitored and therefore could take the responsibility and related liability of deriving identities from an official eID card. With reference to article 6a.3 letter b), it should be better specified that the Wallet will serve as identification and authorization tool to receive and/or use a Qualified Electronic Certificate for Qualified Electronic Signature issued by a QTSP that has been freely chosen by the owner of the Wallet and/or, depending on the process and the use case, by the relying party. The risk of the actual configuration of the law is a dangerous concentration of roles, if the provider of the Wallet can even act as QTSP issuing certificates and/or providing the new qualified service for the management of remote electronic signature creation devices: this could negatively impact the fair competition in the digital single market, paving the way to monopolies or dominant positions. Regarding the Wallet adoption, even if its technical interfaces will be easy to integrate, the result of the authentication could be difficult to manage on behalf of some relying parties, especially the ones that are less technology savvy. A qualified player acting as a “proxy-verifier” could enable a wider variety of companies, especially SMEs and small public authorities (i.e. local municipalities) to benefit from the availability of Digital Identity Wallets, but this kind of players should operate under a clear liability and auditing framework defined by the Commission.

Finally, the last consideration to article 6a) of the Proposal is related to point 6, stating "The European Digital Identity Wallets shall be issued under a notified electronic identification scheme of level of assurance 'high”'.". We agree on the fact that the Wallet shall guarantee a high level of protection, but we would like to point out the risk that the system could depend on the choices of non-European actors, harming the strategy of reaching a strategic autonomy. Even if the Proposal never specifies that the Wallet shall be mobile based, considering current technology trends it’s most likely that this will be the case: the LoA 'high' requires the presence of a certified cryptographic hardware, consequentially the security of the Wallet will be linked to the presence of a cryptographic chip or a secure element embedded into the mobile device. As we know, most of mobile manufacturers are located outside of Europe, and our continent has a strong dependence on microchips and semiconductors too: therefore, we suggest investigating alternative technologies and governance frameworks (i.e. cloud based Wallets) to reach the desired results in terms of both security level and user experience, without being necessarily forced into a lock-in with foreigner mobile manufacturers. In addition, to mitigate the single-point-of-failure risk, the regulation should push for the development of multiple digital Wallets based on different technologies in the same way there are multiple types of QSCD today on the market.  Harmonization and interoperability  We deem very useful that the Commission will adopt implementing acts to ensure a more harmonized approach on Conformity Assessment Reports and Conformity Assessment Body. Interoperability and free circulation of trust services in the internal market are needed to improve competition, innovation and value creation.  In our direct experience as a Pan-European QTSP, we often find barriers in offering the same digital trust solution in different Member States: national lawmakers and authorities sometimes demand additional requirements, usually in second-tier legislation mainly in accessing public services, de facto giving an unfair competitive advantage to local players. A clarifying example comes from our experience in Spain: the eIDAS certificates that shall be used to access public services shall be listed in a trust list created and maintained by an institution different from the eIDAS Supervisory Body. This institution is not accepting certificates issued by a non-Spanish CAs, even if the certificates profiles and the level of assurance are fully equivalent to local ones. The Proposal makes a huge step forward with article 11a) point 2 with the unique identifier for physical persons that we suggest could be extended to companies for example adopting the Legal Entity Identifiers (LEI), that is a perfect example of unique identifier. Nevertheless, we are worried that the approach of adding local requirements could affect the development of the new QTS introduced by the Proposal (Qualified Archiving of Electronic Documents, Qualified Electronic Attestation of Attributes, etc), so we propose to include in the Regulation the ban for the Members States to add further local requirements in the use of trust services, if not specifically approved by the Commission after a public consultation process with all involved stakeholders.

Qualified Website Authentication Certificates (QWACs) We agree with the Proposal to introduce a clear obligation for web-browsers to ensure that websites identity data be displayed in a user-friendly manner. We believe that, within the framework of the European regulations on the use of QWACs, a significant step has been taken in introducing an obligation which, if met by web browsers, can provide users with a better protection when browsing malicious and unsafe websites.  However, the risk is that a non-compliance of behalf of the browser providers produces no sanction, so we deem it important to introduce additional enforcements in order to increase users’ protection. We propose to introduce a hard responsibility on web-browsers, establishing their co-liability together with websites that don’t adopt QWAC, in case of damages to EU citizens, if the browser doesn’t highlight the user that the identity of the website owner is seriously in doubt.   Qualified attestation of Attributes Article 45f) of the EC eIDAS Proposal introduces the obligation to provide qualified electronic attestation of attributes services by means of a “separate legal entity”. Based on such a provision, a company could potentially create a separate legal entity, fully owned and consolidated, that becomes QTSP for attributes but, as matter of fact, acts on behalf of the company itself, that may be bigger, more solid and capable of providing a proper level of assurance. The purpose of such a provision is not clear to us and we would suggest that, if it was introduced just for addressing a safe and secure processing of personal data, this should rather be regulated under GDPR. We propose to remove the requirement, or at least reduce it for companies that are QTSPs providing similar trust services. In addition, even if the Proposal doesn’t have to design and address the possible business models of a QTSP providing qualified attestation of attributes, we need to highlight that the disposition of article 6a.4(b) could potentially harm the possibility to remunerate the activity, and therefore creating no incentive for the market of this type of services. From our experience, in an SSI (Self Sovereign Identity) paradigm, the most successful business model for credentials is the “verifier-pays-the issuer” monetization model, where the verifier remunerates the issuer of the credentials spent by the owner. If the issuer of the attribute credentials cannot receive any data on where the credential was spent, it becomes impossible for it to charge the due fee to the verifier. We agree on the fact that it’s very important that the issuer won’t profile the owner of the Wallet and related credentials, but at the same time it’s important that the Regulation won’t harm the monetization model. We suggest, at this stage, to specify the purpose of the requirement, for example setting limits to the user profiling, but still allowing the issuer to receive the minimum set of data required to trigger the monetization model (of course with the user’s consent).

Qualified Electronic Archiving Service  Article 45g of the eIDAS Proposal introduces the Qualified Electronic Archiving Service for archiving electronic documents as a new Qualified Trusted Services. We greatly appreciate the attention at EU level on the issue of archiving of documents, evidences, data and transactions and the growth of awareness that the Qualified preservation service for qualified electronic signatures is not sufficient to guarantee the maintenance of digital content over time.  In the design of the rules for the new service, the experience of the European Project E-Ark will be crucial, and we recommend that international standard such as ISO 14721:2012 – OAIS Reference Model, the METS schema and the PREMIS standard for metadata, the ISO 20614:2017 for data exchange and interoperability will become the grounds of the future Implementing Act. In addition, for digitally signed documents it will be important to guarantee that an archiving system cannot be qualified if it doesn’t rely on a Qualified Preservation Service for Qualified Signatures (art 33). Finally, given the large and growing volumes of digital transactions archived and preserved over time, we strongly suggest that Member States not define their own specific framework for the long-term archiving of documents, data and transactions, but they should rather refer to the eIDAS Qualified Electronic Archiving Service and benefit of a universal and interoperable standard.   Electronic Ledgers The eIDAS review Proposal defines, in article 3 n. 53), electronic ledger as “a tamper proof electronic record of data, providing authenticity and integrity of the data it contains, accuracy of their date and time, and of their chronological ordering”. The definition provided is widely open and, even with the help of preambles and other references in the Proposal, it is not clear what is the aim of the regulation on this topic, neither what an electronic ledger – indeed – is, according to this article. To clarify the approach, it may be useful to give more relevance to three points: a) Aim of the regulation - according to preamble 34, qualified electronic ledger shall enable governance framework and SSI. A centralized approach to identity is already into place with the current eIDAS regulation, but if the aim of the new Proposal is to move towards a more decentralized solution, it should be made more explicit b) Description of “electronic ledger” - given the broad definition, today a lot of tools can grant – and are used for – immutability and time stamping of data. It may be useful to add more details, to understand which is the value and the dignity of a new Qualified Trust Services, highlighting which tasks such a ledger can do better than an existing system. This clarification will also avoid a double regulation on the topic: a lot of the electronic ledger used today by Governments and Companies are already broadly regulated, internally by each Member State. c) Relationship with other European projects on decentralization and ledgers - Europe is pioneering on decentralized ledgers, SSI and Blockchain with a lot of valuable projects: for example, the EU Blockchain Observatory & Forum and the European Blockchain Services Infrastructure. Cybersecurity and NIS 2 Data Protection, Digital Trust and Cybersecurity are the three pillars of the digital world and Europe is setting the pace for its regulation with GDPR, eIDAS and NIS Directive(s).  The Proposal stresses the need that Digital Wallets, QTSPs and Trust Services guarantee the highest level of security and, in the article 24, fa), mandates QTSPs to comply with article 18 of the NIS 2 Directive. While we agree on the principle, we need to point out that this creates the risk of possible future misalignments between the security requirements applicable to QTSPs located in different Members States, harming the harmonization. Since NIS 2 is a Directive, each Member State can give a different interpretation during the transposition phase; if we also consider that the NIS- Supervisory Bodies could differ from the eIDAS ones, the possibility of conflicts arises, with impact on the harmonization and costs of providing similar trust services across EU.  To prevent this issue, a strict coordination between the Commission, ENISA, the Cooperation Network and the NIS Supervisory Bodies will be very important, as well as clear guidelines for QTSPs on how to comply, for Supervisory Bodies to guide the vigilance process, for CAB to harmonize the approaches during audits.   ***  We would be happy to elaborate further on our comments and Proposals.

Twilio Comments on the European Commission’s Proposalfor a
Framework for a European Digital Identity
Twilio -- both a user of public-sector identificationschemes and a provider of authenticationtools -- has followed the European Commission’s workon a European Digital Identity schemewith great interest. Twilio previously responded tothe Commission’s initial consultation on thismatter.
Twilio welcomes the increased cross-border use ofelectronic identification schemes by thepublic sector, and the requirement for mutual recognition.At the same time, Twilio is concernedabout the scope for interoperability of such schemeswith existing commercially availableauthentication tools, which may be easier to use andjust as secure.
Mandatory requirements for the private sector to usemust also be considered carefully. Twiliobelieves that such mandatory requirements should beconfined to the public sector.
Ongoing and close interaction with stakeholders isalso necessary. While existingstandardization bodies are important, the Commissionshould find a mechanism to allow accessfor all stakeholders that can offer expertise in developingtechnical solutions.

Twilio as a user and provider of e-ID solutions
Twilio offers services that require secure interactionswith public authorities. Examples includethe provision of legally required information regardingits operations in various Member States.Twilio has registered as an Electronic CommunicationService (ECS) in most of them. As aresult, Twilio needs to fulfill various obligations, including  regulatory reporting and verificationofcustomer identity. Twilio also has some public authoritycontracts, and those authorities offer theuse of electronic identities and of trusted servicesfor the execution of said contracts. Twilio’sregulatory and contracting teams therefore make regularuse of electronic ID and electronicsignatures.
Twilio also acts as a provider of identification services.The company offers authentication andverification services that complement initial identification.Twilio is not formally recognized as atrust service provider under the eIDAS framework becauseit does not currently meet anyexisting eIDAS categories of trust services. However,the European Union Agency forCybersecurity (ENISA) has listed Twilio in its overviewof the technological landscape of eIDsolutions as part of its report on eIDAS-compliantEID solutions (March 2020).
Twilio sees many opportunities in the proposal, especiallyin the context of customer vettingacross the EU. The broadening of the eIDAS frameworkto recognise a range of trust services isan important step. Their expansion, particularly fortwo-factor authentication, would help to driveoverall consumer confidence in the digital ecosystemand further uptake of the eIDASframework by both service providers and consumers.However, this benefit should not come atthe expense of eliminating the market for commercial authentication solutions and therebyimpeding further innovation in this field.
Interoperable solutions are key for user-friendlinessand innovation
The European Commission’s proposal foresees creatingdigital wallets thatallow users tosecurely handle interactions that require identificationas a legal person. This system would bebased on a common interface allowing interaction withall relevant parties. Twilio’sunderstanding is that digital wallets would be issuedby Member States or by entities recognisedby a Member State. In order for them to function seamlessly,it should be possible for publicsector authorities as well as for the private sectorto connect wallets with other, user-friendlyauthentication solutions. No mandatory requirements for the private sector
The regulation proposal suggests that private companiesthat use or are required to use stronguser authentication for online identification mustaccept the use of the European Digital IdentityWallet. It also proposes stricter security obligationsfor entities recognized as Trust ServiceProviders, and for Website Authentication Services.
This kind of mandated approach threatens the growthand development of innovative andpossibly higher quality identity-verification solutions.Whilea European digital identity frameworkpromises to become a particularly secure authenticationtool, it may not remain the most secureor the most user-friendly solution. There is alsoa real risk of a cumbersome solution emergingthat results in high administrative and technical burdens at an unreasonable cost onprivate-sector companies. The Commission should avoiddistorting innovation within the widerarea of identity verification.

Effective engagement with stakeholders
The Commission will have to establish technical andoperational specifications and referencestandards within 6 months of the legislation cominginto force. Twilio believes that it is extremelyimportant thatany technical solution aligns withglobal standards, and that thesereferencestandards are developed in partnership with industrystakeholders that have a history ofexperience in authentication services. Twilio wouldbe pleased to participate in any suchprocess. Private sector led authentication solutionshave made immense progress in recentyears, both in terms of security and user friendliness.
By contrast, poor user experience has been a commonweakness of existing governmentalidentity solutions. To avoid this, Twilio recommendsa combined government/ private sector ledapproach toward authentication solutions. This approachshould integrate a wide range ofstakeholders beyond those normally involved in suchtenders, and avoid ‘closed-shop’standardization exercises. Close involvement of expertsacross the private sector will support abetter overall ecosystem for authentication. In thiscontext, it is important to point out thatstandardization bodies like ETSI only allow for theparticipation of industry stakeholders afterpayment of a high annual membership fee. Smaller stakeholderswith less resources, or who donot engage in the full range of  ETSI activities,often find such an investment difficult to justify.
Twilio is looking forward to further discussion andcooperation concerning a European digital IDsolution.

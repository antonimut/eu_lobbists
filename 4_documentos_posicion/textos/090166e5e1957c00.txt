EBF’s assessment of European Commission’s 
proposal for a review of the Consumer Credit 
Directive (CCD) 
 
The EBF and its members have extensively contributed to the consultations prior the CCD 
review. Regrettably,proposals for  improvement on technological developments and 
administrative burden have not been considered. This is a missed opportunity to further 
integrate and modernise credit markets in Europe. A successful and efficient lending 
framework should consider both the needs of clients and providers. Markets that are 
modern, efficient and reduce non-performing exposures are in the interests of all parties. 
• Lack of proportionality in the scope. We agree that all lenders should adhere 
to the same rules on product governance, supervision and consumer protection 
when they provide similar products . However, addressing these concerns by 
extending the burdensome requirements of the CCD to all types of loans of all sizes, 
unnecessary increases administrative burden without increasing protection. Lack 
of proportionality leads to unreasonable administrative costs for small loans, that 
in turn are either more costly for clients or no longer offered  as uneconomical for 
lenders. Consumers in need will be more prone to resort to unregulated providers 
and loan sharks. 
• Increased market fragmentation brought by caps on costs /interest rates.  Caps 
are usually introduced upon repetitive market failures. This is not the case , in 
Europe lending products are competitive and offer a wide array of options. Set caps 
would increase fragmentation , defeat the purpose of supporting integrated 
markets, and prevent lenders to set an IR that reflects the market and the risk 
profile of the  client. Riskier customers would have an incentive to head to 
unregulated lenders that are not supervised  and don’t comply with the cap, nor 
with the anti-usury laws already in place . It would also reduce  existing business 
models and will prevent new entrants. This seems counter-effective for competitive 
pricing and normal market economy. 
• Information overload and requirements not up to the digital age.  Adding 
SECCO to SECCI is excessive, SECCO with additional important points like the right 
of withdrawal or early repayments would be sufficient. The SECCO has the opposite 
effect than the stated objective of clarifying information to consumers , as 
information overload confuses clients and leads to an over production of material, 
making the loan mo re expensive  without increasing quality of information  or 
reducing over indebtedness . Recent events ( e.g. pandemic, floods) show that 
consumers are dependent on fast and smooth help and increasingly rely on digital 
signatures. Also, the information to be included in advertising  should be only a 
minimum and then lead to a web page containing detailed examples and 
information.

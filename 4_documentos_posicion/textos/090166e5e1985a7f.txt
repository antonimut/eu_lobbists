A) Facebook’s approach to identity solutions
Identity solutions, such as ID verification (‘IDV’),are complex and nuanced fromtechnological, policy, and legal perspectives. Asnoted in the United NationsStrategy for Legal Identity for All, “sincethe collection,use, sharing, accessing,merging and otherwise processing of personal datain legal identity systemsconstitutes an interference with the right to privacyand other rights, States mustdemonstrate that each of these acts have a legitimateobjective and are anecessary and proportionate means to achieve thatobjective.”
2
As reflected in the European Commission’s Proposal,there are a variety ofcircumstances under which an identity solution maybe desirable, and equallythere is a variety of identity solutions for thosecircumstances. Additionally, indeveloping such solutions, there are also a numberof competing equities whichmust be considered, like balancing the right to privacyand free expression, inarriving at the right solution which is the most appropriatefor the circumstance inquestion.
We have adopted an approach to identity solutionswhich considers a “tailoredand proportionate” analysis.
Through this approach, we’ve determined that ID verificationis the right solutionfor a limited number of specific circumstances. Thisis on the grounds that inthese circumstances, this solution is proportionateto the risk we’re addressing,and processing such sensitive data is necessary inthat regard.
We set out some of these circumstances below.
1.Account ownership and representationA critical part of building a community on Facebookis ensuring that people arewho they say they are.
That’s why we verify an ID when an account has beencompromised - we want tomake sure that the right person is receiving the rightpermission for the rightaccount.

B) Facebook’s Approach to Individual Control and Privacy
At the heart of Facebook’s product philosophy is thatgreat experiences start bygiving people control, and doing so in a way whichprotects their privacy.
For example, Facebook Login is a fast and convenient way for people to createaccounts and log into apps across multiple platforms. This identity solutionsupports many permissions which determine which informationpeoplechoosetoshare with an app. Individuals always have precisecontrol over what they chooseto approve.
As the Proposal notes, there are practical difficultiesin managing attributes(elements of personal information) that can be reliablydisclosed to third parties.With Facebook Login, people can choose which informationthey share, and weare committed to ensuring that tens of millions ofpeople who use FacebookLogin every day have a safe, reliable and consistentexperience.
As the provision of such identity solutions in theEU involves collecting andprocessing personal data in accordance with the GeneralData ProtectionRegulation

C) Identity Verification and Age Verification
Regarding the use of the European Digital Identityin the context of ageverification, careful consideration should be affordedin ensuring that identityverification and age verification are not seen astwo sides of the same coin. Thereare numerous alternative means of analysing or verifyingage which respect thebest interests of young people, and ensure their safety,without the need for agovernment-issued ID, which many may not hold.
We believe that effectively verifying or analysingage should not focus on asingle-step process, but rather a collection of ongoingefforts that workdynamically to provide effective solutions.
We take a continuous, multi-layered approach to understandingand refining theaccuracy of the age of individuals and have investedin a suite of tools to meet thisobjective. We are committed to developing additionaltechnology-driven solutions- including using machine learning - to increase ourconfidence about people’sages.

D)Authenticity and Alternatives to ID Verification
Authenticity is the cornerstone of our Facebook communityand empoweringpeople to build their communities online -- beyondfriends and family -- is part ofour mission.
Authenticity means that people are able to expressthemselves in a way thatallows them to connect with their communities andwe want people to representthemselves on our apps as they do in everyday life.
People sometimes emphasise different aspects of whothey are, depending onwhere they are and who they’re with, and to discoverand participate inconversations across audiences like friends, family,and work colleagues. For manypeople,who they are according to an ID is not thepersona, or the authentic self,they are presenting to the world.We think it’s importantto enable people toexpress their individuality while also ensuring peopleknow who they’reconnecting with on our apps.
We believe that authenticity helps create a community where people areaccountable to each other, and to Facebook, in meaningfulways.We want to allowfor the range of diverse ways that identity is expressedacross our globalcommunity, while also preventing impersonation andidentity misrepresentation.
This is why we believe there are alternatives to IDverification that may be bettersuited as an identity solution in particular circumstances.
Some examples of alternatives being used by companiesaround the world,including Facebook, are:- email or phone verification, which can be useful forverifying a person behindan account;- two-factor authentication (“2-Fac”), which can beuseful for verifyingconsistent account ownership and access over time;- location verification, which may help in facilitatingverification that a person iswhere they claim, if they make representations abouttheir location;- selfie video, which can be an appropriate tool forverifying that a real person isbehind an account and in some instances that a personrepresents themselvesthe same way online and in the real world; and- othertechnologies that can provide additive signalsto help confirm that aperson is likely to be authentic.
Facebook’s measures to detect fake accounts are anotherillustration of howthere are effective and proportionate alternativesolutions to ID verification whichare better-suited to mitigate risks. We have developedthese measures preciselybecause they avoid the need for us to collect sensitiveinformation, such asgovernment IDs.
For these situations, we have built technology thatcan detect and block accountseven before they are created. For example, our systemlooks for signals thatindicate if accounts are created en masse from onelocation and then blocksuspected IP addresses from creating accounts.
Our advanced detection systems also look for potentialfake accounts as soon asthey sign-up, by spotting signs of malicious or suspiciousbehaviour (e.g., scriptedbehaviour). These systems use a combination of signalssuch as patterns of usingsuspicious email addresses, suspicious actions, orother signals previouslyassociated with other fake accounts we have removed.
Most fake accounts we remove are blocked within minutes of their creation -- andbefore we ask for any ID verification.  For example,inQ2 2021, Facebook disabled1.7 billion 'fake accounts' globally with 99.8% proactivedetection.
E)Additional Issues for Consideration regarding IDVerification
In determining whether to use ID verification forauthentication, we think it’simportant to strike the right balance between protectingpeople’s privacy,well-being, and access to online services (given theprofound implications for theability to connect and to free expression), takinginto account technical andoperational constraints involved in implementing IDverification systems.
In establishing a voluntary framework for a EuropeanDigital Identity, we wouldlike to advance a number of additional issues forconsideration regarding IDverification as a singular identity solution for theEU Single Digital Market.
ID verification is not always effective for addressingparticular risks, and we thinkit is important that services can have available arange of identity solutions toselect the one that is most appropriate for the circumstancesin question.
ID verification as a singular identity solution raisesa number of competinginterests which need to be carefully considered, suchas the impact on:
(i)Fundamental Rights and Freedoms- A tailored and proportionate approach to ID verificationensures thatservices do not unnecessarily block people’s accessto those services, toopportunities, education, and to connect with othersor expressthemselves.
- In particular, requiring ID verification could excludemarginalised groups,like those living in poverty, the trans community,migrants, refugees, asylumseekers, stateless people, survivors of domestic abuse,older people, youth,people living in rural and remote areas -- many ofwhom don’t have easyaccess to these kinds of documents or documents thataccurately reflectthe way they represent themselves in daily life.
(ii)Privacy- ID verification can reveal a lot of sensitive information.We believe thatpeople should have control over how much of theiridentity information isshared with others, including governments, companies,and individuals.
- This is especially important when it comes to government IDs, whichcontain some of the most sensitive pieces of informationabout anindividual and, sometimes, their relatives.
- IDs include numbers that are issued by governmentsand are often used toaccess government services or benefits, which requirepaying particularattention to the actual need to collect them and,if so, how to preventmisuse in such a sensitive area.
- In some cases, government IDs can also include othersensitive categoriesof data, such biometric and health information.
- Collecting and analysing government IDs (or similarofficial identificationdocuments) is a sensitive area and online platformslike Facebook areexpected, and required, to limit their processingto what is necessary tomitigate a specified risk and proportionate to thatrisk.
- If companies were to default, or were mandated, touse ID verification toaddress inauthenticity in all cases, irrespectiveof the risk and the availablealternatives, that would result in an unnecessaryencroachment of people’sprivacy -- without certainty in terms of resolvingthe relevant threat toauthenticity.
(iii)Data Security and Practicality- It is important that the legislation creating anyvoluntary identityframework, specifies that absent a valid legal process,the entities chargedwith verifying electronic IDs should not be able toshare verificationrequests, related information, or other related querieswith othergovernment bodies (including law enforcement and nationalsecurityagencies) or third parties.
- In order for identity solutions to be able to scaleand function across allproducts and services and across all locations wherepeople use them,frameworks should ensure that they are not so prescriptiveas to rendersuch solutions impractical, or unsustainable overthe long term.
To avoid such conflicts among competing interests,identity solutions shouldfollow a risk-based approach which considers the natureof the risk to bemitigated, if there are a range of suitable identitysolutions which would achievethat mitigation, and whether ID verification is theappropriate authenticationsolution in light of the scope of risk and competingequities.
Regulatory initiatives should consider that servicesrequire flexibility to evaluateand adopt new solutions and technologies, and notpreclude them from doing so.This would ensure that services can avoid collectingdisproportionate and/orsensitive information through ID documents but stillfacilitate the authenticationof individuals (or the relevant legitimate goal pursued,including age analysis) inhigher-risk activities.
Conclusion
Facebook believes that approaches to identity solutionsshould: (i) be tailored toaddress specific inauthenticity risks;  and (ii) onlycollect additional data about aperson that is necessary and proportionate.
We welcome the guidance of regulators to help publicand private sector servicesin the Single Digital Market to strike this balance,and the European Commission’sProposal is an important contribution as industryand policymakers collaborate ondeveloping the most effective solutions for thesecomplex and nuancedchallenges.
As the co-legislators consider this Proposal, it isimportant that the framework fora European Digital Identity is designed to preventand address inauthenticitywhile also enabling people to connect with their communitiesand expressthemselves authentically.
This is why we believe a future European Digital Identityregime should notpreclude other identity solutions for lower-risk onlineactivities and more robust,regulated options for higher-risk activities.
Ensuring a voluntary, tailored and proportionate approachto identity solutionswill ensure that people are not unnecessarily, orinadvertently, blocked fromaccessing the internet to connect with others or expressthemselves -- especiallywhen it comes to marginalised and disenfranchisedpopulations -- or that people’s(sensitive or not) personal information is unnecessarilycollected.
We are always working to improve our policies andprocesses to maintainauthenticity and ensure people who use our apps arewho they say they are.
We will continue working to build solutions that addressinauthenticity on ourplatform and for solutions that involve ID verification.We will continue to explorealternatives that account for other important factorslike the ability to connectand self-express, privacy, and data security.
Developing responsible, effective, practicable androbust identity solutionsrequires first working to consider the complex challengesinvolved andcollaboratively building the frameworks, standards,and best practices that will bea necessary foundation and complement to future regulations.
We are excited about the creation of voluntary Europeanidentity solutions, whichcan be used by people when needed to access publicand private services asappropriate in a way which is equitable, and inclusive.Facebook welcomes theEuropean Commission's proposal and is eager to collaboratewith theco-legislators on the development of this Proposal.

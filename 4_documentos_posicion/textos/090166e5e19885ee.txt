Subject: ESD position on the EC Proposal for a Regulation of the European Parliament and of the Council, 
amending Regulation (EU) No 910/2014 as regards establishing a framework for a European Digital Identity. 
Dear European Commission, 
We, the undersigned organizations, represent the leading European trust service providers
(TSPs): European Signature Dialog (ESD) enables secure digital interactions all across
Europe - between public entities, businesses and individuals. We all experience every day
the crucial challenges of trustworthiness, security and cross-border interoperability of digital
services and hence the challenges of acceptance by business and consumers alike. The
effective work in ESD shows that TSPs work very well together in the interest of
harmonization, even though we are in competition with each other. The large number of
active users of trust services shows that we are constantly getting closer to achieving our
goal of helping Europe into the new digital age.
A view at the current crises around the COVID-19 outbreak proofs that digital technologies
like e-Trust infrastructures are essential to keep businesses operative and to mitigate the far-
reaching negative effects on the wider economy. Therefore, we highly welcome the
European Comission’s Proposal for the revised eIDAS regulation. It is very ambitious and it
is an important step forward to harmonize the European Digital Single Market.

We would like to highlight the fve most important advances: 
1. European Digital Identity Wallet : It will facilitate access to value added Trust Services
such as qualified signature services, e-Delivery with real technical interoperability.
2. Supervision of qualifed trust service providers:  It will create comparable Qualified
Trust Services. Here the Commission shall, by means of implementing acts, establish
reference number for the following standards (…): auditing rules, auditing requirements. 
3. New Qualifed Trust Services: It will offer new value-added concepts, new
offers and new markets (Qualified electronic attestation of attributes, e-Archiving,
distributed ledgers...).
4. Provision of QWAC recognition for browsers : It essentially strengthens consumer
protection.
5. Obligation for implementing acts that reference regulatory standards : It facilitates
true interoperability.
But although the current version has already cleared up many ambiguities regarding the
original regulation, we also want to stress the three most critical amendments to be made:
1. Replace Art 19 (Security requirements applicable to trust service providers) and
replace by a link to «NIS2»:  It could lead to differences in implementation levels,
unfair market…as NIS2 is a Directive, and it will probably be transposed differently in
each Member States.
2. Clarify the use of QES for the EDIW:  It contains an identification
credential that can be used to obtain a QCert. Clarify that the wallet
is not a QSCD as it doesn't contain any certifica te, but it can act as an identification
and authorization mean.

THREE eIDAS IMPROVEMENT PROGRAMS
1. OPERATIVE PROGRAM : Increase safety, effciency and trustworthiness of cross-
border interactions 
This entails the following measures:
• Standardize the certification process and requirements for QSCD / HSM
through an implementing decision referring to the newer ETSI standards
• Publish baseline rules for the assessment of trustworthy systems and the
reliability of processes supporting them
• Extend the EU Trusted List with new qualified service type (“pure player”
signature service)
• Create legal effects for each Qualified Trust Services including archiving
services, to encourage its deployment with full confidence

• Harmonize the evaluation of alternative methods and certification processes,
especially for innovation such as remote face to face and new authentication
solutions
2. HARMONIZATION PROGRAM: Enforce harmonization and standardization across 
member states
This entails the following measures:
• Specify individual articles in the areas of trustworthiness of systems and 
products, functionality and security of qualified signature services, equality of 
national qualification procedures and requirements as well as interoperability 
and cross-border deployment of trust services
• Standardize the security requirements for remote qualified signature process 
and officially reference existing CEN and ETSI standards
• Standardize the accreditation process for Conformity assessment Bodies 
(CAB), using, for instance, ETSI EN 319 403 standards
• Create a set of baselines for auditing rules and a baseline for audit plan for 
each trust service
3. SUPPORT PROGRAMM: Establish an advisory/administrative body to support the 
industry by implementing eIDAS
This entails the following measures: 
• Produce and publish implementing acts to create real interoperability
• Create a way to simply validate an electronic signature without relying on
private software

The Proposal doesn’t have to design and address the possible business model of a 
QTSP providing qualified attestation of attributes, but this article seriously harms 
the possibility to remunerate the activity. 
In the SSI paradigm, the most successful business model to provide credentials is 
the “verifier-pays-the issuer” monetization model, where the verifier remunerates 
the issuer of the credentials spent by the owner at the access of the online service. 
If the issuer of the attribute credentials cannot receive any information on where 
the credential has been spent, it becomes concretely impossible for it to charge the 
due fee to the verifier.  
We see the need to be more specific, permitting that the issuer of attributes can 
receive some information that are important for the monetization model.

The definition provided is widely open and, even with the help of preambles and other 
references in the proposal, it is not clear what is the aim of the regulation on this topic, neither 
what an electronic ledger – indeed – is, according to this article. 
To clarify the approach, it may be useful to give more relevance to three points: 
• Aim of the regulation: according to preamble 34, qualified electronic ledger shall enable 
governance framework and SSI. A centralized approach to identity is already into place with 
the current eIDAS regulation, but if the aim of the new proposal is to move towards a more 
decentralized solution, it should be made more explicit. 
• Description of “electronic ledger”: given the broad definition, today a lot of tools can grant – 
and are used for – immutability and time stamping of data. It may be useful to add more 
details in order to understand which is the value and the dignity of a new Qualified Trust 
Services, highlighting which tasks such a ledger can do better than an existing systems. This 
clarification will also avoid a double-regulation on the topic: a lot of the electronic ledger 
used today by Governments and Companies are already broadly regulated, internally by 
each Member State. 
• Relationship with other European projects on decentralization and ledgers: for example the 
EU Blockchain Observatory & Forum and the European Blockchain Services Infrastructure. To 
avoid misinterpretation of the ledger defined by the proposal, it may be useful to clarify the 
relationship between said projects and eIDAS.   
Furthermore, asking a ledger to grant “data authenticity” is a hard task: a better description of 
such requirements is probably needed.

General 
Comment: 
It may be helpful to introduce new definitions of the types of electronic signature/seal, and types of advanced electronic 
signature/seal, and by amending the existing definitions of qualified electronic signature/seal as types of signatures, also 
amending the referenced articles, like 25, 27, 35, 37, we consider the Regulation will become much more clear on the 
following: 
• The correct relation between the terms “qualified electronic signature” and “advanced electronic signature” 
• The fact that the so-called procedural difficulties that come with the use of a qualified signature are given exclusively by 
the need to meet the requirements of the advanced signature referred to in art. 26. 
• Any other type of advanced electronic signature different from the qualified electronic signature, involves technical and 
procedural mechanisms similar to the qualified signature, in order to meet the requirements referred to in art. 26. (+)

3. Preliminary reﬂections on the Regulatory proposal
The proposed revision of the eIDAS regulation includes a number of reasonable policyproposals to enhance the interoperability and use of national digital IDs across the EU.These, however, do not drive our core interest inthe proposal.
Rather, we are moved to engage in these discussions owing to the fact that certainprovisionsintheproposedrevisionoftheeIDASregulationwouldinadvertentlyexposeEUresidents to untenable security risks, and rollback years of advancements insecurityonthe web.
Thesewebsecurityvulnerabilitiesmanifest inArticle45ofthelegislativeproposal, whichdeals with the recognition of Qualiﬁed Website Authentication Certiﬁcates (QWACs). Ifadopted as is, this provision would compel all browsers oﬀered in Europe, includingFirefox, tolowertherigorousstandardsforauthenticationonthewebthathavedevelopedover the last two decades by individual browser vendors and in multi-stakeholder fora(notably the CA/Browser Forum). As such, ourability toplayourroleinenablingsecuree-commerce and e-government in Europe, and protect EU residents fromcyberattackswould be severely undermined.
Inwhatfollows, wewilloutlinepreciselyhowthiswebsecurityconcernmaterialisesinthedraft eIDASregulation, andproviderecommendationsforhowit canbeaddressedbytheEU Council and the European Parliament in the co-decision procedure. But in order tounderstandpreciselywhyarticle45issoproblematicfromawebsitesecurityperspective,we must ﬁrst explain how website authentication works today and the crucial rolebrowsers and certiﬁcate authorities play.

The website security ecosystem- certiﬁcate authorities and browser rootprograms
Thedigital certiﬁcatesusedbybrowsersforwebsiteauthenticationareissuedbyseparateentities called “certiﬁcate authorities” (CAs). These organisations are responsible forverifying the website server’s identity before issuing a certiﬁcate. This is a signiﬁcantresponsibility – if a Certiﬁcate Authority issues a website certiﬁcate that is incorrect,whetherformalignintentorbecauseofpooroperationalprocesses–theconsequencesforEU residents and organisations can be catastrophic. In other words, for websiteauthentication to work, and by extension e-commerce and e-government, CertiﬁcateAuthoritiesmust be trustworthy.
To ensure this trust, most major browsers have a rigorous vetting process before aCertiﬁcateAuthoritycanenterits“rootprogram”(i.e. thesetofcertiﬁcateauthoritiesthatthebrowserrecognisesasbeingabletoissuetrustabledigitalcertiﬁcates). Rootprogramsvet candidateCertiﬁcateAuthoritiesbasedonpublic,establishedcriteria.Rootprogramsalso undertake recurring trust assessments, and can remove CAs which violate thoserequirements.

Qualiﬁed Website Authentication Certiﬁcates (QWACs) in the eIDAS
The existing eIDAS Regulation, passed in 2014, introduced a parallel framework forwebsite certiﬁcates and authentication, known as ‘Qualiﬁed Website AuthenticationCertiﬁcates’.
In the existing eIDAS regulation, a QWACis acertiﬁcateforwebsiteauthenticationthatmakes it possible to authenticate a website and links the website to thenatural orlegalpersontowhomthecertiﬁcateisissued. Theyweremotivatedbyfallout of theDigiNotarattack and inspired by Extended Validation TLS certiﬁcates. So-called ‘TrustedServiceProviders’ (TSPs) issue QWACs, and in eﬀect, seek to play the role of CertiﬁcateAuthorities in the website security ecosystem.
We and the broader browser and CAcommunity have long-standing concerns with theQWACsmodel andsomeof itssuggesteduse-cases. Mostnotably, TSPs–theentitiesthatissue QWACs under the existing eIDAS regulation –are subject to signiﬁcantly weakersecurity, transparency, and auditing requirements compared to the root programsoperated by Mozilla and other browser vendors. 6. Recommendations to address website authenticationissues in Article 45
Based on the above, the elements of the legislativeproposal that concern QWACs, TSPs,and browsers’ relation to themmust be revisited inthe co-decision procedure.
Speciﬁcally, the co-legislators should amend theproposal such that browsers are notforced to support QWACs nor be compelled to mandatorilyinclude certiﬁcate authorities(CAs) notiﬁed by member states as QWAC-issuing TSPsin their browser root programs.

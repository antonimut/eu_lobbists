Confartigianato Imprese considers that the discussion of the Data Act should take into due consideration not only the interests of large economic, financial, and productive realities but that it should outline a balanced framework and identify appropriate access to data also for smaller businesses, allowing them to exploit all the potential offered by the development of the digital market, without unjustified barriers or constraints on access. This represents a major step towards ensuring the general principles of competition that drive the functioning of the European single market, for businesses of all sizes and at all levels of the value chain.  For that reason we are strongly convinced that for the future it would be appropriate to differentiate consultations between companies and SMEs Organisations, because otherwise it is difficult to answer their calibrated questions about companies and the customer/supplier relationship. To supplement our answers to the consultaion, please find here below the main messages that as Confartigianato Imprese we would like to underline more:   o SMEs increasingly rely on (IoT) platforms to provide access to data. Processed data that is relevant to the crafts sector for maintenance and repair services in downstream markets includes mixed data sets that combine technical data with customer data and have thus been processed into information data, i.e. metadata. Since downstream markets are always characterised by B2C relationships, they inevitably also contain personal data dimensions. Only this processed data can provide the skilled crafts enterprises with the relevant information required for their activities in downstream markets.  o However, this access to data is often denied by manufacturers due to conflicts of interest. Through the development of platforms and the creation of digital ecosystems, the latter are increasingly penetrating the domain of the skilled crafts and are increasingly obstructing the creation of value. Cars, heating technology, smart homes and technical installations are just some of the sectors affected by this trend.

o The advantages of fair data exchange for the customer are obvious: on the one hand, craftsmen can offer their customers a much more individual service than just the manufacturer's standard product, thereby reducing the value-added costs and achieving a 30-40% more favourable price advantage for the customer. In addition, lock-in effects with the customer can be prevented. In the area of IoT platforms and their subsequent application in the smart home sector, investments are often made for 20 to 30 years. Various apps, such as "Home Connect", take away the possibility of offering maintenance or repair of IoT objects. Since customers currently have neither the possibility to directly access their data nor the technical ability to give their consent to the desired company to analyse the data, they are trapped in such a system for 20 years due to the lock-in effects. Finally, a fair exchange of data can ensure an optimised use of resources for the customer. o The European Commission's decision to leave it up to industry so far to develop interoperability formats that enable the portability of customer and thus personal data has not shown any improvement for SMEs.   o Contractual solutions would be a first step in the right direction, but are very complex to implement and in reality cannot guarantee the necessary changes in B2B relationships for SMEs and thus provide them with adequate access to data due to the different bargaining power. Moreover, in practice, no data owner would use voluntary model contract terms.   o Horizontal access modalities do not help either if they presuppose a sectoral access right that does not exist in practice. The EU should therefore intervene in a regulatory manner to prevent market foreclosure. A kind of "black list" in which unfair behaviour is listed and clearly prohibited could also be helpful.  o It is crucial to shape the market conditions in such a way that customers are free to decide who they give access to their data. All companies along the value chain should have equal access to data relevant to the development of new services for the customer. This means that all companies that have obtained the consent of the user of a device/machine should be able to analyse the data and offer services to the user.   o It is therefore necessary that the service providers have direct communication access to their (potential) customers via the corresponding smart devices / devices (display). This is also not guaranteed under the current legal situation.

o Data access should be "fair by design". Data solutions should be inherent in the device to allow real-time access to the data generated by the device and to provide a customer-facing communication interface.  o The creation of exclusive property rights regarding data is not appropriate. Data can be used multiple times without losing value. Moreover, data can be used in parallel by several users. Therefore, exclusive property rights are not the right way to regulate the use of data. Accordingly, the discussion should not be about property rights, but about rights of use to which several people can be entitled in parallel. This applies irrespective of the type of data, but especially to machine-generated data.

Belron® is the world’s leading vehicle glass repair, replacement, and recalibration service provider, operating 
in over 25 countries in Europe with brands such as Carglass®, Autoglass® and Safelite®.  In the vehicle 
aftermarket industry, the use of data is becoming increasingly important with the advent of the connected 
car. The large amounts of data generated and the data-sharing arrangements among stakeholders do – and 
will continue to – have profound repercussions on the connected car service market, consumers’ welfare, and 
on innovation.  
 
Belron supports the work being conducted by the European Commission on the Data Act and welcomes the 
opportunity to respond to its public consultation.  
  
The Data Act offers an opportunity to address important issues arising from Business to Business (B2B) data 
sharing, to ensure greater availability of data to businesses that provide services to equipment that they 
have not necessarily manufacturers (Aftermarket Providers). As more and more products become 
connected via the Internet of Things (IOT) and produce increasing amounts of data, a holistic approach to 
access to data – both generally but particularly for Aftermarket Providers (across all industries) is critical.  
Access to data is important to ensure and enable undistorted competition among all market players (i.e. 
between OEMs and Aftermarket Providers) which ultimately benefits consumers, and to truly allow for 
innovation, as access to data is a key facilitator in the ability to create new service offerings previously 
unthought of.   
 
Belron has addressed the questionnaire in its capacity as a business that seeks, and relies on, access to data 
both from objects (i.e. vehicles), and from the original equipment manufacturers (OEMs) of those objects. In 
this sense, Belron is an “Aftermarket Provider” of services to customers.

It is in this capacity that Belron has responded to sections on B2B data sharing II and rights (II) on non-
personal Internet-of-Things data stemming from professional use (IV) of the public consultation.  In relation 
to section IV, while Belron notes the question is framed in terms of non-personal data collected from objects 
used in a professional sense (e.g., farming machinery), Belron considers it appropriate to address this 
question and urges that consideration of how such data is made available is also given to non-personal data 
collected from objects that are used in an everyday sense, for example the connected car.  This is particularly 
important from Belron’s perspective. Technological advances being made in vehicle manufacturing mean 
that from 2022, all vehicles produced will be “connected” and have the ability to transmit data (both non-
personal and potentially personal).  It is crucial to address access to this data to ensure that the Aftermarket 
industry (which in the context of automotive aftermarket services has been recognised by the European 
Commission as a market distinct from the sale of vehicles) are provided with access to such data on a level 
that is no worse than access that OEMs themselves and/or any third party are provided. 
 
Belron has also addressed the sections related to smart contracts (III), cloud services portability for business 
users (V) and Intellectual Property Rights (VII) of the public consultation.  In relation to section VII, Belron 
notes the need to cautiously differentiate data (and access to data) that is simply generated – either directly 
from a machine (e.g., a vehicle), or is a simple derivative from the raw data generated in a usable format – 
from data that is processed within a database (e.g., data that is added to, potentially from multiple sources, 
and then manipulated or arranged in a particular format within a database such that protection is afforded). 
Belron argues that for generated data access on a B2B data sharing basis needs to be required,  and would 
recommend considers that caution needs to be taken to ensure that Intellectual Property Rights are not 
abused so as to prevent access to and use of generated or ‘raw’ data. 
 
Belron would be happy to discuss any of the above points in additional detail with the European Commission 
as it progresses in its work. 
 
About Belron  
Belron® is the parent company for a number of well-known vehicle glass repair and replacement businesses, such as 
Carglass®, Autoglass® and Safelite®. We operate in 35 countries with 28,000 employees, serving 15 million consumers 
a year. Customer service is our key focus and we are proud of our high customer feedback score (average NPS in 2017 
was 83%). More recently, our services have been extended to include vehicle body repair, including full collision 
damage. This is our purpose: to make a difference by solving people’s problems with real care.   
Europe is a very important region for Belron, with three out of our four largest markets here, with over 13,000 
employees in the EU. Belron is part of the automotive aftermarket which in 2017, employed 2.8m people in the EU 
across 500,000 businesses.  When you compare this to the vehicle manufacturing industry, which has 2.5m people with 
around 300 production and assembly plants in the EU, it is significant.

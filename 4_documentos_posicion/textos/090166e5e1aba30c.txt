Q10 Which services/ products based on data sharing exist/ are under development in your sector 
and what type of data are needed for these purposes? 
 
Data sharing / access is key to rising  services unlocked by smartphones/smart TVs/UGC platforms that are 
operated by large gatekeepers, such as addressable television and advertising, more effective content delivery 
and to anticipate consumers preferences in terms of content user experience. A ccurate data on our own 
content’s consumption is needed for this to the final benefit of the public. 
Q11 What benefits from data sharing do you expect to be reaped in your sector?  
 
Content consumption data is key to improve AV products (to inform future d ecisions and improve the 
production process) and delivery, optimise content availability windows, improve user experience and 
recommendations, communicate effectively on programmes, and to optimize advertising, thus supporting fair 
competition and an effec tive level playing field between data -intensive online platforms and commercial 
broadcasters. 
 
Q12 Please indicate the type of difficulties / issues 
 
GDPR is used by gatekeepers data holders to restrict access to data to third parties. As they own 
platforms/OS/etc. they control the presentation of the consent for users in a way to facilitate the user consent 
for themselves, and tighten the  consent conditions and presentation  for others  third parties. Furthermore, 
often different interpretations of existing regulations make it difficult to have a defined framework for data 
exchange. 
 
Q20 What, in you view, could be the benefits or risks of the options mentioned in the three 
previous questions, for example in relation to incentives for data collection, competi tiveness and 
administrative burden 
 
The adoption of contractual clauses, including optional ones, would provide greater guarantees of legal 
certainty and protection of competition. Conditions of access and storage should be provided to ensure not 
only the protection of personal data, but also the protection of trade secrets and intellectual property rights 
 
Q21 Regarding data access at fair, reasonable, proportionate, transparent and non -discriminatory 
conditions, which of the following elements do you cons ider most relevant to increase data 
sharing? 
 
 The party sharing data obtains a reasonable yield on investment and the party requesting access to 
data pays a reasonable fee 
 Distinctions can be made depending on the type of data or the purpose of its use 
 Availability of standards for interoperability that would allow data sharing and exploitation at a low 
marginal cost (in terms of time and money) 
 Other:  Service providers (eg. Commercial broadcasters) should have access to some of the data held 
by large gate keeper intermediaries (eg. device manufacturers, large UGC platforms and operating 
systems) derived from the consumption of their service at FRAND terms.

Q23 Do you consider that smart contracts could be an effective tool to technically implement the 
data access and use in the context of co -generated IoT data, in particular where the transfer is not 
only one-off but would involve some form of continuous data sharing? 
Yes. The use of smart contracts could enable B2B data sharing in a safe and rapid manner. We would welcome 
the adoption of measures aimed at implementing these instruments as they would support the aims of the 
Regulation. 
 
Section VI Complementing the portability right under Article 20 GDPR 
 
It is presumed that individual producers of 'smart co nnected objects' are entitled to let these smart objects 
generate any kind of data, without any conceptual limitation as to the nature of the data generated and the 
purposes of collection/use.  Perhaps, before reflecting on whether third-party access to thi s data  should be 
allowed or not (even if the data owner provides consent), the regulator should define what data can or cannot 
be generated.  
 
Section VII Intellectual Property Rights – Protection of Database s - In your view, how are 
intellectual property (IP) rights (including the sui generis database right) and trade secrets relevant 
for business-to-business sharing of data? 
 
European commercial broadcasters invest billions of euros each year in content and should therefore have 
access to valuable data generated from the consumption of their content, in which they hold IP rights , 
independent of the place/system where there are consumed, for the final benefit of the public.

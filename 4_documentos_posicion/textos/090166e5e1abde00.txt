Fujitsu’s comments to the European Commission’s Data Act Public Consultation 
 
Fujitsu understands the need of a European Legislative Framework for Data and Data Economy. 
The main objective of any initiative within the upcoming Legislative Framework should be to improve access to Data in 
business-to-business (B2B) or business -to-government (B2G) scenarios by focusing on incentives for companies to 
engage in data sharing.  
Fujitsu strongly believes that at this stage, any approach based on mandatory requirements  should be avoided. The 
European Commission should look at new tools, incenti ves and soft -law based mechanisms  instead. Opting for 
Regulatory approached in a phase where the Data Economy is starting flourishing but where it is difficult to predict the 
impact of strict Regulation, could have the opposite effect of discouraging companies to innovate, invest and cooperate 
by exchanging Data.  
It is equally important to put in place measures to encourage and support the flow of non-personal data across borders 
with the Data Act.  Any measure potentially restricting international data flows should be avoided. This will enable EU 
businesses to grow and compete internationally.   
The aim of the Data Act to increase B2B and B2G Data sharing is perfectly in line with the Fujitsu view and position. Our 
Company will put lot effort to support the European Commission in this direction. 
One of the most significant barriers in B2B and B2G business to grow is the creation of trust among the different parties 
and among entire ecosystems. Clear rules, initiatives to harmonize different Legislations among EU Member States and 
more incentives should be the way forward.  In many cases companies are not willing to share their Data with othe rs 
even withing a business contract. This often create s barriers for the adoption of new solutions and new technologies 
that could improve their business and the life of many citizens. These challenges should be overcome by a clear set of 
soft rules that are not creating obligations but are providing incentives for a safe, secured and transparent way of sharing 
the Data following a purpose driven based approach. This will help in particular SMEs and start-ups in Europe.  
Fujitsu also suggests the European Commission not only to ensure but to increase a positive dialogue at international 
level in particular with Countries such as Japan where recent agreements on Data Flow and Digital related Topics have 
been establish. This should improve the B2B cooperation as well as the B2G. The Data Flow with Trust initiative of the 
Japanese Government got already a good political support from the E uropean Commission . We believe tha t such 
dialogue and mutual support should increase in the coming months in order to ensure international Data Transfer and 
consequently technology Transfer and new Business opportunities on both sides. Societies, Citizens and Companies will 
benefit from this a lot. 
Global, industry -driven standards agreed within international standard development bodies would be  the most 
appropriate technical solutions to comply with regulatory requirements. Mandating technical specifica tions in this 
context would risk fragmentation in global markets and potentially create regulatory divergences globally. The 
European Commission should also provide clear guidance on legal compliance, especially with the GDPR. Without 
standardized best practices, organizations may be reluctant to assume liability risks of using data from third parties. 
Such issues can be addressed by endorsing industry standards and model data governance frameworks 
In this sense we strongly encourage the European Commission to always keep strong communication channels with 
Industry and other stakeholders and involve them in all phases of this discussion before and after the approval of the 
Data Act. This would ensure the approval and implementation of a new framework closer to the real needs of business 
and society.

We support the idea  to introduce model contract terms recommended by the Commission as mentioned in the 
questionnaire of the consultation. We see this as an innovative tool to promote trust in B2B data sharing arrangements 
and increase access to data sharing for SMEs. A key condition should be that  such mechanism remains voluntary and 
does not prejudice the possibility for companies to dispose of their data and freely conclude contractual arrangements 
for data sharing.  
The upcoming Data Act pr oposal should be perfectly coherent and complementary with existing and upcoming 
Regulation such as the Data Governance Act. 
Smart contracts are an ideal tool to record real-time data generated by IoT devices onto a secured ledger, and thereby 
capture immutable records and provide trusted transactions. The benefits of blockchain technology can be leveraged to 
support the goals of GDPR, and smart “contracts” can be a very effective tool to implement data sharing, provided 
harmonization of regulatory requirements is ensured and technical considerations, such as interoperability, are 
addressed through global industry-led standards. 
Any measures aimed at regulating access to and use of machine-generated non-personal data should be proportionate 
and take into account the potential risks that may arise from disclosure of certain data. 
In looking at the IoT  ecosystems and potential challenges related to access and use rights of IoT -generated data, we 
encourage the Commission to avoid solutions that would entail mandatory data -sharing schemes, as these may 
discourage investment in data innovation . A risk-based approach combined with innovative tools and in centives for 
non-personal data sharing should be the way forward to encourage Smart Manufacturing in Europe. 
 
Conclusion 
 
Fujitsu strongly encourages the European Commission and co-Legislators to work together with all stakeholders in order 
to have a comprehensive and harmonized framework for Data and Data sharing in Europe that could serve as reference 
at international level. 
We appreciate the opportunity to provide our comments into this to the actual proposal of the European Commission 
and we are committed to work with the European Parliament and European Council during the legislative process.  
The alignment on key principles between the Japanese Government and the European Union is an important common 
ground for further  cooperation at international level. Fujitsu is committed to continue working closely with both the 
Japanese Government and the European Commission by providing advice and contributions from our experts in order 
to achieve a global view with the ultimate intent of delivering benefit for our societies and citizens.

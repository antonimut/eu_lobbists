Bilag til høringssvar til EU marketing standards for agricultural 
products 
 
 
Mandatory MOP+ label  
Animal Protection Denmark suggests a mandatory MOP+ label across all species and all products.  
 
For full transparency, this EU-wide animal welfare related label needs to be 
mandatory, across all species and products and include from minimum EU animal welfare 
standards to premium levels. 
 
Likewise, the standards must apply to imported products, and if not possible, a category of non-
compliant with EU minimum standards must be created. 
 
EU Marketing standards facilitate a sustainable production and consumption and a diary shift to 
plant-based products  
Animal Protection Denmark strongly recommends that the EU policy focus on public goods such 
as health, environment, climate, and animal welfare through promotion of plant-based foods. 
 
EU marketing standards shall not discriminate against plant-based foods in ways 
that undermine consumer information and choice. Full transparency to consumers about the 
compositional nature of their food is necessary and it must be ensured that plant-based products 
are not disproportionately prohibited or otherwise prevented from evoking concepts traditionally 
associated with meat or dairy products. 
 
Alignment of the marketing standards with the revision of the animal welfare 
legislation 
Once relevant legislation like the Broiler Directive (2007/43/EC) or the Laying 
Hen Directive (1999/74/EC) are revised, it is important to evaluate and revise 
the relating marketing standards to make sure they are in line. 
 
Eggs are marked at the production site 
To ensure good traceability of products and correct use of marketing terms it is important that all 
eggs are marked at the production site including eggs delivered directly to the industry.

dual use of the range area

By changing this requirement farmers are allowed more opportunities to provide natural and 
artificial shelter for their hens, while at the same time making sure these activities do not have 
any negative effects on the animals.  
 
Giving the farmers more opportunities to plant trees and provide other kinds of 
shelter is a win-win situation because it benefits the birds and give the farmer an extra (financial) 
incentive. It is advantageous not only for the farmer and the animals, but also for the 
climate and biodiversity. 
 
Moreover, trees in the range can contribute to the prevention of avian influenza (AI) outbreaks in 
domestic poultry, by making the range less attractive for waterfowl that carry the AI virus. 
 
Foie gras production by force feeding has severe negative impact on animal welfare  
A maximum liver weight must be introduced to prevent force-feeding practices. If by contrast, the 
current requirement of minimum liver weights is maintained it constitutes a de facto obligation to 
force-feed the animals. 
 
Any privileged label for producers that use force-feeding is in conflict both with scientific evidence 
and the ethical standards of most EU citizens. All meat coming from force-fed animal - if this 
practice continues or in relation to imports - should be labelled as such to allow consumers to 
make informed choices.

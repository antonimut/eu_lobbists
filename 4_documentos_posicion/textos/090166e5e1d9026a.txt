Repsol response to the Combined Evaluation Roadmap / Inception Impact Assessment 
on “Hydrogen and Gas markets Decarbonisation Package” (Revision of Directive (EU) 
2009/73/EC and Regulation (EC) No. 715/2009) 
 
10/03/2021 
 
Repsol, as a global multi-energy provider, shares the world’s ambition to reach climate neutrality 
in the framework of the Paris Agreement, as well as the EU’s 2050 carbon neutrality objective.  
 
At Repsol, we believe in a model that integrates various technological options a nd combines 
electrification with the use of low -carbon products to offer solutions that meet all of society's 
energy needs. 
 
As part of our 2021-2025 Strategic Plan (rolled out in November 2020), we will allocate €5.5 billion 
to low-carbon initiatives such as renewable electricity generation, new industrial platforms, energy 
efficiency, advanced mobility, and value-added energy services. 
 
We acknowledge that the context for the revision of the 3rd Gas Package is mainly the need to align 
these legislative pieces with the 2030 Climate Target Plan, as well as the integration of the actions 
proposed in the Energy Sector Integration and Hydrogen Strategies, amongst others.  
We also share the main concern of counting wi th the contribution of renewable and low-carbon 
gases in those sectors in which full electrification is unlikely to be technically or economically viable 
despite our understanding that there is still quite a large room for the power generation to be 
decarbonised (mainly shifting from coal to natural gas and renewable sources). 
Repsol welcomes the opportunity to provide feedback to this consultation, and we look forward to 
continuing participating actively in the development of this legislative procedure, by providing 
further input to upcoming consultations and addressing other information requests the 
Commission may need. 
General comment: 
The main objective is to reduce EU’s GHG emissions cost -effectively, therefore, actions and 
measures to be considered should be market-driven and cost-effective and strengthen rather than 
weaken the global competitiveness of EU’s industry. 
The overall review should: 
1- Ensure that the revised regulatory framework builds on the achievements of the Internal Gas 
Market - The integration of the European gas markets, as a result of the implementation of the 3rd 
Gas package, has been of a significant benefit to European consumers. Therefore, the amendments 
should safeguard the achievements while including the decarbonisation obje ctives, as well as 
addressing specific existing barriers (please see bullet 5 below).  
2- Ensure an inclusive technological approach  - The revision should stimulate competition among 
different gaseous fuels  and technologies and bring down the cost of renew able energy sources , 
while providing enough investment certainty in Europe on a competitive level playing field. A 
holistic view, as foreseen in the Energy System Integration Strategy, is a good approach to remove 
barriers for already existing technologies while at the same time supporting innovation in to new 
ones, which would be required to fulfil system needs, such as firm capacity, flexibility, etc.

The potential of using existing natural gas storage options also taken into account in this 
technological approach, so as to fully unlock the potential of an integrated European energy system. 
3- Establish a credible, robust and manageable certification scheme based on voluntary guarantees 
of origin as part of the review of the Renewable Energy Directive (RED II) to valuing life cycle GHG 
intensity reduction of renewable and low-carbon gases - The Gas Market Review should be aligned 
with the European terminology for renewable and low-carbon gases and a system of guarantees of 
origin based on standardised life-cycle GHG emission savings, which the Commission is developing 
for the upcoming REDII revision . The GHG benefits of the different gases should be traded 
separately in a market for carbon. 
4- Enable a competitive hydrogen commodity market irrespective of type - The regulatory 
framework should have  the flexibility to support both specific hydrogen products and hydrogen  
blending into natural gas grid. Moreover, it should provide conditions that would mobilise private 
initiatives and avoid unfair competition between existing businesses and regulated activities. 
Hydrogen projects, including greenfield low carbon projects , pipeline retrofits  and retrofits with 
CCUS, will require policy support until carbon prices are sufficient to develop a viable market and 
infrastructure. 
5- Ensuring a  competitive gas market within the Green Deal  - Well-functioning and liquid gas 
markets are a prerequisite for ensuring affordable energy for consumers, competitiveness of 
industries, and security of supply. Gas also plays a role in achieving the environmental ambitions of 
the European Green Deal, which foresees the decarbonisation of the gas sector via a forward -
looking design for a competitive decarbonised gas market.  Related to that, it is important to 
address: 
- barriers and gaps that may need to be addressed to ensure optimal use of existing LNG 
terminals in the EU. This finding is especially relevant due to the significant increase of LNG 
imports to the EU in the recent years. Some regulation must be address ed to allow the 
better use of existing LNG terminal capacities, improve their link with the gas market, and 
facilitate the response to market demands. 
- the identification of  existing administrative and regulatory requirements to enter and 
trade on the EU wholesale gas markets. Based on the overview of current barriers to entry 
and trade on wholesale gas markets, it may be interesting to develop rules  at EU-level to 
mitigate the barriers , including mutual recognition, minimum requirements, an EU pass 
porting system, and the abolishment of licensing requirements. 
6- Strengthen consumer rights that mirror electricity market design  - Certain features of the 2019 
electricity market reform should be considered for mirroring to the gas markets framework where 
this has tangible benefits and taking into account the structural differences between gas and power.

Context 
 
The European Commission (herein EC or the Commission ) draft amendment to the eIDAS Regulation 
(electronic identification, authentication, and trust services) provides a critical foundation for secure 
digital identities and trust services across the European Union  (EU). IDnow is a strong proponent of 
these amendments. We believe the new regulation will strengthen Europe's goal of digital sovereignty 
by establishing digital identities (EUid). At the same time, the EUid would allow EU citizens to use this 
function throughout the U nion in a self -determined manne r, which aligns with the purpose of the 
original 2014 regulation. 
 
A significant demand for digitization in public and private services has evolved over the past two decades 
stemming from health care and other government services  to banking / finance, mobility, hospitality, 
entertainment, and contract trust services to name a few  market sectors. According to a McKinsey & 
Company study from the end of 2020, statistics show the recent COVID 2019 Pandemic has accelerated 
the need for digital services by seven (7) years. 
 
On the other hand, national laws on digital identity remain fragmented. Fewer than 15 member states 
even offer cross border eIDs, and fewer solutions are used for commercial services. The Commission 
itself concluded that the added value of the eIDAS Regulation “in regard to electronic identity is limited 
because of low coverage, usage, and uptake ,” especially when it comes to cross border services and 
private sector possibilities. This is partly due to differences in national regulations that cannot support 
or recognize specific eID methods for multiple use cases.  
 
The European Commission has rightly recognized this and the fact that digital identities should continue 
to be provided under the sovereignty of Member States. The introduction of new trust services is a 
positive addition to the network of trust services that exists throughout Europe.  
 
The Commission ought to ensure that multiple certified wallets are allowed in cooperation with the 
private sector . Through this combination, a wallet  can coexist in the market with requirements for 
certification remain realistic and practical. In the following section, IDnow provides feedback on select 
topics that are paramount for a viable framework. We strongly believe additional transparency and 
further considerations to include the private industry can better ensure users will benefit from this 
technical progress in the long term. 
 
Additionally, IDnow supports the Commission’s stand on “Gatekeepers.” It cannot be in the interest of 
the Commission that European identities can end up with ownership by companies from outside of the 
EU. We stress that Member State  regulators and the Commission  take an incisive look into equal 
opportunities of member states' national solutions.

Feedback 
 
The Commission recognizes that the digital identity environment has been fragmented . The main 
feature of this fragmentation is limited interoperability and function at inconsistent levels of assurance. 
A new proposal to issue a digital EUid wallet encompasses a broad scope of solutions. IDnow welcomes 
this opportunity to offer feedback  and its opinions  from a private industry perspective . Below are a 
select number of topics, which we would welcome as future discussion with the Commission. 
 
Open Ecosystem and Transparent Rules 
 
It is essential that the digital identity wallets are built for an open eco system which provides fair and 
transparent rules for both public and private sector bodies and which encourages healthy competition 
among the participants for the best and most secure solution. 
 
We encourage the Commission to ensure that private sector bodies are also enabled to provide certified 
wallets. Only by allowing competition the necessary innovation can be triggered which will make the 
digital identity wallets a success. IDnow supports the reliance on identities  that only Member States’ 
competent authorities can provide  with a high degree of confidence in establishing the identity of 
a person . 
 
This means: 
• Member States should be encouraged to certify private sector wallets 
• Private sector bodies should be able to create the base identity (“person identification data”) 
based on a national identity 
• Member States should be encouraged to allow private sector bodies to issue electronic 
identification schemes on level “High” 
• The Commission and the Member States should set transparent rules that ensure a level 
playing field and ensure the security of all solutions 
 
Requirement for different Levels of Assurance 
 
At the moment, the draft mentions that “The European Digital Identity Wallets shall be issued under a 
notified electronic identification scheme of level of assurance ‘high’. ”. For many use cases this is not 
necessary and will limit the adoption of the wallet as identification sch emes on level “high” are not 
widely adopted. 
 
We encourage the Commission to allow other levels of assurance to be used to issue the wallet including 
identification methods approved under Art. 24 eIDAS. Best practice as shown to be so called “step up” 
verification where the wallet can be issue d initially on a lower level of assurance but can be upgraded 
by another verification should a use case require so. How this would work: 
 
• The user creates the wallet for a use case which requires the level of assurance “substantial” 
• Later, the user wants to use the wallet for another use case which requires “high” 
• The user has to use another identification method on LoA “high” to upgrade the wallet to the 
required level

Electronic Attestation of Attributes 
 
IDnow supports and  believes it must be possible to establish person identification data based on 
qualified electronic attributes. The new measures extend the scope of eIDAS to the private sector, and 
it supports new use cases where identity attributes are linked to a pers on with a high level of 
confidence . For example, a digital driver license would allow a user to rent a car quickly and securely, 
proof one’s age, or proof one’s certification (i.e., a medical certificate could be used to confirm a doctor 
has a medical degre e). With user consent, a service provider will be able to access the national data 
source to share these credentials across several use cases. 
 
At the same time, IDnow seeks clarification to Article 45 a) & b), which provides explicit distinctions that 
electronic attributes shall not be denied legal effect should the public sector allow it. When an electronic 
identification and / or authentication is required “to access public data, electronic attestation attributes 
shall not substitute electronic identification unless specifically allowed by the Member State or public 
sector body,” does not specify how this carries over to private sector activity. 
 
As trust service providers need to make sure data can be securely shared with the relying party, further 
clarification is necessary whether an electronic attestation of “person identification data” for the private 
sector carries the same legal effect as the paper-based version of the ID document.  
Freedom of Choice 
 
The citizens of the European Union must retain their ability to choose a solution which best fits their 
needs. Therefore, it must be possible that a citizen from one Member State can use the wallet issued 
by another Member State. A significant number of EU citizens travel a nd work cross bo rder and this 
freedom of movement is one of the central pillars of the European Union. Therefore, it must be possible 
to get a wallet from another Member State as otherwise the ability to proof the identity could be limited. 
 
We encourage the Commission to add explicit statement to the regulation that this has to be ensured. 
 
Transfer of Wallets 
 
We are strong proponents of an open ecosystem to prevent lock-in of the citizens to a single solution. 
We encourage the Commission to add to Art. 6a 3. (a) the possibility to transfer person identification 
data and attributes between different certified wallets.  This would encourage innovation and 
competition and avoid lock-in to a single solution. 
 
Backups 
 
The proposal ought to offer further details on the resilience of a digital identity - if a device is lost, stolen, 
or damaged. As service providers we also see users often change their devices quite often in addition 
to devices being lost or stolen. We encourage the Commission to add to Art. 6a 3. (a) the possibility to 
backup and restore the wallets. 
 
Encrypted server-side backups should be possible as well. There are already industry best practices how 
wallets can be securely stored and restored while at the same time ensur ing that the provider cannot 
decrypt or access the backups.

Biometric Authentication 
  
The draft requires strong user authentication which include biometrics / inheritance. From our 
experience the best way of authenticating the user is by relying on the biometric sensors of the devices 
themselves (e.g. Face ID, Touch ID, etc). 
 
We encourage the Commission to clearly allow this method of authentication while providing the 
necessary technical standards / certification programs that ensure that the security of the biometric 
sensors is high enough. There ar e already existing standards (e.g. FIDO) which can be taken into 
consideration. 
 
Clarification regarding Client-side vs. Server-side Wallets 
 
A client-side wallet is a wallet where the data can be decrypted only on a device (e.g. smartphone) under 
the direct control of the user. The provider would have no possibility to access the unencrypted data  
(e.g. by decrypting a backup). 
 
A server-side wallet on the other side would authenticate the user on the server. Therefore, the provider 
would be able to decrypt data stored on the servers. 
 
The draft does not explicitly mention whether server -side wallets are going to be allowed. Though, at 
various points, implicit references to client-side wallets are made (e.g., regarding the secure element). 
We encourage the Commission to clarify if server-side wallets are allowed . In our opinion, client -side 
wallets are more secure and server-side wallets should not be allowed. Server-side backups (where the 
provider can never decrypt the data) should be allowed though. 
 
Wallets as QSCD 
  
The current draft does not specify if the wallet can only be used as a means of authentication for 
remote qualified electronic signature or if the wallet (including the secure element) can directly act as 
the QSCD itself. 
 
If the later is intended, we encourage the Commission to clarify this in the draft and to provide the 
necessary implementing acts and technical standards. 
 
Toolbox 
 
Common technical standards will be required to ensure uniform operability and security.  
IDnow believes the expert committee which develops the toolbox should be open and transparent both 
to public and private sector bodies. 
 
We encourage the Commission to make the development of the toolbox open and transparent and to 
include outside participation.

Existing Standardization Efforts 
 
IDnow is an active member and participant in Standards Institutes and foundations such as ETSI, FIDO 
Alliance, W3C, Linux Foundation’s Hyperledger and Trust over IP, as well as ID Union. IDnow is well 
known for providing valuable input on remote identity verification and cyber security with Member 
States and public / private  organisations. Most recently, as members of the ETSI task force , STF 588, 
IDnow produced a new standard (ETSI EN 119 461) on identity proofing and trust services, which aims 
to provide updates to policy and security requirements for trust services.  
 
Our commitment to collaboration with both the public and private sector is based on our expertise and 
significant experience serving approximately one thousand (1,000)  customers within the banking, 
finance, telecom, retail, mobility, and media sectors , to name a few. With the recent effects of the 
COVID 19 Pandemic, IDnow has witnessed a significant impact on our customer’s digital services, 
including identity proofing and trust services. Such significant technological change will continue to 
impact our digital economy and requires further regulatory harmonisation to the EUid. 
 
IDnow is a strong supporter of harmonisation and certification of the EUid and new trust services. This 
ought to be developed further with other EU regulations such as the Cybersecurity Act, the NIS Directive, 
as well as the EU Single Digital Gateway regulation. This is vital to the EU economy and the public good.  
 
While the NIS2 Directive  is based on building a more resilient Europe against “state of the art” cyber 
threats and risks , IDnow would like to stress that the goal towards harmonisation is still incomplete. 
There exist variances and fragmentation within NIS2 Directive, therefore a single European approach is 
still some distance off at this time. If the Commission is to consider other regulations and directives, 
IDnow calls for greater harmonisation and regulatory consistency between Member States  to the 
directive’s enforcement and its obligations.

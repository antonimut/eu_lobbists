 
 
FFE contribution to the European Commission report on the PTD 
application  
September 2021 
 
The French Equestrian Federation (FFE) is the French sport federation for equestrian sports with 9500 
equestrian holdings and more than 660 000 licence holders. It represents 35 000 direct jobs, making 
horse riding the largest private employer for sport in France.  
Considered as farm ers by the French law, equestrian centres have an atypical activity, strongly 
linked to education, tourism and of course sport for all, including people with disabilities. Thanks 
to the diversity of its disciplines, the French system is affordable by sharing the facilities, coaching 
and horses with tailored project to everyone.  
In the context of the Package Travel Directive (hereafter “PTD”), the equestrian centres – organised 
either in non -profit organization or with commercial status have concerns regarding  this legislation. 
Indeed, they organise training or equestrian hiking, which are mainly the extension of the daily or 
weekly practice of horse riding. In this perspective, the consumers are already clients of the centres 
(through a sport license for examp le). Only a very few agencies in France are specialized in the 
organisation of travel package of riding trekking.  In the vast majority, the itinerary organisation, the 
board and lodging are organised by the equestrian centre itself. We strongly believe tha t the 
obligations resulting from the Directive are not appropriate for such stakeholders, which are not in any 
case organised as a travel agency. In our country, the organisation of equestrian training for minors 
requires to be compliant with national legi slation (civil liability insurance, several administrative 
declarations (for the accommodation of minors, presentation of the educative project…).  
The PTD creates new obligations, which represent new costs and an administrative burden for SMEs, 
which comply already with sport obligations. The obligations related to the insolvency protection do 
not correspond to the economic reality of these sport and agricultural structures. The Thomas Cook 
bankruptcy or the recent sanitary crisis show that the situation o f equestrian centres are not 
comparable with travel agencies or travel platforms. With regards to the Covid-19, the activities of the 
equestrian centres has been limited by the national rules. They also have to conform to the necessary 
but very strict sanitary regulations decided at a national level, in order to restrain the epidemic. 
It seems to the FFE that the aim of the Directive – which is laudable – is not suitable to the equestrian 
centres which most of the time, propose these activities to their members. We are asking again for a 
specific derogation for members of sport federations , which organise themselves the package 
dedicated to their license holders in their own countries (especially equestrian training for minor 
people).

Growing Media Europe contribution to the public consultation on Digital Labelling  
 
1) Introduction: labelling of Growing Media products and Growing Media Blends 
 
As of  July 2022 for the first time, growing m edia producers can put products and b lends on the 
harmonised EU market , under the Fertilising Product Regulation EU 2019/1009. Labelling 
requirements of these types of products are extensive; sometimes it is questionable if all these 
detailed information is helpful for the consumers. In any case, the FPR requires that the ex act mix is 
individually labelled.  
The document guiding the visua l appearance of the label of f ertilising p roducts (2021/C 119/01) 
states that a Growing M edium cannot contain fertilisers, liming materials, plant biostimulants, or 
products belonging to othe r PFCs. This type of Growing Medium (PFC 4) is placed on the market for 
exceptional applications where the addition of products belonging to other PFCs is not essential. It 
will also serve as the basis for Fertilising Product Blends (PFC 7) containing othe r PFC’s. Any Growing 
Medium (PFC 4) Blended with one or more products of any other PFC (for example fertiliser, liming 
material, plant biostimulants) is consequently a Growing Media Blend (PFC 7).  
Growing Media Blends thus typically consist of bulky Growi ng Medium ingredients with mixed -in 
fertilizers, plant biostimulants, micro -organisms and liming products. This means that the Blend 
typically will consist of more than three different product groups. Such type of Blends should be 
labelled by displaying th e requirements for each of the individual constituent products . Especially 
when multiple languages are needed to be displayed on the packaging space will be limited, 
consequently labelling all requirements of Growing Media will be challenging.   
 
2) What type of Growing Media (Blends) will be put on the market? 
 
Growing Media products find their way in two different market segments , the consumer market 
(B2C) and the professional market (B2B). Commonly the consumer products are bagged into plastic 
bags, for exa mple 10L, 20L, 30L, 50L, or 70L. Alternatively, such products can also be packed into 
bigbags. Consumer products are designed for hobby gardeners and are sold into DIY stores, garden 
centers, and in retail stores, whereas professional products are sold dir ectly to professional growers 
or via professional distributers. Professional Growing Media mixes  can be supplied without 
packaging (via bulk transport) or with packaging, namely bigbags/bigbales for larger volumes or 
plastic bags filled with for example 25 0L, 100L, or 70L of Growing Media products (hereinafter called 
bagged professional products).   
Hobby products  are designed for one or two member state markets and typically have not more 
than 3 languages displayed on the packaging. Furthermore, hobby products are bagged into one type 
of packaging, i.e. there is a 1:1 relationship between the packaging and the specific Growing Media 
mix it contains.

For professional products shipped without packaging an accompanying document is provided to the 
customers, i n which the required labelling information is displayed. Also, products packed into 
bigbales (up to 6 m³) and bigbags (up to 2 m³) are accompanied with a sticker or an attached 
document, displaying the product labelling information. As for the hobby produc ts, no labelling 
issues are foreseen here, and Growing Media Europe (GME) expects smooth labelling for these types 
of professional products.  
However, for the last type of professional products, being the mixes filled into plastic bags and 
provided to the customer or distributor on pallets, GME foresees much more challenges. Unlike the 
hobby products, bagged professional products are sold in multiple member states, sometimes more 
than 10 different member states, and - if no exception is granted by the respective member state -
consequently multiple languages  need to be displayed. Moreover, there is no 1:1 relationship  
between the b ag and the Growing Media mix it contains. Common practice in the industry is such 
that clients order their own custom-made mix to the producer. For economic reasons and to reduce 
plastic use as much as possible, producers do not make individual packaging f or each client. Instead, 
they have one type of professional packaging which they fill with different types of mixes. Sometimes 
the number of mixes per production facility can total up to >1000, with different mixes per client 
varying sometimes even per sea son. The customer is informed about the type of mix and its 
properties by a sticker that is put the pallet cover. Alternatively, a product code is printed on the spot 
on the bags during production. Together with a legend or by (web -based) interactions with  the 
producer the customer can get informed about the product parameters.  
 
 
With the EU 2019/1009 requiring that each packaging is labelled according to the specific 
requirements of the mix it contains, it is clear that this tailor -made approach is compl icating the 
proper labelling of the mix. Therefore GME sees many opportunities in the initiative of the European 
Commission to digitalise the labelling requirements for fertilizing products.  
 
3) Opportunities for digitalization of Growing Media (Blends) 
 
Growing Media Blends need to display the labelling requ irements of each of the products’  groups it 
consists of, making a rather crowded appearance . Example 3 of section 12.1 of 2021/C 119/01 , 
shows a realistic visual appearance of a label of a Growing Medi a Blend. The declaration of each of 
the constituent product function categories  - in the example highlighted in different colors  - already 
take place of several lines, thereafter the ingredients with its corresponding CMC class of each PFC 
constituent needs to be declared, together with instructions for use, storage conditions, information 
                         
               
 
on safety and environment, additional information, production date, manufacturer’s name, and 
address, type number, and quantity. This is only one example in one language; imagine now 10 
languages to be displayed.    
For hobby Growing Media Blends, both the limited rang e of languages and the 1:1 bag - mix 
relationship give good indications that - although crowded- labelling according to the EU 2019/1009 
regulation might be just feasible. For bagged professional products, for which the 1:1 relationship 
bag: mix does not exist and for which the label needs to be written in multiple languages, there is 
just no way to physically  print the label s. Digital labelling is the only solution to properly inform 
the customers.         
Digital labelling will for sure also save lots of costs and also have a positive impact on the amount of 
plastic that needs to be printed. A plastic foil pr oducer only starts the production of new foils and 
corresponding printing plates when a Growing Media producer can place a minimum order of about 
15.000 bags, around 1.5 ton of plastic. With more than 1.000 mixes per production site, this would 
mean more than 1.500 tons of foil to be kept in stock, with consequent losses of foil when clients are 
not ordering or desiring a different kind of custom -made mix.  This is not a desirable situation from 
an economic and ecological point of view. In line with the EU Plastics Strategy, many fossil resource-
use and plastic waste -related problems can be prevented when taking a different approach. 
Therefore GME sees many opportunities in the digitalis ation initiative the European  Commission 
has taken recently. 
 
4) Proposed approach 
 
Therefore, GME would propose to the European Commission that digital labelling is allowed for the 
declaration of the properties and requirements of such types of products. GME sees a pragmatic 
approach, in such a way that an ID code is printed on -the-spot during bagging. The customers are 
then guided to a QR code on the bag, linking to a labelling website. By inserting this ID code into this 
website the customer can find all labelling requirements his/her Growing Media Blend. For sake of 
clarity product ID,  production date, quantity and manufacturer’s name and address could still be 
mentioned on the physical label.  
For the website, it would of course be best if this is a joint initiative  between the producers, the 
European commission, the national market su rveillance authorities and the users of the products. 
GME, therefore, urges the study on digital labelling of EU fertilising products to consider this way of 
working and come to a joint approach across all EU fertilising products.

space-based secure connectivity initiative. 
 
We support the Commission’s goals to provide European citizens with secure, resilient, high-speed and 
ubiquitous connectivity. We welcome the Commission’s openness to different options of how to achieve 
this, including working with various private sector companies which can guarantee the highest possible 
security standards and reliability of service for European users. Intelsat would encourage the 
Commission to consider a mix of technologies in achieving these goals, including not only innovation 
offered by LEO satellites, but also state-of-the-art existing GEO capabilities. 
 
Innovation across satellite connectivity technologies 
 
The connectivity challenge for the coming decade will not be accomplished by one technology alone. 
Satellite will play a unique role in ensuring near -ubiquitous, high -capacity and lower -latency 
communications and will be a key partner in helping the EU achieve its Digital Decade connectivity goals. 
As European Commissioner for Internal Market Thierry Breton has noted, “the next paradigm shift is 
about linking connectivity with space to provide high-speed connectivity everywhere in Europe.” 
  
A more effective approach is needed to build a network that will meet the new bandwidth, coverage and 
reliability requirements. No singular solution can meet all the requirements alon e, therefore the new 
network needs to be a combination of terrestrial and space -based platforms. Only satellite guarantees 
ubiquity and reliability for such a hybrid network. With innovations facilitating both launches and better 
antennas, satellite also p rovides lower costs and higher throughput, helping to remove dead zones, in 
particular important for rural areas in the EU. 
  
The key point of departure for these innovations lies in establishing durable partnerships with industry, 
in view of seizing the benefits of existing technologies in the short-term, while boosting R&D in the long-
term. 
 
Rural connectivity 
 
The Inception Impact Assessment calls for more affordable connectivity in all parts of the EU, especially 
in remote and rural EU regions , and to address the digital divide. This objective can, to a large degree, 
be addressed with existing technologies in many parts of the EU , and we would urge policymakers to 
consider already available state-of-the-art solutions as playing a key role in the new initiative. However, 
for remote areas, particularly in rural Europe, better technological solutions are required to ensure that 
all citizens can enjoy very good connectivity. 
 
Across Europe there are still many communities and land areas where 4G mobile broadband coverage 
does not exist, mostly in rural and hard-to-reach places. This is primarily because high cost and low ROI 
associated with using traditional terrestrial backhaul to build out networks into these areas has made it 
impractical for mobile operators; and in many cases topographical challenges have made it unfeasible. 
 
Satellite-powered mobile backhaul provides a wider, more flexible choice to achieve this vision. While 
fiber backhaul is ideal for urban and suburban network deployments, and microwave backhaul best 
supports suburban-rural edge expansion, satellite backhaul boasts unique advantages that make it a 
leading choice for rural mobile coverage.

International partnerships 
 
The IIA notes that regions of strategic interest to the EU (Artic, the Atlantic and Africa) lack connectivity 
infrastructure, affecting governments and businesses and populations. 
 
Intelsat is running satellite connectivity initiatives falling in the scope of UN sustainable development 
goals. Intelsat’s current engagement focuses on empow ering rural regions of Africa and Asia by 
connecting school buildings and local communities. 
 
A competitive market 
  
Beyond secure -communication procurement, open and fair procurement processes grounded on 
security, competitiveness, and technological-efficiency considerations are crucial in ensuring that the EU 
remains a competitive market.  
  
A strong and innovative European space industry with “autonomous, reliable and cost -effective access 
to space” as outlined by Commission President Ursula von der Leyen can only be achieved by making full 
use of available competitive and diversified solutions. Relying on a limited number of assets from a small 
procurement pool cannot deliver the long -term value-for-money or capacity that the EU and member 
states require to maintain mission qua lity. Security standards and a sound system for accrediting 
suppliers will be key in building the European space market.

ACCIE Statement on the Legislative package on Anti-Money Laundering 
 
 
Through this statement, the Association of Credit Card Issuers Europe ( ACCIE) sets out its view on the 
European Commission’s (Commission) proposal on the Legislative package on Anti-Money Laundering 
and Countering Terrorist Financing (AML/CTF), published on 20 June 2021. 
 
 
ACCIE welcomes the legislative package on AML, and is delighted that the Commission seeks to 
strengthen and harmonise the EU AML/CTF rules. ACCIE especially welcomes the Commission’s aim to 
create a much more consistent framework to ease compliance for operators subject to AML/CFT rules.  
  
1. The new Regulation on AML/CTF 
 
ACCIE embraces the proposal to transpose the legislation on obliged entities under a Regulation. The 
harmonisation of Customer Due Diligence (CDD) measures , further advise on the identification of 
suspicious transactions, and requirements on customer risk analysis, will increase clarity and support 
cross-border operations as the same interpretation of the AML/CTF rules will be in place across the EU 
members states. High-standard fraud prevention and monitoring are some of the core features for 
credit card payments, which are front-runners in fraud prevention practices. ACCIE is pleased that the 
Commission addresses the rapid digitalisation of the EU financial sector, addresses the new financial 
service providers and money transfer methods that were not adequately captured by the 5th AML 
Directive. Thus, ACCIE welcomes the Commission’s aim to increase the protection of the EU financial 
system and citizens by setting stricter rules on transfers which are more prone to fraud, as well as by 
setting higher fraud prevention stand ards, reflecting the best practices applied by the EU financial 
market. Consequently, ACCIE is pleased to not e that the Commission is ensuring a level playing field 
amongst all payment methods, including crypto currencies. This in turn guarantees a high standard of 
fraud prevention across the EU financial sector. 
 
2. Establishing the EU AML Authority (AMLA) 
 
ACCIE welcomes the establishment of a specialised EU AML Authority to ensure the most efficient 
supervision of AML/CTF issues, which  is increasingly necessary due to digitalisation. However, ACCIE 
emphasises that AML supervision must be clearly divided acr oss the different supervisory levels. This 
is necessary to prevent burdening market operators and avoid complicated reporting processes and 
unclarity amongst the market players. ACCIE request s clear guidance for market operators regarding 
which supervisory body they should contact on certain matters, as well as further information on the 
interaction between the different levels of supervision. Prior experience shows that the  creation of 
new EU Authorities can lead to power imbalances, as national supervisory entities continue to follow 
their own interpretation of the law. Such an imbalance of power results in a high administrative burden 
and unclear guidance for the financial market, and such situations should therefore be prevented.

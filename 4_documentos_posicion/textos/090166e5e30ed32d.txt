Subject 
Deltares' statement on the consultation ‘Impact Assessment of a possible revision of the lists of pollutants 
affecting surface and ground waters and the corresponding regulatory standards in the Environmental 
Quality Standards, Groundwater and Water Framework Directives 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Deltares welcomes the opportunity to respond to the open public consultation regarding 
revised lists of surface and groundwater pollutants. In the framework of the EU -strategy 
towards zero-pollution, Deltares firmly believes a system transition is needed in addition to an 
enhancement of current policies . Although the ‘polluter pays’ principle is one of the leading 
principles of EU -environmental acquis, as set out in the Treaty on the Functioning of the 
European Union, in fact there are many areas where this principle is inadequately applied. 
Reinforcement and consistent application of this principle is needed in order to obtain a more 
sustainable water policy.  
 
For industrial pollution, we observe significant discrepancies between emission permits and 
what is happening in practice. Some substances are discharge d without being mentioned in 
permits, discharges exceed permit levels, permit renewal lags behind or is not used as 
opportunity to change to the better, etc. This should not be acceptable. Instead a transition to 
a much more effective permitting system, maybe at communal level, is needed. After all, why 
is it considered acceptable to discharge toxic chemicals into the environment? We recommend 
assessing Member States’ permitting systems, eliciting best practices and a (binding) 
recommendations for national improvements.  
 
For communal water discharges, in fact there are many small polluters (households and small 
businesses), discharging their waste water to a waste water collection system and a waste 
water treatment plant (in line with the Urban Waste Wate r Directive). Households and small 
businesses use many different substances (cleaning products including biocides, cosmetics, 
pharmaceuticals, micro -plastics, etc. as well as many degradation products of these 
substances). If their waste water is treated, many substances are removed from the waste 
water stream but certainly not all. It should be avoided that substances that cannot be (easily) 
removed enter waste streams. This can b e achieved appl ying the precautionary rigorously, 
creating a 'white list' of authorized substances rather than a black list of forbidden or regulated 
substances. Research should be conducted how to fundamentally change this system, in order 
to discharge fewer substances, in smaller concentrations; for those substances that cannot be 
prevented, additional removal techniques need to be developed and implemented. For 
obvious ethical reasons, pharmaceuticals take a special position; but here, too, the industry 
should be challenged to improve the (bio -) degradability of medicinal products / 
pharmaceutical formulation and the European commission to stimulate this by research 
investments and better regulation.

be applied. For agriculture, pesticide pollution can be reduced by stricter regulations, investing 
in (research on and use of) greener pesticides, reverse the burden of proof of exceedances of 
standards, or other approaches that are fundamentally different than current approaches, 
such as multicropping. For nutrient pollution, in addition to stricter regulations, the CAP can 
be used much more efficiently in helping those far mers who want to have a positive impact 
on the environment.  
 
For existing pollution, more investment is needed for remedial techniques and more funds to 
apply these techniques. Application, exchange of knowledge on polluted sites and remediation 
options will result in better management of the historical pollution. There is a huge heritage 
from the past which, if untreated, poses a risk to human and environmental health.

For groundwater, because of its strong interaction with soil, a good soil protection policy is 
needed.  
Finally, awareness raising and communication with stakeholders, including the general 
public, should be enhanced. Gaining insight in the issue for now and for the future, sharing 
knowledge and providing information on pollution and polluting activities and substances is 
necessary. It will help making the urgency of the issue visible and create ownership and 
involvement, and ignite the system transition.  
All by all, if the EU really wants to head towards zero pollution, fundamental changes are 
needed instead of a bit more of what is happening already.  
Deltares, 8 October 2021

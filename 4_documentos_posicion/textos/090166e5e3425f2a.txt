Expedia Group – response to IIA on STR Initiative 
 
Expedia Group welcomes the European Commission’s decision to develop a ‘Short-term rental (STR) 
initiative’ in order to promote a responsible, fair and trusted vacation rental sector – and more broadly 
a well-balanced tourism ecosystem. Having harmonised guidance at the EU level that is tailored to the 
needs and priorities of the sector will help ensure a level -playing field betwe en accommodation 
service providers, consumer protection, evidence -based decision making , as well as protect the 
economic benefits brought about by STR activities . We are proud to support  engagement with 
policymakers and stakeholders to share best practice s and develop regulatory frameworks that 
provide simple, practical and enforceable measures for vacation rentals.  
 
As the  EU Commission states 1, STR services are an important part of the EU tourism ecosystem, 
accounting for over 23% of the tourist accommodation sector. They not only represent an additional 
source of income for  European citizens, but also contribute to the economic growth of local 
communities. This has been the case especially during and post the Covid 19 pandemic, where STRs 
contributed to shifting tourist demand and flows from hotspots (cities) to less well -known, rural 
destinations. The Covid-19 crisis has severely impacted both the vacation rental sector and the tourism 
ecosystem as a whole, globally and within Europe . As t he latest report from the European Travel 
Commission shows, arrivals to Europe dropped 70% in 2020 compared to 2019, and they currently 
represent 58% of international tourist arrivals worldwide. 2  Most experts expect to see the recovery 
of the sector and a return to 2019 tourism levels only from the second half of 2023 - despite the 
ongoing vaccine roll-out3. 
 
In order to support the recovery and regrowth of the sector, there is a need to develop measures that 
are evidence-based, fair and future-proof, and that account for the important role played by short -
term rentals. The sector however currently faces a number of regulatory challenges, including a high 
degree of legislative fragmentation a mong and within Member States (for instance on  registration 
schemes, taxes , night caps etc. ), disproportionate data requests , and a lack of legal clarity  on 
provisions related to professional and peer service providers . In addition, Member States often 
approve and enfo rce national  laws which do not comply with EU legislation on e -commerce and 
unfairly hold platforms liable for not assessing compliance by hosts with the several, national and  
different rules applicable to STRs. For these reasons, we believe that the Commission’s STR initiative 
is not only timely, but also necessary. This being said, there are a number of elements which we believe 
should be carefully taken into consideration and be at the core of the Commission’s proposal:

The STR Initiative should not tackle issues related  to online platforms and online (illegal) content, as 
these have already been addressed by existing and upcoming initiatives, among which the Digital 
Services Act (DSA).  
 
Expedia Group of course recognises the need and importance to address illegal content online, and 
for this reason we have already put in place take-down procedures for fraudulent or otherwise illegal 
listings. Our Notice and Action Policy provides users with information on removing illegal content – as 
defined under applicable EU, national and local laws – from our website in accordance with the EU e-
Commerce Directive. In line with the ‘Roadmap for the short-term rental sector’, we also support the 
delisting of properties after receiving effective knowledge of illegality from appropriate enforcement 
authorities. When a notice from appropriate enforcement authorities is received, owners are  
promptly informed, and the illegal content is delisted expeditiously.  
 
Data sharing regulations 
 
Similarly, the Commission should make sure that the proposal does not duplicate  existing 
requirements for online platforms, for instance the recordkeeping obligations for electronic interfaces 
under the EU VAT Directive4, the data sharing obligations for payment service providers as defined in 
Council Directive (EU) 2020/2845, and other data sharing rules as defined under the DAC7, the DAC7, 
the future Data Act and/or of discussions on a Code of Conduct for data sharing in the tourism sector. 
We welcome and support the work underway within these different frameworks, as moving towards 
more harmonised data access and reporting rules will improve the efficiency of both public authorities 
and platforms, but such work should be coordinated and complementary. However, regulators should 
first identify any gaps left by these initiatives once they have been implemented, before imposing 
additional data sharing obligations on platforms. Any potential new rules should be evidence-based 
and should aim to fill in gaps – if any – in the existing legal framework, rather than impose additional 
disproportionate and unnecessary requirements for online platforms.  
 
Indeed, the vacation rental sector still faces a number of challenges with regards to data requests from 
public authorities, particularly in relation to tax reporting by hosts and guest occupancy for the 
purposes of national security. Platforms are  experiencing an increasing number of personal data 
requests from authorities at multiple levels – local and national – on transactions carried out through 
their sites. These requests place an excessive administrative burden on companies and often concern 
data which platforms do not have access to . In line with the European Holiday Home Association 
(EHHA) and eu travel tech’s (formerly ETTSA) ‘ Roadmap for the short -term rental sector ’, Expedia 
Group supports cooperation with public authorities to facilitate compliance by accommodation 
providers on issues including guest occupancy, taxes and the reporting of earnings.  
 
In addition, we call on the EU Commission to make sure that the recently adopted Council Directive 
amending Directive 2011/16/EU on administrative cooperation in the field of taxation (‘DAC7’) is 
implemented in a uniform way for all reportable platforms , in order to inter alia avoid a ‘double 
taxation’ scenario.  
 
More general ly, Expedia Group advocates that any data requests made by tax  and other local  
authorities must be covered by  existing or future EU frameworks (DAC7, VAT Directive, DSA and/or 
Data Act), rather than by diverging local regulations  to ensure that fragmentation among and within 
Member States is avoided. Such requests must also be mandated by law and should be adequate, fair, 
relevant and focused on information necessary for taxation purposes. In addition, potential new

We further recognise that having access to aggregated and anonymised data for statistical purposes 
can allow more evidence -based policymaking, and in turn a more sustainable and efficient recovery 
of the sector. It is for this reason that Expedia Group has proudly signed an agreement with Eurostat 
to share key data on the vacation rental sector – in line with applicable privacy regulations – including 
guest numbers and the number of nights booked by travellers.  The first set of data published by 
Eurostat clearly shows6 that short-term rental accommodation plays an increasingly important role in 
the tourism sector - and especially for local economies.  The Eurostat data set also provides public 
authorities with a va luable source of reliable information on short-term rentals at Member State, 
municipality and city level. Public authorities, therefore, already have access to a large pool of data on 
short-term rentals . While we agree that a more systematic approach might be beneficial , the 
importance of the Eurostat agreement cannot be overlooked, as the impact of the relevant datasets 
for the future regulation of STR is not yet measurable.  
 
2. Address legal fragmentation 
 
As the Commission acknowledges7, the STR sector in Europe is subject to a wide variety of regulatory 
and often burdensome requirements, including at the local level, which can create barriers to market 
access and make it more difficult to provide STR services  – especially for SMEs. Expedia Group thus 
welcomes the Commission’s intention to  clarify and streamline rules and requirements imposed by 
public authorities on hosts and STR platforms. This would help provide some much -needed legal 
certainty for not only platforms, but also  hosts and guests, who often have to c omply with different 
requirements in different Member States (or municipalities). However, any policy solution chosen by 
the Commission should be evidence-based and rely on the data already collected on the impact of 
STR. 
 
In order to help travellers and hosts navigate the plethora of different regulations across Europe, we 
have taken proactive steps to provide relevant information on legal requirements, which can be found 
on the regulatory pages of national Expedia Group websites - available in national languages (within 
our main markets) and in English.  Expedia Group also provides owners with a so -called ‘Onboarding 
flow’, which includes a list of steps on STR regulations the property owner needs to review in order to 
list his/her property on the platform and include where needed, their registration number. We have 
further launched a new local laws page available to accommodation partners, where they can see the 
information they have provided to the platform, as well as potential regulatory restrictions.  
 
In its Inception Impact Assessment, the Commission explicitly mentions the possibility of a ‘common 
registration scheme’ as a potential solution to address not only legal fragmentation, but also to limit 
the burden of authorities’ access to data on industry players. Expedia Group has been advocating for 
simple registration schemes for STR owners at the national level  across the EU , . Address legal 
fragmentation: Expedia Group welcomes the possibility of a simple, easily accessible, registration 
scheme for STR providers at the national level across the EU – applicable in the jurisdictions where a 
registration number is needed - , as these would represent a l ess burdensome and more effective 
regulatory approach than other options which can sometimes restrict the freedom to provide a short-
term rental service or place illegal obligations on platforms. One such example is the system set up by 
the Portuguese governm ent, which effectively consists in a simple online registration portal which 
owners can easily access.

In addition, Expedia Group believes that regulatory approaches to vacation rentals should 
differentiate between peer (amateur) providers and professional operators.8 This was also addressed 
in the European Commission’s Communication on the collaborative economy  (2016) and its 
workshops on the short -term accommodation rental sector  (2017), as well as in new rules on 
trader/non-trader distinctions within the EU Directive on better enforcement and modernisation of 
EU Consumer protection rules ( 2019/2161). We thus support the Commission’s intention to clarify 
through the STR initiative the applicable legal framework for both peers and professional service 
providers (which is different from the trader/non-trader distinction for the purposes of the application 
of the Consumer Protection Acquis!), and to ensure  proportionally differentiated regulatory 
approaches. However, this initiative should be consistent with the ‘know-your business-customer’ 
principle as envisaged in the DSA proposal (under Article 22 on trader traceability), as well as with the 
obligation to identify C2C transactions under the New Deal for Consumers. To ensure legal clarity, the 
new STR framework should not introduce different rules for peer and professional providers, but 
merely solidify this already existing framework.  This initiative should also avoid imposing excessive 
restrictions on STR activity , as this would disincentivise legitimate amateur activity  and limit peer 
providers’ opportunities to benefit from the platform economy.  
 
3. Provide legal clarity on technical notification mechanisms 
 
Directive EU 2015/1535 laying down a procedure for the provision of information in the field of 
technical regulations and of rules on Information Society services ( TRIS Directive) requires Member 
States to notify the European Commission  about any technical regulati ons they intend to introduce 
for, inter alia, information society services before they are adopted. This includes regulations on short-
term rental services such as registration schemes or night caps.  
 
Addressing legal fragmentation and ensuring regulatory compliance with EU rules would also entail 
guaranteeing an effective and efficient implementation of such notifi cation requirement. In recent 
years, there have been instances where Member States have failed to notify technical regulations on 
short-term rentals or have drafted regulations that were not compliant with existing EU rules such as 
the E-Commerce Directive. In certain cases, the Commission has also failed to act or respond in due 
time. We therefore recommend that the upcoming STR initiative also provides s ome much needed 
legal clarity on such processes and related redress mechanisms.  
 
Expedia Group stands ready to continue to engage with policymakers at the EU, national and local 
level, and to work together to build a more sustainable, fair and innovative European tourism 
ecosystem. We look forward to participat ing constructively in discussions on the STR initiative and 
look forward to seeing what the Commission has in store.

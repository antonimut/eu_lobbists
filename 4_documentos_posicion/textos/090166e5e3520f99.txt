BSEF Response to ELV Directive Review Public Consultation  
 
With respect to brominated flame retardant additives which may be used in some automotive 
components to meet flammability and safety requirements the following should be noted.  
 
The ELV Directive does not have a similar requirement to the WEEE Directive for the separation of 
legacy substances (be they BFRs or others). Assuming the decline in legacy BFRs in plastics parts in the 
automotive plastic waste is equivalent to that seen for such substances WEEE plastics waste (SOFIES, 
2020 – report attached), it may not be cost-effective to introduce specific requirements for separation 
of BFR-containing plastics as most of these plastics will contain BFRs which are not restricted.  
 
Focusing on quality standards for plastics recyclate would be more appropriate and easier for the 
industry to comply with. Already automotive plas tics recyclers are required to ensure and post -
consumer recyclate plastic is both RoHS compliant and also meets the EU POPs Regulation UTC 
thresholds for Deca-BDE (and other PBDEs) of 500ppm for instance.  
 
Furthermore, manufacturers and importers of articles (products) are required to notify their customers 
of the presence of any Substances of Very High Concern (SVHC) in their products exceeding 0.1% by 
weight and provide instructions on safe use of the product , according to the REACH Regulation . The 
recent launch of the ECHA SCIP database designed to assist recyclers with understanding if SVHCs are 
present or not is also relevant here.  
 
Finally, the future shape of materials recycling in the automotive sector will be dictated by the shift to 
electric vehicles due to the need to accommodate heavy battery packs. This will mean more plastics 
and composite materials. This will in turn require technological development and investments in both 
sorting the plastics containing restricted legacy substances (and other additives) as well as introduction 
of chemical recycling to enhance the yield of plastics and destroy the legacy additives.

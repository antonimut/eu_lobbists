PFP Primary Food Processors position on EU Commission 
Roadmap on Food waste reduction targets  
 
The Primary Food Processors (PFP) Association is welcoming the efforts taken at EU level to 
improve the sustainability of the food supply chain, and the initiatives to assess and prevent 
food waste, including the possibility, foreseen in this Roadmap, to set up a Union-wide food 
waste reduction target to be met by 2030. 
 
However, we still consider that the inclusion of some PFP sectors  in the Annex II of the 
European Commission Delegated Decision for the uniform measurement of levels of waste  
(2019/1597) does not reflect the realities of production for these PFP sectors. 
 
PFP sectors acknowledge the definition of 'food' laid down in Regulation (EC) No 178/2002, 
and the definition of ‘food waste’ as defined in Directive (EU) 2018/8513 amending Directive 
2008/98/EC on waste (“Waste Framework Directive). To be considered as food waste, PF P 
believe that products/materials must first and foremost be covered by the definition of food 
as defined in Regulation (EC) No 178/2002. This would not apply to stages of the food supply 
chain where certain products have not yet become ‘food’ as the use of these products has not 
yet been determined.  
 
Many products of PFP sectors are also ‘by-products’, as defined by article 5 of Directive (EU) 
2018/851 and should therefore not be considered as waste. These products are usually not 
edible, and/or (if edible) are not intended for human consumption and provide feedstock for 
materials and chemicals that serve essential roles in the society . They do not, therefore, 
satisfy the criteria to be considered as food waste.  
 
PFP sectors therefore believe that any part of the agricultural raw materials, that is not 
edible, and/or that is not – and never was - intended for human consumption, should not be 
considered as food waste. 
 
Primary food processors strive for resource eff iciency and have a long history of processing 
agricultural raw materials and of separating all of their valuable components for use in food, 
feed and industrial applications  based on their funtionality. Loss/wastage of products  from 
biorefiniery processes from our members  that would actually qualify as ‘food waste’ as 
explained above are considered to be null within PFP sectors. PFP processes optimise the 
valorisation of raw materials by producing a wide range of products and by-products. Industrial 
applications of the latter include, among others, pharmaceuticals,  detergents, cosmetics, 
bioplastics, lubricants, fuels, paper and paint. PFP products and by-products are substituting 
fossil-based ingredients with renewable ones and are hence contributing to the development 
of the EU bioeconomy.  
 
For this reason, while welcoming EU initiatives to improve the sustainability of the supply 
chain and reduce food loss and food waste, we ask once again the European Commission to

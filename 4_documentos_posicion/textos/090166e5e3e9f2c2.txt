      29 October 2021 Freshfel Europe response to Inception Impact Assessment on Proposal for a revision of Directive 2008/98/EC on waste – part on food waste reduction target  Freshfel Europe, the European Fresh Produce Association, welcomes the European Commission’s proposal for a ‘revision of Directive 2008/98/EC on waste – part on food waste reduction target’. This initiative under the Farm to Fork Strategy will further stimulate Europe’s food waste reduction efforts throughout the agri-food supply chain for a more sustainable food system in line with the European Green Deal’s ambitions. Freshfel Europe additionally strongly supports continued work throughout the Union towards meeting Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) 12.3 to halve per capita food waste at retail and consumer level by 2030 and reduce food losses along the food supply chain, which will be facilitated by this initiative.  Please find below Freshfel Europe’s remarks regarding the Inception Impact Assessment.  Definition of ‘food waste’ must be clearly defined As part of the revision of Directive 2008/98/EC, the definition of ‘food waste’ must be very clearly defined, leaving no room for misinterpretation. While ‘food loss’ will not be part of the food waste reduction targets as it is not covered under the Directive, a clear and concise definition of ‘food waste’ should still be provided, especially to avoid a lack of harmonisation across the EU upon implementation. The definition should be in line with previous European Commission initiatives regarding food waste, such as monitoring and reporting obligations to ensure coherence.  Nevertheless, Freshfel Europe supports further assessment by the European Commission of whether the proposal should delineate between edible and inedible food waste. Assessment here should be scientific and evidence-based. This is not clarified in the Inception Impact Assessment document and should be further explored.  Food security and food safety must be ensured  The food waste reduction targets developed must ensure food security and uphold food safety standards throughout the Union. Food security assurance includes maintaining market competitiveness of agri-food sector operators as well as maintaining food affordability across Europe, where sustainable and nutritious food should be both available and accessible to all.  Policy options chosen must be practical while remaining ambitious In reference to the policy options presented in the Inception Impact Assessment document, Freshfel Europe supports the following options regarding the scope, expression, the way the targets are set as well as the level of the targets themselves. The Association’s below preferences are based on facilitating a holistic, but also efficient and effective, effort throughout the entire agri-food supply chain to achieve food waste reduction throughout the Union. Freshfel Europe also welcomes the European Commission’s intention to carefully assess the technical feasibility of each of the options presented in the Inception Impact Assessment to ensure that the initiative remains practical for operators as across the supply chain

Freshfel Europe strongly supports ‘Option S1 – target covering the whole food supply chain, from farm gate to final consumer’ regarding the scope of the initiative. While SDG 12.3 specifically refers to food waste reduction at retail and consumer level, narrowing efforts to these two stages of the supply chain will limit food waste reduction efforts. Freshfel Europe advocates for a more holistic supply chain approach for food waste reduction, which places responsibility on all actors throughout the chain to contribute where possible to food waste reduction efforts. Select stages of the supply chain should not be burdened alone with meeting targets. As emphasized in the EU Code of Conduct for Responsible Food Business and Marketing Practices, collaboration throughout the chain should be encouraged to identify hot spots and prioritize action.  In regard to the expression to be used, Freshfel Europe advises the European Commission to make an assessment of the options outlined that balances robustness in terms of measurement ability, while also allowing Member State’s previous efforts in food waste reduction to be reflected. Currently there is a very divergent landscape in terms of Member State’s pre-existing food waste prevention initiatives, with some Member States having taken direct action and achieving reduction results more than others. Freshfel Europe supports the proposal’s objective of EU-level food waste targets being to facilitate actions across all Member States, ensuring that in the process Member States who have undertaken less reduction and prevention action so far are accelerated so that a harmonised action level is reached across Europe.  The way targets are set for Member States must first be coherent with the expression chosen to be used. Freshfel Europe views that ‘Option T3 a collection target on EU level based on Member State contributions’ is a more systemic approach to food waste reduction across Europe, relative to Option T1 and T2 cited in the Inception Impact Assessment. This option has the possibility to incentivise action across Europe to reach a common target together, while also being inclusive taking into account different Member States’ situations in terms of food waste measurement, monitoring and reduction efforts.  The target range chosen for the initiative must reflect the short timeframe involved, whereby Member States’ food waste data (from 2020) will only be first reported by 2022, with the target year remaining at 2030. Nonetheless, the target range chosen should still be both ambitious to achieve results while additionally not hampering the smooth functioning of the food supply chain without unintended consequences to deliver safe, healthy and affordable food to consumers across Europe during the timeframe. Freshfel Europe supports the European Commission’s intention for close consultation with stakeholders to assess the feasibility of an appropriate evidence-based food waste reduction target.  Highly detailed impact assessment necessary complementing stakeholder engagement Freshfel Europe stresses the need for a highly detailed impact assessment for the proposal for a ‘revision of Directive 2008/98/EC on waste – part on food waste reduction target’. The initiative will inherently impact all agri-food sector operations and should therefore not under any circumstances be subject to an accelerated policy adoption process. Freshfel Europe supports a high level of consultation with agri-food stakeholders throughout the policy-making process, and remains available for further information and consultation.  ***

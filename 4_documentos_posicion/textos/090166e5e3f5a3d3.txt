Open Public Consultation on PS and EQS (Water Framework Directive) 
 
 
EUROFER would like to thank you for the opportunity to provide some general comments to this 
Open Public Consultation on the Priority Substances (PS) and Environmental Quality Standards 
(EQS) review process. In this regard, we would like to highlight the following:  
• The importance of improved transparency.  
This process, has in general, been very difficult to follow. For example, we question how 
the EQS for a substance still being assessed by SCHEER will fit into this process. A better 
communication regarding the scope, terms of reference, and clearer timelin e for 
stakeholder input of an Impact Assessment is needed. 
• Consistent data use and communication to stakeholders 
Article 191 of the Treaty of the Functioning of the European Union and Article 16 of the 
Water Framework Directive state s that all available monitoring, scientific and technical 
data ‘shall be taken into account’. Where relevant monitoring data sets are available, they 
should all be assessed in a consistent manner  and shared with stakeholders  for their 
review.  
• Unclear and unfeasible timelines for the completion of the Prioritisation Process.  
We would welcome a clear and reasonable timeline that reflects the scope and complexity 
of the task at hand, as well as a commitment to allow more time to provide relevant 
responses to comments when needed.  
• Agreed relevant analytical standards a precondition for setting EQS 
Robust and reliable monitoring and analysis of a substance is dependent on the 
availability of agreed analytical standards that take into consideration inter alia aspects 
such as complex physical properties, situations where ambient concentrations are very 
low and close to or below existing possible limits of quantification. 
EUROFER also wishes to inform that some of these ‘procedural concerns’ have already been 
communicated by some industry associations via the Expert Survey (on the 19 October).

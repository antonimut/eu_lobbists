Cefic views on the i mpact assessment of a possible 
revision of the lists of pollutants affecting surface and 
ground waters 
Cefic supports the revision of  lists of  pollutants affecting surface and 
groundwaters based on new scientific developments and on a thorough 
data collection involving a proper consultation of relevant stakeholders. 
Cefic supports the EU Green Deal. To this end, we support the overall strategic direction stated in the EU 
Action Plan Towards a Zero Pollution Ambition for air, water and soil  and efforts to secure the more 
efficient and safer use of water. 
With this explanatory note, Cefic intends to expand on some issues mentioned in the public consultation. 
This note needs to be read in combination with the questionnaire.  In addition, t his note is also 
complementary to the position paper on Cefic views on the Revision of lists of pollutants affecting surface 
and groundwaters. The sequence of statements follows the order of questions of the public consultation. 
Cefic does not provide an opinion on substances related to other industry or economic sectors as these are 
better suited to provide answers on this topic.  
Regarding the presence of substances in the EU surface and groundwater bodies, Cefic provided a reply 
from the perspective of chemical industry emissions to water: 
• Regarding the presence of pharmaceuticals in surface and ground water  (pag 11&12) , Cefic 
recommends focusing on the largest sources of pharmaceuticals entering the environment, as 
specified in the European Commission’s communication on the Strategic Approach to 
Pharmaceuticals in the Environment which rightfully identifies that: “The largest sour ce of 
pharmaceuticals entering the environment is use; the route will likely differ depending upon 
whether human or veterinary use is involved. (…)”. As published in the COM’s Q&A on the strategic 
approach to pharmaceuticals in the environment  90% of pharmaceuticals discharged in the 
environment result from their excretion by animals and humans at t he post-consumption stage 
of their lifecycle, as well -outlined by the European Commission, Cefic thinks that special 
monitoring efforts could be focused on e.g. the treatment of used urban sewage water, on the 
prevention of unnecessary usage and waste of m edicines, and the safe disposal of unused 
medicines. 
• With regards to pesticides and biocides  (pag 12),  Cefic replies focus on ly on  biocides including 
disinfectants. Pesticides are regulated at EU level under the Plant Protection Products Regulation, 
while biocides have their own EU regulation, the Biocidal Product Regulation (BPR), and should be 
addressed separately. Biocides are heavily regulated and t he BPR is effective and existing 
measures are sufficient in addressing surface and groundwater pollution. Cefic recalls that a n 
environmental risk assessment is performed both during the biocidal active substance approval 
and the biocidal product authorisation processes. This is also valid for disinfectants, listed among 
the “substances released from household items” as they are biocides and are covered by the BPR.

Regarding the question on the regulatory measures and their implementation  (pag 12&13), Cefic thinks 
that the Water Framework Directive contains a fundamental framework approach and is well designed to 
work in tandem with other legislation (e.g. IED, REACH, Seveso...). For example, the Industrial Emissions 
Directive prescribes the best available techniques for the industrial activities and the binding limit values 
for emissions, which serves as a basis for plant permits. Therefore, there is no lack of investments as BREFs 
enforce proper investment in the right technologies in o rder to achieve best in class emission reduction, 
including the reduction of any emissions to water.  Above and beyond, the Seveso Directive  requires 
investments in measures in the process and plant safety, thereby ensuring that there are no accidental 
releases to the environment. Together with the other legislations, REACH is  also taking measures on  eg 
PBT substances, to reduce emissions from wide spread uses and contribute to the prevention of EU wide 
risks to the aquatic environment. Cefic calls to maintain this comprehensive and fully coordinated 
approach.  
Regarding the question on the effectiveness of strategies to address surface and groundwater pollution 
(pag 15) , we understand the word  “strategies” refers to  EU legislations . The EU has one of the most 
advanced and sophisticated legislations in the world. By complying to this, along with EU operating permits, 
our chemical industry adheres to the highest levels of protection for human health and the environment 
from adverse effects of chemicals.  
Regarding the question to “what… should the EC improve to ensure its policy actions / strategies address 
more effectively surface and groundwater pollution” (pag 16), and the options provided for “a more swift 
update of the lists with polluting substances for surface waters (under the WFD) ” and “GWD Annexes”, 
Cefic would like to highlight the key importance of risk based assessment process and the  high-quality 
data in the revision process (EQS/ Predicted No Effect Concentration (PNEC), monitoring data). 
• Only the substances that are of EU wide relevance for which EU wide risks are clearly identified 
for surface and ground waters should be considered  for the revision process . Dossiers prepared 
for new short -listed substances need to be properly finalised be fore being made available for 
further use) . It is crucial to set a clear and realistic  timeline/schedule for finalisation of the 
dossiers, allowing relevant stakeholders to submit data and to properly review draft dossiers .  
• In addition, finalised substance dossiers should be fully transparent  and clearly reflect different 
opinions of stakeholders where no consensus can be reached . For cases where full consensus on 
the PNEC for substances cannot be reached  within a realistic timeframe, t he dossiers should 
clearly reflect the different opinions and outstanding issues. This is important to ensure that the 
PNEC values are based on sound science and can be implemented at local level without any further 
considerations. 
• Finally, the de-selection of substances from the priority (hazardous) substance lists as well as the 
substances listed in Annexes I and II of the Groundwater Directive should carefully be evaluated 
and put into effect based on the principles set out above.    
Regarding the question on the access to information (pag 16), we would like to highlight that the chemical 
companies are already informed of the situation of the water bodies in the regions where they operate.  
Cefic is looking forward to sharing its ideas and exploring potential policy solutions with the European 
Commission and Stakeholders in due course.

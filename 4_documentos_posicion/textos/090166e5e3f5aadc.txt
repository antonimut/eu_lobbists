Euromines response to New Soil Strategy - healthy soil for a healthy life - Ares(2020)6391319 
 
Brussels, 10/12/2020 
 
Euromines acknowledges the main goals and problems that the initiative “New Soil Strategy” wants 
to tackle, especially to address soil degradation in a comprehensive way.  
 
As already stated in our Biodiversity Strategy position paper we foresee the need that the different 
policies like the Biodiversity Strategy 2030 and the Zero Pollution Action Plan, but also other related 
policies like the Industrial Strategy and the list of Critical Raw Materials, are coherent.  
 
The soils in Europe are characterized by a great variety of different soil types in different region s of 
Europe with differing characteristics such as soil fertility and buffering potential. Also, the pressure on 
soils is often very different in the Member states. The creation of uniform EU soil standards will be a 
highly difficult or even an impossible task. Many Member states already developed since the Eighties 
their specific (risk based) soil legislation. We therefore doubt that common EU soil standards will be 
the right approach. Against this background, if needed, only non-binding voluntary measures 
supporting member states might be considered.  
 
With a still growing population and a demand for European raw materials we question if “no net land 
take” would be the most appropriate target. We think the focus should shift from “no land take” to 
“sustainable land use”. 
 
Finally, a proactive dialog between our sector and EU decision makers is key to let our sector interact 
and bring forward best practices on soil c onservation and restoration that are applied within our 
sector.

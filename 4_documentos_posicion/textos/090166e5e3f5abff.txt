 
MOL Group’s position on the review of the End-of-life Vehicles Directive 
 
MOL Group is committed to the EU circular economy objectives and is transforming to adapt to a circular and low-
carbon world. Being amongst the top  ten polymer producers in Europe and the biggest in Central Europe , we 
believe that plastics deliver significant benefits to the economy. By reducing weight , for example in automotive 
applications, it also contributes to reducing carbon emission s. On the other hand , the benefits can only be 
maintained if the challenges with resource efficiency and plastic waste are solved by moving to a circular model. 
Transition to a more circular economy requires significant investments, and EU rules on end-of-life vehicles can 
ensure regulatory certainty and increased incentives for investments in innovative solutions.  
MOL Group welcomes the objective of the European Commission to ensure that end -of-life vehicles (ELVs) are 
managed sustainably  and to  encourage the car industry to adopt a sustainable model for the design and 
production of cars. Below we expanded our points on recycled content targets that we believe will be crucial to 
achieve these objectives. 
 
Recycling of pre -consumer plastic waste can increase the circularity of the automotive sector already today, 
incorporation of post-consumer plastics still requires research & development and availability of good quality 
feedstock  
We welcome the Commission’s intention to include in the ELV Directive a recycled plastics content target for new 
vehicles. While we agree that t here are challenges in the wide adoption of post -consumer recyclates in vehicle 
manufacturing, and that regulatory action could help incr ease activities in this area, we believe that the targets 
should be set for both post-consumer and pre-consumer recyclates, as both areas need significant improvements 
to increase the replacement of virgin raw materials and lead to greater circularity in the automotive sector. 
Nowadays, dismantling ELV plastics for recycling appears to be a limited practice yet (e.g. ELV car bumpers). The 
incorporation of post-consumer recyclates still needs significant research and development activities, considering 
that materials in vehicles are required to fulfil various technical specifications. Since automotive parts and vehicles 
are long-lasting products (ca. 15 years) , the use of recycled plastics from automotive post -consumer waste is 
hindered by evolution in specification and substances management over time.   
At the same time, incorporating recycled content from pre -consumer plastic waste in the automotive sector is 
continuously improving, as it d ecreases scrap generation and production costs , while ensuring  homogeneous 
properties of the new compounds. By replacing virgin feedstock in the production of compounds, the use of pre-
consumer waste guarantees CO2 reductions. We do not agree with the Commission’s assessment that growth 
potential is very low in this s ector, as there is further potential in expanding capacity of closed -loop recycling of 
pre-consumer waste in the automotive sector, which could help optimize properties and meet the automotive 
converters’ specifications and the growing demand for high -quality recyclates. Pre-consumer waste also offers 
better quality for additional application with post-consumer waste and can increase the virgin-like quality of the 
final product. 
While a target to incorporate 5% post -consumer recyclates per the mass of plast ics in vehicles would not be 
feasible before 2035 -2040 due to the above -mentioned quality, availability  and durability standards of end -
applications, the u se of pre -consumer plastic waste could  already be scaled up  to 10% by 20 25 if the 
necessary  regulatory drivers are in place. However, without regulatory incentives to increase also pre-consumer 
waste in automotive applications, this development is unlikely to happen. Therefore, we recommend that the 
Commission c onsiders both post -consumer and pre -consumer recycled content targets  to increase resource 
efficiency.

Suomen Motoristit ry (SMOTO) is a national umbrella organization and it represents the interests 
of all Finnish motorcyclists. SMOTO maintains and develops international cooperation with other 
motorcyclist organizations. 
In SMOTO's view, the best and most cost-effective recovery of vehicle parts is their re-use, which 
is typically done by dismantlers and companies specializing in the recycling of parts. SMOTO's 
perception is that the recycling of motorcycle parts is largely handled by hobbyists and enthusiasts 
and there is a demand for them. Almost all parts are reused or refurbished as spare parts. The 
amount of environmentally harmful materials in motorcycles is small compared to cars. 
In 2021, SMOTO conducted a study to determine the level of recycling of motorized two-wheelers. 
The survey was conducted as a questionnaire survey, which was answered by 11 motorcycle 
dismantlers, 20 car dismantlers, one builder and one motorcycle club. 
A total of 540 motorized two-wheelers per year are handled at motorcycle dismantling stations 
that responded to the survey. In addition, a total of 85 pcs over 125 cm3 and 25 pcs over 50 cm3 
motorized two-wheelers per year are handled at the dismantlers. One of the dismantlers did not 
disclose the number of two-wheelers handled, citing a trade secret, which makes the actual 
number larger. 
The number of motorized two-wheelers handled in car dismantling stations is considerably lower:  
total of 16 pcs 125 cm3 and larger two-wheelers were handled per year. On the other hand, 54 pcs 
of 50 cm3 motorized two-wheelers are handled in car dismantling stations per year. The 
dismantlers that responded to the survey thus handle a total of about 720 motorized two-
wheelers per year. 
Although car dismantling stations handle motorized two-wheelers, very few of them end up back 
to consumers through recycling. Through motorcycle dismantlers, about 62 per cent of the 
dismantling parts are recycled back to consumers, in car dismantling only about 14 per cent. 
Instead, through the builder and the motorcycle club that responded to the survey, almost all 
parts are recycled to consumers. 
The EU Commission is currently pushing for a reform of the end-of-life vehicles directive. One 
issue to be clarified is whether vehicles other than passenger cars and vans, such as motorcycles, 
should be included in the scope of the directive. In its study, SMOTO looked at how the end-of-life 
vehicles directive would affect motorized two-wheelers. Of those who responded to the survey, 
motorcycle dismantlers saw the impact as partly negative, partly non-existent. For the builder and 
the club, the impact was seen as negative. In one car dismantling plant, the impact was seen as 
positive and business-enhancing. 
SMOTO is concerned about the impact of a possible end-of-life vehicle directive on the recycling of 
motorized two-wheel dismantling parts. In the case of dismantling plants, recycling is low, but 
parts are recycled extensively among enthusiasts. SMOTO's understanding is that the recycling of 
motorcycle parts is largely handled by enthusiasts and there is a demand for them. Almost all parts 
are reused or refurbished as spare parts. 
An amendment to the directive would make it more difficult to procure used motorcycle parts, as 
the recycling emphasized in the directive is mainly the re-use of vehicle metals in the production

of a new raw material, i.e. the smelting of metals. SMOTO wonders why smelting is seen as a 
better option than recycling parts. In addition, new parts are not being manufactured for older 
motorcycles that have long lifetime, which means that the reuse of old parts also offers a future 
for old models, instead of consumers having to buy a completely new motorcycle. In the case of 
motorized two-wheelers, significantly less workshop-based waste is generated than, for example, 
cars, and appropriate recycling methods already exist for this waste. 
According to SMOTO's research, motorcycles are almost completely recycled by enthusiasts. 
Although the end-of-life vehicles directive aims to increase recycling, recycling and re-use is 
already working better among hobbyists than it could be as shown by the directive.

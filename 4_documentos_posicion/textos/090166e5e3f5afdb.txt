Introduction 
This paper presents background information, rationales and comments related to the EUROFER 
responses introduced into the questionnaire  of the Open Public Consultation on the revision of 
the E nd-of-Life Vehicles (ELVs) Directive. The document contains extensive inputs to some of 
the free text question boxes because of its limited characters. 
Comment to Question 5 "Do you think that specific target(s) for reuse should be implemented 
separately from recycling with the aim to support the transition to the circular economy within 
the EU?" 
The option of reuse of vehicle parts allows supporting Circular Economy  with relevant 
environmental benefi ts. In particular, the reuse of complex parts of cars is an established 
market that has developed in recent decades. The background rational e of this market is based 
on the following aspects. (1) Mechanically complex parts of engines, gears or clutches of old 
cars, or no more produced, create a sustained, although very niche, demand. (2) Complex parts 
of the car usually made by high resistance, versatility and ductility  materials can often be 
dismantled without damag e. (3) These complex parts can be very quickly reconditioned (or 
slightly repaired if needed); however, if these parts are too damaged, they have to be recycled. 
(4) A very light oversizing of these complex mechanical parts of the vehicle will allow fulfilling 
the conditions reported under points (1) to (3), prolonging life expectations. 
According to our knowledge about vehicle parts' reuse or remanufacturing activitie s, almost all 
mechanical parts reused or remanufactured in this niche market are made with steel.

Comment to Question 1 "Taking into account that currently not all vehicle are treated 
according to minimum EU treatment requirements at the end of their life, should the ELV 
Directive also apply to other types of veh icles?" and Question 11  "What could be the 
advantages of extending the scope of the ELVD to other vehicles, e.g. to motorcycles and trucks?" 
It is definitively necessary to expand the scope of the ELVs Directive. Future developments 
linked to electrificati on or the production of new power train systems will need to have more 
dismantling and separation requirements, together with explicit requirements on managing the 
different waste fractions that result from the treatment of ELVs. 
Trucks, motorbikes and any type of motor vehicle or working machine should be included. ELVs 
should become a facilitator for the transition towards EU circularity; therefore, the recovery of 
parts, materials and their possible recycling and reuse has to be fostered to the maximum 
extent possible.

The recycled content makes sense for those materials or parts that are  not recycled enough.

contrary, steel parts of all vehicles are almost entirely recycled; more specifically, certain steel 
products are produced using predominantly ferrous scrap, while others mix primary and 
secondary sources. The limitation of the ferrous scrap intake of some steel qualities is due to 
the mismatch between the quality of the secondary raw material (ferrous scrap) recovered 
from waste and the expected final properties of the steel product. 
Therefore, we do not see the recycled content target as a valid recycling metric for all the 
components or materials of the vehicles, but only for a specific set. Moreover, at the moment , 
it seems also unfeasible to set a recycled content target for the entire vehicle beca use this 
could lead to sub-optimised solutions. 
As a final comment, we would like to add that, in circular terms, it would be more effective and 
efficient to impose a recyclability target for the vehicle because this will automatically imply the 
simultaneous fulfilment of the recovery and recycling targets and the availability, after the 
treatment of ELVs, of high quality secondary raw materials. 
Comment to Question 16 "Which, if any, additional criteria for evaluating exemptions from

indicate which criteria should be integrated into the exemption assessment mechanism"

concern in all recent revision s of the EU laws. However, it is necessary to merge the growing 
scientific knowledge about the substances with their implications with practical risks and socio-
economic impacts . Science -based knowledge should always engage with  societal needs and 
potential trade-offs; although science is a basic ingredient, a pure science -based decision could 
lead to regrettable substitutions or choices that the future generation will have to deal with.

the management of substances and their possible exemptions. For instance, the third option on

Comment to Question 27 "If you are of the opinion that ATFs shall remove certain parts of 
ELVs before shredding to help to i ncrease reuse (see question above), which parts should be 
considered?" 
Our response to the question stressed the importance of reusing the most significant parts of 
the car because this will significantly support circularity and  contribute to climate mitig ation. 
But, of course, this separation should not be imposed unilaterally and in all conditions but only 
when the parts are in good states for being re used. Significantly, the mechanical components 
like gearbox, clutch and alternators, for instance, are the relevant ones. 
Moreover, we would like to stress that in a car body, for instance, there are different qualities 
of steel that can be grouped in carbon steel grades and stainless steel grades. Stainless is much 
less than carbon steel, but it has a much h igher value because of its rich content of alloying 
elements: in particular, Nickel, Chromium and Molybdenum. In particular, certain qualities of 
stainless steel are magnetic, like the carbon steel grades. Therefore, it makes sense to separate 
stainless steel parts for recovering and recycl ing them into new stainless steel grades, 
benefitting the alloying elements' content. 
Comment to Question 28 "Would material -specific recycl ing targets have an impact on 
innovation?" 
Material specific recycling target s might have a positive impact on improving the quality of the 
different material fractions recovered from ELVs. However, this type of target  should be only 
for informative and im provement purposes and  not legally binding. The recycling target of an 
ELV should uniquely be at vehicle level (mass on mass). Material -specific targets should be 
complementary information for public authorities – to evaluate the functioning of the new 
Directive at a deeper level of granularity – and for the automotive industry – to assess the real 
performance of their products and improve their design process and circularity of the vehicles.

Comment to Question 35 "Please indicate other materials for which recycled content targets 
should be considered and explain shortly why" 
The definition of targets for the recycled content should concern the materials with no recycling 
or a meagre recycling rate. The rare earth elements (REEs) and critical raw materials (CRMs) are 
perfect examples. REEs, Gallium, Magnesium , and Niobium have  the highest supply risk in the 
list of CRMs released in 2020 by the EU Commission. Moreover, the recycling of these is 
extremely low if not absent. Therefore, it could make sense to es tablish targets on recycled 
content for these CRMs; however, it should also be taken into account that often these 
materials are a component of certain alloys or used in certain specific products: for instance, 
REEs in magnets or electronic parts , for which it is challenging to extract or separate CRMs.  It 
will also be relevant to consider the recycled content for alloying elements such as Nickel and 
Chromium used in several value chains, other than automotive. 
Therefore, imposing a recycled content target will not be feasible without supporting a 
provision for separating the parts containing the CRMs, as selected on purpose in question 18. 
Comment to Question 36 "Do you see a need for policy/regulatory measure regarding 
requirements on ethical sourcing of raw materials contained in vehicles?" 
We selected the option 'No' because the EU already has its own Regulation on ethical sourcing 
of raw materials, the Regulation on due diligence  (EU) 2017/821. This Regulation is already in  
force, and industries have al ready started their compliance process to its requirements. The 
ethical sourcing of raw materials has to be dealt with only by specific regulations. T herefore, 
there is no need for additional policy/regulatory measures. However, question 36 in this 
respect is somewhat unclear because it practically gives two options for saying no: e.g. selecting 
the first two options (Yes and then a)) or just choosing the No (second-to-last) option. For the 
sake of simplicity, we just selected the No. 
Comment to Question 37 "Do you see a need for policy/regulatory measure regarding 
requirements on carbon footprint of raw materials used in vehicles?" 
The progress and improvements in tackling environmental impacts during the use phase of 
products have been possible thanks to l ife-cycle thinking and assessment. The further 
progressive use of Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) methods in the products policy will make final 
products' footprint or parts an important decisional factor. 
However, this transition towards more LCA -driven choice will require time, and there is still not 
yet a clear and harmonised consensus on how this tool should be use d, how to compare 
product uses and how to link LCA/footprint information to policy. The process seems 
irreversible but still needs guidance and a step-wise approach via initial voluntary 
requirements.

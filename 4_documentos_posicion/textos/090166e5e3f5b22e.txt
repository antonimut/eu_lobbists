LyondellBasell response to public consultation for the impact assessment 
supporting the review of the Directive 2000/53/EC on end -of-life vehicles and of 
the Directive 2005/64/EC on the type-approval of motor vehicles with regard to 
their reusability, recyclability and recoverability  
 
LyondellBasell aspires to be an industry leader in the circular economy. By 2030, our ambition is to 
produce and market two million metric tons of recycled and renewable-based polymers annually.  
 
LyondellBasell supports mandates for recycled content in order to further develop end markets for 
post-use plastics, driving their value and circularity.  
 
We believe that the following targets in vehicles are ambitious yet technically feasible:  
5% by 2025 
15% by 2030 
2040 and 2050 targets could be significantly higher depending on feedstock availability 
 
The introduction of such targets needs to be linked to enabling conditions: 
• Consistent and reliable feedstock supply in sufficient volumes and quality. 
• Pre-consumer and post -consumer material should both qualify as recycled content in 
accordance with the ISO 14021 standard. 
• Both mechanical and chemical (molecular) recycling recognized as eligible sources of 
recycled content. 
• Mass balance is used to attrib ute chemically recycled content to end products at the 
highest aggregated level, not the article level. 
• There should be free attribution of all chemical recycling products including by -products 
but excluding process losses. 
• The mass balance system allocate s sustainability benefits to products without physical 
separation, consistent with the International Sustainability and Carbon Certification 
(ISCC) system for biomass and bioenergy.  
• Certification standards and methodology must be transparent and preferabl y harmonized 
with broadly used global certification standards such as ISCC+. 
• All health, safety and performance requirements for each application must be met without 
exception.  
• Recycled content targets should be ambitious yet technically feasible and cons istent with 
overall policy objectives 
• Value chain collaboration is crucial to fully leverage recycling mandates to achieve 
circularity goals.

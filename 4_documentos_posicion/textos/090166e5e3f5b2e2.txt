SUOMEN AUTOPURKAMOLIITTO r.y., FINLAND, comments and contributes additionally 
on review the EVL DIRECTIVE as follows: 
 
The opinion is based in particular on the Finnish circumstances and practical examples. 
In principle, the current ELV Directive would already be nearly sufficient in terms of its 
objectives and provisions. 
 
The real problem is that it is not enforced. 
 
Already, Article 7 requires that the reuse of dismantled parts is a priority; 
 
1. Member States shall take the necessary measures to encourage the re-use of parts 
suitable for reuse. 
 
However, for example, on the home page of Suomen Autokierrätys Oy 
(http://www.aut.fi/engparisto/auton_kierratys), the company owned by ca importers of 
Finland is the producer responsibility company in Finland, about re-use only a passing 
phrase is mentioned: 
 
“The body of the recycling system consists of reception, de-registration, 
pre-treatment and shredding. In pre-treatment, the end-of-life vehicle is 
drained of liquids. In addition, of the car are removed e.g. tires, battery and 
catalytic converter. Explosive parts such as airbags are removed or 
otherwise rendered harmless. As per demand removable parts such as 
engine, sheet metal, etc., will be removed from the car. ” 
 
It seems that car dismantling companies in Finland are just a camourflage for minimal 
re-use. 
In Finland, for example, only about 20% of ELV vehicles are for re-use of parts destined 
through car dismantlers. This low number does not support reuse goals. Although 
according to waste legislation, sorting shall take place at source. 
The ineffective system means that main part of ELV’s go directly to shredders, without 
possibility of re-use. 
   
The directive shall therefore be clarified so that ELVs shall always be first offered to 
legal dismantlers and also treated in a practical manner before being damaged e.g. by 
forklifts. Only legal dismantlers=ATF´s shall have the right to issue a certificate of 
destruction. 
 
For example, on the basis of car insurance, car tax or fuel tax allocated share (the 
principle is "user pays" = car owners), a reasonable premium shall be paid to the 
dismantlers, to make sure that it is economical also treat all fractions properly e.g. 
separating plastics, glasses, liquids, etc. 
 
Article 8 already requires: 
Coding standards / dismantling 
 
1. Member States shall take the necessary measures to ensure that producers, in 
cooperation with manufacturers of materials and equipment, apply component and 
material coding standards, in particular to facilitate the identification of parts and 
materials that are suitable for re-use and recovery.

3. Member States shall take the necessary measures to ensure that producers provide 
dismantling information for each new type of vehicle placed on the market within six 
months of the date on which the vehicle is placed on the market. This dismantling 
information shall indicate the parts and materials of the various vehicles and the position 
of all dangerous goods in the vehicles, where it is necessary for treatment facilities to 
know them in order to meet the requirements of this Directive, taking particular account 
of the objectives mentioned in Article 7. 
4. Without prejudice to commercial and industrial data protection, Member States shall 
take the necessary measures to ensure that authorized manufacturers of vehicles make 
available on request to authorized treatment facilities appropriate information on the 
dismantling, storage and testing of re-usable parts. 
 
The requirements of Article 8 are today not met in practice. Their compliance shall be 
enforced and sanctions OR incentives shall be imposed on car makers. Only in this way 
will the real circular economy targets be achieved. For example, for plastic parts - as is 
already required by the legislation - manufacturers shall be obliged to mark the exact 
composition on the parts themselves, this is not the case today. 
 
The directive shall focus on the quality requirements of the circular economy, in 
particular the reuse of dismantled parts. 
 
Of the quality objectives, the most important and most effective approach to the circular 
economy and the environment is to increase and optimize the reuse of dismantled parts. 
 
The original used spare parts, engine or body parts, which are also inspected and 
certified by an authorized dismantler for quality and safety. 
 
Unlike manufacturing, using original used spare parts does not result in CO2 at all and 
is therefore the highest opportunity of circular economy in the automotive industry. 
 
In Finland, Suomen Autokierrätys Oy (Finnish Car Recycling Ltd.) actually requires 
that its primary business partner so called “operator” (in total there are four in the 
country) must be a shredding plant, which is case of a wolf in sheep's clothing.  As the 
shredders, in turn, control the reception points, so the system has been shaped to 
their advantage. It is clear that their priority is material recovery only rather than 
reuse. 
 
This is completely contrary to the principles and objectives of a modern circular 
economy.  And even the present ELV Directive. 
This disadvantage of the recycling system has led to material scarcity as EVs vehicles 
pass by car dismantling yards. This has led to e.g. Finnish dismantlers have to buy 
ELV’s and used parts from other countries. 
 
In Finland, 88 362 end-of-life vehicles were scrapped last year. Both national and EU 
legislation have set quantitative and qualitative targets for reuse and recycling. There 
should be defined quantity target for reuse of dismantled parts. 
 
Thus, the primary and qualitatively best practice required by the directive, i.e. the 
reuse of parts of end-of-life vehicles, is not achieved in Finland, even as required by 
the current directive and legislation. 
 
The black economy and, in particular, illegal operators, created by the inefficiency of 
the controls, distort competition and cause the potential of continued environmental 
damage. 
 
At present, the statistics on end-of-life vehicles cannot be considered reliable either. 
In fact, Traficom's vehicle register is not up-to-date at all, as there are about 1 million 
vehicles of unknown whereabouts in Finland, which no longer exist or have actually 
been exported, but are still in the register statistics. 
A common European register is therefore needed. 
 
The emphasis shall be on quantitative requirements of the circular economy, in 
particular the reuse of dismantled parts. 
 
Of the quality objectives, the most important and most effective approach to the 
circular economy and the environment is to increase and optimize the reuse of 
dismantled parts. 
The original used spare parts, engine or body part that is also inspected and certified 
by an authorized, authorized environmental dismantling company for quality and 
safety. 
 
The economic significance is also important, as the re-use of a used part replaces 
imports and lowers the cost of repairs for both businesses e.g. insurance companies 
and individuals. 
 
Recycled parts allow extended use of the vehicle for several years, the emissions 
caused by the continued use of the vehicle are small compared to the production of a 
new car. Older cars typically also have low mileage. 
 
And besides, in the end, the vehicle with all its parts ends up in the material cycle 
when it is no longer economical to maintain it. Car dismantling facilities do not lose 
material, only allowing the dismantled parts to be reused in accordance with the ELV 
Directive. 
 
The coding used by the car manufacturers in various parts often prevents reuse. The 
directive must ensure that coding must not restrict the re-use of components and 
parts. 
 
According to media reports, experts estimate that in Europe demolition parts 
the share of corrections is 2%, while in the USA it is 20%. 
 
SUMMARY OF THE PROPOSED MEASURES AND OBJECTIVES FOR THE RENEWED 
DIRECTIVE: 
 
1. The certificate of destruction may be issued only by a legal car dismantler. 
 
2. Dismantlers may have reception points under their responsibility and control in 
order to achieve sufficient territorial coverage for the individual citizens. 
 
3. Vehicles shall be designed primarily for the purpose of re-use and shall not use 
materials that are hazardous to the environment. 
 
4. Demolition parts should be preferred in all repairs, especially in body parts, thus 
avoiding production emissions. 
 
5. Car manufacturers are required to update the IDIS database within three months 
with new model information in a format that allows efficient reuse of used parts. 
 
6. Materials such as plastics must have complete raw material and composition 
information. 
 
7. Codes that prevent the reuse of parts shall not be used or at least be decoded by 
lawful disassemblers. 
 
8. It must be ensured that dismantlers have economical prerequisites to act in 
environmentally best way. E.g. proper handling of liquids, plastics, glasses, etc. 
 
9. A pan-European vehicle register must be established.

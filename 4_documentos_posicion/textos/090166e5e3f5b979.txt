Introduction 
 
PlasticsEurope welcomes the opportunity to respond to the European Commission’s public 
consultation on the end-of-life vehicles directive.   
 
Plastics have been a key enabler in the modern construction, performance, safety, efficiency and 
functionality of cars. Single mould components have helped manufacturers to decrease vehicle 
assembly time, quickly introduce design innovations and trim costs. Plastics help to make cars 
lighter, thus reducing fue l demand and greenhouse gas emissions. Reducing a modern car's 
weight by 100 kg cuts fuel consumption by approximately 0.2 litres per 100 km and reduces CO2 
emissions by around 10 g/km1. 
 
This paper summarises the views of the European plastic raw material producers about key 
aspects of the European Commission’s consultation 2 (running to October 26th, 2021). In this 
consultation, the Commission has raised several points around ELV treatment such as the use of 
plastics, circularity and recycled content in vehicles, EPR, hazardous substances, and others. In 
the context of the ELV directive, plastics contribute to vehicle’s resource conservation while it is 
necessary to treat the mix of materials at end-of-life in the most optimal way. 
 
Recycled plastics content in vehicles  
 
In regard to a potential target for recycled plastics content in vehicles, PlasticsEurope conditionally 
supports ambitious yet realistic binding legislative targets for recycling of material streams from 
ELV and for recycled content in automotive under given enabling conditions . These should be 
developed and agreed in cooperation with the whole value-chain. 
 
Aligning the value chain is key in supporting any target, and targets should not compromise the 
safety, effectiveness, or performance of vehicles. The material requirements and specificities 
(mechanical, substance requirements, durability, recyclability etc) are critical for this sector and 
must be acknowledged to ensure that targets are both achievable and be neficial for the 
environment and society.    
 
Recycling technologies and material sourcing

requirements for use in automotive. Therefore, appropriate and qualified sources of secondary 
material streams are key. Such qualified streams typically originate from post -industrial/pre-

Potential targets / enabling conditions  
 
PlasticsEurope supports realistic product -based targets for recycled content under enabling 
conditions3. If appropriate, a similar approach may be applied for vehicles.  
 
To foster recycling and recycled content for vehicles, not only the ELVD itself but also the other 
relevant legislative waste regulations need to contribute. Essentially, a re vision of the Landfill 
Directive is key to ensure that plastics-containing wastes, also from ELV and ELV treatment, will 
cease to be landfilled immediately rather than in the mid-2030s as with the current regulation. This 
will enable the establishment of the required  infrastructures and capacities for recycling of end-
user wastes such as treated ELVs. 
 
Targets for recycling as well as recycled content must both lead to tangible environmental benefit 
and balance economy of the waste treatment. Technology -neutral definitions for recycling and 
recycled content (includi ng mass balance approach as a chain -of-custody) which include both 
mechanical recycling and chemical recycling must be acknowledged so that any type of recycling 
is recognised to contribute to the targets for recycling and for recycled content. 
 
Design for sustainability  
 
It is key that any dismantling provisions must not impair the essential targets of safety, comfort, 
environmental performance such as fuel/electricity consumption, costs etc. The dismantling of 
certain parts may not be technologically or technically feasible today  
 
It must be noted that chemical recycling could also increase the varieties of plastics that can be 
recycled, but technology-neutrality needs to be maintained along with enabling conditions.  
 
In conclusion, especially for complex products with long shelf life such as vehicles, the diverse 
product requirements for environment, economy and consumer aspects need to be considered 
and designed from a holistic view taking into account the full life cycle of the vehicle. 
 
Restrictions on Hazardous Substances 
 
In relation to Hazardous Substances, we note that th e consultation appears to place significant 
emphasis on “hazardous” substances without due consideration of uses and exposure.

Industry asks  
 
We therefore call on the European Commission: 
• To develop a policy framework and supporting a market for recycled plastics under enabling 
conditions, which maintains consistency with the results achieved by the CPA 
• Provide for a fertile policy and regulatory ground to foster investment in and development of 
new recycling technologies  such as chemical recycling, while also allowing for new 
technologies such as bio-based plastics, or plastics from CO2 
• To provide the necessary support of authorities to improve the efficiency of waste collection 
and sorting – enabling greater returns of plastics from recycling 
• To only develop targets for recycled plastics content in vehicles in cooperation with the whole 
value chain, and taking account of the needs of the sector

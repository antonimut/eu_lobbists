 
EUROBAT comments on the Public Consultation for the impact assessment 
supporting the review of the Directive 2000/53/EC on end-of-life vehicles and 
of the Directive 2005/64/EC on the type-approval of motor vehicles with 
regard to their reusability, recyclability and recoverability 
 
Additional comment on Q15: Substances used in automotive batteries and their exposure risks 
are already regulated through REACH and Occupational Health & Safety. The majority of 
currently available battery chemistries contain hazardous substances in some form. However, 
we emphasise that these substances are contained in the battery article within sealed units, 
and are not intended to be released during normal or reasonably foreseeable conditions of 
use. Risks from battery materials are therefore largely limited to the workplace, which makes 
OSH the right risk management option. Here, our industries already promote high standards 
of worker and environmental protection. World -leading European recyclers also work to 
ensure that battery materials are safely treated and recovered at th eir end-of-life, both for 
existing and new battery types.  
We therefore urge the Commission to solve this legislative overlap and use the Batteries 
Regulation as key legislative instrument on batteries, and REACH and OSH as key instruments 
on exposure risks and chemicals management.  
 
Additional comment on Q37: carbon footprint requirement could be considered for the individual 
components, more than for the raw materials. The Batteries Regulation could be an example in this 
sense.

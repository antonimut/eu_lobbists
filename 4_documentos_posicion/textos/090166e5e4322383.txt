Position EISAP – European and International Shipowners’ Association of Portugal: 
 
On recent European Commission “FIT FOR 55” initiative: 
 
“FuelEU Maritime” 
 
With the Fit For 55 initiative and the European Commission’s revision of the European ETS Directive, 
FuelEU Maritime regulation and further legislative projects, we as EISAP are sure that European 
shipping can become one of the most advanced maritime regions. As an association representing 
shipowners with ships registered in the Portuguese International Shipping Register of Madeira 
(MAR), we believe that Portuguese flagged ships and especially their owners and respective involved 
stakeholders can play an important role in  contributing to more sustainability, responsibility and 
innovation in maritime transport. 
 
Our position on the new FuelEU Maritime initiative 
 
The shipowners represented by EISAP welcome the new FIT FOR 55 package of the European 
Commission, which was presented mid July. This can be a chance for European shipping and should 
be a blueprint for CO2 measures on an international level.  However, further details should be 
elaborated and some introduced measures need to be more practicable in order to be implemented. 
 
Though this package can only be considered as a European approach, it is an expedient plan to make 
shipping more environmentally friendly and to guide a path for climate neutral maritime transport. 
On the European level, the gap between the future aims of an international CO2 reduction of 50% by  
2050 and the missing incentives for further and concrete action is now more or less closed. The 
European maritime transport sector including the shipbuilding and supply industry now have political 
support. 
 
Together with the European Commission, the member states now need to be further included in the 
process and need to launch and foster accompanying national programs. The member states need 
to bring this issue back on the international stage at IMO. Only international measures will be able 
to sustain European shipping’s worldwide competitiveness. 
 
Despite the Commission’s FuelEU Maritime initiative, the shift of ship fuels from fossil to non-fossil, 
while such fuels are not yet available at market prices, will continue to be challenging. Equally 
demanding, will be the development of marketable climate friendly ship fuels and their climate 
friendly production. Therefore, this should not be borne by the shipowners but mainly be shouldered 
by the fuel providers and the ship supply industry. They will need to offer innovative solutions to 
the shipowners and vessel operators. As soon as there are available solutions at market prices, the 
shipping companies would surely consider such solutions. Clearly, there is a lot more research and 
development needed, accompanied by governmental support, for the production of climate friendly 
shipping solutions.

As a consequence to the FuelEU Maritime initiative, a defacto-abolishment of recently used fossil 
fuels is inevitable. The CO2 reduction path of fuels used in shipping will make it almost impossible in 
a few years to comply with the regulation by operating with conventional heavy fuel oil. 
Undoubtedly, the use of fuel blends (and e-/bio-fuels) in the future will also cause an extraordinary 
rise in costs. Compensation in the form of incentives to use such fuels, together with the avoidance 
of competitive disadvantages for European shipowners and shipping companies and creating a level-
playing-field, are needed and should be developed. 
 
Also not in the shipowners’ direct responsibility is the use of Onshore Power Supply (OPS). Here, the 
ports need to enable and to improve their infrastructure. We doubt that there will be a full EU-wide 
supply available until 2030/35. In case the infrastructure quality has reached a specific level 
shipowners can retrofit the ships but here again the return on investment has to be considered. As 
long as there are only very few OPS infrastructures available in European ports, penalties seem to be 
excessive and have to be linked to the available OPS infrastructure. 
 
We welcome the definition of the shipping company under this regulation. Other than described in 
the recently published ETS revision, the definition follows the “polluter pays principle” in a correct 
and expedient way. In case an optimal and consistent definition is to be used, we refer to the 
company definition used in the European Parliament’s MRV revision report of 2020.  
 
Intention of the presented European initiative 
 
The European Commission intends by this initiative (“FuelEU Maritime”) to encourage the use of low-
carbon fuels – CO2 reduced or climate neutral – and the reduction of CO2 emissions from shipping. 
The share of renewable and low-carbon fuels used in maritime transport shall be increased. 
Therefore, the regulation lays down the limit on the greenhouse gas intensity of energy used on-
board ships in EU ports and also to oblige ships to use onshore power supply or any other expedient 
“zero-emission” technology when in a EU port. The regulation in general targets energy used on 
voyages. Different low-carbon fuels allowed under the regulation are defined and shall be further 
promoted. 
 
Fuels used in maritime transport shall reduce the CO2 reference value successive from -2 per cent in 
2025, -26 per cent in 2040 to -75 per cent until 2050, compared to 2020. For the aimed greenhouse 
gas intensity, the Commission provides a methology. 
 
This means in practice, that also from January 2030 on, ships in EU ports shall use on-shore power 
supply. This shall in a first step be valid for passenger and container ships at berth for a period longer 
than two hours. There will be no obligation to connect to energy supply only in case infrastructure is 
not available. From 2035 on, these exceptions shall not be valid anymore – only five times a year such 
exceptions are possible. 
 
Ships shall be monitored regarding energy used during operations at sea and at berth. Data shall be 
reported to responsible authorities according to MRV regulation. A monitoring plan shall be 
submitted until 31 August 2024 by the shipping companies for each of their ships to the verifiers 
according to this regulation. 
 
On the fuel suppliers side, biofuels, renewable liquid and gaseous transport fuels on non-biological 
origin and recycled carbon fuels shall be certified regarding their respective greenhouse gas emission 
intensity. Every year following on the monitoring date, by 30 June, the verifier shall issue certifications 
of compliance (“FuelEU certificate”) for the ship. Both Commission and flag state shall be accordingly

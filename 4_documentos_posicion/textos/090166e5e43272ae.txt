Europe’s non-ferrous metals industries’ response to the Open Public consultation  
 
We welcome the opportunity to provide feedback on the Open Public consultation on the Impact Assessment of 
the revision of the lists of pollutants affecting surface and grou nd waters and the corresponding regulatory 
standards in the Environmental Quality Standards  (EQS), Groundwater and Water Framework Directives  (WFD). 
We would like to share some specific comments to some of the questions of the public consultation  as well as 
more generic considerations. 
General comments 
We filled this questionnaire as the Non -Ferrous Metal industry and answered the questions targeting our sector. We are 
also concerned about other substances being present in the environment, therefore, regarding where we answered 'no 
opinion' means that these questions do not fall into our area of expertise as Eurometaux. The Non-Ferrous Metals industry 
is committed to continually improving its production , use and recycling performances to prevent, control, reduce and, as 
far as possible, eliminate its pollution , to protect human health and the environment. We support the process regulated 
under the Water Framework Directive, Environmental Quality Standards Directive and Groundwater Directive. As a sector, 
we are actively engaged at all levels, political, and technical to improve surface and groundwater quality with improved 
science and better monitoring.  Below are our main recommendations regarding the current impact assessment of the 
review of the Priority Substances (PS) list:  
Key recommendations: Environmental policies need to 
 
• Be proportionate, based on scientific and factual evidence, and efficiently implemented to support the EU 
Industrial Policy and global competitiveness. 
• Integrate the consideration of local environmental conditions, bioavailability, and natural backgrounds when 
identifying Priority Substances and defining and implementing EQS values.  
• Apply robust criteria to utilise high-quality data and the most up-to-date scientific guidance and knowledge when 
assessing the risks posed by metals in water. 
• Improve data collection, better understand and assess emissions (point and diffuse) and identify the main 
contributing sources to pollution. 
• Allow in-depth exchange between experts before taking decisions.

Comments regarding specific questions  
Question in the Open Public consultation  Eurometaux’s comment 
Question 2 & 3 : How concerned are you about the 
presence of these substances in European surface / 
groundwater bodies? Please rate your concerns on a 
scale of 1 (not at all) to 5 (very much) 
 
- Regarding (Heavy) metals (e.g. mercury, zinc, 
silver). 
The reason why we have rated 3 on this question is the 
following:  
The STE score is the agreed methodology developed by 
JRC and various stakeholders for the monitoring -based 
prioritization, which exploits the inherent variability of 
measurements in the monitoring dataset. It evaluates the 
Spatial, Temporal and Extent of P NEC exceedances 
(STE) to rank and classify the substances for the risk they 
pose to European surface waters  (see Carvalho et al. 
2016 1). In this exercise, zinc ranked as 'low risk'. 
Furthermore, in the latest draft EQS dossier for silver2, 
JRC has concluded that based on the available monitoring 
data, the revised STE score for silver is 1.1, which is also 
considered as "low risk" ( so both Zn and Ag = low 
concern). The most common substance to be attributed a 
poor chemical status in most Member States is mercury 
(high concern; EEA 2018 3). Hence grouping zinc, silver 
with mercury in the questionnaire makes answering 
questions 2 and 3 very difficult, especially since zinc is an 
essential element for life. 
 
In addition, we wanted to share with you a recent paper 
published by Eurometaux on the (mis)use of the term 
"Heavy metals". Probably used to refer to substances that 
could endanger human health and the  environment, 
suggesting that heavy metals are a group of substances 
with some bad features in common. But this is not correct, 
the only thing that "heavy metals" have in common is the 
molecular weight above a certain threshold, and this has 
nothing to do with toxicity or, as written in the question 
"concerns", whatsoever4.

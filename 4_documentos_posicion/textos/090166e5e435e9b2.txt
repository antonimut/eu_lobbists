___________________________________________________________________________	FuelEU	Maritime	Regulation	and	inclusion	of	shipping	in	the	EU	ETS	Directive	–	IACS	Position		___________________________________________________________________________		IACS	recognizes	the	EC’s	‘Fit	for	55’	Package,	implementing	the	objectives	laid	down	in	the	European	Green	Deal	as	an	important	contribution	to	addressing	the	challenge	of	climate	change.	The	great	effort	of	various	European	Commission	Directorate-Generals	and	their	staff	in	compiling	this	‘basket	of	measures’	is	sincerely	acknowledged.	The	idea	of	proposing	a	package	of	EU	measures	-	to	complement	each	other	-	on	the	way	towards	net	zero	emission	shipping	is	laudable	although	IACS	retains	the	view	that	full	decarbonsiation	of	the	global	shipping	industry	can	only	be	achieved	via	IMO	as	the	appropriate	and	technically	competent	body	to	address	GHG	reduction	measures	globally.		IACS	wishes	to	add	to	this	debate	some	aspects	that	we	believe	should	be	taken	into	account	while	fine-tuning	the	proposed	measures.	We	are	eager	to	discuss	with	EU	regulators	in	more	detail	during	separate	meetings	along	the	legislative	process	whenever	appropriate.			In	particular,	IACS	wishes	to	add	some	additional	information	and	considerations	on	the	maritime	dimension	of	the	package,	namely	the	newly	proposed	FuelEU	Maritime	Regulation	and	the	inclusion	of	shipping	in	the	EU	ETS	Directive.			In	principle,	we	wish	to	remind	that		• all	elements	relating	to	maritime	transport	are	closely	interlinked,	and	that	decarbonisation	has	to	be	embedded	into	a	solid	safety	concept.		• the	IMO	is	the	appropriate	and	technically	competent	body	to	address	GHG	reduction	measures	globally;		as	such,	safety	measures	which	may	be	identified	during	this	process	should	also	be	raised	with	the	IMO	so	that	relevant	global	conventions	can	be	updated	accordingly.	IACS	and	its	Members	stand	ready	to	support	with	this	important	transition,	to	make	it	happen	quickly,	efficiently	and	safely.			 ************

About	IACS		By	way	of	introduction,	this	is	to	give	you	a	brief	overview	of	what	IACS	(International	Association	of	Classification	Societies)	stands	for.			Dedicated	to	safe	ships	and	clean	seas,	IACS	makes	a	unique	contribution	to	maritime	safety	and	regulation	through	technical	support,	compliance	verification	and	research	and	development.	More	than	90%	of	the	world's	cargo	carrying	tonnage	is	covered	by	the	classification	design,	construction	and	through-life	compliance	rules	and	standards	set	by	the	twelve	Member	Societies	of	IACS.	IACS	is	a	not	for	profit	membership	organisation	of	classification	societies	that	establish	minimum	technical	standards	and	requirements	that	address	maritime	safety	and	environmental	protection	and	ensures	their	consistent	application.		It	carries	out	this	responsibility	through	its	panels,	expert	groups	and	project	teams	and	provides	a	Quality	System	Certification	Scheme	(QSCS)	that	its	Members	comply	with,	as	an	assurance	of	professional	integrity	and	maintenance	of	high	professional	standards.		IACS	is	recognized	as	the	principal	technical	advisor	of	IMO.		Classification	societies		The	following	provides	a	brief	insight	into	the	role	of	classification	societies,	which	is	essential	for	the	shipping	cycle.			The	purpose	of	a	Classification	Society	("CS")	is	to	provide	classification,	statutory	certification	and	services	as	a	Recognised	Organisation	acting	on	behalf	of	a	flag	Administration,	and	assistance	to	the	maritime	industry	and	regulatory	bodies	as	regards	maritime	safety	and	pollution	prevention,	based	on	the	accumulation	of	maritime	knowledge	and	technology.		Classification	societies	(in	their	role	as	Reconigsed	Organisations	which	they	perform	when	acting	on	behalf	of	a	Flag	State)	are	subject	to	strict	and	efficient	regulation	at	the	EU	and	international	levels:		The	operation	of	classification	societies	recognized	by	the	EU	is	governed	by	Regulation		Regulation	(EC)	No	391/2009	of	the	European	Parliament	and	of	the	Council	of	23	April	2009	on	common	rules	and	standards	for	ship	inspection	and	survey	organisations	and		Directive	2009/15/EC	of	the	European	Parliament	and	of	the	Council	of	23	April	2009	on	common	rules	and	standards	for	ship	inspection	and	survey	organisations	and	for	the	relevant	activities	of	maritime	administrations.		Moreover,	the	international	shipping	regulator,	IMO,	has	adopted	the	Code	for	Recognized	Organizations	(RO	Code)	by	resolutions	MEPC.237(65)	and	MSC.349(92),	which	entered	into	force	on	1	January	2015	under	MARPOL	annexes	I	and	II,	SOLAS	1974	and	the	1988	Load	Line	Protocol.	The	Code,	parts	1	and	2	of	which	are	mandatory,	while	part	3	is	recommendatory,	provides	a	consolidated	instrument	containing	criteria	against	which	ROs	are	assessed	and	authorized/recognized,	and	gives	guidance	for	subsequent	monitoring	of	ROs	by	Administrations.		
   
    
The	role	of	classification	societies	under	the	EU	MRV	Regulation		Classification	societies	play	a	key	role	in	the	implementation	of	EU	Regulation	2015/757	(as	amended	by	Delegated	Regulation	2016/2071),	acting	as	independent	verifiers.	This	important	work	is	largely	entrusted	to	IACS’	members,	provided	their	strong	and	long-lasting	shipping	knowledge	which	proves	to	be	beneficial	for	the	implementation	of	legislation	related	to	maritime	transport.	Given	the	existing	experience	of	IACS	Members	with	this	type	of	work,	a	role	under	the	newly	proposed	FuelEU	Maritime	Regulation	and	the	EU	ETS	Directive	seems	natural.			In	parallel	to	the	new	proposals,	IACS	closely	follows	the	ongoing	revision	of	the	EU	MRV	Regulation	(2015/757).		We	note	that	EU	legislators	are	applying	a	relatively	wide	scope	for	the	accreditation	of	independent	verifiers;	accreditation	pursuant	to	Regulation	(EC)	No	765/2008	setting	out	the	requirements	for	accreditation	and	market	surveillance	relating	to	the	marketing	of	products.	Moreover,	this	scope	is	different	from	the	one	chosen	under	the	IMO	DCS,	with	the	latter	one	being	stricter	and	more	maritime-focused.			As	we	understand	that	there	is	an	intention	to	apply	this	scope	for	accreditation	also	to	the	new	legislations	under	the	‘Fit	for	55’	package,	IACS	wishes	to	re-invite	EU	legislators	to	carefully	evaluate	this	accreditation	principle	with	a	view	to	its	robustness	for	the	shipping	sector	and	a	possible	alignment	with	the	IMO	system	being	the	more	rigorous	one.			The	European	Union	is	in	the	process	of	putting	in	place	essential	regulations	for	the	future	of	shipping	and	the	guarantee	of	a	level	playing	field	between	its	players.	It	is	difficult	for	IACS	to	understand	why	it	chooses	at	this	crucial	moment	to	keep	opening	the	verification	activities,	whereas	it	has	in	regulation	391/2009	on	authorized	classification	societies	an	instrument	allowing	it	to	ensure	that	there	will	be	no	room	for	approximation	in	the	complex	operations	of	verification	and	certification	to	come.		Maritime	issues	present	a	technicality	without	comparison	with	most	of	the	activities	falling	within	the	scope	of	the	EU	ETS.	In	addition,	control	and	certification	activities	will	have	to	be	extended	to	actors	outside	the	European	Union,	which	will	further	increase	the	difficulty	in	appraisal	of	the	consistency	of	the	information	provided.		The	same	difficulty	in	carrying	out	controls	on	extra-EU	information	is	found	in	the	FuelEU	Maritime.	Regarding	this	proposal,	the	fact	that	the	verifier	calculates	the	base	of	the	fine	that	will	be	imposed	on	shipowners	who	do	not	comply	with	their	obligations,	pleads	for	the	search	for	maximum	guarantees	from	them.		Unfortunately,	this	is	not	the	path	that	has	been	chosen	so	far.		IACS	calls	for	the	work	to	revise	the	MRV	Regulation,	carried	out	in	parallel	with	the	work	on	the	inclusion	of	shipping	in	the	EU	ETS,	be	an	opportunity	to	tighten	the	accreditation	conditions	of	verifiers	by	an	explicit	reference	to	Reg.	391/2009.			
   
    
The	highly	technical	nature	of	the	maritime	world	calls	for	no	longer	reference	to	generalist	texts.			Ensuring	the	safety	of	alternative	fuels	and	propulsion	technologies			As	an	Association	whose	primary	objective	is	the	safety	of	maritime	transport,	IACS	is	obliged	to	emphasise	that	all	new	technologies	and	fuels	need	to	be	demonstrably	safe	before	being	introduced	into	commercial	shipping.	IACS’	and	its	Members’	approach	to	the	European	Green	Deal	and	its	proposed	measures	therefore	start	from	the	need	to	fulfill	these	safety	prerequisites.	To	this	end,	IACS	has	created	an	“Expert	Group	on	safety	aspects	of	new	technologies	and	zero/ultra-low	carbon	fuels	(EG/SAFTech)”.	IACS	is	willing	to	contribute	to	the	development	of	separate	guidelines	on	the	safety	of	ships	using	hydrogen	and	ammonia	as	fuel.			Furthermore,	IACS	Safety	Panel	has	been	entrusted	to	prepare	a	paper	on	the	safety	aspects	related	to	the	introduction	of	the	(most	popular)	alternative	fuels,	to	support	and	advise	regulators	on	safety	aspects	of	introducing	new	technologies	and	fuels	to	maritime	transport.	This	position	is	under	development	and	will	be	shared	with	regulators	once	finalized.			Moreover,	a	workshop	on	the	matter	of	alternative	fuels	for	shipping	had	been	organized	at	the	3rd	European	Shipping	Week	in	February	2020,	involving	EU	regulators	(DG	CLIMA)	and	industry.		Also,	IACS	has	recalled	towards	MEPC	76	that	the	safety	aspects	on	the	way	towards	zero	emission	ships	have	to	be	taken	into	account.	IACS	supported	the	proposal	in	document	MEPC	76/7/2	(Norway)	to	identify	regulatory	gaps	regarding	safety	of	alternative	fuels.	The	Committee	noted	IACS	comment,	which	was	supported	by	a	number	of	observers,	regarding	the	need	to	account	for	the	safety	aspects	of	future	measures,	and	IACS'	proposal	to	reflect	the	assessment	of	the	impacts	of	future	measures	on	safety	of	ships	in	Phase	II	of	the	above	Work	Plan	and	determine	a	mechanism	to	bring	the	action	to	the	attention	of	MSC.		IACS	also	informed	the	Committee	that	it	has	started	work	on	the	safety	and	environmental	aspects	associated	with	new	energy	efficient	technologies	and	alternative	fuels,	and	it	plans	to	update	relevant	Committees	of	its	progress	in	due	course.				Specific	aspects	of	the	proposed	FuelEU	Maritime	Regulation	–	for	consideration	and	clarification		While	IACS	and	its	Members	have	no	major	concerns	regarding	the	proposed	text,	some	points	would	merit	closer	consideration	in	our	view	in	order	to	enable	a	smooth	implementation	of	the	legislation.			1. Convergence	of	systems:	IACS	is	of	the	view	that	the	existing	EU	MRV	system	should	form	the	basis	for	proposed	legislation	comprised	in	the	‘Fit	for	55’	package	affecting	maritime	transport	(i.e.	FuelEU	Maritime	Regulation	and	inclusion	of	shipping	in	the	
   
    
EU	ETS).	The	convergence	of	the	systems	should	be	made	legally	clear	and	binding,	so	as	to	avoid	any	proliferation	of	systems	entailing	multiplied	administrative	burden,	lack	of	clarity	and	lack	of	comparability	of	data	for	the	various	entities	involved.		2. With	regard	to	the	responsibilities	of	the	verifier,	further	clarification	and	guidance	is	sought	on	the	following	aspects:	a. It	will,	according	to	our	understanding,	be	up	to	the	verifier	to	assess	the	content	of	the	BDN	(Articles	9	and	10);		b. Provisions	of	Article	18	foresee	flexibility	for	the	shipowner	to	pool	several	vessels	if	they	are	monitored	by	the	same	verifier;		c. In	the	event	of	a	failure	of	compliance,	penalties	are	to	be	calculated	by	independent	verifiers.	Clarification	is	sought	on	the	formula	specified	in	Annex	V;	ideally	regulators	can	provide	a	model	calculation	as	an	example	to	follow.				In	relation	to	the	above,	inconsistencies	with	other	legislation	from	the	package	should	be	avoided,	such	as	between	FuelEU	Maritime	and	the	Renewable	Energy	Directive	(2018/2001).			We	understand	that	the	currently	discussed	text	represents	a	framework	for	the	establishment	of	a	European	‘fuel	standard’.	It	would	be	helpful	if	you	could	clarify	how	enforcement	of	the	details	of	the	text	will	be	supported.	Will,	for	instance,	the	ESSF	(European	Sustainable	Shipping	Forum)	be	involved	in	the	development	of	Delegated/Implementing	Acts	(see	Recitals	40/41)	and/or	guidelines,	as	for	the	EU	MRV	Regulation	in	the	past?	IACS,	being	a	member	of	the	ESSF	and	its	sub-groups,	stands	ready	to	support	with	this	process,	as	done	under	the	MRV	Regulation.

Finnish Shipowners’ Association position on the FuelEU Maritime initiative 
 
The Finnish Shipowners’ Association (FSA) welcomes the European Commission’s Fit for 55 package and 
supports its ambition to deliver the European Green Deal . The FSA recognizes the importance of reducing 
emissions in the transport sector and supports the European Commission’s Sustainable and Smart Mobility 
Strategy in its stated purpose of shifting activity towards more sustainable transport modes such as shipping. 
The FSA is concerned, however, about whether the availability of sustainable maritime fuels will 
develop at a pace that enables reaching the ambitious targets set out in the FuelEU Maritime 
Regulation. The uncertainty of the availability of these fuels creates a risk of raising their price to  
levels that are economically unfeasible . In this context, the  FSA calls on policymakers to take into due  
consideration the following recommendations for the FuelEU Maritime initiative. 
 
Responsibility for delivering emission reductions (Article 4) 
For the GHG intensity reduction targets within the FuelEU Maritime to be reached, it is of paramount 
importance to ensure the sufficient availability of sustainable maritime fuels. As with other modes of transport, 
the transition to using sustainable fuels should be underpinned by a legal obligation  set on fuel 
suppliers to strengthen the Regulation’s enforcement and to give investors a clear long -term signal. 
Supportive incentivizing measures, such as an increase of the multiplier (Article 27) within the Renewable 
Energy Directive (RED II) should be considered. 
 
Financial support during the transition period 
Scaling up the production capacity of sustainable maritime fuels within the EU is a lengthy process and the 
outlook for their availability and price levels remains uncertain.  Revenues generated from the extension 
of the EU ETS to cover maritime transport should be used to provide financial support to the maritime 
sector to diminish the price gap between sustainable fuels and their fossil -based counterparts through an 
appropriate mechanism, such as Carbon Contracts for Difference.  
 
Requirements for energy used at berth (Article 5) 
To make it possible to comply with the proposed requirements for energy use at berth, sufficient availability 
of zero-emission energy should be ensured by upgrading the infrastructure of all ports within the 
EU’s TEN-T core and comprehensive networks as part of the ongoing revision of the TEN-T Regulation. 
 
Vessels within the scope of the regulation (Article 2) 
The proposed gross tonnage limit of 5  000 should be re-evaluated and decreased, as it risks shifting 
activity to only slightly smaller vessels and thereby creating two parallel shipping markets that operate with 
different rules and lead to sub-optimal performance in emissions reduction. 
 
Emission factor of biofuels from food and feed crops (Article 9) 
To alleviate the concerns of insufficient availability and untenable price levels of sustainable maritime fuels, 
all sustainable solutions should be considered for contributing to the sector’s decarbonization. Biofuels from 
food and feed crops should be treated equally between all transport sectors instead of artificially raising 
their emission factor for the maritime sector. 
* * * 
The Finnish Shipowners’ Association has 24 member companies from different shipping sectors with a total turnover of 
2,3 billion euros. The member companies operate more than 180 vessels, of which 111 under a Finnish flag. Around 10 
000 employees are working on board the members’ vessels. The Finnish shipowners are fully committed to cutting 
emissions and are actively supporting the Finnish maritime cluster in seeking and creating solutions that would enable 
the transition to zero-carbon maritime transport.

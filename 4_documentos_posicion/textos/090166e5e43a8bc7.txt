CO2 emissions from shipping – Fuel EU Maritime 
1. VDB as representative of the biofuel producers in Germany 
The Association of the German Biofuel Industry (Verband der Deutschen Bio-
kraftstoffindustrie e. V. - VDB) represents the interests of 15 biofuel producers 
in Germany with a production capacity of 2.3 million tons of biodiesel and 900 
GWh of biomethane. This equals approximately 60% of the total biodiesel ca-
pacity in Germany in the transport sector.  
 
2. General remarks 
VDB welcomes the European Green Deal and the Climate Law to set up an ambi-
tious climate 55% GHG reduction for 2030 compared to 1990. As road transport 
will have to contribute substantially to the achievement of the target, it is nec-
essary to set the regulatory framework for the use of climate-friendly energy 
sources. Biofuels, electromobility and enhanced energy efficiency together 
make climate protection in transport possible. 
Regarding Germany and its transport sector, emissions are about as high as in 
1990, while they have fallen significantly in all other sectors of German econo-
my. Biofuels such as biodiesel, bioethanol and biomethane have saved around 
9.7 million tonnes of greenhouse gases in 2019 in Germany. On these grounds it 
is evident that the greenhouse gas balance of the transport sector would be 
even more devastating if biofuels did not make a substantial contribution to 
climate protection. Compared to fossil fuels, biofuels reduce greenhouse gases 
by up to 90 percent. The entire production process is considered here, starting 
with the emissions from the agricultural cultivation to the transport and the 
industrial processing of the raw materials.  
Given the backlog of the transport sector concerning GHG abatement as a whole 
and the limited effect of measures taken until now, it seems appropriate to pay 
special attention to the supply of climate-friendly fuels in shipping where no 
binding GHG targets have been enforced so far.

3. Remarks on the initiative 
Addressing climate protection in the field of maritime shipping is a crucial initiative of the European 
Commission. So far, this mode of transport has contributed virtually nothing to decarbonising Eu-
rope. This is mainly due to a lack of regulation. In order to reduce GHG emissions in maritime ship-
ping effectively, a binding mandate is needed.  
The mandate could either regulate the share of renewable energy used or the GHG reduction 
achieved by the fuel. Any multiple counting that favours the use of renewable fuels in maritime ship-
ping over road transport has to be avoided. It would only shift efforts from road transport to mari-
time shipping which does not make sense at all since no additional GHG savings would be achieved 
and established value chains as well as transportation routes of the fuels would be interrupted.  
As road transport will have to generate much higher emission reductions in the upcoming years and 
as the energy consumed in this part of the sector is huge compared to shipping and aviation, the 
biofuels already introduced on the market for road transport fuels should continue to fulfil their cli-
mate protection contribution there. This also makes the strict climate targets at national and EU level 
far more likely to be achieved. Decarbonisation of road transport is key while electromobility alone is 
not sufficient but needs to be complemented by renewable fuels to address the existing vehicle fleet. 
All renewable maritime fuels that are offset against a binding mandate must be produced sustainably 
according to the sustainability criteria of the RED II - PtL based on renewable electricity as well. 
Please note that ISO 8217 already allows to blend up to 7 % FAME (fatty acid methyl ester) into mari-
time fuels. It is therefore appropriate to allow FAME as relevant option for a mandate, especially as it 
can be used in existing ships and infrastructure. 
This should be complemented by PtL and renewable LNG like Bio -LNG. Their use can be expanded 
successively, notably by investing in production capacity, new ships, and infrastructure if necessary.

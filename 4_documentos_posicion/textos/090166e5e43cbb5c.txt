 
Position on the proposed regulation on the use of 
renewable and low -carbon fuels in maritime transport - 
FuelEU Maritime 
 
The "FuelEU Maritime" initiative is an excellent opportunity to promote the use of 
alternative fuels, thus allowing to help the decarbonization of maritime transport 
in line with the objectives of the International Maritime Organization (IMO) and 
the EU. 
 
Enagas considers the following aspects to be key: 
 
 
 Competitiveness of European companies and ports, to avoid the 
relocation of commercial traffic to non-EU ports and to less efficient 
means of transport. 
 
All new maritime legislation must take into account the decisions taken by the 
IMO, since maritime transport is linked to the international sphere. The European 
Union must monitor the possible relocation of emissions; European ports could be 
affected by a loss of traffic to non -European ports. In addition to th is loss of 
market, another negative effect could also occur, such as a shift to less efficient 
modes of transport for goods to enter Europe and avoid regulation on arrival in 
port. 
 
 
 Technological neutrality for an effective decarbonisation, where 
natural gas and biogas represent significant savings in emissions 
reduction. 
The approach to be followed in  this initiative should allow for the neutral 
development of different technologies; in the short term, it would make sense to 
develop those that are already available today for direct use and thus begin the 
effective decarbonisation of shipping now, for instance progressively shifting to 
LNG. Furthermore, gas infrastructure and propulsion technologies allows the direct 
use of bioLNG, and progressively the penetration of synthetic gases. 
It is important to analyze the Technology Readiness Level (TRL) of on board 
system for future fuels.

In addition to technology readiness, it is necessary to perform a Well -to-Wake 
(WtW) emissions analysis of the different options. The evaluation of the different 
available technologies must be done from an abatement cost approach, as well as 
the total cost of ownership (TCO) so that in the short term it is possible to choose 
those energy sources that represent the most effective option  by of today, such 
as natural gas and biomethane. In the long term, the door should be open for 
currently less mature technologies such as ammonia, methanol or H2, that would 
naturally emerge as alternatives on the future. 
 
Specifically for the transoceanic transport sector (which is responsible for 85% of 
the emissions of this sector) the TCOs comparison attributes to the use of natural 
gas a saving of around 95% in emission reduction costs, compared to the same 
reduction achieved using other less mature alternatives such as methanol, 
ammonia or hydrogen. In the case of using BioGNL, these emission reduction costs 
are around 88%. 
 
 Interdependence and consistency with Directive 2014/94/EU 
(AFID), as well as with the new regulation proposal on Alternative 
Fuel Infrastructure (AFIR),  to enable the completion of ongoing 
infrastructure developments. 
 
The current demand for alternative fuels is not yet as expected, since the 
necessary infrastructure, which stems from Directive 2014/94/EU, is in the 
development phase and should to be completed in the coming years. From Enagás 
we consider necessary that the new regulation proposal (AFIR) gives continuity to 
these investments, so to allow their consolidation in the coming years as assets 
for decarbonisation. In Spain, through public -private initiatives and European 
funds (CEF), LNG supply infrastructures are being developed for the maritime 
sector, through the institutional strategy LNGHIVE2. These regulatory initiatives 
must take into account the continuity of these projects in order to meet the 
decarbonization objectives in the short and medium term, while preparing the field 

 
for the future. Indeed, investments in gas infrastructure are fully compatible for 
the transport of renewable gases and, to some extent, also for H2 and ammonia. 
 
   
 Achieve zero port emissions targets through the use of biogas and 
alternative fuels. 
 
To achieve decarbonis ation targets in port, it will be necessary to favour the 
reduction of the use of conventional fuels and improve the efficiency of cleaner 
alternatives such as LNG or biogas. The latter is well positioned as an immediate 
zero-emission solution for the production of electricity during the ship's stay in 
port, and is also adaptable to changing demand on berth, as opposed to direct 
connection to the electricity grid. 
In addition to biogas, renewable hydrogen can also be supplied to port in varying 
degrees of blending. In the longer term, the supply of synthetic methane (e -
methane) through the gas infrastructure is also possible. 
 
 
 LNG as a mature and effective solution for fleet decarbonisation. 
 
By investing now in LNG -fuelled ships, shipowners can realize immediate 
greenhouse gas emission benefits, estimated at up to 21% (WtW approach), 
according to the CE Delft study1. In addition, the European Maritime Safety Agency 
(EMSA) identified LNG as the cleanest option for shipping today, capable of 
significantly reducing emissions in the sector. 
Furthermore, LNG has a low risk of creating a lock -in effect. In the long term, 
LNG-based assets, with little or no modification, can use non-fossil methane, such 
as liquefied biomethane (LBM) and liquefied synthetic methane (LSM), initially as 
substitute fuels. LNG could also be complemented by other alternatives such as 
hydrogen, methanol and ammonia. The value of LNG as a transition fuel has been 
recognized in a recent joint report EMSA and the EEA2; it also highlights that LBM 
and LSM can be easily blended with LNG, with the advantage that these 
alternatives can profit from the technology and infrastructure already available; 
its implementation is then progressive and do not disrupt port and ship operations.

 Consistency with Directive 2018/2001 (REDII) in the calculation of 
greenhouse gas emission intensity. 
 
The current formulation of the regulation proposes an emissions calculation 
methodology that is hardly compatible with a certification system for renewable 
and low-carbon fuels based on Guarantees of Origin, such as proposed in 
Directive 2018/2001.  
This certification system, which has yet to be clearly defined through 
implementing acts, will allow making optimal use of the existing gas 
infrastructure to transport renewable gases such as biogas, synthetic methane 
and hydrogen, at different blending levels. However, such a system fits poorly 
with the WtT and TtW concepts, because it assumes that a sustainable fuel has 
emissions even if they are neutral or negative. Enagás proposes a modification 
of the text of Article 4 or Annex I, so that ship operators can make use of 
certification and guarantees of origin to prove the sustainability of their fleets.

Eurogas welcomes the EC’s ambition to reduce the GHG footprint of ships  and its decision to ensure a level playing 
field between decarbonisation technologies by taking into account  the lifecycle emissions  of fuels . Eurogas also 
welcomes the recognition of LNG, bio and e-LNG as effective decarbonisation solutions. 
Recommendations 
• The EC should leave the door open for future revision of the default values to account for 
scientifical/market development. 
• Using actual values instead of default values should also be possible for all fuels: it encourages and 
rewards better performing ships and ultimately decarbonisation efforts. 
• The possibility to enable virtual blending should be considered to boost the demand for renewable 
and low carbon fuels, provided strong restrictions and safeguards should be put in place to ensure a 
rigorous carbon accounting and avoid any double counting. 
• Flexibility provisions and restrictions for individual and pooled ships should be identical. 
• The values taken as Global Warming Potential (GWP) should be consistent with RED II/III.  
• The categorisation of the energy converters should be clarified to ensure consistency.  
Default values 
The EC should leave the door open for future revision of the default values in the future to account for 
scientifical/market development. As demonstrated by the RED development , t he Regulation should ensure that 
potential modification of the default values is possible in the future, be it to account for scientifical/market development, 
or to simply amend a previously incorrect factor.  
The Regulation opens the door to the use of actual values to divert from using default values. It should be made clear 
that using actual values instead of default values should also be possible for all fuels: it encourages and 
rewards better performing ships and u ltimately decarbonisation efforts. This is recognised in the Regulation’s 
annexes: it should be consistent throughout the text.  
Recognition of virtual blending 
The possibility to enable virtual blending should be considered to boost the demand for renewable and low 
carbon fuels, provided strong restrictions and safeguards should be put in place to ensure a rigorous carbon 
accounting and avoid any double counting.

As noted in the recent Hydrogen Strategy, the European Commission should assess the possibility to enable the virtual 
blending of renewable, decarbonised and low carbon fuels. While the production of those fuels is ramping up across 
Europe, the necessary logistics may not be in place to allow for this physical consumption in ship . Replacing virtually 
volumes of fuels will not impact the emissions at local level: for example, replacing methane by biomethane does not 
lead to any changes in terms of TTW emissions. 
This mechanism should be interlinked with the provisions of the RED III to ensure  consistency, full compatibility and 
traceability of the fuels.  In order to offer that possibility, strong restrictions and safeguards should be put in place to 
ensure a rigorous carbon accounting and avoid any double counting. 
Equal flexibility provisions 
Flexibility provisions and restriction s for individual and pooled ships should be identical . The flexibility 
provisions of borrowing applicable for individual ships in Article 17 (2) should also be applicable to pooled ships with 
maintaining the same requirements and restrictions set by the Article 17(1) (a) and (b). 
Reference to Global Warming Potential (GWP) 
The values taken as Global Warming Potential (GWP) should be consistent with RED II /III. Exact values are 
missing from the Regulation proposal. As for the rest of the methodology, it is critical to ensure consistency with other 
(upcoming) EU policies, especially the Renewable Energy Directive (Directive 2018/2011 (EU)) considering the level 
of interlinkage with the proposed Regulation. To ensure a consistent approach, the GWP values should be the same 
in the two policies. 
Categorisation of energy converters classes  
The categorisation of the energy converters should be clarified to ensure consis tency. The proposed 
classification lacks details which could be detrimental for its future implementation.

Position of PGE Polska Grupa Energetyczna  S.A. on the revision of the Directive of 
the European Parliament and of the Council on the promotion of the use of energy 
from renewable sources 
 
Key messages 
 PGE Polska Grupa Energetyczna S.A. (hereinafter: “ PGE S.A.”) welcomes the revision of 
the Direct ive on the promotion of the use of energy from renewable sources as a 
prerequisite to further streamlining green energy in the pursuit to achieve climate 
neutrality by 2050. With a new Union’s target of at least 40% of renewable energy in gross 
final energy consumption by 2030, we believe that there is still room for improvements 
and enablers to reach it. In any case, in ou r view, district heating sector should be 
considered with a special attention.  By 2030, biomass potential will be limited due to 
new sustainability criteria while deploying heat pumps or concentrated solar power at 
scale has also constraints in large systems. 
 To facilitate the uptake of renewables in heating and cooling as well as in district 
heating and cooling, it is necessary to introduce a mechanism to calculate renewable 
electricity as renewable heat while ensuring that no double counting of renewable 
electricity takes place. In our view, neither effective directive nor draft proposal does not 
allow that. These changes would fit for a wider promotion of the electrification of heating 
and district heating. 
 The Commission proposed to increase the indicati ve annual growth of the share of 
renewable energy in the district heating and cooling to 2.1 pp. in the period 2021 -2025 
and 2026 -2030, without the possibility of choosing a different way for fulfilling this 
obligation. We support maintaining the indicative nature of the target, however taking 
into account the specificity of the operation of heating systems in Poland, in particular 
those based on natural gas cogeneration, we believe that the target should not be 
arbitrarily changed and its non -binding natur e should be maintained . Achi eving an 
increase other than 1 pp. annually in the period of 2021-2025 is not technically feasible.  
 Moreover, any potential changes of this target for the period 2026 -2030 should be 
decided not earlier than in 2025 provided t hat the possibility to count renewable 
electricity towards heating and district heating target is introduced. 
 Further restrictions of sustainability criteria, a cascading principle, and a proposal of 
granting no support to electricity -only installations us ing biomass are excessive.

Detailed position 
PGE S.A. considers the revision of the Directive of the European Parliament and of the Council 
on the promotion of the use of energy from renewable sources (hereinafter: " draft RED  
proposal")1 as an essential element in the continuation of the European climate and energy 
policy with a view to achieve climate neutrality by 2050. Setting the binding renewable energy 
share proposed by the Commission at a minimum level of 40%  at the European Union 
(hereinafter: “ EU”) level , is on the one hand an ambitious g oal, but at the same time an 
opportunity to rebuild the competitiveness of EU economies, crucial in the context of the 
economic consequences of the COVID-19 pandemic. Due to falling costs of energy production 
in e.g., wind and photovoltaic installations, the largest increase in the share of green energy 
is observed in the power sector. Yet, t echnological potential for renewable energy sources 
(hereinafter: “RES”) development in the heating and cooling sector is significantly lower than 
that observed in the electricity sector. This is a key aspect for countries like Poland, which, 
due to its conditions and a different starting point, faces much greater challenges related to 
increasing the share of RES, e.g., in district heating. 
1. Calculation of the share of energy from renewable sources (Art. 7) 
According to the d raft RED proposal, the Commission puts forward an updated method of 
calculating the share of energy from RES, consisting in including energy from the use of 
renewable fuels of non-biological origin in the sector in which the energy is consumed. 
In the opinion of PGE S.A. this amendment is beneficial a nd will improve the regulatory 
environment e.g., for hydrogen fuels produced using green energy, and we believe that this

2. Increasing the share of renewable energy in heating and cooling  and mainstreaming 
renewable energy in buildings (Art. 23 and 15a) 
The draft RED proposal, in the changes concerning Art. 23(1), introduces an amendment to 
the nature of the RES target for heating and cooling, from indicative to binding, with levels of 
annual increase of 1.1 pp. or 1.5 pp. for Member States using waste heat and cooling. The 
mandatory minimum RES share growth percentage is designed as an annual average for the 
periods of 2021-2025 and 2026-2030 based on the 2020 level expressed as a natio nal share 
in gross final energy consumption. Moreover, it is proposed that Member States would 
implement additional measures related to the increase of this share as included in the new 
Annex 1a, in a non-binding manner. 
Achieving an additional indicative growth rate of over 1.1 pp. for the years 2021 - 2025 will 
be significantly difficult due to changes in the rules and tightening of requirements during 
this already ongoing period, which will leave truly little time to achieve new targets after the 
entry i nto force of new provisions  and the deadline for its transposition into national 
legislation. According to PGE S.A., the increase of the annual growth rate proposed by the 
Commission in the settlement period of 2026 - 2030 will depend on the implementation of 
the abovementioned postulates formulated in relation to Art. 7. At the same time, it is worth

"draft EED proposal"), in particular in the proposed new definition of an efficient heating and 
cooling system.

4. Bioenergy and criteria for the sustainability and greenhouse gas emission savings of 
biomass fuels (Art. 3 and 29) 
The draft RED proposal anticipates significant limitations in the use of biomass for ener gy 
purposes. Through changes in A rt. 3(3), it is proposed to implement a hierarchy of biomass  
use through implementing a cascading principle , in which the use of biomass for energy 
purposes is in practice in the last place. Additionally, as of 31 December 2026, it would not be 
possible, with few exceptions, to grant public aid to electricity-only installations using biomass 
fuels. PGE S.A. supports the implementation of legislative solutions compliant with the EU 
principles of biodiversity and protection of environmentally valuable areas.  However, the 
limitations proposed by the Commission are too far-reaching. It should be pointed out that 
biomass is one of the key sources in the structure of energy generation from RES in the EU 
and with the current and established criteria for biomass fuels there are grounds for 
guaranteeing that biomass for energy purposes is obtained without causing a significant harm 
to the environment. The introduction of stricter requirements for biomass fuels creates the 
risk that the development of biomass use in the process of transforming district heating will 
be impeded, especially for  large district heating systems, for which it is one of the few RES

5. Power purchase agreements and facilitating system integration of renewable electricity 
(Art. 3, 19 and 20a) 
We welcome the Commission's proposal expressed in the new  paragraph 4a to Art. 3 that 
Member States should establish a framework, which may also include support schemes, to 
promote power purchase agreements (hereinafter: "PPAs"). At the same time, Member 
States will provide in their NECP s a list of measures and policies to promote this type of 
agreements, as well as an estimate of the volume of capacity covered by PPAs. In the opinion 
of PGE S.A. such contracts will ensure security of financing planned investments at the level 
allowing to obtain return on invested capital. Where economically justified, PPAs may 
constitute an alternative to RES support schemes, such as tenders, especially in view of 
decreasing costs of energy generation, e.g., in photovoltaic and onshore wind installations. In 
our view, revenues from PPAs should not be treated as state aid due to their entirely free-
market nature. At the same time, we perceive this amendment as not opposite to maintaining 
the possibility to grant RES support under market-based RES support schemes, e.g., contracts 
for difference, which for some RES technologies is still a necessary condition for financial 
security and maintaining bankability of projects. Depending on the design of the PPAs, these 
may be based on guarantees of origin of renewabl e electricity. In this context, it  seems 
necessary to clarify in A rt. 19, whether the revenues from the sale of guarantees of origin 
will be treated as state aid, which in that case  would affect the reduction of state aid 
granted e.g., in the form of a contract for difference.

6. Joint projects between Member States and offshore wind energy (Art. 9) 
In amendments to Art. 9 of the draft RED proposal, the Commission suggests that by the end 
of 2025, Member States should identify and agree to cooperate on at least one cross-border 
RES project. These can be implemented within the EU renewable energy financing mechanism 
with contributions of the relevant Member States. In additi on, provisions are introduced 
related to the establishment of common targets for the development of offshore wind energy 
in each sea basin by 2050, with milestones in 2030 and 2040.

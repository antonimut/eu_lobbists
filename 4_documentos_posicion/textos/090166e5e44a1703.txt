Position of PGE Polska Grupa Energetyczna S.A. on the draft Regulation on the 
deployment of alternative fuels infrastructure 
Key messages 
 PGE Polska Grupa Energetyczna S.A. (hereinafter: “ PGE S.A. ”) welcomes the draft 
Regulation of the European Parliament and of the Council on the deployment of 
alternative fuels infrastructure, and repealing Directive 2014/94/EU (hereinafter: 
"AFIR proposal") as an important element of the “Fit for 55” legislative package and 
one of the key milestones to achieve climate neutrality by 2050.  
 PGE S.A draws attention to the following key issues including the impact of the AFIR 
proposal on the PGE’s Capital Group d istribution company – PGE Dystrybucja S.A. 
(hereinafter: "DSO"). 
 First of all, t he need for greater electrification of the economy  where possible 
(stationary aircrafts, ports, public transport), as raised in the Art. 9-13 of the AFIR  
proposal, is the right direction for “hard to decarbonise” sectors. 
 However, the newly proposed obligations regarding  a minimum coverage of publicly 
accessible recharging points dedicated to light and heavy-duty vehicles on the road 
network translate i.a. directly into increased expectations on distribution system 
operators (DSOs) to connect recharging points , which will consequently affect 
distribution system in terms of additional investment cost and its functioning on the 
daily basis. Therefore, implementation of the tasks resulting from the proposed AFIR 
regulation should not result in excessive obligations for DSOs. 
 According to the Electricity Market Directive (hereinafter: " EMD"), p riority in 
developing the necessary recharging infrastructure for electric vehicles should be 
given to fully market -based players.  Taking into account the objectives for the 
expansion of the recharging network (publicly accessible recharging points) proposed 
in the AFIR proposal, a detailed and multi-variant analysis of the preparation of the 
electricity network for the implementation by DSOs of potential tasks resulting 
from the AFIR proposal is necessary. Unfortunately, such an analysis is not provided 
by the impact assessment of the AFIR proposal. 
 The Commission proposes  in the Art. 5 of the AFIR proposal greater freedom in the 
range of authentication methods and payment systems, which should create a more 
competitive market.

Detailed position  
With the Commission’s proposal for Regulation of the European Parliament and of the Council 
on the deployment of alternative fuels infrastructure1, the Directive 2014/94/EU2  is replaced 
by a regulation.  
First of all, the need for greater electrification of the economy where possible (stationary 
aircrafts, ports, public transport), as raised in the Art. 9 -13 of the AFIR proposal, is the right 
direction for “hard to decarbonise” sectors. 
The new AFIR proposal aims to ensure faster development of recharging infrastructure and 
the implementation of targets on the minimum deployment of recharging stations, including 
targets for the distance between rechargin g points (publicly accessible recharging points) 
across the whole Trans-European Transport Network (TEN-T). Implementing these aims with 
regulation means a significant acceleration of the entire process , as well as the limited 
possibility to adapt to the different starting points of Member States, including the speed of

However, the newly proposed obligations addressed to the Member States, translates directly

Recital 21 of the draft AFIR indicates that "The increasing number of electric vehicles in road, 
rail, maritime and other transport modes will require that recharging operations are 
optimised and managed in a way that does not cause congestion and takes full advantage of 
the availability of renewable electricity and low electricity prices in the system". In turn, recital 
22 of the draft AFIR mentions that "The development of infrastructure for electric vehicles, 
the interaction of that infrastructure with t he electricity system, and the rights and 
responsibilities assigned to the different actors in the electric mobility market, have to be 
consistent with the principles established under Directive (EU) 2019/944 ". Therefore, 
priority in the development of the necessary recharging infrastructure should be given to fully 
market-based actors. On the other hand, the role of DSOs in this process is important due to 
their participation in the process of connection and supply of electricity. 
 
1. Obligations concerning the development of recharging infrastructure (Art. 3 and 4) 
Member States will be required to ensure an adequate density of recharging network, 
power of recharging points and chargers, separately for light and heavy -duty vehicles 
(publicly accessible rech arging points) . In  the A rt. 3 of the AFIR proposal , the minimum 
power output of all publicly accessible recharging stations for light-duty vehicles is defined in 
relation to the number of battery electric vehicles (1 kW per electric car) and in relation t o 
plug-in hybrids registered in the Member State (0.66 kW per hybrid). 
In addition, in the Art. 3 and 4 of the AFIR proposal, obligations to extend recharging networks 
(publicly accessible recharging points) are also linked to the course of the TEN -T 
comprehensive and core networks, for which minimum capacity of rechargers and maximum 
distances (60  km for light -duty vehicles and 100 km  for heavy -duty vehicles ) between 
recharging pools located along the TEN -T network, for both light and heavy -duty vehicles, 
have been defined for the period until December 2025 and December 2030, respectively. 
Minimum standards for transport pools have also been proposed. The largest recharging 
stations are expected to reach a power output of 3.5 MW by 2035. Taking in to account the 
above figures, a detailed and probably multi -variant analysis of the preparation of the

2. Assessment of the provision of flexibility in the electricity system (Art. 14) 
Art. 14 of the AFIR proposal refers to assessing the potential of electric vehicles to contribute 
to the flexibility of the electricity system, including considering bi-directional electricity flows. 
According to this article, the national regulatory authority until 30 June 2024 (and every 3 
years thereafter) will assess how the deployment and operation of recharging points will 
contribute to their potential to increase the flexibility of the electricity sy stem as well as to 
improve the possibility of RES sources connection. The assessment will be made taking into 
account all recharging points, both public and private. Based on the results of such 
assessment, the regulator will take measures on the need to d eploy additional recharging 
points. This assessment and measures will be taken into account by DSOs in their network 
development plans. It is necessary for this assessment to be conducted in cooperation with 
DSOs, who are obliged to include flexibility ser vices in their network development plans, 
pursuant to Directive (EU) 2019/944, which would require a modification of Art. 14. 
In addition, Art. 14 indicates that the scope of the aforementioned periodical assessment shall 
cover the EVs participation in the  balancing market. In PGE's opinion, any solutions for EVs 
participation in electricity market shall be closely related to the DSO network flexibility model 
as the charging infrastructure for EVs is connected to DSO network. 
These assessment requirements will entail the need to define the processes for  obtaining 
and collecting the relevant data. Principal issues , which should be addressed at national 
level include  an issue of  how to determine the area -based connectivity and efficient 
operation of the syste m (together with  the impact of the deployment and number of 
recharging stations in terms of using EV capacity as a source of flexibility).  Moreover, how 
to assess the impact of the number and deployment of stations on additional RES 
connectivity should also be left to the Member States. 
 
3. Monitoring of progress (Art. 14 and 15, Annex I) 
Monitoring of progress (Art. 14 and  15, Annex I) will be based on the national policy 
framework (Art. 13) for the development of the market for alternative fuels in the transport 
sector and the deployment of the relevant infrastructure. In this process, the impact of the 
new regulation on the electricity distribution network should be considered.

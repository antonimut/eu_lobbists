Position of PGE Polska Grupa Energetyczna S.A. on the proposal for a recast of the 
Directive of the European Parliament and of the Council on energy efficiency 
Key messages 
 PGE Polska Grupa Energetyczna S.A. (hereinafter: “PGE S.A.”) welcomes the recast of the 
Directive 2012/27/EU of the European Parliament and of the Council on energy efficiency 
(hereinafter: “ EED”) as an opportunity for a reasonable adaptation of the current 
regulations to the Union’s climate neutrality objective by 2050. For that reason, the new 
EED provisions should prove further the leading role of Member States in establishing the 
agenda for energy efficiency measures and the need to recognize the role of low-carbon 
high-efficiency cogeneration in the district heating sector. It remains a key aspect for 
countries like Poland, facing the biggest challenges related to  coal phase-out in district 
heating systems and the need to increase the share of renewables in its energy mix. 
 New definition of an “efficient heating and cooling system” as proposed by the 
Commission will have a tremendous impact on district heating systems, especially those 
based on natural gas high-efficiency cogeneration. The Polish specificity pertains to the 
fact that urban agglomerations are covered by large -scale district heating systems with 
high production capacities  (i.e. hundreds of megawatts  of ordered thermal capacity ). 
Nearly 6 million households in Poland, out of a total of around 14 million, use this type of 
heating. High-efficiency cogeneration, mainly based on natural gas or biomass, is the most 
preferred technology due to Poland’s weather conditions, required temperature 
parameters and high capacities of district heating systems, which cannot be fully replaced 
with low-temperature renewables. 
 For existing effic ient district heating systems, an adequate transition period should be 
introduced for adapting to the new requirements in order to avoid that these systems 
suddenly lose their status. In this context, it is crucial to address the specificities of large 
heating systems since in most cases it will not be technically feasible to achieve the 
proposed shares of renewable energy and waste heat. At the same time, these systems 
should not be deprived of their efficient status. PGE S.A. proposes to  maintain the key 
role of the share of cogeneration and high efficient cogeneration and to make the 
milestones and the shares of renewable heat and waste heat more realistic. 
 The combined production of heat and electricity in high-efficiency cogeneration is pivotal 
for obtaining primary energy savings and high generation efficiency, which directly means 
meeting the overarching objectives of the d irective. PGE Capital Group  is currently 
carrying out investments related to the replacement of coal-fired heating units with high-

Detailed position 
PGE S.A. considers the proposal for a recast of the Directive of the European Parliament and 
of the Council on energy efficiency (hereinafter: " EED proposal") 1  as an opportunity to 
reasonably adapt the current EED directive to the new energy efficiency target aligned with 
the EU's efforts to achieve climate neutrality by 2050. New provisions should take into 
account the leading role of Member States in designing specific programmes to sup port 
energy efficiency improvements and recognise the role of heat and power generation from 
high-efficiency cogeneration in the district heating transition. This is a key aspect for countries 
such as Poland which, due to its different specificities and starting point, faces much greater 
challenges in increasing the share of renewable energy sources (hereinafter: "RES"), including 
in district heating. 
1. Energy efficiency targets (Article 4) 
According to the EED proposal, the new binding target at the EU level will be equivalent to a 
reduction in energy consumption of at least 9% by 2030, compared to the reduction effort 
under the 2020 Reference Scenario. Member States will define their individual contributions 
and trajectories for achieving the ta rgets in their National Energy and Climate Plans 
(hereinafter: "NECP"), in line with the new Annex I setting out the reference methodology for 
calculating required energy savings. 
In the opinion of PGE S.A., the amendments proposed by the Commission strive for setting 
of two energy consumption targets, one for final energy and one for primary energy. It is a 
significant change in relation to the current EED directive, where it is left to Member States

It should be pointed out that Member States may find the new level of ambition difficult to

2. New annual final energy savings obligation (Article 8, Annex V) 
The current target of achieving new final energy savings per year of 0.8% from the three-year 
average consumption in 2016-2018 would apply until the end of 2023. From 2024 to 2030, 
this target would be 1.5% of the 2017-2019 average. It is proposed that from 1 January 2024, 
it would not be possible to count towards the target energy savings in sectors covered by the 
greenhouse gas emissions trading scheme (hereinafter: "EU ETS") that would happen due to 
imposed carbon pricing mechanisms and not to count energy savings from the direct 
combustion of fossil fuels (as proposed in Annex V). 
Although PGE S.A. supports the amendments with respect to the extension of the list of 
entities obliged to deliver energy savings to include the transmission system operators of 
electricity and gas, as well as the retained possibility of establishing own primary energy factor 
(hereinafter: “PEF”) based on justified arguments presented to the Commission, we point to 
the significant increase in the obligation rate to achieve new annual final energy savings, 
which may hinder economic growth and is not a step in a good direction. Assuming that the 
higher rate of new annual energy savings will continue to appl y beyond 2030, it seems that 
the cheapest sources of energy savings will be rapidly exploited, as it will become increasingly 
difficult and expensive to achieve new annual savings over time because the efficiency of 
equipment, industrial processes and services will in any case continue to improve due to other 
EU provisions such as ecolabelling requirements.

We also point out that saved energy from fossil fuels has a double beneficial impact on climate 
objectives, on the one hand contributing to significant savings of energy consumed (both 
primary and final), and on the other hand reducing greenhouse gas emissions. The exclusion 
of energy savings based on  a direct combustion of  fossil fuels contradicts the EU's climate 
objectives, as the existing potential fo r primary energy savings will not be realised in the 
future, despite potentially lower costs associated with achieving the same primary energy 
savings than for energy from non-fossil fuels. Moreover, with this exclusion, there will be no 
incentive for consumers to reduce their consumption of fossil fuel -based energy, which will 
have an extremely negative impact on the pace of energy transition in Poland. 
With respect to the proposed restrictions on the eligible sources of energy savings, we also 
point out that there is a large potential for energy savings in Poland resulting from a reduction 
in the use of fossil fuels, resulting from, e.g., modernisation of generation units to high 
efficiency cogeneration units . In the opinion of PGE S.A., eliminating the po ssibility of 
classifying projects aimed at improving energy efficiency with the use of fossil fuels, in 
particular natural gas, contradicts the objectives of energy efficiency policy, including the use 
of primary energy saving potential. Moreover, in the c ase of Poland, natural gas is an 
indispensable link in the process of transformation of district heating sector, which should be 
taken into account. 
3. Energy management systems and energy audits (Article 11) 
In principle, we support the new approach regarding mandatory energy audits, i.e., moving 
away from qualifying entities based on solely the number of employees to a criterion 
related to energy consumption. However, we point out that the period of 4 years concerning 
the cyclicality of energy audits is too short  for the next audit to acknowledge significant 
changes in the company and the development of technology. It is crucial to maintain the non-
mandatory character of audit recommendations, as depending on individual conditions, they 
could involve disproportionately higher costs in relation to the energy savings achieved. 
We also call for the abandoning of a newly proposed obligation to implement an energy 
management system in the case of enterprises with an average annual energy consumption 
exceeding 100 TJ. In the opinion of PGE S.A., the obligation to conduct an energy audit in such 
a case is sufficient to realise the objectives of the directive. PGE S.A. recommends the 
reinstatement of an alternative way of fulfilling the obligation by implementing an energy 
management system or an environmental management system, which includes carrying out 
energy audits.

4. Additional criteria for efficient district heating and cooling systems (Article 24) 
The EED proposal introduces significant changes to the definition of an efficient  district 
heating and cooling system. Decisive for maintaining or achieving the status of the efficient 
district heating and cooling system will be the individual milestones related to meet ing 
specific RES and waste heat shares , set until 2050. In the light of the EED proposal, from  
1 January 2035 onwards, district heating systems could not be considered efficient solely 
based on the share of high-efficiency cogeneration. 
Urban agglomerations in Poland are heated and supplied with heat for domestic hot water 
needs by large district heating systems with high generation capacities. Almost 6 million 
households in Poland, out of a total of about 14 million, use this type of heating, which gives 
a share of about 40%, while Germany, although it has a similar number of connected 
households, has a connection rate almost three times lower. According to the data of the 
Ministry of Climate and Environment 4 and the Energy Regulatory Office 5, the total length of 
heating networks in Poland is 21  701 km, while 230 PJ of heat is delivered each year to end 
users. 
Moreover, cogeneration units are particularly important for reducing low emission and smog, 
the security of heat supply to consumers, as well as for the National Power System - currently 
around 16% of electricity supplied to that system comes from cogeneration units. The results

Without introducing amendments to the EED proposal, the vast majority of heat and 
electricity generation in Poland shall be deprived of its current high-efficiency status, resulting 
in the loss of the status of the efficient district heating and cooling system. At the same time, 
it is necessary to emphasize that a radical reduction in emissions, and thus increasing the 
share of RES heat, in particular in the period of 2025-2030, will not be technically possible for 
most district heating systems. 
In the case of large district heating systems located in Poland, the main technical barriers to 
the radical increase in the share of renewable s and waste heat assumed by the  Commission 
result from the following factors: 
 too little time for transformation towards lo wering the required temperature 
parameters in heating networks , which is a significant obstacle in the use of low -
temperature renewable heat sources; 
 lack of suitable locations for large -scale RES heat storage facilities and the lack of a 
sufficiently developed infrastructure of heating networks for the integration of RES 
to be used beyond the boundaries of urban agglomerations; 
 lack of adequate lower heat sources in the form of watercourses in the area of urban 
agglomerations; 
 lack of large industrial pl ants or data centres, which are most often far away from 
district heating networks allowing for deploying waste heat potential; 
 lack of detailed regulatory framework regarding power-to-heat projects and the need 
to significantly modernis e and expand the el ectricity infrastructure in order to 
increase the volume of RES electricity that could be used in these installations. 
The specific nature of larger district heating systems means that it will not always be 
technically possible to achieve the required shares of renewables and waste heat, due to the 
abovementioned network parameters and the lack of users’ readiness. At the same time, 
these systems should not be deprived of thei r efficient st atus because of the benefits that 
cogeneration brings.

enable the evolutionary transformation of district heating systems by extending the period 
during which a system may be considered efficient based on the share of heat from high -
efficiency cogeneration, as well as to make the proposed share of heat from RES more 
realistic. In this context, the main point is to ensure a socially acceptable price of heat for end-

5. Criteria for high-efficiency cogeneration (Annex III) 
Combined heat and power generation in high-efficiency cogeneration is crucial from the point 
of view of primary energy savings and high generation efficiency, which directly translates 
into compliance with the overriding objectives of the current EED directive.  
The introduction by the European Commission of a proposed limit for direct CO2 emissions 
(for units using fossil fuels) lower than 270 g of CO2 per 1 kWh of energy produced in 
cogeneration (heat, electricity and mechanical energy combined) from as early as 1 January 
2026, in connection with the definition of the efficient heating and cooling system proposed 
in Article 24 paragraph 1(b) is not feasible, as it is not possible to complete all investments 
by the end of 2025 . During this transitional period, a failure to meet the new criterion may 
put at risk the financing possibilities and, consequently, delivering uninterrupted heat supply 
to end users, including industrial and vulnerable ones affected with energy poverty  in result 
increasing its scale . In view of the above argumentation, PGE S.A. postulates 
implementation of the above criterion not earlier than from 2030 , which is based on the 
realistic timeline presented in the Figure 1. 
Furthermore, the EED proposal does no t introduce an appropriate  methodology for 
calculating the level of direct CO2 emissions. For high-efficiency cogeneration units, it would 
only be possible to meet the proposed emission limit under the assumption that they would 
operate at nominal conditions at all times and entirely in cogeneration mode.

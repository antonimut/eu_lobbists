Consultation Response on the Revision of EU legislation on hazard classification, labelling and packaging of chemicals  The LPG Industry welcomes the opportunity to comment on the revision of the CLP regulation as part of the consultation process. This overall ambition is fully supported by Liquid Gas Europe, the European LPG association, representing national LPG associations, European LPG distributors, and equipment manufacturers, to ensure the sustainable, safe and efficient development of the LPG market in Europe. The Association is committed to a safe working environment for its employees and to its customers, protecting human health and the environment, while increasing the competitiveness of the EU LPG industry.   This document refers to a proposed amendment in the scope of Annex VIII of the CLP Regulation, to be considered in the context of the revision. Commercial grades of LPG have varying compositions between commercial propane, commercial butane or a mixture of the two (with possible very small trace compounds). Products that fall under the LPG definition are marketed under different UN and CAS numbers. Liquefied Petroleum Gases, (LPG) (CAS no 68476-85-7) mainly classified between propane (CAS no 74-98-6), butane (CAS no 106-97-8) including also other components (e g. isobutene, 1,3-butadien) with different CAS no 68606-26-8, 68476-26-8, 68476-40-4, 68512-91-4, 87741-9-3, 75-28-5, 68476-86-8, 68783-07-3, 92045-80-2.  Liquid Gas Europe believes that the obligation to comply with the requirements of Annex VIII, for flammable and gas only mixtures as is the LPG, causes unnecessary additional bureaucratic effort and makes the introduction of renewable alternatives more difficult. LPG is not poisonous and in the event of an LPG gas leak, easily identified by its distinctive odour, contacting a poison centre is not expected, Users correctly inform the fire brigade in such a case. The main danger in the event of LPG leak comes from the flammability or the potential to form an explosive atmosphere, which is also known to the users.  Therefore, reporting to the poison information centres and labelling with UFI codes does not reduce any risk. This is particularly evident when considering that substances or mixtures classified exclusively as explosive and/or pressurised gases are already exempted from the notification requirement.  The replacement of fossil LPG with renewable similar alternatives is currently one of the most urgent priorities of the European LPG industry. To date, LPG’s response to de-fossilisation is the introduction of renewable LPG. Alongside renewable LPG production, to de-fossilize the industry, other additional renewable molecules are being explored to be used in blends with renewable or fossil LPG, like renewable dimethyl ether DME (CAS no 115-10-6). In addition, the blend of renewable LPG with renewable DME is also a similar flammable, non-toxic gas. However, due to the current strict conditions for product notification, this blending would involve significant bureaucratic effort, without additional safety and health benefits, thus making the introduction of such lower carbon blends less attractive.  While we support the obligations for additional clarification for the poison centres, we request those obligations to be limited only to mixtures classified for health hazards. We would welcome a simplification of the notification obligations for products placed on the market in several Member States. Liquid Gas Europe would like to request an exemption for flammable gases and asks that the current exemptions in section 2.2 to be further extended to exclude the physical hazards, flammable, and oxidising gases.

IMA-Europe Comments on the Open Public Consultation on the Targeted 
Revision of the Regulation on Classification, Labelling and Packaging of 
Substances and Mixtures (CLP) 
 
IMA-Europe welcomes the opportunity to contribute to the CLP targeted consultation and hereby 
wishes to provide comments concerning the introduction of new hazard classes in the CLP Regulation.  
• Generic comments 
IMA-Europe does not support the introduction of CLP-specific new hazard classes. If new hazard 
classes were to be introduced, this should be done in a harmonised way at the UN GHS level . Only 
by adopting a global approach to hazard classification  and by agreeing on the corresponding criteria , 
the competitiveness of our European industry can be safeguarded, and issues of non-conformities with 
other jurisdictions can be avoided. The introduction of new hazard classes will also substantially 
increase costs and workload for companies, regulators, and scientific committees. It will notably trigger 
new (animal) testing and updates of risk assessments and IT tools (e.g. IUCLID).   
We therefore urge Commission to take a stepwise approach and to reach an agreement at the UN GHS 
level on hazard classes and classification criteria before amending the CLP.  
• Endocrine disruptors (ED) 
The aim of the Commission to include new hazard classes for ED is to protect the human health and 
the environment from hazardous substances. Thus, the criteria and the available data need to solely 
determine whether a s ubstance is an ED or not. All three currently proposed criteria should be 
considered to demonstrate an endocrine mode of action. This is in accordance with the WHO 
definition. IMA-Europe also does not support the introduction of a second ED category as this gives 
rise to a lot of uncertainty and concerns for false classification for many substances. 
There should be clear evidence that the observed adverse effect is a consequence of the endocrine 
activity. Furthermore, a biological link between endocrine activi ty and adversity is needed according 
to the WHO definition to consider a substance as ED. This definition aims to ensure that substances 
are only considered as ED if the caused adverse health effect in an organism, its progeny or 
(sub)population is a conse quence of an effect on the endocrine system via requesting the 
demonstration of a biologically plausible link between endocrine activity and adversity. However, the 
ECHA/EFSA Guidance for the identification of endocrine disruptors in the context of Regulat ions (EU) 
No 528/2012 (BPR) and (EC) No 1107/2009 (PPPR) (ED Guidance) allows the assumption of a “pre -
established” biologically plausible link for a set of adverse effects, not requiring further evidence to 
substantiate an underlying endocrine disrupting mode of action. The implementation of the criteria is 
of high importance to avoid an unjustified classification.

It is important to stress the section 3.11.2.2 ‘Basis of classification’ of the draft proposal that: 
“Endocrine-related adverse effects shall have been observed in the absence of other toxic effects, or if 
occurring together with other toxic effects the endocrine-related adverse effect is considered not to be 
solely secondary non -specific consequence of the other toxic effects ”. For the implementation, the 
time-dependency of any observed changes should be considered. This will help to demonstrate any 
possible link between adversity and endocrine activity. 
• (Very) persistent, (very) bio-accumulative and toxic substances (PBT, vPvB) 
Should the Commission decide to move forward with the introduction of PBT, vPvB in the CLP, the 
criteria of REACH Annex XIII shall be maintained. A modification of the PBT/vPvB criteria, thresholds, 
or the scope of application will lead to the duplication of exist ing assessments and the circulation of 
conflicting information between REACH and CLP. In particular, it should be clear that the new hazard 
classes for PBT, vPvB shall only apply to organic substances and organo-metals. 
Where REACH already provides a clear  regulatory frame and criteria for PBT and vPvB, we ask 
Commission to copy this approach under the CLP.

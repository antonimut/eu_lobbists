Revision of the Renewable Energy Directive II  
Review the Annex IX and preserve high value feedstocks with 
industrial decarbonisation potential – the example of tall oil  
 
DRT welcomes the proposal amending the Directive 2018/2001/EU (hereafter “the Directive”) on the 
promotion of the use of energy from renewable sources (hereafter “the proposal”) presented on 14 
July 2021 as part of the “Fit for 55” Package.  
In the following paper, DRT amends and adapts its  recommendations from January 2021 on how to 
preserve tall oil for its pre-existing industrial higher value uses in line with the ambition of the European 
Green Deal. These updated recommendations are based on the European Commission’s proposal.  
We are  pleased to see that the Commission is supportive of  DRT’s recommendation s pushing for 
Member States to  apply the cascading use principle  when granting support schemes for bioenergy, 
and for specifying the criteria for the implementation of the principle via an upcoming delegated act 
under Article 3 paragraph 3 of the Directive (Article 1(2) of the proposal).  
However, by focusing on woody biomass only, Article 1(2) fails to address the environmental and socio-
economic issues (e.g. GHG emissions increase, undue distortive effects on the raw materials market)  
resulting from the incentivisation of the use of certain biomass by-products or residues for bioenergy 
(listed in Annex IX). Tall oil is one example  of feedstocks threatened by this shortfall, this by-product 
being currently listed on Annex IX Part A of the Directive, hence incentivised to produce advanced 
biofuels. 
DRT key recommendations 
• Amend Annex IX to remove feedstocks having pre-existing higher value uses (such as tall oil), 
as its inclusion leads  to not only an economic sub -optimal situation, but to environmental 
damage and higher GHG emissions; 
• Alternatively, the proposal should ensure that advanced biofuels are produced with the 
highest sustainability standards. To that end, DRT calls on the co-legislators to clarify and 
widen the scope of the delegated act proposed under Article 3 paragraph 3 o f the Directive 
(Article 1(2) of the proposal) to ensure the cascading use principle applies to all feedstocks 
stemming from biomass (including by-products and residues, also listed in Annex IX of the 
Directive).

Biochemistry from tall oil as a key actor of the European Green Deal  
Tall oil-based chemistry fully supports the ambition of the “Fit for 55 ” Package and the objective of  
55% net GHG emissions reduction by 2030 encompassed under the EU Climate Law. It offers a safe and 
sustainable alternative to petrochemicals, substituting fossil-fuel-based virgin materials with biobased 
substances from a renewable by-product, to deliver on the European objectives for circular and low 
carbon products.  
Besides, the distillation and treatment of tall oil for bio based chemistry proposes higher benefits for 
the environment and the economy than the production of tall oil -based advanced biofuels: the 
distillation process emits 25% less CO2 than the advanced biofuels production, it creates 4 times more 
economic value, and 20 times more jobs due to its less energy intensive processes and longer economic 
value-chains.1  
The risks posed by Annex IX of the Renewable Energy Directive 
Despite its long-term pre -existing socio -economic and environ mental benefits,  biobased chemistry 
from tall oil is threatened by the current framework of the Directive, which incentivizes (via mandatory 
advanced biofuel targets and double counting)  the production of advanced biofuels from tall oil, by 
listing this feedstock in Annex IX Part A of the Directive.  
Since the adoption of the RED II in 2018, DRT has been facing increasing prices and  difficulties in 
maintaining a long-term supply of tall o il, threatening the economic viability of its activity, and 
jeopardizing the entire tall oil-based biochemicals value chains. Considering indexes (base 100 in 2007), 
tall oil prices increased by 50% in 2020 and are estimated to raise by 100% in 2030. The m arket 
distortion observed in the past two years, due to the RED II implementation, can be explained by the 
following reasons: 
▪ Tall oil is one of the feedstocks listed on Annex IX presenting the lowest EU volumes available 
(approximately 650.00 tpa)2, with a non-elastic supply as it depends entirely on the paper pulp 
production from pine trees. This also worth mentioning that 100% of the EU available volumes 
were already used while the RED II was not fully implemented yet (until 30 June 2021).

▪ Tall oil is one of the only feedstocks listed on Annex IX having a viable technology

Those market distortions are already having significant co nsequences, not only for the industry, but 
also for the achievement of the European Green Deal’s objectives in terms of circularity, sustainability 
and decarbonisation:  
▪ Higher tall oil prices have an impact on the economic viability of tall oil-based biochemicals, 
leading downstream users to switch to petrochemicals for economic reasons but also due to 
the absence of other renewable alternative nor political incentives. The decoupling of tall oil 
prices (increasing) and crude oil prices (remaining stable and low) impacts downstream users, 
particularly on commodity markets (adhesives for instance), leading to jobs and economic 
revenues losses. 
▪ Moreover, displacement effects would neutralize the benefits of tall oil use and increase GHG 
emissions considering the increasing use of fossil fuel in all sectors currently using biobased 
chemicals from tall oil, in contradiction with the objectives of the European Green Deal3. 
Recommendations on the review of the Renewable Energy Directive 
According to  the waste hierarchy and the “cascading use of biomass” principle 4, which prioritise s 
material recovery over energy recovery, feedstocks available in limited volumes, providing high socio-
economic and environmental benefits and having higher value uses other than energy recovery shall 
be allocated in priority to those industrial uses.   
DRT proposes two options to ensure those feedstocks (including tall oil) are used in order to meet the 
ambition of the European Green Deal: 
1. Remove feedstocks from the Annex IX having a reduced impact on the targets, including tall 
oil 
Annex IX lists the feedstocks that are eligible to produce advanced biofuels and can be counted twice 
towards the national renewable energy targets under the current RED II framework . However ,

2. Differentiating the Annex IX Part A feedstocks having pre-existing industrial uses other than 
energy recovery  
DRT welcomes the inclusion of the cascading use principle and the upcoming delegated act aiming to 
specify its implementation in the Commission’s proposal. Although this is a positive first step, the scope 
of the delegated act needs to be clarified and thoroughly defined (including waste, residues and by -
products listed in Annex IX) to effectively prevent undue distortive effects on the biomass raw material 
market. To that end, co-legislators should specify that the definition of biomass under Article 2(24) of 
the Directive cover s liquid and solid biomass, by -products, waste and residues including biomass 
fraction of wastes and residues from forestry and forest-based industries.  
Several feedstocks listed on Annex IX Part A  (especially by -products and residues stemming from 
wood) have pre-existing industrial higher value uses other than energy recovery and limited volumes 
available5. As such, they  should be prioritized for their higher value uses other than energy recovery 
to ensure substantial GHG emissions reduction as well as fair competition on the raw material market. 
Thus, we consider  that the scope proposed by the Commission under Article 1( 2) of the proposal 
should be clarified and  also cover the abovementioned feedstocks. It is crucial that the criteria 
developed under the delegated act are clear and objective to  safeguard both current and future 
innovation for their highest environmental b enefits. In this light, DRT recommend s the use of the 
following criteria for the implementation of  the cascading use principle as referred to in Article 3 – 
paragraph 3.a(iii):  
1. Total available volume of the feedstock at EU level; 
2. Share of pre-existing industrial uses other than energy recovery;

The establishment of the abovementioned criteria to all residues and by-products from woody biomass

It is therefore urgent that co-legislators seize the opportunity of the RED II review to clarify the scope 
and give a legal basis to the implementation of the cascading use principle while aligning Annex IX with 
the ambition of the European Green Deal to produce bioene rgy with the highest sustainability 
standards and ensure a fair and future proof green transition. 
 
 
About DRT 
DRT is a French medium -size enterprise at the forefront of the biobased chemicals industry. Its headquarters 
are located in Les Landes, where it was founded in 1932. By dedicating 5% of its workforce to R&I, DRT has 
developed a unique know-how in the treatment and distillation of Crude Tall Oil (CTO). DRT uses this by-product 
from the paper pulp kraft process to produce over 300 tall oil -based chemical substances reducing the carbon 
footprint of daily life products with a safe and sustainable life cycle.  
DRT represents an industry at the cornerstone of the European Green Deal (EGD) enhancing sustainability and 
decarbonisation in many key circular value chains of the EU economy, including plastics and packaging, 
construction, and the agri-food sector.  
Since June 2020, DRT is owned by the family company FIRMENICH, leader in the production of organic chemical 
ingredients from plant-based materials. FIRMENICH is the biggest fragrance and flavour company worldwide, 
with a top -tier R&D department.  FIRMENICH puts sustainability at the heart of its business model and  is 
committed to become carbon neutral by 2025 and carbon positive by 2030. FIRMENICH has an overall vision 
of sustainability and promote s the integration of renewables energies hand in hand with  better energy 
efficiency.

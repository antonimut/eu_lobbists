Introduction 
The Italian Banking Association (ABI) appreciates the opportunity to provide 
its feedback through the "Have your say" procedure, given the importance of 
the topic.  
 
We welcome the EU Commission Proposal s and the goal that the entire AML 
Package aims at reaching. The need to  avoid regulatory fragmentation and 
consequently competitive disparities is of paramount importance for the 
banking industry. The fragmentation has so far complicated the work of 
regulators, law enforcement and multinational banking Groups which cannot 
easily put in place EU-wide AML programmes. Even worse, this fragmentation 
enables criminals to exploit regulatory weaknesses and inconsistencies in one 
jurisdiction to launder funds and move these around financial markets. 
 
In summary, the key elements ABI wishes to highlight are the following: 
 
In this context we observe that the Regulation often delegates the completion 
of the proposed provisions to secondary regulation (issued by AMLA or by the 
Commission). From one side this is understandable due to the complexity of 
AML Regulation and the necessity to enter in details, from another side this 
setting may prevent the obliged entities  from understanding clearly, all the 
impact of the new proposed regulation, since now. 
 
In detail, we emphasise to the Commission the following aspects that may be 
optimised in the proposal: 
 
Subject Matter and Scope, including List of Obliged Entities (Art. 1-6) 
 
ABI believes that extending the scope of AML/CFT obligations to crypto 
service providers and crypto-to-crypto transactions would contribute to a 
better level-playing filed. Caution should be given, however, whether all rules 
could be effectively applied to crypto tr ansfers. However, deep coordination 
should be granted among the overall regulations that applies to these new 
“obliged entities”. In particular, with reference to crowdfunding, for example, 
there’s a need for better coordination between art. 5 of Regulation (UE) 
2020/1503 and the proposed provision on KYC. Internal Policies, Controls and 
Procedures of Obliged Entities (Art. 7-14).

Customer Due Diligence (Art. 15-41) 
 
- The risk-based approach (RBA) is key to the underlying purpose of the 
AML/CFT regime. This requires effectiv e risk assessment, based on a clear 
understanding of the customer’s identity and the business relationship. It is 
important therefore to avoid inconsistent and fragmented national 
approaches in MS, for instance in defini ng higher risk categories or in some 
cases extending the scope of risk-sensitive measures and restricting obliged 
entities’ flexibility in how to apply these measures according to the varied 
risks of particular cases 
 
- With reference to Article 17 - which regulates the cases of “inability to 
comply with the requirement to apply customer due diligence measure – we 
underline that in both the European and national AML regulations, there is no 
provision for the activities that intermediaries must carry out with respect to 
funds that refer to existing relationships closed due to the impossibility of 
fulfilling the due diligence obligations. 
 
- ABI welcomes  proposed standardisation of key customer identity 
information that obliged entities have to collect and verify pursuant to Article 
18. However, the new identification measures maintain a differentiation 
according to reduced, normal and increased risk, which remains overly 
complex. In particular regarding EDD measures, Article 16 should be revised 
in order to avoid the application of these measures in case of “increased “risk 
of money laundering or terrorist financing, that does not automatically mean 
“high risk”. The EDD measures should be applied just in case of High risk 
profiles.
  
 
- It should also be confirmed that in case of low risk the obliged entity in 
ascertaining the BO can rely on the intermediary declaration. 
 
- The uniform standard of key customer identity information would potentially 
also facilitate the use of electronic identification , which in turn may 
improve the customer onboarding process, even if the timeline to have the 
new framework completely defined seems to be quite extensive, considering 
that  the reliable and independent sources of information that may be used

Beneficial Ownership Transparency (Art. 42-49) 
 
ABI welcomes the standardization of data  to be collected by obliged entities 
for establishing beneficial ownership as set out in Article 44. 
 
- With regard to the definition and to the ide ntification of beneficial 
owners for corporate and other legal entities, ABI is concerned about the 
proposed changes that - compared to the regulation in force – seem to 
introduce a mix of criteria.  
 
Furthermore, this reading seems to be confirmed in the recital 66 in which is 
stated that “(…) The determination of control through an ownership interest 
is necessary but not sufficient and it does not exhaust the necessary checks 
to determine the beneficial owners”. This approach can generate a lot of 
“fragmentation” among banks in determining the BO and moreover it capsizes 
the approach used in many countries where there is a rigid hierarchy in the 
criteria deputed to investigate the BO.   
 
- The definition of “control via other means” is extremely broad and 
burdensome. In any case, it is considered necessary to supplement the 
sentence with the specification: "where known / known on the basis of 
formally defined acts or circumstances” 
  
- The perimeter of identifiable entities through this provision greatly extends 
the audience of beneficial owners. It is suggested to eliminate point (d), for 
a more effective identification of beneficial owners.
 In any case, it is needed 
to expressly regulate the obligation of the customer to declare that there is 
no control throughout links with family members of managers or 
directors/those owning or controlling the corporate entity (42, letter d) and 
in any case to give a more formal ro le to the declaration of the customers 
and to set up specific AML sanctions in case the declaration is not given or is 
false. 
 
In addition to the comments to article 18, paragraph 4, it is noted that, 
- about “including through bearer sh areholdings” (paragraph 1, second 
indent): this provision seems impossible for the characteristics of the “bearer 
shareholdings”; It is suggested to delete: 
- “including through bearer shareholdings” (into paragraph 1); 
-“through informal agreements” 
 
- “the control … through … informal agreements” cannot be proved. 
 
- Eventually, with reference to exemptions, two categories are exempt: (i) 
listed companies (ii) public entities, in contrast with the general principle in 
force so far that no longer allows any exemption for beneficial owners. We 
would request further clarification and / or alignment of the rules.

Reporting Obligations (Art. 50-54) 
ABI 17/11/2021

- The obligation for FIUs to provide feedback to obliged entities on the SARs 
received as set out in the proposed AMLD6 is a welcome development. The 
legal value of the feed-back received  should be recognised as to be 
used in opposition to disputes or similar cases. 
- The definition of ‘suspicion’ in the proposed AMLR remains broad and 
may continue to lead to a significant flow of SARs towards FIUs. 
- Moreover, difficulties may arise in providing information in response to 
requests for information from FIUs under Art. 50 (1 ) within the set 
deadline of 5 days, or 24 hours in urgent cases. It is proposed that the 
five days deadline be eliminated; leaving it up to the competent FIU to specify 
the response time, based on the complexity of the information to be retrieved. 
 
-With reference to Art. 54, para 2, we propose to delete Paragraph 2 and to 
extend paragraph 1 to anyone who is aware of STR. Confidentiality must also 
bind the Judicial Authority (without prejudice to specific reasoned measures) 
and must not be limited to the name of the whistleblower but must include 
the existence of the RTS itself. 
- Finally, the newly introduced obligation  in the proposed Article 59 (4) 
(b) for credit institutions to report payments or deposits above the cash 
limit of 10 000 euro to the FIU would place additional burdens for both 
banks and FIUs. This further runs against the ambition to reduce the volume 
of reporting to FIUs.

Data protection (Art. 55-57) 
- ABI considers that it is fundamental to ensure more balanced interactions 
between the AML/CFT framework and the GPDR, in particular taking into 
consideration the pursuit of the “public interest” that connotes all the 
AML Regulation. 
- A positive development is the recognition of the importance of 
information exchange between ob liged entities in the AML/CFT 
context and the provision of Article 13 (1) whereby group-wide policies, 
controls and procedures shall also include controls and procedures for sharing 
information within the group for AML/CFT purposes.  
- Nevertheless, to improve the effectiveness of the framework, information 
sharing should not be limited to intra-group sharing, but also extend 
to obliged entities outside the group.  
- Furthermore, to be fully aligned with  the principle of data minimisation, 
information sharing should not be seen as an obligation for obliged

entities but should be based on their assessment on the need for the 
exchange of such information. 
- The Regulation does not provide a legal basis for the exchange of

- Further progress is needed with regard to providing for a safe harbour

- ABI welcomes the proposed Article 55, which provides greater legal

Financial sanctions 
 
- ABI appreciates the attention reserved on financial sanctions matter in the 
new EU AML/CFT Legislative Package since it is in line with the FATF 
Recommendations 6 and 7 and the FATF Guidance on Proliferation Financing 
Risk Assessment and Mitigation. 
 
- ABI believes that it could be appropriate the publication of Guidelines/FAQs 
by the EU Commission, useful to correctly understand as to apply the new EU 
rules by the European Commission and, at the same time, to avoid diverging 
interpretation and application of the new legislation in domestic regulatory 
framework. 
 
- Finally, in ABI opinion it is necessary defining an EU homogeneous context 
concerning restrictive measures throug h a process of harmonisation, given 
that the same EU Member States – at the moment – often apply different 
rules.

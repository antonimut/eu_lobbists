Einride views on proposals within EU Fit for 55
Who we are:Einride is a Swedish technology company that develops and provides freight mobility solutionsbased on electric and autonomous vehicles, leading the transition to sustainable transport. Itwas founded in 2016 by Robert Falck, Linnéa Kornehed and Filip Lilja, and became the world’sﬁrst company to operate an autonomous, all-electric freight vehicle on a commercial route on apublic road in 2019.
What do we do:Einride's mission is to make Earth a better place by Intelligent Movement. We strive for this tohappen through digital, electric and autonomous shipping. Today, Einride operates what isknown to be the largest 100% electric ﬂeet of trucks in Europe with major multinationalpartners. Einride has developed an all electric and autonomous vehicle namedthe Pod, whichis an SAE level 4 autonomous heavy transport vehiclecapable of over 80 km/h and full remote

Issues to consider for Fit for 55 proposals
Einride strongly believes that nearly half of heavy freight transport can be electrifiedimmediately.Technically speaking, it isalready feasible.But to get there, we need to challengethe status quo and in some ways start from scratch.Electrification of the transport sector willrequire a whole new ecosystem when it comes to logistics. It is neither possible nor desirable toelectrify today's goods transport system as it is. Our experience – based on operating one of thelargest fleets of electric heavy vehicles in Europe – is that transport planning has to be done in anew way. It is neither possible, nor desirable, to substitute one diesel truck with one electricvehicle due tothe inherently different characteristicsof electric vehicles.Einride also sees thatthere are new and smarter ways to plan a logistics system than the old system that has servedthe transport sector until today. Data and artificial intelligence can be used to create tomorrow'snetwork of organisations, people, activities, information and resources  in a smarter and morecost- and energy-efficient way. Einride believes that going autonomous and electric is thesuperior method of enabling this development.
Opinion 1:The electrification of the heavy road transportsystem for goods has to be gearedtowards supporting the logistics system of the future, rather than electrifying an old system. Thisis specifically relevant in relation to recharging points. Some of the issues that have to beconsidered in relation to this is that chargers have to be bookable and allow for secure goodshandling at the spot of charging. Moreover, regulations for working and rest hours have to beadapted to electric driving.Charging stations forheavy transport would preferably also haveautonomous charging as an option or the possibility of upgrade.
Opinion 2:Heavy transports in Europe can be transformedright away. Einride sees a cleardemand for fossil free transports from customers all over Europe and we firmly believe that thisshould be taken into account when proposing new emission targets for heavy vehicles in 2022.It is of the utmost importance that the EU’s support is focused on these alternatives rather thanfossil fuels. State aid and EU funding have to be streamlined to support these developments toa much larger degree than what we are experiencing today, where the focus has been onsupporting heavier trucks, diesel trucks etc. The new sustainable transport system will allow fora safer and greener sector and society.
Opinion 3:Technologies develop much faster than anyonecould have imagined. Therefore,Einride believes that the EU has to set very high standards to remain in the lead in green hightech support and deployment. Einrides sees a risk that the EU and its member states' regulatoryand permitting procedures are too slow to keep pace with other global regions. The EU needs tofind a way to allow for new technologies much faster than today and Einrides believes that itshould be done with a goal-based approach which will allow for the innovative greentechnologies sector to develop and manufacture its innovation in Europe. One of the biggestrisks for the EU today might be that companies and jobs will have to leave Europe due tocomplicated regulatory processes despite our high standards on climate action.

Opinion 4:In its proposal for a revision of the Renewable Energy Directive (RED II), theEuropean Commission suggests to add a new article 20aon ‘Facilitating system integration ofrenewable electricity,’ laying down new provisions on in-vehicle data sharing:
“Member Statesshall ensurethat vehiclemanufacturersmakeavailable,inreal-time,in-vehicledatarelatedtothebatterystateofhealth,batterystateofcharge,batterypowersetpoint,batterycapacity, aswell asthelocationofelectricvehiclestoelectricvehicleownersandusers,aswellastothirdpartiesactingontheowners’ andusers’ behalf,suchaselectricitymarketparticipantsandelectromobilityserviceproviders, under non-discriminatorytermsandatnocost,inadditionto further requirements in the type approval and market surveillance regulation.[...]”
Einride welcomes this proposal and believes it is an important step towards transforming thevehicle market to a fully electrical one. In line with this, and as amatter of decarbonisingthecommercial heavydutylogisticsystem, Einridebelievesthat electricheavydutyfleetoperatorsshould have access to data on grid capacity and time needed for charging.
The integration of electrical vehicles with large batteries as storage units into the electrical gridis necessary and will also be pursued by Einride. Our view is, however, that this can only be asmall part of the overall solution and stationary, grid-integrated storage facilities for renewableenergy are the only way to guarantee a stable and emission free electricity network.
Opinion 5:The proposal for an Alternative Fuels InfrastructureRegulation (AFIR) containsprovisions for Member States to ensure minimum coverage of publicly accessible refuelling andrecharging points dedicated to light- and heavy-duty road transport vehicles on their territory.For electrical recharging, the Regulation defines targets in terms of distance between chargingpoints and in terms of power output of charging stations in relation to the vehicle fleet (see Art.3). The proposed AFIR also aims at increasing the user-friendliness and thus the publicacceptance of alternative recharging and refuelling infrastructure. This includes provisions onpayment options, price transparency and consumer information, non-discriminatory practices,smart recharging, and signposting rules for electricity supply to recharging points.
Einride welcomes this proposal and would like to emphasise that while in the past matters ofcharging were mostly associated with hardware modifications, today software plays animportant role both onboard the vehicle, and on a higher layer of abstraction (fleet planning,routing, etc.). For example, accurate calculation of metrics like energy consumption, batterydegradation and charging times will enable the transition to an electrified, emission-free logisticsystem far beyond a 1:1 replacement of diesel trucks with electrical vehicles.To add to theCommission’s proposal, for the electrified heavy duty vehicle sector of tomorrow, it wouldbe of utmost importance to support the deployment of bookable charging stations. The charginginfrastructure should moreover be built in a way so as to offer autonomous charging.

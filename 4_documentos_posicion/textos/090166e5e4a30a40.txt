Executive summary 
 
The GDV supports the concept of a single AML/CFT rulebook on EU level as it has 
the potential to effectively eliminate today's national patchwork of differing AML 
laws and regulations.  
 
We welcome that the proposal on the AML regulation maintains the risk -based 
approach (RBA) enshrined in Directive (EU) 2015/849. However , we are 
concerned that the CDD requirements that will be concretised by Regulatory 
Technical Standards may le ad to disproportionate obligations for obliged entities 
with low or moderate ri sks like life insurers , if the Standards are implemented by 
the new European Authority for Anti -Money Laundering and Countering the 
Financing of Terrorism (AMLA), which supervises the high-risk entities.  
 
Therefore, we would like to highlight the following points: 
 
• Discretion should be maintained for national authorities to specify regular 
and simplified CDD-requirements in case of moderate and lower risk 
exposure.  
 
• The scope of the obliged entities  should be determined in a legally 
secure manner, so that, the scope for life insurers is clear and correspond 
to the previous scope of application of the Directive (EU) 2015/849. 
 
• Moreover, it should also be clarified that the scope for already obliged 
sectors cannot be expanded by Member States. 
 
• Regarding the direct supervision of high -risk entities by the AMLA t he 
criteria of selected obliged entities should be exhaustively defined in the 
AMLA regulation and not be deferred to Regulatory Technical Standards.

1. New regulation on AML/CFT  
While a more detailed anti-money laundering regulation is ineluctable for a single 
AML/CFT rulebook , it is essential that it does not undermine the fundamental 
principle of risk-based compliance with anti-money laundering requirements. The 
risk exposure of  obliged insurance undertakings is deemed to be moderately 
significant.  
 
Therefore, the future framework must continue to ensure sufficient flexibility to 
keep additional financial and administrative burdens proportionate to lower risks. 
Yet, it is not po ssible to make an informed assessment whether this balance is 
maintained as the Draft Regulation delegates several mandates to establish 
Regulatory Technical Standards (RTS) to be prepared by the future EU AML 
Authority (AMLA). Since the perception of the AMLA is likely to be dominated by 
its jurisdiction to directly supervise only the riskiest financial institutions , we are 
concerned that the RTS might impose disproportionate standards on moderate - 
and low-risk entities. Therefore, discretion for national authorities to specify regular 
and simplified CDD -requirements in case of moderate and lower risk exposure 
should be maintained. 
 
The GDV suggests limiting the power to establish RTS to issues directly 
related to AMLAs mandate for direct supervision (e.g., specifying enhanced 
CDD-requirements for selected obliged entities). 
 
We are also concerned about the complexity of the future framework. Next to the 
AML-Regulation and corresponding RTS directly applicable as well as exiting and 
evolving guidelines by the EBA, obliged entities must continue to comply with the 
national implementation of the AML -Directive and subordinated guidance 
developed by national competent authorities (NCA). This is hardly manageable 
particularly for small -obliged entities and will si gnificantly increase the burden of 
compliance and administrative costs. We request the co -legislators to keep this 
issue in mind and to particularly explore ways to prevent redundancies between 
the different levels of regulation. 
 
The GDV understands that the single rulebook should mainly tackle the problem 
of inconsistent implementation of the European AML -framework throughout 
Member States. This implies that the current CDD-requirements are deemed to be 
appropriate and should not be amplified. 
 
Detailed comments on the legal provisions: 
 
▪ Art. 2 (6) (b) extends AML-obligations to insurance undertaking as defined in 
Article 13, point (1) of Directive 2009/138/EC conducting “ other investment-
related assurance activities covered by that Directive”. The reference is unclear 
as Directive 2009/138/EC does not define such activities. We assume that this 
term relates to products which offer both insurance protection and investment 
opportunities. These products would qualify as life insurance policies according

Art. 2 (3) of Directive 2009/138/EC anyway. Hence, we suggest refraining from 
the amendment since it would only create legal uncertainty.  
 
▪ Art. 2 (6) (b)  also captures “ insurance holding companies and mixed -activity 
insurance holding companies”. As their main business is not exposed to any 
reasonable AML/CTF-risk it should be drafted more clearly that the inclusion of 
insurance holding companies and mixed-activity insurance holding companies 
is contingent and limited to the conduct of life insurance activities. 
 
▪ Art. 3 (3) (k) qualifies “creditors for mortgage and consumer credits, other than 
credit institutions defined in Article 2(5) and financial institutions defined in 
Article 2(6), and credit intermediaries for mortgage and consumer credits ” as 
obliged entities. It should be clarified that the exception applies to the entire 
insurance sector. 
 
▪ Art. 9 (1)  requires obliged entities to appoint an executive member of their 
board of directors responsible for the implementation of AML-requirements as 
a “ compliance manager ”. We request to refrain from using this  term as it 
suggests that the designated board member is directly responsible for the 
operational implementation of AML -requirements. Moreover, it blurs the 
separation of tasks and responsibilities regarding the compliance officer (Art. 9 
(3)). In addition , the latter should be responsible to submit annual 
implementation reports to the board of directors. 
 
▪ Art. 13 imposes group-wide requirements irrespective of whether the parent 
undertaking is an obliged entity. The proposal does not explain why this 
significant extension of the current scope (Art.  45 of Directive (EU) 2015/849) 
is considered necessary. If maintained, the group-wide requirements on parent 
entities that are not themselves obliged entities should adequately reflect the 
differences to obliged parent entities. 
 
▪ Art. 18 (1) (b) requires obliged entities to verify the activities of a legal entity to 
be identified. It is not clear why and how corresponding evidence should be 
collected. 
 
▪ Art. 18 (2) and 44 (1) (a) massively extend the identification data to be collected 
from beneficial owners while current regulation only requires obliged entities to 
take reasonable steps to identify the beneficial owner (Art.  13 (1) (b) of 
Directive (EU) 2015/849). 
 
▪ In terms of remote/digital/identification, Art. 18 (4) (b) only refers to the eIDAS 
Regulation, while the current framework also allows Member States to adopt 
other secure, remote or electronic identification processes (Article 13 (1) (a) of 
Directive (EU) 2015/849). Given that customer acceptance especially of eID is 
still limited, it is very important that this option is maintained under the new 
Regulation.

2. New EU AML Authority (AMLA)  
The GDV supports the designation of a European Authority for Anti -Money 
Laundering and Countering the Financing of Terrorism (AMLA) with direct 
supervisory responsibility for selected obliged entities with significant cross-border 
activities and a high inherent risk profile. Yet, it is not possible to provide an 
informed assessment on the latter, as the definition of benchmarks for classifying 
the inherent risk profile is deferred to Regulatory Technical Standards to be 
developed at a later stage. Although we do not expect insurance undertakings or 
groups to qualify as selected obliged entities in the long term given their moderate 
AML/CTF-risk exposure, the eligibility criteria should be exhaustively defined 
in the Draft Regulation  in order to ensure transparency and legal certainty. 
Furthermore, the GDV believes that insurance undertakings should only be 
involved in the designation process if the supranational or national risk 
assessments provide reasons for increased AML/CTF-risks in the insurance 
sector. 
 
The GDV also welcomes that the AMLA can assume supervision over non -
selected obliged entities only under exceptional circumstances and with consent 
of the European Commission. The supervision of obliged insurance 
undertakings should remain in the capacity of national authorities given their 
moderate risk exposure. 
 
It is consistent that the recently extended AML/CFT mandate of the European 
Banking Authority (EBA) is repealed in favor of the AMLA. However, it is unclear 
what happen s to the number of EBA guidelines and recommendations 
already published to date . We are also concerned about the ambitious work 
program of the EBA irrespective of its expiring mandate, which may interfere with 
the positions of the AMLA and add unnecessary complexity to the AML/CFT -
framework. Legal certainty on these aspects is needed.  
 
In terms of financing the AMLA -budget, the GDV believes that fees should only 
be imposed on selected obliged entities according to the cost -by-cause-
principle. Therefore, we  request to reconsider the proposal to collect fees also 
from certain non-selected obliged entities (in particular other financial institutions 
that operate in at least ten Member States). 
 
Detailed comments on the legal provisions: 
 
▪ Art. 90 requires the AMLA appointment as a permanent member of the EIOPA-
board of supervisors, which  would reflect the option of the ESAs to attend 
meetings of the AMLA -general board as observers where matters within the 
scope of their respective mandates are discussed (Art.  46 (4)). It should be 
envisaged to limit the participation rights of the AMLA accordingly.

3. 6th Directive on AML/CFT  
The GDV welcomes the focus of the Draft Directive on the organizational aspects 
of the institutional AML/CFT system  while keeping the requirements of obliged 
entities separate in the AML/CFT regulation. This concept grants Member States 
the necessary flexibility.  
 
Detailed comments on the legal provisions: 
 
▪ Regarding Art. 3 it should be clarified that Member States are not allowed to 
extend the activities of sectors already covered by the AML/CFT regulation. For 
instance, German insurance undertakings are required to apply CDD -
requirements not only on life insurance activities but also on granting money 
loans. In addition, Germany extends AML/CFT -requirements on independent 
legal professionals (Art. 3 (3) (b) of the Draft Regulation) to certain activities of 
in-house lawyers. 
 
▪ In the  Art. 10 (6) , we welcome the Member State s option to defer the 
responsibility to rectify discrepancies regarding the information displayed in 
beneficial ownership registers to the customer in cases of lower risk. 
 
▪ In the Art. 21, the GDV suggests incorporating an obligation for FIUs to provide 
individual feedback to obliged entities upon justified request. 
 
▪ Regarding Art. 39 (3), Member States shall ensure that where obligations apply 
to legal persons, administrative sanctions and measures can be applied to the 
“senior management” instead of members of the management body (Art. 58 (3) 
of Directive (EU) 2015/849). In the absence of a legal definition of senior 
management, personal liability may be extended to compliance officers. This 
would be inappropriate and disincentivize individuals to assume re sponsibility 
as compliance officer. 
 
▪ On the Whistleblower-protection granted to individuals in Art. 43 (3), including 
employees and representatives of the obliged entity who report potential or 
actual breaches to the FIU , w e question whether the FIUs are adequate 
recipients of such reports as opposed to NCA.

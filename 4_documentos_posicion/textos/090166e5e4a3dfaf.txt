 Neste positions  on the European Commission’s proposal for amending 
 the Renewable Energy Directive (“RED III”) 
 Neste  welcomes the overall increase in transport decarbonisation ambition through the Fit for 55 1
 package and the specific target for transport in the renewable energy directive  . However, Neste 2
 regrets that the European Commission proposes changing the GHG calculation rule for biofuels which 
 are currently accepted under REDII so shortly after national transposition. 
 Summary 
 The liquid fuel mix in transport needs to be decarbonized because internal combustion engines will be dominant 
 during this decade 
 1)  A broad feedstock pool is needed for sustainable biofuels - residue feedstocks have to remain eligible, 
 not be unduly penalised. The proposed new and arbitrary indirect emission factor for residues which is 
 added in Annex V needs to be removed. 
 2)  The unconditional cap of 1.7% of Annex IX Part B is no longer justified and needs to be removed. 
 3)  Renewable electricity - like all other forms of renewables - should be subject to a GHG value that takes 
 into account its actual GHG saving in transport. 
 Member States’ freedom to choose their policy instruments to reach the ambitious national climate targets has 
 to be kept 
 4)  Member States should be able to decide how the fuel supplier obligations are implemented into 
 practice, e.g. as energy content or GHG savings. For the sake of clarity, the provision in RED II, article 
 25 (1) which explicitly stated this choice, should be re-established. 
 Increased ambition for RFNBO is welcome. However, a progressive trajectory beyond 2030 and a clearer focus 
 is required 
 5)  A dedicated subtarget for RFNBO in transport is welcome. However, the transport targets should be 
 fulfilled with RFNBO that is consumed in the transport sector in each Member State, not with RFNBO 
 used in the refineries. 
 If, however, RFNBO used as intermediate products for the production of “conventional fuels” is 
 accepted for reaching transport targets, as the Commission proposes, then also RFNBO used as 
 intermediate products for the production of biofuels should be accepted. 
 6)  Neste supports mandatory phasing in of RFNBO hydrogen in industry. A trajectory for the RFNBO share 
 in industry should be introduced to the directive in consultation with the industry. Neste proposes a 
 gradual phasing in of the mandatory target with a mid-term goal and a review in 2030, and the 50% 
 target for example in 2035.

 The liquid fuel mix in transport needs to be decarbonized because internal combustion 
 engines will be dominant during this decade 
 As demonstrated by the Commission,  internal combustion engines will be dominant during this 3
 decade and most transport energy will be supplied by liquid fuels in 2030 and beyond. Therefore 
 electrification and decarbonisation of liquid transport fuels with sustainable biofuels and RFNBO 
 should be pushed strongly forward side-by-side. 
 1)  A broad feedstock pool is needed for sustainable biofuels - residue feedstocks have to 
 remain eligible, not be unduly penalised 
 The Commission proposes adding an indirect greenhouse gas (GHG) emission burden -  i.e. 
 emissions not directly connected to the value chain in question - to non-Annex IX residues (Annex V, 
 part C, point 18). Fuels from Non-Annex IX residues represent today a sizable part of the sustainable 
 biofuels produced in Europe, and are a critical tool for Member States to reach their national climate 
 targets and transport mandates. This provision would - without justification nor proper impact 
 assessment - reduce the GHG emission saving value of  biofuels made from these residues and 
 potentially risk their eligibility to comply with the RED GHG emission saving thresholds. 
 This proposal is a serious deviation from RED II direct GHG methodology. It will, in practice, limit the 
 opportunity to use, find and develop new feedstock sources which are not included in the Annex IX as 
 their eligibility is made uncertain. Current rules under REDII are not even implemented yet by some 
 Member States, so this change appears short-sighted. All biofuel feedstocks which comply under 
 RED II sustainability and GHG criteria and have been used in the Member States under RED II, 
 should be applicable and eligible also under RED III to respect Member States’ national 2030 climate 
 and energy plans. 
 Neste recommendation: 
 ➔  The proposed new indirect emission factor for residues which is added in Annex V needs to be 
 removed. 
 Why: 
 Could freeze future investment plans or strand investments already made on the basis of 
 REDII. 
 Will result in higher prices of biofuels for the consumer, as the choice of biofuel feedstocks 
 narrows down in the EU.

 Will probably lead to export of these local residues to overseas biofuel markets (eg. US), 
 rather than contributing to Europe’s decarbonisation efforts. 
 Adding potential indirect emissions is criticised even by the EC itself for low credibility and 
 sensitivities in modelling. 
 ●  ILUC GHG was not taken to the GHG threshold calculation either in the ILUC Directive 
 nor in the RED II, even for vegetable oils. 
 ●  RED II Annex VIII B says that raw material other than food and feed crops have ILUC 
 = zero. Proposing indirect emissions of a substitute product for a residue is therefore 
 contradictory. 
 ●  Proposal is based on an unclear concept of “closest substitute”, that could lead to 
 residues being treated worse than edible vegetable oils. 
 2)  The unconditional cap of 1.7% of Annex IX Part B needs to be removed 
 Neste calls for removing the cap on Annex IX B. The Commission’s proposal to introduce an 
 unconditional cap of 1,7% of Annex IX Part B - i.e. one that Member States can no longer deviate 
 from - is questionable, especially if and when more feedstocks are added to the list. Other effective 
 measures to limit fraud are being introduced, including the removal of double counting. 
 Neste recommendation: 
 ➔  The Annex IX B cap needs to be removed. 
 Why: 
 Annex IX B feedstocks are critical for cost efficiently achieving transport decarbonisation. 
 Verification has to be performed in a solid manner: the new EU Database will effectively 
 reduce the risk of fraud. 
 3)  Renewable electricity - like all other forms of renewables -  should be subject to a GHG 
 value that takes into account its actual GHG saving in transport 
 The RED proposal excessively favours renewable electricity: 1) The GHG intensity reduction equation 
 in the Commission proposal grants a disproportionately high GHG saving for renewable electricity in 
 transport (-195% GHG saving), including in rail transport which is already largely electrified in Europe  , 4
 and 2) The Commission proposes to establish a credit mechanism enabling operators of charging 
 points to supply renewable electricity to contribute to fulfilment of the fuel supplier obligation.

 ➔  Renewable electricity should be subject to GHG savings that takes into account its actual 
 GHG saving in transport. 
 Why: 
 As stated in Recital 30: “  While supporting electricity  in transport through such a mechanism, it 
 is important that Member States continue setting a high level of ambition for the 
 decarbonisation of their liquid fuel mix in transport.  ” 
 The EU needs real GHG savings in fuels. ICEs running on fossil fuels will keep dominating the 
 European vehicle fleet for the coming decade. 
 When the high GHG saving of renewable electricity is combined with the proposed credit 
 mechanism, a possible result is that large use of fossil fuels in internal combustion engines 
 continues. This would happen as the obligated fossil fuel suppliers could fulfill a large part of 
 their GHG reduction obligation with renewable electricity credits instead of delivering biofuels, 
 biogas or e-fuels, and would keep delivering fossil fuels at their pumps to internal combustion 
 engine vehicles. Therefore, credits generated by renewable electricity recharging points 
 should not be used instead of genuine GHG intensity reduction of liquid fuels in order to reach 
 the fuel supplier obligation. 
 Member States’ freedom to choose their policy instruments to reach the ambitious 
 national climate targets has to be kept 
 4)  Member States should be able to decide how the fuel supplier obligations are 
 implemented into practice 
 Member States are the key players in implementing the most efficient policy instruments for the 
 transport sector in their country. In parallel with the possible establishment of a new dedicated 
 emission trading scheme for transport and buildings, transport’s GHG should remain under the Effort 
 Sharing Regulation whereby Member States can continue to use the most suitable means to reach all 
 their national climate targets. 
 Neste recommendation: 
 ➔  Member States should be able to decide how the fuel supplier obligations are implemented 
 into practice, e.g. as energy content or GHG savings. For the sake of clarity, the provision in 
 RED II, article 25 (1) which explicitly stated this choice, should be re-established. 
 Member States need to continue being able to apply the most suitable means to reach all the 
 ambitious Fit for 55 targets.

 Increased ambition for RFNBO  is welcome. However, a progressive trajectory beyond 5
 2030 and a clearer focus is required 
 5) The subtarget for RFNBO should be fulfilled with RFNBO effectively consumed in 
 transport 
 Neste supports setting a dedicated subtarget for RFNBO effectively consumed in the transport sector. 
 A binding subtarget for RFNBO in transport demonstrates the European Union’s commitment to 
 reducing greenhouse gas emissions of transport fuels and the ambition to harness also non-biological 
 renewable energy sources which are scalable virtually without limit. 
 Neste recommendation: 
 ➔  A dedicated subtarget for RFNBO in transport is welcome. However, the transport targets 
 should be fulfilled with RFNBO that is consumed in the transport sector in each Member State, 
 not with RFNBO used in the refineries. 
 If, however, RFNBO used as intermediate products for the production of “conventional fuels” is 
 accepted for reaching transport targets, as the Commission proposes, then also RFNBO used 
 as intermediate products for the production of biofuels should be accepted. 
 Why: 
 The subtarget is needed to incentivise renewable hydrogen from non-biological origin and 
 e-fuels  towards becoming commercially feasible transport  fuel solutions. 6
 Transport targets should be fulfilled with fuels which are really used in the transport sector, not 
 diluting the RFNBO fuel ambition with RFNBO that is used in the refinery industry. 
 Provides legal certainty and a level-playing field in stating explicitly that the RFNBO provisions 
 relating to “conventional” fuel producers do not only concern fossil fuels but also biofuels 
 producers. 
 6) RFNBO industry target should be phased-in with a milestone in 2030 and the 50% 
 target for example in 2035 
 Neste supports the mandatory phasing in of RFNBO hydrogen in industry, but the timing seems 
 challenging. 
 6  e-fuels refer here to liquid RFNBO. In practice,  we mean with e-fuels synthetic liquid drop-in hydrocarbon fuels, which are 
 produced from captured CO2 and renewable hydrogen from water electrolysis. 
 5  RFNBO in COM(2021) 557 final: ”‘renewable fuels of  non-biological origin’ means liquid or gaseous fuels the energy 
 content of which is derived from renewable sources other than biomass”. For example: renewable electricity is used to 
 produce renewable hydrogen from non-biological origin or further synthetic liquid fuels, so called e-fuels.

 Neste in brief 
 Neste (NESTE, Nasdaq Helsinki) creates solutions for combating climate change and accelerating a shift to a 
 circular economy. We refine waste, residues and innovative raw materials into renewable fuels and sustainable 
 feedstock for plastics and other materials. We are the world’s leading producer of renewable diesel and 
 sustainable aviation fuel, developing chemical recycling to combat the plastic waste challenge. We aim at 
 helping customers to reduce greenhouse gas emissions with our renewable and circular solutions by at least 20 
 million tons annually by 2030. As a technologically advanced refiner of high-quality oil products with a 
 commitment to reach carbon-neutral production by 2035, we are also introducing renewable and recycled raw 
 materials such as waste plastic as refinery raw materials. We have consistently been included in the Dow Jones 
 Sustainability Indices and the Global 100 list of the world’s most sustainable companies. In 2020, Neste's 
 revenue stood at EUR 11.8 billion, with 94% of the company’s comparable operating profit coming from 
 renewable products.

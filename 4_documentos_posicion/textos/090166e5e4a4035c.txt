Neste views on ReFuelEU Aviation initiative
Neste welcomes the ReFuelEU Aviation initiative. A European blending mandate for SustainableAviation Fuels (SAF) is timely and necessary as it will immediately reduce CO2 emissions in thehardest to abate transport sector: aviation does not have an immediate alternative to fossil fuels.
Inaddition, most SAFgrades–suchasNeste’s–areparaffinicandreducenonCO2emissions,suchas particulates, reducing the formation of contrails and thus their heat-trappingimpact ontheloweratmosphere. Whenresponsiblysourced, SAFoffer substantial gainsinlifecycleemissionreductionscomparedtofossil jetfuel.Theyarefullycompatiblewithexistingaircraft(engines)andairportfuellinginfrastructure.
1) A broad pool of feedstock is needed
Inorder toincreasethepotential ofrawmaterialsforSAFandotherapplications,broadeningthepoolof rawmaterialsfor SAFisessential. SAFproducedfromwasteandresiduesthatarenotincludedinAnnex IX of the Renewable Energy Directive (RED) - which represent today the major part of theavailable SAF volumes andreachsimilar GHGsavingvaluesasAnnexIXmaterial - shouldalsobeable to contribute to the minimum objective of the Regulation.
2) EU blending mandate should be increased
With a sufficiently technology-neutral feedstock base, higher minimumblending targetsarerealisticand recommended. Independent researchsuggeststhat thecurrent modest targets- especiallyuntil2030, will make it very difficult - if not impossible- for aviationtoreachitsclimategoals(seeWorldEconomic Forum’s Clean Skies for Tomorrow report, November 2020). Moreover, higher targetswould create greater investment certainty in SAF production capacityinEuropeandworldwideandwould strengthen theEU’spioneeringandleadingroleworldwideinreducingaviationemissions. Asan example, Neste’s SAF capacity will reach 1.5 million tonnes by the end of 2023, which wouldalready exceed the EU’s 2025 2% SAF target, with an estimated demand of 1 million tonnes.
There are sufficient rawmaterialsavailablefor anyforeseeabledemandfor SAF, not onlyinEuropeand worldwide but also for current production technologies and for future technologies that will beintroduced in the years to come.
3) Member States should have the flexibility to set higher national mandates
A number of EU Member States have already introduced blending mandates and/or foresee tointroduce higher mandates before the RefuelEUmandate apply in 2025. Such schemes are oftendeemed necessary to comply with national climate targets and law.TheRegulationshouldnot prevent suchinitiativesandtheproposal mentionsthatall EUairportsaresubject to uniform minimum shares of SAF, not maximum.

4) A robust framework for the implementation of the mandate is welcome
Simultaneous fulfilment of ReFuelEU and either EUETS or CORSIA is a welcome element. Lightreporting needs to be ensured and implementation of this element should - where appropriate-already be available for national mandates and RED II opt-in schemes, as this would acceleratedecarbonisation.
The obligation placed upon airlines and fuel suppliers to report on a yearly basis to nationalauthoritiestodeterminefulfillment of their obligationsisaneffectivemeanstocreatetransparencyformonitoring, reportingandverification. Theproposal iscallingfor simpleprocesses, andthisneedstobe ensured.
A SAF blending mandate can most efficiently be implemented via an obligation on fuel suppliers.Owingtobothlogistical considerationsandexistingregulatoryrequirements,obligatingfuel providersis the easiest method to operationalize a mandate and would help to limit market distortions.
Effective, proportionate and dissuasive penalties placed on fuel suppliers failing to complywiththeobligation on SAF supply to EUairportsareappropriatetoensurealevel playingfieldaswell astosend a clear signal to investors that supply can meet demand.

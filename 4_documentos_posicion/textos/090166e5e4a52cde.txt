Neste’s comments on the European Commission’s proposal for a Regulation on thedeployment of alternative fuels infrastructure (AFIR)
Key messages● The full potential of all alternative fuels must be included into the range of tools to reduce CO2
emissions efficiently. Neste welcomes the proposal’s definition of alternative fuels, which includesRED II compliant biomass fuels as well as synthetic and paraffinic fuels.● The scope of the Regulation should not be limited to those fuels that require major fleet renewals ordeployment of new infrastructure. Neste welcomes the fact that the proposal recognises the value ofalternative and renewable liquid drop-in fuels in facilitating a greater and faster decarbonization,given their compatibility with the existing vehicle park. As noted in the Commission’s impactassessment study, 82% of vans and 76% of trucks are expected to still run on diesel in 2030.● It is paramount that thedefinitionof alternativefuels is appliedconsistently throughout existingandfuture EU legislation to be an effective incentive for the uptake of alternative fuels.● In addition to 100% waste and residue based renewable diesel (e.g. Neste MY) distributed at publiclyavailable fuel stations, HVO100 can also be used by LDV/HDV fleets with their own private refuellinginfrastructure. This also offers cost-effective ways to increase volumes of alternative fuels andachieve greenhouse gas emission reductions by up to 90%.
Acknowledging the full potential of alternative fuels
The full potential of all alternative fuels must be included into the range of tools to reduce CO2 emissionsefficiently. Therefore, Neste welcomes the fact that the proposal recognises the value of alternative andrenewable liquid drop-in fuels in facilitating a greater and faster decarbonization, given their compatibility withthe existing vehicle park.
Neste welcomes the proposal’s definition of alternative fuels in Article 2, which includes renewable fuels andbiomass fuels as defined in the RED II (Article 2, paragraphs 27 and 33) as well as synthetic and paraffinicfuels. (a) ‘alternative fuels for zero-emission vehicles’:-electricity, hydrogen, ammonia(b) ‘renewable fuels’:-biomass fuels as defined in the RED II (Article 2, paragraphs 27 and 33), synthetic andparaffinic fuels, including ammonia, produced from renewable energy(c) ‘alternative fossil fuels’ for a transitional phase:-natural gas, in gaseous form (compressed natural gas (CNG) and liquefied form (LNG),liquefied petroleum gas (LPG), synthetic and paraffinic fuels produced from non-renewableenergy
It is paramount that the definition is applied consistently throughout the EU legislation, since it gives incentivesfor the uptake of alternative fuels. The EU should provide coherence and alignment of legislation with thedecarbonisation ambitions from the Fit for 55 package, and acknowledge all fuels that have a provenenvironmental record, including all alternative and renewable liquid fuels.

Renewable paraffinic diesel (HVO) demand is expected to be more than 20 million tons annually based onexisting and proposed regulation in Europe and North America. The currently identified waste and residuefeedstock pool in 2030 is globally around 40 million tons a year. In addition, there is a significant growthpotential in scalable new technologies e.g. lignocellulosics, municipal solid waste and algae oils. Thesenewscalable feedstocks enable a wider decarbonisation of transport.
Rapid growth and cost reduction in renewable electricity alsodrives thedevelopment of renewablehydrogenandPower-to-Liquids (PtL). They enabletheproductionof sustainableliquidfuels besidesbiomassbyutilizingCO2
as feedstock. In the longer term, the commercialization of these technologies will complement biomassbased liquid fuels. Hence, internal combustion engines (ICEs) remain a viable technology usingalternative and renewable liquid fuels that contribute to the decarbonization of transportation anddecrease the dependence on crude oil. What do we propose?● DespitethemoreprescriptivenatureofthenewAFIRdefinitionofalternativefuels,thepossibilitytoaddinthefuturenewtechnologies andfuels toreplacefossil sources shouldbeassessedandincludedinthe text in the form of a review clause.
● The updated Regulation should continue to recognise the value that alternativeandrenewableliquiddrop-in fuels play in facilitating a greater andfaster decarbonization, giventheir compatibility withtheexisting vehicle park. Dedicated fleets with their own refuelling infrastructure would also offercost-effective ways to increase volumes and greenhouse gas savings of alternative fuels.
● Alignment of legislation is important in order to achieve cost-effective solutions and a predictableregulatory environment. The newRegulation should therefore be developed in full consistencywith existing legislation, such as Clean Vehicle Directive (CVD), REDII, Energy TaxationDirective(ETD), Fuel Quality Directive (FQD) as well as with the Fit for 55 package, in particular RED III,ReFuelEU Aviation Regulation and the vehicles CO2 standards Regulations.
● EU Member States are characterised by different sizes, geographies, population densities, and climateswhich requires some specific powertrains technology over others which better adapt to the localconditions. Member States should therefore be granted flexibility to set up the matching infrastructure,rather than meeting an obligation that may be disconnected from the local reality.

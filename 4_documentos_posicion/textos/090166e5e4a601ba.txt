Fit-for-55 – Renewable Energy Directive 
November 2021  
Eurogas welcomes the higher ambition of the European Commission in its propos ed of revision of the Renewable 
Energy Directive. Nevertheless, this ambition is not enough , nor properly translated for gaseous fuels . There is no 
dedicated driver that would focus on the decarbonisation of gaseous energy. It is also important to define workable 
requirements for the certification of gaseous fuels. Finally, in light of the high ambition, the role of low carbon hydrogen 
in meeting EU climate and energy objectives must be properly addressed.  
Recommendations 
• The Directive should introduce a binding 2030 EU target for renewable gases. 
• The role of low carbon gases  in meeting EU decarbonisation targets  should be properly reflected, 
including though rigorous sustainability criteria. 
• The option for Member States to  issue Guarantee of Origin (GO) for energy from non-renewable 
sources should become an obligation. GO should also be updated to carry sustainability information 
such as the GHG savings.  
• Clarity is required about the treatment of gaseous fuels in the Union Database. In their current state, 
the proposed provisions cannot be implemented. It should be possible to continue to use GO until 
the Database is fully operational.  
• Member States should consider all sources of energy when establishing national support frameworks. 
A level playing field is essential, especially if the technology has a proven overall value for the energy 
system which is not currently accounted for. 
Binding renewable gas targets in the EU 
The Directive should introduce a binding 2030 EU target on demand for renewable gases . Gas – natural, 
renewable and decarbonised – will play a decisive role in the decarbonisation efforts of every sector, including 
buildings, industry, power and mobility as well as agr iculture. To deliver on this potential, the European gas industry 
requires an EU policy framework that promotes the production, market uptake and system integration of renewable 
and low-carbon gases. In order to develop a sustainable gas sector, Eurogas su pports the introduction in European 
legislation of a binding 2030 EU target on demand for renewable gases. 
Low carbon gases and decarbonisation of industry 
Low carbon hydrogen is necessary to kickstart the hydrogen market and to decarbonise the gaseous energy 
mix. Eurogas is calling for a discussion on the contribution of low carbon gases in the European regulatory framework.

If targets for the decarbonisation of the gaseous energy mix are to be defined, those gases should be able to contribute. 
At the very least, the RED should ensure that they are properly certified/tracked, be it through the system of GO and/or 
the Union Database. This represents a unique opportunity for the EU to define sustainability requirements for those 
fuels and take advantage of the necessary provisions already in place in the RED.  
In particular, the RED should leverage the benefits of low carbon hydrogen to decarbonise industry. Low carbon 
hydrogen will be necessary to break the chicken and egg situation for industry. Stable supply of sufficient volumes and 
an acceptable cost are key . Otherwise, as currently designed, the target of 50% RFNBO in the hydrogen being 
consumed could actively discourage to transition to hydrogen, as it would results in a high RFNBO targets that cannot 
be fulfilled in 2030.  
Guarantees of Origin 
The option for Member States to issue Guarantee of Origin (GO) for energy from non-renewable sources 
should become an obligation. Eurogas believes that the potential of the existing GO system must be leveraged. To 
avoid a fragmented GO market and foster its development, this option should become an obligation.  
GO should carry sustainability information to be used as a  common climate ‘currency’. Adding sustainability 
information to GO is possible with only minor modifications and would build on another existing tool, the sustainability 
certificates. It would allow cross-sectoral recognition and use of renewable and low carbon in EU policies. 
Union Database 
The specificities of gaseous fuels (e.g. biomethane and hydrogen) must be properly reflected in the rules on 
the Union Database. The proposed provisions of cannot be implemented as designed. Once injected to the 
interconnected European gas system, the tracking of the molecules and all their transactions is not necessary, 
while simply increasing costs of renewable and low -carbon gases compared to natural gas. At the very least, 
several modifications of the proposed provisions are necessary . The interconnected European gas system  must be 
considered as a single logistical f acility and the tracking envisioned should not apply to gaseous molecules once 
injected. This will not prevent to have information about the withdrawal of these gaseous molecules from the grid which 
would be sufficient to ensure that not more sustainable gas is withdrawn from the system than injected. 
Economic operators deserve clarity and certainty about the implementation of the Union Database. There 
should be a clear timeline for its introduction embedded in the Directive , the lack of information already had adverse 
consequences for economic operators. Prematurely announced as a compliance tool for the recognition of biomethane 
use in the ETS Monitoring and Reporting Regulation as of 2022, ETS operators are left without solution and Member 
States are currently defining ‘quick fixes’ at national level. While the Union Database remains in development, the EU 
should leverage another immediately available and market-compatible tool, the Guarantees of Origin. 
Inclusion of all sources of energy in Member States support frameworks 
Member States should consider all sources of energy, notably when establishing national support frameworks. 
The creation of a level playing field is essential, especially if the technology has a proven overall value for the 
energy system which is not currently accounted for. All options that can make a significant contribution to meeting 
EU decarbonisation objectives should qualify : System integration and level playing field should be ne cessary pillars, 
electrification is not the only way forward the energy transition.

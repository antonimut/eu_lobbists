Re Art. 10 in general: 
 
There is no provision that strengthens the registers as a source of infor-
mation for obliged entities. Obligated entities must be able to rely on 
the information available in the register and use it for their own pur-
poses (also in the context of fulfilling customer care obligations). At 
least in Art. 18 para. 4 of the AML Regulation it should be deposited 
that the verification of the identity of the beneficial owner can regularly 
be carried out exclusively on the basis of the register entry. 
Due to the strengthening of the registers since their creation in the con-
text of the 4th EU Money Laundering Directive, the reservation created 
at that time should now be obsolete. 
 
Re Art. 10 para. 1 sentence 2: 
The exemption should at least include companies whose shares are held 
by the public sector in such a way that no other beneficial owners can 
exist. In any case, if the state holds more than 75% of the shares, no 
natural person can be the beneficial owner. 
 
Re Art. 10 para. 5 lit. (a): 
We appeal to the member states to urgently agree on a uniform under-
standing and uniform standards as to which "discrepancies" are actually 
reportable. There must not be a situation where strict requirements ap-
ply in one member state, but certain discrepancies are considered insig-
nificant in another. Furthermore, it must be ensured that the existing 
data are kept uniformly in scope and format throughout the EU. 
 
Finally, it should be clarified under which conditions cross-border re-
porting to the respective registers must take place. Should this be nec-
essary, digital reporting procedures must be created in a uniform man-
ner that does not unnecessarily delay the existing work processes.

Article 13 
Exceptions to the access rules to beneficial ownership registers  
In exceptional circumstances to be laid down in national law, where the access 
referred to in Articles 11(3) and 12(1) would expose the beneficial owner to 
disproportionate risk of fraud, kidnapping, blackmail, extortion, harassment, 
violence or intimidation, or where the beneficial owner is a minor or otherwise 
legally incapable, Member States may provide for an exemption from such ac-
cess to all or part of the personal information on the beneficial owner on a 
case-by-case basis. Member States shall ensure 
that those exemptions are 
granted upon a detailed evaluation of the exceptional nature of the circum-
stances. Rights to an administrative review of the decision granting an exemp-
tion and to an effective judicial remedy shall be guaranteed. A Member State 
that has granted exemptions shall publish annual statistical data on the number 
of exemptions granted and reasons stated and report the data to the Commis-
sion.  
Exemptions granted pursuant to this Article shall not apply to the obliged en-
tities referred to in Article 3, point (3)(b), of Regulation [please insert reference 
– proposal for Anti-Money Laundering Regulation] that are public officials. 
 
Re Art. 13: 
An exception is needed for obliged entities, especially those belonging 
to the financial sector. It must be possible to use all conceivable 
sources of information at any time in the context of fulfilling customer 
due diligence obligations and in the verification of potentially suspicious 
transactions. Credit and financial institutions in the EU are already sub-
ject to special obligations with regard to their handling of data and are 
also checked by their respective supervisory authorities for compliance 
with these rules. They can therefore be trusted to have access to this 
particularly sensitive data. 
Otherwise, there is a risk that beneficial owners will take advantage of 
this regulation.

Article 21 
Feedback by FIU 
 
1. Each Member State shall ensure that its FIU issues a yearly report on its 
activities. The report shall contain statistics on:  
(a) suspicious transaction reports submitted by obliged entities;  
(b) disclosures by supervisors and beneficial ownership registers;  
(c) disseminations to competent authorities;  
(d) requests submitted to and received from other FIUs;  
(e) data on cross-border physical transfers of cash submitted by customs au-
thorities.  
The report referred to in the first subparagraph shall also contain information 
on the trends and typologies identified in the files disseminated to other com-
petent authorities.  
FIUs shall disseminate the report to obliged entities. Such report shall be made 
public within four months of its dissemination, except for the elements of the 
report which contain classified information. The information contained therein 
shall not permit the identification of any natural or legal person.  
2. Member States shall ensure that FIUs provide obliged entities with feedback 
on the reports of suspected money laundering or terrorist financing. Such feed-
back shall cover at least the quality of the information provided, the timeliness 
of reporting, the description of the suspi cion and the documentation provided 
at submission stage.  
The FIU shall provide such feedback at least once per year, whether provided 
to the individual obliged entity or to groups of obliged entities, taking into con-
sideration the overall number of suspicious transactions reported by the obliged 
entities.  
Such feedback shall also be made available to supervisors to allow them to 
perform risk-based supervision in accordance with Article 31.  
The obligation to provide feedback shall not jeopardise any ongoing analytical 
work carried out by the FIU or any investigation or administrative action sub-
sequent to the dissemination by the FIU, and shall not affect the applicability 
of data protection and confidentiality requirements.  
 
Re Art. 21: 
 
Unfortunately, there is still no provision for feedback in individual cases 
on specific SARs. However, this would be necessary to give obliged en-
tities the opportunity to adapt their reporting behaviour to relevant cir-
cumstances. Otherwise, the reporting behaviour already practised will 
merely be perpetuated.  
The FIUs should also be obliged to respond to enquiries from reporting 
entities in order to coordinate the execution of reported transactions 
prior to the expiry of the detention period. 
The current situation reports focus too much on an ex-post perspective, 
as only the result of the investigations is presented. Of primary interest 
would be information on how, for example, such transactions can lead 
to hits in an automated transaction monitoring system. This should not 
be a mere listing of individual criteria, as was the case with earlier clue 
papers. Rather, it is the combination of individual criteria that would be 
of interest.

Article 53 
Processing of certain categories of personal data 
 
1. To the extent that it is strictly necessary for the purposes of this Directive, 
competent authorities may process special categories of personal data referred 
to in Article 9(1) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 and personal data relating to 
criminal convictions and offences referred to in Article 10 of that Regulation 
subject to appropriate safeguards for the rights and freedoms of the data sub-
ject and the following additional safeguards:  
(a) processing of such data shall be performed only on a case-by-case basis by 
the staff of each competent authority that have been specifically designated 
and authorised to perform those tasks;  
(b) staff of the competent authorities shall maintain high professional stand-
ards of confidentiality and data protection, they shall be of high integrity and 
are appropriately skilled;  
(c) technical and organisational measures shall be in place to ensure the secu-
rity of the data to high technological standards.  
2. The safeguards referred to in paragraph 1 shall also apply to the pro-
cessing for the purposes of this Directive of special categories of data re-
ferred to in Article 10(1) of Regulation (EU) 2018/1725 and personal data re-
lating to criminal convictions and offences referred to in Article 11 of that 
regulation by Union institutions, agencies or bodies. 
 
Re Art. 53, also Art. 47ff. 
 
The possibilities for public authorities to cooperate with each other are 
to be welcomed and it is expected that this will further strengthen the 
prevention of money laundering. However, this should not be limited to 
special categories of personal data, but should cover all necessary data. 
At the same time, however, both Art. 47ff. and Art. 53 lack a regulation 
that at least allows credit and financial institutions to exchange cus-
tomer data with each other as long as this is necessary for the purposes 
of money laundering prevention. The term money laundering preven-
tion explicitly includes the fulfilment of customer due diligence obliga-
tions and the exchange of so-called "KYC data". 
 
The fact that it is possible today for a person to place different infor-
mation with several institutions with relatively little effort is a consider-
able weakness of the current system. This would be reduced if banks, in 
particular, could compare information about the same person in order 
to detect discrepancies. This would also benefit law enforcement. 
Above all, however, a cross-border group privilege is needed in this 
context. The current regulations on data protection and banking secrecy 
do not allow the simple exchange of so-called KYC data within a group.

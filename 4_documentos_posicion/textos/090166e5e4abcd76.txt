About Hubject  
 
Hubject is the world’s leading eRoaming platform for the electric vehicle (EV) charging . Hubject 
connects charge point operators (CPOs) and e -mobility service providers (MSPs) in one place (i.e. an 
eRoaming platform) allowing them to share and exchange data and granting EV drivers access to the 
largest EV charging infrastructure (EVCI) network to date. In addition, Hubject has launched a standard 
business and legal framework for all its B2B users, which enables them to  establish a business 
collaboration, despite size, budget and background. This  network helps to connect more than 1.000  
partners in 52 countries offering over 400 000 recharging stations and is thereby a critical player in the 
EU’s EV charging market. 
 
As the world’s leading B2B platform for cross -provider EV charging, Hubject has been committed to a 
customer-friendly charging experience at public recharging stations since the company was founded in 
2012. The platform ensures simple and efficient network ing of CPOs and MSPs and guarantees a 
uniformly high level of service quality. 
 
Introduction 
 
This paper aims to provide Hubject’s response to and position on the Proposal for a Regulation on the 
deployment of alternative fuels infrastructure (AFIR), repealing Directive 2014/94/EU. 
 
Hubject plays an integral role in the EU’s EV charging market as it enables an open, neutral and non -
discriminatory environment for the players in the EV charging value chain, at the same time fostering a 
safe and seamless EV charging experience for the end consumers. 
 
The revision of the Alternative Fuels Infrastructure Directive (AFID) presents an opportunity for the EU 
to establish the right legal framework  to create the best market conditions for a technological ly 
interoperable EVCI ecosystem for end consumers across the EU. After reviewing the proposal, Hubject 
believes that it could be further improved to allow for a truly interoperable EV charging to prosper in the 
EU. 
 
What is eRoaming? 
 
eRoaming enables the linking of the EVCI market operators in Europe and worldwide for the benefit of 
end consumers to charge their vehicle s where they like . It allows end  consumers with a contract and 
only one access medium (such as an RFID card , a mobile app or Plug&Charge certificate) to have 
access to all publicly available recharging stations that are connected to an eRoaming platform. 
 
The eRoaming platform technology is a readily available solution for enabling the EVCI interoperability 
across the EU, and with the right regulatory environment such technology can make the EU’s transport 
decarbonisation and carbon neutrality ambitions a reality. It creates a space where different actors 
servicing EVs can have access to a significant EVCI market and provide the highest quality seamless 
charging experience for the end consumers, also allowing for value-added services, such as reservation 
of charging points or the integration of parking sensors at the charging locations. 
 
ERoaming platforms help unlock the eMobility market potential  in 
Europe and globally 
 
Hubject believes that simplifying the EV charging experience increases the attractiveness of e-mobility 
and instigates the uptake of EVs. Increasing consumer confidence in e-mobility is critical to make it a 
 
3 
 
reality in Europe.  This con fidence can only be achieved if consumers can enjoy a high level 
technological EVCI interoperability (i.e. not having to worry about finding a charging station or having a 
contract with one or another MSP) which mitigates range anxiet y. Unlike in the set -up where mainly 
bilateral agreements are concluded between the CPOs and the MSPs, Hubject’s eRoaming platform 
allows for a multitude of EV charging market players to connect through an open and non-discriminatory 
platform on which CPOs make a uniform offer to the MSPs, regardless of their size or purchasing power. 
eRoaming platforms hence help to strengthen competition at all levels of the value chain  and, through 
cross-calculation of price, provide end-consumers with the most attractive price offering.  
 
The platform approach has considerable advantages over other EV charging methods. The benefits 
are particularly evident when compared to the peer -to-peer or bilateral networking of market players. 
With this approach, CPOs and MSPs must individually establish and negotiate both the technical and 
contractual interface with each other. This is accompanied by significant disadvantages compared to 
the platform approach, entailing major costs for end-consumers.  
 
• Concerning the technical interface with many actors, multiple issues must be settled 
individually, such as reaching an agreement an agreement must be reached on the 
implementation and architecture of the data transfer or  defining IT security requirements. This 
can lead to the parallel use of several, individual solutions and different versions and interface 
variants. If problems occur during the implementation of the interfaces or during the 
transmission of data, these must be identified and solved in a decentralised manner. This is 
associated with increased costs and can lead to long downtimes of the services.  
 
• The peer-to-peer approach also lacks proper frameworks for the contracts between CPOs and 
MSPs. The contracts must, for example, regulate the quality level of data provision or the prices 
between CPOs and MSPs. Negotiating, drafting and concluding these agreements involves a 
great deal of effort and is a recurring process for each bilateral relationship. As a result, bilateral 
links tend to benefit l arger players in particular. These have the necessary resources to 
establish and maintain complex and therefore expensive peer -to-peer networks. In the long 
term, small and medium-sized CPOs and MSPs could thus be excluded from the peer -to-peer 
network, wh ich could lead to the emergence of oligopolistic structures with few powerful 
players, therefore distorting EV charging market development and increasing the market entry 
requirements for new players.  
 
• The differences in system costs between the platform approach and the peer-to-peer approach 
can be quantified concretely. One difference in the calculation is that in the platform approach, 
end consumers  incur external costs for the service provided by the platform , which are 
transparently displayed in the payment receipt. In the peer-to-peer approach, only internal costs 
are incurred, which are not transparent and are often not fully accounted for in the payment 
description.  
 
Particularly based on the economic effects described, the use of the platform-based model is the more 
cost-effective approach. Independent of the use of the respective interoperability model, however, clear 
recommendations emerge which are of great importance for the further d evelopment of the European 
e-mobility market. 
 
Hubject firmly believes, that the decision to implement the platform -based or bilateral model must be 
determined by the market players - there must not be any mandatory introduction of a specific 
eRoaming protocol, nor a definition of a single roaming protocol. In addition, it must be ensured that 
there is no discrimination by local monopolies, e.g. on price - the access price must also be non -
discriminatory and based on open competition. This is particularly i mportant if the CPOs use public 
funding to build and operate charging infrastructure.

Main concerns 
 
Hubject welcomes the Commission’s proposal for the AFIR, which moves in the right direction by setting 
out (indirect) mandatory EVCI targets and provides for smart recharging obligations.  
 
Unfortunately, the text is a missed opportunity to provide regulatory clarity on the functioning of  
technologically interoperable EVCI and to equip the end-consumers with the right tools for a seamless 
EV charging experience. The proposal does not reflect the current market reality, in terms of payment 
options that the market dynamics have determined and adopted over t he past years, and will continue 
to uphold and further deploy in the future. The proposal raises several major concerns, as it:   
 
• Undermines fair competition in EV charging market to the detriment of consumers . 
Obliging publicly accessible charging stations to be equipped with card readers or contactless 
payment terminals means that the charging infrastructure becomes the CPO’s primary asset to 
monetise. This removes every incentive for the CPOs to collaborate or compete with other EV 
charging market players, such as eMSPs or eRoaming platforms. Consequently, a CPO could 
dominate the prices across its own network , which would translate into higher prices and 
decreased service quality for the end -consumers. This situation is particularly problematic as 
some single local network operators are already obliging consumers to use the very same 
network regardless of their eMSP subscription. 
 
• Breaches technology neutrality principle . Through the obligation of installing ca rd readers 
or contactless payment terminals , the proposal not only attributes the market to CPOs, it also 
eradicates the opportunity for independent eMSPs, eRoaming companies or those providing 
seamless charging opportunities to compete on an equal footing with the CPOs.  
 
• Hinders technological EVCI interoperability.  By removing the price competition incentive, 
the proposal does not support the technological EVCI interoperability which would allow for an 
efficient infrastructure planning and usage. In the same vein, it stimulates over-investment in 
the EVCI, since in the proposed scenario, the CPOs would be  financially prompted only by 
investment into new infrastructure as their primary way to make profit. Therefore, there would 
be less synergies and coordination between different CPOs leading to less efficiently installed 
EVCI.  
 
• Exposes consumers’ data and makes it vulnerable to cyber-attacks. Equipping all publicly 
accessible recharging stations with card readers or contactless payment terminals would mean 
that all the payments carried through these terminals would be unattended and unaudited. The 
 
5 
 
information stored in these readers and terminals would become easy to obtain for hackers, 
especially in rural and remote areas where video surveillance of public spaces is rare . This 
would lead to end consumers’ data privacy breaches , which would hinder their confidence in 
technology. 
 
• Slows down the uptake of most consumer-friendly technologies.  By obliging multiple 
authentication methods onto charging stations, the proposal is slowing down the 
implementation of already existing and most consumer -friendly technologies, such as the 
automatic authentication method uti lized with “Plug & Charge” , enabled by the ISO 15118 
standard. While Hubject appreciates the need for a choice of authentication methods  from the 
outset, we recommend the proposal to be as forward-looking as possible and allow CPOs that 
have opted for auto matic authentication systems to not be obliged to offer alternative 
authentication methods as it would only make the charging journey more complex for the end 
consumer. 
 
Taking all of the above-mentioned challenges into account, eRoaming platforms are facing a market 
and regulatory reality in which  it is becoming difficult to continue to provide workable solutions for EV 
drivers. Hubject believes that a shifted approach to the future of EV charging is crucial, to make 
electromobility a reality in the EU.  
 
Hubject recommendations for AFIR  
 
Hubject believes that technological interoperability is the main pre-condition for strategic EV charging 
market expansion in the coming years  and for the EU reaching climate neutrality by 2050.  Hubject 
foresees that the EVCI expansion will be paired with additional layers of complexity, not only due to the 
increase of EVs but also  due to the integration of new market players . This complexity in an ever -
expanding market needs to be properly managed and technological interoperability is key in this regard.  
 
The proposed AFIR text does not provide regulatory certainty for a large share of market players and 
will not be fit-for-future, provided the below recommendations are taken into account:   
 
1. Technology neutrality to foster market competition  
 
The proposal singles out certain authentication and payment technologies for EV charging stations , 
regardless of the market maturity and consumer-friendliness of existing technologies. It risks distorting 
the already fragile level playing field by granting a significant market share to one single type of market 
player i.e. CPOs  and deprives the end -consumers from ha ving access to the most innovative 
technologies that can provide the seamless EV charging experience. 
 
Contract-based EV charging with digital payment solutions and automatic authentication already exists. 
These technologies are widely implemented and by f ar the most consumer-friendly aspects of the EV 
charging journey. Moreover, they represent the safest technology as they ensure the highest possible 
consumer’s data protection as there are less intermediaries involved in the recharging process.   
 
For the benefit of a well -developed and forward -looking EV charging market  and the consumers’ 
confidence in technology, AFIR should put all existing EV charging technologies and solutions  on an 
equal footing and treat them in a technology-neutral manner: this includes authentication methods and 
payment solutions.  
 
In this respect, Hubject recommends that CPOs should not be obliged to have payment card readers 
or contactless payment terminals installed  on charging stations . CPOs should also not  be obliged to

offer alternative authentication methods in case they have installed automatic authentication on the 
charging stations, and the consumer has previously opted for automatic authentication. 
 
2. Right regulatory incentives and tools for fostering innovations  
 
Hubject welcomes the AFIR proposal , as it  obliges the CPOs to ensure that all publicly accessible 
recharging points operated by them are capable of smart recharging. Smart recharging requires bi -
directional communication and data connection between the EV and the charger. AFIR should not only 
provide for an obligation of smart recharging at all publicly accessible recharging points but also provide 
a level playing field for the technologies that are already mature enough to provide for the possibility of 
smart recharging and ensure equal opportunities for all players. 
 
For the end -consumers to reap concrete cost -saving benefits from the data trade -off that smart 
recharging requires, the recharging station at which the recharging session takes places, needs to have 
a connection with multiple energy p roviders which compete for the best price and the opportunity to 
provide energy for the recharging session. Such connection of multiple energy providers, and hence 
the possibility of smart recharging, is already enabled by the eRoaming platforms which help connect a 
multitude of EV charging market players into one to deliver the most seamless and cheapest recharging 
option for the end-consumers through having a contract only with one entity. 
 
Smart recharging and the eRoaming platforms can, therefore, empower the EU to meet the increased 
consumer demand for EVs and make its transport sector decarbonisation ambition a reality, if they are 
given the right regulatory incentives and tools to operate.  
 
In addition, AFIR will be highly relevant in  defining the market development for the coming years , and 
it is therefore critical that the Regulation supports the development and further deployment of the 
already existing EV recharging solutions, which are currently in the process of becoming a standard 
solution, such as “ Plug&Charge”, based on the open standard ISO15118 . Hubject believes that the 
choice of whether to use such methods should remain with the end consumers, as majority of them 
already opt for such functionality. Therefore, the Regulation should not requ ire double-authentication, 
as it overburdens the recharging process. 
3. Increasing transparency for end consumers

between its users, based on different factors like pricing, quality, value added services and others.

with every eMSP. In addit ion, using different  application programming interfaces ( APIs), tariff 
information between the B2B users can be easily transferred. A s today the price for charging for end 
consumers is based on different influence factors, based on location, power level, n umber of used 
incentives, operator, and others, there are big differences in regards to B2B as well as B2C pricing in 
place. Hubject therefore strongly encourages the proposal of enforcing transparency for end consumers

4. A truly interoperable EV charging market for the benefit of end consumers 
 
Hubject believes that in an ever -growing EV charging market with multip le -types of- market players, 
interoperability of charging infrastructure is key to ensure consumer confidence. Over-investments, 
overlaps of charging infrastructure and lock-ins by certain market players must be avoided.  
 
The AFIR proposal needs to put the end consumer first: this means that all EV charging market players, 
such as CPOs, eMSPs, energy providers, parking facilities providers, and others,  must be  inter-
connected in order to avoid lock-in effects and to provide the most reliable and easy-to-access service 
possible for the end consumer. 
 
Interoperability also means that price transparency will play an important role, as multiple players will 
be involved in the EV recharging operation, requiring them to collaborate to ensure that the 
endconsumers receive correct information. Hubject fully supports price transparency if it applies to all 
segments of the final end-price.  
 
In general, it needs to be noted, that creating clear guidelines for the future EVCI market in Europe is 
relevant and requested. On the other hand, it needs to be ensured, that these guidelines are not affected 
by changes on a too dynamic level and in addition, it should be achieved, that the EU Member States 
are not creating additional or more demanding regulations on a national level, as  this would heav ily 
impact the investment security for the market players. 
  
Similarly, Hubject recommends the AFIR proposal to incentivize CPOs to co -operate with at least 1 
eMSP, so as to maximize network coverage, interoperability and free competition for the benefit of end 
consumers. Consequently, the prices across the CPO network will become competitive, increasing the 
service quality for end -consumers. In terms of price transparency, AFIR would benefit from a clear 
definition of ‘pricing component’ in the EV charging operation.

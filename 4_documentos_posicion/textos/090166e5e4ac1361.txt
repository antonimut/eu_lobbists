Danish Energy_reply to the Renewable Energy Directive hearing  
The European Commission published the "Fit for 55" package on the 14th of July 2021, which 
outlines a plan to reach the 2030 greenhouse gas emissions target and eventually reach climate 
neutrality by 2050.  
The Renewable Energy Directive (RED II(I)) is a crucial part of the FF55 puzzle, and we support 
adjusting it to fit the decarbonization ambitions. First, we find it positive that that the RE -target 
is increased, however we would advise setting a target of 45% RE in 2030. This is in line with 
the swift buildout needed and supported by the Commission’s own impact assessment. Second, 
we support clarification on biomass sustainability criteria, however, advise further studies are 
needed before setting additional restrictions. Third, we warn against setting many sector -
specific targets e.g. on buildings  which could be suboptimal , and better dealt with in the 
upcoming EPBD1 revision.  
Finally, we have submitted separately our comments on the additionality principle (Art. 27.3 
REDII). We still have gra ve concerns that the proposed requirements will severely hamper 
building-out of hydrogen infrastructure, which is a key part of the green transition. We will 
engage further in this process separately.  
In the annex attached please find our updated positions concerning the RED II(I) revision.  
Yours sincerely, 
Dansk Energi

In this section we present elaborated comments on the revised RED proposal: 
RED-Directive (Procedure 2021/0218(COD) amending Directive (EU) 2018/2001, (EU) 2018/1999

Areas Position of Dansk Energi 
Binding overall union target The European Commission, we believe, should consider enhancing the 
directive's ambitions by increasing the binding overall union target for 
2030. To achieve the 2030 goals, a 45 percent target is required. 40  
percent is insufficient, as massive volumes of renewable energy are 
urgently needed. 
Suggestion for amendment: 
Article 3 
Binding overall Union target for 2030 
 
1.  Member States shall collectively ensure that the share of energy 
from renewable sources in the Union’s gross final consumption of 
energy in 2030 is at least 40% 45%. 
RES-collaboration, Offshore 
collaboration (Art. 9(1a), Art. 9(7a)) 
In general, the Co mmission's call for stronger cooperation between 
member states in the deployment of renewable energy projects and the 
development of offshore infrastructure is commendable. As a result, we 
recommend keeping the following article wordings: 
• Art. 9(1a) 
• Art. 9(7a) 
Permitting and Guarantees of 
Origin (GoO) (Art. 15, Art. 19) 
Collaboration on renewable energy projects should not result in 
increased permitting- and processing times or administrative procedures. 
Current Danish application processes (e.g., application, tendering, 
environmental approvals, etc.) already present a barrier for RES 
deployment, which risks being exacerbated by border -crossing/cross-
cutting collaboration . As a result, it is critical that the administrative 
processes run concurrently. 
We commend the wording in Art. 19 regarding GoO, which we continue 
to believe is critical for the deployment of RES. 
We also support Art. 15 on permitting without further wording changes. 
Finetuning the ‘quality 
Roundwood’ definition (art. 2) 
Concerning biomass, we propose some changes to the definition of 
quality roundwood. Overall, changing the word ‘quality’ to ‘high -value’ 
when defining such wood is more consistent with current forestry 
practices and supply chains, and is easier to implement given that price 
data is readily available  and verifiable. Solid wood products derive a 
higher value than wood used for biomass, making value the best 
quantifiable representation of quality. 
3 
 
We also find that r eplacing ‘industrial use’ with ‘use in solid wood 
products’ better captures the Commission’s intention, which is to ensure 
that high-value wood or timber that could be used in other wood products 
is not diverted to the biomass market. This wording is widely recognized 
within the forestry community and implementable in sourcing policies of 
biomass operators 
Finally, allowing for the use of da maged, misshapen and undersized 
trees will support the health and maintenance of the forest by providing 
a market for lower-value wood that has no other market or is undersold, 
and would otherwise be discarded or burned at the roadside. 
Suggestion for amendments: 
Article 2 
Definitions 
 
(1c) ‘ quality high-value roundwood’ means roundwood felled or 
otherwise harvested and removed, whose characteristics, such as 
species, dimensions, rectitude, and node density, make it suitable for 
industrial use use in solid wood products, as defined and duly justified 
by Member States according to the relevant forest conditions. This does 
not include pre-commercial thinning operations or trees extracted that 
are damaged, misshapen, undersize, or otherwise from forests  
affected by fires, pests, diseases or damage due to abiotic factors. 
Rules concerning biomass (Art. 3) 
 
When it comes to biomass sustainability requirements, Denmark is 
already a world leader. Other nations' biomass requirements are 
currently inadequate and differentiated , which is why we urge the 
Commission to draw on Danish experience. 
We focus our attention on two parts of the updated proposal: The first is 
the cascading principle , which we feel is compliant with current 
regulations; the second is the definition of high quality stemwood, which 
is more problematic. While the principle is commendable, there are 
certain practical challenges that must be addressed. It will be especially 
difficult to enforce it in the forests, especially if the Commission does not 
further define/clarify it. Recital 4, for example, stands out as an example 
of a requirement that auditors will be unable to enforce. 
Suggestion for amendments: 
Article 3.3 
Cascading principle 
Member States shall take assess measures to ensure that energy from 
biomass is produced in a way that minimizes undue distortive effects on 
the biomass raw material market and harmful impacts on biodiversity. 
These measures shall be based on the Commission’s impact report on 
biomass support schemes. To that end , they shall take into account the 
waste hierarchy as set out in Article 4 of Directive 2008/98/EC and the 
cascading principle referred to in the third subparagraph.  
As part of the measures referred to in the first subparagraph:

Criteria for PtX-fuels – Renewable 
Fuels of Non -Biological Origin 
(RFNBOs) (Art. 27(3)) 
In the proposal for a delegated act on article 27(3) of the existing RED II 
regulation, which outlines the criteria for cla ssifying RFNBOs as fully 
renewable, the Commission has suggested particularly stringent 
standards for RFNBOs. Dansk Energi believes that the requirements in 
the upcoming delegated act will create unnecessarily high barriers to the 
creation of production fa cilities for renewable hydrogen and hydrogen -
based fuels for the transportation sector if they prove to be overly 
restrictive. 
By 2030, the Renewable Energy Directive shall ensure sufficient volumes 
of renewable fuels for the transport sector, including sh ipping, aviation, 
and heavy transport. We feel that the current proposal does not entail an 
adequate supply of such fuels, owing to the lack of emphasis on boosting 
the production of RFNBOs for difficult -to-abate sectors, such as heavy 
transport. 
5 
 
We urge t he Commission to amend the directive to exempt member 
states with a high proportion of renewables in the domestic power 
generation from the criteria of the delegated act for article 27(3). We 
propose an exemption to be introduced for countries that have a current 
share of at least 85  percent renewable electricity in the total national 
electricity production, computed over the previous year, and have taken 
political steps to achieve 100 percent renewable electricity. 
At the very least, the upcoming delegated act should be subject to 
demands for additional data collection, such as monitoring the actual 
development of PtX-facilities prior to the application of restrictive criteria. 
This will increase the incentives for member states to deploy RES. 
Suggestion for amendment: 
Separate amendments for the delegated act under Art. 27.3 of RED II

Union database (Art. 31) We support the Commission's proposal for a "Union Database" to provide 
common EU certification of renewable and low -carbon fuels, including 
hydrogen and renewable fuels of non -biological origin. This is critical for 
the transition and for combating greenwa shing. It is important, however, 
that such a database does not necessitate excessive administrative 
resources or impose significant burdens on businesses 
As a result, it is essential that the system and database distinguish 
between renewable fuels, which p roduce no emissions, and low -carbon 
fuels, which produce some CO2-e due to the impossibility of capturing all 
CO2 with carbon capture. If renewable and low -carbon fuels are 
effectively equated, neither Denmark nor the EU can expect to meet their 
climate targets.

Sector coupling, district heating 
(Art. 20(a)) 
We applaud the directive's emphasis on sector coupling and flexibility, as 
demonstrated by transparency criteria such as renewables targets.  
Flexible sector coupling between the energy and supply secto rs, with 
cost-effective utilization of consumption and production flexibility, is a 
critical component of an efficient green transition and the achievement of 
overall energy and supply system reduction targets. 
Dansk Energy has – in collaboration with Dansk Industri (Danish Industry 
Association), within the framework of the Business Climate Alliance, and 
together with 24 leading Danish production - and service companies, 
supply companies, and energy companies – prepared the report: 
Recommendations for implementation of sector coupling . In general, we 
believe the directive is consistent with the recommendations contained in 
this document.  We do, however, recommend that specific building 
legislation be included in the upcoming energy performance of buildings 
directive (EPBD) to avoid suboptimal and conflicting demands. 
 
We suggest that the wording below is deleted from  Art. 20a.  This 
requirement is unnecessary double -regulation with regards to Art. 15 of 
REDII. Indeed, the member state, not the DSO, must be responsible for 
making information about the share of renewable ene rgy available. In 
contrast to DSOs, member states have access to information about the 
entire grid and should decide how to make it available.  
Suggestion for amendment: 
Article 20a 
Facilitating system integration of renewable electricity 
1. Member States shall require transmission system operators and 
distribution system operators in their territory to  make available 
information on the share of renewable electricity and the greenhouse gas  
emissions content of the electricity supplied in each bidding zone, as 
accurately as possible and as close to real  time as possible but in time 
intervals of no more than one hour, with forecasting where available. This  
information shall be made available digitally in a manner that ensures it can 
be used by  electricity mark et participants, aggregators, consumers and 
end- users, and that it can be read by electronic communication  devices 
such as smart metering systems, electric vehicle recharging points, 
heating and cooling systems and building energy management systems.

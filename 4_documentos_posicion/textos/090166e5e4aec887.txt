The Proposed Revision of the EU Energy Efficiency Directive Revision  
Enel views 
 
Enel welcomes the EU Energy Efficiency Directive’s “Fit for 55” recast proposal. The “Energy Efficiency 
First” principle constitutes one of the main pillars of the EU’s fight against climate change and a successful 
and sustainable energy transition . Energy efficiency is one of the most cost -effective ways of addressing the 
EU’s strategic policy objectives of carbon neutrality by 2050, ensuring security of supply and enhancing 
competitiveness.  
The proposed increase of the EU target to reduce energy consumption by at least 9% by 2030 
compared to the projections of the 2020 Reference Scenario baseline1 seems appropriate to reach the 
55% GHG emissions reduction  target by 2030. Without such additional significant ambition increase , the 
European Union would simply not reach its 2030 binding GHG reduction target.  Moreover, the binding nature 
of the EU target will compel Member States to step up their efforts and better align their national contributions, 
which till today still show an important collective delivery gap towards the existing EU target.  
The proposal of a harmonized formula to calculate Member States’ national contributions, as well as 
the additional delivery gap mechanism are seen positively.  They should provide more clarity and reliability 
of the effectiveness and outcome of measures taken by the Member States, enable better monitoring of the 
progress towards energy savings milestones , establish a more effective framework to correct any deviation , 
and, in general, ensure that the overall EU target is being met. The use of a harmonized formula provides for 
a level playing field for Member States and enhances proper benchmarking between them. It is coherent with 
other Fit for 55 regulation, particularly with the Renewable Directive and the Governance Regulation, in which 
a similar formula is being used to calculate Member States’ renewable energy contributions. 
We see with perplexity a raise in the energy savings obligations under article 8, as the existing set up 
may lead to the amplification of market distortions in t he EU. Such distortions already exist since the 
Directive doesn’t foresee any mechanism to ensure that Member States implement a balanced mix of 
measures to fulfill their energy savings obligations under Article 8. Some countries exclusively implement  
Energy Savings Obligation Schemes, others a mix of ESO schemes and alternative measures or only 
alternative measures. This leads to a disproportional burden on obligated parties in those countries where only 
ESO schemes have been set in place. A raise of the ESO would further distort the market, i ncreasing even 
more the costs for energy operators and eventually for end consumers. A 1,5% annual energy savings 
obligation, from 2024 onwards, implies that only energy savings obligations would account for approximately 
80% versus a former 50% of the additional savings contributing to the achievement of the increased EU 2030 
target. The implementation of the Fit for 55 package should result in significant additional energy savings – 
such as fuel consumption reductions coming from the reinforced CO2 standards, the extension of the ETS and 
new provisions in the EED. New and more stringent mandatory requirements in other pieces of legislation will 
limit the accountability of new “additional” measures under article 8, and thus hamper significantly the 
achievement of a more ambitious energy savings obligation target . On the other hand, the eventual inclusion 
of the building and road transport sector under the ETS should provide for additional resources for energy 
efficiency projects. Therefore, we propose an increase of the article 8 ambition  of 1,3% instead of 1,5% . 
Further, to avoid market distortions in those Member States that implement Energy Savings Obligation 
schemes, a cap should be introduced to ensure that no disproportioned burden is being put on obligated 
parties. On the other hand, the requirement to target part of the energy savings to people affected by energy 
poverty, vulnerable customers and, where applicabl e, people living in social housing  is highly welcomed as it 
might play a n important part in countering energy poverty and in ensuring a just transition.  Finally, Enel 
supports the proposal to exclude the crediting of energy savings from policy measures reg arding the use of

Enel welcomes the reinforcement of the role of public entities in achieving higher energy efficiency 
targets, as not only they set an example for the general public and other sectors, moreover they are a 
significant energy consumer.  More specifically the requirement to renovate 3% of the total floor area of 
heated and/or cooled buildings owned by all public entities is crucial to reach the overall energy efficiency 
targets, since most of public buildings belong to and/or are occupied by regional and local authorities. Central 
government buildings merely account for less than a quarter of all public buildings. The withdrawal of the 
possibility to take alternative measures to renovation will scale up the renovation rate, which till now only 
reached 1,5%, far below the 3% target. It also provides for a level playing field between Member States and 
will enable better monitoring and harmonized reporting.  
The Member States’ obligation to ensure that transmission and distribution system operators do not 
invest in stranded assets to contribute to climate change mitigation is seen as positive . It will promote 
investments in innovative and clean technologies consistent with the EU’s decarbonization pathway. Stranded 
assets generally relate to fossil fuel technologies and infrastructure, and as such their phase-out is desirable, 
including those for blue hydrogen. 
Enel supports the proposed requirement for Member States to provide and disseminate information 
on available energy efficiency improvement measures and to create access to legal and financial 
advice to all market actors. Non-economic barriers such as insufficient information remain the main obstacle 
to the uptake of energy efficiency measures.  Better quality and more widely available information will increase 
awareness amongst final consumers and should lead to behavioral change, resulting in increased energy 
related renovations and investments. More specifically, the requirement to establish one -stop-shops 
information points or similar mechanisms should have a favorable impact, as they would provide efficient and 
consumer friendly channels for technical, financial, and legal advice. 
Finally, the availability of qualification, accreditation, and certification schemes to ensure the 
appropriate level of competences for energy efficiency profession als is crucial to overcome the lack 
of expertise and qualifi cation. Such lack has shown to be another important barrier to energy efficiency 
related investments. Training and qualification of professionals in the energy efficiency market will create new 
jobs or might help convert brown jobs into green ones for those who’s work may be put at risk by the energy 
transition in light of its association with fossil fuel technologies. This way the proposal contributes to a fair and 
just transition that leaves no one behind.

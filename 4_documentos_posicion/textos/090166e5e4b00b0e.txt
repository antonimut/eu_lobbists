CEPM contribution to the consultation regarding the proposed legislative package « Fit for 55 »  
 
 
Introduction 
CEPM wishes to transmit its comments on various texts proposed in the “fit for 55” package. In 
general, CEPM regrets that the contribution of crop based biofuels, including corn bioethanol, is not 
better supported. CEPM requests that the role of these biofuels be improved, and that it is reflected 
systematically in the various texts, in a more complete way than the CEPM proposals below. 
 
ETS/LULUCF/ESR 
The Fit for 55 package clearly strengthens the GHG emissions reduction ambitions of the sectors 
covered by the effort-sharing regulation which includes transport, buildings and agriculture. 
Commission impact assessment show that in the new climate ambition for 2030, GHG emission 
reduction efforts in the transport sector would increase only moderately, less than the agricultural 
non-CO2 emissions. CEPM hopes that the efforts requested from the agricultural sector will not be 
the result of insufficient efforts in other sectors. Assessments of the impacts of the Farm to Fork 
strategy have shown very mixed results on GHG reductions, once carbon leakage are taken into 
account, and despite drastic consequences on lower crop production and increased prices. The 
European agricultural bioeconomy and agriculture cannot be the bridge to fullfill the target. The EU 
commission proposes to merge agricultural non-CO2 emissions with those from the soil and forest 
sector in a new AFOLU sector. 
 
CEPM considers that the goal of carbon neutrality in 2035 for the AFOLU at EU level must be 
evaluated with caution, and that it could be postponed to 2050. In 2050, energies should be 
completely carbon free, including natural gas. 
 
Renewable energy directive 
COP26 noted that the cumulative National indicative commitments of its members will not allow to 
meet the desirable maximum increase of + 1.5 ° C, or even that of + 2 ° C. COP26 asks its members to 
come back to COP27 with improved commitments. CEPM thinks the European Union should take up 
this challenge in the renewable energies directive by accelerating the decarbonisation of transport 
with more sustainable first-generation biofuels rather than by restricting them. The EU car fleet will 
actually still be largely made up of vehicles with internal combustion engines by 2035. Bioethanol, 
such as from corn, is an immediate, available, efficient and economical solution to contribute to this 
decarbonisation. By eliminating high ILUC-risk unsustainable biofuels, almost all of this indirect effect 
is controlled as shown in the 2016 Globiom study commissioned by the European Commission. In 
addition, the levels of consumption of 1st generation biofuels used in the modeling are far from the 
actual consumption observed in 2020. 
 
CEPM therefore asks that Member States which so wish, to be able to increase their uptake of 
sustainable crop based biofuels beyond their national cap while the EU respecting an overall 
maximum share of 7% of these biofuels in the consumption of European transport. The use of fuels 
with high biofuel blends should also be promoted, such as E10, E20, E85 or ED95 for bioéthanol. 
 
CEPM considers that it would be desirable to keep an overall objective of incorporation of renewable 
energies in transport alongside the new target based on a reduction in the emission intensity of the 
fuels distributed. It also believes that the 13% reduction in fuel emissions intensity in 2030 is 
insufficient and should be increased to 20%. Indeed, the weakness of the emission reduction efforts 
in the transport sector will be reflected in other sectors such as agriculture.

On sustainability, CEPM supports administrative simplification for small biogas plants, and regulatory 
stability. It opposes the retroactivity of the increase in GHG emission reduction thresholds at facilities 
in activiy. It also calls for the maintenance of pragmatic and usable criteria, such as regional 
agricultural NUTS2 emission values and the removal of the “total lack of impact on biodiversity” from 
agricultural practices of carbon storage in soils. 
 
CEPM also wants that cropping systems with intermediate crops to be recognized as virtuous and 
helping to meet the sustainability challenges required of agriculture. These innovative systems 
should not bear, in the European context, impacts on the demand for land. A last, CEPM notes with 
satisfaction the improvements that have long been requested in the calculation of renewable energy 
targets in transport : first with the abolition of fuel multipliers ; second with a share of renewable 
energies in transport measured on the total energy consumed in transport and not on the sole 
energy consumption of road/rail transport. 
 
Energy taxation 
CEPM supports fair and harmonized taxation of sustainable renewable energies. The proposal to tax 
fuels at the unit of energy and no longer on physical units such as volume or weight is a step forward. 
Bioethanol, with a lower energy density than gasoline, will be less disadvantaged compared to the 
latter. 
 
CEPM also supports a lower minimum tax for sustainable renewable energies compared to fossil 
fuels, as proposed by the Commission. On the other hand, the CEPM refuses tax differentiation 
between sustainable biofuels. It is neither acceptable to tax sustainable corn bioethanol more than 
so-called advanced bioethanol, nor consistent, after a period of 10 years, to tax it like fossil gasoline. 
Sustainable renewable fuels definition and energy share targets should be kept to the Renewable 
Energy Directive. Finally, unsustainable fuels should be taxed as fossil fuels. 
 
CEPM therefore calls for a single taxation for all sustainable renewable energies, set at a minimum of 
€ 0.15 / GJ identified in Tables A, B and C of Annex I of the draft directive on energy taxation. For 
consistency, energy taxation should distinguish minimum taxes between fossil fuel based electricity 
from carbon-free electricity. 
 
Car emissions regulations 
CEPM notes that the Commission proposal does not take into account recital 50 of Regulation 
2019/631 of 17 April 2019 which asks the EU Commission to, “no later than 2023, assess the 
possibility of developing a method Common Union for the assessment of CO2 emissions throughout 
the life cycle of these vehicles placed on the Union market ”. 
 
CEPM considers it illogical to assess the environmental impacts of mobility using different 
methodologies, and to base vehicle emission reduction policies on partial assessments of the impacts 
of electric vehicles. It is essential that this regulation follows a principle of technological neutrality 
and then elaborates its assessments and objectives on a complete life cycle analysis. This must 
include the production of electric battery and  its end of life. It is a matter of transparency and 
objectivity towards European citizens, and to rigorous. Bioethanol is indeed a renewable energy, 
unlike what this regulation says due to its flawed methodology based only on tailpipe emissions.  
 
CEPM therefore calls for an acceleration of the implementation of the LCA methodology in this 
regulation and in the definition of the 2035 targets. It would be surprising to pursue the path of

Commission, while plug-in hybrid vehicles running on superethanol may have less GHG emissions on

Aviation renewable fuels 
The aviation proposal refuses to integrate first generation biofuels, for example from corn. It is not 
acceptable banning them in aviation sector in order to avoid potential transfers from the road sector, 
and at the same time aiming for phasing out vehicles with internal combustion engines. CEPM 
regrets once again that first generation biofuels are specifically targeted. 
 
CEPM wants that biojets from maize, like other sustainable biofuels produced from food/feed crops, 
may be used in the decarbonization of aviation as in any other transport sector. The aviation sector 
would then be a positive contributor to the development of a sustainable European bioeconomy. 
 
In addition, CEPM requests that biofuels from intermediate and sustainable crops within the 
framework of the renewable energy directive to be also included in the definition of "sustainable 
aviation fuels" ("SAF") in Article 3 of this proposal.

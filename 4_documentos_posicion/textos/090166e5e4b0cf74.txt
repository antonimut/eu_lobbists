FEEDBACK FOR THE RED III PROPOSAL 
 (hereafter, the “Proposal”) 
 
Reganosa, as Transmission System Operator,  is committed to play an active role in the energy 
transition process framed within the European Green Deal, the National Energy and Climate Plan 
2021-2030 (NECP) and the EU Energy System Integration and Hydrogen strategies.  
In this context, Reganosa welcomes the European Commission’s proposal to review the Directive 
(EU) 2018/2001 on the promotion of the use of energy from renewable sources  (RED II), as part 
of an integrated response to achieve the climate and energy ambitions of the EU by 2030 and 
2050. 
In particular, we welcome the reinforced ambition to boost the penetration of renewable energy 
by 2030  and, especially, the extension of the accounting scope of renewable fuels of non -
biological origin (RFBNOs) to all consumption sectors, as well as the  implementation of specific 
sub-targets for these fuels in transport and industry. 
Hereunder, Reganosa proposes some comments that might be considered in order to  make the 
Proposal more inclusive and a key enabler to materiali se efforts to advance towards climate 
neutrality. 
1. EU-WIDE GUARANTEES OF ORIGIN SYSTEM 
Reganosa considers it of utmost importance that the forthcoming RED III Directive addresses 
more substantially the revision of the system of Guarantees of Origin (GO s) set out in Article 19 
of the current RED II Directive. 
First of all,  it is essential to empower consumers with transparent information so that they can  
make well-informed choices and  clearly identify the origin of the energy they are consuming . 
Therefore, to achieve a level -playing field  and ensure quick supply of large volumes of 
decarbonised energy to the market , the issuance of GOs should be mandatory to all types of 
energy carriers demonstrating GHG emission savings , including low-carbon gases, and not an 
option for Member States as is maintained in the RED III proposal.  Consequently, a definition of 
what is “low-carbon” should also be clarified for this purpose in Article 2. 
On the other hand, an EU-wide Guarantees of Origin system  should be implemented as a basis 
for a coherent European certification system. Reganosa believes that priority should be given to 
enhancing the existing GO system rather than the Union database described in the new Article 
31a introduced by the Proposal, since the latter: 
− Does not facilitate the energy system integration, as its scope is limited to liquid and 
gaseous fuels, not covering other energy carriers such as electricity. This wil l prevent 
consumers and market operators from receiving full information to compare different 
energy solutions on an equal footing and make the right decisions on the energy mix. 
− Imposes the obligation to register the transactions together with the sustainability 
characteristics of the underlying commodities. This requirement does not take into 
account the configuration of the internal gas market, where gases are traded as 
standardised products, with no identification of their origin or other characteristics.  This 
set-up promotes liquidity in the market and guarantees the best pricing for the commodity. 
Hence, linking sustainability information to the commodity will essentially create another 
specific product which means fragmentation of the gas market at the wholesale level , 
likely followed by price fluctuations and negative implications for the security of supply.

− Does not take into account existing certification tools such as Guarantees of Origin. The 
GO schemes are already in place  in most EU Member States and are currently being 
updated to cover all energy carriers, in particular , renewable gases. Therefore, all these 
efforts and developments made at national level should be recognised by EU authorities, 
so that GOs are allowed to be used as a proof of  the origin of the energy  and its 
sustainability characteristics in the Union Database. 
− Does not show a clear purpose. It is necessary to clarif y the scope of application of the 
Union Database (e.g. target compliance, monitoring of the EU ETS obligations, etc.) as 
well as the date of entry into operation , in order to create a more certain and secure 
framework for market players. 
GOs represent a key tool for consumer information as well as for the further uptake of renewable 
and low-carbon energy. Therefore, ensuring a robust and harmonised application across the EU 
will avoid market fragmentation and a highly diverging penetration of renewable energy between 
Member States (although national particularities should be duly taken into account in the specific 
developments of GOs schemes of each Member State). 
In this regard, GOs must be trustworthy and tradeable across borders along the EU, thus creating 
a liquid market with full cross -border trading. Also, the EU-wide certification system should be 
built on the basis of the current GO system using the “book-and-claim” method, which allows to 
transfer GOs representing environmental attributes of the energy independently from the physical 
flows. This allows to reduce administrative costs for market players, facilitate trade in the 
renewable and low-carbon energy and effectively decarbonise the energy sector. 
Moreover, it would be necessary to extend the scope of the information included in GOs  (Article 
19 (7)) in order to stablish an obligation to add information on the carbon intensity and 
sustainability for all energy c arriers, which should be determined according to  a robust 
methodology based on life-cycle analysis. Following the foundation of a level -playing field, this 
methodology should enable faithful and reliable comparisons between different energy carriers. 
Hence, the ultimate goal would be to implement a “multi-purpose” tool that, in addition to including 
the guarantee of origin, also i ntegrates information on sustainability, such as environmental 
footprint, carbon footprint, etc., so that all the environmental attributes are concentrated in a single 
certificate, simplifying the trade and administrative procedures for market players. Therefore, a 
first step would be to facilitate the link between sustainability certificates and GOs (Article 19 and 
Article 30 of RED II) in order to prevent double counting of the climate value and simplify the 
usage of different certificates. 
In particular, Reganosa suggests the following amendment to the proposed Article 30 of the 
revised RED II [proposed addition in bold and underlined text, deleted text strikethrough]: 
“3. Member States shall take measures to ensure that economic operators submit reliable 
information regarding the compliance with the gre enhouse gas emissions saving criteria laid 
down in Articles 29(2) to (7) and (10) and 29a(1) and (2), and that economic operators make 
available to the relevant Member State, upon request, the data used to develop that 
information. Member States shall enab le the recording of such information  on the 
guarantees of origin issued according to Article 19 of this Directive after it has been 
verified by relevant voluntary or national schemes setting standards for the production 
of renewable fuels and recycled carbon fuels.”. 
Additionally, the GOs should be used for measuring obligations or quotas imposed on consumers 
and producers as well as to provide information in markets such as the EU ETS.

2. RECOGNITION OF THE GAS SYSTEM AS A SINGLE LOGISTICAL FACILITY 
Concerning Article 30 of RED II, the European gas infrastructure should be  explicitly considered 
as a single logistical facility for the purpose of the mass balance verification, therefore 
incentivising the cross-border trade of sustainable renewable gases such as biomethane. 
By virtue of Article 1 of the Gas Directive 2009/73/EC, biomethane injected in one Member State 
would enter the EU gas market, relying on a well-interconnected EU gas infrastructure, and could 
be traded across borders. However, this statement is not fully reflected in the  Proposal, which 
could potentially limit the cross-border trade of sustainable biomethane. 
Considering the European gas infrastructure as a single logistical facility would eliminate this 
potential trade barrier and  would ensure that  once renewable gas has been injected in one 
Member State, it could be easily delivered and withdrawn in another, without any risk of double 
counting, due to the interoperability and high interconnection  level of the European gas 
infrastructure. 
Therefore, this will allow renewable gases to enter a liquid EU gas market and get a proper pricing, 
also facilitating interaction between the Guarantees of Origin system and the EU ETS market. 
 
3. OVERALL TARGET FOR RENEWABLE GASES 
Reganosa welcomes the extension of the accounting scope of renewable fuels of non -biological 
origin (RFNBOs) beyond transport to all consumption sectors and the implementation of specific 
sub-targets for RFNBOs  in transport and industry.  However, we believe that these measures 
should be accompanied by an overall binding target at EU level by 2030 for RFBNOs and biomass 
fuels. 
This would trigger greater effort and commitment coming from Member States and market 
players, which will facilitate an accelerated materialisation of the concrete measures and plans to 
decarbonise EU energy and economy. 
Also, Member States shall set individual contributions to meet this binding EU target for renewable 
gases, ideally underpinned with national targets and integrated into their national energy and 
climate plans. This will lead Member States to design their specific pathways, measures and 
incentives according to  the national particularities and potentials, which would indeed create a 
more confident and stable framework for investors thus activating an accelerated and competitive 
deployment of renewable gases.   
On the other hand, the new Article 27a (1) introduce d by the Proposal states that “ energy from 
renewable fuels of non -biological origin shall be counted towards Member States’ shares of 
renewable energy and the targets referred to in Articles 3(1), 15a(1), 22a(1), 23(1), 24(4) and 
25(1) only if the greenhou se gas emissions savings from the use of those fuels are at least 70 
%”. 
However, it is not indicated against which reference value this threshold will be checked, so further 
clarification is necessary. 
4. ADMINISTRATIVE BURDENS AND PERMITTING 
Time is tight for complying with the EU climate and energy ambitions by 2030 and 2050, so that 
guaranteeing timely investments for rapid deployment of renewable energy projects and 
infrastructure will be paramount.

In this regard, to realise the ambitions unfolded in the Proposal  it is essential to reduce the 
administrative burden for renewable energy investors, owners and operators, and to streamline 
permitting procedures. 
Currently, there is a lack of harmonised administrative procedures for the different energy carriers, 
which may lead to uneven  and unfair deployment of renewable energy technologies and 
infrastructures and also create an opaque and ambiguous framework that could discourage 
investment actions. It is thereby crucial to define clear authorisation provisions and to homogenise 
and simplify permitting timelines in a non -discriminatory way for all renewable energy carriers , 
such as hydrogen and biomethane. 
Beyond this, support for research, development and deployment of renewable energy should be 
provided through public and EU financial instruments and grants, which should be implemented 
in a way that minimises the administrative burdens for investors. 
In this regard, Reganosa considers that Article 15 of current RED II should be further revised to 
simplify the authorisation, certification and licensing procedures in order to facilitate the 
integration and deployment of renewable energy infrastructure.

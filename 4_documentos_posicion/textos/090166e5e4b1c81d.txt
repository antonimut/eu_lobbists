1. Introduction 
Business and Science Poland welcomes the possibility to comment on the proposed Regulation on 
ensuring a level playing field for sustainable air transport  (RefuelEU Aviation). We would like to draw 
attention to several issues identified in the analysis of the draft Regulation. 
 
2. Implementation of the blending mandate 
We emphasize the need to extend the proposed deadlines for the implementation of  the blending 
mandate for sustainable aviation fuels (SAF), including in particular synthetic fuels (Annex I).  
In our opinion, this period should be extended as follows: 
• from 1 January 2030, a minimum SAF mandate of 2%. 
• from 1 January 2035, a minimum SAF mandate of 5%. 
The gradual increase in the use of SAF in the fuel mix corresponds to the increase in operating costs of 
the air carriers, which significantly reduces the competitiveness of the European aviation and the 
tourism industry. Without parallel in vestments, as well as  research and development activities, these 
regulations may have a negative impact on the recovery of aviation in Europe after the pandemic, and 
might contribute to the development of transit airports outside the European Union’s territory, where 
the provisions of the Regulation will not apply. 
The potential increase in ticket prices caused by the increase in the cost of aviation fuel may cause a 
decline in demand for travelling  by air and, consequently, a reduction in the number o f passengers 
served at European airports. 
 
3. Availability of technologies and expanding the list of feedstocks 
 
Currently, there is no efficient technology for producing Sustainable Synthetic Aviation Fuels due to the 
limited availability of the raw material, which translated to high capital expenditures. As a result, it will 
be necessary to import synthetic fuels from countries with access to cheap raw materials. We therefore 
believe that at this early stage of market development, Member States should be encouraged, but not 
obliged, to fulfil the proposed mandate of 0.7% in 2030 for synthetic fuels.  
When it comes to the lack of  technological and operational  possibilities, it is advisable to allow the 
unrealized required minimum production of synthetic fuel to be transferred to the production of other 
SAF fuels, e.g. biofuels (paragraph 3 in Article 4). 
Furthermore, we believe that no detailed analysis of the long-term availability of feedstocks has yet 
been conducted. Due to the weather conditions and the availability of feedstocks, Poland is not able to 
meet the requirements envisaged by the European Commission in terms of the production of 
alternative fuels, including synthetic fuels. Importing raw materials from third countries can lead to an 
increase in the carbon footprint of the entire supply chain. Subsequently, it may potentially  increase 
dependence on gas supplies, which in turn will prevent Poland from pursuing a competitive pricing 
policy. We therefore propose to remove the limitation on the use of Annex IX, part B feedstocks as well

4. Tankering 
 
It should be emphasized that the entire document lacks detailed analyses and clear arguments 
regarding the limitation on the use of tankering. In the impact assessment, the European Commission 
refers to the document of  July 21, 2019. In the opinion of Business & Science Poland , a new analysis 
should be performed based on the current level of air operations in Europe due to the  new market 
situation caused by the coronavirus pandemic, the effects of which will persist until at least 2025. 
Regarding the proposal to limit the use of tankering, we believe that the proposed provision setting an 
obligation for aircraft operators to refuel at least 90% of the 'required' yearly aviation fuel in EU ports 
leads to additional operating costs for carriers and may have a negative impact on airport capacity . In 
our view, the obligation should be removed for intra-EU flights up to 700 km (GCD), if the aircraft has 
already been refueled with the required SAF fuel. The additional requirement to refuel the aircraft on 
short-haul routes will extend aircraft rotation and increase its carbon footprint due to the prolonged 
waiting time for the fuel tanker. 
 
5. Administrative fines 
In the view of Business & Science Poland, the level of administrative fines proposed in Art. 11 is too high 
and unjustified.

NABU (Nature and Biodiversity Conservation Union) is the biggest environmental NGO in Germany 
with more than 800.000 members and supporters. 
NABU recognizes the European Commission’s initiative for further action on the de-
carbonisation of transport through the proposal of the Alternative Fuel Infrastructure 
Regulation (AFIR) ‘Low-emission vehicles – improving the EU’s refueling/ recharging 
infrastructure’. 
The AFIR proposal as part of the Fit for 55-package gives an important outlook on the 
necessary path to low-emission transport both in the road transport and the shipping 
sector. NABU supports the general approach of the initiative to bolster low-emission 
fuels for ships. However, the proposal shows serious shortcomings especially in the 
shipping sector which would lead to long-lasting lock-in effects for LNG with disastrous 
climate outcomes. 
With regards to the Commission’s proposal NABU would like to make the following 
comments: 
Road Transport 
We are convinced that battery electric vehicles are the most climate-effective way to 
decarbonize road transport. A reliable, dense, and user-friendly charging infrastructure 
is a key requirement for a quick change to electrified road transport.

a timely rollout of charging infrastructure

Shipping 
In the maritime sector future sustainable and emission-free shipping requires easy 
access for ship operators to sustainable fuels. A clear path on possible means to achieve 
this goal is therefore unavoidable.  
1. The establishment of mandatory refueling points for LNG in core European 
ports by 2025 would have disastrous outcomes for greenhouse gas emission 
reduction and should be removed from the Commission’s proposal.  LNG po-
tentially increases shipping GHG emissions through methane slip. Methane 
slip alone outweighs the CO2 reduction in greenhouse gas balance. Considera-
bly upstream emissions of LNG lead to worse climate balance even compared 
to heavy fuel oil or marine gasoil. Methane emissions from shipping are al-
ready on the rise2 and are counteracting climate action taken in this sector. 
Therefore, LNG cannot be seen as an alternative fuel. It can also not be consid-
ered a bridging technology, other synthetic fuels are preferable regarding 
primary energy input, handling and overall costs. Thus today’s investments in 
LNG will not only foster global warming by increasing methane emissions but 
moreover are likely to become stranded assets. EU policy must channel in-
vestments to sustainable solutions today. Methane (no matter if based on fossil 
or renewable sources) is 86 times more potent than CO2 in its climate im-
pact on a 20-year time period and has therefore no potential to support the 
decarbonisation of shipping3. Accordingly the AFIR must not set further in-
centives to invest in LNG-infrastructure and -vessels. 
2. Alternatives to fossil fuels in shipping are highly necessary and will be essen-
tial for decarbonizing the shipping sector. Favorable alternatives are e-fuels 
(ammonia, methanol, and hydrogen). Especially hydrogen and ammonia as ze-
ro-carbon e-fuels offer both climate and economic benefits, while the latter 
still faces serious challenges regarding its toxicity for eco-systems4. Stronger 
support for Zero Carbon Fuel Infrastructure is essential and would foster en-
visaged zero-carbon shipping in the future. Enhanced support for putting re-
fueling points for zero carbon fuels in place is therefore highly necessary. 
3. A binding mandate for on-shore power supply (OPS) in all ports is highly wel-
comed by NABU. It needs to be ensured and closely monitored that the elec-
tricity used is fully renewable to maximize the effect on the reduction of GHG-

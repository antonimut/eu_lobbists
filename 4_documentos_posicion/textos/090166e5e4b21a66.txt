Consultation response: Renewable Energy Directive 
E.ON welcomes the revision of the Renewable Energy Directive (REDIII) as a key policy lever to 
accelerate the deployment of renewables and their mainstreaming in end-use sectors. We support the 
European Commission's proposal to increase the common target for the share of renewables in gross 
final energy consumption from 32% to 40% by 2030.  
Electricity infrastructure : The necessary expansion of renewable energies also requires substa ntial 
investment in grid modernization and digitization.  We believe this need is not fully recognized in the 
revised RED. Sufficiently dimensioned and intelligently controlled smart grids are an essential 
prerequisite for integrating increasing shares of RES into the energy system and will thus contribute to 
fulfilling the obligation in Art. 20a (1) . That is why we see the digitization of the network as a critical 
element for efficiently achieving the 55% GHG reduction target  by 2030 . Lengthy permitting 
procedures are an important bottleneck for infrastructure deployment, as with renewable expansion , 
and should be addressed accordingly in the text.  
Bioenergy: To reach the ambitious RES target, all available options must be used. Therefore, the tabled 
provisions concerning stricter requirements for eligible and CO2 -neutral biomass are 
counterproductive. Especially, considering that bioenergy - in contrast to the dominant renewable 
energy sources wind and solar - can provide a significant contribution to security of supply. Hence, 
E.ON advocates to first fully implement REDII bioenergy criteria and revise them if they prove to b e 
insufficient for biodiversity protection. 
RES support: E.ON shares the Commission's view that market -financed renewable generation shall 
provide a significant contribution to decarbonization. Therefore, we support the approach of quickly 
removing administrative and regulatory barriers to PPAs. However, additional RES are needed that 
have not yet reached full market viability. Therefore, the Directive should be amended  in a way that 
RES support (if still needed) must be based on Contracts for Difference thus guaranteeing that it is 
proportionate. The revision should also address the fact that support schemes should not be financed 
through levies on the electricity price, which is a strong disincentive for electrification of end-uses. 
Guarantees of origin: E.ON shares the Commission's proposal that both subsidized and unsubsidized 
renewable energy should be eligible to generate guarantees of origin, although care must be taken to 
ensure that the revenues thus generated are subtracted from the support schemes to existing plants. 
RES in buildings, heating and cooling , district heating : E.ON supports the proposed indicative sub -
target of 49% in buildings and a mandatory average 1.1 pp increase of renewables in heating and 
cooling. We expect both targets to be challenging to reach. The availability of bioenergy to achieve this 
level of ambition is critical, therefore measures that can lead to shrinking the market should be avoided. 
E.ON sees the supply of local and district heating - especially in urban areas - as an important approach 
to decarbonize the heating sector and we welcome the renewables and waste heat sub-targets of an 
average 2.1 pp increase . We do not support the mandatory connection of waste heat and renewable 
heat in grids above 25 MWth - even under the restrictions made in the Commission´s proposal. This 
obligation is not needed, since heating grid operators have a strong self-interest in including CO2-free 
energy in their grids in order to promote decarbonization and fulfil the obligations of Article 24. 
E-Mobility: With regard to the promotion of e -mobility, E.ON sees a clear need for improvement. We 
consider the switch to CO2 reductions as a decarbonization indicator for the trans port sector to be 
appropriate. However, in our view the target of 13% is not ambitious  enough. The target should be at

least 20% in order to be able to achieve the overarching 55% GHG-reduction target. Furthermore, we 
welcome the European expansion of the quota trading system, which has made a considerable 
contribution to increasing the attractiveness of e -mobility in Germany. However, limiting the quotas 
only to commercially used electricity is counterproductive.  
Hydrogen: For the ramp up  of a hydrogen market, it is of significant importance to create reliable 
demand. In order to establish a positive investment environment, E.ON strongly supports the target of 
at least 50% of green hydrogen in the industry sector until 2030. This will create a market driven 
development of electrolyzer capacity and import co-operations. The growth of a liquid hydrogen market 
needs to be accomplished at minimum economic costs. Investors eagerly await to take final investment 
decisions in electrolyzers to fulfill this expected demand, predicated on a clear and pragmatic definition 
of RFNBOs in the Delegated Act based on REDII. The timely adoption  of this act  and subsequent 
extension proposed in REDIII is critical to achieve the renewable energy targets.

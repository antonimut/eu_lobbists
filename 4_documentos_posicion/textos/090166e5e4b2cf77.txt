 
Aena welcomes the fit for 55 normative package proposal and supports most of the 
measures described on it for reducing net greenhouse gas emissions by at least 55% 
by 2030 compared to 1990 and would like to thank the European Commission for the 
opportunity to provide feedback on this topic. 
Our comments to the proposal can be found as follows: 
Regarding article 6, airports are not the only subjects responsible for the provision of 
infrastructure for the delivery, storage and uplifting of SAF and therefore the directive 
should set out obligations to all the relevant parties involved. For example, In Spain, a 
single airport operator (Aena) manages all the airports included in the regulation, and 
the fuel logistics at these airports is carried out through a common logistics network not 
owned by Aena. 
The most efficient way of supplying SAF to the Spanish airport network would therefore 
be the injection into the logistics network by the refineries, and the metering and on -
boarding of the SAF by means of a "mass balance" system. 
This solution would resolve the possible effects of refuelling on turnaround times during 
peak demand periods, which could reduce the efficiency of operations. 
As such, Aena does not support the provision according to which Union airports 
shall be responsible for the provision of infrastructure for the delivery, storage and 
uplifting of SAF for two reasons:  
 
 Many airport operators are not owning nor operating the fuel supply 
infrastructure on their sites. Responsibility for the provision of such 
infrastructure can thus not be placed on airport operators only.  
 The key advantage of drop-in SAF is that it can be used with the infrastructure 
already in place for fossil kerosene.  
In accordance with the aforementioned, we propose the following amendments to 
Article 6:   
Article 6 
Obligations of Union airports of the relevant parties to provide the infrastructure  
Parties involved in fuel supply at Union airports shall take necessary measures to 
facilitate the access of aircraft operators to aviation fuels containing shares of 
sustainable aviation fuels in accordance with Annex I and, shall provide the 
infrastructure necessary for the delivery, storage and uplifting of such fuels.  
Where aircraft operators report difficulties to the European Union Aviation Safety 
Agency (‘the Agency’) in accessing aviation fuels containing sustainable aviation fuels 
at a given Union airport for lack of adequate airport infrastructure, the Agency may 
request the parties involved in fuel supply at the Union airport to provide the 
information necessary to prove compliance with paragraph 1. The Union airport 
parties concerned shall provide the information without undue delay.  
The Agency shall assess the information received and inform the Commission if such 
information allows to conclude that the Union airport parties involved does do not

Aena also agrees with the overall ambition level of the proposed mandates and 
supports the principle of dedicated sub-targets for synthetic fuels. 
Regarding the obligation for fuel suppliers to meet the mandate through physical 
SAF supply at each Union airport, Aena strongly disagrees. As an airport 
manager, we think it would be very difficult for some airports to deploy the needed 
infrastructure and provide the required physical SAF. Therefore, we think that the most 
reasonable solution is to allow for compliance based on a book and claim system, 
similar to the Guarantees of Origin for renewable electricity 
Also, one of the risk we have identified regarding the impact of the normative package 
is the one that follows: 
In their current form, the proposals would lead to significant carbon leakage and a loss 
of connectivity of the EU. Whereas EU carriers faced significant cost increases on long 
haul flights departing from the EU, competing non-EU carriers will not even fall within 
the scope   of the new regulation. Higher prices for EU carriers will create an incentive 
for passengers to book cheaper flights with non-EU carriers via non EU-hubs.  
EU network carriers and EU hubs would suffer from a massive competitive 
disadvantage compared with airlines and airports from outside the EU – without 
effectively reducing CO2. As EU airlines and airports, we represent the backbone of the 
EU ‘s global connectivity. The European aviation sector stands in fierce competition 
with non-EU hubs and airlines which already benefit from subsidies and build their 
business case on weak labour and social security standards. Thus, competitive 
neutrality needs to be the key criterion in the overhaul of Fit for 55.  
 
In order to enable the market ramp-up of SAF without competitive distortion 
precautions must be taken to avoid carbon leakage and to maintain connectivity. 
Therefore, the legislation should be improved in a competitive neutral way for the ramp-
up of SAF. Once a robust level playing field will be established an even more ambitious 
SAF-mandate may become possible.  
 
The competitive distortion risk should be further assessed and mitigation mechanisms 
explored, without compromising the overall ambition of the proposed policy framework. 
 
Finally, we believe that fit for 55 package should also be aligned with the Destination 
2050 initiative, which stablishes the SAF availability estimations in a 6% in 2030 and a 
2,2% of PtL.

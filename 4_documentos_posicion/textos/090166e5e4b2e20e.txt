Response of NGVA Europe on the European Commission 
consultation on the Alternative Fuels Infrastructure 
Regulation proposal 
NGVA Europe welcomes the revision of the current Alternative Fuels Infrastructure Directive, 
which should align with the increased GHG emission reduction targets of the EU. NGVA Europe 
is convinced that the only way of achieving a quick decarbonisation of road transpo rt is to 
support all existing solutions, including biomethane (both in compressed and liquified forms 
– bioCNG and bioLNG). 
As recognised by the European Commission in the Impact Assessment of the AFIR proposal , 
“biogas and e -gases use the same refuelling infrastructure as natural gas. Fossil natural gas 
can therefore be increasingly blended and phased out with low -carbon and renewable fuels 
(biogas and renewable synthetic e -gas) and thus fully contribute to the climate -neutrality 
objective” (p.19). 
The CNG and LNG refuelling station network is therefore a potent enabler for increasing the 
uptake of bioCNG/bioLNG and therefore of  a drastic reduction in GHG emissions  of road 
transport. Earlier this year, Europe’s gas fuelling station network has reached 4 000 CNG and 
400 LNG stations. This network is crucial to support the decarbonisation of the 1,5 million gas 
vehicles which currently compose the European fleet, as well as the foreseen 13,2 million gas 
vehicles that NGVA Europe projects for 2030 . Biomethane (bioCNG and bioLNG) already 
represents more than 20% of the gas used in the European vehicles fleet, which contributes 
to reduce GHG emissions by more than 40% compared to gasoline (on a Well -to-Wheel 
basis). 
The CNG and LNG refuelling network is not yet complete across the EU, and indeed there 
remain significant geographical gaps. In order to ensure that the full climate advantage of 
biomethane is realised across the EU, NGVA Europe has the following recommendations  for 
improving the AFIR proposal: 
• Create a coherent Fit-for-55 Package: AFIR, RED, and CO2 standards for vehicles should 
be carefully aligned. Infrastructure supports the continued growth of renewable fuels 
envisioned by RED, and as such the AFIR should continue to  support refuelling 
infrastructures such as bioCNG and bioLNG, which should be translated into ambitious 
CNG and LNG targets within the AFIR. In addition, the new sub-categories of alternative 
fuels introduced in the European Commission’s proposal are not based on any clear 
legal criteria and bring additional unnecessary complexity to the overall FF55 
framework. Instead, the definition of ’alternative fuels ’ should rather mirror the 
definitions of the REDII, and clearly include advanced bio fuels such as bioCNG and 
bioLNG within its scope.

• Following the same logic,  the AFIR should not anticipate the results of the  revisions 
of the CO2 standards for cars & vans as well as for heavy -duty vehicles  by 
prematurely phasing-out support to CNG and LNG infrastructure. These legislations will 
define new targets for cutting GHG emissions of new vehicles, and it is therefore crucial that 
the AFIR creates refuelling networks that support all solutions in achieving  updated CO2 
ambitions. 
• Reintroduce provisions to support the development and maintenance of CNG 
refuelling stations. While the number of CNG stations is growing steadily, distribution 
across Europe remains highly uneven, as underlined in the Staff Working document 
accompanying the AFIR proposal (p.15). Additional investments are therefore needed 
to build  and maintain a  comprehensive network of CNG refuelling stations that is 
necessary to increase the uptake of bioCNG in road transport.  
• Extend the support to LNG refuelling statio ns to at least 2030 . With a high blend of 
bioLNG achievable in the medium term, LNG is a mature, affordable and necessary 
technology to accelerate the decarbonisation of the heavy-duty vehicles, since it is the 
only immediately viable alternative fuel to d iesel for this specific sector . While the 
LNG refuelling network is developing quickly, increasing demand for bioLNG , and a 
rapidly growing fleet of  LNG trucks in Europe  means there is a strong need for 
continued network development. It is therefore crucial to extend the development of 
the LNG stations across the European Union until at least 2030.

 
Position on the Alternative Fuels Infrastructure Regulation 
 
We appreciate the EU’s efforts in leading the energy transition and tackling GHG emissions in the maritime sector. 
Given the cross -border nature of this sector, it is the right approach to address its GHG  impact on EU level and 
thereby set an example for the international shipping sector to follow.  
 
Technological neutrality vs the risk of technology lock-in  
An important aspect of reducing GHG emissions is the availability of alternative fuels. The take -up of such fuels 
requires the development of necessary infrastructure and  creation of an appropriate policy framework . In line 
with the other proposals of Fit for 55, the EU should introduce a goal-oriented, technology-neutral framework for 
alternative fuels infrastructure. Such a framework should not allow for preferential treatment of any technology 
unless it concerns the phasing out of fossil energy. 
 
In its recitals, the proposed Alternative Fuels Infrastructure Regulation suggests that for all the transport modes 
there shall be one instrument that considers a variety of alternative fuels . It also states the requirement of  
increasing blending  of fossil fuels  with or replacement by renewable fuels , such as bio -methane, advanced 
biofuels or renewable and low -carbon synthetic gaseous and liquid fuels . However, following the assumption that 
those fuels can be distributed, stored and used with the existing infrastructure or where necessary with infrastructure 
of the same kind, the scope of the draft Regulation for the maritime sector is greatly limited to increasing availability 
of LNG and electricity supply in core EU ports.  Even though it acknowledges that LNG increasingly needs to be 
decarbonized by blending/substituting with liquefied biomethane or renewable and low-carbon synthetic gaseous 
e-fuels, it only creates a further path dependence for LNG, instead of ensuring technological neutrality.  
 
If the maritime industry should decarbonize quickly and be able to meet the new GHG reduction targets of Fit for 
55, the development of and investment in new in frastructure and retrofits for the technologies with the 
greatest GHG abatement potential should be promoted. While sustainable biofuels, which as drop-in fuels can 
be handled with existing infrastructure, are a viable technology already, we see a lot of GHG reduction potential 
for renewable and synthetic methanol and ammonia , among others. These technologies would need additional 
infrastructure. For example, there is a rising demand for biomethanol as low carbon transition fuel . Due to the 
substantially lower flash point of (bio) -methanol, tanks accommodating this fuel need to be fitted with special 
systems to lower fire risk. The same applies for bunkering infrastructure: The bunkering syst em will have to deal 
with the potentially hazardous flashpoint of the fuel. 
 
In a nutshell, we would  like to ask the Commission to take a neutral standpoint and ensure technological  
neutrality in the draft Regulation. 
 
Closing statement 
FinCo Fuel Group and GoodFuels, frontrunners in decarbonizing the transport sector , are of the opinion that to 
maximize GHG emission reductions, there may be no preferential treatment of any technology, unless it concerns 
the phasing out of fossil energy . We invite the members of the Commission for a dialogue to further discuss our 
viewpoints and solutions to propel decarbonization in the transport sector. 
 
Thank you for your consideration.

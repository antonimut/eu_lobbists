 
 
 
 
Fit for 55 – revision of the Renewable Energy Directive 
(RED) 
Position statement of the German automotive industry 
Berlin, November 2021 
1. Introduction  
 
With “Fit for 55,” the European Commission has presented a comprehensive package aimed 
at reshaping the European Union’s climate policy. The German automotive industry with its 
more than 800,000 employees supports the goal of making road traffic climate-neutral by 
2050 at the latest. We are driving the change by focusing on innovations and technologies. 
One element of the Commission’s proposal is a revision of the Renewable Energy Directive 
(RED), which governs the share of renewable energy in the road transport sector and will 
increase it over time. 
 
 
2. Assessment  
 
The proposal for the revision of the Renewable Energy Directive (RED) is a step in the right 
direction, as it focuses, for the first time, on the reduction of greenhouse gases (GHGs) in 
fuels and in the electricity used to power electric vehicles. We welcome the switch to a GHG 
quota, but the draft is very cautious with regard to the specified targets and, in the view of the 
VDA, is not ambitious enough given the urgent need for reducing carbon emissions in the 
road transport sector. Through appropriate provisions in the RED proposal, the large number 
of existing vehicles with combustion engines could make a greater contribution to the sector ’s 
reduction of CO2 emissions. The electrification of new cars, which the automotive industry is 
emphatically promoting, is already making a significant contribution on the path to climate 
neutrality. 
 
The targets for the reduction of greenhouse gases in the RED proposal therefore need to be 
raised: According to VDA calculations, a share of renewable fuels of around 30 percent is 
needed by 2030, which corresponds to a significantly higher reduction in GHG emissions 
than the proposed target of 13 percent in the current draft. Instead, the GHG reduction 
targets, which encompass green electricity as well as renewable fuels, should be raised to at 
least 40 percent, while crediting of the electricity used for vehicle charging should reflect the 
actual reduction. In addition, the RED proposal should also specify ambitious targets for the 
period after 2030 in order to comprehensively define the road transport sector’s contribution 
to climate neutrality. 
 
We welcome the provisions in the RED proposal that take stock of the dramatic increase in 
the importance of electricity as an energy source for road transport and aim to promote it 
across sectors. 
 
In the VDA’s view, the following points should be taken into account in the further 
development of the RED: 
 
Fuels: 
 
• The proposed 2.6 percent minimum target for eFuels/RFNBO (renewable fuels of non-
biological origin) should be brought forward significantly in order to improve their 
competitiveness. By 2030, the target should be multiple times higher.  
• RFNBO production should be supported by interim targets (for instance for 2026) in order 
to regularly assess whether the objectives have been met and to increase the investment 
incentive for the petroleum industry. Only a rapid increase in the minimum targets will 
provide a sufficiently strong incentive to bring synthetic fuels onto the market and ensure

that the large number of existing internal combustion engine vehicles can contribute to 
the climate protection efforts. 
• The Commission should lay down clear rules for determining the actual GHG reductions 
achieved through RFNBO, for example by establishing suitable standard values and 
fossil fuel comparators. 
• RFNBO production plants should be able to source the green electricity they need 
directly via so-called power purchase agreements (PPAs). As in the case of 
electromobility, there should be as little red tape as possible in order to get the market up 
and running as quickly as possible. For example, the sustainability criteria should be 
flexible enough to allow for a degree of freedom in the delegated acts, notably as regards 
the transition periods. Furthermore, the depreciation rules (GHG emission allowances) 
should be extended. 
• While the Commission rightly proposes to limit the use of conventional biofuels to 
7 percent in total, 
• The envisaged introduction of B10 fuel does not go far enough, as this would require 
thorough and detailed compatibility tests. Instead, we favor the introduction of drop -in 
fuels, such as R33, which are compatible with existing standards and have a significantly 
greater potential for reducing carbon emissions.  
 
 
Electricity: 
 
• We support the ability of charging infrastructure operators to generate credits for the 
share of renewable electricity for vehicles. This instrument should be maintained. 
• We welcome the higher weighting of green charging electricity (fossil fuel comparator 
ECF(e)) proposed by the Commission. In the event of extensive use of green charging 
electricity, however, it should be avoided that the GHG targets can be met even with 
small amounts of renewable fuels, as the latter are indispensable for the urgently needed 
decarbonization of existing vehicles. Consequently, GHG reductions from the use of 
renewable electricity for vehicle charging that exceed certain, yet to be determined levels 
should be added to the overall emissions reduction targets in the coming years. This 
could be based on the GHG target adjustment mechanism in the German Federal 
Immission Control Act (BImschG, Section 37h), which was amended as part of the 
national implementation of RED II. 
• We support the proposals for bi-directional charging. Support for bi-directional charging 
must be targeted at charging points with long idle times.  
• However, we take a critical view of the required disclosure of the battery status and other 
battery data.  
• Under the GDPR, customers always retain ownership of their data. Customers will only 
consent to the use of their batteries and battery data for grid-friendly integration and 
charging if incentives are available in return.  
• Any disclosure of interfaces and the internal data of battery management systems would 
impinge on the IP rights of OEMs.  
• Controlling the power flows of electric vehicles connected to the grid does not  require 
such universal access to battery data. We favor decentralized, intelligent control systems 
based on open standards that allow customers to take advantage of the services offered 
by flexibility providers. 
 
One thing is certain: Reducing carbon emissions by electrifying new vehicles alone will not be 
enough to achieve the goal of climate neutrality in road transport. The underambitious 
provisions in the RED proposal, which leave out important contributions to climate protection, 
greatly increase the risk that the targets for reducing the carbon emissions of the road 
transport sector will not be met. The potential contributions of existing vehicles to climate 
protection remain largely untapped, even though they are essential for achieving th e EU’s 
overarching climate targets.

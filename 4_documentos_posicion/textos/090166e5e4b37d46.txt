FEDIOL draft position in response to RED II review 
 
FEDIOL, representing the EU vegetable oil and protein meal industry, welcomes the possibility to 
provide feedback on the review of Directive (EU) 2018/2001 on the promotion of energy from 
renewable sources. 
With the occasion, we would like to reiterate the sector’s priorities for an ambitious policy framework 
for renewables in transport, in line with the EU Green Deal’s objectives. 
Higher ambition for renewable energy in transport 
While supporting the direction taken by the Commission’s proposal via its overall obligation on fuel 
suppliers to decrease the greenhouse gas intensity of transport fuels, FEDIOL considers that the 
suggested target of 13% by 2030 is still not sufficient. 
This would translate into a  derived renewable energy share in transport (RES -T) of 24 -28%, which 
certainly represents an improvement from the 14% share set in the RED II but could be further 
strengthened to en sure enough space for the different types of renewable fuels and for renewable 
electricity. Also, we consider that Member States should still be given the possibility to choose whether 
to express their national transport incorporation obligations in volume /energy or in GHG emissions 
savings. As such, we call for an increase of the GHG emissions savings target to at least 16%. 
Looking at the limited scale of development of alternative technologies, the above-mentioned targets 
cannot be met without the contri bution of sustainable crop-based biofuels. For this reason, F EDIOL 
calls for a clearer preservation of the 7% cap expressed as an  EU-wide limit  to the  gross final 
consumption of energy, by eliminat ing the reference introduced in the RED II  to a cap set at  2020 
consumption levels (+1%) in each Member State. 
This approach would acknowledge the need for a continued role for crop -based biofuels. These fuels 
have proven to be beneficial in improving food security and have shown not to have any significant 
effects on global agricultural prices, nor to restrict the supply of food products. Moreover, their 
decarbonisation potential makes them an important contributor towards the EU objectives of reducing 
the share of fossil fuels and the GHG emissions in the transport sector and their preservation can 
facilitate the deployment of more advanced alternatives in the longer term.  Yet, in the current 
proposal, there is still  an expectation that conventional biofuels will not be needed in the long term 
and the possibility is maintained for Member States to lower their overall contribution accordingly. 
FEDIOL strongly opposes the reference under Article 26 of the RED III text to this possibility to reduce 
the transport greenhouse gas intensity reduction target if the share of conventional biofuels is also 
lowered. By referring to a standard 50% GHG savings for all crop -based biofuels, the Commission

Additional provisions 
The RED III transport target, if expressed as a GHG intensity reduction target, makes it unnecessary to 
use multipliers to promote certain renewable energy sources. In line with this, FEDIOL welcomes the 
removal of the existing multipliers, which will prevent the reporting of progress towards the renewable 
energy targets to be artificially inflated. 
FEDIOL also welcomes and supports the proposal to introduce B10 as diesel standard grade across the 
EU. The rollout of B10 (at a minimum) as the diesel st andard grade across the EU is an essential 
instrument to decarbonise the existing road vehicle fleet, especially for passenger cars. Moreover, the 
promotion of higher blends for dedicated fleets of heavy vehicles like buses and trucks should continue 
in parallel. The cost of adding a specific B7 pump introduced by the protection grade requirement may 
retard the rollout of B10 and is not necessary. 
Sustainability criteria 
The Commission’s proposal also includes some changes to the sustainability criteria for agricultural 
biomass, under which the elimination of the possibility of using regional GHG values (NUTS2) for 
cultivation is included. We would like to voice our concer n on this issue, which could prove heavily 
detrimental to some Member States  where the agricu ltural sector is still fragmented into relatively 
smaller farms providing their products to the biofuel production units.  
While the process of review of these values should be simplified and clearly defined in the legislation 
procedures, their simple elimination does not appear to be a realistic and practical  alternative. It will 
create additional comp lications for farmers and throughout the whole v alue chain,  by triggering 
important administrative burden  due to the request for each farmer to calculate their own specific 
GHG emissions and subsequently for all downstream operators to keep a much more complex mass 
balance where GHG values of inputs and outputs are clearly identified. In short, with one GHG value 
per farm, a medium-sized oil producer might be obliged to manage hundreds of different GHG values 
for the same oil, as it gets raw material from hundre ds of farms annually. FEDIOL therefore calls for 
the preservations of paragraphs 2, 3 and 4 under Article 31 and advocates instead for a more flexible 
system that allow  to take into account farmers’ efforts to adapt to more sustainable agriculture 
practices in the future.

We would also call for a review of points C and D under Annex VI of the Directive, enlisting respectively 
the disaggregated and total default values for biomass fuels, with a view to broadening its scope to 
other Annex IX feedstocks that are curren tly not in the list. This would provide a fairer and more 
realistic representation of the GHG values among different advanced feedstocks under part A and B of 
Annex IX. 
A harmonised legislative framework 
Finally, we would like to reiterate the importance o f ensuring policy coherence across the different 
legislative proposals under the “Fit for 55 ” package. As such, the proposal for the inclusion of road 
transport under the EU ETS system should be carefully assessed. The main compliance instrument to 
reduce emissions in transport should remain the Effort Sharing Regulation, as allowing the pricing of 
CO2 for road transport fuels to depend on the existing emissions trading system would be 
counterproductive and costly for EU consumers.  
Similarly, the proposals on the FuelEU Maritime and ReFuelEU Aviation regulatory frameworks should 
include provisions reflecting the possibility to allow the blending of conventional biofuels in those 
transport sectors.  
Lastly, the review proposal for the  Regulation on CO 2 Emission Performance Standards for Cars and 
Vans, if maintained, would include a reference to the end of the use of combustion engines by 2035. 
This would hamper any further development of renewable liquid fuels in road transport and would fail 
to recognise the crucial contribution of renewable and low-carbon liquid fuels to the decarbonisation 
of transport and the value of multiple technological pathways to achieve both 2030 and 2050 targets. 
Considering the Commission’s intention to shift the transport target from a renewable energy share to 
a GHG savings target, we believe it is even more crucial to establish a harmonised “Well-to-Wheel / 
Life Cycle” approach for GHG calculations for all transport technologies. This would set a more solid 
and reliable basis for the accounting of CO2 emissions, as opposed to the mere accounting of tailpipe 
emissions (Tank-to-Wheel), which triggers the misleading use of concepts such as “zero emissions” or 
“CO2 neutral” for electric vehicles.

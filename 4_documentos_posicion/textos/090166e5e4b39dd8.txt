ACCIONA’S VIEW ON THE COMMISSION’S PROPOSAL FOR A R EVISION OF THE RENEWABLE 
ENERGY DIRECTIVE 
Acciona welcomes the opportunity to contribute to t he consultation on the revision of the 
Renewable Energy Directive and we hereby offer a few very brief comments and suggestions on 
the proposal, which we believe would improve the text, the most relevant ones trying to adapt 
the provisions for biomass to fit to the specificities of Spanish biomass: 
1 Provisions for biomass  
1.1  Amendment to article 3.3.a):  
It is necessary to provide for exemptions from the general provision, when no other use than 
producing energy is available, and it could also ha ve positive impacts, such as the ones on the 
prevention of fires:  
MS shall grant no support for the use of saw logs, ve neer logs, stumps and roots to produce 
energy, unless no other use is available in accorda nce with the legal framework for forest 
management.  
1.2  Amendment to article 3.3.b):  
The planned elimination of incentives  to the production of electricity from forest biomass from 
31 December 2026 should avoid any negative retroact ive effects. Therefore, this provision 
should not apply to biomass plants already in opera tion before the date of entry into force of 
the revision of the Renewable Energy Directive. 
The rationale behind this proposal is: 
- Operating biomass plants are already complying with requirements set in article 29, related 
to sustainability and greenhouse gas emissions saving. 
- The location of these plants cannot be modified. 
- CCS systems are far from reaching commercial stage and remain subject to considerable 
uncertainties, as it was stated in the “In-depth analysis in support on the COM (2018) 773: a 
Clean Planet for all ” (

support-com2018-773-clean-planet-all-european-strategic-long-term-vision_en ):

- Additionally, the investment needed for incorporating the CCS system into existing biomass

Besides all the aforesaid, the proposed amendment w ould collide with the general provisions 
regarding support schemes contained in the revised Directive and, in particular, with the general 
principle of stability of the incentives (Article 6 : Stability of financial support) . The new 
Directive should abide to, and not contradict, that  general rule that all Member States have 
already agreed to comply with.  
Predictability of financial support is indispensable for renewable electricity producers that have 
built their business case according to the legislat ion in force at the time of the investment. 
Therefore, for the sake of legal certainty, and in order to avoid any potential litigations arising 
from legal changes to the support schemes, no provi sions retroactively affecting the incentive 
systems already in place should be adopted.  
1.3  Amendment to article 29: 
- Paragraph 6, (a)(iv) and (b)(iv): harvesting of stumps and roots should be excluded from this 
requirement, when it is already subject to authoriz ations by the competent national 
authorities (for example, as it is in Spain). 
Sustainable forest management practices specified by Member States have better chances 
to effectively minimize possible impacts on biodive rsity and soil quality, so they should be 
prioritized when they are implemented. 
- Paragraph 10: the retroactive application of  the requirement on GHG emissions savings 
threshold to installations starting operation befor e 2025 (and not to installations starting 
operation from 1 January 2021 until 31 December 2025, as is stated in the Renewable Energy 
Directive presently in force) should be avoided.  
The retroactive application of this requirement would adversely affect the ability of projects 
to deliver on the European climate goals and will surely push out of the market a significant 
number of existing installations. 
Therefore, and for the same reasons provided in 1.2. above, this provision should not apply 
to biomass plants already in operation before the date of entry into force of the revision of 
the Renewable Energy Directive. 
1.4  Amendment to article 30: 
It is important to take into account that hardly an y voluntary or national scheme is currently 
implemented, and there are not enough approved auditors for carrying out the verification, thus

making it very difficult to demonstrate compliance with sustainability and GHG emissions saving 
criteria. The schemes presently implemented are not  prepared to certify all types of biomass 
and are not suited to the specific particularities of each Member State.  
Therefore, at least one national certification scheme should be approved before the beginning 
of the compliance period, tailored to the country’s specificities. A transitional period should be 
included during which operators are exempted from c ompliance with sustainability and GHG 
emissions saving criteria until a national certific ation scheme or any voluntary scheme is 
approved and there are enough valid auditors. 
As regards  new paragraph 6 : simplified national verification schemes should be established for 
every biomass plant, and not only for installations  with a total rated thermal input between 5 
and 10 MW. 
The verification of the fulfilment of the sustainab ility and GHG emissions criteria should not 
result in an undue administrative and economic burden for operators, and use should be made 
of national authorisations already achieved by the installations.   
2 Provisions for Offshore Wind Development.  
ACCIONA welcomed the release of the Offshore Renewa ble Strategy (ORES) on 19 December 
2020, as well as the assumptions and amendment to t he Article 9 of RED II that the revision 
proposes.  
In addition, we would propose to include two amendm ents to the Directive, that would serve 
important goals set out in the ORES: 
2.1   Amendment to Article 4.3:  
The ORES recognized the need for support mechanisms for offshore technologies, including the 
most mature. However, there was no clear mention of  an specific mechanism –  contract for 
differences, CfD - advocated by the renewable sector.  
We propose to explicitly include it as one of the different forms of support schemes. 
2.2  Amendment to Article 3:  
The proposed revision of the Directive remarks the need to work together across borders at sea-
basin level, advocating for joint offshore energy p lanning, under which Member States must 
jointly define and agree to cooperate on the amount  of offshore renewable generation to be 
deployed.  
We consider that this may be sufficient for “mature  countries” in offshore wind, more 
specifically, countries of Northern Europe. On the other hand, new entrant  countries, such as 
Spain, lag behind them.  
We would ask for  the introduction of an amendment to the Directive, calling for specific 
offshore wind goals at country-level – Spain still does not break down wind targets between

onshore and offshore wind -, especially at countrie s where high potential is assumed. These 
specific offshore wind objectives should be reflected in the updated national energy and climate 
plans that will be submitted in 2023 and 2024 pursuant to Regulation (EU) 2018/1999. 
3 Credits for supplying renewable energy to the transport sector. 
The replacement of article 25, introduced by article 1 (14) of the Revision, in which a mechanism 
is established allowing fuel suppliers in their ter ritory to exchange credits for supplying 
renewable energy to the transport sector, particularly operators supplying renewable electricity 
to electric vehicles (EVs) through public recharging stations, should also cover the supply model 
through battery swapping. 
The credit mechanism introduced to promote electrom obility needs to be technologically 
neutral and consider all the different options to r efuel the energy source of EVs. Besides 
recharging stations, another way is through mechani cally swapping the discharged batteries 
with fully charged batteries (“ battery swapping ”, “ mechanical refuelling ” or “ mechanical 
recharging ”). The discharged batteries will either be charged using renewable electricity at the 
service station or centrally collected and charged. 
The Directive should make clear that operators recharging exchangeable batteries for EVs using 
renewable electricity receive credits as well.

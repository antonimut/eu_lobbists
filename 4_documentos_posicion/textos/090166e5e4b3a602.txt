Finnish Energy consultation response on the revision of the 
Renewable Energy Directive 
 
Finnish Energy welcomes the opportunity to comment on the Fit for 55 climate package. We 
strongly support the EU’s climate targets for 2030 and 2050, and we are also committed to 
Finland’s carbon neutrality target for 2035. The energy and climate policy initiatives published 
by the Commission in July set the Europe to the right path towards climate neutrality.  
 
We would like to stress the need of eliminating overlapping regulatory requirements. We are 
particularly concerned about the multi-level target setting and very detailed regulation of the 
production and use of electricity and district heating. This regulation is hampering the cost-
effectiveness and predictability of climate policy. The focus should be in changes that will have 
an effective impact. We do not fully share the Commission's view on the need for a number of 
sub-targets in the Directive. The sub-targets form a complex regulatory framework in which it 
seems challenging to determine and calculate the effectiveness of individual measures.  
 
Achieving ambitious emission reduction targets requires all low-carbon technologies (e.g. all 
waste heat and nuclear energy) to be available, without ranking them or favouring specific 
technologies. Technology neutrality should be the overarching theme of the EU climate 
framework.  
 
The Commission's proposal for the revision of the Renewable Energy Directive contains  a large 
number of detailed obligations, the evaluation of which should be carried out carefully, taking 
into account the different starting points of the various Member States. In the case of Finland, 
this can be seen, for example, in the heating market, including district heating and cooling. In 
this area, Finland is already well advanced in the use of both renewable energy and waste heat 
without the need for mandatory regulation. The flexibility options included in the sub -targets 
for heating and district heating are a very important detail which should be retained in the 
Directive. 
 
The Commission's proposal also contains positive elements, for example streamlining of 
licensing processes, promotion of electrification and ensuring the functioning of the e lectricity 
market, including, for example, the possibility of PPAs more widely throughout the Union.  
 
We believe it is crucial for the decarbonisation of societies to enable the use of existing low -
carbon electricity mix in the production of renewable fuels of non-biological origins. The 
Commission's proposal is a step in the right direction by removing the full requirement to build 
new generation capacity for the share that counts as renewable. There are still a number of 
issues to be resolved in the implementation of the proposal. 
 
The Commission proposes more changes to the sustainability criteria of bioenergy than would 
be necessary. The implementation of the previous directive is still pending and there is no 
information on the effects of the current risk-based approach. The constant updating of 
directives creates uncertainty for the industry. 
 
• Finnish Energy does not agree with the proposed amendments on the application of the 
cascade principle to the use of biomass in the Member States in the form it is currently 
proposed. It could lead to unnecessary restrictions on the energy use of forest biomass 
and not taking account of regional differences between Member States.

• The application of the cascade principle is not a technical detail that could be regulated 
by delegated legislation. In minimum, the content of such delegated act should be 
precisely described and limited to contain a clarification on subsidies and support 
schemes. 
• Finnish Energy stresses the importance of retaining the newly implemented risk-based 
approach on sustainability at the core of any amendments on the sustainability criteria. 
It is not clear how adding no-go-areas for forest biomass fits in this. All additional 
criteria should be implemented in such a way that a risk-based approach is still possible 
without a batch-by-batch demonstrating. The increasing administrative burden and 
costs for operators must be kept to a minimum. 
• We have reservations about extending the greenhouse gas reduction criterion to 
existing installations, especially in combination with the change in the scope from 20 
MW to 5 MW. 
• The extension of the scope is linked to the Commission's proposal to require Member 
States to create a simplified scheme for small operators. The creation of such a simple 
system is highly desirable and should cover all installations which, as a result of the 
extension of the scope, will in future have to prove the sustainability of the biomass 
used, not just some of them, as in the Commission's proposal. In reality, the 
implementation of a simple system must remain simple and not disproportionately 
increase the administrative burden on operators.

CO2 Value Europe’s feedback on the European 
Commission’s proposal for a regulation on 
ensuring a level playing field for sustainable air 
transport (‘ReFuelEU Aviation’) 
CO2 Value Europe (hereinafter ‘CVE’) is the European association dedicated to Carbon Capture and 
Utilisation (hereinafter ‘CCU’) and represents over 70 members along the CCU value chain, primarily 
industrial actors from different sectors. 
CCU is a broad term that covers all established and innovative industrial processes that aim at capturing 
CO2 – either from industrial point sources or directly from the air – and at transforming the captured 
CO2 into a variety of value-added products such as synthetic fuels, chemicals and building materials. 
CVE welcomes the ReFuelEU Aviation initiative aims to boost the supply and demand for sustainable 
aviation fuels in the EU, in order to reduce aviation’s environmental footprint and enable it to help 
achieve the EU’s climate targets.  
1. Definitions: in article 3, the proposal specifies that “sustainable aviation fuels’ (‘SAF’) means drop-
in aviation fuels that are either synthetic aviation fuels, advanced biofuels (…), or biofuels (…), which 
comply with the sustainability and greenhouse gas emissions cr iteria”. It adds that “‘synthetic aviation 
fuels’ means fuels that are renewable fuels of non-biological origin (…) used in aviation”.  
➢ CVE welcomes the inclusion in the definition of RFNBOs and of the reference to the criteria 
within REDIII. 
➢ CVE encourages all EU policy-makers to ensure consistency between the ReFuel EU Aviation 
proposal and the REDIII proposal. 
2. Volume shares: Annex I of the proposal details the minimum targets for sustainable aviation fuels 
and synthetic fuels: 2% SAF in 2 025; 5% SAF, 0,7% synthetic fuels in 2030; 20% SAF, 5% synthetic 
fuels in 2035; 32% SAF, 8% synthetic fuels in 2040; 38% SAF, 11% synthetic fuels by 2045; and 63% 
SAF, 28% synthetic fuels by 2050 (shares of volumes of fuels supplied by aviation fuels suppl iers to 
aircraft operators in EU airports). 
➢ CVE welcomes the setting of minimum targets to boost the uptake of RFNBOs in aviation. 
➢ According to the Impact Assessment of Refuel EU, the approximate amount of jet fuel sold in 
EU airports in 2018 was 47 Mt. Cu rrent SAF production represents about 0.05% of this total, 
which corresponds to 23.5kt. 
➢ The CCU value chain believes that these targets, in particular on synthetic fuels, could be made 
more ambitious, especially in the early stages of RFNBOs deployment. The announced capacity 
from a variety of projects indicates that a target for 2025 can be introduced and that the target 
of 2030 can be increased. 
➢ CVE proposes a revised 2025 and 2030 target, based on existing projects aiming to produce 
synthetic fuels. At the same time, we are suggesting a more even trajectory for synthetic fuels 
by 2050. Current proposal is suggesting a 4.3% difference between 2030 and 2035 dropping to

3. Incentivising producers to go beyond quotas 
➢ Whether targets are revised or not, the CCU community believes that incentives should be put 
in place to ensure that operators that contribute to use of synthetic jet fuels and go beyond 
proposed targets are encouraged and rewarded for doing so. 
➢ We believe such incentives could be implemented via support through innovation fund within 
ETS or provisions in the new proposal on Energy Taxation Directive. 
4. Support for corporate commitments to use more synthetic fuels 
➢ In addition to targets and quotas, CO2 Value Europe calls for EU and national policy-makers to 
incentivise and support for further corporate commitments, pledges and engagements to create 
the appropriate demand for synthetic fuels while recognising the efforts and contributions made 
by companies in reaching EU targets and going beyond. 
5. Other types of synthetic fuels: the proposal in recital 19 provides that  ”other types of synthetic 
fuels, such as low carbon synthetic fuels achieving high greenhouse gas reductions, could be 
considered for inclusion in the scope of this Regulation in the course  of future revisions, where such 
fuels become defined under the Renewable Energy Directive”. 
➢ CVE acknowledges that non-renewable synthetic fuels that use carbon captured from emissions 
(e.g. Recycled Carbon Fuels ) can lead to GHG emissions reductions in aviation (with 70% 
emission reductions as required by REDII and following the GHG calculation methodologies to 
be developed under REDII).  
➢ Such RCF could be already included in ReFuelEU, but since they are not renewable they should 
not interfere with the targets for fuel shares dedicated to RFNBOs. For example, separate goals 
for RCF could be considered, in addition to the ones already suggested for RFNBOs.

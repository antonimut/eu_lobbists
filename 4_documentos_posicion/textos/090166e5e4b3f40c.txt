Bioenergy Association of Finland’s position on the EU 
renewable energy rules – review 
 
General views 
 
Bioenergy Association of Finland supports the target to reach EU climate neutrality in a 
socially, economically and ecologically sustainable way prior to 2050. The Association 
underlines a need to agree a balanced FF55 package that supports the -55 % emissions 
reduction rather than a wide set of other goals. 
 
It is counter-productive and very disappointing that the Commission decided to open 
REDIII biomass sustainability criteria in its proposal. These were just agreed in 2018 after 
long and comprehensive negotiations. The Member States should be given reasonable 
time to effectively implement the recast REDII biomass sustainability criteria. This could 
then be followed by a proper analysis on how the new criteria function, what they deliver 
and if there is a need for corrections. European companies deserve 10+ years of sight to 
be able to create needed structural changes, to invest without self-imposed political 
uncertainty and to get rid of fossil fuels as soon as possible.  
 
The proposal does not support creating negative emissions in the EU. The EU could set a 
general binding and technology-neutral target (or targets for several years or a trajectory) 
for the whole EU, following, for example, a bottom-up procedure similar to the Governance 
regulation. Setting up a separate goal for NET’s without natural sinks would be policy-wise 
most important in order to develop these technologies. We also see that extending GO’s 
for BECCS and PyCCS related negative emissions could be worthwhile investigating as 
well as EU wide reverse auctions for negative emissions with different technologies 
organized by the Commission. 
 
Main points to amend the proposal on sustainability criteria 
The proposal requires several changes on biomass sustainability to ensure a balanced and 
successful bioenergy sustainability framework.

1. There should be no retroactive application of measures, including on the GHG 
savings criteria, to protect legal certainty and ensure businesses’ trust in the energy 
transition. For the transtion to become a success, the EU needs sustainable growth 
of bioenergy. Regulatory uncertainty will not contribute to this objective. Retroactive 
introduction of GHG savings criteria may lead to the closure of existing plants and 
the slowing down or even a possible reversal in the energy transition. The existing 
GHG criteria therefore should only apply to the plants in operation after the entry 
into force of REDII and possible new criteria after the entry into force of REDIII. 
Regarding biomass GHG-saving criteria we consider that if the requirement were to 
go beyond 80%, this would hinder the flexible operation of boilers. This would 
severely damage the use of bioenergy in balancing weather-dependent renewables, 
and would thus limit its complementary role within the renewables. 
 
2. Restrictions of feedstocks in proposed amendment of article 3(3) is arbitrary and 
not based on sustainability science but seems to  stem from industry policies. 
Definition of ‘quality roundwood’ or other similar new definitions should not be part 
of this directive.  
3. When drafting their support schemes Member States should strive to avoid any 
undue raw material market distortions . While an EU -determined regulation on 
cascading would not be the right policy tool to prevent such distortions from 
happening (as recognised in 2018), Member States could benefit from an updated 
cascading guidance document. Delegation of cascading                                                                                                                                            
should be limited accordingly and strictly on possible energy subsidies. 
4. The establishment of no -go areas for carbon -rich and highly biodiverse 
environments can strengthen the biomass sustainability framework only if they can 
be effectively operationalised.  The inclusion of these areas under the risk -based 
based approach in Article 29(6) would better serve this purpose.  The definitions of 
these areas should be unambiguous and based on existing classifications 5. Proposal to lower the threshold for energy plants using solid biomass from 20 MW 
is in our view  acceptable if all the plants below 20 MW can have a simplified 
procedure for verification of sustainability.

6. Indirect emission factor on annex V should be removed. We consider premature to 
include this factor. Indirect emissions are not scientifically sound and would 
constitute great uncertainty both for companies and for national authorities. 
 
7. Co-firing of biomass with sorted waste-based fuels (SRF, RDF) should be 
encouraged as it drives the efficient utilisation of resources and circular economy 
in efficient district heating and industry. Proposed article 29(11) now requires to 
fulfil                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   
EED definition on ”high efficiency cogeneration” where on annex III only the cofiring 
of natural gas is allowed. This provision should be changed.  
 
8. Transport biofue ls target should be set in a                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   
way that advances the markets. It is paramount that all the waste and residue 
feedstocks are allowed. Members states should be left with the choice to decide  
whether the quota is set based on energy content, volume content or GHG-emissions. 
The unconditional cap of 1.7% of Annex IX Part B is not justified and needs to be 
removed 
9. Forestry is, and should remain, the competence of Member States. Provisions 
regulating specifics of sustainable forest management should not be covered by 
European energy legislation,  but rather in accordance with the principle of 
subsidiarity, be addressed by national, regional, or local authorities -including those 
of third countries. Minimising biodiversity and soil quality impacts are key objectives, 
but requirements must work for all different forest types, irrespective of where in the 
world the biomass originates. For this reason, definitions should be based on existing 
and widely accepted definitions that can be applied locally. The tools to achieve them 
should be detailed at the national, regional, or local level to ensure the effectiveness 
of policy actions.

Alternative Fuels Infrastructure Regulation 
November 2021  
Eurogas welcomes the ambition of the EC to develop a strong policy framework for the deployment of the EU 
alternatives fuels infrastructure. However, Eurogas is very concerned regarding the overall lack of ambition for 
LNG/CNG infrastructure, the assumed lock in effect they are associated with and their consequent implicit exclusion 
in EU policies.   
Recommendations 
• The Regulation should provide a coherent alternative fuels definition.  
• The development of LNG infrastructure for heavy duty vehicles should be  addressed by the 
Regulation. Targets for heavy duty vehicles LNG infrastructure should be maintained on a longer term 
to keep sending robust investment signals. 
• The Regulation should ensure that an appropriate infrastructure is developed for the use of LNG in 
rail activities. 
• The Regulation should maintain ambition to deploy CNG infrastructure to ensure consistency and 
deliver transport decarbonisation. 
Create a coherent alternative fuels definition 
The Regulation should provide a coherent alternative fuels definition. Eurogas is strongly opposed to the 
proposed alternative fuels sub-definition proposed by the EC for many reasons: 
• Such sub-definitions would be an issue in case of blending for ex. renewables with so-called alternative fossil fuels.  
• The sub-definitions correctly rely on the Tank-to-Wheels consideration of the CO2 standards for road vehicles. The 
AFID/R vocation is not to be focused solely on this transport mode and it includes support for e.g. maritime. In this 
sector, the FuelEU proposal is clear on the need to account for the fuel contribution along the whole lifecycle and 
the biased concept of zero emissions vehi cles does not exists in such technology neutral approach. The fuels 
listed in the sub-definition are not subject to any sustainability of GHG emissions savings requirement s, and 
theoretically, it is possible to produce them using inefficient process and fo ssil feedstock leading to high GHG 
emissions while still qualifying as alternative fuels for zero-emission vehicles. 
• These sub-definitions serve no direct purpose as they are not referenced elsewhere in the proposal.

In consequence, the EC should remove su ch sub-definitions and reintroduce the definition of the Alternative Fuels 
Infrastructure Directive (EU) 2014/94 which provides a non-exhaustive list of alternative fuels and introduce an explicit 
reference to (advanced) biogas in the definition. 
Support LNG infrastructure for heavy duty vehicles 
The development of LNG infrastructure for heavy duty vehicles should be addressed by the Regulation. 
Targets for heavy duty vehicles LNG infrastructure should be maintained on a longer term to keep sending  
robust investment signals. The ambition of the EC should be higher, and targets should be maintained over a longer 
time horizon, at least until 2030, in order to keep sending robust investment signals to stakeholders. 
Investments in CNG and LNG infrastruc ture are future-proof since they also enable the deployment of decarbonized 
and renewable energy e.g. compressed/liquified biomethane and e-methane. As noted in recital ( 7) of the proposal, 
“those decarbonised fuels can be used in the same infrastructure as gaseous fossil fuels thereby allowing for a gradual 
shift towards decarbonised fuels”. 
Support LNG rail infrastructure 
The Regulation should ensure that an appropriate infrastructure is developed for the use of LNG in rail 
activities, especially for non-electrified railroads still relying on more polluting fuels such as Diesel or fuel oil not limited 
to the TEN-T core network. 
LNG is a necessary option for rail transport: certain railroad cannot be cost-effectively electrified, may be too far away 
from a densely populated area, or the costs for developing the necessary infrastructure may simply too high. In such 
case, this makes LNG the best available option to immediately and significantly reduce GHG and pollutants emissions. 
In the long term, additional GHG benefits could be deliver by using bio-LNG and e-LNG, using the same infrastructure. 
Support CNG infrastructure 
The Regulation should maintain ambition to deploy CNG infrastructure to ensure consistency and deliver 
transport decarbonisation. CNG network is only concentrated in a few Member States which is contrary to the 
ambition to increase the uptake of alternative fu els in the European Union as a whole. A sufficient level of support 
should be put in place to help all Member States to achieve the decarbonisation of their transport sector.

PVD Comments to the European Commission’s AML/CFT Package 
November 2021 
 
 
 
 
The need to combat money laundering and terrorist financing is beyond question for the Prepaid 
Verband Deutschland e.V. (PVD / Prepaid Association of Germany) and its members. The members of 
the PVD are constantly striving to permanently improve measures in the fight against money laundering 
and terrorist financing and to react decisively and flexibly to potential new threats. The risk-mitigating 
measures implemented in particular by the e -money issuers have made a decisive contribution to 
preventing the misuse of e-money for the purposes of money laundering and terrorist financing, so that 
the risks of using e-money for these purposes can be classified as very low.  
 
The PVD appreciates the presentation of the revised anti-money laundering rules by the EU Commission 
and expressly supports the plan to introduce uniform standards throughout Europe in the future by 
transferring numerous obligations of the Anti-Money Laundering Directive  (AMLD) into a Regulation. 
This will lead to further harmonisation of the EU internal market. So far, some central requirements in 
the AMLD were defined as minimum standards, which were interpreted in different ways in several 
Member States, thereby making the cross-border provision of payment services more difficult.  
 
However, from the perspective of the PVD, there is a need for change in some areas of the proposals 
in order to ensure their practical implementation. This applies in particular with regard to the 
implementation of the risk-based approach in connection with low-value, low-risk e-money payments.  
 
 
Retention of the exemption option of the previous Article 12 of the Anti -Money 
Laundering Directive  
 
The provision previously contained in Article 12 AMLD for Member States to be able to waive the 
application of certain customer due diligence measures for e-money on the basis of an appropriate risk 
assessment demonstrating a low risk, if several risk -mitigating conditions are met, has been deleted 
PVD Recommendations 
▪ The possibility for Member States to allow obliged entities not to apply certain CDD 
measures for e-money with a proven low risk, currently contained in Article 12 AMLD, 
should be retained. In the current proposal, this important exemption has been deleted 
without justification. Maintaining the exemption for low -value, low -risk e -money 
payments follows the spirit of the risk -based approach and is also in the interest of  
customers and data protection. 
▪ Clear rules for the application of Simplified or Enhanced Customer Due Diligence  are 
needed. Respective requirements should already be defined in AMLR as concretely as

▪ Permit the option provided for in Art. 19 (3) AMLR not only for the opening of accounts,

▪ Clearly define the term "control" used in Art. 14 (1) of the AMLD6.

Consistent implementation of the risk-based approach  
 
Art. 5 AMLR contains an option for Member States to exempt certain persons from the application of 
obligations under anti -money laundering law if various conditions are met. This is a change from a 
product-based approach (which is based in particular on Ar t. 12 of the AMLD5) to a person -based 
approach, which, however, only applies if this person does not carry out the relevant financial activity as 
their main activity. As a rule, professional financial institutions are therefore usually not eligible for this 
exemption. 
 
The product-related exemption from identification requirements for e -money with a proven low risk of 
money laundering or terrorist financing (waiver of certain due diligence requirements based on an 
appropriate risk asses sment demonstrating low risk, subject to low amount thresholds and other risk 
mitigating factors) was only adjusted in 2018 with the amendments to the 4th Money Laundering 
Directive (also 5th Money Laundering Directive, AMLD5). This was the result of long negotiations, at the 
end of which balanced rules were agreed that could be implemented in practice.  
 
In the current proposal, this important exemption has been deleted. The AMLR does not contain a 
rationale for this. The Supranational Risk Analysis of the  EU Commission does not justify abandoning 
the product-related exemption for e-money.  
 
In our view, this step is incomprehensible. The draft regulation ignores the still existing need for this 
exemption. Moreover, it also contradicts the risk-based approa ch and disregards the technical 
capabilities of effectively mitigate and minimise the risks of money laundering or financing of terrorism.  
 
Consider risk-mitigating measures of e-money issuers  
 
Transactions with e-money are very well traceable due to the availability of detailed transaction records. 
All e-money products automatically leave an electronic footprint, which is not the case when using cash, 
for example. This makes it possible to record and analyse every single transaction.   
 
The transaction monitoring by e -money issuers has constantly evolved  and improved and today uses 
state-of-the-art technologies to detect and stop suspicious transactions in real time. The close -meshed 
transaction monitoring includes:  
 
• the recording of the place and time of issuance and redemption of the e -money product, so 
that the entire cycle of the product can be monitored,  
• device identifiers for recognition of the end user devices involved in e-money transactions,  
• the determination of certain spending behaviour or patterns on the basis of the transaction 
history of certain end user devices, and  
• the recording of IP addresses in the case of online payments.  
 
These "unique identifiers" allow transactions to be attributed to a person, even if the e -money product 
was issued without prior customer identification. E -money products are usually designed for a specific 
use on the internet, so that conspicuous behaviou r patterns that lie outside the expected use can be

Proven exemption is in the interest of consumers  
 
From the PVD's point of view, the deletion of the exemption is disproportionate and considerably restricts 
the use of low-risk e-money products for consumers without taking into account the actual risk of those 
e-money products. For many market participants in the e-money industry, the possibility of making use 
of the Article 12 exemption plays a fundamental role in order to maintain the attractiveness of their 
products for the benefit of consumers and to provide them with uncomplicated access to digital me ans 
of payment.  
 
A waiver of the existing exemption for e -money, which in any case can only be applied within a very 
restrictive regulatory framework, would also lead to consumers in Europe no longer having any 
possibility of making low -risk purchases ano nymously in e -commerce. Maintaining the exemption for 
small-value payments is therefore also in the interest of data protection, especially for those services 
that do not always require full identification of the user because, for example, no physical good s are 
sent (e.g. online games or streaming services).  
 
 
Clear rules for SDD and EDD  
 
With regard to the drafting of Regulatory Technical Standards in connection with the application of 
Simplified or Enhanced Customer Due Diligence by the AMLR, as provide d for in Article 22 AMLR, we 
would like to point out that it is for this purpose of central importance to already formulate requirements 
in the AMLR as concretely as possible, which would support the swift adoption of clearly 
applicable rules.  
 
Protracted discussions and different interpretations, which still, to some extent, occur in connection with 
the application of the "Regulatory Technical Standards on strong customer authentication and secure 
communication under PSD2", even four years after the adoption of the RTS, should be avoided.  
 
Practical customer identification rules are elementary for new digital technologies and to promote 
innovative products. Overly burdensome onboarding processes are a massive barrier for financial 
institutions t rying to attract new customers in order to grow and scale. Companies offering financial 
services need to keep up with the rapid developments in technological innovation. This benefits 
consumers, financial institutions and other market participants alike. M any innovative FinTechs are 
building their business model on e -money because it is a simple, fast and secure way to pay small 
amounts online. Simplified customer due diligence offers the opportunity to let customers try out new 
products and give them benef its without having to make a long -term commitment at the outset. The 
likelihood of customers trying new products decreases significantly if the onboarding process is 
cumbersome and causes friction.

Allow later date of identity verification for the establishment of business relationships 
 
Art. 19 (3) AMLR provides for the possibility that accounts may be opened under certain conditions 
without all due diligence obligations having to be completed by that time. However, the AMLR does not 
contain a definition of the term "account".  
 
The PVD does not consider it expedient to make certain allowances only for specified (banking) products 
without explanation and not to clearly define the requirements applicable to them. In the PVD's view, 
the concept of business relationship as defined in the AMLR should not be deviated from when allowing 
delayed fulfilment of due diligence obligations. Apart from this, it is not apparent to the PVD why the 
opening of accounts should be treated differently from the establishment of other business relationships.  
 
In this connection, the PVD recommends that the option provided for in Art. 19 (3) AMLR, should 
not only  be permitted  for the opening of accounts, but generally for the establishment of 
business relationships, while retaining all other requirements for all obliged entities . 
 
 
Clear "control" term in account register management  
 
Art. 14 (1) of the AMLD6 provides for the creation of a central account register in which all persons who 
hold or control an account are to be entered. The AMLD6 does not contain a definition of the term 
"control".  
 
The PVD considers it expedient, especially for data protection purposes, to clearly define the conditions 
under which persons are entered in a register. This particularly applies to the term "control". In particular, 
cases should be excluded from the scope of Art. 14 (1) AMLD6 in which an e -money institution or a 
payment institution sets up a trust account with a separate IBAN for each customer, but only where this 
allocation serves the sole purpose of clearly allocating payments received on this account for accounting 
purposes. Various e-money and payment institutions have switched to this accounting practice because 
the existing alternative (all payments for all customers are made on a trust collective account and the 
allocation is made exclusively via the reference line) has proven to be error-prone. If the reference line 
required for the allocation is missing or contains errors, incoming payments cannot be clearly allocated. 
This causes manual post -processing efforts and, in the worst case, can lead to a payment not being 
assigned to the correct customer and the customer suffering a loss. The PVD does not have the 
impression that the Commission intended to pursue the above case with Art. 14 (1) of the AMLD6, but 
the case where customers of payment or e -money institutions use the assigned IBAN instead of a 
standard bank current account.  
 
The PVD advocates defining the term "control" used in Art. 14 (1) of the AMLD6  and subsuming 
the following specific cases under this category:  
 
• The customer of the payment or e -money institution has power of attorney over the account 
to which the IBAN was originally assigned.  
or  
• Each entry in an account to which an original IBAN is assigned is directly reflected in a 
payment account or an e-money account held by an e-money or payment institution under the 
same IBAN.

About the Prepaid Verband Deutschland e.V. 
 
The Prepaid Verband Deutschland e. V. (PVD) represents the interests of the prepaid industry operating 
in Germany. This includes, for example, issuers of prepaid products (e.g. credit institutions and e-money 
institutions), providers of loyalty systems , processors, card organisations and  distributors of prepaid 
payment products in the retail sector.

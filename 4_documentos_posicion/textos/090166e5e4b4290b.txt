TERNA RESPONSE TO THE EC CALL FOR CONTRIBUTION TO THE FIT FOR 55 PACKAGE 
Proposal for a Regulation on the deployment of alternative fuels infrastructure “AFIR” 
1. Introduction 
Terna welcomes that the European Commission recognises the importance of e -mobility as a key tool for 
cutting down the greenhouse gas emissions in the transport sector; we also agree on the Commission’s 
choice to propose a Regulation instead of a Directive so as to establish clearly binding and directly applicable 
obligations for Member States at national level avoiding the risk of delays and inconsistencies in national 
transposition processes.  
To this end, we also welcome that the Commission is promoting the phase out of polluting car sales as of 
2035 through the proposal for an amendment of Regulation 2019/631 setting CO2 emission standards for 
cars and vans.  
In this context, where a significant growth of electric/plug -in vehicles is expected over the coming years , 
accelerating the rollout of recharging infrastructure becomes a key policy priority to ensure that the market 
uptake of zero-vehicles is not impeded by the lack of infrastructure.  
To this purpose Terna considers that, from an electricity TSO perspective, the following elements should be 
addressed in the legislative initiative at hand. 
 
2. Recharge infrastructure targets 
The Commission proposes specific targets with the aim of expanding charging capacity in line with  the 
foreseen sales of zero-emission vehicles in the next decade and further on. In particular, the draft Regulation 
proposes targets for the installation of charging points at regular intervals on the comprehensive and core 
TEN-T network every 60/100 kilometres for electric charging of both light-duty and heavy-duty vehicles.  
Terna strongly support s the introduction of targets  in Articles 3 and 4  which are aimed at ensuring an 
adequate spatial coverage of the recharge infrastructure.  
However, even though the proposal goes in the right direction, we believe the Regulation should be more 
ambitious. This can be done by strengthening the requirements for the development of the national policy 
frameworks by each MS pursuant to Art.13. Those national plans should adequately address the deployment 
of the recharge infrastructure and anticipate the quick spread and increase of the EV demand. 
Specifically, we consider that the Regulation should provide for  MSs not only to define forward looking 
targets related to the deployment of charging points on the basis of the minimum requirements reported in 
Articles 3 and 4 – as requested by Art.13(1)(b)  –, but also  to introduce in the respective national policy 
frameworks: 
− specific targets on high and medium-levels road networks for both light and heavy mobility , i.e. which 
are lower in the road hierarchy compared to the comprehensive and core TEN-T networks1, in a way to 
ensure a widespread coverage in all the national territory; 
− specific targets for the deployment of recharge points on urban areas for light mobility, regardless of the 
dimension of the city . In the urban  context, recharge points in fact can find many types of off -road 
applications (e.g. parking lots in  railway stations, commercial areas). It is also worth to note that the

3. Recharge infrastructure features 
We fully support that operators of recharging points must ensure that all publicly accessible normal power 
recharging points operated by them are capable of smart recharging from the entry into force of the 
Regulation (Article 5.8). This provision would complement the similar requirement proposed by the 
Commission for all private charging points in the revision of the Renewable Directive3.  
We also consider that the former should be a “minimum requirement”, meaning that Member States should 
aim at accelerating the deployment of an infrastructure which suppor ts vehicle-to-grid functionalities. This 
need is reflected in many provisions of the Proposal, which requires Member States to ensure that 
bidirectional charging contributes to the penetration of RES and to the flexibility of the electricity system 
(Art.13(1)(i)) and to take appropriate measures to adjust the availability and geographical distribution of 
bidirectional recharging points (Art.14(4)). We stress the importance of a correct planning and rollout of V2G 
points by the entry into force of the Regulation ; this will minimise the need for retrofitting operations of 
existing points, which might not be always feasible under both technical and economical points of view.

5. Technical specifications  
We strongly encourage a quick development of EU-wide standardized communication protocols (vehicle-to-
grid and back-end), considering that  interoperability is an essential feature for using EVs as a flexibility 
resource for the gr id through smart charging and V2G applications and the correct communication among 
each component is a must.  
In particular we consider a pivotal factor for the correct operation of the entire system  the definition of an 
EU-wide common protocol that describes how the EV should communicate with the infrastruc ture ( the 
overall recharging point instrumentation), how the infrastructure should interface with the electric network 
to which it is connected and other way round, not only for ensuring interoperability of the connections, but 
also for allowing an efficient management of data exchange in real time.  
In addition, we consider that TSOs should be identified as relevant parties to be involved for the development 
of the common tec hnical specifications listed in Annex II. Among the others, it is particularly relevant that 
TSOs participate in the definition of standards related to the Technical specifications for communication 
exchange in the electric vehicle recharging ecosystem , in order to ensure that EU -relevant standards are fit 
for purpose for the delivery of grid services from the EVs.

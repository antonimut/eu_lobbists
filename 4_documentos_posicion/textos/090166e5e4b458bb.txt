1. VDB as representative of the biofuel producers in Germany 
The Association of the German Biofuel Industry (Verband der Deutschen Bio-
kraftstoffindustrie e. V. - VDB) represents the interests of 15 biofuel producers 
in Germany with a production capacity of 2.3 million tons of biodiesel and 900 
GWh of biomethane.  
 
2. General remarks 
VDB welcomes the European Green Deal and the Climate Law to set up an ambi-
tious climate 55% GHG reduction for 2030 compared to 1990. As road transport 
will have to contribute substantially to the achievement of the target, it is nec-
essary to set the regulatory framework for the use of climate-friendly energy 
sources. Crop-based, waste based and advanced biofuels, electromobility, fuels 
produced from renewable electricity and enhanced energy efficiency together 
make climate protection in transport possible. 
Regarding Germany and its transport sector, emissions are about as high as in 
1990, while they have fallen significantly in all other sectors of German econo-
my. Biofuels such as biodiesel, bioethanol and biomethane have saved around 
13 million tonnes of greenhouse gases in 2020 in Germany. On these grounds it 
is evident that the greenhouse gas balance of the transport sector would be 
even more devastating if sustainably produced biofuels did not make a substan-
tial contribution to climate protection. Compared to fossil fuels, biofuels reduce 
greenhouse gases by up to 90 percent. The entire production process is consid-
ered here, starting with the emissions from the agricultural cultivation to the 
transport and the industrial processing of the raw materials.  
Given the backlog of the transport sector concerning GHG abatement as a whole 
and the limited effect of measures taken until now, it seems appropriate to fur-
ther roll out the contribution of sustainable renewable fuels.

3. Remarks on the initiative 
Overall RES target [Article 2] 
The increase of the target for the share of renewable energy in final energy consumption from 32 to 
40% by 2030 is supported. In the light of the long-term goal of the EU climate law to achieve green-
house gas neutrality by 2050, the early definition of further expansion targets beyond 2030 seems 
necessary in order to provide security for the economy. 
 
GHG mandate [Article 25] 
VDB welcomes that a real reduction of GHG emissions in the transport sector will be made binding by 
establishing a GHG mandate. However, the target of 13% GHG reduction by 2030 must be raised 
significantly. A higher mandate prevents renewable compliance options from displacing each other, 
which would counteract climate protection efforts at national and EU level.  
This can be either done by setting a mandatory sub-target for crop-based biofuels at the level of their 
2020-contribution or by significantly increasing the overall transport target of 13% to a least 16% 
GHG reduction.  
As the draft proposes, multiple counting of certain compliance options in road transport is to be can-
celled. It is evident that multiple counting overstates the contribution of selected energy sources in 
terms of climate protection as it does not represent real GHG savings. 
Yet, the comparison of renewable electricity with a higher reference value leads to an overrating of 
its actual GHG reduction, indeed comparable to multiple counting. This has to be changed. 
 
Furthermore, it is recommended that the increase of the mandate is carried out in annual steps in 
order to offer economic actors planning and investment security while at the same time avoiding the 
displacement of crop based and waste-based biofuels. 
 
Crop-based biofuels [Article 26] 
The option for member states to reduce the GHG mandate if the climate protection contribution of 
biofuels from crop-based biomass is below its cap of 7% should be dropped. Lowering a GHG target is 
not justifiable in terms of serious climate protection policy. If a member state decreases the cap for 
crop-based biomass, the climate target in transport must be fulfilled by other options respectively 
(e.g., renewable electricity).  
 
The category of “cover crops” (intermediate crops) has to be properly defined as currently the differ-
entiation from food and feed crops is difficult when it comes to sustainability certification.

Concerning the GHG balance of crop-based biofuels, we ask for clarification that biofuel producers 
can continue to use the NUTS2 values for the part of the agricultural cultivation. 
   
With regard to the crediting of electricity to the GHG mandate, VDB suggests the establishment of a 
mechanism to automatically increase the mandate when given thresholds are exceeded. The adjust-
ment mechanism anchored in German legislation (§ 37h BImSchG) could serve as a model here as it 
prevents the displacing of renewable fuels effectively. 
 
Biofuels according to Annex IX Part A [Article 25] 
The sub-quota for advanced biofuels according to Part A Annex IX by 2030 should be raised from 2.2 
to 2.6 percent and increase in equal steps until then.  
Any expansion of the feedstock list for advanced biofuels must be accompanied by a respective in-
crease of the sub-target as well as the overall GHG mandate. 
 
Biofuels according to Annex IX Part B [Article 27] 
The current regulation should be maintained: Member States must be entitled to adjust the cap of 
1.7% if this is justified in view of the availability of the feedstock. Such changes require approval by 
the Commission anyway.  
Any expansion of the feedstock list for waste-based biofuels must be accompanied by a respective 
increase of the cap as well as the overall GHG mandate. 
 
Exceptional rules for the supply of electricity and RFNBOs [Article 25] 
The provision according to which “Member States may exempt fuel suppliers supplying electricity or 
renewable liquid and gaseous transport fuels of non-biological origin from the requirement to com-
ply with the minimum share of advanced biofuels and biogas produced from the feedstock listed in 
Part A of Annex IX with respect to those fuels” should be deleted entirely. Such a derogation rule 
would inadmissibly pit renewable compliance options against each other. 
 
Higher Blends [Recital 45] 
In order to maintain stable, sustainable biofuel volumes in the market in the event of declining fuel 
sales as a result of the increasing electrification of road transport, the percentage mandates have to 
be increased accordingly. Higher blends of biodiesel and bioethanol are required in the future (B10 
instead of B7 and E20 instead of E10) to use todays’ biofuels volumes and to maintain their GHG re-
duction contribution.

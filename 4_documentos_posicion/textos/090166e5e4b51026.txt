ADH CR‘s opinion on the revision of the Directive on the promotion of energy 
from renewable sources 
          November 18th, 2021 
The Commission presented „ Proposal for a DIRECTIVE OF THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND OF THE 
COUNCIL amending Directive (EU) 2018/2001 of the European Parliament and of the Council, 
Regulation (EU) 2018/1999 of the European Parliament and of the Council and Dire ctive 98/70/EC of 
the European Parliament and of the Council as regards the promotion of energy from renewable 
sources, and repealing Council Directive (EU) 2015/652“ (hereinafter  „RED II“). 
Approximately 4 million people are connected to District Heating  (“DH”) systems in the Czech 
Republic. These are mostly panel blocs of flats inhabited by middle - and low-income households at 
risk of energy poverty. DH plants also supply the tertiary sector, including schools, hospitals, social 
services, small businesses and micro-enterprises. 
In 2020, 55% of the DH heat supplied came from coal, 11% from renewable sources and waste heat, 
and 27% from natural gas. The year-on-year increase in the price of EU allowances means an increase 
in the price of DH heat produced fr om coal by approximately 15% and in the case of natural gas by 
7% for the next year. 
The Czech DH sector pledged to coal exit by 2030 , while the Czech Republic's current strategies 
assume its use beyond 2040. The Coal Commission of the Czech Republic recom mended coal exit in 
2038. The cost of this transformation is estimated at CZK 100 billion (EUR 4 billion) . There is a need 
to keep sufficient electricity production and provide ancillary services for the stability of the 
electricity grid with a growing sha re of electricity from intermittent sources. There is an imperative 
to keep acceptable heat prices for households. 
As part of the National energy and climate plan, the Czech Republic already counted on the 
maximum use of available biomass for coal replacem ent in DH plants by 2030 . However if the 
projects currently under preparation are to be successfully implemented stable investment 
environment must be created and constantly changing requirements on biomass must be avoided.  
Taking into account needs of the DH sector in the Czech Republic in order to transform and 
substantially decrease GHG emissions by 2030 while maintaining acceptable heat prices 
Association of District Heating of the Czech Republic (ADH CR) has the followin g comments on the 
above-mentioned proposal for the revision of the Directive promotion of energy from renewable 
sources: 
Article 3: 
We agree with the requirement to minimize the excessive disruptive effects of the energy use of 
biomass on the wood market a s the wood is raw material for material use. We respect the 
requirement that the energy use of saw logs, veener logs not be encouraged. On the other hand we 
cannot support the Commission to be able to further intervene in the promotion of the energy use of 
biomass by means of a delegated act. It is not at all clear what this delegated act should actually 
regulate - its content is described very vaguely. The very existence of a delegated act will create 
additional uncertainty for biomass energy projects. The Commission is to issue a delegated act within

one year of the entry into force of the Directive  - it can be expected during 2024  or even 2025. 
However, at that time implementation of biomass projects must already be in full swing if Member 
States are to meet ambitious targets for renewable energy production in 2030. 
The RED II and the Commission are strongly pushing for increasingly ambitious targets for the use of 
renewable energy sources in heat production. It is not possible to undermine the preparation  of 
projects for energy use of biomass, which is absolutely crucial for the Czech Republic in meeting 
these targets, by constantly setting additional conditions. We believe that the RED II, after the 
proposed tightening, even without a delegated act, conta ins sufficiently strong instruments  to 
prevent any misuse of biomass, which, however, also bring a considerable administrative burden. It is 
now necessary to test the functioning of the entire system in practice and further changes are 
possible only at the end of the decade.  
We recommend replacing do delete words "harmful impacts on biodiversity". Commercial forests are 
grown and harvested primarily because of the wood for material use, and thus the use of harvesting 
residues can hardly affect biodiversity in any way. There is no clear relation between harmful impact 
on biodiversity and waste hierarchy or cascading principle. 
We agree with the requirement that no support should be provided for the separate production of 
electricity from forest biomass. We do not consider the exceptions proposed by the Commission  
from this prohibition  are justified. Biomass is generally a scarce resource that must be used as 
efficiently as possible in combined heat and power  technology or heat production. We propose to 
move the date of application of the ban on the support of separate electricity production from 
31.12.2026 to 1.1.2026. It is necessary to respect projects that are already in the implementation 
phase. However, by 1.1.2026, in our opinion, there is enough time for these already started projects 
to be completed and new ones should not be launched. We propose to reformulate the ban not as 
an separate electricity generation (electricity-only installations), but as a  mandatory provision of the 
use of useful heat in the amount of at least 40% of the heat produced. The Czech Republic has 
already introduced this requirement. The wording proposed by the Commission can very easily be 
circumvented by ensuring only the symbo lic use of the heat produced, when it will no longer be 
"electricity only installation" and the condition of RED II will be formally met. 
Article 7 
We believe that the principle that energy produced from renewable fuels of non -biological origin 
should be i ncluded in the sectors where it is consumed should also apply to renewable fuels  in 
general. 
Article 15a 
In our opinion the whole of Article 15a should be deleted and the requirements for the use of RES in 
buildings should be dealt with  in the framework of  the Directive on the energy performance of 
buildings. We do not consider appropriate Member States to set a target for the use of RES in 
buildings as it inevitable  overlaps with the target for renewable electricity and heating and cooling. 
Moreover it is not clear how, for example, electricity from renewable energy sources produced 
outside buildings will be allocated between the buildings sector, industry, transport and other 
sectors. Thus, only an administrative exercise without real content would be crea ted. It should be 
also noted that there is no statistic on renewable energy use in buildings available in Eurostat so that 
Member states will not be able to follow their target for RES use in buildings.

Article 23 
We understand the need to increase ambiti on in the heat sector and its partly different distribution 
among Member States. However, it is not clear what Annex 1a actually expresses and how the 
increase in the share of RES was determined for individual countries. Should there be an increase of 
0.5 percentage points for the Czech Republic and the resulting value therefore an increase of RES by 
1.6 percent per year? In our opinion, such a requirement is beyond the real possibilities of the Czech 
Republic. Electricity and gas from renewable energy sour ces consumed for heating, including district 
heating, should also be included in the target, and corresponding method should be clarified in the 
Article. We support the obligation for Member States to carry out an evaluation of the potential for 
the use of energy from renewable energy sources and waste heat and cold in the heating sector. We 
support the expansion of the tools that Member States can use to achieve this goal. In paragraph 4, 
point (a) however, it should be specified that renewable fuels supplied to district heating through gas 
network should also be counted and connection of buildings to efficient district heating and cooling 
should also be supported. 
Article 24 
In paragraph 1, we propose to replace the terms " final consumers" and "customer or end-user" with 
"final customers". The concept of final consumer and end-user is unclear and RED II does not contain 
a definition. We therefore propose to use the term " final customer ", which is introduced in the 
Energy Efficiency Directive. We also propos e to insert the definition of " final customer" in RED II by 
referring to this term in the Energy Efficiency Directive. 
The energy performance of district heating systems will be primarily the input for assessing the 
energy performance of buildings and proc essing energy performance certificates for buildings. It is 
therefore necessary in this case to follow the terminology of the Energy Performance of Buildings 
Directive. The reference to this directive should be added to the last sentence of paragraph one. 
It should be also clarified that  the share of heat from RES can be reported as a "residual" mix after 
deducting the heat from RES supplied to customers with the guarantee of origin  (as the heat from 
RES).  
It is not clear how paragraph 4 set the required i ncrease in heat from RES and waste heat in heat 
supply systems by 2.1 percentage points per year. The percentage should be linked to the overall 
share of RES in the heating sector and should be based on the Member State's target under Article 
23. It should be explicitly stated that electricity and gas from RES can also be included in the target if 
they are covered by guarantees of origin. 
With regard to paragraph 4a, we do not agree that the criteria for the mandatory purchase of heat 
from RES and waste heat should be set by the competent authority of the Member State. We believe 
that the purchase of heat should be primarily an agreement of the concerned parties. Only in the 
event of a refusal should the competent authority play a role in resolving the dispute and assessing, if 
necessary, whether the reasons for the refusal are relevant and whether the heat supply system 
operator's behaviour is non-discriminatory - this is already covered in paragraph 5. Each heat supply 
system has unique conditions and i t is  impossible to set uniform requirements within a Member 
State and, moreover, it is unnecessary.

Article 29 
We do not consider as necessary to lower the threshold for the application of sustainability criteria 
and greenhouse gas emission savings in the cas e of biomass fuels in installations with a power input 
from 20 to 5 MW, because this change will have negligible impact in the Czech Republic  because 
amount of biomass consumed in the given power input range is very low . Given that in the power 
range of 5 to 10 MW Member States should introduce a simplified verification scheme (see Article 30 
(6)), it seems more appropriate to set a limit of 10 MW instead of introducing a simplified verification 
scheme. 
Article 30 
In paragraph 6, we do not consider the requ irement for Member States to introduce a simplified 
verification scheme for installations with a heat input of 5 to 10 MW to be meaningful and effective. 
Such a scheme would be introduced in the Czech Republic for a very small amount of biomass. It 
seems more appropriate to adjust Article 29 to 10 MW and not to introduce a simplified verification 
scheme at all. 
Article 31a 
We do not understand the added value of setting up an EU “database to enable the tracing of liquid 
and gaseous renewable fuels and recyc led carbon fuels” in parallel with the EU -wide guarantee of 
origin scheme. We consider it absurd for guarantees of origin in the first step to be issued and then in 
the second step to be cancelled when the traded gaseous fuels are introduced into the new database 
(as proposed in paragraph 4). Without guarantees of origin, it is also not possible to report the use of 
these fuels to customers. The main disadvantage of a database against guarantees of origin is that, 
unlike guarantees of origin, which are separately transferable, in the case of database gas must be 
physically transported. This means paying the appropriate transit fees. With regard to the liquidity of 
the EU wide market for gaseous fuels from renewable energy sources, we consider a more 
appropriate scheme of guarantees of origin, which can be traded separately  from physical 
commodity trades.

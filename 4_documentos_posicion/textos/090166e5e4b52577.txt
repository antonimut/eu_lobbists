www.carbonengineering.com Sustainable Aviation Fuels – ReFuelEU Aviation Carbon Engineering supports the ReFuelEU Aviation proposal to deploy Sustainable Aviation Fuels (SAF) with a sub-target for synthetic aviation fuels to enable a climate-neutral EU economy.  As mentioned in the proposed Regulation (recital 19), ”When produced from renewable electricity and carbon captured directly from the air, synthetic aviation fuels can achieve as high as 100% emissions savings compared to conventional aviation fuel.”  We ask that the emissions savings of synthetic aviation fuels using atmospheric CO2 be recognised by the ReFuelEU Aviation proposal through potentially a multiplier on credits or equivalent mechanism enable investment in Direct Air Capture (DAC)-powered synthetic fuels. Carbon Engineering's AIR TO FUELS™ solution is a highly scalable SAF production pathway, and SAF made from our process is drop-in compatible with existing aircraft equipment.  We are working in Canada and the UK with plant developers, to engineer and build plants using DAC technology capable of producing up to 100 million litres of synthetic fuels annually.   In order to support the business case for investment, Carbon Engineering recommends: (i) incentives such as a multiplier for credits to close the business case for synthetic aviation fuels using atmospheric CO2, (ii) inclusion of Greenhouse Gas (GHG) accounting on a well-to-wing basis; and (iii) consideration of carbon removals. Specifically:  Investable policy framework: Synthetic aviation fuels require a financeable revenue stream.  For example, contracts for difference for those first-of-a-kind plants and/or a multiplier which rewards the emissions savings of synthetic fuels from atmospheric CO2;  Environmental benefit:  Under the current definition of synthetic aviation fuels, carbon dioxide can be sourced from air, biomass or from industrial processes. SAFs should participate in the SAF mandate on the basis of stringent life-cycle assessment which accurately measures their net physical contribution of GHGs to and from the atmosphere; and  Carbon removals: We support the concept of penalties for non-compliance, and we also recommend that proven carbon removals using DAC and storage (DACS) should be eligible for compliance under the ReFuelEU Aviation proposal. For example, California’s Low Carbon Fuel Standard (LCFS) includes DACS, whereby projects can directly generate credits so long as they comply with the quantification methodology and permanence removal protocol. This enables the deployment of DAC technology which will be critical for near carbon neutral synthetic fuels and the EU’s target of net-zero emissions by 2050.   The EU has the opportunity to build an investable and coherent policy framework for DAC-powered synthetic aviation fuels. www.carbonengineering.com Carbon Engineering (CE) is a Canadian clean energy company providing Direct Air Capture (DAC) technology. Our technology is proven, scalable, and provides climate relevant-scale collection of pure Carbon Dioxide (CO2) from the atmosphere.  CE's AIR TO FUELS™ process combines atmospheric CO2 and clean hydrogen. Synthetic aviation fuels made from our process is drop-in compatible with existing aircraft equipment, and the fuel not only offers significant GHG emissions reduction, but also additional pollution reduction benefits because of its cleaner burning properties.  CE has successfully developed and demonstrated its AIR TO FUELS™ technology since 2017 at our pilot plant in Squamish, British Columbia (see photo), and as part of Natural Resources Canada’s “Sky’s the Limit” competition. CE is now focused on advanced development and commercialisation. For example, CE and Huron Clean Energy, with its partners The Upper Nicola Band and Oxy Low Carbon Ventures, have begun preliminary engineering and design on a large-scale, commercial facility in British Columbia that would produce up to 100 million litres of ultra-low carbon transportation fuel a year. The plant has a target operational year of 2026.  In the UK we are part of the Green Fuel Green Skies competition, in partnership with LanzaTech, on a first-of-a-kind project to produce over 100 million litres of SAF a year out of CO2 directly from the atmosphere.     CE also captures CO2 which can be safely and permanently stored deep underground (diagram above). In the US, we’re currently engineering a Direct Air Capture (DAC) facility in partnership with 1pointFive, targeting operations in 2024, expected to capture up to 1 million tonnes of CO2 a year.  Preliminary engineering and design have begun on a large-scale facility targeted for North-East Scotland that is expected to capture between half a million to one million tonnes of CO2 annually, in partnership with Storegga Geotechnologies, with an expected operational date of 2026.

Executive Summary  
ENTSO-E welcomes the revision of the Renewable Energy Directive recast (“RED III”) to be fit for purpose 
for the achievement of the 2030 decarbonisation goal.  
The decarbonisation process deeply relies on one hand on maximizing the integration of renewable energy 
sources (RES) in the electricity, transport, heating and cooling sectors, and, on the other hand, on developing 
synergies among the energy carriers in all the steps of the value chain starting from energy production to the 
final consumption. It is undisputable that renewable electricity will be the dominant energy carrier on the 
path to 2050 and beyond, due to the intrinsic greater efficiency of the carrier and the maturity of renewable 
electricity technologies.  The direct use of electricity with consequent reduction of primary energy needs, 
together with the development of renewables, must be encouraged wherever this is technically and 
economically efficient and sustainable.  At the same time, indirect electrification will be necessary in certain 
sectors where decarbonisation is technically otherwise not possible. Hence, cross-sector integration must be 
adequately promoted by the legislation.  
The ENTSO-E response to the open consultation on the revision of the RED focuses on six main points:

participation of those units to service markets, which would otherwise be allowed to 
participate through aggregation.  
o Locational elements in support schemes can ensure a cost -efficient system integration of 
RES.

ENTSO-E’s Position on Main Amendment Proposals  
Integrating RES in the electricity grid 
We fully support that RED III provides  a technologically neutral framework which enables  renewable energy 
sources (RES) participation on the ancillary services markets for grid services (c.f. Recital 5 and Art. 20a RED 
III). With the rapid increase of RES connected to the power system and in a context of a progressive 
replacement of traditional thermal plants in favour of RES, in fact, it is increasingly importan t that RES units 
are enabled to provide grid services on the ancillary services markets . For instance, support schemes should 
avoid accounting rules that may in practice discourage generators from offering ancillary services.  This helps 
to ensure system se curity and supply (es p. voltage and frequency control) and also mitigating possible 
distortions of market functioning and competition.  
 
Facilitating system integration of renewable electricity (Articles 20a(2) and 20a(4)) 
Concerning Art. 20a(2), which requests Member States to ensure that manufacturers of domestic and 
industrial batteries and vehicle manufacturers enable real -time access to basic battery management system 
information as well as to in-vehicle data, we would like to suggest to inclu de “system operators” to the list of 
potential parties to get access to the related information . In our view it is relevant to explicitly recognise the 
access to data from batteries, and in general from all digitally connected  decentralised assets (e.g. he at-
pumps, solar PV) to system operators in order to perform their regulated tasks. For instance, access to such 
data will help creating a better picture of the status of the grid and enhance decisions in terms of 
maintaining and operating the system in the presence of large amounts of mobile assets. 
Furthermore, in Art. 20a(4), the Commission’s proposal asks Member States to ensure that the national 
regulatory framework does not discriminate against participation in the electricity markets, including

Support schemes for energy from renewable sources  
 RES s upport schemes should be improved with additional economic signals aimed at targeting the 
development and connection of RES – including offshore RES - consistently with the needs of the electricity 
grid capabilities . In this regard, we suggest that Article 4 1  of the current RED II should be further 
strengthened. In paragraph 4, the third subparagraph can be replaced by the following:  
Member States may also consider establishing shall establish mechanisms to ensure the regional diversification in 
the deployment of renewable electricity, in particular to ensure cost -efficient system integration of renewable 
sources. In particular, support schemes shall be designed so as to provide locational signals which incentivise the 
development and connecti on of RES plants, including offshore RES, compatibly with the electricity grid 
capabilities, if economically beneficial. 
This would allow reaching the climate goals at the least cost for the overall energy system. 
Moreover, it shou ld be ensured  that authorisation  timings for power plants and related network 
developments are taken into account when defining the support schemes schedule and planning, in 
particular by organising and scheduling the tendering procedures in due advance, i n order to ensure RES 
operators have a clear framework in place for investment decisions and sufficient time to obtain the needed 
authorisations and consequently to decrease the risks (and related costs) associated to the investment.

Joint offshore energy planning per sea basin (new paragraph Article 9 (7a)) 
With reference to offshore renewable development (with an EU target of at least 60 GW and 300 GW of 
offshore wind by 2030 and 2050 as well as 1 GW and 40 GW of ocean energy respectively), the European 
Commission (EC) proposes that Member States bordering a  sea basin shall cooperate to jointly define the

steps in 2030 and 2040, taking into account among others the importance of ensuring the associated 
integrated grid planning.

It is widely acknowledged that offshore RES generation needs an adequate grid development  and to be able 
to deliver such in a timely manner, more clarity on deployment pace, quantities and location is welcome . We 
fully support the refer ence to an integrated planning process; however, in this respect, we consider the 
proposal should refer more clearly to the need for an integrated planning in terms of RES and networks . 
Offshore RES deployment needs to be done in consideration of the power system development potential. As 
a consequence, TSOs must have a pivotal role in RES planning for each sea basin.  
In particular, with regard to the obligation for Member States to define the amount of offshore RES planned 
in each sea basin by specified future dates such as 2040 or 2050, we consider that these targets shall be 
consistent with the need for a holistic on - and offshore view, such that offshore RES can be transported to 
shore. In this light, achieving the offshore wind potential  for each basin must be established in strong 
coordination with the electricity TSOs  in the framework of the regional groups (e.g. NSEC , BEMIP) . 
Coherence between offshore planning  and national network development plans must be ensured.  
Furthermore, as offshore hybrid projects are likely to be further extended and integrated with other projects 
as part of the modular development of the future offshore grid, particular care should be given during the 
planning phase to enable anticipatory i nvestments. As has already been highlighted in the Offshore RES 
Strategy, anticipatory investments will be key to prepare for future upscaling and development, and to speed 
up the widescale integration of Offshore RES to the onshore system. 
Finally, particular care should be given to avoid the potential risk of double -counting both interconnection 
levels and offshore RES generation shares among Member States towards achieving their build -out targets. 
Criteria supporting Member States in estab lishing mechanisms for the distribution of these shares would 
thus be welcome.  
 
 
Cross-border pilot projects (new paragraph under Article 9(1a)) 
ENTSO-E welcomes the proposal from the EC to have an obligation for Member States to have at least one 
joint project with one or more MS  for the production  of RES by 31 December 2025 . Cross-border support 
schemes, if well designed, may be particularly well suited to address new challenges that require closer 
bilateral or regional coordination (e.g. deploying offsh ore hybrid projects) and can lead to a more efficient 
distribution of renewable power capacity as whole.  
A more coordinated approach across Europe for RES support schemes would improve the economic 
efficiency of the energy transition in Europe by optimizi ng the development of RES based on Member 
States’ endowment. While cross-border participation in RES support schemes could help align the 
deployment of new RES capacity with grid development, few countries have effectively introduced a cross-
border element in their national support instruments  to date, despite the provisions on cross -border 
participation in RES schemes in RED I and RED II.  
The obligation to have cross -border pilot projects would in this context encourage the testing of various 
solutions and setups, which would help facilitate the identification of best practices in later years. This would 
also help foster t echnical standardization, which would be beneficial to facilitate cross -border coordinated 
planning of offshore transmission infrastructure.  
Differing regulatory frameworks  across countries (including grid connection cost sharing regimes) may lead 
to unequal conditions under which generators compete for support under cross -border schemes . Careful 
design and transparenc y of such schemes is therefore needed to avoid unintended incentives and to 
guarantee a level playing field. Furthermore, requirement s on Member States must support network 
investments needed to ensure an adequate interconnection capacity between the concerned countries.

Permitting and Administrative Procedures  
The EC considers it necessary for Member States to take action to simplify the authorisation procedures for 
the construction of renewable energy plants, as the lack of simplification of permits and administrative 
procedures appears still to be the greatest barrier to the development of renewables.  
However, in this respect the EC proposes that on the basis of the measures to improve administrative 
procedures for renewable energy installations that Member States are to report on by 15 March 2023 in 
their first integrated national energy and climate progress reports pursuant to Regulation (EU) 2018/1999 of 
the European Parliament and of the Council, the EC should assess whether the provisions included in this 
Directive to streamline these procedures hav e resulted in smoot h and proportionate procedures. If that 
assessment reveals significant scope for improvement, the EC should take appropriate measures to ensure 
Member States have streamlined and efficient administrative procedures in place ”.  Article 1(5)(d), 
amending Article 15 of the RED II, provides in this regard that “By one year after the entry into force of this 
amending Directive, the Commission shall review, and where appropriate, propose modifications to, the 
rules on administrative p rocedures set out in Articles 15, 16 and 17 and their application, and may take 
additional measures to support Member States in their implementation”. 
While we acknowledge that considerable efforts are being taken toward RES development through the 
National Resilience and Recovery Plans, we would like to note that waiting for “checking” the fitness of the 
measures taken by MSs in their NECP , m ost likely, no earlier than  in 2024 , rather than introducing more 
prescriptive and ambitious provisions on permittin g already in this Directive  could increase the risk of 
missing the 2030 targets.

Guarantees of Origin 
Article 1(8) amending Article 19(2) and (8) removing Member States’ ability not to issue 
guarantees of origin to a producer that receives financial support 
ENTSO-E welcomes the amendment brought forward by the RED III removing Member States’ ability not to 
issue guarantees of origin (GO) to a producer that receives financial support. TSOs believe this will support 
the growth of the GO market in the coming years, which will play a significa nt role to achieve the EU’s long -
term targets. At the same time,  this new market revenue stream may further support the phase -out of 
operating aid for already mature technologies.  Nonetheless, particular care should be given at the Member 
State level to take into account the market value of GOs and avoid the risk of double compensation.  
Additional proposals on enhancing the existing Guarantees of Origin scheme 
From the point of view of TSOs, th ere is no need to track actual electrons. We acknowledge the current 
book-and-claim system as the established basis  for certification of renewable electricity. However, looking 
to the fundamental changes in an energy system with increasingly RES -based and fluctuating energy 
production the certification of renewable energy should take into account the real energy flows. .Therefore, 
it is important that GOs have a finer time and space granularity . With this in mind, ENTSO -E strongly 
encourages the co-legislators to create the necessary adjustment and regulatory space to ensure that  EC the 
provisions in the RED III on renewable certification are as future proof as possible.  
The actual consumption of electricity needs to be “matched” on a  time-granular basis wit h renewable 
electricity production (because electricity cannot be easily stored in large quantities), taking also into 
account cross border capacity to guarantee the link between the produced renewable energy and 
consumed energy. While recent initiative s for “granular GOs” have focused on matching consumption with 
renewable generation on an hourly basis, the target should be an even finer resolution that is aligned with 
the target imbalance settlement period in the wholesale market, i.e. 15 minutes. This will be  a key step 
towards enhancing the way in which we can better align the remuneration of renewables with system 
operation. This is particularly the case when considering the evolution of the power system towards high -
RES shares in the coming decades, which will place further emphasis on flexibility needs and the importance 
of adequate economic signals. 
Furthermore, relying only on the physical location in the definition of the GO is not sufficient to take into 
account the physics of the grid , rather the bidding zones has to be known. This is particularly crucial when 
considering the expected uptake of large -scale storage. The situation may arise where, with only “time 
granular GOs”, storage operators m ay be receiving a significant premium through GOs by taking advantage 
of the high -price volatility across time. However, this premium, without distinction to location, would in 
effect incentivise a large -scale deployment of storage with no consideration fo r the existing state of grid

Finally, ENTSO-e, which represent whole interconnected system of Europe, strongly believes that Article

Energy Sector Integration 
Renewable fuels of non-biological origin (RFNBOs) targets 
ENTSO-E welcomes the messages contained in recital 22 of the revision of the Renewable Energy Directive 
recast, in particular stating that support to the uptake of R FNBOs “should not result in net pollution increases 
due to an increased demand for electricity generation that is satisfied by the most polluting fossil fuels” . ENTSO-E 
considers this principle to be key for the successful  integration of renewable fuels and proposes to move or 
add it under Article 3 (General objectives).  
The legislator must carefully take into account that, in general, the production of RFNBOs entails a less 
efficient use of electricity compared to the direct use of the carrier  for final consumption; hence, setting 
RFNBOs targets would result in installing more RES than would be needed for reaching the EU targets if the 
same electricity would be dedicated to direct end uses.  
This aspect must be carefully taken into account by the legislator when deciding on the rules for determining 
the renewable origin of Hydrogen, also considering that the massive -scale deployment of RES is still facing 
many obstacles and the overall electricity demand (including future P2X demand) risks being  covered by 
polluting sources in case RES installations do not proceed at an adequate pace.

District heating & cooling to provide system services, including balancing (Article 24(8)) 
In Art.  24, a new paragraph (8) requests Member States to establish a framework under which electricity 
distribution system operators  will assess, at least every four years, the potential for district heating and 
cooling systems to provide balancing and other system services. This should include demand response and 
thermal storage of excess electricity from renewable sources, and whethe r the use of the identified potential 
would be more resource - and cost-efficient than alternative solutions. It needs to be noted that balancing is 
a task that is conducted by transmission system operators. Thus, the framework on what entity should 
assess further potential of district heating and cooling in system services should not be restricted through 
the Directive and rather leave it for the Member State to decide what actor is best suited to fulfil the task 
according to existing mandates and knowhow. We therefore propose to adapt Article 24(8) to ensure that 
Member States are responsible to establish the framework under which the assigned entity will perform the 
assessment on the potential contribution of district heating and cooling systems to provide  balancing and 
other system services. 
In addition, it should be assessed if similar tasks should be foreseen also within other sectors, such as 
buildings (Art. 15a) or heating and cooling (Art. 22). 
 
Information obligation for TSOs

The new Article 20a would require TSOs and DSOs to make available in near real-time (under one hour) the 
share of RES and GHG content supplied in each bidding zone, with forecasting where available , w ould 
provide a signal to consumers and market players sensitive to renewable -based electricity penetration.  
ENTSO-E would emphasise the following two remarks:  
First, the provision of estimated RES generation data is already widespread among TSOs, with the ENTSO -E 
Transparency Platform providing actual or estimated generation from renewable energy sources in the 
electricity mix for each bidding zone  on an hourly basis. However, the provision of data in close -to-real time 
on actual amounts of renewable electricity generated may prove to be a sizeable challenge , taking into 
account (a) the limited  rollout of smart meters in certain countries  able to provide real -time measurement; 
(b) the lack of access to private  data depending on the legal framework in place , and  (c) and the local 
organisation for the provision of data, especially for small -scale units. Similarly, the calculation of GHG 
content may be highly complex, as it would have to be power plant -specific and involve significant efforts in 
terms of calculation and, if necessary, certification.  
With this in mind, ENTSO-E would welcome further clarification on the level of detail and accuracy 
required by the new Article 20a for the provision of information on RES and GHG data to make sure the 
requirements are feasible and proportionate to the actual objective . This would furthermore help address

clear that in case of forecasting errors which could lead to market revenue losses for market parties , TSOs

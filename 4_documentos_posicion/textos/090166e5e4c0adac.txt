 
 
 
 
Edison’s response to the public European Commission’s consultation on the recast of the Energy 
Efficiency Directive (EED) 
 
Edison welcomes the possibility to share its considerations on the European Commission (EC)’s proposal for 
the recast of the Energy Efficiency Directive (EED). This process of revision is timely, as are the EC’s efforts to 
consult with stakeholders from th e private sector that are engaged in the energy transition and whose 
business will be impacted by the important legislative proposals that are currently under inter -institutional 
negotiation.   
Edison is a leader in the provision of energy efficiency and environmental services to industry and buildings 
(both private and public). In light of the European objectives already included in the Italian National Energy 
and Climate Plan, we intend to actively develop and promote new consumption models through distributed 
generation and the promotion of initiatives developed on a public -private partnership model. We also aim 
to participate in the redevelopment of the real estate portfolio, especially for the Public Administration.  
We believe that the revision of this directive, and subsequently also of the Energy Performance of Buildings 
Directive (EPBD), is absolutely fundamental as reaching energy efficiency targets in all sectors, which are 
currently lagging behind expectations, will be crucial in order to achieve EU's climate ambitions. Moreover, 
we support the decision taken by the European Commission to adopt the energy efficiency first principle in 
the Fit for 55 as  well as in the EU's economic recovery policies.  We have welcomed the publication of the 
Recommendation (EU) 2021/1749 on Energy Efficiency First and the related guidelines and examples for its 
implementation. We have also welcomed the publication of the Renovation wave initiative, designed to 
provide a comprehensive framework to optimize European policies for the renovation of public and private 
buildings. It will be crucial to maintain harmonisation between all of the above-mentioned proposals in order 
to give clear signals to operators and investors. 
The new binding targets proposed by the European Commission for the reduction of final and primary 
energy consumption ( -36% and -39% respectively), although  very challenging, go in the right direction . 
Those targets together represent a 9% increase in the target for reduci ng energy consumption at European 
level compared to the ambitions pledged by Member States in 2020 within their National Energy and Climate 
Plans (NECPs). We agree with the approach of the European Commission and the will to fill the existing gap 
in achieving the original target of 32.5% improvement in energy efficiency by 2030. However, we note that 
the new energy efficiency targets do not give due consideration to the efforts made by Member States, such 
as Italy, that have adopted ambitious objectives in their NECP and that have already introduced effective 
measures.  
We welcome the greater attention given by the text of the Directive to the reduction of consumption and 
energy renovation in the public sector, which now rightfully includes all public bodies at all administrative 
levels. The public sector offers considerable potential for energy savings and targeted efficiency measures in 
public buildings can bring great results in supporting the achievement of aggregate objectives. However, we 
believe that in setting targets it is necessary to consider the national specificities of public buildings (some 
countries have many historical buildings) in order to provide a more complete picture of potential courses of 
action. 
We also welcome the introduction of the obligation (i) for companies with an average annual consumption 
higher than 100 TJ of energy in the three years prior to entry into force  of this Directive  to adopt energy 
management systems; (ii) and for companies  with an average annual consumption higher than 10 TJ and 
that do not implement an energy management system to be subjected to an energy audit carried out at least

every four years . It will also be important to ensure, through targeted policies and regulatory tools, that 
recommendations from those energy audits will be duly implemented. 
We note potential discrepancies between the CO2 abatement ambitions and the Commission’s proposal that 
from 1 January 2024 energy savings from measures involving the direct use of fossil fuels, including natural 
gas, can no longer count towards meeting energy efficiency obligations. This could lead to the exclusion of 
energy efficiency interventions that involve the use of efficient technologies such as cogeneration powered 
by natural gas. There are still installations that use particularly polluting fuels (e.g. diesel), where a switch to 
natural gas would represent a clear improvement in reducing emissions and energy consumption. We believe 
that energy saving obligations should allow the use of all efficiency solutions, through end -use, 
transmission, distribution and supply, with a focus on renewable or low-carbon ready solutions allowing 
the immediate switch from the most polluting technologies (coal and oil products). For the same reasons, 
we consider it positive that the restriction does not apply to the indirect use of fossil fuels. 
We welcome the establishment of a new framework for heating and cooling to reduce emissions in buildings. 
The proposed requirements for high -efficiency cogeneration in Annex III are complemented by a threshold 
for direct emissions from cogeneration (if not fuelled by renewables, waste or industrial residues), to be set 
at less than 270 gCO2 per kWh of energy produced by the cogeneration (including heating/cooling, electricity 
and mechanical energy). We believe t he proposed threshold is highly challenging because it requires total 
efficiency rates of more than 75%. These ca n be technically reached but not in all cogeneration facilities, 
especially if these values refer to average load rather than full load. We therefore propose to adopt  a 
threshold not lower than 310 gCO2/kWh, which appears to be more realistic in light of the current energy 
efficiency of the CHP stock. Our proposal also rests on the consideration that high-efficiency cogeneration 
allows a better use of the primary sources used, whether fossil or renewable, and a reduction in greenhouse 
gas emis sions. In addition, we believe that, in order to preserve the continuity of operation of existing 
cogeneration facilities, new constraints should only apply to new cogeneration projects. 
Besides, we call for further strengthening the role of energy prosumers and energy communities in line with 
the European Commission's communication published on 13 October 2021 "Tackling rising energy prices: a 
toolbox for action and support". In fact, the text of the communication identifies support to prosumers and 
the development of energy communities as measures that can help tackling future price fluctuations.

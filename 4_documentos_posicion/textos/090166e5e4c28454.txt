 
European Alliance to Save Energy on the evaluation initiative on the Energy 
Efficiency Directive recast 
19 November 2021 
The European Alliance to Save Energy (EU-ASE) welcomes the opportunity to provide feedback to the 
European Commission’s evaluation on the recast of the Energy Efficiency Directive (EED). The Alliance 
brings together businesses, think -tanks and Members of t he European Parliament to ensure that the 
voice of energy efficiency is heard across the business and political community. 
On 14 July 2021, the European Commission unveiled its Fit for 55 package, including the key proposal 
to revise the Energy Efficiency Directive (EED) to make it fit for the increased 55% GHG emission 
reduction target for 2030.  
 
The proposal contains a number of positive elements. First of all, it enshrines the Energy Efficiency 
First principle (EE1st) in the Directive, introducing the much-needed obligation to apply the principle 
in the decision making of energy and non-energy sectors. However: 
• The application of the principle still remains vague. EU-ASE recommends that the application 
of the principle should be systemic, not just concerning major investments. Limiting the scope 
to projects with a value above a 50 million EUR threshold would leave out  of scope most of 
buildings renovations and thus would hinder the effectiveness of the EE1st principle. 
• We believe publicly available indicators should be used for the purpose of the monitoring in 
the NECPs and to harmonise the approach on the cost-benefit analysis.  
• The good wording appearing in the EE1 Guidelines published in September 2021 should be 
transferred into the EED.  
 
EU-ASE regrets that the Commission chose not to propose binding national targets on energy 
efficiency. Furthermore, we are critical of the Commission’s decision to propose an EU wide energy 
efficiency target for 2030 that, even if binding, is not aligned with the EU’s decarbonisation pathway.  
• The Commission introduced a target for 36% reduction in final energy consumption and 39% 
reduction in primary energy consumption, which does not catch the cost -effective 
opportunities for increased energy efficiency. EU-ASE recommends to set a binding target of 
at least 40% energy efficiency by 2030. 
• The EU binding target should be complemented by binding national contributions. Member 
States should calculate their contributions by using the formula of Annex I and not deviate 
from its result by using the subjective criteria included in Article 4.2. 
• National trajectories should be set with a linear pathway and include binding milestones. 
 
Another important element of the proposal is the extension of the public owned buildings renovation 
obligation to all public bodies as well as the introduction of a new obligation to cut energy 
consumption of public bodies by 1.7% annually. While EU-ASE welcomes this important step forward, 
we would like to raise the following points: 
• The buildings renovation obligation would only fall on buildings owned by public bodies but 
not those only occupied. EU -ASE recommends to extend the scope to buildings owned and 
occupied by public bodies.

• The EED proposal only targets buildings owned by public bodies . However, private non -
residential buildings (tertiary sector)  have similar energy consumption patterns than those 
from the public sector. Furthermore, tertiary buildings represent 25% of the total buil ding 
floor space. EU-ASE recommends extending the renovation obligation of the EED to buildings 
from the private non-residential sector.  
• The recital 29  acknowledges that  public bodies can realise  energy savings  in wastewater 
management, sewage and water p urification. This is a good basis to untap the full potential 
of saving water and energy alike . EU-ASE recommends to include in Article 2 a definition of 
the water-energy nexus. Article 5 should also require that regional and local authorities must 
consider both water and energy efficiency measures in their decarbonisation plans. 
 
The revised Directive also reinforces the annual energy savings obligations target after 2024 by 1.5%, 
almost doubling the current obligation. EU-ASE welcomes that the EED excludes the accountability of 
direct fossil fuel combustion technologies and clarif ies that a reduction of the energy use through 
measures under the ETS cannot count towards the fulfilment of the energy savings obligation.  
However: 
• An increased EED target to at least 40% means that the annual energy savings obligation must 
go beyond the proposed 1.5% annual savings. 
• EU-ASE recommends reinforcing the provisions on energy poverty to define more precise 
actions and objectives.  
 
The EED recast also addresses the need to increase energy efficiency in the private sector  through 
audits and energy management systems. The EED introduced already an obligation to large companies 
to undertake an energy audit every four years.  Yet many companies still consider these audits an 
administrative burden rather than a useful tool for improving efficiency.  
• EU-ASE recommends to make mandatory the application of recommendations stemming from 
energy audits, especially those with a short payback period. We also recommend to set 
financial incentives for companies applying recommendations from energy audits having an 
energy management system. 
• We recommend as well to extend the scope of audits to cover the water use, to identify the 
energy savings potential that can be delivered though water efficiency measures. 
 
About us 
The European Alliance to Save Energy (EU-ASE) aims to advance the energy efficiency agenda in the 
European Union. The Alliance allows world’s leading multinational companies to join environmental 
campaigners and a cross -party group of Members of the European Parliament. EU -ASE business 
members have operations across the 27 Member States of the European Union, employ over 340.000 
people in Europe and have an aggregated annual turnover of €115 billion.

 
 
1 
EEB comments to the Open Public  
Consultation on the Targeted Revision of 
CLP  
November 2021 
Additionally to the issues raised in the public consultation, the EEB asks the Commission to:  
• Modify the decision-making procedures so there is no political discussion about classification, 
labelling and packaging of hazardous chemicals. The procedure to classify a substance or mixture 
must facilitate the objectives of the CLP Regulation, that are to determine the hazardous properties 
requiring classification, and to properly identify and communicate these hazards. Hence, CLP has no 
room for political considerations such as the economic impact on companies of classification or 
communication on the hazards. In our view, harmonised classifications and labelling should be an 
ECHA’s Executive Director’s decision that follows the scientific opinion of the Risk Assessment 
Committee (RAC). 
• Improve transparency: the development of product passports should centralise CLP data on 
classifications and labelling of hazardous chemicals: a safety data sheets’ database; and labelling to 
ensure that citizens have information on hazardous chemicals in products. CLP revision should also 
oblige ECHA to publish the contact data of all notifiers. 
• Introducing clarifications on the definition of UVCBs in the legal text to ensure a proper identification 
and classification of the substances. 
• Guarantee that derogations for classifications are only possible when supported by scientific 
evidence. 
• Establish an automatic harmonisation on self-classifications to avoid diverging self-classifications, 
where the most hazardous self-classification is the one harmonised. 
• Consider options for implementing the polluter pays principle and give enough resources to 
authorities to implement and enforce REACH and CLP. 
• Substantially improve impact assessment: make sure that due consideration is given to benefits of 
regulation for human health and environment.

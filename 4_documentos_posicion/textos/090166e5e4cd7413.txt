COMMENTS TO THE OPEN PUBLIC CONSULTATION ON THE TARGETED REVISION 
OF THE REGULATION ON CLASSIFICATION, LABELLING AND PACKAGING OF 
SUBSTANCES AND MIXTURES 
Below are complementary comments on the Open Public Consultation on the Targeted 
Revision of the Regulation on Classification, Labelling and Packaging of Substances and 
Mixtures (CLP), given by IKEM – Innovation and Chemical Industries in Sweden. 
 
5a Consumers, professional and industrial users often have diverging needs on 
chemical information as knowledge and training in chemical safety can differ a lot. 
Using digital labels, we see the opportunity to tailor information to different users to 
ensure maximum benefit for everyone. In any way, simplification and digitalization 
must not be accompanied by the loss of safety-relevant information. 
7a Depending on the packaging, use and point of sales, the information can be shared 
in different ways, digitally and/or physically. 
8a Depending on the packaging, use and point of sales, the information can be shared 
in different ways, digitally and/or physically. 
15 Differentiation between Cat 2 and 1 should refer to the degree of evidence, in this 
case the degree of evidence for causality/plausibility of the adverse effect being 
caused by an endocrine mode of action (MoA). 
 
All three elements of the criteria have to be met by an endocrine disruptor: 
• it shows an adverse effect in an intact organism or its progeny; 
• it shows endocrine activity; 
• the substance has an endocrine disrupting mode of action, i.e. there is a 
biologically consequence between the endocrine activity and the adverse 
effect. 
19 Care should be taken not to confuse existing pictograms under UN GHS with 
PBT/vPvB. Therefore we suggest no pictogram or possible GHS07 for PBT/vPvB.    
22 Care should be taken not to confuse existing pictograms under UN GHS with 
PMT/vPvM. Therefore we suggest no pictogram or possible GHS07 for PMT/vPvM.    
28 We are currently running an impact assessment. The results from this will be 
presented as soon as they are available.  
29 European Commission has the mandate to decide on harmonized classification 
which is proposed by RAC. We therefore think it would be biased if the Commission 
both have the power to initiate CLH dossiers and decide on the same. The 
commission should ensure that the risk management is appropriate, and this would 
no longer be possible if the commission is also involved in the CLH process by 
initiating dossiers. 
 
IKEM would however welcome simpler and smoother procedures and the ability for 
industry to directly submit CLH dossiers to ECHA, to revise existing CLH cases when 
new data becomes available that could challenge the existing classification.  
32 IKEM believe that it is important to prioritize substances of e.g. very high concern 
for CLH dossiers. It is important to note that the current system already allows this 
prioritization. Furthermore, ECHA’s integrated regulatory strategy prioritizes 
substances for regulatory actions among which the CLH process is one. An additional

34 The answer to this question would depend on the endpoint and the 
validation/reliability of the study in question. In order to take an in vitro finding on

35 There may be good reasons that classifications differ for the same substance. 
However, ECHA should ensure that companies can remove and/or change 
classifications in case these are wrong or not valid anymore.    
36 Re-labelling may be needed for each individual shipment entering or leaving EU 
since CLP labelling and GHS labelling for other jurisdictions are not always

46 Sectorial legislation takes into account use-relevant information/instruction for each 
product sector. Question 46 is therefore to be discussed with experts in the specific 
industry sectors, and should not be addressed under the CLP regulation. 
49 IKEM would like to stress the importance of taking downstream uses into account 
when revising the CLP regulation. In this survey there are no questions addressing 
the possible consequences for uses of chemicals under downstream legislation, or 
any questions concerning the costs for downstream sectors as a result of e.g. new 
hazard classifications. All different interactions between CLP and downstream 
legislation should be thoroughly taken into account in the impact assessment as the 
real impact lies in the resulting regulatory consequences like restrictions.

France Invest’s comments on the Commission’s proposed  
Legislative package on Anti-Money Laundering and Countering the Financing of Terrorism 
 
 
Established nearly 40 years ago, France Invest brings together venture capital, private equity, 
infrastructure and private debt teams based in France, as well as the associated professions which 
support them. Its membership currently counts 387 management firms and 173 associate members1.  
Venture capital and private equity support unlisted companies for a fixed period of time and provides 
them with the equity capital, through the acquisition of minority or majority stakes in their capital, 
needed to finance growth and transformation projects. It supports the creation of start-ups (venture 
capital), participates in the growth and transformation of many regional SMEs and mid-caps (growth 
capital) and contributes to the transfer of companies (replacement capital).  
France Invest’s members represent one of the main growth drivers for the French and European 
economy and support a significant portion of employment in France and Europe. In 2020, French private 
equity and infrastructure players invested €23.1 billion in 2,150 companies and infrastructure projects. 
They raised €23.5 billion from investors, a third of which at international level, which will be invested 
over the next 5 years. In addition to that, private debt players (structures financing companies and 
infrastructure projects) invested €8.1 billion in 209 transactions and raised €7.7 billion that will finance 
new transactions in the coming years. European companies, in particular start-ups and SMEs, are the 
main recipients of these investments. In 2019, companies backed by French venture capital and private 
equity created 56,000 jobs.

Preliminary remarks 
France Invest considers the fight against money laundering and the financing of terrorism as a 
priority. In France, the 5th AML-CFT Directive was transposed in February 2020 and “an extremely 
strong regulation and a robust preventive framework”2 for financial services was put in place.  
France Invest is very active in the field of AML-CFT, in particular in the context of its Compliance 
Committee (created in 2013), and provides its members with tools to enhance their capacities in this 
respect (e.g. training sessions & e-learning tools (also available in English)3).   
Our members are regulated entities authorised by the French financial markets authority (AMF) and 
subject to AML-CFT obligations with regards both their clients, in the context of their fund-raising 
activity, and their investment and divestment operations, in the context of their transactional activity.  
 As far as their fund-raising activity is concerned, they are required to perform due diligence 
following a risk-based approach, which in particular includes the identification of politically 
exposed persons and beneficial owners. In general, institutional fund investors are considered 
as lower risk customers who tend to undergo thorough due diligence processes, including 
AML-CFT related know your customer, before being accepted as investors. On the other hand, 
non-institutional investors (such as family offices) are likely to pose a greater risk from an AML-
CFT perspective due to their more complicated ownership structures and the presence of trust 
arrangements.  
 
 As for their transactional activity, risks may arise not only at the time of an original investment, 
but also throughout its ongoing ownership and its exit. 
A key challenge for our members lies in the identification of the ultimate beneficial owners of “unit 
common funds” (“fonds communs de placement”). Indeed, no register of beneficiaries of these 
vehicles is currently available in all Member States. Therefore, we ask that a register of beneficial 
owners of “unit common funds” is put in place in all Member States, which would allow our members 
to fulfil their legal obligations. 
 
Comments on the legal package proposed by the Commission  
From a general standpoint, France Invest welcomes the package of legislative proposals published by 
the Commission to strengthen European AML-CFT rules.

We understand that the main objective of this package is to focus on the fight against money 
laundering and the financing of terrorism and to respond to scandals which caused severe reputational 
damages to the financial system, and which highlighted inefficiencies and a lack of coordination among 
authorities. We support the aim of a more harmonised implementation of the rules and supervision 
throughout the EU in order to ensure a level playing field among Member States. 
In our view, the legislative package proposed by the Commission will also have an impact on the fight 
against tax evasion. For instance, the Authority for Anti-Money Laundering and Countering the 
Financing of Terrorism (AMLA) will interact with national tax authorities and thus contribute to 
improved tax enforcement. 
As explained above, our members and the funds they manage (AIFMs and AIFs) are already subject to 
AML-CFT regulations. It should therefore be ensured that the requirements set out in the proposed 
Regulation and the proposed Directive articulate smoothly with those included the AIFMD.  
In this context, we would like to raise the attention of the Commission on the broad scope of obliged 
entities as defined in the proposed Regulation, which includes financial entities, in particular collective 
investment undertakings and more specifically AIFs. Indeed, some of the requirements set out in the 
proposed Regulation, such as requirements on internal policies, controls and procedures, are not 
relevant for alternative investment funds. 
We support the aim of streamlining the existing rules in this field and the creation of a Single Rule Book 
and of a consistent implementation across the Member States. In particular, we understand the need 
for a more harmonised reporting and welcome the introduction of a template for reporting suspicious 
transactions for use throughout the EU. However, we would like to warn against any additional 
reporting obligations for our members.  
We welcome the proposed enhancements to the effective beneficiaries’ registers. The detailed rules 
on the identity of beneficial owners of companies and other legal entities contained in the proposed 
Regulation will allow a full harmonisation at EU level and increased powers for entities in charge of the 
national register of beneficial owners to verify that the information submitted to the register of 
beneficial owners is accurate, adequate and up to date, including through on-site audits will improve 
the quality of data in these registers. Also, the interconnection of registers and a single point of access 
will ease the access and use of this data for our members. However, we warn against any additional 
burden for our members in this field.  
We would like to take this opportunity to highlight that a key challenge for our members lies in the 
identification of the ultimate beneficial owners of “unit common funds” (“fonds communs de 
placement”). Considering that the AIFMD covers alternative investment funds (AIFs) whatever their 
legal form (please refer to recital 6 of the Directive), we believe that registers of beneficial owners 
should exist for all AIFs, whether they have legal personality or not, in all Member States, so that our 
members can meet their legal obligations. Therefore, we call for putting in place a register of beneficial 
owners of “unit common funds” in the Member States where it is currently not available. 
It will have to be ensured that the collection of data in such registers is compliant with the rules 
contained in the GDPR. 
We support the creation of the AMLA, which will become a key player in an integrated AML-CFT 
supervisory system, together with the national authorities with an AML-CFT supervisory mandate, to 
address the current shortcomings in AML-CFT supervision in the Union. In our opinion, the AMLA will 
 
 
contribute to an enhanced regulatory framework and a more harmonised supervision across the EU. 
We would like to note that, according to the Commission’s current proposal, most of our members are 
unlikely to fall under direct supervision of the new AMLA. They might however be in some specific 
cases supervised, and possibly sanctioned, by the AMLA. Therefore, it is important that a smooth 
articulation is ensured between the remit of the AMLA and that of national authorities, in particular 
during the transition period when competences will be transferred to the AMLA. For instance, the 
French ACPR and the AMF are granted sanctioning powers: it should be ensured that supervised 
entities are not subject to a double supervisory and a double sanctioning regime, at French and EU 
level. 
Last, we would like to note that clarifications on internal policy procedures and controls (obligations 
regarding the policy to be put in place to identify and assess AML-CFT risks, the role of the compliance 
officer, groups) and due diligence on clients (sufficient knowledge and identification of customers, 
including through electronic means), will be included in future level 2 measures; it is therefore difficult 
for us, at this stage, to comprehensively assess the potential impact of the new rules on our 
members.

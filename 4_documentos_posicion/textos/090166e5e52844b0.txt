Introduction 
Onfido is a global remote identity verification provider that partners with 1,600 organisations 
worldwide, including many EU fintechs such as Adyen, Bunq, Getaround, Nickel, Revolut, Wise 
and Monese.  
 
Our leading biometric and AI technology enables clients to prove that their customers are 
who they claim to be, enabling them to comply with regulatory obligations including AML and 
KYC requirements in the EU. Onfido works by scanning a user’s photo ID to detect whether 
it’s genuine and compares this to a selfie or a short video taken by the user to check facial 
biometrics match those on the photo ID and ensure the person is the live and genuine owner. 
We use a hybrid model that combines machine learning with human expert oversight, which 
delivers best-in-class speed, consistency, and accuracy.   
 
Started just nine years ago, we have scaled rapidly and now employ over  100 people across 
the EU and more than 550 worldwide, with offices in Paris, Lisbon, Amsterdam, London, and 
Berlin as well as the US, Singapore, and India. We are continuing to invest and grow our EU 
presence to meet increasing customer demand in both the fintech space and other verticals.  
 
We are thought leaders in AI bias, ethics, fraud, security, and privacy. We were winners of the 
2020 CogX Award  for “Best Innovation in Algorithmic Bi as Mitigation” and “Outstanding 
Leader in Accessibility” and were “Highly Commended” in the SC Europe Awards 2020  for 
“Best Use of Machine Learning”. We work with Interpol to develop leading p ractice in fraud

We embrace the opportunity to share our expertise and experience to help develop

Overall assessment 
Remote identity proofing services such as biometrics, ID/document verification and database 
checks are proven methods of establishing genuine identity to a high level of assurance. Such 
checks can establish that the user requesting the service: 
 
● is tru ly there without the need of a witness (with further advances, this will also 
capture if the person is being forced); 
● is a genuine user with non-fraudulent documents; and 
● is not a politically exposed/sanctioned individual. 
 
These are key factors in facilit ating compliance with “know your customer” and associated 
AML customer due diligence requirements. Furthermore, remote identity verification and 
remote identity authentication services have the flexibility to add additional safeguards 
where needed, allowing these methods to adapt to stay ahead of bad actors. It is crucial to 
the success of AML and associated fraud measures that EU regulation facilitates the adoption 
of such services without national barriers.  
 
The AML package provides an opportunity to further harmonise the existing ruleset and thus 
facilitate a more unified, highly robust, and standardised framework which reduces Internal 
Market barriers, promotes security, and more effectively prevents money laundering and 
terrorist financing. 
 
We welcome the decision to shift private sector AML obligations from a Directive to a 
Regulation. This change is long overdue, and we hope and expect it will lead to a significantly 
more harmonised implementation and enforcement of European AML rules.  
 
It is also important to recognise the global nature of the issues addressed in these proposals. 
It is vitally important that standards and rules set in the EU have interoperability at their heart, 
so firms and associated third parties helping them to comply are able to scale efficiently and 
without barriers.  
 
AML Authority 
The establishment of the EU’s AML Authority (AMLA) is also  a very welcome development 
and shows the EU’s clear commitment to better resourcing AML supervisors. Through the 
AMLA, the EU will create a vehicle through which national competent authorities will be able 
to closely cooperate, and we would hope and expec t that this will lead to less divergence in 
rule implementation at Member State level. Single supervision of larger entities by the AMLA 
will also allow such entities to complete their AML obligations without having to worry about 
differing levels of supervision in each Member State. This should result in the streamlining of 
compliance functions, and the realisation of associated efficiencies.

The main drawback of the current proposal setting up the AMLA is the amount of time it will 
take to set the organ isation up. The 2024 operation date is too far ahead, and we urge the 
co-legislators to push for a shorter implementation timeline. This is both to allow for 
supervision of large entities to transition to the AMLA at a faster pace, as well as to let the 
AMLA begin its work on AML technical standards sooner, thus creating more policy and 
operational certainty for those impacted by the new AML package. 
 
Separately, we also believe that limiting the AMLA’s jurisdiction (specifically only for financial 
services) is problematic. We align ourselves with the same risk same rules principle, and thus 
believe that the jurisdiction of the AMLA should be framed based on the risk profile of obliged 
entities rather than the sector the entities find themselves in.

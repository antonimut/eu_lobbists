EBA feedback – CountEmissions EU initiative 
 
The EBA considers as important the development of a  common framework to calculate and 
report transport-related greenhouse gas emissions. We acknowledge that the task is demanding 
and laborious. We do not ignore that the development of the PEF calculation method by the JRC 
took 10 years.  
One of the most determinant issues is how to use the calculation method. The discussions on 
how and where the method should be used should take place once the  method has been 
developed. The assumptions of the calculation method should  be comprehensive and 
technology-neutral and the method should be based on accurate data. Without this, the results 
of the calculation will not be precise and cannot be extrapolated , certainly not in legislative 
obligations.  
Another aspect to consider is the potential overlapping with other pieces of  legislation already 
in place to limit CO2 emissions of fuels and vehicles: REDII for renewable energy (life-cycle; CO2 
and other environmental impacts), Regulations of CO2 performance standards for vehicles (use 
phase; only CO2), ETS for maritime and road transport (use phase; only CO2), energy tax directive 
(production and use phases; CO2 and other environmental impacts). In order to have zero -
emission in transport, overall control on the emis sions over the whole life cycle of the 
product/service is needed . However, a t present, there is no  comprehensive management of 
transport emissions. The legislation is being developed and split between different Directorates-
General of the European Commission.  
METHODOLOGICAL ISSUES 
Measuring the GHG-footprint is a major step, but the consideration of  other environmental 
impacts is also relevant. For instance, impact on ozone layer, losses in biodiversity and soil quality 
are important global  challenges. Th is impact  should be adequately considered  in th e 
development of legislation.  
For GHG emissions, the method to calculate emissions should include the real emissions from 
cradle to grave. We support life-cycle approach, which is in our opinion the most comprehensive 
way of assessing environmental impacts of products and services.  
The methodological solutions and assumptions of the calculations will have a substantial impact 
on the results. As biomethane production is based on biowaste, the GHG protocol sees its usage 
as biogenic CO2, coming from the CO2 captured by the plant during its lifetime, contrary to the 
CO2 emissions from fossil fuels that comes from a thousand -year carbon sink . Additionally, 
decisions such as how to allocate emissions between by -products are critical for biomethane. 
Biomethane production is closely linked to waste management and nutrient recycling, so in most 
cases the purpose is not only energy production. For instance, digestate, a natural fertilizer, can 
be used to revitalize the soil and in turn, this minimizes the use of mineral fertilizers (also called 
chemical fertilizers), and therefore avoids further CO2 emissions (production and transportation 
of fertilizers) and impacts on soil qual ity. Biomethane is also produced locally avoiding CO2

DATA 
It is crucial to use accurate data in the calculation. When it comes to biogas and  its upgraded 
form, biomethane, the type of feedstock used is key to understand the environmental impact of 
the production process: manure or dedicated crops have not the same impact. Different values 
or references should be used at the European level depending on the feedstock used . For 
instance, biomethane (fuel) produced out of manure can have negative CO2 emissions. The CO2 
emitted from biomethane production using biowaste as feedstock  is about 8 5-60% lower 
compared to fossil fuels. 
EXPERTISE  
The JRC has done a comprehensive work on assessing CO2 emissions from Well-to-Wheel 
approach and developing PEF-method. Additionally, it is important to include stakeholders in the 
development work.

About the EBA  
 
The European Biogas Association is the voice of renewable gas in Europe since 2009. EBA advocates the 
recognition of biomethane and other renewable gases as sustainable, on demand and flexible energy 
sources that provide multiple knock -on socio-economic and environmental bene fits. Supported by its 
members, EBA is committed to work with European institutions, industry, agricultural partners, NGOs and 
academia to develop policies which can enable the large -scale deployment of renewable gases and 
organic fertilisers throughout Eu rope, supported by transparent, well -established sustainability 
certification bodies to ensure that sustainability remains at the core of the industry. The association counts 
today on a well -established network of over 200 national organisations, scientifi c institutes, and 
companies from Europe and beyond.

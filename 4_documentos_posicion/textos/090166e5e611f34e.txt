December 2021  
 
Response to Call for evidence 
Count your transport emissions – ‘CountEmissions EU’ 
ECG – The Association of European Vehicle Logistics  is the voice of the Finished Vehicle Logistics 
industry in Europe, representing the interests of over 140 member companies and partners operating 
rail, road, maritime and fluvial modes at European level.  
ECG appreciates the opportunity to provide feedback to the “CountEmissions EU” initiative aiming at 
providing a common framework for calculating GHG emissions of transport operations in the freight 
and passenger transport sectors.   
ECG believes that greenhouse gas (GHG)  accounting is one of the instruments towards achieving the 
climate objectives established at EU level amongst others . A common European framework would 
enable the transport and logistics sector to account for GHG emissions in a coherent, comparable, and 
meaningful way across sectors. 
By welcoming this initiative, ECG also recommends to particularly consider the aspects below. 
Avoiding duplication of effort 
There are currently efforts to harmonise existing methodologies to account for emissions from the 
transport sector at global level. Furthermore, some EU Member States have  already mandated 
transport companies to report on emissions at national level  according to specific 
guidelines/frameworks. These ongoing practices should be extensively researched and taken into 
account for the deve lopment of a common European framework  in order not to increase the 
administrative burden , costs  and hinder efficiency of international companies  which have already  
carbon accounting structures in place to account for emissions.  
Defining geographical scope 
The geographical scope of the common framework should be European. However, many companies 
operate beyond the borders of Europe, and they should be able to account for emissions in a 
harmonised way. An effort should be made to allow alignment and flexibility between a European 
standard and a global one such as the upcoming ISO 14083. 
Harmonising use of emissions factors 
The European effort in this context should be aimed, amongst others, at harmonising GHG emissions 
accounting aspects which would already enable transport operators to calculate own emissions using 
similar data sets i.e. EU emissions factors for different types of fuels or electricity mixes. A mechanism 
should also be  put in place to ensure timely updating and review ing to ensure accu racy and reflect 
changes and innovations. 
Allowing for specificities 
The common framework should give guidance but also acknowledge and allow for  specificities of 
certain sectors such as the finished vehicle logistics sector . Specificities are in this case originated in 
the types of assets used  or in the way the assets are used which is unique to the sector . An example 
is Ro-Ro shipping at European level which is different from deep sea shipping at global level.

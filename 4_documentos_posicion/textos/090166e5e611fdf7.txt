1. Varied sector – a “one size fits all” approach does not reflect the hospitality market
In the public consultation on STRs, thechoices under respondent “categories” arelimitedtohotels,online booking platforms, other services, individual host, professional host, public authority or others.
We find that these discrete options do not reflect the variety of business models in this space, inparticular hybridbusiness models suchas Sonder’s - whereweoffer traditional hotel,aparthotel andprofessionally-managed servicedapartment properties. Whilethis perhaps reflects alimitationof thesurvey format, wehopethat goingforwardpolicymakers takeintoaccount thedynamicnatureofthehospitality market and the diversity of business models within it.
2. Registration and licensing schemes should be targeted
Webelievethat afutureinitiativeshouldbetargetedatspecificpolicyconcerns,andnotautomaticallyimpose regulatory burden onawiderangeof operators. TheCommissiondraws auseful distinctionbetween professional operators (who generally have processes in place to manage issues) andamateur or “peer” operators (where there are potentially more concerns about safety and security).
One good use case, and potential source of inspiration for EU regulators, could be Scotland.Recently, the Government adopted a new Short-TermLets regulation which excludes “aparthotels”(including managed serviced apartments) from licensing obligations. The Scottish Governmentapproach takes into account distinctions in business models - in particular, there is a targetedexemption for “aparthotels” that arerunby professional operators with5+units inabuildingandnoshared access with mainstream residents.
FromSonder’s point of view, thereis anobvious differenceinthetypes of operationsundertakenbyprofessional hospitality businesses in comparison to independently owned private flats/housingrentals which are the focus of these regulations. Sonder accommodations are high-qualityprofessional hospitality operations. Wehaveapartments that occupy awholebuildingandareunderthe control of one Sonder entity as operator. Importantly, Sonder apartments are already fullycompliant withexistinghealthandsafetymeasures,includingfireriskassessments,gasandelectricalsafety.
A new Scottish licensing regime that also covered operators like ourselves would have meantsubstantial additional costs tolicenceaparthotel /managedservicedapartmentbuildings,wherehighstandards are already in place. We therefore believe that the Scottish regulatory approachdemonstrates a sensible recognition of different business models.
3. New data collection requirements should be meaningful
The EU’s future regulatory framework for STRs shouldnot beduplicativeof existingmeasures, andshouldalsobefocusedonspecific areas that arethemost important forpolicymaking.Thisistrueinrelation to data, where there are already existinginitiatives onplatformdatasharing, includingdataobligations from the Digital Services Act (DSA). Good quality data is important for good policy,however care should be taken to avoid imposing disproportionate additional data collection andreporting burdens on business.

EHHA Position Paper 
SHORT TERM RENTAL INITIATIVE 
 
European Holiday Home Association welcomes the chance to input into the European 
Commission’s work on an EU short-term rental (STR) initiative. 
As a voice for STRs, our membership is diverse: representing individual owners, nano, micro 
STR property managers, entrepreneurs, property managers (SMEs), online platforms, and other 
business owners who cater to the STR sector. All of these groups were severely impacted by COVID-19 
but struggled to receive access to support funds and programs, which were often not built with a 
diversity of actors in mind. Nonetheless EHHA members are now playing a vital role during the COVID-
19 pandemic by providing offerings that are widely dispersed across European towns and regions (not 
just in city centres), and by funnelling the economic benefits of travel into wider communities by 
encouraging local spending and contributing income to STR providers.  
We continuously engage with policy makers to support harmonized, proportionate, clear and 
justified regulation at all levels. We trust this will ensure the safe and sustainable growth of STR activity, 
such that it can contribute to Europe’s recovery, while providing authori ties with the necessary 
transparency and oversight they need to enforce local rules. Unfortunately, too often local STR rules 
continue to be unclear, unjustified, disproportionate, and, on occasion, contravene EU legal 
frameworks. Many are under constant review, not published in a central website, introduce different 
obligations for different categories of STR service providers, and even have different rules within any 
one city - creating a hyper -fragmented regulatory landscape that exacerbates legal uncert ainty and 
poses challenges for effective enforcement.  
This is why the EHHA stresses the importance of – and strongly welcomes - the EU -level 
intervention to help reduce regulatory fragmentation and increase legal certainty for all (STR players, 
national/local governments and consumers). While local authorities should always have the power to 
legislate according to local situations, the EU has a role to play in1: 
● defining an EU standardised STR registration (notification) scheme that provides the 
procedure in some parts of the EU to give public authorities means to identify STR 
market players for the purposes of transparency and better policy making; 
● specifying the EU rules public authorities should always respect when deciding to 
impose market access requirements to STR services, to ensure that such requirements 
respect Single Market principles (i.e. necessity, justification, proportionality and non -
discrimination)2, that they are not overly-restrictive or burdensome, and duly reviewed 
via the TRIS process; 
● creating common and consistent approach for online STR platforms to share and report 
data, in line with GDPR, and clarifying what additional data should be shared (if any) 
which is not already covered by the EU legal framework (e.g.  DAC7, DSA, Data Act);

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY  
The STR ecosystem goes far beyond the major and well-known peers (individual hosts) and STR 
platforms and includes a wide variety of STR players of different sizes and shapes. In support of these 
different STR players we find a landscape of STR ancillary services providers.  
EHHA highlights that STR players would benefit from clear market access conditions across the 
EU if a following tiered approach would be strictly followed by authorities in Member States (the 
introduction of each more far -reaching step is only possible if the previous step has been taken and 
clear evidence shows that taking the next step is necessary and proportionate to achieve the policy 
objectives): 
TIER 1: Understanding of the complexity of the STR Ecosystem shall be in place:  EHHA stresses 
that the first step towards a better policy making at a national/regional/local level is to understand the 
complexity of the STR ecosystem, its players and the impact on local communities. No (or low level of) 
understanding of a STR ecosystem at a national/regional/l ocal policy making level always leads to 
stricter, unjustified, disproportionate and discriminative STR rules (such as change of use of a property, 
consent of neighbors, night caps, hotel like requirements for individual hosts and bans). 
TIER 2: An opt -in EU registration (notification) scheme at a national level (where cities can 
opt-in or not): EHHA supports the necessary transparency needed for local authorities to oversee STR 
activity and enforce their rules and we believe that STR registration is a great starting point. EHHA is in 
favour of an opt-in EU registration (notification) scheme for Member States (where cities can opt in if 
needed or not) as an important and a must take second step which allows collection of the missing 
data over STR activity. The European Commission could standardize such a registration (notification) 
scheme at the EU level by introducing  a number of principles (as elaborated below) which should be 
strictly followed by Member States which wish to adapt their schemes o r simply opt in . EHHA also 
supports a STR database at the EU level to the extent that the underlying STR rules in Member States 
are proportionate, justified and non-discriminatory. 
TIER 3:  Proportionate and non -discriminative authorisation scheme at a loca l level only if 
data collected under TIER 2 and additional impact assessment prove a causal link between STRs and 
the need to protect the general interest: EHHA understands that there may be a limited number of 
local situations, where registration (notification) scheme provides data over the STR activity and proves 
(in addition to impact assessments)  that there is a causal link between the STR activity and the general 
interest which needs to be protected (for example, collected data shows that there are ma ny STRs in 
an area within a city which faces pressure in housing). In such cases, local authorities could move 
beyond the registration (notification) scheme and introduce an authorisation (licensing/permit) 
scheme, subject it strictly complies with the Services Directive and a number of principles (elaborated 
below). Authorisation (licensing/permit) should never be a compulsory third step but rather as an 
option to protect the general interest subject the causal link was proved. 
In light of the data sharing , EHHA believes that the STR initiative should not create an 
unnecessary duplication of rules on data sharing, but rather seek to fill in eventual gaps, achieve 
specific policy objectives that have not yet been reached, and most importantly provide some much-
needed harmonisation to data requests. Any data requests made by tax and other local authorities 
should be covered by existing or future EU frameworks (DAC7, VAT Directive, DSA and/or Data Act), 
rather than by diverging local regulations. Such requests must also be mandated by law and should be

1. GENERAL MARKET INFORMATION: STR ECOSYSTEM AND ITS IMPACT 
Short term rentals have been around since the 19th century, but they became more visible in 
the last decade due to the rise of online platforms.  
While in principle sh ort term rentals provide an overnight stay for leisure and business 
travellers as other accommodation channels, by nature they are very different: 
● STRs can be owned or managed by property owners , property mangers and/or other 
STR players; 
● Pricing can be set by an owner or by a company; 
● STR service (for example, cleaning) can be done by owners themselves, by professional 
cleaning companies or by property managers.  
● While other accommodation channels usually provide a single room for an overnight 
stay, STR accommodation provides a vast choice for consumers: they can stay in shared 
or non-shared rooms, apartments, homes, caravans, lodges, camping caravans, villas, 
tree houses, castles and many more.  
● STRs are usually family oriented (with children and pets) accommodation channel which 
allows an overnight stay like at home away from home.  
● By nature, STRs do not provide other services than of an overnight stay – all services 
which are needed to further cater the STR guests and/or STR players to start and 
maintain the STR activity are supported by businesses in local communities (please have 
a look at the chart below, the part on ‘Impact on Local Community’).  
● As many supporting the STR activity services are being outsourced, STRs are a labour 
heavy activity3.

2.ISSUES RELATED TO TRANSPARENCY 
EHHA understands the need for and supports the use of STR data to facilitate evidence -based 
policymaking and to gain more visibility on the STR sector. A more harmonised framework for data 
collection and reporting would help address these needs – as well a s those of the industry. Such a 
framework should however be based on basic principles including the fair, proportional and justified 
(data) requests, and the protection of personal data (GDPR).  
At the moment, the vacation rental sector still faces a numbe r of challenges with regards to 
data requests from public authorities, particularly in relation to tax reporting by hosts and guest 
occupancy for security purposes. STR platforms often experience an increasing number of requests for 
personal data from mult iple levels of governance – local, regional and national – on transactions 
carried out through their sites. Such requests not only place an excessive administrative burden on 
companies, but often concern data which platforms do not have access to in the first place. On top of 
this, often the requests are unjustified or not evidence -based. In light of this, EHHA would strongly 
support the creation of a more harmonised, principle -based data sharing framework to be applied 
across Europe. As outlined in the EHH A and EUTT’s Roadmap for STR sector 8, our members will 
continue to support cooperation with public authorities to facilitate compliance by accommodation 
providers on issues including guest occupancy, taxes and the reporting of earnings. 
This being said, the European Commission should make sure to avoid duplication with already 
existing or upcoming measures related to data sharing. There are already a high number of initiatives 
addressing data sharing, such as recordkeeping obligations for electronic interfaces under the EU VAT 
Directive; data sharing obligations for payment service providers under Council Directive 2020/284;

TIER 3. Proportionate and non -discriminative authorisation scheme at a local level only if data 
collected under TIER 2 and additional impact assessment prove a causal link between STRs and the 
need to protect the general interest 
EHHA understands that there may be a limited number of local situations, where registration 
(notification) scheme provides data over the STR activity and proves that there is a causal link between 
the STR activity and the general interest which need s to be protected (for example, collected data 
shows that there are many STRs in an area within a city which faces pressure in housing). In such cases, 
local authorities could move beyond the registration (notification) scheme and introduce an 
authorisation (licensing/permit) scheme, subject it strictly complies with the Services Directive and the 
following principles: 
● Step-by-step approach : Authorisation (licensing/permit) scheme can only be 
introduced if the complexity of the STR ecosystem is clearly unde rstood and data 
collected under TIER 2 and additional (regular) impact assessment prove a causal link 
between STRs and the need to protect the general interest. Authorisation 
(licensing/permit) should never be a compulsory third step but rather as an optio n to 
protect the general interest subject the causal link was proved. 
● Limited in time : authorisation (licensing/permit) schemes should be constantly 
revised22 and stopped when there is no need anymore to protect the general interest 
or regulation of STRs had no significant effect on improving the situation. 
● Limited to local level/areas : authorisation (licensing/permit) schemes should be in 
place for those local areas only where data proves a need for a protection of a general 
interest. For example, the need to address housing issues in a city’s old town (where 
data collected under TIER 2  and impact assessments  prove a causal link) must not 
contaminate the narrative and allow the introduction of an authorisation 
(licensing/permit) scheme for STR players across the entire city. Market access 
requirements shall never be created following extreme circumstances of v ery 
localised areas.  
● Suitable23: authorisation (licensing/permit) schemes can be introduced only if a variety 
of factors that contribute to the protection of general interest are assessed and 
addressed equally without bias against STRs 24. For example, ban ning STRs solely (not 
issuing permits/licenses) will not meaningfully contribute to the lack of housing as 
people will keep moving from rural areas to cities, no new buildings will be constructed, 
etc.   
● Notified to the European Commission : any intentions to impose authorisation 
(licensing/permit) schemes must be notified to the European Commission.

Serviced Apartments (Corporate Housing) 
Serviced Apartment is the umbrella term for a type of furnished apartment available for short -term or long -term stays,

most standard rooms, and often having access to gyms, restaurants, meeting space, concierges, maids and other hotel-like 
services. 
Serviced apartments usually cater business customers (85%), but leisure customers can stay at the apartments too.  
Serviced Apartments can be split into 2 parts: Aparthotels and Corporate Housing.  Corporate Housing usually do not have 
on-site reception, concierge is offered remotely if at all, cleaning is done weekly, there is no on-site restaurant or bar. 
Corporate Housing Providers are the following (non -exhaustive list): City Apartments, Flying Butler, The Residence, 
Madisson Hill London, Berkshire Rooms and other.

STR Ancillary Services 
The STR Ancillary Services landscape in the STR sector is by far the fastest growing area when considering new entrants 
entering the market.  
The Property Management technology area is especially fragmented, with different players offering property management 
systems, channel management, customer relationship management, digital marketing solutions, website builders, revenue 
management systems, and guest -facing tech like messaging, app builders, digital guidebooks, chatbots, and voice 
assistants. 
While the short-term rental sector has grown strongly, the number of STR Ancillary Services providers has too. The 
fragmented nature of the sector means there are still many small property managers who will not have significant funds to 
purchase software, instead tr usting their own local knowledge and experience to manage processes, market their listings 
and set prices. 
It is still hard for STR Ancillary Services providers to scale Europe -wide or internationally and become dominant players 
(nearly all of them are SMEs). Most players instead focuses on a few tools or systems, based on the demand in their initial 
core market. Vertical expansion usually follows growing demand amongst the client base, rather than seeing a true gap in 
the market. 
The ecosystem goes beyond technology players, and includes hardware providers, service and operations providers, and 
miscellaneous support services.

The rationale (explanatory memorandum) without any 
justification blames short -term rentals of the spread of COVID -
19, links the sector to terrorism activities and crime.  Even 
though rationale notes that the proposed amendment is 
primarily intended for a prevention of further COVID-19 spread, 
it emphasises on the need to track the movement of 
“foreigners” and obliges online short -term rental platforms to 
provide the Municipal Trade Licensing Office the personal data, 
such as: the number of tourism service contracts concluded; the 
total price for tourism services for the period specified; the 
address of the place where the tourist services are provided; 
and the designation o f the service provider with which it has 
mediated the conclusion of a contract relating to the provision 
of tourism services to the customer (for a natural person, his or 
her name and surname, date of birth and permanent address 
must be provided). According to the amendment, the Municipal 
Trade Licensing Office will have a right to transmit to the other 
authority exercising public authority the data obtained upon 
request. The law was announced and adopted in 4 working days 
as part of COVID -19 package of law s. EHHA notified European 
Commission of this issue.

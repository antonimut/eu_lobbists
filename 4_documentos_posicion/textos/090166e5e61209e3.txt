The EU Needs a Short -Term Rental Initiative  that Protects the Affordability and Availability of 
Housing  
FEANTSA is the European Federation of National Organisations Working with the Homeless. We are 
the only European NGO focusing exclusively on the fight against homelessness. Our goal is an end to 
homelessness in Europe.  
The European Commission is currently consul ting on a Short-Term Rental Initiative to improve 
regulation in this important and fast-growing sector of the tourist industry that is transforming housing 
markets. This represents an opportunity to ensure that public authorities can successfully pursue goal 
of ensuring the availability of affordable housing.  
In November 2020, FEANTSA and the Fondation Abbé Pierre published our report The City is Ours: How 
to Regulate Airbnb in the Face of A Housing Crisis.  It gathered evidence of the impact of STR platforms 
on rental prices and availability of housing, particularly in large cities. It described how local, regional 
and national authorities have attempted to regulate t he sector  to ensure the a vailability and 
affordability of housing for citizens, and the challenges they face  in designing and implementing this 
legislation.  The report showed that EU regulation in this area was inadequate to protect citizens from 
the negative effects on availability and affordability of housing.   
In this context, FEANTSA strongly welcomes the Commission’s  proposal to develop a new legislative 
initiative in this field. We hereby share our observations on the state of play and recommendations.  
Observations on State of Play   
1. STR platforms have a demonstrable impact on availability and affordability of housing. There 
is a growing  body of evidence that clearly demonstrates a causal relationship between STR 
activity, rising prices and reduced availability of affordable housing in various international 
contexts  
2. EU Citizens are worried about this. Eurobarometer shows that 42% of EU citizens think short 
term rentals booked through online platforms have a negative effect on prices and availability  
3. Public authorities, often at the local level , are increasingly taking measures to regulate the 
STR sector to protect availability and affordability of housing.  Recent case law has confirmed 
such measures to be both justified and proportionate. Nonetheless, uncertainty about exactly 
what measures public authorities can take in line with EU law in the context o f the single 
market (notably the Services Directive and the Digital Services Act , which is currently being 
negotiated).   
4. The implementation and enforcement of  regulation of STR is constrained by the absence of 
accurate and comprehensive data , which Platforms have  but do not currently share  
systematically with the public authorities.

5. There is considerable evidence of a rapid professionalisation of the STR industry with players 
like large landlords and management companies taking an increasing share of the market , 
facilitated by technology .  This urgently necessitates a different regulatory toolbox to 
individual homeowners acting as “hosts” in the sharing economy.  
6. During the COVID19 crisis, some public authorities developed innovative short-term measures 
to mobilise tourist accommodation including hotels and STR to accommodate homeless 
people in the interest of public health . This is a timely reminder of the need for public policy 
to strike a balance between different policy objectives in the housing and real-estate market. 
Ensuring the availability  and affordability of housing for all citizens, including the most 
vulnerable, is a key public interest objective . In the context of the Recovery, EU regulation 
needs to enable public authorities to design and enforce measures in the interest of ensuring 
that Europeans can access and maintain adequate and affordable housing in places where the 
STR sector operates. 
Recommendations on Transparency and Market Access   
The Commission plans to address transparency and market access barriers in the context of the STR 
initiative. FEANTSA recommends the following:  
1. Transparency: The proposed initiative should ensure that p ublic authorities can access t he 
data that they need to enact and enforce regulation of STR from a housing policy perspective. 
Public authorities need to know who rents out what, for how long, when and where. Platforms 
should be required to share the necessary data  in the appropriate timeframes, in line with  
GDPR. This would address a huge information gap that is the major obstacle to designing and 
enforcing regulation.  A common European registration scheme would be helpful.  
Furthermore, Platforms should be held liable for illegal content ( e.g., posts lacking a 
registration number when required).  
 
2. Market access: When it comes to market access conditions for STR players and trying to create 
a level playing field for different actors, it is critical that public authorities have the freedom to 
develop an d enact justifiable and proportionate measures to protect the availability and 
affordability of housing for citizens as a public interest priority. A new EU initiative in this sector 
could provide a framework and further guidance on establishing public interest and 
proportionality, whilst leaving the necessary flexibility for local authorities to respond to the 
highly localized realities.

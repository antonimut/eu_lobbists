BASEL III TRANSPOSITION IN EUROPE : HOW TO FULFILL THE 
POLITICAL MANDATE
➢ First and foremost, the European Parliament and the European Council have given a clear political
mandate to transpose only in the absence of a significant increase in capital requirements for banks.
➢ Then, we consider that the Basel package should be assessed in the light of:
➢ the lessons drawn from the health crisis: the European banking and financing model is relevant, solid and
able to support citizens and companies.
➢ the coming challenges of the green and digital transformation, which implies huge investments:
€330bn/year for the ecological transition and €125bn/year for the digital transition according to the ECB.
➢ Against this backdrop, the European Basel III transposition is not merely an issue of banking supervision, but
one of the key issues involved in financing the European recovery, in financing the EU sustainability and
digitalisation industrial revolutions, while also allowing the strategic autonomy of the European Union.
➢ To meet the European specificities and to ensure no significant increase of capital requirements due to the
implementation of the output floor, the Commission chose the way of proposing different adjustments. We would
like to state that this is a viable way to achieve the overarching goals.
However, the path chosen is more complicated and requires essential changes (and thus more political debates)
to make the measures permanent. Because of many uncertainties during the EU negotiations, we propose to limit
the impact of the output floor, which is currently uncapped, by the implementation of the cap set out in the CRR
with a limitation more in line with the mandate of the EU co-legislators and the G20 an also on a permanent
basis.

BASEL III TRANSPOSITION IN EUROPE : HOW TO FULFILL THE 
POLITICAL MANDATE
➢ The European Commission indicated that “Implementing the preferred option and taking into account all the
measures in its [transposition] proposal is expected to lead to a weighted average increase in EU banks’ minimum
capital requirements of +6.4% to +8.4% in the long term (by 2030)”.
➢ This impact assessment cannot be considered satisfactory since all the favorable provisions that the
European Commission has made precisely to recognise the specificities of the European financing will
come to an end or may be called into question from 2030/2032, especially for GSII where the impact is
much higher that the average.
➢ To fulfill the EU political mandates in a permanent way, it is therefore essential, beyond maintaining the
application of the output floor at the highest level of consolidation so as not to further fragment the EU
banking landscape, to make these proposed measures permanent and to complement them with targeted
amendments in order to bring clarity and certainty to this regulation, especially on these aspects: :
▪ Permanent preferential treatment for proven high quality residential real estate financing,
▪ Same treatment for unrated companies of high quality as in the US at least till sufficient coverage,
▪ Enhance the necessary level playing field on market financing activities evaluated on an “outcome
based” approach for FRTB, aligning the treatment of derivatives for commercial end users with the
one in the US (SA CCR) and avoid penalizing the fragmented EU sovereign debt markets by removing
the 3 bps PD under default credit charge for sovereigns weighted at 0% under standardized
approach, and adapt RW for repos and securities lending by recognising their very short maturity
▪ Extend the current weighting of 100% for strategic equity holdings (new concept rightly introduced
by the Commission), regardless of the prudential approach used, to preserve the variety of business
models, allow the diversification of activities of the activities of banking groups while reinforcing the
level playing field within the EU

REAL ESTATE FINANCING (1/2)
Observations
➢ Real estate is a key sector of the European economy, central to the concerns of all citizens (ownership of property, preparation for
retirement), which has direct impacts on employment
➢ There are different approaches to granting a residential loan: either based on the value of the asset (Anglo-Saxon approach) or by
examining the borrower’s repayment capacity and prioritising fixed-rate loans (French approach, secures financial stability).
➢ Mortgage loans in Europe are mainly double recourse loans for which the borrower is personally liable (no “walk away” option as in
the US), generally leading to lower risks. This is further reinforced by very robust property markets in most member states and
generally by the fixed rate nature of the loans.

PRESERVE THE VARIETY OF BUSINESS MODELS IN THE 
EUROPEAN BANKING MARKET
Observations
➢ It is essential to preserve the variety of business models in the European banking market while
reinforcing the level playing field between the different players, regardless of their legal nature or the
prudential treatments adopted.
➢ It is therefore appropriate to equally apply the current 100% risk weighting for strategic holdings (a
new concept rightly introduced by the Commission), irrespective of the prudential approach used
(standardised or internal model one), in order to preserve the variety of business models, to allow for
diversification of banking groups' activities and to strengthen the level playing field

CAPITAL MARKET ACTIVITIES: THE EUNEEDS TO MAINTAIN ITS 
SOVEREIGNTY ON GOVERNMENT AND CORPORATE FINANCING
Market activities are essential to finance governments and corporates 
and to hedge the financial risks arising from a globalized economy:
➢ Government financing, issuance and distribution of sovereign debt 
➢ Corporate financing, debt and equity issuance and distribution
➢ Market making of debt and equity instruments, in order to provide liquidity to 
investors, as well as hedging instruments for their equity and credit exposures
➢ Management of interest rate risk, to preserve corporates and households from 
interest rate volatility by offering them fixed rate instruments, including home 
loans
➢ Management of foreign exchange risk, given the predominance of US dollar 
in international trade, European corporates tend to use derivatives more than 
their US counterparts
➢ As a result of this client-driven business, banks bear risks associated with 
the creditworthiness of the counterparty: Credit Valuation Adjustment risk 
(volatility of the market value of counterparty credit risk) and Counterparty 
Credit Risk
➢ Transactions in the form of derivatives, are subject to collateral and/or clearing, 
that result in residual exposure to clients, in particular to corporates and 
governments that call for rightful exemptions
Europe needs to have competitive Corporate and Investment Banks (CIBs) 
to ensure its financial independence and economic sovereignty:
➢ EU CIBs have only a 35% market share in the EMEA market, while US CIBs have 
reached almost half of this market
➢ Conversely, US CIBs are highly concentrated and dominant on their home market 
(vs. insignificant EU presence) , which reinforces the competitive disadvantage
➢ Capital markets activities will see their capital charge significantly increased, 
due to the cumulated impact of Counterparty Credit Risk, CVA risk and market 
risks requirements. More conservative transposition in Europe will further 
hamper the European CIBs competitiveness
➢ EU capital markets are underdeveloped compared to the US. The strong 
momentum to further develop the Capital Markets Union should not be 
compromised by Basel III standards : it is essential to preserve domestic market 
making capacity

OUTPUT FLOOR: PROPOSAL TO KEEP THE POLITICAL AGREEMENT 
AND PRESERVE RISK-SENSITIVITY
➢ To respect the mandate “of no significant increase in the capital requirements and to guarantee a level
playing field”, it is important not to over-transpose Basel III, this calls for maintaining the application of the
floor at the highest level of consolidation, as provided for in the Basel Accords and designed by the
Commission in order not to further fragment the EU banking landscape.
➢ Make sure European buffers do not double with the Output floor, not by freezing them as proposed by the EC
but by making sure they will immediately decrease accordingly
➢ For the output floor to serve as an effective and permanent backstop while respecting the political mandate,
one should cap the output floor at 10% by:
 Making permanent the framework for managing the effects of the floor to ensure that the EU politique mandate is
fulfilled on a permanent basis and not only for the few years to come
 Complementing the floor with a cap, at 10% of unfloored RW under IRB approach, that would allow to respect the
primary mandate of the G20 as well as Council and EU Parliament not to significantly the capital
 Restrict the cap to the risk exposure amount that are under the IRB approaches to match the objective of the
output floor to limit the benefit derived from internal models

OPERATIONAL RISK
Observations
➢ The Basel Committee has completely revised the rules for calculating operational risk.
➢ The most risk-sensitive approach has been eliminated. Only a simplified approach based on historical losses
and the size of the entity is now available.
➢ The new approach anchors a certain vulnerability in capital requirements, and thus European financing, to
external third-party sanctions.
➢ This over-simplification, as well as setting the ILM at 1 is expected to generate a significant increase in
capital requirements for a number of EU Banks
Proposal
➢ Past observed losses are not indicative of future risks. They should thus not be permitted to
significantly adversely affect the measurement of risk.
➢ Our recommendation is for the term that reflects such losses not to exceed a value of 1.
➢ Technical amendement: Art. 314.6.a. When defining the components of the business indicator by developing a list of typical
sub-items, EBA, as regards to the financial component, shall not prevent an institution from addressing sub-items to the
Trading or the Banking components according to their prudential classification as stated by the Basel text and not mapping to
the FINREP aggregates.

ISSUES RAISED BY IMMIXION OF SUPERVISORS IN THE DAILY 
MANAGEMENT
➢ Observations
Many measures in the draft Directive reinforce the "intrusive" nature of supervision in the management of companies.
Governance/Fit& Proper : specific governance issues would need to be address notably the definition and ex-ante
evaluation of the members of the management body and key position holder. The fit and proper assessment by the
supervisor ex ante, and no longer a posteriori, of the members not only of the boards of directors but also of the holders
of key functions within the banks with an extensive and vague definition (with a 120-day deadline which in practice leads
to a multiplication of interim periods and to not being able to confirm appointments decided before obtaining the
authorities' decision).
Supervisory power as regards to sustainability: While the profession considers it natural that the sustainability 
dimension is included in the regulatory and supervisory framework, it considers extremely dangerous the new powers 
envisaged for the supervisor which go far beyond the original and legal mandate given to these authorities.  Indeed, 
Article 104 ii m of the CRD provides for the supervisor to enable him, in the event of a lack of alignment of the bank with 
EU transition policies, to impose a reduction in risk, in particular through adjustments to business models, governance 
strategies and risk management.
Supervisory power as regards to M&A : the possible ex ante validation of acquisitions as soon as the intention to 
carry out an operation and of divestments or transfers of activity
➢ Proposal
Amend all parts of the CRD/CRR where de facto management of banking institutions by the supervisor is
envisaged in order to ensure the natural separation of tasks and duties and to avoid any risk of legal dispute
with external parties

OTHER KEY TOPICS THAT REQUIRE AMENDMENTS:
➢ RGLA/Public Sector Entity: To the newly created exposure class to regional and local authorities and to public sector entities, apply
preferential input floor for PDs (3bps) and LGDs (5%) that would reflect the high quality of these borrowers and not the provisions for large
corporate currently foreseen
➢ Maturity adjustment for short term product (corporate financings, trade finance) : use the actual maturity instead of a fixed maturity
whatever the IRB approach. The current drafting doesn’t contemplate the possibility for IRB-F exposures to use the actual maturity, since
this possibility is only granted as part of the permission to apply the IRB approach, not for banks already using the A-IRB that will be
required to apply the F-IRB for asset classes reverting to the F-IRB as per Basel III finalization requirement.
➢ Transactors review the definition contemplated in order to ensure that European revolving retail exposures would benefit from this
preferential 45% Basel risk weight
➢ Prudential consolidation perimeter: ensure that the planned changes in the definition and scope of prudential consolidation, which were
introduced in the first place to capture core services outsourcing and fintech, do not compromise traditional banks' desire to diversify their
activities by imposing on them unsustainable levels of capital over their non-banking activity compared to the competition.
➢ Trade finance restore CRR2 Credit Conversion Factor (CCF) settings (i.e. 20% for trade finance guarantees and 0% CCF for uncommitted
facilities) to ensure the continuation of the financing required for the export of goods produced in Europe
➢ Agriculture Financing: Ensure the proper review of the Agriculture financing that could be endanger by EBA and by end of 2024 (Art 505)
rather than 2030 considering the potential impact of the new RW.
➢ Securitisation: Due to the very conservative calibration of the SEC-SA, the output floor would very significantly reduce or annihilate the
efficiency of securitisation transactions. Considering the delays on the “securitisation comprehensive review”, which is an essential part of
the CMU Action Plan, and without prejudice to its outcomes, a transition arrangement providing that the “p” factor is divided by two for the
purpose of the Output Floor calculation, in order to mitigate the unintended impact of the SEC-SA calibration, seems both efficient and
reasonable
➢ Credit Risk Mitigation: ensure the implementation of a reduced LGD representative of the credit insurance that reduces the risk
➢ UCC definition should be clarified vs undrawn facilities and corporate including in Annex 1 of the EC proposal

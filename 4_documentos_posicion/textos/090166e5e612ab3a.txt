We strongly support this initiative, provided that the common framework pushes for : 
- accounting of emissions based on primary data  as opposed to modelling (“calculating”) 
emissions based on general emission intensity factors 
- integration of recording and accounting  for CO2 in standard IT systems used in the industry 
- allocation of emissions to the value added by the transportation and storage activities, being 
the translocation of goods and/or persons from origin to destination 
- the aggregation of data from subcontracted segments (legs) in supply chains or transport 
chains to integrated outputs 
- support for carbon pricing and carbon trading, which means that results can be audited and 
certified by auditors 
- support for a “ladder” of participation and data quality, accommodating the wide variety in 
IT-maturity of companies in Member States, especially SME’s. 
- KPI’s that acknowledge the shared responsibility of shippers and transporters in logistics for

For the last 5 years we have been active in testing and developing the practice of 
applying the methodology first described by the COFRET working group, and lately 
incorporated in the ISO14083 standard:. 
 
In these test we have found solutions in practice for all the above questions and 
demands. The methodology has been applied in: 
- All modalities, including maritime, air, rail inland shipping and road 
- International, continental and local distribution up to last mile delivery 
- All kinds of goods (bulk, container, distribution, e-commerce, food, plants, chemicals etc.) 
- All kinds or roles (producer, retailer, 4PL, forwarder, transporter, platform, etc.) 
- All size of companies, form small SME tot multinational 
Currently over 50 different types of supply chains have been benchmarked in groups, 
with over 300 companies participating. In 2022 another 50 will be added, covering the 
large majority of what is the current practice.

We have found a solution to the conundrum that in practice ad-hoc sub-sub-contracting 
of activities is very common. Collecting primary data from these SME’s or self employed 
contractors is currently cumbersome. Using the so called “emission intensity factors” as 
promoted by many to fill the gap  renders misleading or even very wrong results. We 
have developed the so called COFRET performance Indicator (CPI) which does not have 
these drawbacks and can be applied to even individual shipments. 
 
Both solutions remove the threshold to start. 
 
Transportation companies are service companies that work within the design of a supply 
chain, within the constraints and planning demands that are dictated by shippers. 
Shippers are dependent on transportation companies for efficient execution and using the 
best possible equipment. 
Both share the responsibility for the resulting CO2-emissions caused by the 
transportation of a good in a supply chain. 
We have developed interacting but slightly different KPI’s for transporters and shippers to 
take this interaction into account. 
 
The commercial relation between shippers and transportation companies is a-
symmetrical: shippers purchase transportation. KPI’s that monitor CO2-emissions for 
transporters must take this reality into account: they should be designed in such a way 
that they cannot distort (or be abused in) the commercial negotiations. We have 
developed KPI’s with this reality into account. 
 
As with all accounting, data collection and processing should be highly automated in IT-
systems, and auditable by regular auditors: specialized auditing would be prohibitively 
expensive and limiting. 
We have shown that the methodology and the application in tools is easily integrated into 
IT-systems, and that auditors can certify results during their normal auditing work.

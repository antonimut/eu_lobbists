ASD response to  the European Commission call for evidence document on an  EU framework for 
harmonised measurement of transport and logistics emissions – ‘CountEmissions EU’. 
Aerospace and Defence Industries Associations of Europe (ASD) is the voice of the European Aeronautics, 
Space, Defence and Security Industries, representing over 3,000 companies and actively supporting the 
competitive development of the sector in Europe and worldwide. It has direct members, active in 18 
countries, including 19 major European industries and 23 National Associations. 
ASD thanks the European Commission for this opportunity to provide comments to their call for evidence 
document on ‘CountEmissions EU’.  
Europe’s industry has committed to decarbonise  air transport and accelerate efforts to make Europe a 
carbon neutral continent by 2050 through the reduction of CO2 emissions in absolute terms, supported 
by a robust decarbonisation roadmap published by European industry associations in February 2021  
(https://www.destination2050.eu/). 
In this context, ASD welcomes CountEmissions EU’s potential support to the sector’s efforts to better 
communicate on its environmental footprint. 
ASD is of the views that it is important to have the right set of data and a common metric to evaluate the 
complete GreenHouse Gas (GHG) footprint of various means of transport. The way the various modes of 
transport must be evaluated in terms of GHG , are defined within ISO 14083 and other applicable 
standards. Those standards should serve as the basis for new developments in this area. 
Aviation is often pointed out by media as a big GHG contributor and surveys have proven that public 
perception is dis torted about the actual climate footprint of this mode of transport. A common and 
objective metric would help address this misperception. 
It should be noted however, that comparisons between GHG emissions of different means of transport 
with aviation are only pertinent for routes for which a non-aviation option is viable.  
Where this is the case,  non-aviation options such as road and rail require a lot of infrastructure to be 
created, such as tunnels, bridges and concrete structures. This also creates GHG e missions that need to 
be taken into account in order to adequately compare GHG emissions from different transport modes, as 
aviation may produce higher emissions in the use phase, but lower emissions in non-use phases.  
Therefore, the scope of what to measure needs to be clarified. Such metric should comprise all emissions 
over the life cycle of the transport modes related to each transport mode,  including supporting 
infrastructure deployment impact, in addition to the use of the vehicle. Life Cycle Analysis emission of the 
product should be taken into account as well as the impact, in terms of CO2 emissions, of the associated 
required infrastructure, as documented within ISO applicable standards (ISO 14067). Externalities (soil, 
land use, biodiversity) shou ld be taken into account to enable a fair comparison between the various 
modes of transport. When at product level, intensity metrics (e.g. CO2eq per passenger -kilometer or 
CO2eq per kilometer) shall be preferred compared to other types of metrics. 
Before the work can start, a mapping of existing initiatives should be performed to avoid duplications.  
For instance, the various carbon calculators (e.g. ICAO or IATA Carbon Calculators) or reporting 
mechanisms (e.g. through EU-ETS for aviation) that already exist across different types of transport should 
be identified. The CountEmissionsEU initiative should use as much as possible what has been established 
already.

Counting GHG emissions of various transportation modes and allowing comparison between them, was 
the purpose of the currently developed ISO 14083.  It is currently unclear how this initiative fits in the 
context of the CountEmissionsEU. It will be important that a holistic approach is developed to avoid 
duplications or differences in methodologies. 
As far as aviation is concerned, we would like to bring to the Commission’s attention that EASA is working 
on eco-label including on metric to measure externalities including CO2. This work is a good approach and 
should serve as a basis for the Commission’s work on aviation GHG emissions. 
We would also recommend concentrating on CO2 emissions in the first instance, as further research is 
required regarding non-CO2 elements. 
Further, the harmoni sation of methods for accounting for  any emissions needs to be global, as the 
aviation sector is a global sector. ASD therefore encourages dialogues with the international partners to 
identify methodologies and solutions that fit all needs. 
Finally, any reporting obligation should not create a disproportionate a dministrative burden to industry, 
cognisant that reporting mechanisms already exist in Europe or at the international level.

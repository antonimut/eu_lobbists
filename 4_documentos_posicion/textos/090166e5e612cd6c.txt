General remarks 
Rolls Royce welcomes the Commission’s initiative to provide a common framework  for calculating 
greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions of transport operations in the freight and passenger transport sectors. As 
we aim to achieve the decarbonization of the transport sector, it is important to be able to have the right data 
and a common metric to compare and evaluate the full life cycle GHG emissions of the different transport 
modes. 
 
Reflect the internationality of transport 
 
As transport is an inherently international sector with important cross-border activities, Rolls-Royce strongly 
appreciates the European approach to a GHG calculation framework. 
To date, there is indeed no such framework that allow s to adequately compare GHG emissions from different 
modes of transport across Europe. 
We would, however, like to underline that work on a common GHG emission framework should not be limited 
to European level. Ultimately, an international instrument would allow the most adequate comparison of 
transport modes and emissions globally. Therefore, we invite the Commission to push for more work in this 
direction on the international level, taking into account for example ICAO, IATA and IMO activities in this 
field, and encouraging international cooperation on a common framework across the different transport 
modes. 
 
Objective metric to fight misconceptions about transport modes 
 
Aviation and maritime transport are often depicted as the most emitting GHG sectors. In addition, surveys have 
proven that the public has a distorted view of the actual GHG footprint of aviation and maritime. Therefore, 
we would appreciate a common and objective metric to show the actual emissions of these sector s, and to 
fight eventual misconceptions. 
 
Compare what is comparable 
 
It needs to be taken into account that the GHG framework cannot necessarily compare all modes of transport 
for all routes adequately. A comparison with aviation and maritime for example, is only appropriate, if there is 
another means of transport, such as rail or road, available for the same route. 
Where this is the case, the infrastructure that is necessary for the same route for different means of transport 
needs to be considered, as the construction of roads etc. also creates GHG emissions. Therefore, aviation and

Base the metric on life cycle emissions 
 
Rolls-Royce favors a metric that takes into account all emissions over the life cycle of the different transport 
modes rather than only the emissions from the use of the mode. For maritime, this would for example mean a 
well-to-wake rather than a tailpipe -emission approach . This would also ensure coherence with other 
legislative proposals, such as the FuelEU Maritime proposal. 
 
Base the framework on existing calculators 
 
In the preparation of CountEmissionsEU, the Commission should map the already existing GHG emission 
calculators for the different transport modes, to make sure that the framework is based as much as possible 
on established instruments.  
For aviation, the framework would need to take EASA’s work on a GHG emission metric, as well as on an Eco -
label into account. They are indeed building a database on aviation metrics. This work is a good approach and 
should serve as a basis for the Commission’s work on aviation GHG emissions.  
The Commission should ensure coherence with other existing GHG emission calculation methodologies and 
legislations that include  emissions reporting. Duplications and too high an administrative burden should be 
avoided. 
 
Take into account the complexity of emissions 
 
Furthermore, the EU framework needs to take account of the differences between the different GHG emissions 
and, especially for non-CO2 emissions, of their high complexity. A simple comparison on mass basis may not 
capture all elements that would be important to capture the emissions adequately (altitude, time of day etc.). 
More research to clarify these elements is needed. 
Therefore, for the framework, we would recommend concentrating on CO2 emissions in the first instance, so 
that science can clarify the afore-mentioned non-CO2 elements. 
 
Scope of the framework 
 
The GHG emission framework needs to have a broad enough scope to take account of the emissions of a 
maximum of the transport sector.  For ships, for example many regulations, including the MRV regulation, so 
far only cover  ships above a gross tonnage of 5000. This excludes many kinds of ships that are generally 
smaller, such as tugboats or yachts.  Nonetheless, Rolls -Royce believes that also smaller ships need to be 
decarbonized in order to achieve our climate goals by 2050. For this, it is imperative to have an oversight over 
their emissions, as well.

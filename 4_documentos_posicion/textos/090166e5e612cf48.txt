A4E INPUT ON AN EU FRAMEWORK FOR HARMONISED MEASUREMENT OF TRANSPORT 
AND LOGISTICS EMISSIONS – ‘COUNTEMISSIONS EU’ 
 
European airlines are fully committed to decarbonize air transport and accelerate their efforts to 
make Europe the world’s first carbon neutral continent by 2050 through the reduction of CO2 
emissions in absolute terms and through CO2 mitigation. Acknowledging its responsibilities despite the 
current crisis, the EU aviation sector recently published “Destination 2050 - A route to net zero 
European aviation” roadmap showing a pathway to reaching net zero CO2 emissions by 20501. 
 
 
General statement  
 
Europe’s airlines have committed to decarbonize air transport and accelerate their efforts to make 
Europe the world’s first carbon neutral continent by 2050 through the reduction of CO2 emissions 
in absolute terms, and through CO2 mitigation.  
In this context, A4E welcomes the initiative's potential support to the sector’s efforts to better 
communicate on its environmental sustainability.  
A sector’s contribution to climate change should assess the whole life cycle of air transport’s 
sustainable activities through a holistic "door to door" approach.  
A future framework for harmonised measurement of passenger and cargo transport emissions 
should be based on a Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) as well as the emissions impact associated to the 
infrastructure used by the respect transport modes. In addition, a credible and comprehensive 
comparative measurement framework needs to consider other environmental externalities of the 
mode of transport beyond emissions (land use, biodiversity). 
In the comparison between modes of transport, the methodology needs to adopt a true well-to-
wheel approach instead of a narrow well-to-tank definition of the environmental footprint of an 
activity. Any indicator adopted to assess the environmental performance of aviation should allow a 
comprehensive comparison of the environmental footprint across different modes of transport. 
Environmental performance of airlines / KPIs 
 
In this context, it is important to consider a range of different indicators as, across different 
measures, there are very different relative rankings: 
The environmental performances of airlines can be clustered into two groups. 1/ Those factors 
under control of the airline and 2/ those requiring support of other i.e. Air Navigation Service 
Providers (ANSPs) and the Network Manger. The first group includes e.g. business structure, 
aircraft size and fleet age. The emissions metrics show correlations with the business model and

performance outside the control of airlines includes additional fuel burn (compared to the ideal 
trajectory calculated by an airline for a specific flight) due to procedures at airports, when 
arriving/departing, military restrictions or missing ANSP capacity. In this case, metrics need to be 
defined to identify the root cause and allow for improvements to be implemented. This distinction is 
notably part of the European Commission’s proposal to update of Single European Sky SES 2+2 and 
should not be considered as airline KPI. 
Focus on any one single measure would be misleading. The definition of environmental metrics for 
airlines should therefore go beyond the mere assessment with regards a threshold to integrate a 
range of elements such as the use of SAFs, the use of offsets, etc. as well improvement in emissions 
across both traffic and revenue measures.  
Performance metrics need to be comprehensive, reflective of the nature of the airlines’ market and 
ensure not to discriminate between business models as all KPIs are shaped by the nature of the 
company business models (e.g. point-to-point vs hub operations, cabin densification, or presence or 
not of cargo business on top of passenger transport).  
Since 2019, A4E member airlines have also been involved in the development a so-called EcoLabel 
under the coordination of EASA whose objectives match the CountEmissions initiative. For 
efficiency's sake, and to avoid confusing messages, it would be advisable for the CountEmissions 
initiative to build on the EcoLabel discussions and take on board the input and positions the 
aviation sector have provided to it. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
About A4E: Launched in 2016, Airlines for Europe (A4E) is Europe’s largest airline association, based in Brussels.  The 
organisation advocates on behalf of its members to help shape European aviation policy to the benefit of consumers, 
ensuring a continued safe and competitive air transport market.  A4E is one of the initiators of European 
aviation’s Destination 2050 decarbonisation roadmap, with a pledge to reach net zero CO2 emissions from all flights within 
and departing Europe by 2050.  In 2019, A4E members carried more than 720 million passengers via a modern fleet of over 
3,000 aircraft – accounting for more than 70 per cent of the continent’s journeys.  Members with air cargo and mail 
activities transport more than 5 million tons of goods each year to more than 360 destinations either by freighters or 
passenger aircraft. Follow us on Twitter @A4Europe.

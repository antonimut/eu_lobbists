p.4
SHIPMENTS NOT CONDITIONED ENOUGH FOR OECD
Shipping to OECD countries is by default authorised
Scrutiny is possible, but reasons to dismiss possible 
shipments to OECD seem less stringent than conditions to 
authorise shipments to non OECD
 Risk is that more shipments towards OECD will take place, 
even if not fair and truly equivalent to EU standards
 Despite IA (SWD) stating there would be less shipments
outside EU, we still do not really secure that most of 
recycling potentials will be done in EU (making us 
producer & exporter of recycled materials)
Suggestion:
As possible, set a scrutiny on ESM before allowing
shipments even to OECD;
Include a clear evidence that receiving country is already
managing its own domestic waste – equivalent to the 
imported streams – before allowing export from EU (= 
avoid dumping and dedicated facilities to treat imported
waste but neglecting/at the expenses of domestic waste) 
p.5
TOO WEAK ALIGNMENT WITH WASTE HIERARCHY
Within EU, within OECD, there is no distinction between
various recovery operations, notably not between
recycling and energy recovery.
=> Risk is that material recycling is not given precedence
and priority attention
Suggestion:
Create a notification procedure for shipping waste to 
energy recovery or, if deemed inappropriate, at least 
create a mandatory publicly available evidence that the 
concerned waste could not be better sorted and (shipped
for being) recycled.
Note: In the proposed text, MS can object a waste shipment but may not, MSW 
cannot be shipped if not pre-treated

p.6
POOR TRANSPOSITION OF BASEL AMENDEMENTS ON 
PLASTIC WITHIN EU
Within EU, some plastic waste (e.g PVC, PTFE) and some
mixed plastic waste can still be shipped for recovery, 
while they should require notification under Basel 
convention.
 EU creates a poor example for other Basel parties
 Risk is that problematic plastic waste is shipped towards
sub-optimal processes, including for energy recovery not 
even for recycling
Suggestion:
Respect Basel Convention amendements for intra-EU 
shipments (= notification for all Y48), and at the very least 
do not allow without notification the shipments of 
problematic plastic waste streams for lower forms of 
recovery but recycling.

p.7
NEGLECTED END OF LIFE OF ITEMS SHIPPED FOR REUSE
The revised text does not address end of life of items 
shipped for reuse from EU (even if proposing a better
enforcement of false shipments for reuse).
 EU de facto delegates waste management to receiving
countries of reused items
 Not anticipating the risk of increased shipments for reuse
due to longer lasting products and technology progress
(Electric cars, 5G)
Suggestion:
Seize the opportunity of the WSR reform to address the 
end of life stage of items shipped for reuse according to 
polluter pays principle, at the very least for items covered
by EPR in EU and national levels: fees paid by consumers
to ensure a proper waste management should follow the 
products.

p.8
GOVERNANCE TO SUPPORT EU COMMISSION ASSESSMENTS
The acceptability of non OECD countries or scrutiny of OECD 
countries related to waste shipment is left to the discretion
of the EU Commission to prepare delegated acts
 Risk of misunderstanding by some stakeholders
 Need for assistance for received evidence verification
Suggestion:
Make clearer in concerned articles what governance could
be adopted (e.g consultation forum or ad hoc committee) 
and what verifiable criteria would be possibly
established.

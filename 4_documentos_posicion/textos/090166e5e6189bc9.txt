Revision of the Ambient Air Quality Directives 
 
 
 
A-1 
Introduction 
 
Aurubis is a leading worldwide provider of non-ferrous metals. We process complex metal concentrates and 
diverse recycling raw materials. Aurubis is a global leader in copper recycling.  
Aurubis is committed to the European Green Deal and its targets. Our activities contribute to the more efficient 
use of resources, increased recycling, environmentally friendly production, the fabrication of environmentally 
compatible products, and the development of a circular economy that is friendly to the climate. 
We welcome the opportunity to comment on the revision of the Ambient Air Quality Directives. Air quality affects our 
health and our quality of life. Our industry is therefore fully committed to contributing to clean air. 
 
Cost-benefit analyses shall be taken into account when setting air quality standards 
» Air quality should be tackled in a resource -efficient manner. The greatest benefit for society will be achieved if 
policy measures focus on identifying and addressing the most important pollutants and their most important 
sources.  
» WHO Guidelines have to be taken into account however other important considerations shall be included when 
setting the EU air quality standards such as the technical feasibility, costs, and benefits. A new air quality policy 
should not lead to disproportionate and harder achievable standards.  
» A cost-benefit analysis must therefore be a cornerstone of air quality policy and its implementation. The health 
benefits should be quantified on the basis of a realistic exposure assessment of the population. The costs should 
consider technical feasibility and socio-economic factors. This ensures that the health benefits for the population 
are maximized with the available resources.  
» Ensure that existing EU air quality standards are met before setting more ambitious standards Current air quality 
standards are already very strict and difficult to meet for many EU Member States, who are struggling to 
implement effective measures and facing a number of infringement procedures . 
» To develop effective and proportionate policies, it is crucial to improve data collection, better understand and 
assess emissions and identify the main contributing sources. 
» It is important to target those activities where there is the biggest potential for further reductions at the minimum 
cost. Further relative reductions will be hardly achievable for activities that already meet low levels.  
 
Industrial processes have achieved significant emission reduction 
» Industrial processes are easy to measure and identify , and they have accordingly been tackled rigorously over 
the past decades.  
» Emissions from EU industry have significantly decl ined over last years, thanks to significant investments in 
advanced emissions prevention and reduction techniques.  
» Aurubis deploys the best available techniques, and has signi ficantly reduced emissions to air : 96 % decline in 
specific dust emissions, 8 4 % reduction of sulphur dioxide emissions  since 2000. Aurubis has invested more 
than 670 Mio. € capital expenditure in enviro nmental protection measures since 2000 . Aurubis copper cathode 
has a lower life cycle footprint than global competitors e.g. twice lower CO2 emissions and only a third of the 
SO2 emissions.

» It is important to recognize there are technological limits to reducing emissions, so a continued high level of 
investment leads to lower improvements compared to past years. 
» If new air quality standards are set, then the high cost, the technological feasibility, and the time required for 
the industry to adapt must be considered.  
» The target values for metals shall be kept . The nonferrous  metals industry has already applied the BAT 
techniques for the prevention and reduction of emissions to air and cannot be expected to go beyond what 
is established in NFM BREF. Improvements in air quality must be achieved in the most cost-effective manner 
possible. It is important to take into account local conditions.  
 
Other sources and external factors shall be considered 
» Air quality is not determined by industrial sources alone. Other sources such as energy production, 
household heating, shipping, construction work, transport , etc play an increasingly important role . External 
factors such as climate and meteorological conditions and local geographical situations also influence air 
quality. These sources and factors should be considered together when developing and implementing 
measures to improve air quality. The industry should not face further restrictions before emission reductions 
from non-industrial sectors are not achieved. 
» Measures to improve ambient air quality shall be derived from an impact assessment to understand  the 
contribution of different sources and identify the most cost-effective measures 
» Contributions from natural sources shall be considered when assessing compliance . External factors such 
as unstable meteorological conditions (dry weather, thermal inversion) or  local geographical situation are 
important to consider at specific monitoring stations.  
 
Holistic approach and competitiveness of the EU industry 
» The EU Green Deal calls for a holistic policy to enable the transition to a sustainable society and ec onomy. 
Improvement of the CO2 reductions and climate objectives shall be coherent with air quality objectives as 
well as a functioning circular economy and raw  material policy while at the same time guaranteeing an 
international level playing field. 
» Our industry has to comply with a wide range of rigorous environmental legislation. It is important to consider 
the links between the various policies and environmental objectives  to avoid the negative impact of climate 
policies on air quality, or vice versa. For example, the Renewable energy directive calls for increased use of 
biomass, biomass is considered as a reference energy source in the update of the ETS fallback benchmarks 
but biomass combustion has negative impacts on air quality. 
» Global competitiveness of the industry is very important. In face of strong international competition, metal 
manufacturers cannot pass on additional costs resulting from EU legislation to customers but must instead 
compensate for this with continuous increases in production and efficiency.

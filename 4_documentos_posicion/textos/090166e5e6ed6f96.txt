Summary 
On 22/09/2021 the European Commission published a draft amendment to the Solvency  2 Directive, 
which will then be submitted to the European Parliament and the Council of the European Union. At the 
same time, the Commission must define level 2 measures (delegated Regulation)  to clarify numerous 
provisions. This point must be the subject of considerable vigilance in discussions between Member 
States and the European Commission so that level 2 measures do not distort the draft level 1 text and 
the objectives stated by the Commission. 
We would like to reiterate that FNMF represents most of the mutuals that fall under the Mutual Code , 
with some 150 mutuals that are members of the FNMF being subject to Solvency 2. While most of these 
mutuals provide medical expense insurance and, to a les ser extent, provident insurance (disability, 
invalidity, and death), some ten or so mutuals specialise in the pension-savings segment, covering around 
1.5 million members. As a result, these mutuals are seriously affected by the shortcomings of the long-
term businesses of the Solvency 2 prudential regime on the one hand, and by the proposed changes to 
the method of extrapolating the  risk-free interest rate curve and the interest rate shock on the other. 
Due to their specialisation , they are affected to a greater extent than com peting organisations that 
benefit from the diversification of their activities.  
We note that the measures envisaged by the European Commission will lead to a reduction in the SCR 
cover ratio for mutuals operating on pension-savings segment (- 11 pts of cover ratio on 31/12/2020). 
Consequently, the FNMF would like to see compensatory measures taken specifically for this type of 
organisation, for example via an even greater reduction in the risk margin or adapting the method of 
extrapolating the yield curve. 
On the other hand, we welcome the direction taken by the Commission regarding proportionality and 
simplification measures but regret the fact that the requirement for an external audit of the balance 
sheet has been maintained and that the thresholds for the Directive have not been raised even further.

1. Measures affecting the SCR and technical provisions 
 Overall impact 
The FNMF set up a fictitious mutual insurance company based on the characteristics of its main member 
mutuals operating in the pension-savings segment. A model was developed to calculate the technical 
provisions of this fictitious mutual and its Solvency Capital Requirement as well as the related cover ratio.  
Inter alia, this model has enabled us to estimate the impacts at 31/12/2020 of the Commission's proposed 
amendments to the Solvency 2 Directive. Such an approach makes it possible to get an estimate of the 
impacts specifically for mutuals specialising in pension/savings, as opposed to the impacts initially 
determined by Eiopa, and then by the Commission, on the entire market. The limits of this exercise lie in the 
dependence on the eco nomic and financial context: the results determined at 31/12/2020 are not directly 
comparable with the results of Eiopa/the Commission, for which the closing dates have been set at 
31/12/2019 and 30/06/2020. 
The following table summarises the impacts on the SCR and the overall impact of the various measures on 
the SCR cover ratio: 
 
Estimated impacts at 31/12/2020 
Best estimate + 0.9% 
SCR Rate +326.8% 
SCR Market +6.4% 
SCR Life +5% 
Overall SCR +6.6% 
Risk margin -31.9% 
Cover ratio - 11 pts 
 
Thus, overall, the measures envisaged by the Commission in the framework of the Solvency 2 review would 
lead to a decrease in the SCR cover ratio for mutual pension funds of around 11 points, at a minimum, 
attributable to a considerable increase in the interest rate SCR, partially offset by the other measures. 
The aim of a neutral revision in terms of cover ratio - the current system being considered by the FNMF 
and more widely by the majority of the sector's players as sufficiently prudent - is thus not achieved for 
organisations that only manage pension/savings. 
Below is a summary of the impacts we have  estimated for the different amendments proposed by the 
Commission and propose alternatives to achieve the objective of a neutral Solvency 2 revision (in terms of 
SCR cover ratio).  
 Risk margin 
We note the Commission's proposal to go further than the Eiopa proposal, by reducing the cost of capital by 
one point and removing the 50% floor for the " lambda" factor, thus enabling a significant reduction in the 
risk margin, in particular for organisations operating on long-term branches.. 
Nevertheless, despite these improvements, the cost of capital set at 5% still appears too high and without 
any relevant and realistic economic justification. Indeed, in previous consultations we provided an analysis

showing that a much lower cost of capital - in the order of 3% - would be fully justified from an economic 
viewpoint. Moreover, if this study were updated with current financial parameters, it is likely that the cost of 
capital would be even lower. Solvency 2 should cause organisations to value their liabilities in an economic 
way, so we continue to argue strongly for a further reduction in the cost of capital. 
Taking these elements into account and the fact that additional compensation must be obtained to 
counterbalance the impact of the revised rate shock and the new method of extrapolating the yield curve, 
we propose a further reduction in the cost of capital to 4% or even 3%.  
 Risk-free rate curve, interest rate shock and volatility adjustment  
The revision of the method for extrapolating the risk-free yield curve and the interest rate shock has a very 
significant impact on the capital requirement of insurers, particularly mutuals specialising in the 
pension/savings segment. While we understand the need for these changes, particularly given the low and 
negative interest rate environment, we would like to remind you once again of the need to provide significant 
compensation to mitigate the impact on insurers with a l ong-tail business.  
In this respect, we note that the changes planned for the volatility adjustment mechanism have only a very 
limited effect on the SCR cover ratio, and in any event do not allow for the absorption of the impacts of the 
interest rate shock or the revision of the risk-free yield curve. 
On these issues, we have identified two relevant avenues that could be pursued by the Commission: 
- Increase the GAR (General application ratio) used in the volatility adjustment mechanism to 100%, 
to enhance its effect. 
- Retain a convergence parameter greater than 10% in the method of extrapolating the yield curve. 
Indeed, the convergence parameter is currently envisaged to  gradually increase from 20% to 10% 
over a transitional period. We propose that the final convergence parameter should be higher than 
10%. We feel this measure is entirely appropriate, insofar as its impact would be directly linked to 
the duration of the commitments; long-tail businesses would thus benefit more. 
 Long-term equity investments 
The FNMF welcomes the European Commission's intention to significantly broaden the category of shares 
eligible for the reduced shock under the LTEI scheme.  
However, the draft text proposed by the Commission does not tell us whether  this political will is to be 
correctly translated into the level 2 texts. We will also be particularly vigilant with regard to the level 2 texts 
that will be produced, so that they correctly reflect the will expressed by the supervisor, by the market, 
and then by the Commission, that is to facilitate and reduce the capital cost of investment in shares, when 
it is carried out with a medium to long term view,  to strengthen the role of insurers as financiers of the 
economy and to ensure that the effective capital cost of these investments, for organisations with long term 
liabilities, correctly reflects the risk actually borne. 
2. Proportionality 
 Organisations with a low risk profile 
We note that the category of low-risk undertakings is defined through objective criteria. However, some 
criteria are to be specified in the level 2 measures (e.g. the limit on non-traditional investments). In addition, 
the draft text proposed by the Commission provides that national supervis ors could, in exceptional 
circumstances, remove the benefit of certain proportionality measures from undertakings classified as low 
risk (2° of Article 29b).

We feel it is  essential that safeguards are added to ensure that the provisions contained in the 
Commission's draft text are not distorted by the practices of national authorities (e.g. by misusing their 
right not to apply the full range of proportionality measures to organisations when they fall under the low-
risk regime). 
It is also important to ensu re that the concept of non -traditional investments is not used to render the 
measures in the draft Directive meaningless. 
 Increasing the liability thresholds 
The proposed increase in thresholds is good news as it will allow smaller organisations to avoid i ncurring 
disproportionate costs just to comply with Solvency 2. 
However, we regret that the Commission , even though it chose to set the new thresholds directly in the 
Directive rather than leaving it to the discretion of  national authorities, did not go further and propose an 
increase in thresholds in line with the Eiopa proposals . In particular, we feel that the organisations we 
represent, mainly composed of mutuals, which are mainly involved in health insurance, could have benefited 
from a broader exemption, given the nature of their activity and the associated risks, which do not 
necessarily justify the implementation of an organisation, governance, and risk management as complex (and 
indeed costly) as that caused by Solvency 2. 
At the very least, the proposed thresholds should be raised to take account of the inflation expected between 
2021 and the effective entry into force of the measures. For mutual insurers belonging to the FNMF, the 
increase in premiums in 2021 compared to 2020 (a direct reflection of the increase in health expenditure 
observed in the country) amounts to 2.6%.

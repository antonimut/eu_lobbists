Solvency II review – Unipol’s Position Paper 
Bologna, 12 January 2022 
 
Thanking the EU Commission for the opportunity to contribute to the debate on the 
Solvency II review, Unipol Gruppo acknowledge s that the proposed revision of the 
Solvency II Directive appears to be consistent with the stated objectives of the reform, 
namely: 
• incentives for long -term sustainable investments by insurance companies to 
support the economy; 
• introduction of new safeguards and tools for the prevention of systemic risk in 
the insurance sector; 
• mitigation of excessive short-term volatility on the solvency position of insurance 
companies; and 
• enhancement of the quality of supervision and coordination between Authorities, 
especially with regard to cross-border activities. 
In particular, the Commission's objective (albeit largely passed on the Delegated Acts) of 
encouraging investment by insurance companies in the real economy is appreciable  and 
coherent with the new economic policy guidelines and with the projects of the Capital 
Markets Union and the European Green Deal. 
Furthermore, the changes to the functioning of the Volatility Adjustment (VA) represent 
a significant improvement over EIOPA ’s proposal. The VA measures the excess volatility 
of bond spreads in relation to the assets of the insurance sector and its correct calibration 
allows a better stabilization of the own funds of the insurance companies in case of 
idiosyncratic shocks not linked to a deterioration in creditwo rthiness (artificial volatility). 
Current legislation provides that the V A is measured both within the reference currency 
area (currency component) and within the home State (country component). Over the 
years, various critical issues have arisen regarding the functioning of the VA, which has 
shown a poor ability to mitigate artificial volatility both in the currency component and in 
the activation methods of the country component, which is particularly relevant for Italian 
insurance companies. The Commission ’s proposal guarantees both a greater mitigation 
potential of excess vo latility generated in financial markets at EU level and a more 
constant mitigation of excess volatility generated at the domestic level. 
Having said that, on first analysis, several critical issues can be found in some of the 
Commission's proposed rules, which risk hindering the achievement of the reform 
objectives (e.g. with regard to the proposed correction of the risk adjustment mechanism 
for the VA ) or introduce disproportionate burdens for businesses. Below is a summary

1 Group Supervision 
1.1 Scope of group supervision (Art. 214) 
The Solvency II provides for a broad notion of group  and stringent conditions to exclude 
one or more companies from the scope of group supervision.  In the Commission ’s 
proposal, it is envisaged:  
(i) A further broadening of the notion of group, mainly aimed at bringing 
companies managed on a unified basis or subject to dominant influence 
within the scope of g roup supervision, even regardless of the existence of 
shareholding relationships with the parent company;  
(ii) The intro duction of new conditions which would restrict the possibility of 
excluding one or more companies from the perimeter of group supervision; 
in this respect, it is provided that the exclusion of the ultimate parent 
undertaking from group supervision shall not be possible if it “would result in 
a material improvement in the solvency position of the group”; And  
(iii) an extension of the powers of the Authorities, which may request changes 
the organizational structures of the group in order to identify a subject 
responsible for the obligations relating to group supervision.  
While agreeing with the rationale of the proposed changes, aimed at limiting the 
possibility of regulatory arbitrages and the circumvention of the rules on group 
supervision, on the other hand it is noted that a further tightening of the criteria for 
identifying the scope of group supervision risks producing distorting effects and would 
determine a more restrictive discipline than the specular one envisaged by the CRR 
(Regulation No. 575/2013, Art. 19), without a real need to do so.  
Indeed, the discipline on group supervision has the ultimate goal of ensuring an adequate 
monitoring of the risks stemming from the group entities  and is based on the fact that 
financial groups (and conglomerates) may pose peculiar risks, such as: 
(a) Double (or multiple) gearing , which occurs whe re the same capital is used 
simultaneously as a buffer against risk in two or more legal entities  (e.g. through 
intra-group loans), which can show increased assets on the balance sheet, but are 
not reflective of true risk; 
(b) Excessive leverage, which occurs where the debt is issued by a parent company 
and the proceeds are down-streamed in the form of equity to regulated entities 
of the group; 
(c) Contagion, as the financial difficulties in one group entity may spill over the other 
 
7 
 
ones, for example due to the existence of loans and guarantees between the 
group entities; 
(d) Lack of transparency , as some significant risk position within the group could 
remain unnoticed in case of particularly complex corporate structures; 
(e) Conflicts of interests , as the holding company could be “looting” the available 
capital of some subsidiary in favor of other subsidiaries. 
To oversee those risks , the ultimate parent undertaking s are included in the scope of 
group supervision, regardless of their legal form (banking, insurance undertakings or mere 
holding company), based on the assumption that such companies can pose peculiar risks. 
Provided that there is broad consensus on the rationale of the group supervision, it shall 
be noted that in certain cases the ultimate holding companies are not able to pose  the 
aforementioned risks , especially where they do not exercise coordination and c ontrol 
powers over the subsidiaries. This is the reason why banking and insurance regulations 
provide the possibility to exclude some entities from the scope of consolidation and group 
supervision, especially where those entities represent a “negligible interest” for the 
purpose of group supervision.  
The Commission, based on EIOPA’s advice, proposes to restrict the possibility to benefit 
from such exemption, imposing new conditions and limitation s when assessing the 
“negligible interest” that may justify the exclusion from the  scope of group supervision. 
The main issue with the proposed restrictive conditions is that they neglect the actual 
ability of the companies to turn into sources of peculiar risks that would require adequate 
monitoring through group supervision.  
In particular, the Commission proposes to clarify that exclusion the exclusion of the 
ultimate parent undertaking from the scope of group supervision shall not be possible 
where it “would result in a material improvement in the solvency position of the group ”. 
However, the proposed provision represent  a mere factual condition which does not 
necessary mean that the ultimate parent undertaking is able to pose particular risks from 
a group perspective. Instead, the crucial element t o be assessed when determining the 
scope of group supervision is the abil ity of the undertaking to perform the activities of 
management and coordination of its subsidiaries, which, ultimately, can turn into source 
of risks for the financial entities of the group.  
In many groups the activity of management and coordination of the subsidiaries is 
performed at an intermediate level  by another holding company or by a financial entity  
that has controlling shareholdings over other financial entities (banks, insurance 
companies and/or investment firms). In such cases, the ultimate parent holding act s just 
as a passive investor  with the aim of collecting dividends and has nor the actual power 
nor the legitimacy to directly influence the activities of its subsidiaries, as the coordination 
and manageme nt activities are performed at an intermediate level.  In this case, the 
 
8 
 
ultimate holding company lacks the capacity of i mposing to the participated 
bank/insurance companies to undertake actions that m ay harm the best in terest or the 
financial soundness of such companies. 
In the envisaged scenario, many natio nal competent Authorities excluded the ultimate 
parent companies from the scope of supervision s on the basis of the abovementioned 
“negligible interest”. Even EIOPA, in its Opinion on Solvency II review, acknowledges that 
“where an intermediate entity in the group is known (or proven) to actively manage the 
insurance activities in the group” it is “particularly plausible” that the holdco acting as 
passive investor has “no significant interest for the purposes of group supervision” (see p. 
527 of EIOPA’s Opinion). 
Following the above reasoning, it is not surprising that some national competent 
Authorities clarified in their regulations the possibility for ultimate parent undertakings to 
“opt-out” from the sc ope of group supervision  (see Bank of Italy, Circular 285 and the 
repealed ISVAP Reg. No 15/2008), subject to the following conditions: (i) the statutory by-
laws of parent company exclude the possibility for such company to perform the activities 
of coordination and management o f its subsidiaries; and (ii) the activity of coordination 
and management o f the subsidiaries is performed at an intermediate level by a 
participating financial entity.  This possibility is reasonable as it ensures a prevalence o f 
economic substance over the legal appearance in the exercise of supervisory powers.  
According the above reasoning, it could be claimed that an ultimate parent company 
acting as a mere passive investor with no coordination power over the subsidiaries (as the 
management and coordination is performed by another company at intermediate level) 
could still be excluded from the scope of group supervision pursuant to art. 214, par. 2, 
Solvency II, which allows such possibility when the inclusion of the company within the 
scope of group supervision would be "inappropriate or misleading for the purpose of 
complying with the objectives of group supervision". 
However, it appears necessary to seize the opportunity of revision of the Directive to 
expressly include the case discussed case among those that would justify an exclusion of 
the ultimate parent company from the scope of group supervision. Such solution would 
also be coherent with the regulatory provisions and supervisory practices of many 
national competent Authorities.  
To conclude, it is also worth considering that the Commission’s proposed amendments 
related to the scope of group supervision would lead to stricter discipline that the specular 
one set forth by CRD and CRR for credit entities, without a clear need to do so. Indeed, 
there is no clear evidence that the current provisions created major issues or overlooking 
on relevant risks within the group supervision. Indeed, the exclusion of an entity from the 
scope of group supervision is already carefully assessed by the national competent 
Authorities. In this regard, the Commission itself acknowledged that  “as for group 
 
9 
 
supervision, th e framework has been assessed as robust, laying emphasis on capital 
management and governance, and allowing for a better understanding and monitoring of 
risks at group level” (see Impact assessment accompanying the proposal for the Solvency 
II review, p. 2 48). More in general, legislation should not be amended on the basis of 
spurious events or to solve hypothetical issues  without solid evidence of the need to 
intervene on legislative level. 
In order to avoid distorting applications of the rules on the scope of group 
supervision, it is proposed to explicitly include in the Solvency II the possibility 
of excluding the mixed insurance or financial holding compan ies if: (i) such 
companies do not carry out business management and coordination activities 
insurance and financial investments, nor are they entitled to do so, as such 
possibility is not foreseen or is precluded by the statutory provisions; and (ii) 
have as a single significant shareholdi ng a stake in a company (re) insurance, 
financial or participation which, on the other hand, at a lower level, exercises 
management and coordination activities in relation to the others (re) insurance 
companies and financial entities of the group.  
In addition, suggestion is deleting the proposed restrictive conditions to assess 
the “negligible interest” for the purpose of group supervision, as they would 
unduly penalize certain groups where the coordination and management of the 
subsidiaries is performed a t an intermediate level and also because the 
Commision’s proposal would create a more restrictive discipline than that 
provided for banks, even though nor the Commission nor EIOPA provided clear 
evidence on the need to do so. 
1.2 Related credit institutions, investment firms and financial 
institutions (Art. 228) 
Solvency II Directive  provides that, in the calculation of the group solvency, the 
contribution to the Group eligible own funds of the related financial entities should be 
calculated as the sum of proportional share of the own funds of each undertaking, where 
those funds are calculated according to the respective  sectoral rules. The Commission 
proposes to entitle the Authorities to impose the deduction of non-distributable reserves 
or other components of own funds that according to the opinion of the same Authorities 
have a reduced loss-absorbency capacity - preference shares, subordinated accounts paid 
by members of mutual societies, liabilities subordinated, DTA - and which are included in 
own funds in excess of the requirements of  own funds calculated according to sectoral 
rules. In the Commission proposal, the company could overcome the deduction of these 
elements if it demonstrates that these elements are equally available to cover the group

SCR.  
In this regard, while sharing the rationale of the provision, which substantially follows that 
already present in art. 330 of the Delegated Acts concerning insurance subsidiaries, it is 
worth noting that the inclusion of DTAs in the above list does not s eem correct from a 
methodological standpoint, as the calculation of DTAs in the banking sector regulation 
follows different mechanisms and requirements compared to the corresponding 
insurance discipline.  
It is therefore proposed to eliminate the reference to DTAs in the new Art. 228, 
par. 2  
1.3 Calculation of the group solvency (Art. 230 ff.) 
Current market conditions, characterized by exogenous and endogenous structural 
changes, require massive investments by the insurance undertakings , especially in  
innovation and technological infrastructures. These investments are necessary to develop 
and maintain a business model that is sustainable in the long term and, more in general, 
in order to maintain the systemic role that has always characterized the insurance sector. 
The resources needed to this end require however greater dimensional scale and, 
consequently, a consolidation of the insurance sector.  
The prudential framework, on the one hand seems willing to favor the consolidation of 
the sector as it is i nstrumental to the development of an increasingly diversified, sound 
and sustainable market, on the other poses significant limitations the computability of the 
excess of third-party capital by the parent companies, a case that typically represents the 
result of an aggregation between undertakings.  
In order to allow the necessary consolidation of the insurance sector, it is therefore 
essential to modify the rules for calculating the group solvency which, in the hypothesis 
of application of the line -by-line consolidation method to non -controlling majority 
shareholdings, does not allow the inclusion of the share of the subsidiaries’ capital 
surplus, thus determining a negative impact on the calculation of the group solvency. 
Indeed, the computability of the s hare of third-party own funds to an extent maximum 
equal to the value of the corresponding capital requirements, in fact determines a dilution 
of the group solvency ratio, given that the ratio of eligible own funds and capital 
requirements of third parties would be equal to 1.  
It is therefore proposed to revise the solvency group calculation criterion by 
removing the aforementioned penalization in the calculation of the own funds  
of the parent companies , as the current provisions hinder the growth by

external lines, which is a consolidated trend for other industrial sectors.  
1.4 Supervision on the system of governance (Art. 246) 
As part of the provisions on the system  of governance of the group , the Commission  
proposes amendments aimed at further clarifying the responsibility of the ultimate 
participating company to ensure that there is “a clear organizational structure, with well-
defined, transparent and consistent lines of  responsibilities and segregation of duties 
within the group”.  
On this point, the proposed Directive adds that: “when the people who manage  actually 
a (re) insurance group or are responsible for other key functions are also the persons who 
actually manage one or more (re) insurance companies or others related enterprises, or 
are responsible for other key functions in one of these enterprises,  the participating 
company should ensure that the roles and responsibilities at the group level  are clearly 
segregated from those applicable at the individual level of each company" (Art. 246, par. 
5).  
While sharing the rationale of the provision, essentially intended to strengthen a sound 
corporate governance and, in particular, the independence of the key functions' holders, 
it is worth noting that the proposed provision presents significant issues, as:  
(a) there is no need of tightening the current provisions, given that the corporate 
governance systems are already governed by an extensive and prescriptive regulatory 
framework, which also includes the EIOPA guidelines on the corporate governance system 
(GL No. 65), and that in a recent peer -review EIOPA itself recorded significant 
improvements over supervisory practices on key function holders (link);  
(b) they establish principles of an absolute nature but are not clear about the ir 
practical implications, which seem potentially very prescriptive, to the point of being able 
to disproportionately compress the principle of organizational freedom of business; 
(c) they fail to consider that  in certain circumstances  the accumulation of offices is 
instrumental to strengthening the expertise of corporate bodies and fundamental 
functions with reference to certain areas, as demonstrated by the fact that,  by way of 
example, some national authorities request or expressly support  the accumulation of 
positions in the administrative, management and  control and ownership of the risk 
management function (see EIOPA - peer review on key functions, p. 6).  
It is therefore proposed to eliminate par. 5 of the revised Art. 246, in the part in 
which provides, with an apodictic formulation and uncertain applicative 
solutions, that "The participating company should ensure that the roles and

2 Long-term guarantee measures 
2.1 Extrapolation – (Art. 77e, par. 1, point (aa)) 
The Commission proposed to amend the provisions related to the extrapolation of risk -
free interest rates, envisaging an overall change of the extrap olation formula in future 
amendments to the Delegated Acts, in order to take better account of the long -term 
interest rates observed in the markets. The new extrapolation method should be 
introduced gradually (transitional mechanism), over a period of time  starting from the 
date of application of the Directive until 1st January 2032.  
The Commission asked EIOPA to publish, in the context of the technical information 
provided monthly by the Authority, the structure by maturity of risk -free interest rates 
with and without the application of VA, MA and new extrapolation methodologies.  This 
calculation is aimed at assessing the impact of the risk -free curve on the technical 
provisions reported in the "Solvency and financial condition report" (SFCR) purs uant to 
the new Art. 51 paragraph 8, net of the effects determined by the long term  guarantees 
and the new extrapolation method. 
It should be noted that this proposal entails excessive disclosure obligations in the phase-
in period for the insurance companies, with particular regard to the need to calculate the 
technical provisions for the SFCR both through (a) the use of risk-free curves and the new 
extrapolation methodologies and (b) the use of risk -free curves  without applying such 
methodologies.   
It is proposed that the technical provisions reported in the SFCR shall be 
calculated only through the risk -free curve that uses the new extrapolation 
method. 
2.2 Volatility Adjustment / Scaling factor 
Even though the proposed changes to the functioning of the VA represent a step in the 
right direction, there is a need to clarify the role of the "scaling factor" in the VA formula. 
This factor - included by EIOPA in its Opinion on the Solvency II review – allows to correct 
the weight of fixed-income investments included in the representative portfolios bringing 
this weight to 100%.  
In the Commission proposal the scaling factor is absent both from the permanent VA 
formula and from that of the macro-VA, despite the Commission's intention to carry out 
a review of the representative portfolios in order to ensure a correct weighting of bond 
 
14 
 
investments hedging insurance liabilities, probably to offset the elimination of the scaling 
factor from the VA formula.  
There is a need to clarify the role of the "scaling factor" in VA formula. Also, it 
is needed to adequately evaluate the effects of the revision of the 
representative portfolios provided in the Commission’s proposal , in order to 
consistently calculate the weight of fixed-income investments in the insurance 
sector. 
2.3 Volatility Adjustment – CSSR (Art. 77d, par. 3) 
In the Commission's proposal, the Credit Spread Sensitivity Ratio (CSSR) is defined as the  
ratio between “the sensitivity of the assets of an insurance or reinsurance undertaking in 
a currency to changes in credit spreads" and the" sensitivity of the technical provisions of 
that undertaking in that currency to changes in interest rates". This provision does not 
appear fully consistent as it seems entailing a comparison between sensitivities (of assets 
and liabilities) with different exogenous variables (credit spreads vs interest rates).  
In order to align the numerator and denominator calculation it is suggested to 
use the wording provided in the EIOPA Opinion on the revision of the Solvency 
II for the purposes of calculating AR4, which defines the CSSR as: "the ratio of 
the 'Sensitivity o f the undertakings fixed income investments against changes 
in credit spreads 'over the' sensitivity of the best estimate liabilities against a 
change in the amount of the volatility adjustment".  
2.4 Volatility Adjustment – Risk Correction (Art. 86, par. 1, point 
(i)) 
The draft Directive under discussion entitles the Commission to set forth in the Delegated 
Acts the "percentage of the spread that  represents the portion attributable to a realistic 
assessment of expected losses or  unexpected cred it or other risks of the assets ", in 
relation to each asset class. This delegation  indicates the clear will of the Commission to 
make changes to the current calculation method of the VA Risk Correction and could lead 
the legislators to adopt the solution proposed by EIOPA in its Opinion on the Solvency II 
review. Indeed, EIOPA proposes fixing the risk correction equal to a certain percentage of 
the spread recorded in the market (30% for government bonds and 50% for corporate 
bonds), thus abandoning the current ap proach which instead corrects  for the risk at the 
long-term average (30 years) of the spread over risk-free interest rates.  
 
15 
 
The amendment proposed by EIOPA would determine a lower reactivity of the VA in 
phases of financial markets  distress, thus providin g incentives pro -cyclical investment 
behavior by insurance companies. The Risk Correction would instead be extremely 
correlated with the spreads’ volatility, thus mitigating the Volatility Adjustment 
effectiveness during periods of high financial turbulence. 
For the reasons set out above, it is therefore proposed to evaluate  Risk 
Correction estimation methods alternative to that proposed by EIOPA, with the 
aim of granting greater stability over time to the Risk Correction and allowing 
the Volatility Adjustment to increase its effectiveness in the mitigation of excess 
artificial volatility in the financial markets during periods characterized by high 
spreads.

3 Macro-prudential tools 
3.1 Accounting systemic risk in the ORSA calculation (Art. 45) 
The Commission proposes to amend the rule on internal risk and solvency assessments. 
The proposed changes would introduce the obligation for insurance companies to  
consider within the ORSA the way in which their activities affect the financial markets’ 
developments and the possibility that such activities turn into sources of systemic risk. 
In this regard, it is worth considering that the assessments of a macro-prudential nature 
are not coherent with the purpose of the ORSA, which is a  document of strategic value 
for the administrative body,  aimed at assessing the prospective sustainability of the 
company at a micro-prudential level. Rather, assessments regarding systemic risks should 
be a responsibility of the Authorities, which are the only entities possessing all the tools 
and information necessary for this purpose (QRT, RSR, SFCR, stress test), unlike insurance 
undertakings, which only have idiosyncratic information  relating to their business 
perimeter.  
It is therefore proposed to eliminate the obligation for insurance companies to 
introduce macro-prudential assessments within the ORSA, or alternatively, that 
the Delegated Acts provide a simple description qualitative theme, without 
imposing additional quantitative metrics of reference.    
3.2 Liquidity Risk Management (Art. 144a and 246a) 
The proposed implementation of a Liquidity Risk Management Plan meets the objective 
of ensuring that the insurance undertakings  have the necessary liquidity to fulfil the ir 
obligations even under stressful conditions. Even though the purpose of the Commission's 
proposal is agreeable, it is worth noting that the current regulations already  set forth 
numerous and solid safeguards ai med at guaranteeing the stability of company liquidity 
(Emergency Plan, ORSA) and, therefore, there is no need to implement a  further 
contingency plan on liquidity management that would have a negligible added-value 
compared to the robust safeguards already in place.  
Furthermore, the Liquidity Risk Management Plan at group level risks turning into a 
useless exercise for those  groups that do not adopt centralized liquidity management 
(cash pooling) provided that, in this case, liquidity management is entirely attributed to 
the subsidiaries and, thus, is not decided at group level.  
For the reasons set out above, suggestion is to eliminate the provision relating 
 
17 
 
to the implementation of a "Liquidity Risk Management Plan", both at the level 
of individual company and at group level. In the alternative, it is proposed that: 
(1) the Authorities are only entitled to issue non-binding guidelines in relation 
to the contents of the Plan, without imposing the calculation of new indicators; 
and (2) the Group's “Li quidity Risk Management Plan” is provided only for 
groups adopting centralized liquidity management.  
3.3 Suspension of redemption rights and dividends payment (Art. 
144b and 144c) 
The Commission proposal would entitle the competent A uthorities to require the 
temporary suspension of the redemption rights of policyholders on life insurance policies 
in the event that there are "material risks"  related to the liquidity of the insurance 
undertaking. On this point, there is a n urgent need to define clearly what is meant by 
"material risks" (by accurately identifying the  metrics for measuring materiality). Also, it 
is crucial taking into account the different characteristics of the Life and Non-Life business 
lines, since the calculation of th e prospective liquidity requirement s and the related 
methods for cash flow projections  are significantly different: in particular, for the Non -
Life business it is necessary to provide  cash flow projections that also include forecasts 
relating to new business and not only certain and accounted flows.  
Similarly, with reference to the provision empowering the competent Authorities to 
require the suspension of the distributions to shareholders  in the event of exceptional 
shocks affecting the market, it is appropriate introducing ex ante a quantitative metric of 
reference acting as a trigger for the activation of this exceptional power. Indeed, only by 
clarifying ex ante the relevant metrics , insurance companies would be able incorporate 
them within their own  risk monitoring and management system, in order to prevent 
situations impeding the payment of dividends.  
These indicators should consider what is the illiquid component of the assets of the  
individual undertaking that are vulnerable to the shocks pointed out by the legislator. In 
general, it is worth noting that the two new p owers conferred on the Authorities  
(suspension of redemption rights and prohibition of distribution of dividends) overlap the 
already existing safeguards and tools, considering that insurance undertakings already 
adopt a risk management framework that includes the liquidity risk, in order to prevent, 
mitigate and manage such risk. The safeguards adopted for this purpose by the insurance 
undertakings are also already subjected to stress tests and numerous other checks: in this 
sense, the introduction of new powers by the supervisory Authority seems to question 
the effectiveness of the entire existing regulatory provisions related to the risk 
management framework.  
 
18 
 
In light of the above, it is suggested to reconsider the appropriateness of 
conferring the new powers to the supervisory Authorities relating to the 
suspension of redemptions rights and distributions to shareholders, as liquidity 
risks are already widely included in the overall risk management framework of 
insurance undertakings and subject to timely and accurate supervision by the 
Authorities. 
In the alternative, we propose the introduction of clarifications about the 
conditions enabling the Authorities to exercise the new powers, preferably by 
indicating objective quantitative metrics that restrict the scope of discretion 
conferred to the Authorities and that would allow insurance undertakings to 
prevent the occurrence of stressful situations by including these metrics as part 
of their risk management framework.

4 Reporting 
4.1 Regular Supervisory Report (RSR) - (Art. 112, par. 7, art. 35 par. 
5a e art. 256 b) 
There are significant criticalities in the  Commission's proposal  to oblige insurance  
companies that adopt (total or partial) internal models to report the SCR calculation also 
applying the standard formula. The introduction of this obligation would nullify  the 
usefulness of adopting an internal model, which responds to the need to represent the  
risks to which the insurance company is exposed more precisely than the standard model. 
Processing the calculations for the SCR also with the standard model would increase the 
compliance burden for the companies that a dopt internal models, against little to none 
information benefit for the Authority and for policyholders. 
It is proposed to eliminate the obligation for insurance companies that adopt 
internal models to also communicate the calculation of the SCR with a standard 
formula. 
4.2 Solvency and Financial Condition Report (SFCR) – (Art. 51) 
The Commission proposes the division of the SFCR into two parts: the first addressed to 
policyholders and beneficiaries, the second addressed to other market participants. The 
subdivision of the SFCR would result in a duplication of the document, considering that 
the information to be reported in the part of the SFCR addressed to policyholders and 
beneficiaries consists of a subset of the information to be illustrated in the part of the 
SFCR addressed to other market participants. 
It is proposed to eliminate the rule relating to the subdivision of the SFCR, which 
would result in a minimum gain in terms of information benefit, not 
proportionate to the increase in the compilation costs for insurance companies.

5 Future amendments to the Delegated 
Regulation 
In an official Communication - published at the same time as the draft Directive - the 
European Commission has announced the main political priorities on which it intends  to 
propose future amendments to the Delegated Acts. Some of the  changes appear 
particularly relevant to the Italian insurance industry and, therefore, we take the 
opportunity to share some considerations about that.  
5.1 Long Term Equity 
The Long Term Equity category was introduced by the Delegated Regulation (EU) 
2019/931 - which amended the Delegated Regulation (EU) 2015/35 - in order to relax the 
capital requirements (from 39% for listed shares and 49% for unlisted shares to 22%) for 
equity investments classifiable as "long -term equity investments". The eligibility criteria 
provided for by the Delegated Regulation 2019/931 are however particularly restrictive 
and have therefore led to an extremely small number of equity investments eligible for 
the envisaged capital relief.  
In our view, the main issue is that the discipline confuses the assessment of the ability to 
hold long -term holdings  - which is sufficient to justify an adjustment of the related 
prudential treatment - with the obligation to maintain such holdings for a period of at 
least 5 years on average (Article 171a paragraph 1, letter e) of the Delegated Regulation). 
Likewise, the ban on using the portfolio of assets identified as Long Term Equity to cover 
losses from other activities of the company (paragraph 1, letter c), of article 171a) appears 
to be difficult to implement and does not fit the purpose of justifying a different prudential 
treatment of long-term equity investment.  
Without prejudice to the need to guarantee the financial soundness of the insurance 
undertakings, the Commission has anticipated that it wants to relax the eligibility criteria 
of long-term equity investments in order to incre ase of at least 15% the total amount of 
equity investments eligible for the capital relief. In such a scenario, the Commission has 
foreseen a reduction of the capital requirements of at least 10.5 billion Euros. Such  
resources could be used to finance the economic recovery and European businesses in 
the long term, especially SMEs and infrastructure s, in line with the new policy agenda 
pointed out in the new Strategy for the Capital Markets Union.  
In order to ensure an effective relaxation of the eligibility criteria, suggestion is 
erasing the ban on using the asset s identified as Long Term Equity to cover 
 
21 
 
losses deriving from others business activities (Article 171a, paragraph 1, letter 
c). Besides , suggestion is eliminating the requirement of a minimum  holding 
period for Long Term Equities (at least five years on average , as provided by  
Art. 171a, paragraph 1, letter e). 
It is also proposed to set forth similar rules for Long Term Equity Investments 
regardless of whether they cover life or non-life liabilities. In this regard, it is 
noted that EIOPA (Art. 171 a, paragraph 1, letter g) in its Opinion on the 
Solvency II review proposed a different approach between investments covering 
life liabilities and investments covering non-life liabilities. The solution proposed 
by EIOPA  would make particularly complex to apply to non -life insurance 
portfolios the preferential treatment envisaged  for Long Term Equity 
Investments.  
In particular, suggestion is replacing the holding period requirement with the 
request to provide  a liquidity test , taking properly  into account the peculiar 
characteristics of both life and  non-life business. Non-life companies are indeed 
characterized by liabilities with a shorter duration than life companies and by a 
high retention level of the premiums underwritten. For these reasons, a 
liquidity-test applied to non -life companies should not only consider the cash 
flows deriving from the contracts signed and currently in force, but also the 
prospective cash flows deriving from the new business, appropriately adjusted 
taking into account an appropriate margin of uncertainty.  
Finally, a reduction of the capital requirements is also urged for direct 
investments in non -strategic equity investments, infrastructure or funds of 
infrastructures, private equity and private debt funds, where these types of 
investments comply with requirements currently envisaged for “investments a 
long term". Such relaxation of the capital requirements would not jeopardize 
the patrimonial and financial soundness of insurance companies, as adequate 
Asset Liability Management (which appropriately takes into account duration 
mismatch bet ween assets and liabilities in the insurance balance sheet) and 
liquidity risk management can prove to the capacity of insurance companies to 
hold risky investments for a certain period of time (for example at least 5 years), 
without having to resort to forced sales of such assets. 
5.2 Other topics 
The Commission's intention to reduce the Risk Margin through a reduction of the Cost of 
Capital from 6% to 5%  is to be welcomed . The industry has repeat edly challenged the 
 
22 
 
calibration of this measure, submitting to the attention of the Authorities and  of the 
legislators the excessive absorption of capital determined by the high level of the Cost of 
Capital and, thus, proposed a reduction to 3% based on an alysis and empirical evidence. 
The reduction announced by the Commission represents in any case a positive novelty, 
which will allow insurers to free up resources in favor of investments linked to the real 
economy.  
With reference to the Matching Adjustmen t (MA), although the Commission has 
proposed to extend the diversification benefits even to the portfolios on which the MA 
applies, there are still restrictive regulatory requirements in the Solvency II Directive 
which make this instrument not applicable in most Member States. It is therefore of great 
importance to create the regulatory and supervisory context to grant the application of 
this measure in all the national markets.  
Regarding the interest rate risk sub -module, the Commission endorsed the  EIOPA's 
proposal on the new methods of calculating interest rate shocks, but only  up to the last 
liquidity point of the risk-free curve (20 years). Considering the orientation expressed by 
the Commission on the interest rate risk sub-module, it is worth remarking the need for 
introduce less prescriptive interest rate shocks than those proposed by  EIOPA. With 
particular regard to the negative shocks on interest rates,  it is crucial to introduce a less 
penalizing floor than that proposed by EIOPA (-1.25%), which would also more in line with 
the empirical evidence currently recorded in the market. EIOPA’s proposal on the interest 
rate risk sub-module could indeed lead to a sharp increase in the capital requirements for 
insurers, which would be forced to simulate the effects of unlikely cuts in interest rates 
on their balance sheets.

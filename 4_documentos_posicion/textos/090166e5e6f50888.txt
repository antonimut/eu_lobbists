 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Zurich Feedback to the proposed amendments to the Solvency II Directive 
 
 
Zurich welcomes the proposed amendments to the Solvency II Directive, as well as the 
outlined revisions to the Delegated Acts. We are pleased with the targeted 
improvements of the Commission to the initial EIOPA advice, which result in a more 
balanced outcome and look forward to understanding the full implications of the 
Solvency II review when the proposed changes to the Delegated Acts are released. We 
trust the sentiments expressed by the Commission in their Communicati on 
accompanying the proposal, are borne out in the details of the Level 2 text, particularl y 
regarding the Long-Term Equity Risk and Risk Margin. 
 
Nevertheless, we see some areas that off er scope for further improvements to the 
Solvency II framework, while strictly maintaining the requisite and high level of 
policyholder protection the current regulations provide. 
 
First, the new proportionality requirements are welcomed, particularly the new 
designation for Low-Risk Undertakings. However, the criteria deserve to be refined to 
ensure the desired outcome is achieved and to avoid a too narrow applicability in 
practice. Rather than setting a single limit for the size criteria a corridor could be 
introduced, taking the current limit as the minimum and introducing a new maximum 
level. This would allow NSAs to tailor limits to the local market and more undertaking s to 
benefit from proportionality without causing undue risk. The 5% limit on cross -bord er 
business could then be applied to only insurers that are larger than the common 
minimum level. 
 
Second, the review is an opportunity to review the calibration of the Standard Formul a 
to account for developments since the initial formulation over 5 ye ars ago and 
inconsistencies in the current regulations. A recalibration of the property risk factor, to 
account for the EU market would be appropriate. In addition, the use of the dynami c 
volatility adjustment should be available for Standard Formula users . By recalculating 
both the assets and liabilities in accordance with the credit spread stress, this will ensure 
the capital is commensurate with the risk and more accurately reflect the capital requi red 
in such a spread-widening scenario.  
 
Third, there are a number of key parameters that would benefit from specification in the 
Level 1 text, for example the convergence parameter and First Smoothing Point for the 
calibration of the risk-free rates, and key Risk Margin parameters. Enshrining these in the 
Level 1 text would ensure stability and avoid these key parameters potentially being 
altered and causing undue volatility. 
 
Fourth, the proposed macro -prudential requirements, especially the early intervention 
powers in periods of exceptional distress, are in our view unwarranted as it is 
unnecessary to intervene before the Solvency Capital Requirement is breached. Indeed , 
Solvency II has been designed with early intervention powers for supervisors in mind 
with two complementary levels of capital (the Minimum Capital Requirement and the 
significantly higher Solvency Capital Requirement intervention point). We are of the view 
that these proposals could negatively affect the competitiveness of the EU insurance 
market. 
 
Finally, an important area t hat should be reviewed further is the level of reporting 
required of undertakings. While the Commission has previously recognized the overall 
reporting burden was too high, the proposed revisions, in conjunction with the changes 
to the implementing technic al standards proposed by EIOPA, will actually result in 
increased reporting burden. By streamlining some of the regulations, the sought -after 
reduction in the effort for insurers could be achieved, for example by allowing 
undertakings to only provide quantitative data in the SFCR to other market participants 
with no mandatory narrative, or through removing the quarterly reporting requirement a t 
year-end.

The Commission’s proposal blended well EIOPA’s advice and constructive feedback 
provided by the industry. We hope that in developing the amendments to the Delega ted 
Acts, the Commission continues to heed balanced and constructive comments, to 
ensure that the final review is one which continues to provide the highest levels of 
protection to policyholders, wh ile addressing the potential for improvement of the 
current framework.

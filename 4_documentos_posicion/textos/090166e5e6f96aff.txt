    
  
Unipol position paper on Insurance Recovery 
and Resolution Directive (IRRD) 
Bologna, 13 January 2022 
 
Introduction 
Unipol Group welcomes the possibility to share its views on the Insurance 
Recovery and Resolution Directive (IRRD ) and it would like to take this 
opportunity to provide some targeted comments and proposals. 
On a general note , we acknowledge th at the ambitious legislative initiative 
presented by the Commission could help to build a more resilient environment 
in which policyholders and market participants may feel safer, thus enhancing 
the efficiency of the entire economic system.  
In our view, an appropriate and proportiona te set of minimum  and 
harmonised rules governing the recovery and resolution framework could 
effectively help in establishing a level playing field with respect to the 
management of insolvency crisis among undertakings operating in different 
Member States. 
That being said,  while we are aware of the positive effect that the banking 
sector has experienced from a  similar legislation, we are of the opinion  that 
some of the requirements of the proposal  may still be improved , even if 
already ‘customized’ to take into consideration the different characteristics of 
the insurance sector.  
Firstly, it is important that the development of recovery and resolution

It has to be noted that, differently from the banking sector, there is a limited 
possibility that the financial distress of an insurance undertaking may 
contribute to increase  systemic r isk. In addition, due to the very different 
characteristics of the insurance’s liabilities, the financial p osition of 
undertakings deteriorates at a much slower pace than that of banks.  
Secondly, focusing on the specific requirements of the proposal, there is broad 
consensus among stakeholders that the Recovery and Resolution framework 
may introduce unnecessary administrative burden on the sector while, at the 
same time, reducing the scope of action for the management and the 
shareholders in favour of resolution authorities’ decisions. In this respect, it is 
worth considering that: 
(i) the Solvency II framework already provides some recovery measures 
that have to be activated in case the solvency ratio of the company falls 
below a given threshold; 
(ii) it is a necessary condition for the insurance undertakings to be  fully 
aware of the explicit thresholds that would trigger the implementation 
of the recovery or resolution measures. In this way, insurance  
companies could be able to monitor and manage the evolution of the 
financial distress , so as to limit any surge in uncertainty  that could 
favour the widespread of financial distress; 
(iii)  decisions concern ing how to manage an undertaking  throughout a 
financial crisis are explicit responsibility of the management of the 
company; therefore , the recovery process should recognise 
shareholders’ rights and wills. 
As previously stated, we firmly support the objectives of the IRRD proposal but 
we believe that, building on the banking sector’s experience, some further 
clarification and/or exemption are necessary.  
Therefore, at this stage and as better explained below we advocate for clearer 
definitions of the contents of the pre-emptive Recovery plan, as well as a 
transparent communication between undertakings and authorities with

I. Pre-Emptive Recovery Plan and Resolution Plan 
Article 5 of the proposed Directive prescribes that “Member States shall ensure 
that insurance and reinsurance undertakings  […] draw up and keep updated a 
pre-emptive Recovery plan. Such pre -emptive R ecovery plan shall contain 
measures to be taken by the undertaking concerned to restore its financial position 
where that position has significantly deteriorated. ” In this sense, Member States 
shall define a framework of indicators to identify the deterioration of the financial 
position.  
With respect to this set of indicators , the Directive refers to “ capital, liquidity, 
asset quality, profitability, market conditions, macro -economic conditions and 
operational events. Indicators relating to the capital position shall as a minimum 
contain any breach of the Solvency Capital Requirement”.  
In our opinion, it should be recognised with respect to the breach of the Solvency 
Capital Requirement (SCR), that it  represents the first pillar of the Solvency II 
regime and, thus, the Directive already prescribes remedial actions. For this 
reason, we believe that remedial actions concerning the breach of the SCR 
prescribed by the IRRD should be aligned with those alre ady proposed by the 
Solvency II regime 
Further, the pre -emptive Recovery plan has to set out a “ range of remedial 
actions”. With this respect, our view is that it should be adequately considered 
that, as shown by evidence, insurers very rarely fail rapidly in a disorderly manner. 
In addition, m ost insurance groups do  not perform critical operations and, in 
general, are far less susceptible than banks to " runs", have  greater liquidity 
buffers, and are limited interconnected to each other.  
For these reasons, foreseeing binding prescriptions for insurance undertakings 
may seem inefficient. Art. 5(9) prescribes that “ An insurance or reinsurance 
undertaking […] that decides to refrain from taking such remedial action although 
 
 
  
4 
 
an indicator as referred to in the first subparagraph has been met, shall notify such 
decision to the supervisory authority without delay ”. Unipol Group does not 
agree with the approach suggested by the wording of this Article, i.e. that an 
insurance is expected to automatically implement remedial actions defined in 
the plan when the specific conditions are met. Unipol Group would opt for a  
different and more flexible approach , which  should consider the remedial 
actions described in the plan as a “ toolbox” to which the undertaking may 
recur to restore the financial position after consultation with the Authority.  
This interpretation seems in line with what is already foreseen with regard to the 
Resolution plan, in relation to which point (i) of Article 9(6) refers to “a detailed 
description of the different resolution strategies that could be applied in light of 
the different possible scenarios and the applicable timescales”.  
Hence, in light of the above , we believe that  for both the pre-emptive Recovery 
Plan and Resolution Plan , the proposal should recognise that the idiosyncratic 
components of each stress event are inherently unique and, for this reason, 
hard to predict and foresee.  As a consequence, the proposal should not set 
out, ex-ante, all the possible actions \strategies that need t o be  taken for a 
stress event on the basis of  a “one size fits all” approach.   
Our suggestion would be that t he pre-emptive Recovery plan , as well as the 
Resolution plan  are presented and considered as a flexible guide  for the 
undertakings and the public Authorities to manage crisis conditions  rather 
than a set of binding requirements . In this sense, the two plans should be 
perceived as complementary to the fruitful and consolidated process of 
dialogue between the Authorities and the insurance undertakings.  
Moreover, with specific regard to  the content of the Resolution plan, article 
9(6) prescribes that only “ a summary of the key elements of the plan  shall be 
disclosed to the insurance and reinsurance undertaking concerned ”. Our view is 
that clarification is needed on what would be included in the key elements of the 
plan that would be disclosed to the undertakings. The essence of risk management 
hinges in a continued and precise tracking of “objective” variables. Therefore, 
given the consequences of a re solution and to ensure legal certainty , the 
insurance undertaking should always be aware of the precise indicators that

would trigger the resolution, and to which extent the management will be 
involved in the resolution process.  
As stated in article 19 of  the Directive, “resolution authorities take a resolution 
action […] when determined that the insurance is failing or likely to fail ”. This 
implies that there is the possibility that shareholders are wound up before the 
insolvency of the company. We believ e that, given the powers granted to the 
resolution Authorities, insurance undertakings should be fully aware  of all the 
indicators that would led to the intervention of the Authority. Only be ensuring 
legal certainty,  the management could perfectly implement all the necessary 
measure in order to avoid resolution and, hence, preserve stakeholders’ interest.  
II. Resolvability 
Unipol Group fully understands and supports the introduction of a Resolution 
Authority. In our view, it is necessary indeed that sensitive processes, as those 
concerning the possible resolution of an undertaking , are managed by a 
dedicated, separated Authority, empowered with the necessary knowledge 
and standings to fulfil the eventual steps of resolution. 
It is for these reasons that we recognise the need to award all the proposed 
powers to the new Authority.  
That said, in our view, it is important to make the undertakings fully aware of 
the entire set of information that could lead the Authority to pursue  a 
resolution action. As previously stated, with respect to  the stake holders’ 
interest, it is fundamental that the management could set up all the necessary 
measures to avoid the resolution outcome. To this end, we believe that article 
13 of the Directive,  where it is prescribed that “ resolution authorities, after 
having consulted the supervisory authority, assess the extent to which insurance 
are resolvable without the assumption of any extraordinary public financial 
support. An insurance or reinsurance un dertaking shall be deemed resolvable 
where it is feasible and credible for that undertaking to be liquidated under normal 
insolvency proceedings, or for the resolution ”, leaves room for open 
interpretation. By definition, the assessment of resolvability hi nges on 
idiosyncratic or, at least, highly volatile, scenarios.

Therefore, given that according to  the proposal the draft regulatory technical 
standards that EIOPA is expected to develop  (level 2 of the legislation) have the 
task to specify the  matters and criteria for the assessment of the resolvability , 
we consider necessary that the proposal (level 1 of the legislation)  provides for 
clear and certain quantitative and verifiable triggers.   
 
In conclusion, we are of the opinion that , according to the text of the proposal,  
the assessment of resolvability by the Resolution Authorities entails two possible 
drawbacks:  
i) ambiguity, due to the fact that the definition of what “resolvability” 
means is vague  and, as such, does not include  a detailed reference to 
the framework for its assessment , thus leaving room for mis -
interpretations; 
ii) uncertainty on the implementation of powers by the resolution 
Authorities. Article 15 of the Directive introduces “ alternative 
measures” that could be implemented by the Autho rities to remove 
“impediments to the resolvability ”, such as the restriction or the 
prevention from the development of new or existing business lines or 
changes to the legal/operational structures of the insurance. In light 
of the pervasive characteristics  of these kind of measures, the 
conditions that could eventually lead the Authorities to their 
implementations should be clearly  defined. In particular, the 
assessment on the basis of which the measures are required should be 
based on quantitative and objective valuation.  
Concluding remarks 
Generally, we  support the European Commission’s attempt to define a 
Recovery and Resolution scheme for the insurance sector that, although being 
structured on the banking experience, recognizes the fundamental differences 
with respect to the insurance sector and, hence, provides for the introduction 
of necessary amendments. 
 
 
  
7 
 
However, even though we mostly agree with the adopted approach (i.e.: pre-
emptive measures to rapidly handle possible crisis, as well as the introduction 
of a new Authority in charge of managing the resolution process), we see the 
need to amend the following:  
a)  the mechanical implementation of the remedial actions set out in the 
pre-emptive Recovery plan, once the defined triggers are met.  In our 
opinion, it is essential to provide a set of unified procedures to deal 
with possible crises, captured by the definition of the plan, in order to 
avoid disorganized failure  of undertakings. That being said , the 
remedial actions defined in the recovery plan should not elide tout-
court the fruitful process of dialogue between the Authorities and the 
undertakings, and provide an adequate degree of flexibility to preserve 
the possibility for the management to monitor and govern episodes of 
financial crisis. For this reason, and given the specific characteristics of 
the industry, where evidence shows that insurers very rarely fail rapidly 
in a disorderly manner , the proposal should be emended in order  to 
foresee adequate flexibility of intervention; 
b) the assessment of insurance resolvability, which should be based on 
clear and measurable quantitative parameters . Unipol Group believes 
that it is necessary to unambiguously define the conditions that would 
determine the resolvability of an undertaking. In particular, given the 
set of possible remedial actions that could be implemented in case the 
insurance is deemed as not resolvable,  such conditions should be 
known in full and ex-ante by the undertakings.

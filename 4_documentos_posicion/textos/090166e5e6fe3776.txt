 
 
Federation of Norwegian Industries: Comments to the Commission 
proposal that revises the regulation on shipments of waste and 
amending Regulations (EU) No 1257/2013 and (EU) No 2020/1056 
 
About the Federation of Norwegian Industries 
The Federation of Norwegian Industries represents industry branches such as oil and gas 
contractors, onshore petroleum activities, aluminium, biotechnology, cement, chemical 
industries, electro and energy equipment, furniture, glass and ceramics, machine and hardware 
industry, maritime industry, aquaculture and aquaculture suppliers, metals, mining, paints and 
coatings, graphic arts and communication, paper and pulp, pharmaceuticals, plastics, recycling, 
facility services and textiles, etc. We represent 3.000 companies with approx. 130.000 employees. 
 
Multilateral cooperation 
Article 30 in the proposal for a new regulation concerns border area agreements and the 
possibility for MS to conclude bilateral agreements to make the notification procedure for 
shipments of specific waste flows less stringent. We believe that this possibility should be 
expanded to multilateral agreements between EU/EEA countries in same geographical vicinity.  
 
The Commission proposal (article 30) only opens for border area agreements in exemptional cases 
where the specific geographical or demographical situation warrants such a step. We see the 
Commission proposal as too restrictive, as it does not address the need for cooperation on waste 
treatment between Member States within the EU/EEA. Not to create loopholes, but in order to 
reduce unnecessary administrative burdens and allow for efficient and sound waste treatment. 
 
There are many areas within the EEA/EU where it is not feasible for individual countries to have 
national capacity for all waste fractions, neither financially, technologically nor environmentally. 
Efficient and sound waste treatment depends on cooperation across borders. In many cases, it 
makes more sense that Member States can benefit from specialized existing facilities in one 
country instead of building new capacity for the same specific waste flows. Sufficient volume of a 
waste flow is also necessary to invest in specialized treatment facilities, and in certain cases this 
calls for access to cross-border markets. For instance, it should be avoided that all countries must 
invest in disposal facilities for "peak amounts" of every specific type of hazardous waste that can't 
be recycled. Possibilities for smooth transitions between neighbouring countries will contribute to 
more robust waste treatment in cases of emergency (i.e. lockdowns) in one country.  
 
• Article 30 in the revised regulation must open for multilateral agreements between 
neighbouring countries, where such agreements demonstrate that the waste covered will be 
treated in accordance with the waste hierarchy, the principles of proximity and self-sufficiency, 
as well as legally binding EU environmental protection standards (i.e. BAT-requirements, etc.). 
 
Shipments of waste to laboratory testing 
The Commission proposes that shipments of waste explicitly destined for laboratory analysis or 
experimental treatment trials to assess either its physical or chemical characteristics or to 
determine its suitability for recovery or disposal can be subject only to the general information 
requirements (art. 4, no. 4). The conditions for this are that the amount of waste does not exceed 
the quantity reasonably needed to perform the analysis and does not exceed 150 kg or any higher 
amount agreed on a case-by-case basis by the competent authorities concerned and the notifier.

The current regulation only allows for shipments of waste to laboratory analysis up to 25 kg for 
waste shipments subject to the general information requirements. Hence, the new limit of 150 kg 
(or more on a case-by-case basis) is an improvement and an important step to enhance analysis or 
experiments leading to recovery of waste which is destined for disposal as of today. However, we 
firmly believe that the weight limit should be further increased, as waste volumes of 150 kg, in 
many cases, will not be sufficient to carry out pilot testing to determine suitability for recovery.  
 
• We would welcome an increase of this weight limit to 5 tons, for shipments of waste bound for 
laboratory analysis or experimental treatment trials, aiming to increase the recovery rate. 
 
Electronic notification 
The Commission proposes that electronic notification shall be mandatory. The Commission will 
set up a central database for electronic notification within the EU/EEA. Member States electronic 
systems can be used but must be interoperable with the central system. All documents shall be 
made electronically (notifications, movement documentation, consent, pre-consented facility 
information, certificates of recovery or disposal, prior information of actual start of shipment).  
 
• We strongly support a mandatory electronic notification system at EU/EEA level. An electronic 
system will facilitate intra EU-shipments and increase the traceability of waste streams. 
 
Signing of notification, where the notifier is not the original waste producer 
Article 5 (2) in the proposal for a revised regulation requires that a notification is signed by the 
original waste producer or, in cases there the notifier is not the original waste producer, by eith er 
(a) the new waste producer who carries out operations prior to shipment or (b) a collector who, 
from various small quantities of the same type of waste collected from a variety of sources, has 
assembled the shipment which is to start from a single notif ied location. Today, it is normal that 
dealers or brokers act ing on behalf of waste producers or waste collectors may sign notification 
documents. It is a requirement that a dealer or broker must be authorised in writing by the 
original waste producer, new waste producer or (licenced) waste collector to act on his/her behalf 
as notifier. We strongly advise to keep the  possibility for dealers or brokers acting on behalf of 
waste producers to sign notification documents, to avoid massive and unnecessary bureaucracy. 
 
• The last paragraph in the Regulation Article 5 (2) should be modified in the following way so 
that dealers or brokers acting on behalf of waste producers may sign notification documents:  
o When the notifier is not the original waste producer referred to in Article 3, point 
(6)(a)(i), the notifier shall ensure that the original waste producer or one of the 
persons indicated in Article 3, points (6)(a)(ii) or (iii) or (iv), also signs the notification 
document. A dealer or a broker shall be authorised in writing by the original waste 
producer, new waste producer or waste collector to act on his/her behalf as notifier. 
 
Harmonization of practice 
Compliance with the current waste shipment regulation suffers from different interpretations and 
practice in Member States. For instance, different competent authorities classify the same types 
of waste differently, which create distortions within European waste markets. There is a need for 
a more harmonised practise to the regulation amongst competent authorises. In the revised 
regulation, it is proposed that, in order to ensure uniform conditions, the Commission is 
empowered to adopt delegated acts to establish criteria on contamination thresholds for certain 
wastes and to establishing criteria to distinguish between used goods and waste. The Federation 
of Norwegian Industries welcomes clear uniform conditions on the classification of waste and 
believes that this will lead to a more common implementation and participation of the regulation.

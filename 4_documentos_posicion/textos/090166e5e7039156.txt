VCI POSITION  
Proposal of the EU-Commission 
for a new regulation on waste shipments 
 
 
 
General comments 
 
With the proposal for new rules on the shipment of waste, published on November 17th, the 
EU-Commission purses three main goals, which in principle are supported by VCI: 
 Shipments of waste for preparation for reuse and recycling in the EU are facilitated 
 The EU does not export its waste challenges to third countries 
 Illegal shipments of waste are more effectively addressed 
 
Especially the measures to consequently simplify and accelerate all procedures related to 
transboundary waste shipments are of fundamental importance. The planned system for 
the legally binding digital exchange of information and documents must be implemented 
as quickly as possible. This would not only facilitate an efficient flow of material, which is a 
prerequisite for the envisioned effective intra-EU market for secondary raw materials, but it 
would also allow for an improved control of waste shipments by the relevant authorities. In 
addition, an improved harmonization of the relevant rules and a uniform and consistent 
implementation by the relevant authorities should be ensured. 
 
VCI supports adequate measures to ensure that the waste challenges of the EU are not ex-
ported to third countries. However, the restrictions suggested by the Commission on im-
ports and exports of wastes must not lead to a collapse of the global trade of waste in-
tended for recovery which is imperative for an efficient circular economy. Complicated and 
tedious approval processes must be avoided. 
 
The new requirements on companies exporting waste to proof that facilities to which waste 
is exported are managed in an environmentally sound manner, must be proportionate and 
practical. The planned restrictions on intra-EU shipments of waste intended for energy re-
covery are considered critically by VCI, especially concerning the incineration of hazardous 
waste. 
 
The better control of illegal waste shipment is vehemently supported by VCI.

Comments on the individual articles: 
 
Art. 4 − Overall procedural framework 
 
It is welcomed that up to 150 kg of hazardous waste (larger quantities with consent of the 
competent authorities concerned) can be the shipped without prior notification if intended 
for analysis or experimental treatment trials. This is an important prerequisite for the de-
velopment of novel recycling technologies. However, 150 kg often will not suffice for pilot 
trials. In this context, a further increase of the amount that can be shipped without specific 
consent by the authorities to 1 ton would be desirable. 
 
 
Art. 5 - Notification 
 
The first sentence of Article 5 No. 1 contradicts the definition of "notifier" in Article 3 No. 6 
of the Waste Shipment Regulation and should be deleted. Otherwise, it would not be possi-
ble for an industrial waste producer to initiate a notification. 
 
 
Art. 7 − Financial guarantee or equivalent insurance 
 
Unfortunately, it is planned that the harmonized calculation method is established by an 
implementing act by the Commission at a later date. A short-term implementation would 
highly desirable. 
 
 
Art. 9 − Consents by the competent authorities and time periods for transport, recov-
ery or disposal 
 
According to our experience the time limits for the processing of submitted notifications is 
not observed by the competent authorities in various countries. This results in unneces-
sary delays. According to Art. 9 tacit consent of the competent authorities in the countries 
of dispatch and the countries of transit can be assumed if no objections are lodged within 
the 30 day time limit. It remains unclear how the notifier, if necessary, can proof with legal 
certainty that no objections were lodged by the authorities (especially in the county of dis-
patch). 
 
 
Art. 11 − Prohibition of shipments of waste destined for disposal 
 
An intra-EU transboundary shipment of waste destined for disposal must remain possible. 
A thermal disposal/recovery of (especially hazardous) wastes in facilities located in another 
country must still be allowed.

Art. 14 − Pre-consented recovery facilities 
 
The plans to introduce a fast-track procedure for the approval of shipments destined for a 
recovery in a pre-consented facilitiy are welcomed. 
 
However, it remains unclear, in which „duly justified cases" the competent authorities 
might nevertheless shorten the period of validity to less than three years. This should be 
limited to absolute exceptions. 
 
 
Art. 16 − Requirements following consent to a shipment 
 
According to paragraph 2 the so far applicable period of full three working days for the 
prior information about a planned shipment is abandoned. A complete abandonment of 
the prior information would be helpful, as it causes unnecessary administrative burden. 
 
 
Art. 21 − Public access to notifications 
 
It must be ensured that no confidential trade secrets become public due to this require-
ment. 
 
 
Art. 26 − Electronic submission and exchange of information 
 
The introduction of a system for the digital exchange of information is of utmost im-
portance and should be pushed forward with highest priority. Unfortunately, a lot of issues 
remain unsolved in this context. Despite considerable efforts the member states were una-
ble to agree on a common system. Instead, according to the proposal, the Commission 
should operate a central system as hub to allow the digital exchange of information and 
documents. Member states are permitted to operate their own national systems but must 
ensure that based on the requirements determined by the Commission in an implementing 
act at a later date, an exchange of information with the central hub and the respective na-
tional systems is possible in real time. There are severe concerns that the establishment of 
the planned systems will take much longer than reasonably necessary. 
 
Until such a system is implemented and running, binding transitional rules should be intro-
duced to allow the digital submission of movement documents (prior information, confir-
mations of receipt or recovery) as pdf-file as email attachment without requiring a quali-
fied electronic signature (In analogy to the recommendation by the Commission on the 
handling of waste shipments in the corona-crisis).

Art. 27 − Language 
 
In general documents in English should be accepted by all competent authorities. Only in 
duly justified cases the authorities shall be granted the right to refuse to accept a docu-
ment in English and insist on a translation in the corresponding national language. 
 
 
Art. 28 − Disagreement on classification issues 
 
Whether a material has to be considered as waste or not should be determined by the rules 
and regulations of the Waste Framework Directive and not by the provisions of Waste Ship-
ment Regulation. 
 
 
Art. 43 − Obligations on exporters (Proof of an environmentally sound management) 
 
It remains completely unclear how this provision can be implemented in a practical man-
ner. 
 
According to paragraph 4 periodic audits of the disposal facilities are required every three 
years. An assessment every five years should be sufficient. 
 
Paragraph 5 stipulates that the results of the audits must be made available to other per-
sons intending to export waste to the facility in question, under fair commercial conditions. 
It remains completely unclear how this should work in practice. A potential solution could 
be a central EU-register of all disposal facilities that have been audited. 
 
Paragraph 8 allows the EU to enter agreements with OECD-member states that recognize 
that waste is treated in an environmentally sound manner in facilities located in these 
states, and that companies intending to export waste to that third country shall be ex-
empted from the obligation to audit these facilities. For a large number of countries, it can 
be reasonably assumed that waste is generally treated in an environmentally sound man-
ner. With all of these countries the EU should enter such agreements unconditionally (Swit-
zerland, Great Britain, United States).  
 
Annex X explains the conditions, to be fulfilled by foreign facilities. It is unclear how para-
graph 2 must be interpreted. Is it necessary to prove the unconditional and complete ob-
servance of EU standards (BREF)? This would in most cases de facto be equivalent to an Ex-
port ban, which according to the proposal was explicitly not intended or desired.

Annex II Part 3: Additional information and documentation that may be requested by 
the competent authorities: 
 
The requirements concerning the nature and extent of the listed documents differs signifi-
cantly between competent authorities. Not only between the authorities of different mem-
ber states, but also between the various competent authorities within a single member 
state (E.g. concerning transportation permits and insurance certificates). In this respect 
more specific guidelines or templates could be very helpful.

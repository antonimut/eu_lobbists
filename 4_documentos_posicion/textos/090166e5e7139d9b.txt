Eramet contribution to the next European Waste Shipment regulation 
January 14th, 2022 
 
Eramet, a global French mining group , is a key player in the extraction and transformation of metals. 
Eramet supports the transition to a carbon neutral economy  by developing activities strategic for 
Europe to sustain its leadership and autonomy in this transition, including nickel, cobalt, lithium mining 
extraction, refining, and recycling around the world. 
Eramet welcomes and supports the E uropean Commission  proposal for a n ambitious new Waste 
Shipment regulation  – an occasion to update waste shipment mechanisms for intra and extra EU 
transfers as well as for banning illegal shipments. 
As a preliminary remark, Eramet emphasizes the importance of an updated and strong Waste Shipment 
Regulation to support the development of waste recycling industries in Europe, especially in the Li -ion 
batteries field.  
 
 
 
INTRA EU TRANSFERS: SIMPLIFICATION OF PROCEDURES IS KEY  
Eramet supports the ambition to simplify waste transfer authorisation s and follow up procedures 
within the European territory.  
 Eramet suggests that  processes should be eased especially as for waste reclamation 
procedures. 
- Europe must for instance include industrials and actual waste market players in the development 
and the test of the Electronic Data Interchange (EDI) to make it as much effective and workable 
as possible (art 26). 
 
 Eramet is in favour of a single market for waste in Europe , harmonized and unified over all 
European countries, as it is for other existing markets. 
 
- Eramet regrets that the  logic of the Basel Convention  (green, orange, and red lists as well as 
having to detain prior administrative authorisation system for the orange list) regarding product 
traceability is not being questioned within the European Community.

- Eramet argues that  only traceability should be retained via Annex VII  (strict equivalent of the 
French BSDD1) of the original regulation on shipment of waste (No 1013/2006). 
- In France, regional environmental authorities (DREAL) have a posteriori control logic once the 
waste transfer is complete, with BSDD 2 traceability. This system could be extended to  the 
European level, with the obligation of an annual review, with “annex VII”3. 
 
 
THIS UPDATED REGULATION SHOULD SUPPORT THE DEVELOPMENT OF A STRONG METAL RECYCLING 
INDUSTRY IN EUROPE  
 Eramet would like  this review to be the opportunity to support the development of a strong 
and ambitious critical raw materials recycling industry in Europe. 
 
- This would allow the EU to get out of the dilemma of either getting rid of waste and importing 
primary mined critical metals OR exporting wastes for recovering metals abroad to re-import 
them later, only if and when possible. 
 
- Eramet points out that article 49 could jeopardize the development of this recycling industry. 
This article provides for a ban on all imports into Europe of waste for recovery, except from 
countries to which the OECD decision applies, from other countries that are parties to the Basel 
Convention or from other countries with which all or part of the EU has concluded an agreement 
with. 
 
 Eramet recalls that  one of the biggest challenges in the recycling industry for the upcoming 
years concerns the lithium-ion batteries that are used in electrical vehicles. 
 
- The European battery industry is now a reality, but the European Union has not yet developed a 
battery recycling industry  aligned with the expe ctations of Europe  (in terms of volumes, 
efficiency and sustainability), while having however the skills, innovation capacity and relevant 
players.  
 
- The emergence of an EU recycling sector is conditional to a better control of waste flows abroad.

 Eramet claims that the waste shipment regulation review should implement a real export ban 
of end-of-life Li-ion batteries – and related production scraps -, at least for the period of time 
required to develop this industry in a sustainable way in Europe. This logic can be extended to 
other wastes as we have numerous industries in Europe importing critical raw materials while 
Europe has the capacity to develop on its territories “secondary mines” raw materials 
production.  
 
- When a real export ban can not be implemented , putting in place equivalent conditions of 
recycling for industrials inside and outside the EU remains mandatory. Rules to secure the level 
playing field for European recyclers and enforce that waste s sent outside the EU are recycled in 
the same way as it is within the European territory, should be negotiated, defined and enforced 
as soon as possible.   
 
 For very specific wastes, for which no recycling industry is , or will be in a foreseeable future,  
available within Europe and in case no critical raw materials can be sourced out of these wastes, 
prevailing regulations should continue to ease the  removal of a “waste status” attached to a 
product that can be recycled outside Europe (than can be shipped as a product and not a waste) 
rather than to be landfilled in Europe with no economical neither environmental benefit.  
 
 
 
Eramet will take part in future discussions on this regulation  and will await secondary legislation 
considering the  current lack of details and technical information  on various issues: exchange of 
electronic data, list of countries, method of calculating financial guarantees, etc.

NRK Recycling 
 
Contribution of NRK Recycling about the consultation 
“Waste transport – evaluation and documentation of EU rules”. 
 
NRK Recycling represents 24 Dutch recycling companies that are involved in the 
collection, sorting, and processing of plastic waste. 
 
General: 
Recycling of plastic waste streams is crucial to achieve a circular economy. 
NRK Recycling supports the European Green Deal. In the March 2020 new Circular 
Economy Action Plan (com 2020 98 final), measures have been taken to ensure that 
shipments of waste for reuse and recycling within the EU are reflected. 
NRK recycling also endorses the need to tackle illegal shipments of waste. 
 
Export of collected and sorted plastic waste is of great importance to recycling 
companies. 
Recycling companies specialize in selected plastic flows. Not all plastic waste streams 
can be recycled within the recycling company. That is why the export of these flows 
remains important. Export can take place within Europe as well as outside Europe. 
It is logical that additional requirements are set for outside Europe with regard to the 
management and processing of waste flows. The obligations that are executable flows 
for the executable flows as well as for the notifier. 
 
Export ensures healthy market functioning and a balance between supply and demand. 
In addition, a lot of production still takes place in EU countries. It is logical that the 
waste is transported there to be used as a raw material. 
 
The proposal for the Regulation of the Regulation on cross-border transport of rules 
compared to the current Regulation. Specific mention is made of the digital exchange 
of documents and the shortened registration period for transports. 
 
End of waste status 
NRK Recycling favours the establishment of End-of-Waste Criteria at the European 
level for various types of waste streams. If that is not possible, NRK Recycling 
advocates mutual recognition of national end-of-waste criteria. In doing so, the EU 
supports the need to achieve a fully-fledged market for recyclate. Recyclate as a raw 
material also yields more financially than recyclate with the status of waste.

Specifically: 
Notification 
Now that far fewer waste streams will come under the Green List waste, the procedure 
of prior written notification will become more common. This applies to both exports 
within the EU and exports outside the EU. 
 
The members of NRK Recycling notice that the countries, both within the EU and 
outside the EU, have far too little capacity and knowledge to process all these 
notifications. This leads to long waiting times. We ask the EU, together with the 
Member States, to pay attention to this. 
Where necessary, the EU should assist countries outside the EU in transferring 
knowledge and training staff, to help the recipient countries in dealing with 
notifications.  
 
General information obligation 
A new requirement is that the recovery facility: 
1. Confirm to the notifier and the authorities concerned that the waste has been 
received within one day of receipt of the waste; 
2. declares as soon as possible that the recovery has been completed. 
 
With regard to Article 18 paragraph 4. It is completely unclear which authorities are 
meant by “authorities concerned”. Article 1 does not contain a definition of 
“concerned authorities”. However, from “competent authorities”. Please clarify. The 
receiving institution cannot be expected to know all the “authorities concerned” and 
certainly not to have all the addresses of them. 
 
The deadline for confirming the receipt of one day is far too short. The waste often 
arrives in containers. These are only opened later, namely when the load is processed 
within the device. 
 
Moreover, it is highly questionable whether the receiving facility does have the Annex 
VII document. 
After all, the document is a transport document. Certainly in countries outside the EU, 
this document does not have to be present with the load. 
 
According to the literal text of the Regulation, the Document is only intended as 
information accompanying the shipment of waste. The document is now used as a 
notification document. 
 
There is no legal basis for the receiving facility to make mandatory use of Annex VII to 
confirm receipt of the waste and to declare that recovery has been completed. 
Certainly when it comes to receiving establishments located in countries outside the 
EU.

External control of the receiving facility outside the EU 
NRK Recycling is positive about the obligation referred to in Article 43 to subject 
facilities outside the EU that manage waste to an inspection by an independent and 
accredited third party. 
 
When it comes to the prior inspection of establishments pursuant to art 43, it is crucial 
to clarify at EU-level what the certification must comply with. Many exporters are 
accustomed to certification according to, for example, the German Verpackunhsgesetz 
und Verpackungs Vorordnung, if this can be used as a starting point, that would be 
very nice. Extensions (e.g. recycling percentages per facility/material flow) can 
certainly be implemented, but this must be clear in advance. 
 
However, it is a pity that no European database is established where the approved 
establishments outside the EU are registered. Then exporting companies can make use 
of the certification for a fee. This could significantly reduce the costs of accreditation. 
This is also much more practical than the arrangement proposed in Article 43(5).

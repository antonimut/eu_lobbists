POLYECO S.A. COMMENTS ON: 
Proposal for a REGULATION OF THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND OF THE COUNCIL on the 
shipments of waste and amending Regulations (EU) No 1257/2013 and (EU) No 2020/1056. 
[2021/0367 (COD)] 
 
Article 4, par. 4 (b): even though the amount of waste destined for laboratory analysis or 
experimental treatment trials, has increased from 25 kg to 150 kg, request is that it is further 
increased to 1.000 kg/lt, to be able to actually perform an experimental trial. 
 
Article 5, par. 8: “Only one identification code shall be covered for each notification”. 
Comment: Must be clear that this is Basel Code, and at the same time more than one EWC 
code may be accepted in the same notification. 
 
Article 8, par. 2: “The notifier shall provide the information and documentation referred to 
in paragraph 1 within seven days after request by the competent authority of dispatch”. 
Comment: No deadline in previous version. Very strict deadline, based on the fact that 
sometimes translations of large documents are requested.  
We must have in mind that the Notifier is the most interested party in getting the license 
finalized the soonest. 
Our proposal is not to have this deadline, or at least: “…within seven working days, unless 
more time is requested from notifier…” 
 
Article 8, par.5: “The notifier shall provide the information and documentation referred to in 
paragraph 4 within seven days after request by the competent authority concerned”. 
Comment: No deadline in previous version. Very strict deadline, based on the fact that 
sometimes translations of large documents are requested.  
We must have in mind that the Notifier is the most interested party in getting the license 
finalized the soonest. 
Our proposal is not to have this deadline, or at least: “…within seven working days, unless 
more time is requested from notifier…” 
 
Article 9, par.1: Tacit consent by the competent authorities of dispatch [NEW] and transit 
may be assumed if no objection is lodged within the 30-day time limit referred to in the first 
subparagraph.

Comment: The proposal does not differentiate between countries of simple transit and 
transit countries where container transshipment takes place, throughout the respective 
Ports. 
In our opinion, we believe that the licensing procedure would be simplified if written 
consent was given only by the transit countries where transshipment of containers takes 
place at their ports. 
When the waste containers are not unloaded from vessel (which means there is no 
transhipment of the waste), then a written consent is not requested. 
 
Article 11, par. 3: “Where the competent authorities concerned have not authorized a 
planned shipment of waste destined for disposal within the 30 day time limit referred to in 
Article 9(1), the notification of that shipment shall cease to be valid and the shipment shall 
be prohibited …”. 
Comment: very strict measure for notifiers since some competent authorities, due to 
heavy workload, cannot manage to keep the 30-day deadline. This must be omitted, or at 
least be increased to a 90-day deadline, since shipments destined for disposal, within the 
EU, cannot be ceased especially from Greece, who does not have any disposal facility. 
Waste export for disposal (i.e., high temperature incineration, landfilling, etc.) from 
Greece to EU countries is a must to keep and restore the sustainability of the country’s 
waste management. 
 
Article 13, par. 1(a): “… similar physical and chemical characteristics...”. 
Comment: it must be clear that physical characteristics does not include the separation of 
solid and liquid waste contaminated with the same pollutant i.e., in cases of washings. 
Such waste has the same hazardous components and should be included in the same 
notification document, as most competent authorities do accept. 
Also, it must be clarified in this article, that “similar physical and chemical characteristics” 
refers to each separate shipment of waste and not to all shipments included in the same 
notification.  
 
Article 13, par.2: “Where, the routing modification is known before the shipments start and 
involves competent authorities other than those concerned by the general notification, the 
general notification may not be used, and a new notification shall be submitted in 
accordance with Article 5”. 
Comment:  
 
3 
 
Routing issue is very common, and especially shipments with containerships involve 
several alterations.  
Proposal is that alternative routing should and may be provided in the notification file, to 
cover possible alterations. 
Another proposal is that in case of routing modification a new notification shall be 
submitted, unless all the competent authorities concerned (new and old) indicate, that the 
proposed changes do not require a new notification. 
 
Article 13.  
Comment: Proposal is that paragraph 4 to be added, as following: 
In case a renewal of identical notification is to be given, procedure will be considered as 
“fast track”. 
In such case, renewed notification file including only renewed dates, amount, and 
shipments to be submitted, from the same or even different producers of waste, and 
competent authorities to provide consent within 15 days.  
 
Article 14. Pre-consented recovery facilities. 
According to this Article, a recovery facility may submit a request to be listed as a pre-
consented recovery facility.  
At the same time, a list of pre-consented “Notifiers” may also improve a great deal the 
bureaucracy for licensing.  
Our proposal is that this as a measure for significant work load reduction. 
 
Article 15, par. 2: ..in accordance with Article 12, [and Article 11 should be added], to the 
shipment(s) of waste… 
 
Article 15, par. 3: Within one day of the receipt of the waste by the facility which carries out 
the interim recovery operation… 
Comment: Was three (3) days. One day is too tight, what happens if it is on a holiday or 
weekend.  
Our proposal is to keep it three (3) days. 
 
Article 17: Changes in the shipment after consent. 
Comment: For flexibility and not to cause further complexity, proposal is that paragraph 3 
is omitted and essential changes to be main alterations of intended quantity and date of 
shipments.

Additionally, an increase of 20% in the shipped quantity to be allowed for every 
notification file. 
For routing, route, carriers, a written consent from all competent authorities concerned 
should be adequate. In case a competent authority does not provide consent (tacit) in the 
primary notification procedure, again no written consent for such changes is required. 
Aim is of course to reduce bureaucracy for all parties involved, notifiers and competent 
authorities. 
 
Article 27, Language. 
Our proposal is that all competent authorities to accept official translations in English 
language. 
 
Article 28, addition of paragraph 5 as following: 
5. In case the countries of dispatch and destination agree on the classification and main 
text of a notification is correct and complete, the transit country competent authorities 
cannot request changes (such as Basel Code, EWC codes, e.tc.) to the notification. 
 
Article 74A. 
In Regulation 1013/2006, Article 57 “Meeting of the Correspondents” there is provision for 
periodical meetings of correspondents to examine the questions raised by the 
implementation of this Regulation. However, there is no provision for this in the new 
proposal. In our opinion, not only should this provision be included in the new Regulation, 
but an additional provision should be included to make the Guidelines legally binding for 
Member States in the same sense as the Regulation. Up to now, the issued 
Correspondent’s Guidelines, although they represent the common understanding of all 
Member States on how Regulation (EC) No 1013/2006 on shipments of waste should be 
interpreted, are ultimately non-binding. This means that a competent authority of a 
Member State can choose not to follow the guidelines, which in turn, causes discrepancies 
in the implementation of the Regulation.

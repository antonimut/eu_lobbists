    Waste Shipments Regulation revision  
     European Aluminium’s First Reactions to the Commission’s Proposal 
     January 2022   
 
 
Introduction  
The European aluminium industry welcomes the Commission’s proposal 1 for the revision of the Waste Shipments 
Regulation and considers it an important first step towards the modernisation of the EU’s waste legislation , which is 
pivotal in the transition to a resource-efficient, low-carbon and overall sustainable circular economy.  
Aluminium is a circular material, capable o f being recycled over  and over again without losing its original properties 
(lightness, conductivity, formability, durability, permeability and multiple recyclability) and as such  plays a leading role as 
a climate and resource frontrunner, contributing to the objectives set under the European Green Deal and the 2020 Circular 
Economy Action Plan.  
In our Circular Aluminium Action Plan2 we highlight our vision in which the amount of aluminium available for recycling will 
more than double by 2050, aiding to avoid up to 39 million tonnes of CO2 emissions per year. Boosting the recycling of 
aluminium in Europe could reduce the volume of carbon-intensive aluminium imports and would reduce Europe’s import 
dependence while generating €6 billion per year for the European economy.  
The revision of the Waste Shipments Regulation c an serve as an enabling policy signa l, supporting our global 
competitiveness by reducing the administ rative burden and  adding flexibility and efficiency in INTRA-EU shipments and 
movements of waste, while at the same time establishing a level playing field by defining the EU-equivalent conditions for 
EXTRA-EU movements of waste. Addressing the issue of illegal shipments is important in ensuring that end-of-life products 
can be sustainably recycled in Europe and valuable materials can be reintegrated into our production loops.  
First reactions and policy recommendations  
European Aluminium would like to share our first reactions to the Commission’s proposal and bring forward our 
recommendations in the continuous effort of 1) improving the overall harmonisation and increased efficiency of INTRA-EU 
transports; and 2) to concretise the ambition regarding the necessary strict controls on EXTRA-EU exports and the required 
proof about the EU-equivalent conditions in destination countries.  
1. General provisions & Intra-EU shipments  
We welcome t he Commission’s  intentions to simp lify administration,  improve the overall efficiency and improve 
alignment and harmonisation between the Member States. To that extent:  
• The proposed EU-wide electronic data interchange (EDI)  is a welcoming and needed development (Art. 26). Its 
inception and design phase are key to ensure  a smooth operation system. There are several issues already 
encountered at Member State  level regarding similar systems, which could be used as learnings . Therefore, we 
suggest that all relevant stakeholder s and the developer of the European system should collaborate closely 
throughout the development stage and at leas t during the early implementation period of the EDI. Regarding 
information requirements, the obligation to get the confirmation of receiving a shipment after 1 day is too short. 
For example, in cases when a shipment is rejected by a recovery facility and it is not possible to return the material

In addition, we invite all relevant stakeholders to consider certain aspects which will serve to improve the harmonisation 
and coherency of the WSR:  
• The Green List is undoubtedly one of the strongest tools at the disposal of the EU to pr omote and ensure 
harmonisation. The addition of new Green List codes will ensure harmonisation across Member States and 
decrease unnecessary administrative burden. To that extent, such a development will have a higher and faster 
impact in resolving potential disagreements on the classification of waste, compared to the Commission’s proposal 
(Art. 28) to harmonise classification of waste via the adoption of delegated acts . In this regard , we propose the 
inclusion of a new entry in Annex IIIB of the WSR to in clude windows, doors, curtain walls, framing profiles, and 
other aluminium elements also when coated, consisting of mainly aluminium and plastic, rubber o r foam3. For 
tangible examples of barriers and missed opportunities to increase circularity please refer to ANNEX I.

coherency with EU funding mechanisms (e.g. Horizon Europe). The Commission’s proposal in Art.  4 to increase 
the waste for laboratory analysis or experimental treatment to 150 kg and to allow for a higher amount on a case-
by-case basis, is a positive step towards the right direction but the overall framework could provide for a higher

2. Extra-EU shipments  
We welcome the positive step forward in developing a stronger regime for Extra-EU shipments and the intention to define 
EU-equivalent criteria for exported waste, which is key to achieving a global level-playing field. We believe it is due time to 
aim for a higher level of ambition . Art. 56 on the ‘Environmentally sound management’ refers to “requirements that are 
broadly equivalent to the human health and environmental protection requirements laid down in Union legislation ”. We 
propose the requirement to be as closely as possibly aligned with Union legislation, making the word ‘broadly’ effectively 
redundant and no longer needed in the text . In addition to ESM, the requirements should take into account social and 
labour conditions and standards.  
3. Illegal shipments  
Addressing illegal shipments and stepping up the effort on enforcement is a positive development, which could safeguard 
the environment and minimise the loss of valuable end-of-life (EoL) materials. For example, such a development could allow 
for more end-of-life vehicles (ELV) to enter the European recycling system, instead of being illegally exported and recycled 
in the country of destination, without any assurance on the compliance with equivalent human health and environmental 
protection requirements. As it stands today, despite the ELV Directive  and the current guidelines No 9 (on shipment of 
waste vehicles) to the Waste Shipment Regulation, which defines non-binding criteria for the differentiation between used 
vehicles and ELVs, a lot of ELVs are exported outside the EU and illegally treated there.   
To that exten t, the Commission’s proposal  to “differentiate between used goods and waste ( Art. 28)”, an important 
consideration for the EU Circular Economy, could also serve to clarify and differentiate between used vehicles and ELVs.

Examples of lack of harmonisation and barriers to circularity  
❖ Case study 1: The shipment of waste from aluminium window profiles  
 
Aluminium window scrap crimped with plastic profiles (acting as thermal break) shipped from France to Austria through 
Germany. The producer classified the waste as aluminium scrap (code B1010), using the general information procedure. 
But German authorities regarded it as a mixture of aluminium scrap and plastics. Consequently, the waste should have 
been shipped following the prior notification and consent procedure.  
 
❖ Case study 2: Aluminium wrought scrap consisting of aluminium and plastic/rubber attachments 
 
German supplier delivery to Poland. A shipment of aluminium wrought scrap consisting of aluminium and plastic/rubber 
attachments was stopped and returned by the Polish customs at the border. The shipment followed the general 
information requirement for waste recovery under Art. 3(2) (b) of the Regulation 1013/2006 EC as mixture not classified 
under one single entry in Annex III of two or more waste listed in Annex III. 
 
Such mixtures are listed in Annex IIIA in accordance with Art . 58 of Regulation 1013/2006 EC and its (mixture) 
composition does not impair the environmentally sound recovery since it is processed on a shredder line equipped with 
eddy-current enabling efficient separation of non -metallic particles as plastic, rubber or foam from aluminium scrap. 
After processing the separated plastic, rubber or foam c an be delivered to companies which produce alternative fuel. 
The shipment satisfied the VDM norm of 2% non-metallic foreign substances. 
 
In this case the mixture is consisting of aluminium code B1010 (170402 EWC) and plastic B3010. The Polish customs 
justified their decision because of the plastic content.

Proposed Solution  
A new entry in Annex IIIB of the Waste Shipment Regulation (EC) 1013/2006. This change will be later amended in the 
relevant annexes of the Basel Convention or the OECD decision: “windows, doors, curtain walls, framing profiles, and 
other aluminium elements also when coated, consisting of mainly aluminium and plastic, rubber or foam”

ANNEX II  
Supporting Innovation: The case of EU -funded projects  
❖ The RemovAL project 4 aims at  combining, optimiz ing and scal ing-up developed processing technologies for 
extracting base and critical metals from such industrial residues and valorising the remaining processing residues 
in the construction sector.  The projects’ ambition is to overcome  environmental issues and technological 
barriers related to aluminium industry, by combining and advancing existing technologies for the sustainable 
processing of Bauxite Residues(BR), Spent Pot lining (SPL) and other by-products, generating revenue. 
❖ The ReActiv5  aims at creating a novel sustainable symbiotic value chain, linking the by -product of the alumina 
production industry and the cement production industry. The proposed ReActiv technologies will modify the 
properties of Bauxite Residue, transformin g into a reactive SCM material suitable for new, low CO2 footprint, 
cement products. In this manner ReActiv proposes a win-win scenario for both industrial sectors reducing wastes 
and CO2 emissions respectively.  
Examples of lack of harmonisation and barri ers to circularity  
With reference to the above projects, an aluminium producer attempted to ship 2 tonnes of bauxite residue from one 
Member State to another as part of a European research project funded by the E uropean Commission. The bauxite 
residue was to be tested in a small pilot scale for use in ferro-silicon production. The preparation of the shipment took 
more than 6 months and involved email exchanges between the two Member States’ environmental ministries causing 
unnecessary delays and administrative burden.  
Proposed Solution  
A flexible and straightforward procedure to ship waste which will be exempted from the  notification procedure, for 
amounts which can facilitate large scale innovation projects is needed, keeping in mind that one container which is often 
required for representative tests could be 15 tonnes. In addition, ensuring coherency and complementarity between 
the different EU policy areas will ensure that administrative barriers will be eliminated, and the ambitions of the EU 
Green Deal will be faster achieved.

ANNEX III  
Examples of inefficient harmonization:  
❖ Some competent authorities (COs) are asking for administrative fees that are hard to verify (from a foreign 
company’s point of view) to be eligible. E.g. you are referred to a web page that might be/might not be official. 
❖ Some COs ask for translated contracts between sender and receiver that should be signed and stamped by 
notary public, when these documents are sent by mail to the COs in the senders country (which should be the 
single point of contact), there is no system for this to be transferred to the relevant competent authority abroad. 
❖ Some COs ask the application owner (the sender) about information about the receiver company, such as official 
documents (in native language) that the facility owner are not in a legal conflict regarding environmental 
regulations.  
❖ There is not standard for what a permit for transport of hazardous waste shall contain. Still COs ask to get 
confirmation that a transporter are approved for the whole trip. 
❖ Their current system doesn’t consider transhipping/reloading of goods at port facilities or similar.  
❖ COs ask for more details on renewal of permits, meaning that they request information that was not requested 
in the first application (one year before). 
❖ Information requested by mail (e.g. scanned documents) are too big for the COs mail system to receive it.  
❖ Some information is sent by post and some by mail from the different authorities. We have experienced several 
times that post is lost in transport and are not sent to the correct contact person. 
❖ Some COs have public registers of approved transporters of hazar dous waste. This might not be good enough 
information or have the correct language for other COs. 
❖ Different COs have given permits with different validity period due to very long processing time.  
❖ Not all countries ask for permits to transport hazardous waste. Some COs ask also for insurance documents for 
the transport companies, other do not. 
❖ Different Member States classify the same waste as hazardous and non-hazardous.  
❖ Some COs approves applications where the same type of waste is planned sent from different waste generators, 
some COs don’t. 
❖ Some COs accepts requests for changed transport route (e.g. due to winter closed roads), other COs demands 
that a new applications is sent.

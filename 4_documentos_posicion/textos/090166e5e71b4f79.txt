Subject: Position on the Waste Shipment Regulation 
Plastics Recyclers Europe (PRE), the association representing the plastics recycling 
industry, welcomes the proposal for a revision of the Waste Shipment Regulation 
(WSR) and appreciates the opportunity to provide comments. 
General Comments 
PRE welcomes the extension of the Regulation’s scope to include and align Art. 3 and 
5 of the Waste Framework Directive (WFD)
1  and provide for specific m easures 
according to Art. 4 on the waste hierarchy. PRE supports the implementation of WFD 
Art. 16 in the proposed revision, as setting specific rules for EU Member States (MS) 
and their cross -border waste management systems. Combined, while reflecting the  
reality of the market, these greatly enhance the direction of waste in line with circular 
economy principles and uptake a market for recyclates. 
Specific Comments 
Below are specific initial thoughts on certain provisions in the WSR proposal. 
150 kg threshold for laboratory analysis and experimental treatment 
trials 
We appreciate the language included in the WSR on the possibility to perform 
experimental treatment trials in addition to the currently recognised laboratory 
analysis. We also appreciate that the weight limit for such analysis has been increased 
to 150 kg and that higher amounts may be agreed on a case -by-case basis by the 
competent authorities involved.  
It should however be noted that for certain treatment trials in the field of plastics 
recycling a greater volume of material would be needed to complete the trail run. 
Volumes such as 1 big bag or 1 bale can be needed to perform a trail with experimental 
sorting technology. The weight of such a volume would be roughly 1000 – 1500 kg.  
During the coming legislative procedure,  we would encourage the expansion of the 
volume allowed for plastic waste shipments to such a weight range.

Requirement for the Notifier to be in possession of a waste permit 
Article 6.1 specifies that only those facilities that have a permit or are registered as a 
waste treatment facility may submit a notification. This means that only plastics 
wastes generated by the virgin value chain (polymer producers, masterbatchers and 
compounders, and converters) that is compliant to B3011 and EU3011 may be 
shipped to recyclers across borders. Shipments of other plastics waste for recycling 
would need to first be directed to a waste management comp any located 
domestically, a movement without environmental benefit.  
On the other hand, we do understand the rationale for requiring notifiers to be in the 
possession of a waste permit. By ensuring that notifiable wastes only flow between 
waste management companies a greater level of protection of human health and the 
environment could potentially be achieved.  
Financial guarantee 
We understand the need for a financial guarantee or equivalent insurance for notifiable 
wastes. What we do observe is  that there is a wide divergence of the financial 
guarantees required by Member States and sometimes for similar shipments of 
different companies by a single Member State. As the costs of such financial 
guarantees are determined in a large part by the heig ht of the financial guarantees, 
this results in diverging costs of doing business in different Member States and an 
unlevel playing field.  
It is thus with great anticipation that we witnessed the Article 7.10 where the European 
Commission is tasked with i nvestigating whether it would be possible to establish 
harmonized calculation rules for the establishment of such guarantees.  
Objections to shipment of notifiable wastes 
We fully support the right of Member States to refuse notifications and are in general 
supportive of listing the grounds on which objections can be made. We  support the 
position that past involvement in illegal activity be a ground for refusal of a notification 
as outlined in Article 12(f) provided the applicants for the notifiable waste  can prove 
the absence of violations in a certain time period (e.g. 5 years) to be established at EU 
level.  
Pre-consented facilities 
With the changes in the plastics waste entries that were already adopted under the 
waste shipment regulation, it is our expe ctation that a greater share of intra- EU 
plastics waste shipments will become subject to the prior informed consent 
procedure under EU48. Furthermore, as the EU is fast becoming a recycling 
powerhouse, it may increasingly import waste from outside of the E U. On top of it, 
existing trade with the UK falls under the international regime as well. Very few

Considering the above, we see as a great improvement the update on the provisions 
on pre -consented facilities in the WSR proposal. The new language introduced in 
Article 14.7 stating that “the competent authority may refuse to approve the request for 
pre-consent when they are not satisfied tha t issuing the pre -consent will ensure a high 
quality treatment of the waste concerned” is particularly relevant for us. It indicates that 
pre-consent status will in the future be awarded only to those facilities where 
competent authorities are satisfied th at there will be a high- quality treatment of the 
waste concerned; making pre-consented status not just an administrative benefit, but 
an implicit acknowledgement that the recycler in question is ensuring a high quality 
treatment.  
Traceability of green listed wastes 
Under the current waste shipment regulation, green listed waste shipments need to 
be accompanied by an Annex VII document, which can then be checked in the case of 
an enforcement action. In the absence of enforcement action there is little to no  
traceability for Member States of such shipments. The Waste Shipment Regulation 
proposal remedies this situation by requiring those involved in the shipment of green 
listed wastes to make available the information contained in Annex VII to the relevant 
authorities.  
This is an additional administrative burden on the waste management sector, but it is 
an understandable requirement and thus perfectly acceptable. Therefore we support 
this change to the WSR. 
Electronic submission and exchange of information 
The establishment of electronic data interchange as specified in Article 26 is likely the 
most important part of the Waste Shipment Regulation proposal. A centralised IT 
system for the execution of the regulation would bring the waste shipment activities 
of the European Union into the 21 st century. PRE strongly supports this technical 
development and believes it to be of paramount importance.  
Exports from the EU and ESM 
The WSR Proposal contains a new Annex X with criteria that non-EU facilities need to 
be audited against before being able to receive waste from the EU. PRE fully supports 
this change and believes it to be of great importance to ensure that the EU does not 
export any (plastics) waste to facilities that would treat the waste in substandard 
(cheaper) conditions.

Delegation of power to the European Commission 
It is an important  step forward to expand the delegated power to the European 
Commission. In the current WSR the power was only conferred to the EC to make 
changes following amendments in oth er international treaties (Basel and OECD 
systems). The ability for the EC to adopt amendments could result in a greater 
dynamism in facilitating the circular economy goals of the Union and responding to 
emergent environmental threats.  
Firstly, the provis ion to enable the EC to amend the new A nnex X would enable it to 
respond dynamically to any aberrant activity with waste generated in the Union and 
treated elsewhere by imposing additional requirements.  
Secondly, the ability to adopt EU specific waste codes and acceptance for green listed 
waste mixtures could be grandly beneficial for the circular economy. To give an 
example; due to the metal component of clothing hangers, bales of clothing hangers 
would fail to meet the 6% threshold of EU3011. Within the EU a limited number of 
recyclers have however specialised in the recycling of this stream. In their 
specialisation they have developed a process to identify clothing hangers that are 
reusable and marketable as such, the remaining broken, damaged, or otherwise not fit 
for purpose hangers are shred and the polymers and metals separately recycled. Only 
minute amounts of waste are generated (e.g. the paper labels).

Packaging Types under the form Annex IA, IB 
Under block 7 of the forms related to notifications and movement documents, various 
packaging types are mentioned for waste, however one crucial one for the plastics 
recycling industry is missing: “baled”. A bale is a volume of material compressed into 
a c uboid shape and bound by metal or plastics straps to retain their compressed 
shape and volume. The addition of this option would be greatly appreciated.  
On the change in the plastics waste entries EU3011 and EU48 
We noted the changes in EU3011 and EU48 that effectively prioritise shipments to 
plastics recycling facilities by limiting the EU3011 entry to only that waste which is 
destined for recycling. We support such an approach as it is in line w ith the waste 
hierarchy and should facilitate the shipment of waste to plastics recyclers. 
Conclusion 
PRE welcomes the WSR proposal as a potentially massive leap forward in terms of 
circular economy, digitalisation and modernisation, and protection of huma n health 
and the environment.  
PRE remains available to discuss the elements tackled in this position paper with the 
relevant team at the EC. 
 
About Plastics Recyclers Europe 
Plastics Recyclers Europe (PRE) is an organization representing the voice of the European plastics recyclers who reprocess 
plastic waste into high-quality material destined for production of new articles. Recyclers are important facilitators of the 
circularity of plastics and the transition towards the circular economy. Plastics recycling in Europe is a rapidly growing sector 
representing €3bn in turnover.

EuroCommerce feedback to the consultation about 
the proposal for a regulation on shipments of waste 
and amending Regulations (EU) No 1257/2013 and 
(EU) No 2020/1056 
 
Retailers and Wholesalers welcome the proposal to revise the Waste Shipment Regulation, because 
it should enable more circular business activities. In our opinion the proposal is a great opportunity 
for the EU to create a real internal market for waste, which will support recycling of waste and re -
use of valuable materials in the EU and helps to boost markets for secondary raw materials. 
 
After analysing the proposal that wa s published in November 2021, we would like to highlight the 
following points that are very relevant for textiles but are not part of the propo sal yet or have been 
insufficiently regulated: 
 
• Footwear waste should be added to Annexes III and III.a so that i t can be treated as non -
hazardous waste 
• The Waste Shipment Regulation should include sufficient controls to prevent that textile waste is 
exported for reuse but ends up in landfills in third countries and hampers recycling in the EU.  
• Until end -of-waste criteria for textiles are adopted, recycled textile fibers destined for the 
manufacturing of new products should not be considered waste, instead they should be 
considered raw materials. This must be regulated in the upcoming revision of the Waste 
Framework Directive. 
The concept of footwear waste needs to be added to  Annexes III and 
III.a of the proposal for a regulation on shipments of waste  
Both the existing Waste Shipment Regulation and the proposal do not include any provision 
regarding footwear waste, which is also not listed in any of the document's annexes.  Usually 
consumers also consider their shoes to be clothing and dispose them together with other textiles. 
Without regulation for the category of "footwear waste", this generates several problems for the 
waste management, which are currently not addressed by the proposal.  
 
Under the current proposal, consumers would place the textiles and footwear together, without 
separating them, in the containers set up for this purpose.  These containers would contain classified 
waste (textiles) and unclassified waste (footwear), which prevents waste management companies 
from taking advantage of the provisions included in the new regulations for non-hazardous waste, in 
particular, the proce dures for intra -EU shipments of waste.  This would seriously impact waste 
management financially and would also hamper the circular economy, which the proposal aims to 
support and strengthen , since this situation means that, in fact, the intra -EU shipments of a very 
large percentage of the textile waste produced on European Union is being limited. 
 
We believe that footwear waste should be included as such in Annexes III and III.a, so that it can be 
treated as non-hazardous waste, because footwear waste is made up of materials  that can easily be 
reconducted to other materials that are not considered hazardous waste, such as fabrics and fibers 
and components derived from wood and plastics.  By facilitating intra-EU shipments of footwear and

The Waste Shipment Regulation should include sufficient controls to 
prevent that textile waste is exported for reuse but ends up in landfills 
The proposed regulation at the moment fails to adress that a very significant amount of textile waste 
leaves the EU with the declared purpose of reuse, which,  if this is not possible, ends up being 
disposed in third countries under much lower standards than those in force in the EU.   
At the moment  in many Member States reuse of textile waste is facilitated though consumers 
donating their used textiles to non -profit organisations or social enterprises, which in turn find new 
uses for the textiles or sell them to third parties to finance their operat ions. These parties very often 
export the used textiles to third countries and try to sell the used textiles. However, because there 
are no follow-up mechanisms in the current regulation in reality textiles that cannot be sold in these 
third countries end up in landfills or are disposed in other ways. As a result, the waste hierarchy is 
not fulfilled, because instead of reusing the textiles , they are disposed/end up in landfill. In line with 
the waste hierarchy, reuse should always be the first priority for a garment’s second life, but when 
reuse has been an evaluated/tested route that could lead to risking garments ending in landfill, then 
recycling should be seen as a more responsible option. This could also support  the European textile 
industry with material th at, through appropriate treatment, could have a second life as recycled 
textile fibers. 
The revision of the Waste Shipment Regulation should be used to end this waste of valuable material 
through adopting finally end-of-waste criteria for textiles, introducing sufficient controls and via new 
clarifications that, without undermining the waste treatment hierarchy, would allow textile waste to 
be recycled when reuse is not assured.  
The Waste Shipment Regulation should include sufficient controls to prevent that textile waste is 
exported for reuse but ends up in landfills in third countries and hampers recycling in the EU.  
Recycled textile fibers destined for the manufactur ing of new 
products should not be considered waste, instead they should be 
considered raw materials 
The lack of end-of-waste criteria for textiles  leads to the situation that that recycled fibers, before 
they are used to manufacture a new product, are considered waste. This makes their shipment, both 
for export/import and intra-EU, unnecessarily difficult. We experience at this moment obstacles and 
limitations to the free movement of recycled fibers , which should  be considered a raw material 
within the Circular Economy. 
 
Under Circular Economy principles recycled fibers destined for the manufactur ing of new products 
should be recognized as raw materia ls. We would therefore propose that until the adoption of end-
of-waste criteria for textiles  and for the purposes of this regulation, recycled textile fibers destined 
for the manufactur ing of new products s hould not be considered waste.  This must be urgently

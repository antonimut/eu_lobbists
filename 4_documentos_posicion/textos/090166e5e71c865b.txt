In 2016, export of waste from the EU to third countries outside the EU amounted to around 
40 million tonnes, around 20% of the global  export of waste. At the same time, approximately 13 
million tonnes of waste were imported into the EU  1. Thus and given the objective to reduce waste 
shipments to third countries as part of the Green Deal and the New Circular Economy Action Plan , 
the European Commission is currently revising the Regulation n°1013/2006 on waste shipment s. 
The Commission published the Draft Regulation on November 17, 2021, currently subject to public 
consultation.  
In response to questions asked on the French Presidency of the Council during her hearing 
before the French Senate on January 5, 2022, the French Minister for the En vironment, Barbara 
Pompili, gave her full support to the proposed revision of the Waste Shipments Regulation. 
In this perspective, Citeo, the French company in charge of the Extended Producer 
Responsibility for household packaging and graphic papers, is also delighted to be supporting the 
European Commission in its fight against illegal waste shipments aiming to the development of reuse 
and recycling infrastructure within the EU.  
Citeo welcomes measures of the Draft Regulation covering intra -EU and non -EU 
exports and imports within its scope of activity . It integrates clear improvements compared to 
existing law such as the request for notification and transmission of information to the Commission 
in electronic format  and thus reducing administrative burdens , the European Commission’s 
monitoring levels of EU waste exports to OECD countries but also the strengthening of existing rules 
on administrative penalties for illegal shipments. 
The Draft Regulation is consistent with Citeo’s efforts carried out in the redesign of 
internal standards. 
 
1. On the ban of non-hazardous waste exports to non-OECD members 
Pursuant article 37 of the Draft Regulation, the exception becomes the principle, banning exports 
of green-listed non-hazardous waste to non-OECD members.  
Citeo fully supports the Commission's proposal in this respect and welcomes the ambition of this new 
regulation. It will foster intra-EU trade and trade between OECD members, thereby further ensuring 
that the principle of proximity, implying that waste should generally be managed as near as possible 
to its place of production, is respected. Indeed, it could further restrict the consumption of materials 
in France and in Europe and  consider exports outside the E uropean Union only in exceptional 
circumstances. 
Articles 38 and 39 outline the exception, with a list of criteria when exports are authorized.  Exports 
to non-OECD members are allowed under cumulative conditions:  
✓ The destination facility is authorised to carry out recovery operations for the waste under the 
national legislation of the country concerned.

2. On the obligation to demonstrate through an audit that the facilit y manages waste in an 
“environmentally sound manner” 
Pursuant article 43, the possibility to export waste outside the E uropean Union to OECD members 
is subject to an audit requirement, demonstrating that the host country has facilities capable 
of managing waste in an “environmentally sound manner”. 
Citeo supports the Commission’s proposal regarding the 3 years  frequency of the audits  and the 
realisation by a third accredited party before the exportation. Citeo also share s the Commission’s 
requirement to verify that the facility manages waste in “environmentally sound manner”. 
As a matter of fact, the audits requirements detailed in Annex X are of the same nature as those  
used by Citeo. During our compliance audits, we focus on 3 main themes. Authorizations  
(authorization for importing and proces sing household packaging waste) , process compliance  
(especially if there is any system of environmental management implemented on site / existence and 
keeping an hazardous waste register, water and air pollution indicators) and management of waste 
from the recycling process (especially if the orientation of waste comply with the EU waste treatment 
hierarchy regarding the national context and the fact that waste from the recycling process is treated 
in authorized facilities. 
 
3. On the notion of “environmentally sound manner” 
Article 56 defines the notion of “environmentally sound manner”  required for exports of non -
hazardous waste to OECD and non-OECD members.  
Citeo welcomes this definition and in particular the fact it implies the demonstration by the country of 
destination that it guarantees a level of protection of human health and the environment similar to 
that required by the European Union legislation. Thus, it ensures the same level playing field between 
countries and avoid discrepancies.

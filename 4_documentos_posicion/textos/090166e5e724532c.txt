Microplastics pollution – measures to reduce its impact on the environment  
 
Plastics Recyclers Europe (PRE), the association representing the plastics recycling 
industry, welcomes the European Commission (EC) initiative to reduce the presence in the 
environment of unintentionally released microplastics from tyres, textiles and plastic pellets, 
as an opportunity to build a comprehensive EU approach to tackle microplastics. 
In the context of the EU Green Deal1, the new Circular Economy Action Plan2 and the EU 
Plastics Strategy3, addressing the unintentional release of microplastics originating from 
pellets is key to achieve the circularity for plastics.  
The definition of microplastics currently cover plastics particles with a size between 0.1 µm 
and 5 mm, therefore including pellets, which are not only the output of chemical 
manufacturing but also the output of the recycling of plastics waste. Pellets are an integral 
part of the plastics recycling value chain since they are used for the manufacture of new 
products which incorporate these recyclates.  
PRE would like to stress the importance of differentiating between the pollution from nano 
plastics originating from a manufacturing process and plastics pellets which cannot be 
considered as pollutants. Therefore, to clearly distinguish pellets from other plastics 
particles, PRE believes that the initiative should mention microplastics as well as nano 
plastics. 
The initiative aims at addressing the lack of measures to reduce the unintentional release 
of microplastics. In this regard, plastics recyclers have already introduced measures 
targeting microplastics originating from pellets, with the aim to reduce pellet loss during the 
process.  
EuCertPlast4, third-party certification scheme for plastics recyclers, which focuses on the 
traceability of plastic materials throughout the entire value chain and on the quality of 
recycled content in the end-product, includes a requirement to prevent pellet loss. Such 
requirement allows to monitor the prevention of pellet loss during the recycling process and 
activities related to the recycling process (e.g., comminution process), and is verified 
through independent audits.

Additionally, PRE is involved in the study conducted by EC to support measures to tackle the 
unintentional release of microplastics.5 This study will help in providing data that is currently 
lacking, especially on the identification of the main sources of release and the drivers. It will 
also serve to update the EuCertPlast requirements on pellet losses for plastics recyclers, 
with the aim to ensure that certified recycling plants have effective measures in place 
regarding the handling and management of plastic pellets, for example water purification 
systems to prevent microplastics release.  
Overall, PRE would like to outline that tackling the unintentional release of microplastics is 
a key concern for recyclers, which are committed to further develop and implement 
measures to prevent such release. Harmonised measures throughout the plastics recycling 
value chain would be key to monitor microplastics release from pellets and ensure it is 
effectively handled by the relevant actors. 
 
PRE remains available to discuss the elements tackled in this position paper with the 
relevant team at the EC. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
About Plastics Recyclers Europe 
Plastics Recyclers Europe (PRE) is an organization representing the voice of the European plastics recyclers who reprocess plastic waste 
into high quality material destined for production of new articles. Recyclers are important facilitators of the circularity of plastics and the 
transition towards the circular economy. Plastics recycling in Europe is a rapidly growing sector representing €3bn in turnover.

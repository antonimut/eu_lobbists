Microplastics Pollution – Measures to Reduce Its 
Impact On The Environment 
 
Plastics Europe, welcomes the opportunity to provide input to this call for evidence on microplastic 
pollution.  We provide the following feedback on behalf of Europe’s plastic pellet producers and are 
available for further dialogue on this matter. 
Industry Efforts to Preventing Plastic Pellet Losses: 
Containment of production pellets is at the core of the plastic industry ’s commitment towards  
sustainable production.  For this reason: 
• In 2015: Plastics Europe joined Operation Clean Sweep® (OCS) , to align and concentrate 
industry’s pellet loss prevent efforts under a common global approach.  
• In 2019: we furthered our commitment towards zero pellet loss by making OCS a compulsory 
requirement of Plastics Europe membership. 
• In 2022:  Third party auditing of OCS implementation will commence as part of a performance 
certification scheme1. 
The OCS certification scheme aims to further improve the programme and its implementation  by 
assessing compliance with OCS requirements on a pass/fail basis.  Common auditing requirements 
are being set, including the need to use accredited third-party auditors.  This will enable plastic pellet 
producers, and participating value chain handlers, to transparently evaluate and demonstrate their 
efforts towards zero pellet loss into the environment by benchmarking their performance against the

Reducing Plastic Pellet Losses: 
Whilst the OCS signatories continue to work towards the goal of zero pellet loss into the environment, 
results from available studies and assessments show that, when it is well implemented, OCS has a 
direct reduction impact on pellet loss.  Therefore, any future measures should utilise those already 
in place and seek to enhance their uptake and effectiveness across the plastics value chain. 
A 2015 report, published by  the Danish Environmental Protection Agency 3 estimated that the 
average emission factor for the sector is within a range of 0.0005  % and 0.01  % of raw material 
consumption for companies committed to OCS. This estimate includes any leaks outside the 
premises of the companies and compares favourably to a worst-case emission factor of 0.5% of raw 
material consumption used by the OECD4. 
A trend of reducing pellet loss is also  emerging in the latest results of the OCS Port of Antwerp 
Activity Report 20215. Since 2017, the Port Authority and plastics value chain actors within the Port 
of Antwerp have been collaborating to optimise the implementation of OCS.  What followed was the 
2019 launch of the “Action Plan for Antwerp”.  This work involved:  
• identification of locations in the port where pellets accumulate (‘hotspots’) e.g. roads & docks,  
• intensification of efforts to prevent pellets ending up in the environment due to port activity, 
• financing of the hotspot clean-up by pellet producers & logistics companies based in the port. 
In turn, the quantity of pellets collected from clean-up activities has been recorded since 2017.  The 
resultant data suggests that the situation is improving. 
Latest results show a drastic decrease in the quantity of collected pellets.  From 8.10 tons collected 
in 2017 to 2.50 tons collected in 2021.   Although the quantity of collected pellets remains far from 
the target of zero, and the Action Plan for  Antwerp represents a gold standard, these data  tend to

Perpetuating the Trend: 
Plastics Europe believes that the Commission  introducing measures to reduce the impact of 
microplastic pollution on the environment is an opportunity to build upon work already done.  To do 
so, it must be acknowledged that no two production sites, are the same.  Sites differ according to 
design, age, surrounding infrastructure, surrounding environment.  Therefore, the measures required 
to prevent pellet loss are truly site specific and require a bespoke approach. 
Plastics Europe believes that supply chain certification (according to the OCS approach) is a proven, 
effective, and efficient approach to mitigate plastic pellets loss to the environment.  We  call on the 
Commission to build upon and further support this work by bringing greater harmonisation to the 
regulatory framework, whilst enabling the continuation of bespoke solutions to pellet loss prevention. 
More specifically, the Commission’s proposed intervention can add value by: 
• Aligning definitions for terms and value chain actors such as spills, losses, producer, logistics 
operators, converters etc. 
• Harmonising methodologies, data requirements, and mechanisms related to loss reporting. 
• Facilitating the extension of best practi ce pellet loss prevention measures to the entirety of 
the plastics value chain in a fair, transparent, and proportionate manner. 
Such an intervention would help to establish common monitoring and quantification requirements for 
microplastic pollution .  In turn enabling a ction and the implementation of the many bespoke 
responses necessary at Europe’s many plastic production sites.

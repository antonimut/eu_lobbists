H&M	Group’s	initial	contribution	related	to	the	EU	Call	for	Evidence	addressing	unintentionally	 released	microplastics	from	textiles		January	2022		As	part	of	our	sustainability	strategy,	H&M	Group	is	highly	concerned	about	the	environmental	impact	of	microfibres	and	is	driving	research	to	find	industry-wide	solutions.	We	therefore	welcome	the	opportunity	to	contribute	to	the	EU	Call	for	Evidence	on	measures	aiming	to	reduce	the	presence	in	the	environment	of	unintentionally	released	microplastics	from	textiles.		As	an	industry,	we	have	become	increasingly	aware	of	the	issue	of	fibre	fragmentation	and	its	potential	as	a	serious	environmental	pollutant.	H&M	Group	is	committed	to	researching	and	addressing	the	issue	of	microfibre	emissions.	H&M	Group	identifies	five	urgent	needs	of	the	industry	to	address	along	the	product	life	cycle:		1. A	global	standardised	test	method	to	enable	credible,	comparable	and	transparent	product	testing.	Both	RISE	and	The	Microfibre	Consortium1	contributed	to	the	harmonised	test	method	developed	by	the	Cross	Industry	Agreement2,	and	currently	going	through	CEN	adoption	process;	2. A	full	understanding	of	how	various	production	processes	contribute	to	the	problem,	and	what	can	be	done	to	avoid	it	(e.g.	installing	filters,	changing	processes);	3. Greater	 knowledge	 of	 how	 yarn	 and	 fabric	 constructions	 and	 compositions	 contribute	 to	microfibre	release	so	that	effective	solutions	can	be	found;	4. Prevention	and	mitigation	measures	at	the	production	stage,	including	final	product	processing,	wastewater	treatment	and	discharge	to	the	environment;		5. Better	filters	for	washing	machines,	including	retrofitting	existing	washers,	to	capture	microfibres	before	they	enter	the	water	system.			Executive	summary			We	call	on	the	EU	Commission	to	develop	a	holistic	approach	integrating	the	following	criteria:			- Scope:	cover	all	types	of	unintended	fibre	fragmentation	and	all	relevant	stages	of	the	product	life	cycle	(i.e.	design,	production,	use,	end-of-life);	- Research:	support	and	recognise	ongoing	industry	research,	while	allocating	funding	according	to	the	 needs	 identified	 under	the	 Microfibre	 2030	 Commitment	 and	 Roadmap,	launched	in	 September	2021;	- Full	impact	assessment	of	potential	policy	measures:	pre-wash	at	production	phase	will	not	be	a	solution.	The	global	nature	of	value	chains	would	make	this	hard	to	regulate	as	it	removes	the	problem	from	the	EU	jurisdiction	into	geographies	over	which	the	EU	has	little	to	no	influence.	- Step-by-step	 and	 risked-based	 approach:	firstly,	adopt	 one	 common	 methodology	 to	 measure	microfibre	release	at	product	and	production	levels	based	on	current	cross-industry	efforts;	secondly	identify	 which	 shed	 fibres	 cause	 a	 higher	 risk	 for	 the	 environment;	thirdly	define	 legal	 thresholds;	finally	implement	global	solutions	to	remove	or	prevent	the	unintentional	release.		- Global	dimension:	the	unintended	release	of	microfibres	should	be	addresses	in	a	collaborative	and	harmonised	manner	 with	 other	 jurisdictions	 worldwide	to	 prevent	 legal	 fragmentation.	 Integrating	production	countries	and	research	in	the	USA	would	contribute	to	create	positive	impacts	at	a	larger	scale. Acknowledge	 the	 wide	 industry	 efforts	 and	 individual	 companies’	 contributions	 to	support	 the	knowledge	gaps			Industry	 collaboration	 is	 crucial	 to	 find	 common	 and	 scalable	 solutions.	Cross-industry	 initiatives	 and	individual	ones	have	been	developed	the	past	several	years.	They	should	be	taken	into	consideration	under	the	Call	for	Evidence	as	solid	starting	points	to	accelerate	research	and	innovation	and	create	impactful	programs.				1. Address	 the	 current	 knowledge	 gap	 by	 supporting	 industry’s	 research	and	innovation	efforts		The	 call	 for	 evidence	 overlooks	 an	 industry-wide	 initiative	 facilitating	 the	development	 of	 practical	solutions	to	minimise	fibre	fragmentation	and	release	to	the	environment	along	the	product	life	cycle:	The	Microfibre	Consortium	(TMC).			As	member	of	the	TMC	advisory	board,	H&M	Group	strongly	recommends	the	European	Commission	and	the	consultants	in	charge	of	the	impact	assessment	to	engage	sorely	with	the	TMC.	This	would	allow	 an	 in-depth	 understanding	 of	 the	 needs	 for	research	 funding	 according	to	the	 Microfibre	2030	Commitment	and	Roadmap,	launched	in	September	20213.			The	Microfibre	 2030	 Commitment	 and	 Roadmap	 is	 an	 industry	 first	 in	 globally	 aligning	 the	 topic	 of	microfibres	against	a	collaborative,	target	based	and	accountable	roadmap.	The	expected	key	milestones	are:		- Access	 to	 the	 Microfibre	 Knowledge	 Hub	 (2023	 delivery),	 an	 online	 platform	 that	 will	 make	microfibre	 research	 outputs	 accessible	 so	 that	 signatories	 can	 keep	 pace	 with	 the	 latest	information,	guidance	and	tools	and	apply	this	to	their	eco-design	approach	to	new	products.		The	TMC	Microfibre	Knowledge	Hub	is	underpinned	by	primary	research	within	the	TMC	Microfibre	Data	 Portal,	 an	 industry	 first	 in	 bringing	 together	 testing	 results	 with	 the	 underlying	 technical	details	required	to	run	deep	technical	analysis.			- The	Target	Based	Agenda:	Signatories	to	the	Commitment	will	deliver	against	a	strategic	plan	to	understand	 and	 mitigate	 fibre	 fragmentation.	 This	 sets	 out	 a	 transparent	 agenda,	 avoiding	duplication	of	effort	and	holding	participants	to	account.				2. Recognise	standardised	test	method	developed	by	the	Cross	Industry	Agreement		H&M	 Group	 calls	 for	 a	 global	 standardised	 test	 method	 to	 enable	 credible,	 comparable	 and	 transparent	product	testing.	Both	RISE	and	The	Microfibre	Consortium,	working	closely	with	H&M	Group,	contributed	to	the	harmonised	test	method	developed	by	the	Cross	Industry	Agreement	(CIA),	currently	going	through	CEN	adoption	process.			The	CIA	initiative	was	set	up	in	response	to	a	direct	ask	from	the	European	Commission	in	2018.	We	believe	the	CIA	has	successfully	delivered	the	first	aim	of	the	agreement,	the	development	and	certification	of	a	globally	harmonised	test	method	which	is	a	necessary	pre-requisite	to	the	development	of	effective	and	economically	feasible	solutions	as	well	as	towards	effective	policy	development.												  3	The	Microfibre	2030	Commitment,	2021:	https://www.microfibreconsortium.com/2030	and	The	Microfibre	Roadmap,	2021	https://www.microfibreconsortium.com/roadmap

Integrate	H&M	Group’s	individual	research	and	knowledge	in	the	impact	assessment			In	 addition	 to	 our	 contribution	 to	 TMC	 work,	H&M	 Group	 has	taken	 several	 actions	related	 to	 fibre	fragmentation	and	unintentional	release4:		- Design	yarns	and	fabrics	to	minimise	microfibre	shedding	and	seek	alternative	materials;	- Investigate	new	production	processes	and	requirements	to	minimise	shedding;	- Provide	 customers	 with	 repair	 services	 and	 microfibre-reducing	 laundry	 bags	 and	 support	 the	development	of	laundry	machine	filter	systems.	H&M	brand	and	ARKET	continue	to	offer	laundry	bags	capturing	microfibres	before	they	enter	water	systems;	- Develop	technologies	to	enable	reuse	and	recycling;	- Together	 with	 the	 Hong	 Kong	 Research	 Institute	 of	 Textiles	 and	 Apparel	 (HKRITA),	we	 have	undertaken	two	research	projects:		o A	Management	Tool	for	Microplastics	from	Textile	Production	Process5. The	goal	 was	to	 help	 the	 textile	 industry	 design	 clothes	 made	 of	 synthetic	 fabrics	 that	 do	 not	 emit	microfibres.				We	remain	available	to	share	in	more	details	the	conclusions	of	the	research	projects	and	actions	taken	within	the	Group.				H&M	Group’s	initial	comments	on	the	suggested	policy	measures			1. Widen	the	scope	applying	to	the	textile	industry	to	all	microfibre	unintentional	releases			Fibre	 fragmentation	 can	 originate	 from	 all	 textiles	 and	 therefore	 can	 be	 comprised	 of	 both	 synthetic	(petrochemical)	 and	 natural	 (cellulose/protein)	 materials.	 All	 textile	 products	 and	 garment	 experience	fibre	 fragmentation:	 fashion	 garments,	 sport/outdoor	garments,	 industrial	 textiles,	 home	 textiles,	automotive	textiles,	geotextiles,	or	personal	care	products.	It’s	a	significant	issue	throughout	the	product	lifecycle,	 from	 material	 washing	 and	 processing	 during	 production	 through	 to	 customer	 product	 use.		We	 call	 on	 the	 European	 Commission	 to	 include	 all	 types	 of	 fibres	 in	 the	 scope	 of	 the	 impact	assessment,	while	providing	one	common	definition	of	microfibres.				2. Coherent	and	science-based	eco-design	requirements	across	EU	policy	initiatives				Eco-design	requirements	should	be	driven	from	science-based	understanding	of	how	fibres	are	shed	from	 textiles.	 By	fostering	 a	greater	 knowledge	 of	 how	 yarn	 and	 fabric	 constructions	 and	compositions	contribute	 to	 microfibre	 release,	effective	 solutions	 will	 be	 implemented.	Root	 cause	changes	could	be	factored	into	the	development	of	new	fabrics,	into	product	design	and	development	as	highlighted	in	H&M	Group’s	Circulator8.			The	 potential	 eco-design	 requirements	 defined	 under	 this	 policy	 initiative	 should	a	holistic	 approach,	taking	 into	 consideration	 their	 ripple	 effects	 and	 consequences	 on	 other	 parameters	 enabling	circular	economy.	Each	fibre	has	its	own	qualities	and	for	every	piece	of	clothing	we	design	at	H&M	Group,	we	 make	 conscious	 decisions	 about	 which	 fibres	 we	 use	 based	 on	 environmental	 impact,	 durability,	functionality,	fit	and	recyclability. We	should	be	very	careful	regarding	biodegradable	yarn	as	a	solution.	We	support	TMC	position	on	biodegradability	in	the	context	of	fibre	fragmentation10.	Biodegradable	yarns	should	not	be	focused	on	as	 a	 solution	 due	 to	 evidence	 around	 the	 lack	 of	 biodegradation	 potential	 of	 so	 called	these	 fibres	in	locations	where	factors	that	promote	biodegradation	(UV,	temperature,	oxygen,	appropriate	pH,	microbes	etc)	are	absent	–	e.g.	deep	sea,	soil	etc. H&M	Group	calls	on	the	European	Commission	to	ensure	policy	coherence	with	the	upcoming	product	eco-design	requirements	 to	 be	 set	 under	 the	 Sustainable	 Product	 Initiative	in	 Spring	 2022.	Coherence	should	also	apply	regarding	the	measurement	of	environmental	impacts	under	the	EU	Product	Environmental	Footprint	methodology	expected	in	2023.				3. Prevention	measures	at	production	stage	in	a	global	value	chain			The	global	nature	of	textile	value	chains	would	make	hard	to	regulate	microfibre	release	at	production.	It	removes	the	problem	from	the	EU	jurisdiction	into	geographies	over	which	the	EU	has	little	to	no	influence.	Pre-	wash	requirements	would	have	very	limited	impacts:	in	every	wash	shedding	will	occur,	mainly	in	countries	where	wastewater	treatment	might	not	be	implemented.			To	 our	 understanding	and	 ensure	 impactful	 measures	 at	production	 phase,	several	angles	 should	 be	investigated:	how	to	stop	the	microfibers	release	through	wastewater	discharge,	impacts	of	cutting	textiles	methods,	etc.			H&M	Group	would	support	manufacturing-based	interventions	through	providing	the	pending	release	of	the	 TMC	 Manufacturing	 Guidelines	 (which	 include	 manufacturing	 best	 practices	 around	 water	 filtration	within	industrial	facilities)	to	a	broader	industry	outreach	supported	by	the	EU	network.	This	action	will	support	a	consistent	and	aligned	approach	at	the	manufacturing	level.					4. Implement	filters	for	washing	machines	as	part	of	a	holistic	approach		Better	 filters	 for	 washing	 machines,	 including	 retrofitting	existing	washers,	will	 allow	to	 capture	microfibres	before	they	enter	the	water	system.			Nonetheless,	it	is	of	the	utmost	importance	to	include	this	measure	in	a	full	product	life	cycle	approach	to	mitigate	impacts	of	unintentional	releases.	Policy	measures	should	always	prioritise	root	cause	change	over	down	pipe	interventions	where	possible.				5. Conditions	to	drive	impacts	through	customer	information		To	investigate	customer	communication	 on	 fibre	 release,	H&M	 Group	would	 recommend	 the	 European	Commission	to	analyse	the	following	aspects:		- Policy	 coherence	with	upcoming	 customer	 facing	 legislation	 such	 as	 guidance	 of	 Unfair	Commercial	Practices	Directive,	Green	Claims	initiative	and	Empowering	Consumer	initiative;		- A	 comprehensive	impact	 assessment	to	 be	first	undertaken	 to	 ensure	 that	 labelling	 will	influence	consumer	intervention	to	the	level	required	to	support	the	identified	impact	required	at	point	of	purchase.			We	also	recommend	monitoring	TMC	work	development	in	this	field.	Once	the	TMC	Data	Portal	scaling	of	primary	data	is	at	the	required	level,	TMC	will	be	able	to	support	this	action	further	with	the	work	of	the	Microfibre	Rating	system,	which	has	been	outlined	in	the	Microfibre	Roadmap	for	launch	in	2025.							  10	The	position	is	available	on	The	Microfibre	Consortium’s	website	here.

Limited	opportunities	to	include	shedding	into	EPR	rules		Before	 including	 any	 rules	 related	 to	 fibre	 shedding	in	 take	 back	 schemes,	 further	 research	 would	 be	required	to	identify	the	relevant	factors	and	the	actual	shedding	ratio	of	all	fibres	at	the	end-of-life	stage.			Although	this	approach	is	suitable	within	circularity,	due	to	the	small	and	inconsistent	nature	of	fibres	once	they	have	fragmented	from	textiles,	H&M	Group	does	not	support	this	recommendation.

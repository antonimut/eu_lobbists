EBF position to the proposal of the European Commission of 27 
October 2021 amending Regulation (EU) No. 575/2013 and 
Directive 2014/59/EU, with regards to:  
1. Indirect subscription of instruments eligible for meeting the 
minimum requirement for own funds and eligible liabilities (so 
called “Daisy-chains”)  
2. Prudential treatment of G -SIIs groups with a MPE resolution 
strategy  
 
1. DAISY-CHAINS 
BACKGROUND 
The legislative proposal to move from an approach of risk weighting investments in own 
funds instruments of subsidiaries within the scope of consolidated supervision to a full 
deduction of both own funds and eligible liabilities instruments represents a major change 
to the current capital requirements applied to banks. Under the new regime, such entities 
will be forced to (1) issue further internal MREL (iMREL) at the intermediate entity level 
and (2) optimize the capital structure within subsidiaries in order to meet the  new 
obligations. 
 
In the more immediate term, however, this could result in intermediate parent entities 
breaching their MREL requirements as a result of having to deduct both own funds and 
eligible liabilities instruments, which in turn could result in i mpacted entities having to 
reduce lending to the economy at this critical juncture. 
 
It should also be added that this legislative proposal was not accompanied by an impact 
assessment, while the European Banking Authority’s initial work on the regime only 
produced a “draft qualitative” assessment, which makes it challenging for co-legislators to 
fully understand the true impact of the changes. Additionally, the proposal goes beyond 
the existing regime and imposes a harsher treatment of own funds than is alr eady the 
case. 
 
EBF SUGGESTED SOLUTIONS 
In order to mitigate against any unintended consequences, there are a number of 
measures which could be considered in order to ensure a more proportionate treatment is 
applied to impacted entities. In our view, these could include:  
• OPTION 1: Applying the deduction regime to eligible liabilities initially and tasking the 
EBA to conduct a full quantitative impact assessment on the inclusion of own funds: as 
such, entities would continue to risk weight own fund requirements of subsidiaries until 
potential further legislative changes;

RATIONALE 
1. CRR Art 49(2) al ready incorporates a deduction regime:  The existing Art 49(2) CRR 
already permits competent authorities to introduce a deduction regime under specific 
circumstances. In our view, this should be taken into consideration by co-legislators when 
making the cur rent legislative changes. This existing approach ensures that where 
authorities are concerned about the resolvability of a particular entity, for example in the 
case of complexity, the existing powers are appropriately tailored to deal with such 
situations, without unintentionally impacting all entities. 
 
2. Differences between internal own funds instruments and eligible liability instruments:  
It should be noted that BRRD Art 45f(6) looks at developing a deduction regime for 
instruments that are indirectly subscribed by the resolution entity. Indeed, for the purpose 
of internal MREL, liabilities must be issued to the resolution entity, either directly or 
indirectly. For own funds instruments, however, there is no requirement that these 
instruments must be issued to the resolution entity. Own funds instruments must only be 
issued to another entity in the resolution group, which may or may not be the resolution 
entity (BRRD article 45f(2)(b)(ii)). Therefore, we believe the deduction mechanism should 
not apply t o own fund instruments (at least until the EBA carries out a full impact 
assessment), as these instruments are not required to be issued to the resolution entity, 
either directly or indirectly. As such, the scope should be limited to eligible liabilities 
initially. 
 
3. Avoiding unintended consequences:  In our view, not including own funds into the 
deduction regime will avoid unintended impacts that we believe are contrary to the aim of 
co-legislators. Specifically, by capturing all holdings of own funds and eligible liabilities, 
we are concerned that th is will capture excess eligible items/instruments, incentivizing 
firms to actually reduce MREL holdings to a minimum, contrary to the overarching 
objective. For example, the current drafting is broad in scope and looks to apply deductions 
to all holdings o f own funds and eligible liabilities. However, in practice, firms may issue 
internal MREL above regulatory requirements so that other eligible items/instruments 
(e.g., CET1) can be used for other business purposes, like acquisitions.  
Unless amended, we believe entities will be incentivized to reduce this excess amount to 
the regulatory minimum, potentially reducing the resilience built up in many subsidiaries.  
 
4. Level -playing field considerations  among different legal structures:  HoldCo (Holding 
Company) structures, where the main operating entity is the one required to comply with 
MREL on an individual basis and subject to daisy -chain rules, may be affected differently  
by the legislative proposal. 
 
TIME FOR IMPLEMENTATION 
Regardless of the final approach chosen, given the impact on MREL levels for intermediate 
entities, an appropriate implementation period should be set to allow banks take actions 
to avoid breaches of their MREL requirements. Absent an appropriate implementation 
period, there is a  risk that the impact could have implications on the ability of affected 
entities to lend and support the economic recovery pending delivery of remedial actions 
(e.g. capital optimization, regulatory approvals or additional MREL issuance).

2. PRUDENTIAL TR EATMENT OF G -SIIS GROUPS WITH A MPE RESOLUTION 
STRATEGY 
With the objective to make the amendments of the legislative proposal more effective, we 
would like to suggest the following: 
- We believe that, with regards to article 12a CRR, and with the purpose to ensure 
equivalent treatment between SPE and MPE resolution models, some amendments 
to the BRRD would also be necessary. This is due to the fact that the proposal on 
CRR, when including amendments to make possible the consideration of 
subsidiaries located in third countries, refers to the BRRD. As BRRD refers to 
“Member States”, and not to “Member States and third countries”, the CRR 
amendment would not be effective; 
- Misinterpretation on the term “equivalent resolution requirement” should be 
avoided: we believe it should be understood in accordance with the enforceable 
national law that applies the agreed international standards; 
- Article 72e shall include a grandfathering period until 31 December 2024 (deadline 
for TLAC implementation in third countries). During this transitional period MPE 
banks shall be able to adjust the deduction on the holdings on third country 
subsidiaries without an equivalent resolution requirement, calculating the surplus 
using the total capital requirement applicable in the third country. 
Otherwise, for MPE banks, the need to issue eligible liabilities will temporarily 
increase as a result of not being able to adjust this holding on third country 
subsidiaries while, from 2025 onwards, the parent institution’s requiremen t will 
decrease once again.

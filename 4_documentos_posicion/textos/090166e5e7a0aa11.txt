EACA Position on the European Commission’s consultation on the proposal for a 
Regulation on the transparency and targeting of political advertising 
The European Association of Communications Agencies (EACA) represents more than 2,500 
communications agencies and agency associations from nearly 30 European countries that directly 
employ more than 120,000 people. EACA members include advertising, media, digital, branding and 
PR agencies.   
We welcome the opportunity to provide feedback on the proposal for a Regulation on the 
transparency and targeting of political advertising, as adopted by the European Commission, ahead of 
the discussions in the European Parliament and in the Council.  
Increasing transparency on political advertising is crucial to foster trust in commercial communications 
and democratic processes. A clear set of harmonised rules applying to advertisement at  a European 
level is essential to ensure the efficiency of the digital single market and to help controllers comply 
with necessary transparency obligations. Likewise, we fully agree with the Commission that a clear 
definition of political advertising is need ed to establish the scope of application of the harmonised 
transparency obligations. However, we believe this definition still needs to be clarified to avoid legal 
uncertainty for marketing and communications actors.  
To ensure effective implementation of the above and to avoid confusion between commercial and 
political advertising, we believe it is essential to further sharpen the scope of “political advertising” as 
laid out by Article 2 .2 of the Proposal . Commercial advertising is absolutely crucial to ensure the 
competitiveness of businesses operating within the Single Market. We therefore believe it is crucial 
to make a clear distinction between commercial and political advertising.  For instance, commerc ial 
advertising could be defined as ‘seeking to promote products, services or the image of a brand that 
are directly related to influencing consumers’ transactional decisions’, thus not falling under the scope 
of political advertising . This would significa ntly sharpen  the scope of application of the current 
definition. In addition, referring to Article 2.2.a.,  we would highly recommend  to specify which types 
of messages would be considered to be of a  “purely private or a purely commercial nature”. Indeed, 
it is crucial for communications agencies to have legal certainty on the set of rules applying to a 
marketing and communications campaign to successfully comply with relevant transparency 
obligations.  
Likewise, in Article 2.2.b., we consider it necessary  to narrow the definition of political advertising 
because the current wording of the text is likely to conflict with purpose-driven marketing operations 
which are not intended  to influence voting behaviour. For instance, marketing practices in which a 
brand supports social causes aligned with its core values would be  likely to fall under  the current 
definition of political advertising even if they serve solely a commercial purpose.  
 We therefore would suggest two clarifications on  Article 2 of the Proposal :

Article 2 
2. ‘political advertising’ means, the preparation, placement, promotion, publication or dissemination, 
by any means, of a message :  
(a) by, for or on behalf of a political actor, unless it is of a purely private or a purely commercial 
nature; or  
(b) whic h is liable has the clear intent  to influence the outcome of an election or referendum, a 
legislative or regulatory process or voting behaviour. 
 
To conclude, we are fully supportive of the general intent of the Proposal as we believe that increasing 
transparency on advertisement is crucial to foster trust in democratic processes as well as to increase 
consumer trust in the European Single Market. W e however believe that for the satisfactory 
application of the Regulation by all relevant stakeholders  and to avoid hampering economic 
development within the Single Market , the scope of application of what qualifies as “political 
advertisement” needs to be further sharpened. Finally, we also want to emphasize the importance of 
reducing the financial and administrative burden that compliance with this upcoming regulation might 
entail.

Comments of the Polish Confederation Lewiatan to the draft regulation on the transparency and 
targeting of political advertising 
At the outset, we would like to note that the applicable national regulations regarding political advertising 
to some extent meet the objectives of the proposed Regulation (hereinafter referred to as the Regulation), 
especially in terms of transparency and identification of political advertising. Both television and internet 
broadcasters are required to inform whom the material originates from, whom it is financed by and what 
(political) nature it has (Articles 4 and 5 of the Regulation). 
Issues which raise significant doubts are definitions and vagueness of a number of the regulation 
provisions. Below, we present our detailed comments. 
The definition of " political advertising" (Article 2(2)) includes a reference to a communication "which is 
liable to influence the outcome of an election or referendum, a legislative or regulatory process or voting." 
It seems that according to this definition, press materials (" liable to influence " the outcome of an 
election/referendum) could be included in this group, which is not the purpose of the act. It would 
therefore be advisable to make certain exclusions from the definition or make it more specific in order to 
avoid such doubts. 
It is not understandable why, in the definition of "political advertising services", provision of this service 
"without consideration for the placement, publication or dissemination " has been indicated as the 
exclusive feature of this service in terms of remuneration. ( Article 2(5)). The imprecise translation of the 
English text into Polish should probably be taken into account in this case, however, the English text itself 
may also raise some interpretation doubts in this regard. The recitals of the Regulation would even point 
to the opposite feature (paym ent), and those messages which refer to political views, yet expressed 
without financial support, such as the context of media activity, should fall outside the scope of the 
Regulation. Furthermore, dissemination/broadcasting of any political advertisement free of charge is not 
possible due to the requirements of tax laws regarding taxation of free services. Such an action would also 
give rise to a number of suspicions about lack of transparency and the risk of accusations of favoring a 
particular political option. In other words, the basic definitions of the Regulation need to be clarified and 
correlated with the definition of "political advertising publisher." It should also be noted that introducing 
the principle of free political advertising would depriv e broadcasters and publishers of the possibility to 
commercialize this type of activity, which, as we assume, is not the purpose of the Regulation. 
Another problem regarding terminology concerns the definition of entities financing political advertising, 
which in the Regulation are called " sponsors" ( Article 2(7) of the Regulation ). This stands in clear 
contradiction to the ban on entities sponsoring political parties pursuant to the provisions of the Polish

Act on broadcasting. 
There are cert ain doubts as to who, in the context of online advertising, should be understood as a 
"political advertising publisher" in the light of the definition referred to in Article 2(11) (a natural or legal 
person that broadcasts, makes available through an inter face or otherwise brings to the public domain 
political advertising through any medium), in particular who is the publisher of this advertisement in the 
programmatic model. At the same time, this definition contains no reference to imperfect legal persons. 
The definition of "political advertising" does not include materials originating from a given political option 
that are broadcast outside political campaign period. Including these materials in the definition of political 
advertising would, on the one hand, solve the problem of social advertisements originating from political 
parties. On the other hand, it would narrow the definition of political advertising solely to advertisements 
originating from a given political party, leaving important socio -political initiatives, such as NGOs, in the 
spectrum of social advertising. 
Article 5 contains terms "providers of advertising services" and "providers of political advertising services". 
The Regulation does not explain their meaning in the definitions, but the su bjective scope of the 
Regulation (Article 1(1)(a)) primarily points to " providers of political advertising " as addressees of the 
obligations set forth in the Regulation. 
It is unclear who should be obliged to ensure that the advertising medium complies with the requirements 
of the Regulation regarding placement of certain information on the medium (Article 7(1)). The provision 
in question merely explains that publishers " shall use efficient and prominent marking and labelling 
techniques that allow the political advertisement to be easily identified as such and shall ensure that the 
marking or labelling remains in place in the event a political advertisement is further disseminated ." In 
particular, it is unclear how the publisher should "ensure" that "the marking or labelling remains in place 
in the event a political advertisement is further disseminated." 
Article 7(1)(b) refers to " ultimate control " over the sponsor, which in the opinion of the Polish 
Confederation Lewiatan is unclear. The same applies to the  "transparency notice" referred to in Article 
7(1)(c). Paragraph 3 of that article, in turn, refers to " reasonable efforts" of the publisher. This wording 
also leaves considerable room for interpretation. Such a language should not be used in the Regulation, if 
its non-compliance would result in the imposition of penalties. 
In the context of the proposed regulations, the Polish Confederation Lewiatan expresses its doubts as to 
how the issue of media broadcast within the framework of programmatic platforms s hould be 
approached. It does not seem justified to impose such obligations on the publisher understood as the 
holder of the advertising space, but only on the suppliers of such advertisements/advertising customers. 
The regulation does not address this issue. Consequently, it is unclear who, in the light of the above, would 
be the entity subjected to penalties for the violation of the said obligations (Article 15). 
Article 8(1) imposes an entirely unjustified reporting obligation on publishers. In the case o f direct 
channels, though not only in this case, the transactional amount is a business secret. In the case of 
                                                                                                                                             
 
 
 
 
  
 
 
 
programmatic advertising, it is in practice impossible for publishers, understood as the holders of rights 
to advertising space in websites/apps, to comply with this obligation. 
 
In the context of Article 9, in the case of programmatic advertising, it is not the publisher (the holder of 
the advertising space) that should provide such a notification mechanism, but the provider of the 
programmatic platform under which the advertisement was sold and displayed. In an online context, such 
a mechanism for notifying " objections" is quite conceivable. However, doubts arise as to how the 
proposed regulations should apply to outdoor advertising, traditional press, radio or television. Although 
in the latter case, technology would make it possible to implement such a mechanism, its use - and costs 
incurred in connection with its implementation - for this particular purpose alone would be an 
unreasonable burden for broadcasters. 
 
In Article 10, a " provider of political advertising services " appears as an addressee of the obligations set 
forth in Articles 6, 7 and 8 instead of the publisher. These entities are also addressees of the next article 
in the Regulation (Article 11), though it is not clear what entities the project proponent has had in mind. 
Thus, there is an obvious gap in the definitions. 
It is unclear in Article 12(1) how to understand the prohibitions set forth in this provision by reference to 
the context of "political advertising", i.e. what is the de facto scope of this prohibition. 
It is worth noting that the provisions of the drafted Regulation partly implement provisions of the Digital 
Services Act regarding the necessity to inform  about the reason and form of online advertising, i.e. to 
indicate its source and purpose, while prohibiting ad targeting based on personal data, with the exclusion 
of the environment (Article 7 of the Regulation) in which a given entity openly declares its affiliation with 
a given social group. Further work is needed to ensure consistency between provisions of these two 
regulations. 
In addition, the above provisions on targeting are imprecise since a significant percentage of targeting 
(Article 12 of the Regulation) is based on aggregated data that is not personal data in the GDPR meaning. 
The Regulation imposes extensive obligations on the providers of political advertising services, including 
requirements of openness and transparency with regard to remune ration for political advertisements, 
reporting, provision of information by publishers upon request of publishers of political ads ( inter alia 
Article 11 of the Regulation), or a requirement for 5-year archiving of campaign materials and data as well 
as th eir accountability. Compliance with these requirements shall require organizational effort and 
financial outlays by the obligated entities. It is important that during further works on the Regulation 
within the European Commission, Council and European Par liament, this factor be duly taken into 
account, also taking into consideration a not too distant prospect of adoption and implementation of the 
Digital Services Act and ePrivacy Regulation provisions. 
In conclusion, the Polish Confederation Lewiatan belie ves that the adoption of the Regulation in the 
proposed form will have a significant impact on the commercialization of political advertising. Therefore, 
the draft requires a number of clarifications and changes, in particular at the level of basic definit ions 
                                                                                                                                             
 
 
 
 
  
 
 
 
which should take into account specific nature of the advertising market and its division into different 
media segments - television, the Internet and the press.

 
 
Feedback from the European Publishers Council  
to the draft proposal for a Regulation on  
Political advertising – improving transparency 
European Publishers Council Transparency Register ID is: 4456380381-30 
31st January 2022 
The European Publishers Council thanks the European Commission for the opportunity to comment 
on the draft proposal. 
Europe’s news media publications play a fundamental role in supporting democratic societies, and, 
as publishers of diverse titles - large and small - national regional and local, with many different 
editorial stances, the EPC’s members’ publications provide an independent voice throughout Europe 
- a voice that holds power to account and provides fact-based, professionally edited information to 
citizens – a role which is especially important at times of elections. 
The professional media organisations also play an important role in publishing responsible political 
advertising that falls within the law – these are typically national laws that cover defamation, fraud 
and the strict rules applicable to elections according to national traditions.  This is especially 
important to ensure the visibility of legitimate candidates running for office, or the showcasing of 
campaigns from official political parties. 
 
We consider the draft regulation, whose objective is to bring social networks and platforms into line 
with rules applicable to traditional media online, and offline, both timely and necessary to ensure 
transparent, well-informed and widely participative elections, alongside the benefits from the other 
proposals in the package. Nowadays, at least 50% of money spent on political campaigning goes to 
global platforms (notably Facebook and Google) where online political advertising is unregulated. 
Consequently, citizens who use search, content sharing platforms and social media to access 
information, have become vulnerable to highly organised disinformation campaigns, as well as 
micro-targeted political messages, and state-sponsored propaganda. 
 
Citizens have the right to base their decisions on as wide a choice as possible of accurate 
information and access a multiplicity of views and details, from sources they can judge to be 
credible on search engines, content sharing platforms and social networks as well as in the news 
media. 
 
Therefore, the scope of this regulation should be limited to online platforms, and not include the 
media which are already under the editorial responsibility and legal liability of the publisher and/or 
broadcaster. This approach would allow the Regulation to complement national legislation already 
in place.

The draft text raises concerns in a number of areas. Before we can fully support the proposal 
clarification and some key changes are required mainly to reduce the overlap between this 
proposal, the DSA, national laws on elections and political campaigning and existing legislation 
applicable to advertising, in particular: 
1. DEFINITIONS 
• The definition of political advertising must be further clarified to ensure that political 
messaging in editorial content is not unduly captured in the definition. 
• Article 2 should be amended to ensure the full exclusion of commercial advertising or 
at least clarify what is meant by “purely commercial nature” as otherwise wording is too 
broad and risks bringing commercial advertising into scope. There is a definition of 
“commercial communication” in Article 2(f) of the Directive on electronic commerce 
(2000/31/EC). 
•  Issue-based advertising is too broad a definition and must be clarified to exclude 
commercial advertising as there is no justification to restrict commercial advertisers in 
their issue-based ad campaigns. For example, an issue-based ad, such as one provided 
by a charity, could be deemed political in nature or even political enough to influence 
the outcome of an election, even though this was not the original intention. 
• The wording of “a political campaign organisation […] established to achieve a specific 
outcome in an election or referendum” is too imprecise and risks bringing commercial 
advertising into scope where brands ally themselves to societal issues or their corporate 
social responsibility objectives. 
•  More clarity is needed on what messages would be considered to be of a “purely 
private or a purely commercial nature”. 
• There is no clarity around the status of advertising by charities which is not necessarily 
commercial, or indeed political, and should be addressed. 
2. REGULATORY SCOPE 
• The Regulation's main objective is to bring social networks and platforms into line with 
rules applicable to traditional media. It is not the purpose of the text to add to 
regulatory requirements already existing at national level.  Otherwise, the level of 
transparency information required from publishers under the disclosure provisions 
might be overly burdensome. In addition, the required information might not be made 
available in full by the advertiser, to the publisher. 
• As with other forms of information disclosures required in online advertising, some of 
the information could be supplied more easily, and indeed more fully and therefore in 
more comprehensible ways to users, via linked information from the advertisement, or 
through the use of an easily identifiable icon.

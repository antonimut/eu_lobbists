CECA COMMENTS ON THE COMMISSION PROPOSAL FOR A DIRECTIVE OF THE EUROPEAN 
PARLIAMENT AND OF THE COUNCIL AMENDING DIRECTIVE 2013/36/EU AS REGARDS 
SUPERVISORY POWERS, SANCTIONS, THIRD-COUNTRY BRANCHES, AND ENVIRONMENTAL, 
SOCIAL AND GOVERNANCE RISKS, AND AMENDING DIRECTIVE 2014/59/EU 
 
The Spanish Association of Savings and Retail Banks (CECA) welcomes the opportunity to 
comment on the Proposal for a Directive of the Euro pean Parliament and of the Council 
amending Directive 2013/36/EU as regards supervisory powers, sanctions, third -country 
branches, and environmental, social and governance risks, and amending Directive 2014/59/EU 
(hereinafter called, proposal for a Directive), issued by the European Commission on 27 October 
as part of a banking package.  
 
We believe that some adjustments should be made in order to improve the regulatory 
framework. 
 
 
I. SPECIFIC REMARKS 
 
Fit and proper 
 
➢ Article 91b. S uitability assessment of members of the management body of the 
entities by competent authorities 
 
The proposal for a Directive introduces detailed procedural rules for the suitability assessment 
of members of the management body and key function holders. In particular, new Article 91b 
on the suitability assessment of members of the management body of the entities by competent 
authorities establishes that competent authorities shall complete the assessment within 80 
working days (assessment period) that may be extended for maximum of 40 working days, not 
exceeding 120 working days.  
 
We consider that an assessment period of 120 days (six months) is extremely long and may 
impact negatively to the stability and good governance of credit institutions. It would lead to 
long vacancies for members of the management body. On this regard it should be taken into 
account that in some cases the General Meeting of Shareholders appoints new members of the 
management body and considering that the calling of the General Meeting of Shareholders must 
be published one month prior to the meeting, it would be extremely difficult to have a final Fit 
& Proper decision by competent authorities within such a tight timeframe.   
 
In order to avoid this kind of situations,  we believe that the ex-ante assessment period  by 
competent authorities should be reduced as much as possible  and in any case it should not be 
longer than 60 working days (3 months) in total (including any extension) . Spanish regulation 
(Article 29 (2) of the Royal Decree 84/2015, of 13 February) already establishes a deadline of 3 
months for the suitability assessment since the communication of the proposal for a candidate 
(and positive silence is foreseen).

➢ Article 91c. Suitability criteria and assessment by the entities of key function holders 
 
New Article 91c of the proposal for a Directive does not allow for the immediate replacement of 
key function holders (KFH) where it is urgently needed. We believe this could have a negative 
impact in the governance of credit institutions, especially if we consider KFHs importance to the 
day-to-day business, strategic decisions-making and primary roles in the organisational structure 
of credit institutions.  
 
We propose including the possibility for immediate replacement of KFHs in Article 91.c, allowing 
the assessment of the suitability of KFHs after they have taken up their positions, where is strictly 
necessary as set out in Article 91a.2 sub-para 2 for the members of the management body.  
 
➢ Article 91d. Suitability assessment by competent authorities of the heads of internal 
control functions and chief financial officer 
 
New Article 91d of the proposal for a Directive introduces a mandatory ex-ante assessment by 
competent authorities of the heads of internal control functions and chief financial officer (CFO). 
We consider that the assessment of the heads of internal control functions and CFO should 
remain within the responsibility of the management body  of the credit institution, for the 
following reasons: 
 
• The functions carried out by the heads o f internal control functions and the CFO are not 
comparable to the functions and responsibilities of the members of the management body. 
 
• Introducing an ex-ante assessment of the heads of internal control functions and the CFO 
by competent authorities will slow down the process of new appointments. 
 
• The heads of internal control functions and the CFO reports frequently to the management 
body of the credit institution, whose members are assessed by the competent authority. 
 
• The management body is responsible for setting, approving and overseeing the 
implementation of the selection and suitability process for key function holders. 
 
Environmental, social and governance risks (ESG risks) 
 
According to the detailed explanation of the specific provision of the proposal, Article 87a of the 
proposal for a Directive introduces ESG risks  in the prudential framework  with the aim of 
incentive a better allocation of bank funding across sustainable projects. On this regard, we think 
that the prudential regulatory framework should not force credit institutions to change or adapt 
their business models to the policy objectives of the European Union. New requirements related 
to sustaina bility have to be justified by a risk assessment  under a prudential point of view , 
avoiding the pursue of  political objectives, such as  redirecting investments. The progress 
towards those objectives is being adequately tackled through the Disclosure Direct ive (NSFD)

favouring specific economic activities that are more or less risky than others only because they

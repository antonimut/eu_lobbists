HCWH Europe feedback - Environmental impact of waste management  In reviewing the EU waste framework, the priority must be to decrease waste generation altogether - this can be achieved by respecting the waste hierarchy and looking at upstream solutions.   Crucially, this initiative cannot be achieved without addressing waste from the healthcare sector, which is often overlooked. The large majority of waste produced by the healthcare sector is non-hazardous (85%) and is similar to household waste. Over 50% of non-hazardous waste from hospitals is paper, cardboard, and plastics, while the rest comprises discarded food, metal, glass, textiles, and wood.1  HCWH Europe recommends:  1. Improve healthcare waste segregation  Improper separation of non-hazardous waste means that it is often incorrectly treated as hazardous - over 30% of waste currently classified as hazardous could be uncontaminated, non-hazardous waste. When non-hazardous waste is mixed with hazardous waste, it must all be treated as hazardous. This unnecessarily increases the volume of hazardous waste and the need for specialised disposal and treatment - this not only has an increased environmental impact, but also higher financial costs for healthcare systems.  2. Phase down incineration  Incineration (including waste-to-energy), is the most harmful waste disposal method, especially for plastics. Phasing out waste incineration has been a priority issue for HCWH since 1996. Incinerators emit more CO₂ per megawatt-hour than coal, gas, or oil power plants, and plastic incineration can also release toxic gases such as dioxins or furans, and toxic ash. Waste incinerators could also lock us into a high-emission management approach, as they require a continuous stream of waste to function - incineration is not compatible with sustainable waste management.2                                                    1 WHO, (2014).  Despite the proven negative health and environmental impacts of incineration many European public health agencies and national governments still mandate incineration as the only “safe” waste management solution for hazardous healthcare waste. No-burn waste treatment methods are effective, safe, and have a lower environmental footprint – these methods should be prioritised.3  3. Treat waste within the European Union  In 2020, six of the world’s top ten waste exporting countries were EU member states. This is not only an unfair scenario, with countries from the Global North exporting to the Global South, but it also leads to risks of waste being potentially mismanaged. Waste should no longer be exported outside the EU, and intra-EU exports should be stringently monitored and include prior-informed consent.  4. Improve healthcare recycling rates  In a survey conducted by HCWH Europe, 60% respondents reported that recycling companies refuse to collect and recycle certain waste from hospitals – primarily from fear of contaminated materials. This misconception has to be resolved through information campaigns and awareness raising, as healthcare waste can provide high quality recyclable material - most healthcare waste is comparable to household waste.  For plastic recycling, a clear European-wide plastic waste contamination threshold of 0.5% should be set. When the threshold is higher, it is harder to recycle waste and increases the risk of mismanagement. A distinction should be made between mechanical and chemical recycling, prioritising mechanical recycling. Chemical recycling currently has many limitations - it requires high energy consumption and produces potentially hazardous residues. A precautionary approach should be adopted to also avoid other unknown impacts on health and environment.  5. Build a toxic-free future  Materials containing toxic chemicals should not be recycled to prevent legacy toxics from contaminating new products. Hazardous chemicals should be phased out from products at the design phase.  6. Protect workers and their rights  Workers managing waste must be recognised as essential workers and provided with education, occupational health protection, and adequate pay - they are a vital part of environmental health and infection prevention and control.                                                       3 HCWH Europe (2020).

Introduction  
The International Credit Insurance and Surety Association is the global trade association 
representing the leading providers of trade credit insurance and surety bonds. We welcome 
this initiative and the continuing efforts to improve EU regulations, as well as ensuring their 
alignment with international standards. Parallel developments aimed at improving the 
Solvency II framework should contribute to further strengthening European financial 
services. 
Credit insurance is a well-established product which, in its most basic form, provides 
protection against the risk of non-payment of receivables from the trade of goods or 
services. It is offered worldwide by specialist insurers (or via specialised units), but it is 
primarily found in European markets. As a result, the majority of (re)insurers active in the 
credit insurance sector are EU-based and regulated under the Solvency II regime.  
Enabling clause for credit insurance  
Due to the protection it offers, credit insurance has become an important credit risk 
mitigation technique (CRM) utilised and adapted by banks to fulfill current CRR 
requirements. This relationship facilitates hundreds of billions of Euro
  worth of trade-
related financing to the real economy. Credit insurance used in this way operates similarly 
to guarantees under CRR. This is explicitly acknowledged by regulators, albeit without 
specific recognition within Basel or CRR rules. We therefore strongly welcome the 
proposed enabling clause included within the CRR review package (Article 5062) which 
mandates EBA to report to the Commission on the eligibility and the use of credit insurance 
as a credit risk mitigation technique and on the appropriate risk parameters they should be 
associated with under the SA-CR and foundation IRB approach. This should ultimately lead 
to legislative proposals from the Commission which accurately reflect the role and nature of

As this topic relates to the interaction between banks and insurers, and in particular, to the 
characteristics and performance of an insurance product, ICISA believes it is essential that 
EIOPA is included on a formal basis within the investigation that EBA has been mandated to 
undertake. This could be achieved by amending the enabling clause to mandate that 
EIOPA, alongside EBA, participates in the production of the report to be provided to the 
Commission. We understand in any case that EIOPA is engaged on this matter and 
welcome their continued involvement. 
ICISA believes it is important that the report, and the subsequent legislative proposal the 
Commission may produce, should recognise the characteristics of credit insurance and its 
use as a CRM technique by banks. In so doing, it should also ensure that the LGD applied 
to a credit insurance policy is appropriate and reflective of the risks involved. At present, 
the LGD applied to a credit insurance policy is the same as for an exposure to a credit 
insurer as a debtor. These are clearly different things and should be treated as such. ICISA 
will be happy to contribute to any efforts by EBA, EIOPA, or other relevant bodies tasked 
with identifying a more appropriate LGD to be applied to credit insurance policies. 
The recognition given to credit insurance as a CRM technique should also not seek to 
change features of credit insurance policies which are standard to insurance products more 
generally or which may be subject to negotiation between a bank and insurer as a 
commercial decision. This includes elements of conditionality which may be found in credit 
insurance policies. Such elements may be included as a function of the insurer
 s wider 
management of its risks (including in meeting requirements under Solvency II), but which do 
not affect the quality of the protection provided to the bank. 
Proposal related to fraud by the obligor under  amended Article 183(b )(iii) and Article 
213(d) 
The intended aim of the proposed amendments to Articles 183(b)(iii) and 213(d) is 
understood as seeking to ensure that any credit protection contract which can, in the event 
fraud of the obligor, be cancelled or of which the extent of credit protection can be 
diminished, shall not be considered as unconditional. However, we believe the proposal as 
currently drafted requires further clarification, as well as investigation into its potential 
impact on credit insured invoice financing.

As noted above, credit insurance provides protection against non-payment of receivables 
related to the specific trade(s) of goods or services. While a lending institution is expected 
to have performed reasonable and appropriate due diligence (including as noted in line 7 of 
the proposed subparagraph iii), it is still possible that despite all best efforts, financing may 
be provided for an invoice which turns out to have been fraudulent (fraud committed by the 
counterparty of the lending institution). As the invoice is fraudulent, no specific or 
identifiable trade has occurred, which is the essential requirement of a credit insurance 
policy. Without an object of insurance, indemnification of the loss would stand outside the 
terms of the policy.  
If left unamended, the proposed text could have significant negative impact on the 
provision of credit insurance in such circumstances, which as outlined above, is an 
important tool for banks, underpinning large volumes of financing arrangements. However, 
if credit insurance is given proper recognition as a result of the report prepared by EBA (and 
preferably also EIOPA) and any subsequent proposal put forward by the Commission, this 
should acknowledge such fundamental characteristics of credit insurance. This would 
include that credit insurance requires, as the object of its protection, the performance of a 
trade for which financing is provided.  
For the reasons noted above, ICISA will be happy to contribute to a deeper examination of 
how the proposed text could impact certain financing arrangements where credit insurance 
is an important element. This investigation may lead to more appropriate wording of Article 
183(b)(iii), which acknowledges (explicitly or implicitly) that a credit insurer cannot be 
required to indemnify a loss which stands outside the terms of the policy. Similarly, this 
point would also likely be addressed through a future proposal on the recognition of credit 
insurance which acknowledges the fundamental characteristics of the insurance product 
and the strength of the protection it provides.  
Conclusion  
ICISA remains available to all relevant parties to discuss the points raised above in more 
detail. The use of credit insurance by banks in the EU represents the interaction of highly 
sophisticated and knowledgeable counterparties, regulated robustly under EU banking and 
insurance prudential frameworks. It forms a key part of the highly important trade financing 
ecosystem within the EU. It is essential that credit insurance is understood and 
appropriately recognised within the framework while avoiding requirements which

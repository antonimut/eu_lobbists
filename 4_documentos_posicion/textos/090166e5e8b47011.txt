VISION AND RECOMMENDATIONS ON THE EUROPEAN COMMISSION’S PROPOSAL FOR          
A RECAST OF THE ENERGY PERFORMANCE OF BUILDINGS DIRECTIVE (EPBD) 
 
 
VISION FOR THE ENERGY PERFORMANCE OF THE BUILDING 
 
The proposed revision of the Energy Performance of Buildings Directive (EPBD) is an opportunity to drive the post-
pandemic growth, upscale climate action and improve living conditions for millions  – as the Renovation Wave 
strategic communication states it1. 
 
The proposed provisions of the EPBD send a strong signal to the construction market: it paves the way for a long 
term and sustained decarbonisation of the EU building stock through energy performance requirements. Yet, the 
revision is a once in a decade opportunity to secure a well performing building envelope and delivering better 
buildings that are more sustainable, comfortable, healthy, and safe future-proof buildings.  
 
Knauf Insulation formulates recommendations in 4 areas to deliver a game-changer EPBD: 
1. To set best -in-class standards for new buildings and introduce “energy needs  for heating and cooling ” 
parameter to secure a well-performing building envelope; 
2. To create an enabling environment to stage and strengthen the renovation efforts ; 
3. To fully integrate co-benefits of energy renovation for better buildings; 
4. To ensure an EU-wide harmonised approach to carbon metrics. 
 
DETAILED POLICY SUGGESTIONS TO CREATE BETTER BUILDINGS 
1. To set best-in-class standards for new buildings and introduce “energy needs for heating and cooling” 
parameter to secure a well-performing building envelope 
The Commission’s proposal setting maximum energy consumption thresholds for “zero-emission buildings” (ZEB) 
depends on clearly defined climatic zones. However, these thresholds are not sufficiently ambitious for new 
buildings and their minimum levels would exceed the European Commission’s recommend ed lower values2  from 
2016. Knauf Insulation recommends applying lower levels of thresholds (in Annex III) embedded in the European 
Commission’s recommendation from 2016 for the new built to realise the “zero-emission” ambitions. 
Furthermore, a stronger focus on reducing  the energy needs  for heating and cooling from the build ing stock  is 
required to pave the way for its energy electrification, reduce peak demand and ensure a wider coverage of 
renewable energy supply.  A highly performing building envelope is a condition sine qua non  to making buildings 
technically ready for incorporating renewable energy sources and for making buildings a flexible and active part of 
the energy system. In this regard, Knauf Insulation calls for complementing primary energy performance metrics 
(proposed in Annex III) with maximum limits expressed in terms of energy needs for heating and cooling for 
both new buildings and renovations.

2. To create an enabling environment to stage and strengthen the renovation efforts 
Minimum Energy Performance Standards (MEPS) are the backbone of the Commissions’  proposal aiming at 
increasing renovation rates. MEPS can become a useful tool to roll-out renovation efforts across the EU. However, 
MEPS are a mean to an end and shall be  accompanied with other measures to really improve the rate and depth 
of energy renovations. Knauf Insulation identifies 5 elements that will strengthen the MEPS and help the EPBD to 
deliver its expected ambitions: 
2.1. To map the energy performance of a building stock  
As the Commission acknowledges it3 “better coverage of the building stock with Energy Performance Certificates 
(EPCs) is a precondition for its improvement, but at the same time Member States would need to ensure that they 
are affordable”. 
Knauf Insulation recommends setting a n ambitious but feasible goal that eve ry building  - that uses  energy for 
heating and cooling - is assessed in terms of its energy performance. Some exemptions could be applied for 
buildings below certain surface or with limited use. Homeowners who are not commercially using their properties 
will need to be subsidized for assessing the energy performance of their respective buildings.  
2.2. To involve local level to effectively accompany citizens in their renovation efforts   
Municipalities can be a transformative player in the decarbonisation of the building stock.  They can provide 
the means to map out the building stock (e.g.,  door-to-door visits of qualified and/or certified professionals who 
would assess buildings’ energy performance and propose integrated renovation services and financing 
opportunities). This in turn will create a demand for technical assistance facilities, such as “one -stop-shops”. 
 
Local players (municipalities and one-stop-shops) will be central to the success of planning and executing 
renovation efforts especially among low -income households , who might need dedicated assistance  to be 
accompanied in the climate and energy transition. In his context, local renovation plans for individual buildings 
should accompany any potential plans for financial subsidies for covering energy costs of households. 
Energy efficiency is the most effective and long -term solution to eradicate energy poverty, while improving health 
and comfort of inhabitants.  
 
2.3. To support the development of commercial “one-stop-shops” 
 
Technical assistance facilities, including through o ne-stop-shops, are paramount to deliver energy efficient 
renovation. The market can complement public services and help policymakers to make such roll-out faster. 
 
Assessing energy performance of a building should be combined with advice about technical possibilities 
to reach a deep renovation standard allowing to move towards a “zero -emission building” on an energy 
class scale. Technical advice should be accompanied with information about available sources of financing and 
current legislative requirements on MEPS.  
 
Given the scale of the renovation challenge4 it is fundamental to enable and facilitate the deployment of commercial 
“one-stop shops” whilst allowing them an access to public financing based on an EU -wide certification scheme to 
ensure quality of their services.

2.4. To reward measured energy savings   
 
A proposed requirement for Member States to link their financial measures for energy performance improvements 
in the renovation of buildings to the targeted or achieved energy savings is a positive development. European and 
national financing schemes need to explicitly reflect the Energy Efficiency First principle by incentivising 
higher results in terms of energy savings and by supporting comprehensive energy renovations improving 
building fabric before installing a new energy source. Such incentives are fundamental to secure economically 
sound, future-proof and socially acceptable decarbonisation of buildings. 
 
The EPBD can open up the way for digital solutions such as energy efficiency meters/sensors that can demonstrate 
measured savings and account for a performance -based approach. Member States should be given an option 
to use digital energy efficiency meters to determine the energy performance of buildings within the EPCs 
and to use digital energy efficiency meters to benchmark the overall operational energy efficiency of 
buildings as an alternative approach to EPCs. To ensure quality and certainty, t he EPBD should include a 
requirement for the European Commission to establish a European certification approach for energy  
efficiency meters.  
 
Once effective financial schemes are in place, it is important that Member States widely communicate about them . 
Communication activities should aim at explaining financing options and advantages of comprehensive 
and deep renovations based on measured results. 
 
 
2.5. To develop and upgrade skills among renovation professionals  
 
The success of the Renovation Wave depends also on the availability of professionals trained to perform 
construction works. Development of skills and technical assistance should be  financially supported by 
existing and new instruments including the EU Social Climate Fund and the EU Emission Trading Sche me 
(ETS 1 and ETS 2 if the latter is adopted).  
 
Within their National Building Renovation Plans, Member States should report on policies and measures 
with quantifiable targets on how they plan to ensure sufficient workforce  for renovation and develop and 
upgrade skills and training of renovation professionals.  
 
 
3. To fully integrate co-benefits of energy renovation for better buildings  
 
Constructing new buildings and executing renovations provide unique opportunities to deliver future-proof buildings 
that not only help to save energy, but also lead to improved health and well-being. For Knauf Insulation, it is crucial 
that the EPBD recast paves the way to mainstreaming the core areas of truly sustainable buildings. In particular, 
two areas can be strengthened:  
 
3.1. Good air quality in buildings is vital to health and well-being 
The impact of poor air quality in our homes, schools and workspaces is becoming a major concern worldwide. 
According to t he World Health Organisatio n’s figures5 3.8 million people a year die prematurely from illnesses 
attributable to household air pollution such as pneumonia, stroke and heart disease.   
Knauf Insulation suggests that the core indicators on sustainability in bui ldings – from the common European 
framework Level(s) 6 - could be used as inspiration for introducing regulatory requirements on indoor air 
quality including on volatile organic compounds (VOCs) at the European and national levels.

3.2. Green roofs and walls prevent urban temperatures from rising, while reducing buildings’ energy needs for 
cooling  
The EPBD recast is a golden opportunity to bridge climate mitigation and adaptation policies in the urban built 
environment. Consequently, Knauf Insulation suggests that the EPBD integrates recommendations of the EU 
Strategy on Adaptation to Climate Change 7 that specifically referred to green roofs and walls as key 
measures to make buildings resilient and adapted to climate change.  
Whilst the proposed EPBD update  rightly requires Member States to address for new buildings dimensions going 
beyond energy perfo rmance, including indoor air quality , adaptation to climate change and fire safety, these 
important aspects are absent from the definition of a “zero -emission building” (ZEB) and therefore should be 
integrated. Member States should also be required to address these dimensions for renovations. 
 
 
4. To ensure an EU-wide harmonised approach to carbon metrics and defined sequencing, while keeping 
the focus on improving energy performance of the building envelope  
The decarbonisation of the building stock  requires tackling  both the operational and embodied carbon . The 
approach must fit the situation and take stock of difference between the new and existing built environment – 
knowing that the biggest potential for reducing energy and carbon emissions rests in the existing building stock and 
hence in reducing operational carbon. 
 
Knauf Insulation supports the integration of carbon metrics based on a predefined seque ncing and the Level(s)8 
framework. At the same time, Knauf Insulation emphasises the crucial importance of ensuring the right methodology 
and sequencing of introducing the reporting process according to building type and whether the carbon is 
operational or embodied . In this respect , Knauf Insulation recommends clarifying and strengthen ing the EPBD 
proposal:  
 
• Every national calculation tool for measuring carbon must fulfil the minimum criteria laid down by the 
Level(s) common EU framework and building level standard EN15978.  
 
• The EPBD recast should start allowing to gather data both on operational and embodied carbon, which 
is a necessary step before defying benchmarks and setting targets and/or thresholds. This needs to be 
done according to a pre-defined sequencing:

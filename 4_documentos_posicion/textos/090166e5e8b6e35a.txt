HAVE YOUR SAY EUROPEAN COMMISSION PROCEDURE 
 
 Alignment EU rules on capital requirements to international standards 
(prudential requirements and market discipline) 
 
 
 
GENERAL COMMENTS. 
 
The Confederación Española de Organizaciones Empresariales  (CEOE) is the 
Spanish Trade association that defends the interests of approximately two million of 
corporates and self-employed who, at the same time, are represented by more than 
4.500 associations. 
 
CEOE welcomes the transposition of the Basel III agree ment and thanks the 
opportunity to participate in the “have your say” processes opened by the European 
Commission.  
 
Our financial committee, who is focused on following all the regulatory 
developments that could impact the financial health of our corporates, has been on 
the lookout during the last years on how the Basel III agreement was going to be 
transposed in the EU regulatory framework. Our committee has carefully read the 
European Commission proposal and would like to mention the following ideas:  
 
- First, the Banking industry plays a very relevant role in financing EU projects, 
trade finance and corporates (particularly SMEs). In other jurisdictions, 
corporates have a solid access to the capital markets; in Europe the corporates’ 
access to the capital markets is very constrained. Spain is not an exemption; 
capital markets are very limited. This circumstance has been more 
exasperated with the materialization of Brexit. 
 
- Second, it is also relevant to underline that the main corporates/SME´s 
counterparties to cover their financial risks with derivatives are Banks. In other 
jurisdictions, other specialized financial players can play this role; In a different 
manner, such specialized players are very limited in Spain and banks are the 
only providers of hedging products. 
 
- Third, it is our understanding that capital requirements are a key driver to 
determine the interest rates our corporates/SMEs have to pay for banking 
funding and for financial risks hedging. The higher the capital requirements are,

SPECIFIC COMMENTS. 
 
1. SME and infrastructure supporting factors. 
 
During the negotiations of the CRR and CRR2 the European Authorities decided to 
adapt the Basel Accord to the specificities of the European financial markets; in this 
sense CEOE fully support the EC decision to maintain both the SME and infrastructure 
supporting factors. Both measures have already proven to have a positive impact in 
the real economy.   
 
2. CVA exemptions. 
 
We also welcome that this exemption will remain in the EU framework. Similarly, to 
the SME and infrastructure supporting factor, CVA exemption was included in the EU 
framework to face certain EU specificities. Maintaining this exemption will help our 
corporates to continue to hedge their financial risks.  
 
3. Counterparty Credit Risk (SA-CCR).  
 
SA-CCR (Standardized Approach for Counterparty Credit Risk), the new prudential 
framework for counterparty credit risk that entered into force in June 2021, increased 
capital requirements for this risk by approximately 40%. As a result, banks applying 
SA-CCR have had to pass on these additional capital requirements to the costs of 
derivatives, therefore affecting the hedging of business risks by corporates.  
 
In our view, this impact is the consequence of technical weaknesses of the SA -CCR 
formula and, in particular, the excessively conservative and outdated calibration of 
the methodology (alpha factor).  
 
The EC´s CRR3 proposal allows for a temporary relief of the SA -CCR requirement, by

setting the alpha of the formula at 1, but which is only applicable for the output floor 
calculation purposes. In addition, the proposal states that such temporary relief can 
be made permanent by the Commission after consulting with EBA.  
 
This means that only corporates whose bank services providers are affected by the 
output floor will be benefited by this capital relief. It is important to note that the vast 
majority of Spanish banks are not affected by the output floor and, therefore, will not 
be able to reduce the hedging costs for Spanish corporates. 
 
In addition, we would like to highlight that other relevant jurisdictions, like the US, 
have decided to deviate from the Basel standard and set the alfa at 1 for all the 
Standardized Appro ach, which also Spanish corporates in competitive 
disadvantage versus their US peers. 
 
The CEOE supports extending this more favorable treatment of SA -CCR to all the 
Standardized Approach so that all banks and corporates in the EU can be equally 
benefitted from this capital relief in the application of the SA -CCR. This measure 
should be applicable on a permanent basis, or at least until the global standard is 
reviewed by the Basel Committee so that it is applicable to all the relevant 
jurisdictions.  
 
 
4. Unrated corporates of investment grade credit quality. 
 
In the same vein as SA-CCR, the Commission’s proposal allows for a temporary relief 
in the capital required for exposures to unrated corporates of the highest credit 
quality (investment grade) only for the output floor calculation procedure. For such 
exposures, banks affected by the output floor will be allowed to apply lower risk 
weights. In addition, the Commission could propose extending this measure by the 
end of 2031. 
 
The CEOE believes that the sol ution should not be restricted to certain banks and 
therefore supports the extension of this measures to banks using the Standardized 
Approach and not affected by the output floor. Although a pure extension would not 
be possible since Standardized banks don´t have regulatory PDs, we recommend to 
explore other alternatives that allows other unrated corporates to be benefited by 
this measure, such as the use of accounting PDs or those calculated for economic 
capital purposes.  
 
It should be noted that most of  the companies in Spain are SME and therefore the 
percentage of companies that do not have an external rating assigned is very high, 
so, although the use of rating for regulatory purposes is authorized, the vast majority 
of companies do not have one. Howev er, as mentioned earlier, the majority of

without rating will be different. 
 
- Second and because of the previous point, among corporates based in 
different jurisdictions since their banks will charge different interest rates. 
 
 
5. Trade Finance 
 
Trade Finance is a key facilitator of commercial flows playing an important role to 
support economic growth. Wherever there is a company, large or small, that needs to 
import or wants to export its products, they typically rely on some form of trade finance 
to achieve competitive financing or mitigate the commercial risks. Overall, the World 
Trade Organization (WTO) estimates that over 80% of international trade relies on 
some form of Trade Finance1. 
Considering its importance for the real economy, we think there is a potential risk for 
the new regulation to unintendedly penalize trade finance. One example could be the 
suggested increase in the Credit Conversion Factor (CCF) for Performance bonds, that 
could move from  the current 20% to as high as 50%. In our view, this not only overly 
punitive considering the historical evidence that shows that default rates are below 
0,25% with a real CCF of 8% 2, but it would also have a sizeable impact on the different 
corporate companies. It is important to bear in mind that companies, especially in the 
infrastructure and energy sector, require to provide this kind of performance

6. Specialised Lending 
 
The CRR3 proposal acknowledges that promoting viable infrastructure projects and 
other specialized projects is of vital im portant for the economic growth of the 
European Union. Specialized lending by institutions is also a defining characteristic of 
the Union economy, as compared with other jurisdictions where such projects are 
predominantly financed by capital markets.  
In the same way that the CRR3 proposal has introduced some amendments in the 
standardized approach, the CEOE also believes that the slotting approach should be 
reviewed. In particular, it would be necessary to introduce more granularity, 
particularly in the ca se of project finance of higher quality, to ensure there is 
consistency among the three prudential approaches: standardized, slotting and IRB.

 
 
 
Feedback to the Call for evidence:  Environmental impact of waste management – revision of EU 
waste framework 
 
ERFO welcomes the initiative of the European Commission that aims to improve the overall 
environmental outcome of waste management. The Waste Framework Directive  has served well to 
improve the management of waste in Europe. Revising it may offer opportunities to improve the way 
in which waste is handled even further. Although efforts have been undertaken and should be 
continued to collect material streams separately at source, still high amounts of wastes are and will 
be generated in mixed form.   
 
An optimum way to deal with mixed (residual) waste and also with sorting residues of different 
waste streams (light plastics, used paper, biowaste, …)  is a combination of (additional) sorting and 
production of SRF (Solid Recovered Fuel). SRF is not a mere waste derived fuel. It is produced in 
accordance with CEN and ISO standards and therefore it must and can be sharply distinguished from 
undefined “RDF”.  
 
SRF is the link between sorting/recycling and energy-from-waste. The ability of SRF to improve the 
yield of energy from waste has been confirmed in the Commission communication “The role of 
waste-to-energy in the circular economy”. Sorting of waste and production of SRF is a combined 
process, linking recycling and efficient use of the energy in waste. The non-recyclable part of mixed 
waste is upgraded to standardised fuels with known and reliable qualities. With increasing recovery 
of plastics from waste, SRF contains relevant biogenic contents. SRF contributes to the reduction of 
GHG emissions at facilities using it. Its high energy yield, due to the pre-processed form, can be 
utilised in a multitude of facilities to replace fossil fuels. A revision of the Waste Framework Directive 
is an opportunity to strengthen the combined process of sorting of waste and the production of SRF, 
thus benefiting optimum material recovery and optimum energy from waste.

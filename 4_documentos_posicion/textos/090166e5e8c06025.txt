COMMENTS ON THE PLANNED REVISION OF THE WASTE FRAMEWORK DIRECTIVE  Luxembourg, 22 February 2022  Clariter welcomes the Commission’s initiative to revise the Waste Framework Directive (WFD) and would like to thank them for the opportunity to contribute to the ongoing stakeholder consultation.  We welcome the alignment and cohesion between the implementation of the Waste Framework Directive with the goals of the European Green Deal as well as the Circular Economy Action Plan and the Packaging and Packaging Waste Directive. This will enable a strong European market for secondary materials to develop and will support the transition to a circular economy as more collection and recycling will take place and more materials are kept in use.  Given our expertise on chemical recycling of hard to recycle plastic waste, we would like to draw the attention of the Commission to some aspects that, in our view, need to be addressed in the revision.   Using waste as a raw material for high-value products such as Clariter's requires a "circular" policy that links production, consumption, disposal and reuse.    Therefore, we would like to underline the importance of the waste hierarchy. The main objective is to prevent any waste from being incinerated, landfilled or leaked into our precious environment. Accordingly, the revision of the WfD should also address:  1. Limiting recovery and landfill in the EEA region. For this the EC should consider to:  a. A centralized increase on levies or taxes on waste disposal via incineration and landfill. b. Prohibit waste shipment outside the EEA region.   2. Stimulating design for reuse or recycling (mechanical or chemical):  a. Clear labelling for the consumer  b. Downcycling of materials should be avoided, hence a clear definition on "high-quality recycling" is needed c. Replacing fossil feedstock (carbon) for secondary raw materials is of the utmost importance. Hence using secondary raw materials should not be limited to produce plastic (packaging) but could facilitate a much broader array of products like paints, detergents and cosmetics.

3. Improving collection & sorting capacity that is needed to increase the supply of secondary raw materials    a. Increase capacity of collecting and sorting throughout the EEA region. b. The establishment of EPR schemes is important in this regard.  c. Biobased plastics in plastic waste hinder both mechanical and chemical recycling and should therefore be biodegradable and thus collected as biobased waste 4. Stimulating the uptake of Chemical recycling. Chemical recycling takes on a clear circular approach as the definition of chemical recycling excludes energy recovery: “Chemical Recycling is defined as any reprocessing technology that directly affects either the formulation of the polymeric waste or the polymer itself and converts them into chemical substances and/or products whether for the original or other purposes, excluding energy recovery”1.  a. Chemical recycling should be recognized in the overall definition of “recycling” guarantying a level-playing field. b. Fuel output within the chemical recycling value chain is energy recovery, for this the sector needs both a clear definition on policy boundaries (WfD and REDII) and recognition for the value created.   c. Policy is need on the implementation of a chain of custody model, more specific the mass balance approach.   About Clariter • Clariter SA is a global cleantech company founded in 2003 and headquartered in Luxembourg with operations in Israel, Poland, the Netherlands and South Africa.  • Clariter upcycles low value plastic waste into high-quality, pure and standard industry grade products including solvents, waxes and white oils.  • The company’s technology is proven and operational and has a clear path to scalability.

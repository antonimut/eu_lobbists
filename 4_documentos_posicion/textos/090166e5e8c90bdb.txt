Subject: Environmental impact of waste management – revision of EU waste framework 
 
European countries are expected to set up schemes to separately collect and properly dispose of textile 
waste by the end of 2024 . While some Member States actively research options, the complexity and 
the urgency to create an efficient mechanism for separate collection , handling and valorisation of 
textile waste appears not to be properly addressed at national and European level. The revision of the 
Waste Framework Directive is an opportunity to remove barriers  
 
EURATEX, represents the European apparel and textile industry and actively supports the European 
Commission’s political commitment towards circular economy and the revision of the Waste 
Framework Directive (WFD) 1 and welcomes the objective s of the initiative to improve the waste 
management and the harmonisation of separate waste collection rules across EU. 
 
To extend the lifespan of textiles, an appropriate collection system must first be established. EURATEX 
calls for a well-functioning textile waste collection and valorisation system across the EU, and ask for 
product-specific and use specific solutions.  
 
To achieve this goal, there is a need for:  
• Transparent and easy to understand separate textile collection system; 
• Open dialogue with the stakeholders involved in the textile collection system in Member 
States; 
• Consideration of factors beyond the European influence on the textile market; 
• Waste reduction targets which maximize circularity, while reducing waste; 
• Policy decisions to focus on industry and consumer behaviour. 
 
To remove barriers in textile separate collection, EURATEX takes this opportunity to stress limitations 
and to share key recommendations needed, in order to significantly reduce textile waste genera tion 
and ensure availability of high-quality secondary materials for the business. 
 
1. Need for a definition of “waste” & “reusable” 
 
The definition of “textile waste” is not only inconsistent between EU Member States, but also between 
regions within one Member State. Different national definitions cause major burdens and hinder 
recycling and/or the use of recycla tes. The WFD should provide clear set of definition on “waste”, 
“reusable” and “high-quality recycling”, reflecting circular economy perspective.

2. “Textile" is not just "textile" 
Textiles are complex materials  and u nderstanding the complexity of textiles is essential . A precise 
differentiation of the term "textile" is needed, since the different application areas and usage scenarios 
of textiles result in different product’ requirements (e.g. safety, quality, function) and their circulation 
options (e.g. recyclability, reparability, longevity, reuse, etc ). The special features of textiles as a 
material offer opportunities, but also pose challenges. To actually close loops, textile materials must 
also be able to flow back into loops. Moving forward, there is a need to promote, research, 
development, as well as mutual support between different disciplines to overcome technical 
challenges. 
 
There is still no viable solution to deal with textiles , that are not compliant  with the current  and 
upcoming REACH restrictions (e.g. garments place d on market long before the current restrictions 
entered in force), yet are still in use. The life cycle of textiles is not comparable to the much shorter 
life cycle of packaging. This is why the collection  can still contain textiles that were produced under 
completely different legal framework conditions (e.g. REACH) and with completely different permitted 
threshold values. 
 
3. Different & non-transparent collection systems 
Mandatory separate collection of textiles  by end of 2024 requires economically effective solutions , 
which are replicable throughout Europe for authorities to manage textiles collection and the business 
to give it a meaningful value. 
 
Overall reuse of materials can operate at large scale only , if an efficient system to collect, sort, track 
and deliver is in place. This represents a challenge for textile reuse and recycling, as a comprehensive 
and widespread collecting system throughout Europe is still missing. Depending on the EU member 
state, different systems for the collection of used textiles have been or are being established. In some 
cases, there are even regional differences in member states. 
 
Additionally, figures or statistics  providing a clear picture of which quantities  textiles are actually 
collected and how they are recycled and disposed of are barely available. There is also no information 
available on where used textiles are shipped and what happens in final destination. 
 
As textile materials can have very different propertie s, and different fibres require different 
treatments, an efficient mechanism for separate collection and handling of textile waste needs to be 
properly addressed at national and European level . To ensure optimal handling of textile waste, a 
proper collecting-sorting-recycling system is vital. EURATEX believes, that harmonised collection 
  
 
 
 
specifications, describing how used textiles should be handled and sorted is essential, as high efficiency 
is gained by people bringing textile items to collection points or collection bins. 
 
4. Need to create recycling capacitates and options 
The treatment of secondary raw materials and the EU-wide definition of waste in legislation are among 
the important issues . Increasing the collection of waste makes sen se and is particularly effective if 
sufficient recycling options and capacities are available. Today, the necessary amounts of used textiles 
collected are not yet available for recycling to take place in an industrial form, despite advances in 
technology. An increase in collection rates would ensure that more textiles are collected, but not that 
sufficient capacities are available to reintroduce these textiles into suitable cycles . There are new 
upcoming technologies, which will increase efficie ncy and financial viability of sorting and recycling.  
Recycling t echnologies for complex products, shall be scaled up as soon as theirs effectiveness is 
proven.   
 
Since 2020, EURATEX actively works on ReHubs initiative, which aims at establishing an integ rated 
system based on recycling hubs (ReHubs) within Europe, to upcycle textile waste and scale up 
industrially the collecting, sorting, processing and recycling of pre -consumer & post -consumer 
materials. One of the goal of the ReHubs is to maximize collec tion of textiles within EU. The ReHubs 
are proposed well in advance of the 2024 deadline and will offer further solutions, helping EU Member 
States to comply with the separate textile waste collection by end of 2024. 
 
5. Waste reduction targets 
The right political framework should be in place, in order to increase the demand from consumers and 
authorities for recycled contents in products. Reduction must be set within  the limits of what is 
economically and technically feasibl e. Correspondingly long transition periods must be granted in 
order to be able to maintain level playing field. EURATEX suggests to set realistic targets based on 
growing national capacity, which could stimulate the demand and could benefit the business. 
6. High cost of waste handling and processing 
Waste handling across Europe appears non-harmonised and leads to costly and complex procedures, 
which discourage the use of post -consumption or post -production waste.  This adds onto 
manufacturing costs that are already increasing due to the other EU regulations. Thus, the European 
manufacturers lose their competitive power, while  the European consumers face higher prices. A 
financial tool to support this transition is very vital.  
 
To achieve a viable business model, EPR system s, if implemented2 in a goal-oriented manner, can be 
an effective approach to ensure financial contribution to the costs of waste management. If EPR

7. Paramount importance for Circular design  
Collection, s orting and recycling are starting  points for an end -of-pipe solution. In order to meet 
challenges in the establishment of a circular economy, it is necessary to think "from the beginning". 
Through better product knowledge and product design. Circular design in particular must be brought 
to the fore. If aspects of closed-loop and closed-cycle management are not integrated from the outset, 
problems will continue to be shifted to the end of the product's life. These strategies must  be 
promoted and supported in a way , that they can be perceived and implemented as an opportunity  
(e.g. by using product and usage scenario based approaches) and not act as an additional hurdle. 
 
8. Harmonisation with Sustainable Product Initiative, EPR & other upcoming proposals 
The revision o f WFD must be jointly developed with the textile strategy, SPI , polluter pays principle 
and other relevant initiatives, to find the highest possible common ground. This shall be done through 
harmonization of specifications/guidelines, common standards and n orms and with exchange across 
all stakeholder groups. 
 
It is important to keep in mind that each industry has different structures and conditions that require 
a different approach to collecting -sorting-recycling, implementation of circular design, use of 
recyclates or other recyclable materials  and to new or different business model s. Solving problems 
requires a common understanding on what are the current barriers to widespread use of circularity in 
textiles, what are the costs of industrial transition towards circular economy and what are the 
necessary organizational changes. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
About EURATEX 
As the voice of the European textile and clothing industry, EURATEX works to achieve a favourable environment 
within the European Union for design, development, manufacture and marketing of textile and clothing products. 
The EU-27 textile and clothing industry, with around 160,000 companies  employing 1.5 million workers, is an 
essential pillar of the local economy across many EU regions. 
Working together with EU institutions and other European and international  stakeholders, EURATEX focuses on 
clear priorities: an ambitious industrial policy, effective research, innovation and skills development, free and fair 
trade, and sustainable supply chains.

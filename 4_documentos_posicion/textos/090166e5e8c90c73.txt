Contribution to call for evidence on 
‘Environmental impact of waste 
management – revision of EU waste 
framework’  
 
With the revision of the Waste Framework Directive, the European Commission aims to protect 
public health and the enviro nment through the proper management of waste. This is done by 
applying the EU’s waste hierarchy, which promotes  
1. waste prevention (reduce) over 
2. preparing for reuse; over  
3. recycling; over  
4. other waste recovery options; over 
5. disposal of waste.  
However, while waste prevention (see No 1. above) and recycling (see No 3. above) are largely 
addressed in the call for evidence, very little attention is drawn to the role that reuse systems 
(see No 2. above) can play in waste prevention. 
Reusable Packaging Europe (RPE) fully supports the European Commission in its ambition and 
calls on the Commission to respect the waste hierarchy in its activities, thus promoting reusable 
(transport) packaging. RPE is convinced that reusable transport packaging plays, in line with the 
waste hierarchy, a crucial role in preventing waste – it ranks at the top of the waste hierarchy, only 
reduce is superior – and therefore welcomes the initiative to introduce overall and product specific 
waste prevention measures, as well as expanding the role of EPR schemes. 
 
Who we are 
 
Reusable Packaging Europe (RPE) is the European association which represents the interests of 
companies active in the business of pooling reusable transport packaging, primarily in the pooling 
of Reusable Packaging Containers (RPCs) and the pooling of pallets.  
The business of pooling reusable transport packaging has been successfully operating for many 
years as a superior business model in comparison to the business of one-way transport packaging 
from a waste prevention perspective. Such one -way transport packaging can , at its best , only

As a result, the business of pooling RPCs and pallets has constantly reduced the environmental 
impact of supply chains by constantly reusing reusable transport packaging, and therefore 
reducing the use of natural resources for the production of transport packaging (virgin material). 
RPE believes that the use of reusable transport packaging (as opposed to one -way transport 
packaging material) is essential for reaching a clim ate-neutral economy. Reusable transport 
packaging is the most efficient and sustainable solution for transport packaging.  
The business of pooling reusable transport packaging can be qualified as a Product-as-a-Service 
model – or rather (Transport) Packaging-as-a-Service ((T)PaaS) –, leads to a truly waste-free 
and circular economy, facilitates the further sustainable development of the internal market and 
contributes to a climate-neutral economy. 
 
In RPE’s view, specific consideration should be given to the following 
elements: 
1. Distinguish more clearly between reuse and recycling in the waste hierarchy 
RPE would like to draw attention to the existing discrepancy between the aforementioned waste  
hierarchy and the method by which waste management objectives have been defined. Both in the 
initial Waste Framework Directive (2008/98/EC) and in its report on the implementation of the EU 
waste legislation (COM(2018) 656 final), the European Commission defines the objectives for 
reuse and recycling as combined objectives in the same quantitative objective  (art. 11 of the 
Waste Framework Directive). 
Counting reuse and recycling together under the same target does not result in a correct 
application of the European waste hierarchy  and does not reflect the reality of the industry, 
especially with regards to transport packaging. Scientific research shows that reuse models, such 
as pooling systems for reusable transport packaging, constitute  far more favorable modes of 
waste management in comparison to recycling. Further data also demonstrate that reusable pallet 
and RPC pooling initiatives have a far lower  environmental impact than one-way alternatives (see 
also Fraunhofer & SIM, 2013). Furthermore, a 2016 report, which was written at the request of the 
Commission’s DG Environment, describes the issues that are connected to combined targets for 
reuse and recycling: a combined target does not suffic iently promote reuse, while also a lack of 
proper data -gathering complicates the introduction of potential targets. A more ambitious 
approach from the EU towards promoting the data gathering on reuse by Member States is 
therefore essential. 
2. Introduce ambitious and binding reuse quota 
Targets and limits will force all industries to (re)consider their (transport) packaging 
solutions in line with the EU’s sustainability and circular economy ambitions. RPE believes 
that binding targets are the only way to ensure significant increase in reusable transport packaging

In order to promote the most efficient forms of waste manage ment, RPE therefore calls for 
separate binding and quantitative targets for reuse, nex t to recycling targets. Under the current 
formulation of targets, the waste hierarchy is not sufficiently promoted as Member States can 
reach their target by mostly focus ing on merely recycling, without ambitiously pursuing more 
sustainable reuse strategie s (being ranked higher in the waste hierarchy than recycling – see 
above). To take into consideration the unique characteristics of each industry, these reuse targets 
should be sector specific and translated into concrete incentives.  
Furthermore, the European legislative framework should also aim at abolishing any burdensome 
administrative (and fiscal) hurdles (relating to for example VAT or EPR) on circular businesses 
contributing positively to waste reduction, such as reusable transport packaging  pooling 
companies.  Moreover, to support and incentivize the use of (Transport) Packaging-as-a-Service 
and other reuse-based business models, which undoubtedly prevent waste generation (and 
therefore positively contribute to waste reduction ), RPE calls on the European Commission to 
create legal certainty for circular business models concerning the property ownership rights with 
respect to such transport packaging (that is used as Transport Packaging -as-a-Service). When 
the legal ownership rights (to the transport packaging, that is rented out to users of such reusable 
transport packaging) can be easily enforced by these pooling companies,  the benefits of these 
reusable business models can be optimized. 
3. Ensure an appropriate role for Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) 
In order to incentivize the implementation of the waste hierarchy, a broad and proper introduction 
and implementation of the  EPR principle is crucial. RPE thus believes that the  EPR principle 
should be mainstreamed, primarily for manufacturing (and use) of one-way (transport) packaging.  
RPE would like to underline that Extended Producer Responsibility still fails to properly take 
into account (and promote) reusable (transport) packaging. In line with the waste hierarchy, 
all packaging and packaging waste frameworks and tools must take better account of reuse if the 
EU wants to attain a circular economy and reduce waste generation. With a clear differentiation 
between different kinds of packaging  (i.e. differentiation between reusable transport packaging 
and one -way transport packaging) , EPR could facilitate an increased (re)use of reusable 
packaging, prevent the amount of waste and promote circular business models. 
Reusable transport packaging should not be subject to EPR fees given their inherent strength and 
reuse characteristics, therefore reducing (the use of one-way) packaging in general. RPE would 
therefore support the establishment of either an exemption for reusable transport packaging from 
an EPR contribution or , if such would not be created, establishing a system in which reusable 
packaging will only be liable once to EPR fees, i.e. only when the reusable (transport) packaging 
is put on the market for the first time.

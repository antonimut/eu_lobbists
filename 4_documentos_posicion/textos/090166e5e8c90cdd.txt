Contribution to call for evidence on 
‘Environmental impact of waste management – revision of EU waste 
framework’ 
 
The members of the Closed Pallet Pooling Coalition (CPPC) – CHEP/IPP/LPR – (hereby 
referred to as “the Coalition”) welcome the European Commission’s ambition to revise the 
EU waste legislative framework to further protect public health and the environment through 
the proper management of waste . This is done by applying the EU’s waste hierarchy, as 
defined in the current Waste Framework Directive which promotes  
 
1. waste prevention (reduce) over  
2. preparing for re-use; over  
3. recycling; over 
4. other waste recovery options; over 
5. disposal of waste.  
 
However, while waste prevention (see No 1. above) and recycling (see No 3. above) are 
largely addressed in the call for evidence, very little attention is drawn to the role that 
reuse systems (see No 2. above) can play in waste prevention. 
 
The Coalition fully supports the European Commission in its ambition to develop an economy 
that i s mor e sustainable and circular and calls on the Commission to respect the waste 
hierarchy in its activities, thus promoting reusable (transport) packaging and “packaging-
as-a-service” business models. The Coalition is convinced such business models play, in line 
with the waste hierarchy, a significant role in preventing waste – reuse ranks at the top of 
the waste hierarchy, only reduce is superior - and therefore welcomes the initiative to 
introduce overall and product specific waste prevention measures.  
 
The Coalition also welcomes the Commission ’s ambition to strengthen Member States’ 
contributions, the necessity of  which was correctly emphasized in the call for evidence, 
since several Member States are reported to not be on course for reaching  the defined 
objectives.  
 
What is Closed Pallet Pooling?  
 
A system of closed pooling of wooden pallets, as promoted by the Coalition, maximizes the 
balance between an optimized utilization and a sustainable “Product -as-a-Service” (or 
rather (Transport) “Packaging-as-a-Service”) business model by issuing, collecti ng and 
reusing wooden pallets through a managed and tracked internal system. The coalition places 
great emphasis on, and investment in ensuring that their wooden pallets are designed to be 
highly resilient, can be repaired and reused many times and that an y end -of-life-cycle 
materials can be reused or recycled.  
 
The business of pooling reusable transport packaging, which can be qualified as a Product-
as-a-Service model – or rather (Transport) Packaging-as-a-Service ((T)PaaS) –, leads to a truly 
waste-free and circular economy and facilitates the further sustainable development of the 
internal market and contributes to a climate-neutral economy.

The Coalition would like to stress the importance of promoting sustainable waste 
management and calls on the Comm ission to ensure a correct and effective application of 
the European waste hierarchy through the revision of the Waste Framework Directive. In 
accordance with the waste hierarchy, concrete objectives should be set for Member States 
for reuse, especially wh en it comes to packaging. These objectives should incentivize the 
use of reusable transport packaging (as opposed to one-way transport packaging material), 
which is the most efficient and sustainable solution for transport packaging , and therefore 
essential for reaching a climate-neutral economy.  
 
In CPPC’s view, specific consideration should be given to the following elements: 
 
1. Distinguish more clearly between reuse and recycling in the waste hierarchy 
 
The Coalition would like to draw attention to the existing discrepancy between the 
aforementioned waste hierarchy and the method by which waste management objectives 
have been defined. Both in the initial Waste Framework Directive (2008/98/EC) and in its 
report on the implementation of t he EU waste legislation (COM(2018) 656 final), the 
Commission defines the objectives for re -use and recycling as combined in the same 
quantitative objective  (art. 11 of the Waste Framework Directive).  
 
Counting reuse and recycling together under the same target does not result in a correct 
application of the European waste hierarchy and does not reflect the reality of the industry, 
especially with regards to transport packaging. Scientific research shows that re-use models, 
such as pooling systems for reusable transport packaging, constitute far more  favorable 
modes of waste management in comparison to recycling. Further data also demonstrate that 
reusable pallet and other reusable transport packaging pooling initiatives that promote the 
sharing of standardized pallets  (or other reusable transport packaging)  have a far lower  
environmental impact than one-way alternatives. Furthermore, a 2016 report, which was 
written at the request of the Commission’s DG Environment, describes the issues connected 
to combined targets for re-use and recycling: a combined target does not sufficiently 
promote reuse, while also a lack of proper data -gathering complicates the introduction of 
potential targets. A more ambitious approach from the EU towards promoting the data 
gathering on reuse by Member States is therefore essential. 
 
2. Introduce ambitions and binding reuse quota 
 
Targets and limits will force all industries to (re)consider their (transport) packaging 
solutions in line with the EU’s sustainability and circular economy ambitions. In order to 
promote the most efficient forms of waste management, the Coalition therefore calls for 
separate binding and quantitative targets for reuse, next to recycling targets. Under the 
current formulation of targets, the European Waste Hierarchy is not sufficiently promoted 
as Member States can reach their target by focusing on merely recycling, without ambitiously 
pursuing more sustainable reuse strategies (being ranked higher in the waste hierarchy than 
recycling – see above). To take into consideration the specificities of each  industry, these 
reuse targets should be sector specific and translated into concrete incentives.  
 
Furthermore, the European legislative framework should also aim at abolishing any 
burdensome administrative (and fiscal) hurdles (relating to for example VA T or EPR) on 
circular businesses contributing positively to waste reduction, such as the circular business 
model of the members of CPPC (closed pallet pooling companies).  Moreover, to support and 
incentivize (Transport) Packaging -as-a-Service and other reuse-based business models, 
which undoubtedly prevent waste generation (and therefore positively contribute to waste 
reduction), the Coalition calls on the European Commission to create a  legal certainty for 
circular business models concerning their property ownership rights with respect to such 
 
 
transport packaging (that is used as Transport Packaging -as-a-Service). When the legal 
ownership rights (to the transport packaging, that is rented out to users of  such reusable 
transport packaging) can be easily enforced by these pooling companies, the (circular) 
benefits of these (circular) reusable business models can be harvested in full. 
 
By removing barriers that impair the optimal collection of reusable packaging (such as the 
barriers that the members of Coalition experience with respect to securing the continued 
ownership of their pallets, by sometimes failing to reclaim such pallets from parties who 
“possess” them without being entitled to such possession) , and therefore their effective 
reuse, the packaging sector will be better incentivized to invest and move towards reusable 
packaging systems. Otherwise, less sustainable one-way and single-use packaging systems 
will continue to benefit (despite being ranked below “reusable packaging”  in the waste 
hierarchy), and the EU waste reduction targets will be impossible to achieve. 
 
3. Ensure an appropriate role for Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) 
 
In order to incentivize the implementation of the waste hierarchy, a broad and proper 
introduction and implementation of the EPR principle is crucial. The Coalition thus believes 
that the EPR principle should be mainstreamed, primarily for manufacturing (and use) of 
one-way (transport) packaging.  
 
The Coalition would like to underline that Extended Producer Responsibility still fails to 
properly take into account (and promote) reusable (transport) packaging. In line with the 
waste hierarchy, all packaging and packaging waste frameworks and tools must take better 
account of reuse if the EU wants to attain a circular economy and reduce waste generation. 
With a clear differentiation between different kinds of packaging  (i.e. differentiation 
between reusable t ransport packaging and one -way transport packaging) , EPR could 
facilitate an increased (re)use of reusable packaging, prevent the amount of waste and 
promote circular business models. 
 
Reusable transport packaging should not be subject to EPR fees given their inherent strength 
and reuse characteristics, therefore reducing (the use of one-way) packaging in general. The 
Coalition would therefore support the establishment of either an exemption for reusable 
transport packaging from an EPR contribution or, if such would not be created, establishing 
a system in which reusable packaging will only be liable once to EPR fees, i.e. only when the 
reusable (transport) packaging is put on the market for the first time. 
 
Closed Pallet Pooling is leading the way 
 
Closed pallet pooling companies have been operating fully circular even before the concept 
of circular economy was introduced by the European Commission. Through their pooled 
reusable packaging business model, they have been contributing efficiently for ye ars to 
packaging waste reduction. The members of the Coalition would therefore welcome the 
opportunity to share their experience and best practices (as well as challenges to be 
addresses) with the European Commission, with the view of constructively shapin g the 
circular economy and waste management legislative framework.

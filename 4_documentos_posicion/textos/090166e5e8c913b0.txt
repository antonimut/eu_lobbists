European beverage carton industry views in light of the call for evidence 
on the environmental impact of waste management – revision of EU 
waste framework 
The Association for Beverage Cartons and the Environment (ACE), together with its members 
BillerudKorsnäs, Elopak, SIG Combibloc, Stora Enso and Tetra Pak, welcomes the European Commission’s 
initiative’s objective to improve the overall environmental outcome of waste management, and improve 
separate waste collection to yield optimal recycling results.  
General comments 
1. Enhancing waste prevention  
ACE fully supports the European Commission’s objective to enhance waste prevention, preparation for re-
use and recycling via an enhanced cooperation with Member States and enforcement. The review of the 
Waste Framework Directive must contribute to:  
1. Strengthening the harmonisation and enforcement of EU regulatory requirements to improve 
recycling 
2. Tackling insufficient collection of packaging waste to achieve the EU waste recycling targets 
3. Avoiding a narrow focus on end-of-life that risks undermining packaging’s contribution to food waste 
prevention 
2. Waste hierarchy: re-use and recycling should be allowed to co-exist in a complementary way 
We endorse the objective of safeguarding the waste hierarchy, and implement measures supporting waste 
prevention. Turning waste into resources for a circular economy is key in waste prevention; this can be 
achieved both with recycling and re-use systems, which are equally important in this process. Thus, the 
Commission should refrain from imposing horizontal targets.  
3. High-quality recycling 
While expanding the role of EPR schemes in attaining waste prevention objectives, waste reduction efforts 
should be focused on minimising the amount of residual waste generated. We acknowledge the need for 
recycling operations to yield high value outputs. Should the new WFD introduce a new definition of high-
quality recycling, we propose the following:  
• High-quality recycling is a recovery operation by which waste materials are processed into new 
products or materials that can replace virgin raw materials. High-quality recycling is supported by 
design for recycling criteria, collection, and sorting to ensure that the materials are collected, sorted 
in separate streams, with sufficient quality output for secondary use. 
4. Policy coherence 
The European beverage carton industry recommends that EU policy makers ensure the necessary policy 
coherence between the forthcoming WFD, expected in Q2 2023, is carried out in line with the revision of 
the Packaging and Packaging Waste Directive (PPWD) which will precede it on the EU’s legislative agenda 
(2022).  
Specific comments  
A. Measures to decrease waste generation 
1. Strengthened EPR  
The Commission intends to expand the role of EPR schemes in attaining waste-prevention objectives. EPR 
fees should be based on a net-cost principle, meaning that the costs of collection, sorting and treatment 
are covered by EPR fees (in case they are not covered by the value of the secondary raw material obtained) 
and used only for the material the fee is paid for (to avoid cross -subsidisation). This way, material 
recyclability can be improved, as recyclability is a pre-condition for obtaining a valuable secondary raw 
material after collection and sorting.

ACE also notes the Commission’s intention to further explain how to improve compliance with extended

• Policy ask 1: Upcoming Commission Guidelines on EPR should recommend to Member States that

2. The role of packaging in preventing food waste loss   
A too narrow focus on the end-of-life can undermine the role of packaging of preventing product and food 
waste and supporting resilient EU food systems. By protecting the product, packaging also prevents waste 
of raw materials and natural resources that contributed to be making of the product therein. Increasing the 
shelf life of a product has a direct impact on food waste, helping to reduce it from 8% to 3% (documented 
cases). 1  
Products packaged in beverage cartons can be stored up between 6 to 12 months without refrigeration, 
removing the need for added preser vatives, saving additional energy, and eliminating carbon emissions 
associated with powering the refrigeration process.2 EU regulators, in line with FAO recommendations3, 
should promote appropriate measures for increasing shelf-life and improving packaging and storage to 
extend consumption time and to reduce food loss.  
• Policy ask 2: While important to continue the improvement of packaging sustainability, any measure 
intending to eliminate or reduce the use of packaging must be thoroughly assessed to avoid the creation 
of additional product and food waste or negative implications for consumer health and safety. 
3. Role of packaging in supporting resilient food supply systems  
In a recent publication, the UN FAO stressed4 that “Packaging plays an important role in not only reducing 
food loss and waste but by extension boosting food security, nutrition and livelihoods too. A circular 
approach to how we package our food can make a big difference to the food security of our communities 
and the health of our planet”.  
Packaging role in securing the resilience of food systems has also been highlighted in the European 
Commission’s Contingency plan for ensuring food supply and food security in times of crisis.  
• Policy ask 3: The role of packaging in supporting a resilient and secure EU food system should be 
recognized in the upcoming WFD.  
• Policy ask 4: The food processing and packaging sector is included in discussions to support food loss 
and waste reduction goals. 
B. Measures to increase separate waste collection  
1. Harmonising separate waste collection and sorting  
Increased recycling rates are inherently a result of well-functioning collection systems. Thus, the paramount 
role of collection as a pre-condition for recycling should be taken into account.  
Sorting is an essential part of the overall recycling process, which should be further strengthened as part 
of the upcoming revision. ACE endorses collection systems where the material is sorted into fractions at the 
source (e.g. households), and which prevents recyclable materials from ending up in the residual waste.  
2. Supporting national waste management systems that work 
High recycling rates depend on a plurality of factors which need to be accounted for in the upcoming 
proposal, among which, the national specificities of waste management systems. Member State with well-
functioning sorting and collection systems should be able to continue applying collection models that work

Ensuring convenience for end-users is fundamental to improve collection and recycling. We also call on 
policy makers to ensure the right environment for citizens, Member States and the industry to exchange 
best practices and learn from top-performers, while addressing existing and emerging gaps at local, regional 
and EU level.  
3. Banning the landfill of recyclable packaging  
Furthermore, packaging that is recyclable should be adequately sorted, collected and recycled, avoiding 
that they are landfilled. Consequently, we believe that landfilling of recyclable packaging waste should be 
banned. The landfill restrictions that are to come into effect only in 2035, as defined in Directive 1999/31/EC 
on the landfill of waste, should be anticipated.  
• Policy ask 5: We recommend that the new WFD puts forward measures to broaden and harmonise 
collection and sorting processes while taking into account of national specificities, increase consumer 
awareness via labelling (or pictograms) and ban the landfill of recyclable packaging.   
4. Disallow separate waste collection exemptions  
ACE agrees on the necessity to prioritize harmonised separate collection by revisiting the scope of 
derogations provided for in Article 10(3) WFD. Separate collection ensures that fibres are not lost and return 
back in the paper loop and it enhances the quality of fibres by avoiding soiling of material from the source 
to the sorting line. In this sense, Mem ber States should not continue to benefit from exemptions from 
separate collection unless separate collection does not deliver the best environmental outcome. EU market 
actors which continue to bring forward sustainable and innovative collection and recycl ing technologies 
should be supported.  
• Policy ask 6: EU policy makers should strengthen the obligations for Member States to separate waste 
by disallowing any exemptions in this sense under Art 10(3) WFD.  If, however, environmental reasons 
can justify an exemption this should be acceptable.  
 
 
Summary of our policy asks 
1. Future EU policy on waste should strengthen and harmonise separate waste collection, for example, 
by setting a mandatory collection for recycling target for beverage cartons.  
2. The forthcoming revision of the WFD should be aligned with the policy objectives and provisions of 
other EU legislation governing waste management, e.g. the PPWD.  
3. The new WFD should put harmonise separate waste collection, for example, via labelling / pictograms 
to help consumers sort waste more efficiently. We also recommend that further standards on sorting 
are taken forward at EU level.  
4. The new WFD should ensure that all Member States separate waste by removing existing legislative 
exemptions under Art 10(3) WFD. 
5. Any measure intending to eliminate or reduce the use of packaging must be thoroughly assessed to 
avoid the creation of additional product and food waste or negative implications for consumer health 
and safety. 
6. The role of packaging in preventing food loss and supporting the resilience and security of EU food 
systems should be recognised.  
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
About ACE 
The Alliance for Beverage Cartons and the Environment (ACE) provides a European platform for beverage 
carton manufacturers and their paperboard suppliers to benchmark and profile beverage cartons as a safe, 
circular, and sustainable packaging solution with low-carbon benefits.

EuroCommerce feedback for the call for evidence for 
an impact assessment about the revision of the Waste 
Framework Directive 
 
Our sector welcomes the planned revision of the Waste Framework Directive  (WFD). Retailers and 
wholesalers support the circular economy and work towards reducing waste in general, from 
packaging and food waste to other waste streams like textiles and waste from electrical and electronic 
equipment. With the revision of the WFD there i s a big opportunity to make a positive impact on 
climate and resource use by adopting circular business models  and create a real Single Market for 
Secondary Raw Materials. In order to use more recycled content in new products, our members and 
their suppliers depend on a steady supply of secondary raw materials in sufficient quality and volume.  
 
We support the objectives of the planned revision and are ready to further engage with the 
Commission on this topic, because the roadmap lacks a detailed descriptio n of the envisaged 
measures and to provide further feedback, we would need more information. We call on the 
Commission to: 
 
• Pursue harmonisation and improved interoperability  of waste legislation in general and in 
particular of all elements of Extended Producer Responsibility  schemes ( scope definition, 
reporting, eco-modulation etc);  
• Ensure better enforcement and cooperation between Member States and authorities; 
• Use the revi sion to address the recent proliferation of national labelling and information 
requirements to be placed on the packaging of products  and ensure a common approach in the 
EU; 
• Make sure that new Extended Producer Responsibility schemes for new waste streams like textiles 
are harmonised from the beginning and share common principles and definitions;  
• Ensure coherence of the Waste Framework Directive with all legislative proposals related to 
products and waste, in particular the Sustainable Products Initiative,  the Waste Shipment 
Regulation and the Packaging and Packaging Waste Directive and the food waste reduction targets 
that are supposed to be introduced together with the revision of the Waste Framework Directive.  
 
1. We support the initiative ’s objectives to reduce waste generation 
and to improve separate waste collection to yield optimal recycling 
results and call for a real Single Market for waste 
The Waste Framework Directive allows Member States to implement their own, sometimes diverging 
national waste legislation within the EU-wide framework. We experience different requirements and 
rules for Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) schemes in the  EU. These scattered EPR schemes 
with different scope, definitions and reporting requirements (incl. eco -bonus/eco-malus) must be 
harmonized in order to support circular product development. The Waste Framework Directive has 
an important role to play here. In addition, when companies want to ship waste and waste intended 
for reuse from one Member State to another, they encounter difficulties and most waste management 
is handled at local level, which leads to locally differentiating rules. While such an appr oach allows 
greater adaptability, it also creates disparities among Member States.

A Single Market for waste  and waste intended for reuse  means that products, their packaging and 
waste can circulate freely in the EU. If a material ceases to be waste and obtains again the status of a 
product/secondary raw material in one country but not in another, this greatly hamper s the Single 
Market. The WFD revision is key opportunity to address such differences and should revisit the waste 
definition to ensure more harmonisation. 
 
Retailers and Wholesalers think that for the circular economy to work, all the incentives and 
requirements from product design to the end-of-life stage must be coordinated and aligned. Only with 
a true single market for waste and waste intended for reuse and more alignment and harmonisation 
of various EU regulations, ranging from the Waste Shipment Regulation, requirements for Extended 
Producer Responsibility scheme s to the end -of-waste criteria, it will be possible to ensure  efficient 
waste management in the EU that will help reach ing high recycling rates and the uptake of recycled 
material in new products. 
 
Therefore, the Commission  should aim to enabl e a well -functioning market for secondary raw 
materials, which should include free movement of waste intended for reuse or recycling and recycled 
material within the Single Market. To ensure the latter this initiative should be aligned with the 
ongoing revision of the Waste Shipment Regulation.  
 
2. The policy options should lead to more harmonisation and support 
the transition towards more reuse, recycling and a circular economy 
EuroCommerce supports that for the revision of the Waste Framework Directive measures to prevent 
waste and to strengthen EP R schemes for specific waste streams.  Regarding the measures that will 
also be examined in the context of this initiative, we would like to contribute the following: 
Policy option: Promote full implementation of the provisions on waste prevention, 
preparation for re-use and recycling (e.g. enhanced cooperation with Member States 
and enforcement) 
Member States are responsible for enforcing the existing rules for waste management but changing 
market dynamics and non-cooperation between authorities lead sometimes to a lack of enforcement. 
This disadvantages law-abiding companies who put much effort into ensuring compliance. 
 
EPR schemes have the potential to offer  cost-effective ways to fulfil the obligations, but for them to 
work properly we need proper implementation and enforcement.  A harmonisation of EPR schemes is 
being supported by retailers and wholesalers as well as better enforcement and cooperation between 
authorities like networks for authorities and Producer Responsibility Organisations to exc hange 
information and best practices, more controls and a central website like the single digital gateway 
(one-stop-shop-approach) to simply compliance for companies. 
Policy option: Provide additional guidance, e.g. clarify and explain in detail waste -
prevention provisions; explain how separate collection can improve waste re -use, 
preparation for re -use and recycling and how to improve citizen participation in 
separate collection; and explain how to improve compliance with extended producer 
responsibility (EPR) requirements 
Retailers and wholesalers support this policy option because only with a Single Market for Secondary 
Raw Materials we can reach the goal of a Circular Economy . To promote circular economy principles 
in the whole EU in a similar way and to  avoid distorting the Single Market more harmonisation and

increased interoperability is needed.  However, we still experience differences and diverging 
implementation, especially of EPR  requirements, in the Member States. Therefore, we welcome 
additional guidance and harmonised requirements and in addition would also recommend: 
 
• A clear definition of reusable/recyclable is needed for textiles: 
− No specific criteria exist for an objective classification of discarded textiles into reusable 
or recyclable.  
− This lack of definitions directly hampers a homogeneous classification of garments  and 
the application of the waste hierarchy. 
− Once waste is classified as reusable or recyclable, rules for shipment  and exports are 
different regarding controls, notifications and assurance. 
• Implementing acts should be adopted to ensure harmonisation of the general minimum 
requirements listed in Article 8a of the Waste Framework Directive. 
• Harmonising end-of-waste criteria EU-wide and creating such criteria for additional waste streams 
like textiles or mattresses. In addition, clarifying when the waste status can be removed earlier in 
the value chain e.g. at point of separate collection, is needed . From a circular economy 
perspective, carefully considering when something should effectively not be considered waste is  
focal. 
• Further harmonisation related to EPR, for example on issues like scope of EPR schemes, reporting 
requirements and criteria for eco-modulation, as well as facilitating the exchange of best practices 
between Member States to avoid fragmentation.  
• Any future eco-modulation requirements must be consistent with the anticipated product design 
requirements under the Sustainable Product Initiative, to create consistent incentives for more 
sustainable product design across the whole EU Single Market. 
Policy option: Reduce waste generation by introducing overall and/or product -
specific prevention measures, including targets on waste reduction and/or residual 
waste reduction and expanding the role of EPR schemes in attaining waste-prevention 
objectives 
In the context of textile waste, one needs to keep in mind that textiles are diverse and there is no one-
size-fits-all solution. The different types of products (clothing, footwear, etc.) need to be considered 
individually because issues as sorting requirements or reuse preparation may differ between different 
types of products. In the case of reuse specific challenges must be addressed, e.g. for footwear aspects 
like comfort and consumer hygiene concerns need to be considered. This needs to be taken into 
account for waste prevention measures, but also Extended Producer Responsibility and end-of-life.  
 
When targets on waste reduction  are considered, for the case of textiles such measures need to be 
prepared together with and take into account the EU Textile Strateg y. The upcoming EU Textile 
Strategy will address the whole life cycle of textiles and very likely touch upon issues like eco -design, 
Extended Producer Responsibility or support for circular business models that will be important for 
the area of waste prevention. All the measures need to be designed with the others in mind in order 
to avoid inconsistencies and to create synergies. Such targets tend to give businesses more flexibility 
how to reach the reduction  and in the case of textiles it would be necessar y to specify in legislation 
what kind of textile would be in the scope of this measure.  Any waste prevention efforts should not 
hamper the development of the recycling infrastructure. 
 
EuroCommerce, as a member of the Commission’s expert group on eco -design, the Eco -design and 
Energy Labelling Consultation Forum, is a huge support of the eco -design approach that was applied 
successfully to many different energy-related products and lead to significant improvement regarding 
energy consumption and consumer information about the energy performance of products. We 
support this approach because the products -specific regulations allow for more targeted and for

In the context of the revision of the Waste Framework Directive it is also foreseen to introduce food 
waste reduction targets. On this matter we would like to comment that s uccess will be anchored to 
the involvement of all the actors of the supply at their level of responsibility . Retail and consumers 
cannot bear the responsibility alone for reducing food waste - more so when retail and wholesale 
account for 5% of total food waste. EuroCommerce recommends relying on international protocol and 
guidance such as the WRI Food Loss and Waste protocol for measurement of food waste. Any data 
collection should however allow some flexibility.  Concerning targets, EuroCommerce favours a 
harmonised approach, while keeping the flexibility to adapt to local needs.  We would like to also 
highlight the importance of policy enablers to attain the objectives of the future legislation: supporting 
awareness ra ising campaigns for consumers; amend legislation according to new purchasing 
behaviour such as frozen food legislation to adapt to online purchases or limit the administrative 
requirements to facilitate food donations; and supporting the implementation of policy and fiscal 
incentives to promote further food donations, like VAT reductions. 
Policy option: Improve the separate collection of waste by clarifying and/or 
restricting the scope of derogations provided for in Article 10(3) WFD, introducing 
minimum requirements for source segregation and separate collection of waste that 
facilitate separate collection of used items for re -use and preparation for re-use and 
high-quality recycling of waste 
We are not able to comment regarding the scope of derog ations provided for in Article 10(3) WFD, 
but we agree in general that actions to improve re-use and high-quality recycling of waste are positive 
and often needed. Separate collection of specific waste streams will be essential to close the loop and 
allow for higher levels of reuse and high-quality recycling.  
 
Furthermore, we are concerned about the recent proliferation of national labelling and information 
requirements to be placed on the packaging of products – also regarding for information of separate 
collection/recycling of products at end of life.  Our sector fully endorses improving waste collection 
and w e know that consumers need to receive adequate information about the products they are 
discarding/delivering for recycling. Unfortunately, with diverging national measures that are planned 
or even already implemented we see more uncertainty, confusion and in some cases even more 
waste/use of material because packaging needs to be re -labelled or even discarded completely. 
Furthermore these new requireme nts lead to high costs for the companies that need to change 
labelling, in our experience for a single medium sized country like Spain it will cost just one company 
20 million Euro to change the labels. We are calling on the Commission to address this and ensure a 
common approach to avoid any harm to the goals set for sustainability and environment and to 
prevent a serious disruption of the Single Market.  
Policy option: Reinforce the polluter pays principle by expanding EPR schemes to 
other product categories, like textiles and oils, and improving enforcement of EPR 
requirements, in particular for products sold online to customers in the EU 
While the Commission is looking into expanding EPR schemes and improving enforcement of EPR 
requirements, further harmonisation needs also be undertaken. Analogous to the Commission 
Implementing regulations and decisions 2019/290 and  2019/2193 formats for registration and 
reporting could be harmonised. Furthermore, we see with the introduction of eco-modulation for EPR 
fees, urgent  need for EU-wide harmonisation  and to avoid additional fragmentation and 
contradictions.  
 
In the case of textiles, we see an enormous opportunity in establishing an EU-wide framework on EPR

to ensure that the textile sector can become circular. Considering the separate collection obligation 
of textile waste by 2025, Europe has an enormous press ure to build a competitive market for 
secondary raw materials. Many Member States are currently developing national EPR schemes for 
textiles with different scopes, responsibilities, and types of products covered (e.g., France, Italy, 
Sweden, the Netherlands). Therefore, in our view it would be very important that for this new area of 
EPR schemes the Commission provides harmonisation . An EU -wide framework on EPR for textiles 
should include: a set of common principles, a clear scope definition, harmonised reporting and eco-
modulation requirements and allocation of responsibilities among all relevant stakeholders, a robust 
mechanism of mo nitoring, control and surveillance; proportional financing and a transparency and 
traceability system. The Commission should facilitate the exchange of best practices between Member 
States themselves and with relevant stakeholders to avoid fragmentation.  
 
Similar to the Waste Framework Directive a dedicated Textile Waste Framework Directive should be 
explored to ensure common rules and criteria about secondary raw materials, reutilization and end -
of-waste status. This would not only help to prevent waste but also support recycling of textile waste. 
The free movement of waste purposed for sorting, preparation for reuse or recycling, both within the 
EU and globally is important to reach the goals of the Green Deal and the new Circular Economy Action 
Plan.

1. Leasing  
 
The ASF, together with Leaseurope, the European association of leasing industry, consider that 
the Commission’s proposals on leasing exposures should at least remain unchanged. Some 
provisions could yet to be improved. 
 
Introduction of the proposal 
In a paragraph entitled “Enabling clauses for leasing exposures and credit insurance ”, the 
Commission recognises that “a  high level of expertise and risk management has been 
developed by institutions in the EU in the area of leasing…”.  
The ASF  is satisfied that the importance of leasing in the financing of the EU economy is  
underlined. Almost 1 SME on 2 in the EU has experi enced leasing. The volume of new leases in 
the EU in 2020 is 352 Bn€ and is growing every year. 
 
Art 495c 
The ASF and Leaseurope ha ve been advocating for many years on the lower risk profile of 
leasing compared to a regular loan since the lessor (the lender) owns the asset that is financed.  
It is partly recognized in Article 495c with two proposed measures: 
 
1. a transitional arrangement of the haircut on the value of the «  collateral » for the 
calculation of the LGD in the IRB approach.  
 
“1. By way of derogation from Article 230, the applicable value of Hc [the haircut ] 
corresponding to ‘other physical collateral’ for the exposures referred to in Article 199(7) 
where the property leased corresponds to the ‘other physical collateral’ type of funded 
credit protection, shall be the value of Hc for ‘other physical collateral’ provided for in 
Article 230(2), Table 1, multiplied by the following factors: (a) 50 % during the period from 
1 January 2025 to 31 December 2027; (b) 80 % during the period from 1 January 2028 to 31 
December 2028; (c) 100 % during the period from 1 January 2029 to 31 December 2029. “ 
 
This part answers the leasing industry’s concerns and should remain unchanged. 
 
2. A mandate to the EBA for proposing in a dedicated report an adequate calibration for 
leasing exposures, but which perimeter is limited to the IRB approach. It seems not to be 
enough since it would exclude from the prudential adjustments those leasing institutions 
using the standardized approach (SA-CR). It is estimated that 70 % of leasing institutions 
regulated and supervised in the EU are currently using the SA-CR.

2. Factoring  
 
The ASF, together with EU federation, the European federation  of factoring industry, consider 
that the Commission’s proposals on factoring should at least  remain unchanged . Some 
provisions could be written in a more precise manner. 
 
Introduction of the proposal 
In a paragraph entitled “Enabling clauses for leasing exposures and credit insurance”, the 
Commission recognises that “ a high level of expertise and risk management has been 
developed by institutions in the EU […] in the use of credit insurance, in particular for trade 
finance purposes. 
Factoring is part of the trade finance industry.

Article 506 
The ASF, together with EU federation, has been asking for many years that credit insurance be 
considered as a credit risk mitigation technique. Credit insurance is widely used by factoring 
institutions to reduce the credit risk exposure.  
So, we welcome new article 506 which mandates the EBA to analyze credit insurance as a 
credit risk mitigation technique for the SA approach and the IRB foundation approach.  
This article should at least remain unchanged and c ould be improved as regards the  
publication deadline of the report of the EBA proposed by 31st December 2026. Considering 
the role played by factoring for the short -term financing of corporates  in the EU , especially 
SMEs, we estimate that the EBA report should be published by the end of 2025. 
We propose that the article should be reworded as follows: 
 
Article 506 Credit risk – credit insurance 
By 31 December 2026 [2025], EBA shall report to the Commission on the eligibility and 
use   of policy insurance as credit risk mitigation techniques and on the 
appropriateness of  the associated risk parameters referred to in Part Three, Title II, 
Chapter 3 and 4. 
Based on the report by EBA, the Commission shall be empowered to amend this  
Regulation by adopting a delegated act, where appropriate, in accordance with 
Article 462, to amend the treatment applicable to credit insurance referred to in Part 
Three, Title II.’;

3. Guarantees on home loans: prudential equivalence with mortgages 
 
Prudential equivalence between mortgages and guaranteed residential loans  has been 
included in December 2017 Basel III finalization agreements with the condition that the 
guarantor be submitted to capital requirements « comparable » with CRR.  
 
Basel III agreements – Art. §60 P.18 Footnote 35 
In certain jurisdictions, the majority of  bank loans to individuals for the purchase of 
residential property are not provided as mortgages in legal form. Instead, they are 
typically provided as loans that are guaranteed by a highly rated monoline guarantor 
that is required to repay the bank in full if the borrower defaults, and where the bank 
has legal right to take a mortgage on the property in the event that the guarantor 
fails. These loans may be treated as residential real estate exposures (rather than 
guaranteed loans) if the following additional conditions are met:  
(i) the borrower shall be contractually committed not to grant any mortgage lien 
without the consent of the bank that granted the loan;  
(ii) the guarantor shall be either a bank or a financial institution subject to capital 
requirements comparable to those applied to banks or an insurance undertaking.  
(iii) the guarantor shall establish a fully -funded mutual guarantee fund or equivalent 
protection for insurance undertakings to absorb credit risk losses, whose calibration 
shall be periodically reviewed by its supervisors and subject to periodic stress testing; 
and  
(iv) the bank shall be contractually and legally allowed to take a mortgage on the 
property in the event that the guarantor fails.

Article 75 e) 
The EC proposal recognizes the principle of the equivalence in its article 4-point 75e): 
(75e) ‘exposure secured by residential property’, or ‘exposure secured by a mortgage on 
residential property’, or ‘exposure secured by residential property collateral’, or ‘exposure 
secured by residential immovable property’, means an exposure secured by a mortgage 
on residential property or secured by any other     mechanisms other than mortgages but 
which are economically equivalent to mortgages and recognised as collateral on 
residential property under the applicable national law setting out the conditions for the 
establishment of those  mechanisms; 
 
Article 108 4) 
Yet in Article 108 4) e), an undue goldplating prevents the disposition from being operational. 
The conditions to be respected by the guarantor differ from Basel III requirements  that were 
internationally agreed in 2017. The Basel III condition that the guarantor is submitted to capital 
requirements « comparable » to CRR becomes in the European Commission proposal a 
condition that it is submitted to capital requirements « at least equivalent » to CRR.  
The result is that the equivalence cannot be operational. I n France for instance , the main 
guarantors cannot be credit institutions, since they do not collect deposits. But they are 
financing companies strictly regulated and supervised by national competen t authorities and 
have to respect « comparable » capital requirements. 
 
It is essential to come  back to the Basel agreement ’s wording that allows the prudential 
equivalence between guaranteed home loans and mortgages.

4. Ancillary undertakings 
 
Leasing and factoring are both activities subject to different regulation and supervision regimes 
among EU Member states.  In some EU countries, these activities have to be  conducted by 
credit institutions and financing companies  and are duly regulated and supervised . In other 
Member states  these activities  may be conducted under commercial regime, without any 
prudential framework. 
 
 
6 
 
From this perspective, Article 4 (18) 1 that defines “ancillary services undertakings”  seems not 
clear enough. The concern of the ASF is the articulation between i) this new article 4 (18), ii) the 
definition of “financial  sector entity” (ex point 27 of art 4 in CRR2 that has been cancelled  in 
the EC proposal) and iii) the fact that “financlial leases” and “factoring” remain listed activities 
in Annex 1 of CRD  that lists the activities in the field of the reglulation. 
 
We consider at least that the articulation between Article 4 (18) and Annex 1 of CRD should be 
further precised.

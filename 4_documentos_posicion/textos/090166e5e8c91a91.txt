revision of the Waste Framework Directive 
 
The European Commission has adopted the Circular Economy Action Plan in March 2020 - one of 
the main blocks of the European Green Deal, Europe’s new agenda for sustainable growth.  
The built environment requires vast amounts of natural resources and accounts for about 50% of all 
extracted raw materials. The construction sector alone is responsible for over 3 6% of the EU’s total 
waste generation1. Consequently, the EU Green Deal and the Circular Economy Action Plan  rightly 
identified the importance of recovery targets for construction and demolition waste to increase 
circularity  also in the light  the EU ”Renovation Wave”. In particular, the  Commission made a 
commitment to ‘pay special attention to insulation materials, which generate a growing waste 
stream’.2 The Chemicals Strategy for Sustainability also included commitments from the to ‘support 
investments in sustainable innovations that can decontaminate waste streams, increase safe 
recycling and reduce the export of waste (…); develop methodologies for chemical risk assessment 
that take into account the whole life cycle of substances, materials and products’. 
The mineral wool insulation industry welcomes the revision of the Waste Framework Directive, and 
is committed to take its full responsibility and play its pro -active part in the transition towards a 
circular economy. 
The Waste Hierarchy – reuse and recycling 
Our industry supports reuse and recycling as the top priority routes. For the mineral wool insulation 
products, it is proven that they remain durable in terms of thermal and mechanical properties at the 
moment of a renovation. For this it has to be highlighted that a critical part  are the deconstruction 
skills. For this, a proper training and mandate for deconstructio n practices shall be prioritized  and 
be a separate part of the Construction and Demolition Waste Protocol. The same should apply for 
the early sorting as close as possible to the waste source and their appropriate handing.  
At the time of a deconstruction or a renovation, it is necessary to qualify materials f or future reuse3: 
what are their physical properties, acoustic, reaction to fire, their state of wear, etc. The operations 
of reuse must then be the subject to a technical control  for the certification of those  products (ref. 
CE marking - CPR). It is a question of checking that the materials are correctly integrated to the 
work, with a good level of risk (solidity, ten -year certificate, accessibility, thermal, acoustic and 
energetic performance). All these aspects have to be addressed through a  comprehensive 
regulatory framework. The same should apply for the re-manufacturing practices.

In addition to th e reuse and re -manufacturing, recycling of post -consumer waste is a must to 
achieve the objectives of the Green Deal and the Circular Economy Action Plan . The mineral wool 
insulation industry is committed to these objectives and our products  are increasingly being 
recycled. However, we need a policy framework that will better enable recycling. Our challenges 
include: 
 EU Member States apply different waste codes for mineral wool insulation waste 
 Divergent national practices to the classification of our waste  and the interface between 
waste and chemicals legislation 
 Lack of implementation of the provisions on notification in WFD Art. 7 
We would encourage the Commission to clarify these issues for mineral wool insulation products by 
for example updating the Technical guidance on the classification of waste.  
Eurima supports the prioritization of the Waste Hierarchy, for which landfill should be the last 
possible route for disposal of mineral wool waste. At the same time due to continued landfill options 
against relatively low landfill prices it is difficult for alternative recovery options to become 
economically viable.  Banning the landfilling of products that could be a significant source of 
secondary raw material such as mineral wool insulation(while not favoring the landfilling of products 
that cannot be recycled at the end of their life ) or increasing landfill prices would create the right 
economic incentive to enable growing recovery-capacity. The WFD should clearly set definitions on 
recycled content, recyclability, secondary raw materials etc.  
 
EURIMA’s requests to the Commission are: 
o Increase the costs of landfill suppress for recyclable products and ban landfill 
progressively. 
o Clear EU wide applicable guidance on the handling of post-consumer Mineral Wool 
insulation waste. 
o Mainstream deconstruction practices instead of demolition, in order to ensure proper 
waste quality for further recycling 
o Set material specific recovery/recycling targets mineral wool 
Until the recycling center: 
o Consider the destination of the shipment, whether it is destined for landfill or 
recovery. In case of recycling, the Waste Framework Directive and the Waste 
Shipments Regulation should provide clear guidelines that are EU-wide, simplified 
and faster. 
o In any other case, there should be clear guidelines between the Member States on 
the treatment and recovery of these shipments of waste.   
o A database of certified/permitted recycling plants for the operations of recyclable 
materials including mineral wool waste; 
o Fast track permits should apply for cross-border transportation, the same for sorting 
and storage of such waste. 
o Any proof/documentation needed should require a digital format that will be easy to 
be tracked down by the authorities/stakeholders; 
o Facilitate and reduce administrative permitting requirements combined with a 
 
 
commitment to value-chain education.  
o Maintain existing worker protection requirements in any case of type of waste across 
the handling, sorting, collection and treatment process. 
o Long-lasting shipment permits to recycling plants with EU wide criteria. 
o EU Harmonization of the criteria on Shipments Financial guarantees, allowing better 
terms when the waste is destined for recycling;  
o In the context of the further d evelopment of circular economy across EU, 
recycling projects with pilot phase will need to be set -up. The legislation (EU & 
national) should support such development through a temporary (pro -rata of a 
defined pilot phase plan) minimized procedure for cross  border transportation of 
waste. The principle would be that this is only valid for a limited quantity and 
time period; both to be defined in advance. 
The quantity of insulation products will continue to increase significantly in order to reach EU 
climate neutrality by 2050. The MW industry is committed to increase recycling and re -use, it is 
one lever to reduce carbon footprint and resource used per square meter insulated. 
To allow the maximum potential of circularity the interface between Chemicals, Produ cts and 
Waste Regulations (e.g. CLP, CPR and WSR) should act in a harmonizing approach and not 
as an obstacle.   
We remain at your disposal for any further exchange on the points mentioned above and we 
look forward to work with the European Commission , other policymakers and stakeholders on 
the revision of the Waste Framework Directive.

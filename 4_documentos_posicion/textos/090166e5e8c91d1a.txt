PGNiG POSITION AS REGARDS THE REVISON OF EU WASTE FRAMEWORK 
 
PGNiG welcomes th e opportu nity to  contribute to the Commission’s preparation of an impact 
assessment for the revision of EU waste framework. 
 
 
HIGHLIGHTS OF THE POSITION 
 Aims of the  initiative should be extended in order to include a promotion of a priority 
principle for secondary anthropogenic raw materials which support the protection of natural 
resources. Essentially, the principle means the replacement of natural resources with 
materials of anthropogenic origin when it is justified from sustainable and economic 
perspective. 
 Regulatory measures should include simplifying procedure of granting the status of a by -
product through a modification of a requirement concerning the certainty of further use of 
an object or a substance. 
 
1. Priority principle for the use of secondary anthropogenic resources 
The proposed review of EU legislation should consider the introduction of the priority principle 
for the use of anthropogenic secondary resources which support the conservation of natural 
resources. Essentially, the principle means the replacement of natural resources with 
materials of anthropogenic origin  when it is justified from sustainable and economic 
perspective.  
The implementation of the priority principle for the use of secondary anthropogenic resources 
could take place through the introduction of appropriate provisions mobilizing investors  at 
the stage of environmental im pact assessment of the project, as well as at the stage of 
obtaining a construction permit  to analyze  the possibility of using w aste or non -waste by-
products –as an alterna tive to using natural resources – with priority given to secondary 
anthropogenic resources. In this way, the principle of using secondary anthropogenic raw 
materials over natural resources would be ensured. 
In view of increasing usage of raw materials from waste there is a need for a synergy between 
various areas of economic life. A material that is a useless waste in one sector may be a 
valuable secondary raw material for another industry, replacing in this vein the use of natural 
raw material. T here are already technologies available in the EU that allow to redirect a 
material stream into the economic cycle. For example: the use of fly ash from coal combustion

2. Simplifying procedure of granting the status of a by-product 
The EU initiative to revise the waste framework does not address the potential of secondary 
raw materials from energy sector waste, in particular as regards combustion by-products from 
electricity and heat production. Such raw materials include ashes and slags, which are already 
used today for example in the production of building materials, in road-building mixtures, and 
in soil fertilization (in the case of biomass ashes).  
Unfortunately, sometimes the above -described use of ashes or slags is treated as a waste 
recovery process despite the fact that ashes and slags, without the need for any interference, 
meet European requirements for products applicable for  such use.  The Waste Framework 
Directive allows the treatment of ash es and slags as a by -product but due to the wording  
(‘further use of a given substance or object is certain ’) it is challenging to obtain the status of 
a by-product. In practice, competent authorities confirming the recognition of a substance as 
a by-product impose on applicants the obligation to submit long-term contracts for the use of 
the by-product as evidence of the certainty of its use. It seems that the rationale for such a 
requirement was to prove the existence of a market for the requested by-product.  
Therefore, we propose a modification of the requirement that the certainty of further use of 
the object or substance could be linked with a waste recovery agreement with the same 
properties as the object or substance for which the notification for recognition as a by-product 
is submitted. The consequence of such a change will be a significant simplification of the 
formal procedure for obtaining the status of a by-product while maintaining the objectives of 
this procedure. It is worth noting that the effective achievement of the status of a by-product 
for the produced waste will not only contribute to the implementation of the idea of a circular 
economy, in which waste from one industry sector becomes a product in another sector, and 
to replacing natural resources with secondary anthropogenic raw materials, but also to reduce 
the volume of waste generation.

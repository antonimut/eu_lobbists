Deutsche Telekom’s comments on Right 
to Repair (Public Consultation) 
 
1. BACKGROUND  
Deutsche Telekom sells smartphones, media receivers, routers  and tablets. These devices leave 
ecological footprints by consuming energy during production as well as  operation and by using  
resources for production and disposal. In case of smartphones, the share of energy consumption 
attributable to production is quit e large at around 80%. The objective should be to achieve the 
longest possible lifecycle. Important conditions are the highest possible reliability and easiest 
reparability in the event of a defect.  
 
With regard to the public consultation on the sustainable consumption of goods, we support the 
Commission's aim of extending the lifecycle of these devices by improving their repairability. This 
is already adequately regulated for the warranty period because it is already free of charge for 
customers (with the exception of defects caused by the customer). In this respect, the right to 
repair should only be about repairs that are chargeable to customers.  We advise against changes 
to the warranty period by amending the Sales of Goods Directive. 
 
To promote sustainable consumption and avoid unnecessary waste, the period of use should be 
extended by better product design. Moreover, repairs after warranty should be made easier and 
more attractive with binding specifications on manufacturers.  The focus on out -of-warranty 
defects is important because the customer already has a legal right to repair from the seller for a 
period of 24 mont hs. In these cases, sellers are highly dependend on manufacturers for the 
organization of repairs. The situation is different for damages after the warranty period: If there is 
no attractive repair offer, the consumer will probably buy a new device.   
 
2. RECOMMENDATIONS 
• Lifecycle: The service life of goods mentioned at the beginning has increased in recent 
years, but is still far below the physically possible level. The need for repair must be 
differentiated between produc ts, because there are considerable differences  between 
smartphones, fixed networks, media receivers or routers  (let alone completely different 
product groups). While smartphones are more fragile, there is hardly any need for repair 
for stationary devices. They are less mobile and do not have displays. Media receivers and 
routers - taken back as part of DTAG's leasing model - are already regularly checked and 
go back into circulation. In the case of smartphones, focus should be on  self-repair and

• Spare parts : Original parts for authorized repairs are sometimes diffic ult to obtain. 
Manufacturers use their market power to increase prices for spare parts or have repairs 
carried out only by authorized partners. The seller usually has no access to spare parts.  
 
• Manufacturers: Requirements should be directed at manufacture rs because they have 
control over spare parts, necessary expertise, and determine by the nature of their design 
how easy or difficult it is to replace certain parts. Subsequent parties in the supply chain 
have no control over decisions made by the manufact urer. Sellers, in particular cannot be 
held responsible for a right to repair as they would just be a non-value-adding intermediate. 
We reject any obligation on the part of the seller in this context. 
 
• Warranty: In the case of the warranty period, sustainability questions are not as urgent, 
given that defects are repaired and not exchanged anyway. In warranty, there are no costs 
for the consumer . Moreover, consumer protection is already harmonized across the EU .  
Therefore, differentiation between in- and out-of-warranty is of elementary importance. 
 
• Labels: A multitude of obligatory labels would not be helpful. Obligations should build on 
existing initiatives, be product-specific and as streamlined as possible. For mobile phones, 
Eco Rating would be well placed as a baseline as  it assesses ecological performance 
holistically and is addressing more than  just energy consumption criteria. Moreover, it is 
already up and running.  
 
• Modularity: Devices must be developed so that they can be repaired easily. Main parts 
such as batteries and displays must be technically distinct and interchangeable. There has 
to be a repair-friendly arrangement of components to be able to disassemble components 
that have to be changed frequently, such as the battery or display  or to plug-in or screw 
connections instead of gluing. A modular design is necessary.  
 
Lastly, w e see a right to repair best included in  the Eco  Design Directive as opposed  to being 
included in consumer law, given that product design and spare parts are of significant importance.

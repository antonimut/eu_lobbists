floor tiles, refractories, sanitaryware, table- & ornamentalware, technical ceramics, expanded clay and flower pots. The 
industry generates over 200,000 direct jobs and a production value of €26 billion in the EU.

Cerame-Unie’s feedback on the Proposal for protection from economic 
coercion by third countries 
 
 
 
9 March 2022, Brussels 
 
 
Cerame-Unie would like to thank the Commission for the opportunity to submit its feedback on the 
proposal for a Regulation on the protection from economic coercion by third countries. 
We welcome the introduction of  the so-called “anti-coercion” instrument as a powerful deterrence 
tool to contrast the increasing weaponi sation of trade. With more than one third of its production 
exported outside of the EU, the European ceramic industry is well positioned to understand the impact 
of coercive practices in global trade.  As emphasised in previous submissions, our industry has been 
exposed to such actions in the past in the form of threats to impose additional tariffs in the context of 
international trade disputes. 
As highlighted in the text of the Proposal , the introduction of this instrument would be particularly 
important given that the EU currently lacks a legislative framework for acting against economic 
coercion. If adopted, the Pr oposal would allow the EU to dissuade or offset coercive actions with 
effectiveness and rapidity.  
The Commission text currently foresees the anti-coercion instrument as a last resort mechanism. To 
avoid a dangerous escalation that would harm global trade and give rise to more protectionist policies, 
we believe that it is essential that the instrument is activated only when consultations have failed, and 
all other avenues are closed.  
 
It is equal ly important that stakeholders, including industry associations, be consulted before the 
adoption or amendment of anti-coercion response measures, as provided by Article 11 of the Proposal. 
This would allow due consideration of, inter alia, the impact of these measures on EU operators and 
business partners, and the administrative burden which may be occasioned by the measures. 
 
Finally, Cerame-Unie welcomes the anti-coercion instrument as a key tool for the achievement of the 
EU’s open strategic autonomy. As a signal to international partners that the EU is not willing to accept 
economic coercion , the tool would, ultimately, help defend an open and multilateral global trade 
environment.

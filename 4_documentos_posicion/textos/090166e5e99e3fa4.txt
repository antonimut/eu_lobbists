 
Clean Air Task Force comments to the review of the 
Regulation on emission standards for heavy-duty vehicles  
March 11, 2022 
Clean Air Task Force (CATF), a global non -profit organi sation working to 
safeguard against the worst impacts of climate change by catalysing the rapid 
development and deployment of low -carbon energy and other climate -
protecting technologies, welcomes the revision of Regulation (EU) 2019/1242 
setting CO2 emission performance standards for new heavy-duty vehicles.   
In 2019, t he transportation sector  was responsible for 30.9% 1 of total energy 
use in the EU  and is significantly lagging  behind in emissions decline when 
compared to other sectors. Specifically for heavy-duty trucking, the energy use 
results in 19%  of transportation sector emissions or 237 million metric tons 2 of 
the 2.3 billion metric tons 3 of CO2 emitted globally each year. Th e established 
EU rules and CO 2 reduction targets4 provide a foundation that Member States 
can use to reduce GHG emissions from heavy -duty vehicles, but stronger 
regulations will be needed to achieve the deep emissions reductions that will 
allow the EU to reach its climate goals.  
To that end, CO2 emission performance standards for new heavy-duty vehicles 
should be strengthened in order to:  
• properly incentivi se necessary investments into zero emission truck 
technology,  
• increase the scale of vehicle production, and  
• support the build -out of the infrastructure needed to facilitate deep 
decarbonisation. 
Regarding the development and deployment of specific technology, emissions-
free electric drivetrains are likely to be a powerful tool. Trucks powered by 
onboard battery energy storage (BEV), similar to the battery systems used in 
electric cars, will play a major role in trucking decarbonisation efforts, especially 
considering that the vast majority of cargo routes in the EU are 500 km or less.  
The regulation should also be revised to incentivise zero -emission vehicles 
(ZEVs) only and require that fleets be made up of all ZEVs no later than 2040. 
To help facilitate this transformation, a manufacturer -based ZEV sales 
requirement could be included in the regulation —for example, a policy that 
requires a certain percentage of all truck sales to be ZEV by 20355. Combining 
a mandate of this type with the above-mentioned stronger CO2 reduction targets 
would make this regulation more effective, paving the way to 100% ZEV fleets.

The heavy -duty vehicle regulation should also be expanded to small and 
medium trucks, even though  those vehicles contribute to a le sser extend  to 
GHG emissions.  
In addition, CATF notes that hydrogen fuel cell vehicle (FCEV) technology is 
likely to play an essential role in decarboni sing heavy trucking, so the fact that 
the current definition of ZEV in the regulation does not preclude FCEVs is a 
critically important attribute of the policy, especially for the difficult -to-electrify 
long haul routes. The utility of hydrogen -powered electric drivetrains in long -
haul applications is evident from recent estimates from the National Renewable 
Energy Laboratory (NREL), a US research laboratory, on the weight, range, and 
refuelling time for heavy trucks with FCEV drivetrains.  FCEV drivetrains a re 
similar to those in a BEV in many ways, but the FCEV drivetrain has a much 
smaller battery and includes several compressed gaseous hydrogen storage 
tanks and fuel cells that the BEV does not have.   
By allowing the use of FCEV drivetrains, the regulation can create flexibility that 
will likely result in environmental and economic benefits. According to recent 
NREL research, the advantages of the hydrogen fuel cell drivetrain lead to 
reduced costs for truck owners on certain routes. That reduced cost, in addition 
to the operational flexibility of hydrogen drivetrains, suggests that availability of 
hydrogen fuel cell electric trucks and fue lling infrastructure would be a 
significant decarbonisation promoter. Major manufacturers see this opportunity 
and are developing commercial products to pursue it. 6 In addition to long haul, 
other trucking segments may benefit from hydrogen in the EU. Drayage trucks 
operating near ports will be able to take advantage of synergies with marine 
decarbonisation efforts, where hydrogen and ammonia are expected to be 
critical zero-carbon fuels; while trucks with very high usage patterns can avoid 
the adverse effects of long charging times on their business. Together with long 
haul, there is an opportunity to leverage hydrogen in parts of the trucking sector 
to accelerate decarbonisation efforts.  
In summary, by strengthening the CO 2 reduction targets, strongly incentivising 
ZEVs with manufacturer sale requirements, including small and medium trucks 
in the regu lation, and allowing hydrogen to play a role where sensible, a 
modified regulation could significantly abate trucking sector emissions. 
Adopting these improvements will reduce the climate impact from trucking, 
helping the EU reach its climate goals.

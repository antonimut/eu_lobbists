The Slovak Soil Science Society (Societas pedologica slovaca) appreciates the adoption of the new Soil 
Strategy 2030 in 2021 (COM (2021) 699 final) as part of the European Green Agreement, as well as 
the proposals for a soil health law, which aims to have soils in good health by 2050 and make soil 
protection, sustainable use and restoration as the norm. The issue is in line with the 8 objectives of 
the EU Mission: A Soil Deal for Europe, the Farm to Fork initiative, the Common Agricultural Policy 
and other initiatives. 
The main threats to soil resources in Slovakia include: 
 Soil / land degradation: deterioration of quantitative and qualitative soil properties caused 
by inappropriate use of agricultural land, man-made industrial-urbanization activities, or 
inappropriate methods of forest management; 
 Land cover / soil sealing; 
 Global threats – degradation of quantitative and qualitative soil properties caused by global 
threats such as climate change, biodiversity loss, desertification, food security and others. 
Overview of the health of agricultural land in Slovakia (Kobza 2019): 
 potential water erosion 52% 
 actual water erosion 15% 
 potential wind erosion 4.7% 
 actual soil compaction 10% 
 potential soil compaction 25% 
 reduction of organic matter in the soil (not detected) 
 soil contamination (local and diffuse)  1% 
 soil salinization 0.15% 
 land take / soil sealing by construction and infrastructure, approx. 10%. 
In accordance with the call for evidence for an impact assessment, the aim of the Soil Health Act is to 
specify the conditions for healthy soil, set soil health indicators, address the requirements for 
sustainable soil management, which will contribute to reducing nutrient losses, respectively, reduction 
of the use of mineral fertilizers, determine conditions for soil monitoring, creation of soil databases 
and reporting.

1) Definition of soil health 
Soil health was defined in the Mission "A Soil Deal for Europe" as the continued soil capacity to support 
ecosystem services (Bonfante et al. 2020, Veerman et al. 2020). We agree with this definition, in which, 
in terms of providing ecosystem services for soil h ealth, we primarily support two groups of 
ecosystem´s services: provision and regulation services as the key ones. Other services (cultural and 
supporting) will depend on the possibility of obtaining available databases. 
In this respect, further research will be needed to address environmental factors more accurately and 
in detail, as well as impact of the applied agricultural and forestry management. 
 
2) Soil health indicators and their range of values 
We recognize that defining soil health indicators and their range of values is crucial for the soil health 
monitoring. For each country, it i s a group of specific indicators reflecting the climate, type and soil 
texture of the soil and its use. A unique framework of indicators needs to be developed and established 
for the concept and development of soil health policy in Europe . E .g. the referen ce values of the 
indicators should represent a combination, or the sum of individual indicators according t o the 
availability of databases. The problem will be indicators of soil biodiversity, which are not sufficiently 
researched in the conditions of th e Slovak Republic. Indicators should be part of new tools (e .g. soil 
health certification for soil transactions), they should be in line with soil health indicators. 
 
3) Requirements for sustainable land use 
These requirements are to be supported by the de finition of a set of sustainable land management 
practices, i. e. which enable the provision of soil -related ecosystem services (these are those agro -
ecosystem processes and functions that contribute to the fulfilment of human needs and are defined 
as soil ecosystem services) (Guideline for Sustainable Soil Management, FAO, 2017). With regard to 
agricultural land, it is necessary to define the natural potential of soils for growing individual crops and 
the possibility of applying sustainable management practices, such as: 
• crop and cultivation systems: diversified crop rotation, cover crops and catch crops, legumes on 
grasslands, temporary forests, perennial crops, incorporation of crop residues; 
• consolidation of agricultural land due to the possible degradation effect of external influences; 
• tillage and cover: non-inverse / reduced tillage, no tillage, direct sowing; 
• nutrient management and crop protection: use of organic and mineral fertilizers suitable for the 
site and production system, efficient fertilization, use of soil treatments and bio -fertilizers; 
reduction of pesticide use; 
• water management: apply appropriate drainage systems, efficient irrigation, improve 
accumulation water capacity; 
• alternative practices (rotational legumes, precise – digitized farming practices, timing and location 
of mineral and organic fertilizers) and nutrient losses (cover crops and catch crops) can b e used 
to reduce fertilizer use; 
• appropriate agroforestry systems have the potential to improve the condition of agricultural land 
in terms of multiple risks and undesirable conditions. They are longer -term cultivation systems 
and should therefore be set up and operated on the one hand, based on experience with proven 
combined land use (especially silvi -cultural systems) and on the other hand, scenarios of likely 
climate change and innovative approaches of land use need to verify. 
• for forest soils, sustainable land use is an integral part of sus tainable forest use, so specific 
requirements will be in line with the EU's New Forest Strategy 2030 (COM (2021) 572 final) as well 
as the EU Biodiversity Strategy 2030 (COM (2020) 380 final). Sustainable use is not only associated 
with appropriate forest management, but also take into account preventive measures that 
prevent linear erosion and soil destruction (method of approaching wood, condition of forest 
roads, etc.). 
Sustainable management should be integrated and linked to a validated database of possible 
degradation impacts. It should comply with CAP ecosystems and other international regulations and 
restrictions concerning soil health. Sustainable management should be integrated and linked to a

4) Monitoring and reporting on soil status 
The Slovak Republic contributes to the European database with a relatively rich so il data database 
(ESDAC JRC, EU Soil Observatory - EUSO). Participates in activities for analysis, synthesis and 
harmonization of databases (INSPIRE  Directive). We believe that in this context it is necessary to 
prepare and develop standardized methods of soil analysis, harmonize soil information, increase the 
sharing of soil data between different institutions, both within agriculture and from other s, and  
harmonize national soil monitoring systems (e.g. through the LUCAS project). 
Each Member State should: 
• create a network of land data owners / administrators, including the implementation of the 
INSPIRE Directive; 
• create a system of monitoring and interpretation of monitoring results in the unified reporting 
system; 
• develop and implement national transposed laws in relation to the sharing of information on 
land and other related entities; 
• link the results of the monitoring to the political and legal context, i.e. the issue of penalties for 
damage to soil health, subsidies for improving and maintaining soil health; 
• there is a great variability in fertilizer directives in European countries, reflecting the application 
of different crop systems, technological levels, historically developed methods of soil and 
climate analysis, as well as proper soil conditions.

No environmental protection without soil protection  
Soil protection is essential for the environment, is a key component of the EU's 2030 Biodiversity Strat-
egy and plays a special role in the implementation of the Green Deal.  
German industry is particularly committed to environmental protection. The intensive and efficiency -
oriented soil management in the past century as well as the long tradition of use of many sites has led 
to harmful soil changes and burdens.  
The enforcement of the Federal Soil Protection Act and the many obligations arising from the technical 
regulations on prec autionary soil protection law (Ordinance on Installations for the Handling of Sub-
stances Hazardous to Water, etc.) have led to soil contamination being prevented or, if it has occurred, 
remediated without delay. 
Thus, national German law already deals intensively with the handling of soil, old and new bur-
dens, precautionary soil protection and other specific regulations. Industrial activities are largely 
regulated and management decisions are strongly influenced. Soil as a protected good has no 
cross-border effect (subsidiarity principle). Soil protection in other EU member states is also al-
ready comprehensively regulated by specific laws. A coherent and integrating European Soil Protection 
Framework must take this into account.

In order to be able to achieve the set goals of the EU biodiversity strategy and to protect soils 
in the EU, the following aspects must be taken into account: 
▪ A risk-based approach should be set in connection with the respective land use when as-
sessing and deriving measures for contaminated soils. 
▪ The mere presence of contaminants in the soil does not justify the classification as "contami-
nated". Not every substance classified as a pollutant is environmentally hazardous by its mere 
presence. There needs to be consideration of concentration as well as the performance of 
rigorous risk assessments. 
▪ The assessment of soil contamination should take into account the use of the site. So -called 
"brown fields", i.e. previously used sites, should be considered differentl y than use on new 
sites, so-called "green fields". 
▪ There should be no mandatory soil and/or groundwater investigations for (planned) land trans-
actions. Private agreements and all information between contracting parties absolutely require 
special protection of confidentiality. 
▪ If the soil as an object of protection is used, protective and compensatory measures are an-
chored in the differentiated statutory approval requirements, and no further elaboration is nec-
essary. 
▪ Likewise, the monitoring regulations alre ady in place in Germany have already comprehen-
sively mapped the soil as an object of protection and are demonstrably documented in corre-
sponding life cycle files; a further tightening of the monitoring is not necessary.  
▪ 'Contaminated site protection' and ' contaminated site remediation' should be given equal pri-
ority. 
▪ A clear distinction must be made between "remediation" and "restoration".  
▪ Bureaucratic effort and benefits should be weighed up regarding “passports for excavated 
soil”. For industrial sites, we recommend creating a "relocation clause" at the site.  
▪ Soil biodiversity is a very complex system that needs to be better understood in order to define 
biodiversity protection measures. Any measures to this effect should be expanded.  
▪ Stakeholder participation in the development of the EU priority list for pollutants in soils and in 
the definition of soil health indicators and their value range is strongly recommended. 
A European regulation must refer to the fact that existing topics are solved regionally or nationally in 
the sense of the nationally existing regulations. Against the background of the subsidiarity princi-
ple, double regulation should be avoided at all costs.

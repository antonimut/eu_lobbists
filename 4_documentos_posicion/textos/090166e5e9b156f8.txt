FuelsEurope supports the EU’s goal of achieving climate neutrality by 2050, recognising that this will 
require breakthrough technologies and an enabling policy framework. We welcome the opportunity 
to contribute to the “Call for Evidence ” on the revision of the emission standards for heavy-duty 
vehicles, however we regret that the scope of the consultation remains relatively limited in the 
recognition of the role of renewable and low carbon liquid fuels for  the long-term decarbonisation 
of heavy-duty vehicles.  
 
We strongly believe that the decarbonisation of transport should be technology neutral and holistic. 
Therefore, whilst all low technology options are expected to drive the decarbonisation of the road 
transport, the contribution of renewable and low carbon liquid fuels in the heavy -duty segment 
appears critical now and in the future.  Moreover, low-carbon fuels will allow to seize the immediate 
environmental benefit of decarbonising the existing fleet.  
 
Low-carbon liquid fuels are fully compatible with existing vehicles and do not requ ire the 
implementation of new, dedicated distribution infrastructure and refuelling facilities , which is also 
of high importance for long-distance heavy-duty transport.  
 
We emphasise that the EU Commission should therefore consider an appropriate mechanism, which 
whilst keeping the separation of responsibilities between vehicle and fuel manufacturers, still allows 
the regulation to account for the CO2 benefits generated when renewable and low carbon liquid 
fuels replace fossil fuels. A fuel crediting system  (see for example Crediting System – Crediting 
System (crediting-system-for-renewable-fuels.eu) could represent a viable solution.

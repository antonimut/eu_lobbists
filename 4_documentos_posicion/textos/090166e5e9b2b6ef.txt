Eni S.p.A. position on the Consultation for the Revision of CO2 emission 
performance standards for heavy-duty vehicles  
 
Eni welcomes the EU ambition of climate neutrality by 2050, which calls for a strong contribution of each economic 
sector. In particular, the transport sector is asked to reduce its CO2 emissions by 90% by 2050. 
 
Eni believes that a broad range of technological solutions are needed to achieve such a level of decarbonisation. 
Therefore, the EU framework will be crucial to ensure that all technologies that can deliver significant GHG 
emissions reductions can contribute and compete on a level -playing field. Given  the interaction of different  
policies on market decisions, Eni reiterates that it is necessary to define and implement  a fair, competitive and 
stable framework  and seek close coordination of policies. This is essential to unlock long-term industrial 
investments and reduce uncertainty.  
 
We believe that  is important to assess the emissions associated to any vehicles according to a well -to-wheel 
approach (WtW), therefore  Eni’s responses provided to the questionnaire should be seen in a well -to-wheel 
perspective.  
 
Contribution of low-carbon and renewable fuels 
 
Eni believes that mobility characterized by internal combustion engines fuelled by decarbonised products can 
effectively complement electric and hydrogen mobility to achieve the decarbonisation of all modes of transport.  
 
For heady-duty transport, low-carbon and renewable fuels represent a reliable and readily-available solution to 
reduce CO2 emissions by maximizing the use of  the current fleet and existing infrastructure technologies. The 
environmental benefits of biofuels can be enhanced only if assessed according to the comprehensive life cycle 
approach. While developing the regulation, it will be important that the Commission follows a holistic approach 
and considers the contribution of all low-carbon and renewable fuels, assessing the emissions associated to any 
vehicles at least according to a well-to-wheel approach. 
 
Some biofuels, such as hydrogenated biofuels (HVO, Hydrogenated Vegetable Oil), can be used up to a 100% pure 
form, thus allowing a much higher level of CO2 abatement. They could immediately be dedicated to more recent 
heavy vehicles (buses and trucks) , whose engines are already homologated for its use. I n addition, HVO can be 
mixed in fossil fuels in higher percentages (up to 30%)  in all the existing fleet unlike traditional biodiesel (FAME) 
that at present has a maximum mixing limit in fossil fuels of 7%.  
 
Design of a possible mechanism for renewable and low-carbon fuels under the HDV Regulation 
 
Eni is in favour of introducing a mechanism to take into account the potential contribution of renewable and low-
carbon fuel when determining manufacturers’ compliance with their targets, including the option of a voluntary 
crediting system. Eni favours a market for certificates and credits that will allow the differen t technologies to 
compete in order to reward the solutions that are able to achieve the transition objectives in the most effective 
and efficient way. To achieve the CO2 targets, OEMs should be able to use the credits generated by fuel suppliers 
which exceed their own decarbonisation mandate. In this way the overall CO2 reduction in road transport will be 
a combination of various strategies and functional to the achievement of national targets. 
 
All vehicles with an overall low CO2 footprint, calculated on the basis of a well-to-wheel approach, should be 
eligible for incentives . If OEMs can demonstrate that the CO2 footprint of a heavy -duty vehicle is fully 
compensated over the lifetime by additional amounts of renewable fuels that are brought into market, it should 
be taken into account when establishing an incentive scheme or a fuel crediting system.  
 
The Commission should also be open to the introduction of a carbon correction factor while designing the 
upcoming regulation.

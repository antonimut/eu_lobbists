 
 
Fair Trials’ written response to a call for feedback on the Proposal for a Regulation 
and Directive on Digitalization of Judicial Cooperation 
 
1. Introduction 
Over the past two years , the COVID-19 pandemic has proven that digitalisation has a great 
potential to ensure more efficient and equal access to justice. When adopted and used with 
proper safeguards, digital tools have a potential to enhance equality and access to justice in 
criminal proceedings.  Fai r Trials welcomes the search for ways to make criminal justice 
systems more accessible through digitalisation. It is essential to place the defendant at the 
heart of these efforts and to ensure that respect for defence rights enshrined in the six EU 
Procedural Rights Directives and the Charter of Fundamental Rights  are upheld. We believe 
that digitalisation presents a real opp ortunity not only to promote cooperation between law -
enforcement agencies, but to better implement existing EU standards on defence rights in 
cross-border proceedings. Therefore, we offer our views on the proposals for a Regulation on 
the digitalisation of judicial cooperation and access to justice in cross-border civil, commercial 
and criminal matters, and amending certain acts in the field of judicial cooperation (the 
Proposed Regulation) and the Directive amending Council Directive 2003/8/EC, Council 
Framework Decisions 2002/465/JHA, 2002/584/JHA, 2003/577/JHA, 2005/214/JHA, 
2006/783/JHA, 2008/909/JHA, 2008/947/JHA, 2009/829/JHA and 2009/948/JHA, and 
Directive 2014/41/EU of the European Parliament and of the Council, as regards digitalisation 
of judicial cooperation (the Proposed Directive). 
 
Efficiency of  communication and access to justice in  cross-border cooperation i s a major 
driving force behind the Proposed Regulation and Directive (the Proposals). While efficiency 
of proceedings is undeniably important, especially in cases where the suspect or accused 
person is placed in detention , criminal proceedings must  first and foremost ensure  a just 
outcome, which require full and effective access to rights for suspects or accused persons and 
thorough judicial review. 
 
While the Proposals present a n important step forward in modernising judicial cooperation, 
Fair Trials believes that certain areas which are crucial in order to ensure effective access to 
justice and respect for certain key aspects of the fundamental rights of the defence are not 
addressed. Below we will shortly outline the gaps we have identified with respect to access to 
information, videoconferencing, access to dual legal representation in European Arrest 
Warrant (EAW) proceedings and promotion of alternatives to the EAW. 
 
2. Access to information

understand the language of the proceedings , which is highly likely in cross-border criminal 
proceedings. Therefore, digital tools should facilitate the possibility to make such submissions

Electronic access to case file 
Digitalising the exchange of information in cross-border proceedings and reducing the reliance 
on paper files  has clear benefits for all the parties involved in the proceedings.  However, 
electronic access to the case file  should also be ensured for defence to enable timely and 
adequate preparation for procedural activities, in particular f or judicial review proceedings 
under the EAW or suspect interviews under the E uropean Investigation Order (EIO). These 
rights are not addressed in the Proposals.  Some Member States have already made electronic 
access to case file available and provide good examples of such efforts. In France and the 
Netherlands, digital platforms have been developed that allow lawyers to access case files 
digitally and download them to their computers. 1  Such access significantly reduces the time 
necessary to access and copy the materials in police stations or courthouses and allowing for 
quicker and more effective preparation for investigative activities or judicial proceedings.  
In the context of EAW proceedings, the requested person an d their lawyer in the executing 
state are often unable to access the case file in the issuing state to enable effective judicial 
review before the requested person is surrendered.2 Lack of access to case file  prevents the 
defence from effectively challenging the legality and proportionality of the EAW before 
surrender and thus obtaining an effective remedy.3 Access to case file is of crucial importance 
also where the suspect or accused person is interviewed under the EIO and enjoys the right 
to access case materials in pre -trial stage in accordance with Article 7(2) of the Directive 
2012/13/EU. Digitalised case files would enable electronic transfer of relevant case materials 
from the issuing state to the suspects or accused persons and their lawyers in the executing 
state. In EAW proceedings such access should at least include the EAW form and all essential 
documents4 relied on in support of the national arrest warrant on which the EAW is based.

3. Videoconferencing 
During the COVID-19 crisis, videoconferencing was instrumental in enabling access to justice 
and played a key role in ensuring the continued functioning of the  criminal justice systems. 
Fair Trials welcomes essential procedural safeguards included in  Article 8 of the Proposed 
Regulation which addresses videoconferencing. Informed consent of the suspect accused or 
convicted person taken in consultation with their lawyer is a key safeguard to identify and 
prevent negative effects video hearings could have on the participation of certain suspects. It 
is also an essential safeguard against any potential ill-treatment or violations of rights caused 
by inadequate detention conditions.  We also welcome the need for a nuanced approach to 
the use of video conference that allows to take into account the particular circumstances of 
the case and availability of technology  under Article 8(1)(a) and (b)  of the Proposed 
Regulation.   
 
Confidential communication 
We also welcome the inclusion of an expressed duty to ensure confidentiality of 
communications with the lawyer before and during a hearing through videoconference in 
Article 8(4) of the Proposed Regulation.  
However, we would like to  draw your attention to our observations during the COVID -19 
pandemic which have shown that  guaranteeing confidential communication  through 
videoconference ha s been difficult in practice.  Lawyers and their clients have expressed 
serious concern about the ability to safely communicate through remote assistance tools.5 In 
some cases, platforms used did not allow for such communication and the proper functioning 
of the equipment often required prison staff or court officials to be present in rooms where the 
conversations are held. 6 Therefore, it is essential to pay particular attention to  the 
confidentiality of communications in practice when  conducting monitoring and evaluati on of 
the implementation of the Proposed Regulation under Article 17 of the Proposed Regulation. 
  
Effective participation in videoconference hearing 
In order for the individual subject to criminal proceedings to fully exercise their rights and 
ensure equality o f arms, videoconferencing technology must also allow for the possibility of 
sharing and reviewing evidence presented at a hearing . While Article 8 of the Proposed 
Regulation includes a number of essential safeguards,  Fair Trials suggests  including a 
reference in the Preamble of the Proposed Regulation to the need to ensure that the available 
technology allows for effective participation in hearings though videoconferencing and other 
distance communication technology, including , if necessary, submission and review of 
evidence and questioning of witnesses.

4. Access to dual legal representation 
  
Article 10(5) of  the Directive 2013/48/EU requires Member States to cooperate to facilitate 
appointment of a lawyer in the issuing stat e. Specifically, Article 10(4) prescribes that the 
competent authority of the executing state is required, without undue delay, to inform an 
individual arrested under an EAW of their right to appoint a lawyer in both the issuing and 
executing state. Recita l 46 of the Directive specifies that the competent authority of the 
executing Member State should provide the requested person with information to facilitate the 
appointment of a lawyer in the issuing Member State. In EAW proceedings, digitalisation 
should seek to address a long-standing implementation gap7 in dual legal representation.   
 
In particular, digital tools should be used to exchange information such as lists of available on-
duty lawyers and how to apply for legal aid. This is particularly relevant in the EAW proceedings 
which envisage  dual legal representation  as an important legal safeguard. Facilitating the 
exchange of information that allows for effective appointment of a lawyer, e.g., lists of lawyers 
specialized in cross -border cases, or the names of  available on duty lawyers in the issuing 
State would ensure respect for the right to dual legal representation as set out in Article 10(4) 
of Directive 2013/48/EU on the Access to a Lawyer.8  
 
Digital tools should also enable cooperation between lawyers in the issuing and executing 
states to prepare an effective defence and seek an effective remedy where necessary. 
 
 
5. Promotion of alternatives to the EAW 
Currently the EAW is the most widely used mutual cooperation instrument in criminal matters. 
Our research also shows that the EAW is used far more broadly than i ntended under the 
Framework Decision 2002/584/JHA. For example, a European Arrest Warrant is often used 
instead of a European Investigation Order is often issued for the purposes of investigation 
(suspect interview).9 Similarly, an EAW is preferred to the European Supervision Order due to 
unfamiliarity of the latter  instrument and lack of generally available information about 
alternative measures that exist in other Member States.10  
Digital tools can significantly contribute to increasing the availability of information  and 
possibility to exchange details about available alternatives to the EAW in other Member States.

FNADE’s position  
Call for Evidence for an Impact Assessment regarding Soil 
health: protecting, sustainably managing and restoring EU 
soils 
 
March 2022 
 
FNADE, the French association for waste management and environmental services, fully 
supports the European Commission’s initiative on soils strategy as it is crucial to promote soils 
in healthy condition. Indeed, providing an  European legal framework for s oil protection and 
granting this valuable natural resource the same level of protection as water and air  could 
make it possible to achieve the objectives of restricting soil degradation, promoting high level 
of biodiversity restoration and supporting carbon sequestration.  
 
Nevertheless, FNADE’s members who are key players in soils remediation  would like to take 
the opportunity to make some comments: 
 
First of all, FNADE regrets nothing has been said on prevention in the call for evidence, which 
should be the first step to avoid bad practices, loss of biodiversity, contamination with 
hazardous substances…. 
 
Then, the legal framework p roposed by the Commission should set harmonised targets 
regarding decontamination in order to avoid dilution and legacy substances in healthy soils  
through reuse. FNADE advocates for European guidelines leaving some flexibility for Member 
States to adapt to the geochemical background  and also to the purpose of the reuse . 
Furthermore, FNADE fears that a soil health certificate for land transactions  would not be 
enough to reassure buyers with regard to land and legal traceability  and would propose an 
analytical characterisation methodology especially in regards of new hazardous substances.  
 
Another crucial issue concerns the use of excavated soils wh ich need to be really framed  to 
avoid some abusive reuse under the guise of  recovery. Indeed, excavated soils are often 
exported for recovery. FNADE believes that these exports should be better supervised and 
comply with s ome thresholds. Recovery under poor environmental conditions is always less 
favourable than treatment or disposal.  
At this stage of the work on the soil strategy, FNADE cannot give an opinion on the benefit of 
a passport for excavated soil but clearly express the need of guidelines. 
 
Regarding the bad physical and biological condition of soils, especially those used for farming, 
FNADE members, very involved in the production of compost from bio-waste, sewage sludge, 
compliant with high quality  standards (French standards NFU 44 -095) and agronomic  
recovery of sewage sludges (from was tewater trea tment plants)   would recall their great 
potential in the ability to restore soils and improve their quality, such as carbon sequestration. 
But producing high quality sludges enabling to avoid contamination by toxic substances needs 
that domestic and industrial wastewater are separated upstream. France has set up an action

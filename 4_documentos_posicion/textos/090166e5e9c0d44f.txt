The European Consortium of the Organic-Based Fertiliser Industry (ECOFI) welcomed the new 
EU Soil Strategy for 2030 adopted in 2021, including the initiative of this legislative proposal 
for EU targets to protect, ensure the sustainable use of, and restore soils in Europe. The call 
for evidence correctly outlines a number of the critical services delivered by soil ecosystems 
and how much of our agricultural production and environmental services rely on healthy soils.  
 
However, ECOFI would like to urge the European Commission to consider wildly varying soil 
properties – not just across Europe, but sometimes within the same Member State – and the 
importance of tailoring soil protection and restoration measures to different physical, chemical, 
and biological compositions of soils. In particular, any legislative proposal should treat 
agricultural soils separately from other soils as they are subject to substantially different uses 
and applications than other soils. 
 
By definition, organic-based fertilisers (OBFs) and soil improvers add organic matter to soils.  
Therefore, OBFs aim not only to provide nutrients to plants but also improve soil fertility thanks 
to their organic matter content, with a positive effect on soil microbial activity too, and a 
significant role in improving the carbon capture and storage capacity of agricultural soils. Soil 
health has always been high on our agenda, and an improved understanding of how to protect, 
maintain and improve soil fertility is a priority for our industry. As the European organisation 
representing the organic -based fertiliser industry in Europe, ECOFI would like to offer its 
insights on the Call for Evidence to inform the development of an EU legislative proposal on 
healthy soils. 
 
ECOFI will continue to engage with the following steps in the consultation stage and is eager 
to contribute to this legislative process over the coming months. Please do not hesitate to get 
in touch with us for more information and details relating to organic-based fertilisers and the 
concrete contributions they make to the health of agricultural soils. 
 
Organic-based fertilisers help to improve the health of agricultural soils: 
 
 Processed organic-based fertilisers and soil improvers increase the organic matter 
content of soils. Because they are concentrated and hygienised, these refined products 
are in a form that can be transported economically and applied safely, which helps to 
improve soils in areas that may not benefit from many locally sourced organic materials. 
These products enhance soil fertility, soil structure, water retention, and biodiversity, 
which also reduce erosion. 
 
 Combining organic-based fertilisers with mineral fertilisers (= organo-mineral fertilisers) 
has been proven to achieve the highest crop yields for farmers  and improve nutrient 
use efficiency. Organic matter helps increase plants' nutrient uptake and reduce inputs,

 A co-formulation of organic material with mineral inputs gives organo-mineral fertilisers

 There is more carbon in soil o rganic matter than in plants and the atmosphere

ECOFI calls for the EU legislative proposal on soil health to include concrete policy options to 
promote and support organic-based fertilising products, including organic fertilisers, organo-
mineral fertilisers, and organic soil improvers, which could deliver some of the benefits listed 
above directly to agricultural soils. 
 
The Fertilising Products Regulation 2019/1009 will provide organic-based fertilising products 
with access to the EU Single Market for the first time, providing (in theory) equal access and a 
level playing field for farmers to access these organic-based products, which will be an 
essential tool for farmers to contribute to EU Green Deal objectives, including improved soil 
health. 
 
However, for organic-based fertilisers to contribute to the objectives outlined in this call for 
evidence, the right policy, and regulatory coherence, frameworks and implementation are all 
required, including – as well as reaching far beyond – the implementation of the Fertilising 
Products Regulation 2019/1009. 
 
Coherence  crucial across EU policies  
 
There must be coherence between all relevant policy frameworks that govern agricultural 
production, inputs, and land use to preserve and enhance soil health across Europe. This 
includes the Fertilising Products Regulation (FPR), Common Agricultural Policy (CAP), Organic 
Production Regulation, Farm to Fork Strategy, Biodiversity Strategy, Circular Economy 
Strategy, the new EU soil strategy, and more. But this coherence needs to go beyond 
soundbites and include impact assessments of specific measures to understand the trade-offs 
and make informed decisions.  
 
Agricultural soils are regulated by different regulatory frameworks, including those which 
cover agricultural inputs (e.g., fertilising products). The technical regulatory frameworks also 
concern soil health: in the specific case of fertilising products, the Regulation (EU) 2019/1009 
defines safety limits regarding contaminants and pathogens and quality provisions to 
preserve agricultural soils. These provisions should be considered in elaborating all legislative 
activities on soil health to avoid incoherence, overlaps, barriers to uptake, or conflicting 
regulatory requirements. 
 
Ensuring a Single Market for all relevant products should also be central to policy coherence. 
This is currently not the case for organic production, where acceptance of agricultural inputs 
varies from one Member State to another and from one certifying body to another. Such

Training and Education needed 
 
Amongst other things, the CAP should ensure adequate training, advisory, and incentives for 
farmers, distributors, advisors , and consum ers to learn about and  better understand 
sustainable, site-specific practices to improve soil health  (e.g., integrated plant nutrition and 
soil fertility management  principles within both conventional and organic farming ). This is 
especially important in light of recent research showing that farmers have more demanding 
standards for bio-based fertilising products fertilisers than mineral fertilisers yet are not willing 
to pay as much for bio-based fertilisers.

Feedback on the European Commission’s text proposal on the AIFMD Review 
 
 
Executive summary 
 
BNP Paribas Asset Management (BNPP AM) thanks the European Commission for the opportunity to 
provide feedback on its text proposal on the AIFM Directive review adopted on 25 November 2021.  
 
BNP Paribas Asset Management (BNPP AM) is  the asset management arm of BNP Paribas, a leading 
banking group in Europe with international reach . BNPP AM aims to generate long -term sustainable 
investment returns for its clients, based on a unique sustainabilit y-driven philosophy. BNPP AM’s 
investment capabilities are focused around five key strategies: High Conviction Strategies, Private Debt 
& Real Assets, Multi -Asset, Quantitative & Solutions, Emerging markets and Liquidity Solutions, with 
investment processes incorporating quantitative and fundamental analysis.  BNPP AM currently 
manages EUR 537 billion of assets (EUR 669  billion of assets  under management and advisory)  and 
benefits from the expertise of around 500 investment professionals and over 4 00 client servicing 
specialists, serving individual, corporate and institutional clients in 69 countries. 
 
We globally welcome the E uropean Commission (EC)  text proposal on the revision of the AIFM 
Directive and the UCITS Directive. We appreciate that the proposal is targeted on key aspects for which 
significant improvements can be achieved.  
 
This note presents the provisions for which we consider that some amendments are required to ensure 
that final version of the text is fit for purpose and leading to better functioning of the fund investment 
industry, while maintain high standards for inves tor protection. Namely, our input covers following 
areas: 
- Liquidity Management Tools (LMTs) 
- Supervisory Reporting 
- Loan Originating Funds 
- Ancillary Services 
- Transparency requirements 
- Depositary regime 
 
For each topic, you will find below the modifications w e recommend and justification for such 
amendments, for both the AIFMD and the UCITS Directive.

Member States of the European Union (EU). This proposal aims at addressing the current differences 
in supervisory practices  and legal/regulatory frameworks  on this matter  across the EU. Other 
modifications refer to the concern about potential risk contagion that investment funds could cause 
to the whole financial system would extremely significant volumes of redemptions happen due to the

2. Supervisory reporting 
 
The text proposal intends to address two-fold objectives: 
- On the one hand, the text proposal is seeking to streamline existing reporting requirements and 
remove any kind of duplications and overlaps. This should result in reducing the reporting burden 
when data reported do not bring any added -value on the activities and risks generated by  one 
investment fund. 
- On the other  hand, the text proposal  considers that some data are still missing to have a 
comprehensive vision of the profi le of certain  given investment fund s and/or that further 
granularity would be beneficial on some aspects for the supervisory assessment.  
- Lastly the text proposal introduces a supervisory reporting for UCITS funds although management 
companies of UCITS funds are already requested to report a comprehensive range of data on the 
assets and liabilities of the UCITS funds they manage. 
 
We have the following suggestions on amendments which could be brought to the European 
Commission’s text proposal. 
 
 
AIFM supervisory reporting - Article 24 (a) 
 
6. ESMA sha ll develop draft regulatory technical standards specifying the details to be reported according to 
paragraphs 1 and 2. ESMA shall take into account other reporting requirements to which the AIFMs are subject  
and the report issued in accordance with paragraph 2 of Article 69b (2). The benefits of any change to the current 
supervisory reporting should be properly demonstrated with sufficient proof of evidence, in particular in terms 
of added-value from a financial stability and investor protection perspective. 
 
Justification: we welco me the intention of the E uropean Commission to improve the cu rrent supervisory 
reporting by removing any unnecessary duplications and overlaps. At the same time, we consider that the current

3. Loan originating funds 
 
The text proposal introduces new measures specific to loan originating funds. These modifications are 
justified by the European Commission with the following reasons: 
- Local rules applied at national level diverge from one Member States to another, 
- It is necessary to address the micro and macro risks that these funds could cause to the financial 
system.  
 
We understand the European Commission’s purpose of creating more convergence across the EU and 
having these funds to operate on a cross -border basis. However we consider that the AIFMD should 
remain an actor directive which regulates the AIFMs and not the AIFs as such. Introducing specific rules 
on loan originating funds would result in having specific rules for a category of prod ucts, which is 
 
6 
 
 
contradictory with the AIFMD initial spirit and mandate.  We also believe that the level of risks caused 
by these funds remains quite limited and that in any case  and that these funds would not create a 
threat on the stability of the financial market.  As a consequence , putting more restrictions on loan 
originating funds does not seem justified from a risk perspective. 
In addition, we do not believe that a directive is the right legislative tool to ensure harmonisation across 
the EU as it could result in diverging interpre tations, as it is the case today from one jurisdiction to 
another. Introducing new rules through a new regulation would be more efficient. In that case, new 
measures should in counterparty provide some positive developments to AIFMs managing loan 
origination funds, as passporting of these funds across the EU 27 Member States. The current proposal 
is not clear and not detailed enough on how modifications will allow to distribute loan originating funds 
on a cross-border basis throughout the EU.   
 
As a conseq uence, we recommend  in particular  that following modifications referrin g to loan 
originating funds be removed from the European Commission’s text proposal.

4. Ancillary services 
 
The EC text proposal is extending the list of ancillary services referred to under Article 6 by extension 
to benchmark administration and credit servicing. 
We welcome this extension of the range of the ancillary services that an AIFM can perform with an 
AIFM a pproval. However we consider that it would  be more relevant to have a principle -based 
approach on this specific provision instead of an exhaustive list,  providing that certain conditions are 
met (as preventing any conflicts of interests). 
 
In this respect, we have the following suggestion: 
 
Ancillary activities - Article 6 (4) 
 
By way of derogation from paragraph 2, Member States may authorize an external AIFM to provide the 
following services: 
(…) 
New (e) – any other activities than those mentioned in paragraph 4 provided that: 
(i) these activities are in continuity of the services already undertaken by the AIFM for its own AIFs and could 
be undertaken for third parties, and 
(ii) these activities do not create any conflicts of interests that could not be managed by existing additional 
measures. 
 
Justification: asset managers who have the AIFMD license can provide the activities as listed in Article 6 an d 
Annex 1 without needing to have another license (as the MIFID license). They can also perform some activities 
which are not listed as such in the AIFMD, but which are complementary to the services provided by the AIFM 
and enable the AIFM’s clients to benefit from a coherent package of services. This approac h also allows the 
AIFM to rationalize the provision of some services instead of duplicating some tasks and/or using third parties.  
In these conditions, we recommend that a principle-based approach is retained instead of having only a list of 
limited activities which may not be fully representative of services that could be in the scope. Strict rules as the 
continuity of services and absence of any conflicts of interest should apply in any case.

Justification : we consider that information on fees and charges is relevant for investors only when they apply to

On staff, the only information that could be provided is the one relating to headcounts.  
Lastly, on originating loans portfolio, the information that could be disclosed to investors should be l imited to 
the aggregated amount  of these loans. Detailed information on individual borrowers is not to be disclosed to 
investors but rather to supervisors who are best-placed to assess this information from a risk perspective. 
 
 
6. Depositary regime 
 
The text proposal does not retain the introduction of a depositary passport due to lack of 
harmonization of securities laws across the EU. The text proposal introduces two main changes: 
- CSDs are considered as any other intermediary in the custody chain when keeping some assets 
under custody and as a consequence will be subject to the same liability provisions, 
- One Member State can authorize one AIF domiciled in its own jurisdiction to appoint a depositary 
in another Member State when the offer of depositary services is quite limited. 
 
We welcome the decision not to introduce the depositary passport and the changes on the CSDs as 
they both contribute to maintain high investor protection standards. On the possibility to have the 
depositary in another Member State than the AIF’s domicile, we are of the view that clarity should be

provided on the conditions under which such flexibility could be authorized by the E uropean 
Commission.

to the AIF shall be made available by the competent authorities of the  Member State wher e the  AIF is

to the depositary.” 
 
New. The competent authorities of the Member State where the AIF is established is responsible for ensuring 
that the depositary complies with these rules. Supervisory checks are carried out by the competent authorities

authorities of the home Member State of the AIF.  
 
Justification 
We welcome the decision of the European Commission not to introduce a depositary passport, believing that the 
requirement for the depositary to share the same domicile as the fund is an essential safeguard for investor 
protection. Such provision guarantees legal certainty and swift means of redress for investors in case of a loss of 
fund’s assets. At the same time, the proximity between the depositary and the fund facilitates activities such as 
safe-keeping, oversight and cash monitoring dutie s, communication with the fund’s manager and respective 
authorities. The latter also benefit as they can carry out supervisory mandates – inspections, examinations or 
investigation of any misconduct - in a more efficient way. 
 
It is therefore quite importa nt to specify in the proposed Article 61 the conditions under which NCAs are 
authorised to allow an AIF to appoint a depositary established in another Member State, as well as the rules that 
the depositary and the competent authorities supervising the depo sitary must respect. 
 
We suggest that the following aspects are clarified in the amended Article 61: 
 
- Evidence of a shortage of depositaries must be  made available to the European Commission by the 
competent authorities of the AIF home Member State to actually limit the following risks already identified 
by the European Commission in its impact assessment, i.e. (1) Impractical cross-border supervision may lead 
to an  overlooked risk accumulation and to depository market concentration in a few jurisdic tions of the 
Union (which could negatively affect financial stability; and (2) a failure of a depositary holding a significant 
volume of the market’s assets would have outsized implications for the financial stability.  
- The protection of investors may be co mpromised if the updated national rules applicable to the depositary 
and applicable to the AIF to which the depositary provides its oversight are not made available by the 
competent authorities of the home Member State of the AIF on a website in written form and in a language 
customary in the field of international finance. This is due to the following reasons :  
- 1. National rules apply to the AIF and thus to the depositary in the exercise of its oversight  functions, in    
particular for: (a) the sale, issu e, repurchase, redemption and cancellation of units or shares of the 
investment fund; (b) the calculation of the value of units or shares of an AIF; (c) the instruction of the AIF to 
the depositary (for example: within the framework of the constitution and  authorisation of a fund, the 
investment policy of a fund, the merger or liquidation of a fund); (d) the distribution or reinvestment of the 
income of an AIF. 
 
10 
 
 
- 2. The depositary must perform its oversight functions on the AIF. There would therefore be a conflict of 
interest if the national rules applicable to the AIF and the depositary for the exercise of its oversight functions 
were transmitted to it by the AIF on the basis of the written contract between the AIF/AIFM and the 
depositary. 
 
- It must be clarified that only the   competent authorities of the AIF member state can allow it to appoint a 
depositary in another member state to avoid that the competent author ities of the AIFM home Member 
could allow an AIF which is not under its supervision to appoint a depositary in another Member State that 
the AIF’s. 
 
- The competent authorities of  the AIF Member State are responsible for ensuring that the depositary 
complies with these rules. Otherwise investor protection may be undermined due to the difficulties for NCAs 
to supervise the correct application of laws and rules. This clarification is key to ensure consistency with the 
proposal changes of  Article 21 (16) (“If the competent authorities of the AIF or the AIFM are different from 
those of the depositary, the competent authorities of the depositary shall share the information received 
without delay with the competent authorities of the AIF and the AIFM”).  
 
- Supervisory checks are to be carried out by the competent authorities of the depositary Member State on 
instruction received from the competent authorities of the Member State where the AIF is established.

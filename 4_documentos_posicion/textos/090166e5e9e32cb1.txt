18 March 2022Move EU feedback on adopted ITS proposal
Move EU – the association of the leading ride-hailing platforms – welcomes the EuropeanCommission’s for a revision of the Directive on Intelligent Transport Systems (ITS). Theassociation and its members believe it to be an important piece of the EU’s architecture forsmart mobility, currently undergoing rapid development and innovation. The update of the ITSDirective is therefore timely.
We welcome, in particular, the European Commission’s:
● recognition in the proposal of the increasing demand for green, safe and efficient urbanmobility and of modern commute models which require a combination of mobilitysolutions and services to fulfill a diverse pool of needs.● due attention to ensuring that the revised ITS Directive is consistent with andcomplementary to the creation of a European Mobility Data Space, built on ever-closercollaboration between the public and the private sectors.● Acknowledgment of the need to respect the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)and to ensure privacy or data protection by design in any data exchange.
As we are advancing in the legislative process, we take the opportunity toreiteratea few areaswhere we see room for improvement, to ensure the proposed revised directive fits with therealities of ride-hailing players:
Data Sharing and a common European Mobility Data Space
Data sharing, which supports ITS and digital mobility solutions, must be done in a way thatencourages innovation, balances commercial and public interests, and protects privacy anddata. The uptake of Mobility-as-a-Service (MaaS) solutions can benefit from a forward-thinkingITS Directive.
In this regard, we believe that the data-sharing architecture should determine the appropriateand necessary volume of providing data.We believethat data should be shared based on aconcrete use case with a clear purpose.Furthermore, it should be further distinguished betweenB2B data sharing and B2G data sharing. Business-sensitive data can only be shared with otherthird parties based on bilateral agreements while B2G data sharing must serve specificregulatory goals (e.g., enforcement).

In this respect, we ask the legislators to ensure the security of the data transferred and toestablish explicit guidelines for the use and processing of the data collected. The authoritiesthat acquire information for system maintenance should also be clearly stated. This ensures thatthe data provided do not contain information that could hurt the organizations sharing the dataor violate the rules of fair competition.
To this end, sharing data should be standardised for the entire ecosystem through thedevelopment and implementation of common coherent Application Programming Interfaces(APIs), with the participation and input of the industry, as opposed to allowing the coexistenceof different standards locally. This would contribute to enhancing cooperation in the sector andenable a level playing field, whilst reducing fragmentation and minimising red tape.
Ride-hailing platforms make significant investments to design the operational procedures andbuild the infrastructure needed to collect, evaluate and analyse data correctly, all of which arenot generated without a cost. Processed data become a company's intellectual property, whichshould be adequately protected to incentivise continuous investments and innovation. In thislight, the revised directive should recognise this reality and build in, to the extent possible,incentives for the private sector to engage in data sharing.
Stimulating a competitive market
End-users (consumers, but also transport operators) can choose between different providersthanks to a diversified offering and a competitive market. We can anticipate that many moreMaaS platforms, both public and private, will coexist in the future. For a market that is in fulldevelopment and has not reached maturity, overly prescriptive approaches, including to datamanagement, could deter players from proposing innovative solutions and therefore hamperthe growth of MaaS. A balance should therefore be struck between creating the right conditionsto stimulate the uptake of MaaS solutions and uphold free market principles.
Interoperability
The proposal's Article 6a Availability of data requires mandatory data exchange with ITS servicesin accordance with Annex III, which must be made available and provided by Member States viaNational Access Points (NAPs).
Interaction with other files
Move EU would welcome further clarification from the European Commission and co-legislatorson the interplay of the proposed revised directive with other files such as the recently proposed

Data Act, the Data Governance Act, the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and theproposed ePrivacy Regulation, but also upcoming initiatives on Multimodal Travel InformationServices, the Multimodal Digital Mobility Services and the Mobility Data Space. To guaranteelegal certainty and effective compliance, it will be important to ensure clear, streamlined andcoherent obligations across all these relevant pieces of legislation.
We fully support the European Commission’s initiative to set up a European-wide Mobility DataSpace that encourages all relevant players to cooperate more and build a connected mobilitysystem of the future. This new framework will also help to standardize and simplify datasharing.

March 21, 2022 
Response to European Commission Call for Evidence 
 to update the Chemical Agents Directive 
 
ISOPA/ALIPA and its downstream users’ associations welcome the opportunity to provide 
feedback to the European Commission on the review of the Chemical Agents Directive 
(98/24/EC) which intends to set Binding Occupational Exposure  Limit Values (BOEL) for 
diisocyanates and lead. We will not comment on the OEL setting intentions regarding lead. 
We have closely followed the OEL process for diisocyanates and generally welcome the 
Commission’s efforts to pursue EU harmonisation for occupational safety legislation covering 
diisocyanates.  
Today, there is no harmonized OEL for diisocyanates in the EU. In the past, different Member 
States set partially different OELs for the same substance . As a result, EU member states 
have scattered OEL levels for individual diisocyanates across the Union  and some of them  
have different OEL for specific diisocyanates based on NCO concentration. Currently, the 
average EU limit value is 17,5 µg NCO / m³. Therefore, ISOPA/ALIPA welcomes the approach 
to assess the issue and to establish a level playing field in the EU. 
We welcome the ACSH opinion as it takes into account  the socio-economic impact 
outlined in the Commission’s consultant’s report  
We welcome the opinion adopted by the EU Advisory Committee on Safety and Health at 
Work (ACSH)  in November 2021, proposing a stepwise transition to comply with a  time-
weighted average (8-hour TWA) of 10 µg NCO / m³ by 2025 and 6 µg NCO / m³ by 2029 and 
a short-term exposure limit for 15 min (STEL) of 20 µg NCO / m³ by 2025 and 12 µg NCO / m³ 
in 2029 for diisocyanates.  
These new values will require a very significant investment in the major part of the value chain, 
as indicated in the RPA/FoBiG Socio-Economic Report  (2021) 1  commissioned by DG 
Employment. The report notes that “58% of companies will require risk reduction measures. 
Typical exposure reductions could be achieved by enclosures, ventilation and extende d use 
of personal protective equipment” (p. 265). Notably, these measures should also be taken with 
the proposed values for 2025 (10 µg NCO / m³ TWA and 20 µg NCO / m³ STEL), as they also 
require significant efforts from employers and companies.  
The report confirms that additional costs of EUR ~8 billion are required for industry to adopt 
to the level of 6 µg NCO / m³ TWA. Several use sectors will face significant challenges with a 
mid-term reduction by a factor of three  from the current EU median OEL . The proposed  
stepwise transition period is therefore  important to allow the development of mitigation 
measures and to implement best practices by the EU downstream sectors of polyurethane 
systems and to enable adaptation to the new limit values in EU countries.  
We consider that the proposed values are just technically feasible for most applications based 
on commercially available technologies . For an OEL to be an effective tool and being

RPA/FoBiG also raised concern that “an OEL below 10 μg NCO  / m³ could lead some 
companies to remove continuous monitoring, because the warnings cannot be set sufficiently 
lower than such an OEL” (p. 194) . This technically feasible level of 10 µg NCO / m³ is very 
closed to the proposed STEL which means that these direct reading devices are just starting 
to work properly slightly below a STEL of 12 µg NCO / m³.    
Complementarity with the existing REACH restriction is essential when considering 
adoption of an EU OEL  
These OELs have been set in complement to REACH Restriction (Regulation (EU) 2021/979) 
which sets out mandatory training requirements for workers handling diisocyanates . We 
strongly believe that the OEL values adopted by the ACSH are  complementary with the 
REACH Restriction and the resulting exposure reduction expected from the implementation of 
mandatory trainings.  
With regards to the REACH Restriction’s implementation, ISOPA and ALIPA , together with 
industry partners, are moving forward in the roll -out of the training programme for industrial 
and professional users of diisocyanates.  After kick-starting the online training programme in 
English and German in 2021, we are working to make the trainings available in all EU official 
languages this year to ensure safe use of diisocyanates all over Europe. These trainings will 
improve workers' knowledge and raise awareness of the safety-related information disclosed 
in the safety data sheets. It is expected that workers will become more aware of their personal 
influence on daily safety at work.    
 
Several sectors play a significant role in achieving the EU’s Green Deal objectives 
Finally, we want to highlight that diisocyanates are essential to produce polyurethane, a key 
material for a range of applications such as insulation in buildings and appliances, which 
contributes to the European Green Deal targets by reducing CO2 emissions through energy 
efficiency by enabling light -weight solutions and high -performance insulation materials . 
RPA/FoBiG emphasize that “several sectors play a significant role in achieving the EU’s Green 
Deal” and give the example of manufacturers of electric vehicles who are increasingly 
considering replac ing heavier materials in cars with polyurethane to offset the weight of 
batteries (p. 17). Moreover, “s ophisticated polyurethane coatings are also used in many 
applications including the rotor surfaces of wind turbines.” (p. 19) 
 
In summary, w e believe tha t the proposed ACSH OEL values , in combination with the 
REACH Restriction’s mandatory training for workers , will ensure  a framework which 
fully protects workers , leading to a significant reduction in the number of occupational 
asthma cases, while taking into account socio-economic and feasibility factors.

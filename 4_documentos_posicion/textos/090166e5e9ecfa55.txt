Schibsted contribution to call for evidence for an Impact Assessment (IA) -Safeguarding media freedom in the EU (Media Freedom Act)
WewelcometheEuropeanCommission'srecognitionofthesocial roleofthemediaandtheimportance of a diverse, strong media ecosystem in Europe that provides citizens withrelevant, critical and independent information and contributes to the protection andenhancement of democratic values across the Union.
In particular, there is cause for concern when the role of private actors in the mediaecosystem weakens, primarily due to strong competition in the advertising market fromdominant global giants and political pressure in some markets.
The role of independent publishers is especially important in an information environmentwhereweareseeingincreasingpolarizationandthespreadofdisinformationandconspiracytheories. The question is, however, whether a Media FreedomAct initiative, either in theformof aRegulationorasanon-legislativerecommendationtoMemberStates,isasuitableinstrument for effectively addressing these trends.
Schibstedsupportstheexistingframeworkthat seespolicydecisionsonmediacontentandplurality regulatedbyMember Statesandnational regulatoryauthorities, recognisingmediapolicy is strongly anchored in national culture. Any attempt to strengthen the EUinternalmarket must strikeacareful balanceandappreciatethediversestructural,cultural,political,and economic contexts that exist for media across the EU.
Attempts to adopt supranational regulation of the media industry is likely to beproblematic, inprinciple,forseveral reasons,notleastthatsuchregulationislikelytoendupbeing insufficient to tackle the challenges faced in particularly vulnerable markets whileatthe same time overly intrusive in other markets.
The Commission's call for evidence mentions four problem(s) the initiative aims totackle. Of these, interference in the independence of newsrooms is, in our opinion,the most serious.

Suchinterventionswill beillegitimate,butnotnecessarily illegal. Classic examples include discriminatory financial measures, such astaxingadvertisingrevenues,disproportionatelydirectinggovernmentadvertisingrevenuesto"friendly" mediaoutlets, creatingbarrierstoaccessingpublicinformationandusingstrategiclawsuits (SLAPPs). In our experience media pluralismcan also be fostered through national self-regulation ofthe media sector. TheotherchallengesmentionedinCall forevidenceare"differentnationalrulesonmediapluralism" / "lack of media pluralismsafeguards..." and "insufficient structures forcooperation between independent media regulators".
"Lack of media pluralism safeguards..."
The underlying reasons for a lack of media pluralismin a market arelikelytobecomplexandanyattempt at regulationmustbeextremelycareful toavoidunintendedconsequences.We are particularly skeptical of any newproposals to regulate media ownership at anEUlevel, which we consider to be already well served by the existing competition lawframework. If properlydeployed, EUcompetitionlawcantakeintoaccount dimensionsthatdrivecompetitioninthemedia, includingquality, varietyandoriginality.Combinedwithotherregulationsrelatedtotransparencyandvariousreportingrequirements,thisprovidesasolidbasis for overseeing media ownership in the EU.
"Insufficient structures for cooperation between independentmedia regulators".
The Commission’s call for evidence also sees a problem with a lack of cooperation andcoordination between independent media regulators. At first glance this may appear asimple, administrative, procedural issuetoaddress. But whenweassesstheprincipledandpractical consequences of possible changes in this area, we are again forcedtoraisetheneed for proportionality and caution.
Althoughweseemeritinhavingacoordinatedapproachbetweennational mediaregulators,for exampleonissuesrelatingtocross-borderaudiovisual regulation,weareskeptical aboutmoving national enforcement of the media to an EU level body.
As strong supporters of safeguarding editorial independence and the independentmanagement of the media, we are in principle cautious of overly intrusive externalsupervision of the mediasector, especiallyif thisisdoneat asupranational level unabletoadequatelytakeintoaccount national cultural andlinguisticdiversity.Ifincorrectlydesigned,such a structure could result in over-regulation and see large parts of media regulationmoved away from national democratic control.
In conclusion, though we applaud the Commission´s intentions to safeguard aEuropeanmediamarket that remainsindependent, innovativeandsustainableforthelong term, we urge caution in the approach and careful consideration of possibleunintended negative consequences on the legitimacy of editorial independence.

Should the European Commission choose to move forward with its initiative, wesuggest exploring Option 1 and the issuing of a Recommendation addressed toMember States.

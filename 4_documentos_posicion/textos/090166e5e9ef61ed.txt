KGHM Polska Miedź S.A. (KGHM) is one of the world's leading companies in the mining 
and production of copper and silver. The company also produces other accompanying 
metals, which results from the properties of the exploited polymetallic deposit. 
The lead production process is an extremely important element /stage in the copper 
production process at KGHM. Lead is a product recovered from lead -bearing materials 
produced during the smelting and refining of copper. This method of lead production has 
an important environmental aspect, as it allows effective management of the lead -
containing materials generated in the production of copper. 
KGHM is a producer of lead from primary sources. In 2020, lead production in the EU 
amounted to 1.65 million Mg, of which 73% was produced from secondary sources. 
Annual production of refined lead by KGHM Polska Miedź S.A. is about 30 thousand Mg, 
representing 6.7% of produ ction from primary sources. Our company employs  
176 people in lead production. However, the workers exposure risk to the harmful effects 
of lead occurs throughout the entire chain of pyrometallurgical processes carried out at 
KGHM Polska Miedź S.A. due to the fact that the divisions producing lead use intermediate 
products generated in the earlier stages of copper production. 
Taking advantage of the opportunity to present the position of our company as part of 
the public consultation on the values  of OEL and BLV for lead, we would like to indicate 
that in our opinion these values should be as follows:  
Occupational Exposure Limit Value 
• 50 µg / m³ (8hr TWA) with an 8-year transition period after inclusion in CAD 
Biological Limit Values  
• 35 µg / dL with a 5-year transition transition period after inclusion in CAD (no 
earlier than 2028)  
• 20 µg / dL with a 10-year transition transition period after inclusion in CAD (no 
earlier than 2033) 
Below is the justification of the position of KGHM Polska Miedź S.A. 
KGHM is a leader in implementing modern solutions to protect the health of our 
employees, local communities and in environmental protection. We can boast of many 
investments in this area and ongoing educational programmes, as well as  
the implementati on and execution of a programme for reduction of lead absorption  
by the human body in the workplace in the years 2011-2022.

Occupational Exposure Limit - OEL  
The OEL value currently in force for lead in Poland at the level of 0.05 mg / m3 is one  
of the most stringent standards currently in force in the EU Member States in this regard. 
The research carried out in our company for many years, carried out in cooperation with 
the Wroclaw Medical University, shows that in both of our divisions producing lead these 
standards are met and our employees are provided with safe working conditions. 
Moreover, according to the analysis of data from 2011 to 2022, we observe a t endency 
related to the decrease in the concentration of lead in the air at workplaces in both 
divisions, among others thanks to the simultaneous programme of limiting lead 
absorption by the human body and hermetization of industrial processes. The internal  
target of KGHM Polska Miedź S.A. is reaching the level 0.04 mg/m3. We are fully aware 
that the OEL values should reflect the best practices currently applied and the latest 
scientific knowledge in terms of the effects of substances on the health of worker s. 
Therefore, we are constantly expanding our knowledge in the field of managing exposure 
to lead in the workplace and applying the latest currently available related solutions with 
the protection of the health of our workers who are exposed to lead.  
The programme to reduce the lead absorption by the human body has been divided into 
five elements: 
1. Technical and technological activities,  
2. Organizational activities,  
3. Selection of personal protection equipment,  
4. Educational activities,  
5. Biological monitoring.  
Each of the above elements is continuously implemented and, if possible, improved.  
In the case of Personal Protective Equipment, the equipment is selected individually  
for the workplace and the employee, after each work environment test. Employees have 
access to the latest solutions in the field of respiratory protection with a protection factor 
of NPF = 500 (eg Versaflo M-407, M-300 produced by 3M). 
Biological Limit Value – BLV 
In the case of BLV, the standard that is in force in Poland i s 50 µg / dL in blood,  
and as a company, we meet the requirements of this standard. As part of the program  
for limiting the absorption of lead by the human body described above, we set ourselves  
the internal goal of reducing this value to the level of 35  µg / dL. Our long -standing 
practice, supported by cooperation with university research centers, has shown that due  
to the maintenance of blood lead concentrations below 50 µg / dL, we do not find any 
occupational diseases due to occupational exposure to l ead and its inorganic 
compounds.

The studies carried out by the International Lead Association show that there is a wide 
range of results in managing employee lead exposure in companies producing and using 
lead in the EU. Moreover, as the consultant (working on behalf of DG EMPL) in the Impact 
Assessment for lead has already recognized, primary metal producers that are processing 
dusty lead containing ores and concentrates currently employ significantly more workers 
with blood lead levels above 15 µg / dL,  than recycling companies and other sectors. 
Therefore, while a health-protective BLV of 15 µg / dL may eventually be technically 
feasible for most companies, it can be a significant and disproportionate challenge  
for primary lead producers. 
The smelting of source materials from primary sources requires more production steps 
than the production of lead from recycled materials. The technological process  
of smelting lead from primary sources also leads to the generation of waste, which carries 
harmful chemi cal compounds. Production from primary sources is associated with  
a multi -stage refining process related to separation from other metals with similar 
physical and chemical properties.  
Summary 
Our success related to the fulfillment of the currently in force very strict, binding OEL 
standard for lead in Poland results from the implementation of wide -range activities 
aimed at protecting the health of employees of KGHM. In the period 2013 -2021,  
the company modernized the existing production installations or built new ones. In 2016,  
a completely new flash furnace and electricity installations were launched at the Głogów 
I Copper Smelter, considered BAT. Additionally, many investments related to the 
encapsulation of industrial processes were carried out. 
The same activities mentioned above are carried out in terms of meeting the standards 
set out for BLV. As a primary lead producer, we believe that the proposed new BLV value 
of 15 µg / dL and 4.5 µg / dL is unacceptable to us. Meeting the requirements of this 
standard by primary lead producers, set at such a low level, is practically impossible with 
the current state of the technique. Because it would require further hermetization  
of the process with the elimination of standard technical and technological operations 
related to the production of metals. These activities would additionally multiply the cost 
of primary lead production as compared to secondary lead production.  
In the coming years, KGHM Polska Miedź S.A. is planning further investment projects that 
will be implemented in the metallurgy area (some have already started), which will have  
an impact on further improvement of working conditions and protection of the working 
environment of employees exposed to lead.

Therefore, we support the proposal of the International Lead Association, which proposes 
an OEL value for lead at the level of 0.05 mg / m3. At the same time, in our opinion  
the position of the RAC Committee for the OEL value for lead of 0.004 mg / m3  
is unacceptable, also due to the particularly negative impact on the production of lead, 
copper and other associated metals in Europe.  
Tightening the OEL and BLV standards for lead would also put into question the 
profitability of our production, especially as already 5 years ago we practically changed 
the production technology in one of our divisions, which operates as BAT. In our opinion, 
such tightening of both standards is not economically viable, nor is it technically feasible 
at present for any lead production plant in the world.  
When new lead hygiene standards are established, it is also very important to ensure 
sufficient time - transition periods - to allow primary source lead producers to make  
the necessary changes to the processes related to the management of exposure to lead 
in the workplace, organizational and technological changes. However, in the case of BLV, 
for the values w e propose, the transition periods should be, respectively, 5 years for  
the 35 µg / dL standard and a minimum of 10 years for the 20 µg / dL standard from  
the entry into force of the Chemical Agents Directive (CAD). 
From our point of view it is very important to emphasize the differences related to 
working conditions and technological limitations between primary lead producers 
and companies dealing with the recycling and processing of lead.

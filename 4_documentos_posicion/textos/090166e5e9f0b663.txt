Introduction  
Aurubis is a leading worldwide provider of non-ferrous metals. We process complex metal concentrates and diverse 
recycling raw materials. Aurubis is the global leader for copper recycling.  
Aurubis welcomes the possibility to provide an input to the feedback to the study on collecting information on lead with 
the view to analyse health, socio -economic and environmental impacts in connection with possible amendments of 
Directive 98/24/EC (Chemical Agents)  
Summary of position  
 
- We do support the update of the existing EU binding occupational exposure limits for lead.  
- EU binding exposure limit value for Pb shall be proportional and based on adequate consideration of 
the socio-economic and technical feasibility factors as well as the role of lead as enabler of circular 
economy and green energy transition. 
- The biological limit value should be the primary tool for the protection of workers from occupational lead 
exposures. 
- There is a need to secure the future safe use of lead, otherwise Europe will not be able to produce and 
recycle strategic metals for the green transition.  
- Lead metal is a key enabler for the circular economy as carrier metal in recycling of numerous non -ferrous 
metals, including precious and special metals.  
- Focus on substitution will endanger the future development of the circular economy and the transition to a 
green economy given the potential negative impact on recycling.  
- Excessive cost to comply with unrealistically low exposure levels will have a negative impact on the 
competitiveness of the EU industry and may lead to loss of recycling capacity of valuable raw materials  
 
Lead is present in nature   
Different metals occur together in nature. Hence, the primary materials as well as the secondary materials of one 
metal will contain a number of other metals, including both base metals and precious metals. The same applies to 
post-consumer wastes, with metals being used in combination in various products.  Lead ores and concentrates 
contain many valuable metals such as copper, precious metals and minor metals. Lead is also present in most 
copper concentrates as well as in most secondary copper raw materials and end -of life products 
Lead is an enabler for circular economy  
Copper and lead are carrier metals in both primary production and recycling for many of the critical and valuable 
metals needed for today’s sustainable technologies. Negative impact on these metallurgies would make the EU 
circular economy difficult to realize, as it would diminish the EU’s capacity to recycle complex materials as well as 
supply critical metals using its own sources. The EU economy benefits from the recovery of metals ena bling them to 
be put to new use. This has numerous health and environmental benefits, for example by reducing demand for virgin 
product that often has high impacts at the point of extraction and during manufacturing.  
The increasing complexity of consumer products require an industrial network of metallurgical production to 
maximize recovery of all elements in end-of-life products. The carrier metal properties of copper and lead make the 
use of copper and lead metallurgy an efficient and effective way to recycle a broad range of recycling raw materials 
of various qualities and composition.  
Our plants serve as the end of the recycling chain for complex metal-based materials such as copper and copper 
alloy scrap, electric and electronic waste, circuit boards, shredder materials, various industrial residues/intermediates 
such as copper-bearing residues , slimes, slags, ashes and filter dust.  
Complexity of input materials is increasing through the evolution of modern technologies and miniaturization. 
Recycling those complex and diverse streams is a significant challenge and continuous investments and adaptations 
to changing recycling materials are required. The modern processing facilities in Aurubis also enable the input of 
industrial residues and waste that would formerly have found their way to landfills. At the same time, no production-
related waste arises in the recycling plants. Various procedures enable Aurubis to process and convert the wide 
range of recycling materials into products in a competitive and environmentally sound manner.  
High environmental , safety and health requirements are implemented on a continuously ambitious level. Aurubis 
operations comply with the best available techniques and extensive legislation, supported in part by voluntary 
initiatives, that is designed to protect workers, the general public and the wider environment.

Aurubis applies highly developed metallurgical refining processes in different combinations as part of multi -metal 
recycling. In this way, a wide range of additional valuable metals besides copper are recovered such as precious 
metals, lead, tin, nickel sulphate, selenium, tellurium , iron silicate.  
An ambitious process improvement and emission reduction program with a number of measures has been 
established during last years. More than 630 million Euro were invested in environmental protection measures in 
Aurubis group since 2000.  
Lead is present in many scrap materials, conferring important mechanical properties on alloys and products. It exist 
in nature, with the result that secondary materials contain a mixture of different elements, including lead. Lead will 
continue to be present in waste materials for the foreseeable future, as a result of the materials that are 
already in circulation.  
Smelters have very little potential to reduce lead content or avoid the lead containing raw materials as this 
would affect not only the lead, but also other valuable metals present in the same streams, or whose 
recovery is linked to the lead properties and thus will make the recycling process not possible or not 
efficient.  
Disproportional measures will impact the global competitiveness of the lead and copper industry in Europe 
Prices of metals are listed globally on the stock exchange (London Metal Exchange). Price level for metals from 
primary or secondary production is the same. In the face of strong international competition, smelters cannot 
pass on additional costs resulting from national or European legislation to customers but must instead 
compensate for this with continuous increases in production and efficiency. However, this is increasingly 
reaching the limits of technical possibility.  
On the other hand, access to materials for recycling is already big challenge for European companies and export is 
already happening of materials for recycling due to the high intrinsic value.  
Unrealistic binding occupational exposure limits for lead and associated excessive compliance cost would lead to 
less efficient and less competitive non-ferrous metals recycling industry for complex materials. The very real threat is 
that these materials would then be landfilled, which could have an overall negative e nvironmental impact. Another 
possibility is that lead materials would likely be exported for recycling outside EU, which represents a loss of value to 
the European economy and shift lead exposure to regions with lower quality recycling and lower standards on 
environmental and human health protection. 
Impact on manufacture and recycling of copper alloys 
Aurubis has a broad portfolio of alloys, including over 60 made of special copper materials, brass alloys, the 
phosphor bronzes, and special alloys. Copper alloys generally contain lead. The lead content can vary from below 
0.1% in some ‘lead-free’ alloys such as BlueBrass®, where it is present in the final product only as an impurity, to 4% 
where it is used as a functional alloying element, for example in leaded brass (free cutting brass).  
Various risk management measures are present to prevent or reduce exposure for workers and to the general public 
and the environment.  
It is appropriate to highlights the potential for significant costs to be incurred if alternative input materials, or 
alternative ways of operating are used for the production of ‘lead free’ and leaded copper alloys.  
To avoid contamination with lead, the substitution of virgin metals for recyclate when manufacturing alloys would 
increase costs and lead to a significant loss of competitiveness with non-EU producers. It also has wider societal 
consequences for the Circular Economy in terms of making recycling unattractive.  
It must be noted that in recent years, the copper alloy industry has spent significant resources developing lower lead 
containing solutions (such as BlueBrass®, developed by Aurubis), though has not removed lead altogether from 
many applications, because of the utility provided by lead, including safety critical uses, for example in transport. 
Uncertainty of derivation of air limit from the biological limit value 
The biological limit value should be the primary tool for the protection of workers from occupational lead exposures. 
There no simple correlation between blood lead concentrations and air levels as factors such as hygiene and 
behavior plays important role. The decades of experience illustrates that risk management measures based upon 
internal dose (i.e blood lead measurements) are the most effective method of controlling lead exposure in the 
workplace. 
The approach to derive an OEL using the ratio of the existing BLV and BOELV is unsound and lacks scientific rigor.

It must be understood that there is an extremely high level of uncertainty in deriving air lev els based upon blood lead 
measurements. The currently proposed OEL value of 4 μg/m3 (8hr TWA) fails to adequately account for this 
uncertainty and would result in excessive cost to industry whilst failing to reduce significantly the numbers of workers 
with blood lead levels below the target values.

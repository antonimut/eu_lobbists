- Boliden Response - 
 
 
 
For more than 90 years, Boliden has been exploring for, extracting and processing 
metals and precious metals based on experience, innovation and modern technology. 
We are a part of the non -ferrous metals industry and o ur main products are zinc, 
copper, lead, nickel, gold and silver.  
At Boliden we consider a healthy and safe work environment, value driven leadership 
and diversity and equal opportunities to be some of the most important success factors 
to be able to fulfil our purpose  – to provide the metals essential for society for 
generations to come, by being the most climate friendly and respected metal provider 
in the world. For that reason, w e welcome an update of t he E U binding OEL and 
biological limit value (BLV) for lead and its compounds.  
We are members of ILA and support the “ILA position paper - protecting workers from 
Occupational Lead Exposures ” and their response to this call for evidence. But as a 
multi-metal producer, we want to lift the fact that lowered OEL and BLV for lead and its 
compounds will have an impact on not only the lead metal producing part of our 
smelters, but also on the parts producing e.g. copper and zinc  where lead is present 
as a naturally substance in the raw material.  
We realize the hazards on human health when working with lead metal and  we have 
spent much resource in our efforts to minimize the risks connected to lead metal in our 
plants. Improvements of and install ment of  new ventilation, local exhaust systems , 
building of new control rooms are a few exa mples of investments during the last few 
years, with a cost of several hundred thousand  euros. The effect of this on the  
concentration of lead in air is low; howev er, in paral lel with the investments into

Although we estimate that the binding limits of 50 µg/m3 of lead in air and 15 µg/dl of 
lead in blood are achievable, we have some concerns connected to this. 
 
Key concerns 
Challenges we will be facing if the OEL and BLV are decreased even further are to find 
protective equipment, with a higher protection factor , while being suitable for our hot 
environments. In order to make  a large  impact on the air lead concentrations , we 
would, as a worst-case need new buildings  and facilitie s to enable to  further 
development of our processes, with ventilation that is more efficient, more automation 
and easier separation between clean and dirtier zones. This would mean a cost in the 
order of magnitude of € 50 million with an added annual cost of €1 -5 million. These 
costs include not only our lead producing facilities, but also the parts producing other 
metals where lead is present.  
Another challenge is the fact that a large amount (up to 50%) of our workforce on our 
lead producing smelters are long time employees, who  have been working in our 
industry for over 15 years. The time it will take for any effect to occur on the blood lead 
levels, when lowering the air levels, is of course longer for these long time employees.  
 
Boliden recommendations 
Although we estimate that binding limits of 50 µg/m3 of lead in air and 15 µg/dl of lead 
in blood are achievable, large  investments and time is required to make the 
improvements necessary to achieve this. 
With this in mind, we support the ILA suggestion of a stepwise transition after inclusion 
in the Chemical agents directive: 
For BLV for Lead: 
20ug/dL with a 5-year transition after inclusion in CAD 
15ug/dL with an 8-year transition after inclusion in CAD 
 
OEL for Lead: 
75 ug/m³ (8hr TWA) with a 5-year transition after inclusion in CAD 
50 ug/m³ (8hr TWA) with an 8-year transition after inclusion in CAD

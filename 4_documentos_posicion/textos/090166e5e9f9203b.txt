Introduction 
Bitkom welcomes the opportunity to contribute to the public consultation on the Euro-
pean Media Freedom Act (“EMFA”) and understands that the European Commission 
seeks to collect views from stakeholders on the most important issues affecting the func-
tioning of the internal media market, including different types of interference in media, 
as well as economic trends. Still, the potential scope of the EMFA remains vague, making 
it challenging to provide targeted answers for the consultation. Since far-reaching regu-
latory requirements to ensure media diversity and transparency already exist at EU and 
member state level, the need for additional regulation is not apparent. In particular, the 
European Commission’s intention to add another regulatory layer to the Audiovisual Me-
dia Service Directive (“ AVMSD”), Digital Services Act (“ DSA”) and Digital Markets Act 
(“DMA”) seems counterintuitive, as these obligations have not been adopted or fully 
transposed in EU Member States yet, which would allow the proper impact assessment 
needed to justify additional regulation.   
As such, Bitkom has decided to call for evidence where necessary and to suggest a clearer 
distinction between news/ information communications in media and entertainment 
services. Bitkom has provided its contributions drawing from its experience with the Ger-
man media market and has answered EU -wide questions to the best of its knowledge, 
however acknowledges Bitkom may be missing some data it is unaware of. 
It is important to keep in mind the original motivation for regulation when assessing the 
answers to the questionnaire; to protect media from undue influence from governments. 
Bitkom believes the best way to ensure media plurality is free competition from multiple 
private offerings, promoting different views an d perspectives. As long as none of the 
market players is dominant (i.e. the traditional press model), regulatory intervention 
should be the exemption which requires evidence -based justification. Due to the vast 
amount of services and devices that provide access to media content, there is no bottle-
neck in the entertainment sector that justifies and/or requires a proactive shaping of 
media offerings by state intervention (which might have been the case for early elec-
tronic mass media (TV) due to scarcity of resources and high costs for market entry and 
service production and distribution , but these limiting factors no longer apply  to the

Safeguarding the EU internal media market, media independence and pluralism  
Bitkom supports the European Commission’s view that media independence and pluralism 
are essential for functioning democracies. However, we would like to emphasize that a dis-
tinction must be made between news/ information and entertainment media services. 
While it may be justified to establish a higher degree of precautionary measures with regard 
to editorial independence when regulating the news/information sector, there is no com-
parable threat to the influence on public opinion formation for media services in the enter-
tainment sector. Therefore, it is important to conduct an evidence-based assessment of the 
status quo to define targeted political goals and measures the EMFA seeks to achieve.  
▪ Media concentration and ownership: Bitkom believes there is no evidence for market fail-
ure related to concentration of media ownership which justifies new regulation in the 
media entertainment sector. For instance, in the video market sector, the choice of con-
tent offers and platforms has increased to an unprecedented level.  New international 
players are entering the market. At the same time, a large and growing number of local 
providers co-exist alongside free-to-air traditional players with their own specific offer-
ings. Every service provider attempts to offer the best cust omer experience, offer, and 
content proposition allowing it to differentiate its proposals from other providers. There 
is more plurality of media services now than ever before as a direct result of competition. 
In addition, in Germany, there have been no cases, except one (Axel Springer/ProSieben - 
the intervention was held to be without basis and was consequently cancelled by a court 
ruling.), where the German Commission on the Assessment of Concentration in the Me-
dia (KEK), competent for concentration in the TV sector, had a reason to intervene. 
 
▪ Rules restricting market-entry or operation: Bitkom agrees with the Commission’s obser-
vation that the media sector in the EU is not as integrated as it could be and therefore 
fails to capitalise on its untapped potential for growth. Bitkom welcomed the AVMSD as 
it aims to harmonize European media policy. Consistent European rules strengthen the 
Single Market by ensuring predictability and reliability, which are crucial for our mem-
bers' business activities. However, Member States increasingly undermine the country-
of-origin principles of the AVMSD and tend to interpret EU rules in an onerous way which 
leads them to go beyond their scope (“gold plating”), leading to fragmentation in the sin-
gle market and undue operational burdens for (cross-border) services. This is particularly

the case regarding prominence obligations for content of general interest and European 
works, as well as for levies and investment obligations in local content which constitute 
market-entry barriers. The new media laws on Member States level (e.g. Germany and 
France), among others, require the production of hardware and software specifically for 
certain EU territories as they apply the marketplace principle (vs. the country -of-origin 
principle). As a result, in addition to fragmenting the internal market even further, the 
new media laws create undue compliance burdens for (cross-border) media companies, 
platform service providers and device manufacturers, eventually reducing media plural-
ism and customer choice in the EU media entertainment sector.  
 
▪ Areas for action at EU level: Bitkom does not believe there is a need for further regulations 
to be added to existing media law, especially in the entertainment sector. Instead, Bitkom 
suggests the best way to prevent legal fragmentation and undue burdens for media ser-
vice providers (content and platforms) as well as for device manufacturers is to improve 
the functioning of the internal media market and to better enforce the country-of-origin 
principle. Existing mechanisms between Member States and the EU should be reinforced 
in order to provide a harmonized, proportionate and appropriate implementation of Eu-
ropean rules with the aim to promote media pluralism at Member State level (particularly 
in relation to prominence obligations for content of general interest). The TRIS procedure 
has not proven itself to be particularly effective in fostering media pluralism in the EU. 
Instead, market entry and (cross-border) operations have become more burdensome and 
costlier. 
 
 
Transparent and independent media markets  
▪ Transparency of media ownership: From a German perspective, media ownership is suf-
ficiently transparent. However, it would be beneficial to collect this information and 
make it accessible in one central place (e.g., via the European Council’s European Audio-
visual Observatory by way of an expansion of its MAVISE database). In some EU regions 
or Member States, the concentration level may already be significant, but this should not 
be addressed at EU level unless it is necessary to ensure that affected regional or national 
markets remain accessible for non-domestic players. 
 
▪ Assessing the impact of media transactions on media pluralism: Bitkom does not consider 
new EU-level action nece ssary in relation to media market transactions. Assessing the 
impact of media transactions on pluralism remains possible, at Member State level, under 
the EU Merger Regulation. This mechanism works and should be retained as is. It cannot 
be centralized because the regulator must be government-independent and knowledge-
able of the relevant local market.

▪ Media market scrutiny procedures and restrictions to media market entry and operation: 
Bitkom believes that German media regulation (e.g. Media State Treaty introducing new 
obligations on prominence, transparency and notification of the service in relation to me-
dia platforms, user interfaces and media intermediaries) imposes requirements on media 
platform service providers, media intermediaries and device manufacturers that are in 
some cases disproportionately high by European standards. In addition, the interpreta-
tion of European requirements, e.g. AVMSD, is relatively strict, excessive and possibly in 
conflict with European Union law (country-of-origin principle). German regulations may 
create serious obstacles to the free movement of goods and services in the internal mar-
ket.  
 
▪ Audience measurement: Audience measurement should not be limited to one methodol-
ogy and must reflect different types of media consumption that differs heavily by gener-
ation, education and from Member State to Member State. Whatever measure is taken, 
the general confidentiality of business data needs to be respected and should therefore 
be restricted to areas where more transparency is needed for particular regulatory needs. 
It should be acknowledged that some business models, such as ad -financed media, re-
quire more transparency. In other areas, such as non-linear services which rely less on the 
broad success of individual titles, plurality can be enhanced through the presence of dis-
tinctive or alternative content. This could be put at risk if too strict reporting on viewing 
is required. To ensure fair competition where audience measurement is relevant for spe-
cific business models, national competition authorities can look at the audience meas-
urement standards from an anti-trust perspective, as they have done already in certain 
cases to remove entry barriers. Competition law provides a solid basis to address this, if 
there are any concerns.  
 
 
Conditions for healthy media markets  
▪ Regulatory convergence and cooperation: Bitkom is of the opinion that the consultation 
process under Art. 3, 4 AVMS -D is impractical for cross-border enforcement; this proce-
dure could be streamlined. Bitkom welcomes the cooperation of NRAs in ERGA but sug-
gests a need to improve cooperation and legally binding enforcement: obstacles in the 
cooperation between national regulators result from (1) the l ack of full AVMSD imple-
mentation in all Member States and (2) lack of enforcement agreements between EU 
Member States for legal action/enforcement e.g. in the section of minor protection (de-
leting, or even blocking of illegal content).  
 
▪ EU-level action to  be taken: Bitkom supports the harmonization of key areas of media 
regulation relevant for media pluralism as long as it is limited to areas where there is a

justified and evidence-based threat to regulatory convergence which necessitates inter-
vention. For instance, Bitkom urges the EU to strengthen mechanisms and processes to 
enforce the country -of-origin principle more coherently under the AVMSD and the E-
Commerce Directive. Particularly, providers of user interfaces (“UI”) for access to media 
content need clear and fully harmonized requirements across the EU that enable con-

must be reflected on user interfaces (media (platform) service providers and devices) is

▪ Balanced media coverage:  Bitkom believes that in order to  promote democracy and a 
healthy media market at local, regional, national and EU levels, any lack of balanced me-
dia coverage or dominating viewpoints must be avoided. This is particularly the case in 
the run-up to elections, including elections to the European Parliament. 
 
Fair allocation of state resources in the media markets: Independence of public service me-
dia 
In many EU Member States, public service broadcasters (“PSB”) are either under enormous 
pressure to justify their actions o r are being restructured/ politically instrumentalized in 
such a way that they can no longer fulfil their function in the media and democratic system. 
While respecting the programming freedom of the broadcasters, a readjustment of the ir 
role and remit is urgently needed. Particularly in view of the special privileges granted to 
them, a high degree of responsibility must be demanded of the broadcasters. Therefore, 
Bitkom’s suggestions for reform are to focus public service offerings on their specific profile 
and to not expand them; to take into account economic efficiency and frugality when it 
comes to financial requirements; to leverage potential savings. 
 
Governance options  
Bitkom advocates for a coherent and uniform application of EU legislation. To achieve this 
goal more coordination at EU level is needed to address cooperative obstacles between na-
tional regulators that result from the lack of enforcement agreements between EU member 
states for legal action e.g. in the section of minor protection (blocking of illegal content). In

principle, it is important that at EU level a uniform application of EU law is safeguarded and 
can be enforced. Stronger governance mechanisms should be implemented to ensure har-
monized application of EU law and the application of the country of origin principle. Existing 
organisations such as ERGA could play an important role in assuring that national peculiar-
ities are respected.  
The cooperation of the national media authorities within ERGA has not resulted in a more 
effective, consistent and/or uniform application of EU legislation in the media sector. The 
issue is not the lack of cooperation between national regulators but a lack of interstate 
agreement to enforce legal acts. National media regulators should cooperate closely to en-
sure strict compliance with the country-of-origin principle. ERGA may need more resources 
to handle cross -border issues and alleged jurisdictional conflicts. An additional, fully -
fledged layer of oversight or the establishment of a fully -fledged EU-regulatory agency is 
neither necessary nor desirable. This would create additional burdens for the media indus-
try since national media regulators will not cease to exist. The Commission cannot play a 
substantial role here as it is not sufficiently government-independent.

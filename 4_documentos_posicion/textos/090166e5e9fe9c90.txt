 
Sky is delighted to answer this public consultation. The Media Freedom Act can be important to 
tackle clear and clearly identified problems, in particular with undue interference with the 
independence of media. 
Sky is Europe’s leading media and entertainment company and is proud to be part of 
Comcast Corporation, a global media and technology company that connects people to moments 
that matter. Across six countries, Sky connects 23  million customers to the best e ntertainment, 
sports, news, arts and to our own award-winning original content.  
The MFA consultation takes place at a key mo ment. There has never been more content written, 
filmed and produced in Europe than during the last few years by private broadcasters and 
streamers, of which Sky is a key European player.  
This golden age is occurring not thanks to regulation, but in some cases despite limitations placed, 
in particular gold plating that created significant barriers to the internal market by having very 
different national systems that go for beyond the goals of the AVMS. We have seen a proliferation 
of “country of destination” rules that have in essence bypassed European legislation to place almost 
limitless new rules on providers. The MFA could address this issue – not with new regulation, but by 
rolling back the possibility to use transposition on European Directives such as AVMS to impose far 
broader rules on players. If this were to happen, we would quickly see that many European markets 
would once again become viable investment opportunities for European players, not simply the 
largest global ones. This would in turn lead  to far more creation and investment and jobs, which is 
ultimate goal for all.  
Key to Europe’s diversity is the fact that private broadcaster content  (viewed either on linear, 
catchup or also on streaming services) is often of extremely powerful benefit to society and viewers. 
The MFA can enshrine that fact – that not only public broadcaster content can be of public value, 
whether it be news and factual or entertainment.  
The MFA can focus on ensuring the independence of public service media while also ens uring the 
independence and continue strength of commercial broadcasters and streamers in a market that 
has never been so competitive.  
Another area of critical concern is the regulatory gold plating that has occurred in many EU Member 
States. This has unfo rtunately broken much of the coherent and uniform application of EU 
legislation and has harmed the internal market as well as cost significant investment in Europe and 
European content production. It has hobbled the growth of parts of the sector.  
Areas for the MFA: 
1. To tackle the egregious attempts by some governments to unduly interfere in the 
independence of both public service media and private media. These examples are well 
documented.  
2. We believe that the MFA can be a helpful tool to expand the independence and capabilities 
of ERGA, which has proven itself to be a highly positive presence in the media regulation 
space. Thus we support the idea that ERGA be strengthened to support the Digital Single 
Market vs. protectionist policies of NRAs and ensure th e enforcement of the country of 
origin principle vs. the country of destination principle. However, the EU Commission is not

sufficiently state -independent to play a role as media regulatory body. Regulators on 
national level even if there are diverse resp onsibilities in one country (14 state media 
authorities in Germany) cooperate in an effective and sufficient way together 
3. More cooperation between regulators can only be a positive. On area in which this is very 
clearly the case is to tackle piracy, where despite years of discussions, we have yet to see a 
clear European approach, European legislation and European cooperation. The MFA can help 
to lay the foundations by strengthening ERGA to tackle piracy and by creating the right 
structures and independence to achieve real results.  
4. The AVMS had been seen as an opportunity to liberalise the European media space while 
placing real regulation on VSPS. That was not achieved but the goal remains there and would 
be a positive outcome for Europe  and one the MFA cou ld address by expanding rules to 
VSPs in a uniform manner.  
However, we caution against expanding the scope of the Act. We do not believe the MFA should 
enter detailed sectoral or cross sectoral new regulation including: 
1. Prominence discussions: these have already taken place with the European Accessibility 
Act, the AVMS, the DSA and DMA. The MFA is not the place to continue these discussions.  
2. Content production or ownership rules: this is a large area and the MFA should not enter 
this space as no detailed studies have been undertaken.  
3. Diversity of media: again, the creative sector value chain is highly complex and adding in new 
rules here that have not been modelled could disrupt a sector that is in full growth and 
health.  
4. Contractual freedom: the cornerstone of the sector. Too many instruments have begun to 
impinge on contractual freedom, and certain national legislation has gone significantly 
further than Europe rules. If these types of rules and over -reach continue to occur, we 
question why any European legislation is needed at all, as each Member State will simply be 
able to institute completely different rules.  
a. We do suggest re-enshrining a robust defence of the country-of-origin principle and 
common European rules that cannot be circumvented or misused by exemptions. 
There is significant friction today and we suggest not introducing the possibility of 
more friction or diminishing of the European approach.  
5. Regulating devices: the MFA is not the place to go into these very detailed issues that have 
far wider ramifications on operating system s, devices, interoperability, data, commercial 
freedom and customer choice. Each of these areas is already a highly regulated area.  
6. Audience measurement: this issue linked to advertising is already addressed in many 
legislative tools. It is also an issue of great importance for contractual freedom . Rules 
already regulate our sectors so forcing new data sharing will not help. Rather rules could be 
foreseen for VSPs.  
7. Transparency of ownership: this is not a significant issue. Media consolidation is indeed 
occurring, as is consolidation in most sectors. It is healthy and can lead to more competition, 
more pan-European offers and availability of content. One of the ways in which the EU’s goal 
of the MAAP can be achieved is with stronger players growing across Europe, which is why 
the MFA should not halt ambitious players, nor should it be used to place an protectionist 
measures on companies entering the market.

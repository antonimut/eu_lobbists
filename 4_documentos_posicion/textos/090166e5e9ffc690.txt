 
 
March 22, 2022 
 
EU Consultation: Sustainable Consumption of Goods – Promoting 
Repair and Use 
 
Sonae is a multinational corporation  managing a wide portfolio  of businesses in retail, financial 
services, technology, wood-based panels, shopping centres and telecommunications , creating value 
across several geographic areas.  
We have a sense of commitment to the place we inhabit, and we seek to reduce the enviro nmental 
impact resulting from our activity, in shops and in other company facilities, adopting progressively 
efficient practices in all operations. 
We want to contribute increasingly to a sustainable global footprint, managing our activity towa rds 
decarbonisation and zero waste. We continuously seek to ensure a responsible and informed offer, 
acting across the entire value chain. 
Thus, we recognise the importance of the Commission's proposal and the need to increase the 
circularity of goods, namely by promoting their repair and use.  
 
Sonae's main concerns regarding the intention of this initiative, as well as its conjunction with the New 
Consumer Agenda, are: 
1. We believe that should be foreseen in the proposal communication and awareness-raising 
actions for consumers on the benefits of repairing products and, consequently, increasing 
their useful life.  
2. We believe that there should be tax exemptions or benefits for the repair, reconditioning 
or sale of used goods to promote this solution. 
3. The intention to restart the legal guarantee period after repair does not seem appropriate 
to us. This right could result in a significant increase in the legal guarantee period to be 
borne by the business, since the costs cannot be charged to the consumer. 
4. Finally, we have difficulty with the proposal for a new right to claim repair in situations 
not covered by the existing legal guarantee scheme. The producer or distributor should 
not be liable for non-compliance if the consumer is responsible for it, and even more so if 
it is not covered by the guarantee.   
 
As regards the contradict ions between this initiative and the New Consumer Agenda, we would 
highlight the following: 
1. The period of replacement of the product in the first 30 days is not in line with the value 
of sustainability that the initiative aims to address.

2. The obligat ion to repair should be considered whenever technically possible and 
economically viable. 
3. Whenever replacement is the only possible solution, the possibility of substituting the 
good by a reconditioned product should be foreseen. 
4. We consider that the  absence of special conditions for return within the first 30 days, 
namely returning the good with all manuals, accessories, and original boxes, does not 
guarantee the repair and subsequent placing on the market of the product. 
5. The joint liability for re pairs for Marketplace sales, given the multiple geographies, does 
not guarantee the effective repair of the equipment, being more likely the replacement. 
6. It does not seem reasonable to us to establish the same legal guarantee period for new 
and second-hand and/or refurbished goods. The assumption of a greater risk, in the case 
of used goods, for the same period, implies an increase in prices for used goods, which is 
contrary to the spirit of the initiative.

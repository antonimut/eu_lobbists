  
 
Brussels, 22 March 2022 
 
EPTA Position on the EU asbestos initiative 
 
About the European Power Tools Association 
The European Power Tool Association (hereafter “EPTA”) represents 25 European power tool 
manufacturers who stand for more than 60.000 employees in Europe and ca. 85% of all corded 
and cordless electric hand-held power tools sold on the European market.  
The power tools manufactured by EPTA members companies are often used by professionals, 
skilled tradesmen and DIY (do it yourself) consumers on construction sites and at home for 
renovation and demolition works.  Therefore, EPTA would like to take the opportunity to 
comment on the new EU asbestos initiative as its members are well aware of the reality of 
construction sites and contexts where people could potentially be exposed to asbestos fibres. 
 
The need for an implementation-oriented European asbestos policy 
EPTA welcome s the EU’s objective to increase worker protection against asbestos while 
ensuring a greater level playing field for economic operators. Fatal cases linked to asbestos  
are regrettably high and the fragmentation of asbestos measures in EU Member States does 
not lead to a uniform protection of workers within the EU. 
When evaluating the potential solutions to this problem, EPTA member companies and their 
customers observe that the excessive exposure to asbestos fibres is mainly a result of the lack 
of knowledge and protection. Exposure ultimately depends on the awareness and handling of 
the asbestos concentration on a given worksite : workers and those who are responsible for 
construction sites need Guidelines and Best Practices as well as a cost-efficient way to apply 
effective measures of protection, avoid exposure and the consequences thereof. 
Simply revising the OEL would be an insufficient stand-alone measure to achieve more worker 
protection without job-site-suitable rules. The practical side of exposure, meaning the effective 
enforcement of the OEL is far more important to reduce exposure than a simple de jure change 
of the OEL itself.  
In this respect, EPTA considers that the non -legislative policy option is going to be the most 
effective one to truly reduce asbestos expos ure. For example, an EU-wide campaign (linked 
to the EU renovation wave) or coordinated educative measures could be a good means of 
raising awareness and increasing monitoring, measuring and protection. 
Some EPTA manufacturers are also testing their dust extraction systems  and power tools in 
connection with asbestos. These could one day be certified and serve as an effective means 
of significant exposure reduction. 
Overall, EPTA would be glad to put its construction -site-know-how at the EU Commission’s 
disposal to help develop the most effective and financially viable EU asbestos policy making 
construction sites as safe as possible for workers.

EPBD : Recommendations and amendment proposals 
by EdEn  
EdEn welcomes the draft recast of the directive on the energy performance of buildings 
(EPBD) proposed by the Commission. The proposal introduces important evolutions in the 
EPBD, notably as regards GHG emissions in the EU building sector.   
There are however several shortcomings in this proposal and EdEn would like to make the 
following recommendations to address them: 
 
Article 2 (2) Including low -carbon energy and renewables provided by the grid in the 
definition of “zero emission buildings” 
In the proposed text, zero-emission buildings are defined as buildings “where the very 
low amount of energy still required is fully covered by energy from renewable sources 
generated on-site, from a renewable energy community or from a district heating and 
cooling system”. With this provision, the renewable energy that is provided by the grid 
would not be eligible for consumption in zero -emission buildings despite Member 
States having heavily invested for the integration of renewables into their grid over the 
last years. More generally, t his provision excludes electricity  from non-fossil origin, 
despite its role in the energy transition, which is confirmed by the decision to include it 
in the green taxonomy. whose role in the energy transition  
EdEn recommends amending the proposed definition so that it includes renewables 
that are produced locally and renewables provided by the grid as well as low -carbon 
energy. A maximum threshold for GHG emission shall be set up so as to define which 
energies can be considered as low-carbon.

reason, EdEn recommends deleting this provision.

Article 15 (11) Incentivising renovation and programmes that result in final energy savings  
EdEn recommends using final energy savings, rather than primary energy savings as 
a criterion for the renovation and programmes Member States shall incentivise. Final 
energy represents the energy consumed and paid for by the consumer, which is why 
it is a more appropriate criterion in order to incentivise renovation. 
Furthermore, promoting both programmes that address a high number of buildings  
and deep renovation, which is by nature very expensive and therefore only available 
to a limited number of households, does not appear to be the most efficient pathway. 
EdEn recommends promoting equally deep renovation and staged -deep renovation 
as the latter option can be available to a greater number of households and therefore 
result in greater GHG emission reductions as a whole.

Including the life -cycle Global Warming Potential (GWP) of a building in the 
energy performance certificate 
EdEn recommends including the life -cycle Global Warming Potential (GWP) of a 
building in the energy performance certificate . This provision that is parallel to our 
recommendations on Articles 9 and 11 will maximise the contribution of energy 
performance certificates to the reduction of GHG emission of the EU building stock. 
 
In line with our previous recommendation on Article 15, EdEn recommends expressing 
the energy performance of a building by a numeric indicator of final – instead of primary 
– energy use in kWh/(m2.y) as final energy represents the energy consumed and paid 
for by the consumer whereas primary energy is a conventional criterion. However in 
order to give to Member States the possibility to use either final energy or primary 
energy, the criterion could refer to “used energy”. 
EdEn also recommends adopting different reference values for individual buildings and 
collective residential buildings.

  Recommendations for an innovative and protective European Media Freedom Act (EMFA)    21 March 2022    The gravity of the challenges related to the right to information both within the EU and in the rest of the world requires the adoption of a legislative act rather than recommendations to Member States. The tendency of some Members to distance themselves from EU values creates a dangerous democratic imbalance within the Union that mere non-binding recommendations cannot address. Information warfares also require systemic, harmonized and binding solutions.   1/ Impose democratic guarantees in the information and communication space 1.1/ Declare that the Union relies on the Partnership on Information and Democracy to promote democratic guarantees in the information space ➔ The proposed law’s preamble could refer to the Partnership, already signed by 24 EU member States, allowing the EU to take the leadership within a coalition of democracies.   1.2/ Give the Forum on Information and Democracy a mandate to evaluate the information space and make regulatory proposals ➔ The Forum could be given the following mandate: - Evaluation of the information space by the International Observatory on Information and Democracy. - Issuing of recommendations for updating regulations  ➔ The Commission could use the annual and thematic reports of the Forum to feed its initiatives.   1.3/ Establish a system of protection for the EU’s democratic information space ➔ The EMFA is the appropriate vehicle to establish a system of protection as proposed by RSF ● An equality of treatment to impose the same obligations on all audiovisual media, regardless of their distribution channels and their country of origin. These obligations would, in particular, concern respect for pluralism, honesty and independence of information, as well as respect for the dignity of individuals.

● A reciprocity mechanism would provide guarantees for access to the EU’s information space. Access to the EU for media based in a third country would depend on the degree of openness in the third country (See memo on the subject dated 25 February 2022).  ➔ The EMFA could harmonize the rules on foreign participation in the media and limit the participation of actors dependent on the authorities of closed countries. The disparity of national rules makes it possible for actors from countries hostile to journalistic freedom, pluralism and independence to acquire stakes in the European media, and weakens the EU in international negotiations.   2/ Create mechanisms to promote trustworthy news and information 2.1/ Impose a due prominence obligation on platforms with regard to reliable news and information ➔ Platforms should be required to promote trustworthy sources of information online, using tools such as the JTI. The Journalism Trust Initiative (JTI) is a market solution proposed by RSF and supported by the Commission. The JTI is now a standard established under the aegis of the European Committee for Standardization1. It would enable platforms to ensure the visibility and amplification of trustworthy online sources of information - in line with the unanimous declaration2 of EU member States on 8 March 2022, which called on platforms to take stronger measures against online disinformation, in particular the “prioritization of reliable and recognized information sources”.  2.2/ Use JTI as a normative indicator for the allocation of public funds ➔ The EMFA could promote the JTI as an eligibility criteria for the allocation of public interest media grants, whether direct (financial support) or indirect (such as tax benefits); and as a transparent tool for allocating state advertising.  ➔ The EMFA could promote the use of the the JTI by member States as a reference for the allocation of media development aid. 2.3/ Promote transparency in private sector’s investments in the media ➔ Building on the EU Code of Practice on Disinformation’s requirements and benchmarks for transparency, the EMFA should extend those to the advertising sector and particularly to intermediaries like ad exchanges. Based on mechanisms like the JTI, the usage of trustworthiness indicators and the resulting re-monetization of public interest journalism should be encouraged.  3/ Include measures to ensure sustainable quality journalism in the digital age 3.1/ Incorporate the New Deal for Journalism’s recommendations  1   CEN Workshop Agreement CWA 17493:2019 - https://rsf.org/sites/default/files/cwa17493_0.pdf 2https://presidence-francaise.consilium.europa.eu/en/news/member-states-united-in-supporting-ukraine-and-strengthening-the-eu-s-telecommunications-and-cybersecurity-resilience/   
➔ The EMFA could integrate the recommendations of the Forum’s report entitled A New Deal for Journalism3, in particular: - specific international measures on the taxation of digital platforms in order to support journalism - an undertaking by governments to earmark 1% of state development aid for support for independent media and promoting a favorable environment for them - an undertaking by governments to guarantee up to 0.1% of GDP a year for journalism 3.2/ Combat conflicts of interest and ownership concentration ➔ The EMFA could provide for ● Horizontal concentration thresholds per country and for the whole EU ● safeguards against conflicts of interest in the field of news and information.   ➔ The EMFA should also protect the independence of public service media.   3.3/ Condition the allocation of European funds on respect for media pluralism and independence ➔ The EMFA must include respect for media pluralism and independence among the conditionality criteria for the provision of European funds to Member States.    4/ Ensure the freedom and safety of journalists 4.1/ Reinforce the physical safety of journalists in Europe ➔ The EMFA should strengthen the respective capacities of the European Public Prosecutor’s Office, Europol and Eurojust to support the investigations of competent national authorities into crimes against journalists. 4.2/ Reinforce the judicial safety of journalists in Europe ➔ The EMFA should also include dedicated mechanisms to protect journalists against abusive lawsuits.  ➔ The EMFA should, from the top down, harmonize the rules governing the protection of the confidentiality of journalists’ sources and establish protection for journalists against undue surveillance, whether by the state or by private sector companies.

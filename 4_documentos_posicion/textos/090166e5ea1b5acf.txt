Position Paper by Assogestioni on the European Commission’s proposal for the 
review of the AIFM and UCITS Directives 
 
Overall, Assogestioni1 welcomes the Commission’s proposal for the revision of the 
AIFM and UCITS Directives, except for some considerations with reference to the new 
proposals on the subject of a) delegation; b) loan originating  funds; c) liquidity 
management tools; d) supervisory reporting; e) disclosure to professional investors. 
 
1. For what concerns delegation, the European Commission proposes to amend 
Article 20(1) AIFMD [Article 13(1) UCITS] to clarify that the rules on delegation apply 
to all functions listed in Annex I and to services referred to in Article 6(4) [similar 
references in UCITS].  Such a broad r eference attracts also marketing of collective 
investment undertakings and portfolio management in the UCITS/AIFMD regime for 
delegation. 
 
Assogestioni believes that the UCITS/AIFMD delegation regime should not be 
extended to the marketing of collective investment undertakings or to the provision 
of investment services that fall within the MiFID perimeter. In fact, in Italy, MiFID rules 
currently apply to these activities and services. If they were attributed to 
AIFMD/UCITS, on the other hand, in the event of the delegation of such functions and 
services, the responsibility would remain with the asset manager. 
 
The proposed amendment to Article 8(1) AIFMD [Article 7(1) UCITS] requires an AIFM 
to employ at least two full-time persons or two persons who are not employees of the 
AIFM but are nevertheless committed to carrying out the business of the AIFM on a 
full-time basis and who would be resident in the Union.  In the opinion of this 
Association, this provision is not considered sufficient to ensure the req uirement of 
“substance” (especially in the case of large asset managers), the application of a 
principle of proportionality based on the size of the asset manager and the nature of 
its activities should be required instead. 
 
It is proposed to amend Article  7(5) AIFMD [Article 13(3) UCITS], to allow ESMA to 
receive, from the competent authorities, at least on an annual basis, notifications of 
delegation agreements in which a more significant part of the risk or portfolio 
management than that maintained is delegated to third country entities. Assogestioni 
considers it reasonable that national authorities notify ESMA only of extra -EU 
agreements, since the provision is mainly aimed at monitoring the delegations 
entrusted in extra -EU countries where controls are lower than those of the EU.  
However, it would be necessary to clarify what is meant by the expression “more than 
it retains” also in connection with the provision referred to in Article 82(1) (d) of the 
Del. Reg. 231/2013.

2. For what concerns loan originating funds, it is proposed that AIFs are required to 
maintain an economic interest of 5% of the notional value of the loans they originated 
and subsequently sold on the secondary market [Article 15(4e) AIFMD]. We believe 
that the 5% measure on an ongoing basis per se  is not positive. More precisely, 
Assogestioni believes that it makes sense to maintain a threshold (to be quantified, 
but it could be also 5%), but not “ on an ongoing basis” as required by the proposed 
provision, but for a limited period of time (to be quantified) and with some exceptions. 
 
The proposal provides for a limit not exceeding 20% of the capital of the AIF when the 
borrower is a financial undertaking or a collective investment undertaking pursuant 
to Article 4(1)(a) of the AIFMD or 1(2) of the UCITS [Article 15(4a) AIFMD]. Assogestioni 
suggests clarifying that this limit will be calculated taking int o account the ultimate 
underlying borrower and clarifying the fact that this limit does not apply to loans 
granted by the AIF to its affiliates, which does not represent a contagion risk to the 
financial system. Finally, considering that the funds may be s ubject to a “ramp up 
period” or “drawdown” structures, it is requested to clarify that the capital of the AIF 
refers to the “target size” of the AIF or to the commitment of its investors. 
 
3. With reference to liquidity management tools, the proposal provides that national 
competent authorities (NCAs) may, in the interest of investors or the public, require 
AIFMs to activate or deactivate a liquidity management tool (LMT) referred to in Annex 
V, point 1 or 2, or selected by the AIFM. A similar power is prop osed in Article 47, 
paragraph 4, letter d) AIFMD for ESMA with reference to non -EU AIFMs that market 
AIFs they manage in the Union or to EU AIFMs that manage non-EU AIFs. Similar rules 
(although not entirely coincident) are proposed for the review of the UCITS directive. 
About this, Assogestioni believes that the part where it is foreseen that the RTS to be 
drawn up by ESMA on the basis of Article 50(7) AIFMD [Article 84(3f) UCITS] to indicate 
the cases in which NCAs can request the activation of LMTs, shou ld be amended to 
clarify that ESMA must assess, in addition to investor protection and financial stability, 
the fact that primary responsibility for liquidity risk management must remain with 
the asset manager and NCAs must only intervene as a last resort. 
 
The proposal provides that an AIFM that manages an open-ended AIF must inform the 
competent authorities of its home Member State without delay when activating or 
deactivating a liquidity management tool referred to in the new paragraph 2b of 
Article 16 (i.e., those selected by the AIFM). Similar rules are provided for UCITS.  
Assogestioni in principle believes that, beyond suspension, the remaining LMTs can 
be used in both ordinary and exceptional circumstances. As now drafted, however, 
Article 16(2d) AIFMD [Article 84(3) UCITS] would require notification of any activation 
or deactivation of the LMTs referred to in Article 16(2b) AIFMD [Article 84(2)(a) UCITS]. 
We therefore suggest modifying the text so that the NCAs are informed only of the 
use of LMTs in situations of stress and not in the case of ordinary use provided for by 
the regulation or by the articles of association. 
 
4. As regards supervisory reporting, the proposal provides for ESMA to be tasked 
with evaluating, together with the ECB, other ESAs and national competent authorities, 
the technical feasibility of developing a common model for reporting supervisory data 
 
 
3 
 
and statistical data. More specifically, the work will focus on how to: a) reduce the 
areas of duplication and inconsistencies and b) improve the standardization, sharing 
and efficient use of data already communicated. Based on the outcome of this 
assessment (to be carried out within two years since the directive’s entry into force), 
the AIFMR reporting regime will be revised  and a harmonized reporting regime for 
UCITS will also be introduced. The Commission will then have the task of adapting 
these new regimes through delegated and implementing acts. 
 
Assogestioni agrees with the Commission’s proposal which lays the foundations for 
a radical revision of the supervisory reporting obligations at European level in the 
medium-long term, only where a favorable output is provided by the analysis to be 
carried out by ESMA, ECB, ESAs and NCAs and a reduction in reporting at n ational 
level is also envisaged. In addition, we propose to further extend the timeframe for 
both AIFMD and UCITS, as the two years foreseen for the outcome of the evaluation 
(and subsequent adoption of Commission acts) may not be sufficient for the radica l 
revision that it is proposed. We also reiterate that it should be made clear that the 
revision proposed by the Commission must lead to a reduction in reporting at national 
level. 
 
5. Disclosure to professional investors. The proposal amends Article 23 of  the 
AIFMD in order to increase the flow of information from AIFMs to AIF investors. In 
particular, it is proposed to make the following (additional) information available to 
the investors of the AIF: (i) the possibility and conditions of use of the liquid ity 
management tools selected pursuant to Article 16; (ii) a list of fees and charges that 
will be applied in relation to the AIF’s operations and that will be borne by the AIFM 
or its affiliates; (iii) the composition of the portfolio of originated loans;  (iv) on a 
quarterly basis, all fees and charges charged or allocated directly or indirectly to the 
AIF or to any of its investments; (v) on a quarterly basis, any parent company, 
subsidiary or special purpose entity established in connection with the investments 
of the AIF by the AIFM, the staff of the AIFM or the direct or indirect affiliates of the 
AIFM. 
 
With reference to the disclosure obligations referred to in points (ii) and (v), we ask to 
clarify the extent of the obligation when it is requested to communicate the fees and 
charges borne by the affiliates of the asset manager. As regards the timing of 
quarterly communications, it should be noted that it is inconsistent with the reporting 
timing of the funds and thus it should be eliminated, apply ing the general 
requirement referred to in Article 23(4), which refers to a periodic information.

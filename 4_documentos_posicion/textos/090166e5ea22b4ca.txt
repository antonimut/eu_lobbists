Position of PGE Polska Grupa Energetyczna S.A. on the proposal for a recast of the 
Directive of the European Parliament and of the Council on the energy performance 
of buildings 
Key messages 
 PGE Polska Grupa Energetyczna S.A. (hereinafter: " PGE S.A. ") acknowledges revising 
current provisions on the energy performance of buildings as another important element 
of climate and energy policy leading to the achi evement of climate neutrality of the 
European Union (hereinafter: "EU") by 2050.  
 Buildings account for 40% of energy consumed and 36% of direct and indirect greenhouse 
gas emissions associated with energy supply in the EU1. It is therefore advisable to create 
an appropriate legal framework that allows the building sector to make a greater 
contribution to new greenhouse gas reduction targets. Yet on the other hand, any new 
requirements should primarily be achievable by all Member States, taking into account 
their different starting points and characteristics of energy supply.  
 In Poland, there is a very large potential for modernisation of the individual heating 
sector, which accounts for approximately 75% of heat consumed in Poland. Bearing in 
mind the need to minimise air pollution and improve its quality, the Polish Government 
has adopted the objective of limiting the use of solid fuels in individual heating systems. 
According to Poland's Energy Policy until 20402, one of the priorities is to move away from 
coal combustion in households in cities by 2030, and in rural areas by 2040. 
 Given the role of district heating in Central and Eastern European as well as  in 
Scandinavian countries, it is crucial for the new EBPD provisions to enable greater use 
of di strict heat from efficient district heating and cooling systems, including heat 
generated in high-efficiency cogeneration. District heating produced in efficient district 
heating systems as well as heat from high -efficiency cogeneration should provide a to ol 
for the gradual decarbonisation of building sector, eliminating low -stack emissions and 
inefficient individual heating sources. For that reason, PGE S.A. encourages to enable 
connecting new buildings to every efficient district heating system after 2030, not only 
to those based on renewable energy sources (hereinafter: "RES") and waste heat.

Detailed position 
PGE S.A. acknowledges the revision of the Directive of the European Parliament and of the 
Council on the energy performance of buildings (hereinafter: "EPBD proposal")3 as another 
important element of climate and energy policy leading to the achievement of climate 
neutrality of the EU by 2050.  
Buildings account for 40% of energy consumed and 36% of direct and indirect greenhouse gas 
emissions associated with energy suppl y in the EU. It is therefore advisable to create an 
appropriate legal framework that allows the building sector to make a greater contribution to 
new greenhouse gas reduction targets. Yet on the other hand, any new requirements should 
primarily be achievab le by all Member States , taking into account their different starting 
points and characteristics of energy supply.

1. National building renovation plans (Article 3, Annex II) 
In the recast of the Directive of the European Parliament and of the Council on the energy 
performance of buildings (hereinafter: " EPBD proposal")4, the European Commission has 
introduced far-reaching changes to the developmen t of strategic documents for building  
sector. The current long -term renovation strategies will be replaced by national building 
renovation plans drawn up by Member States every 5 years, with the first draft plan to be 
submitted to the European Commission by 30 June 2024. The overarching goal of these 
national plans is to ensure that the entire EU building stock is zero-emission by 2050. 
Newly introduced Annex II of the EBPD proposal sets out a template for a national building 
renovation plan. One element of the plan is to identify policies and measures leading to the 
decarbonisation of the heating and cooling sector, including through district heating and 
cooling, and the phase out of fossil fuels in heating and cooling with a view to a complete 
phase-out by 2040 at the latest.  
The specificities of large district heating systems mean that it will not always be technically 
feasible to use only RES and waste heat, due to the need to meet the parameters required by 
heat users. It also results in a significant limitation of the available RES technologies that can 
ensure a sufficiently high temperature of heat fed into the system on a larger scale. 
Given the important constraints identified above, the EBPD proposal  should better 
recognise the diversity of district heating transition pathways in the 2050 perspective.  
2. Requirements for new buildings (Article 7, Annex III) 
In the EBPD proposal, the European Commission proposed ambitious targets for new and 
renovated buildings, both residential and public. From January 1, 2030, new and renovated 
buildings, and from January 1, 2027, new and renovated buildings occupied or owned by 
public authorities would have to be  zero-emission ones. Annex III requires that the total 
annual primary energy use of a new or renovated zero -emission building is fully covered, 
on a net annual basis, by: 
 renewable energy produced on-site; 
 renewable energy provided from renewable energy community; or 
 renewable energy and waste heat from an efficient district heating and cooling 
system. 
This means that for new and renovated zero-emission buildings, the entire primary energy 
demand could only come from such an efficient heating and cooling system where the entire 
heat volume is produced from RES or waste heat f rom as early as January 1, 2030, and for 
public buildings from January 1, 2027. It should be noted that in the case of most district

For large district heating systems located in Poland, the m ain technical barriers for a radical 
increase in RES and waste heat in the short term result from the following factors: 
• too little time for transformation to lower the required temperature parameters, 
both on the side of the district heating network and on the side of buildings, which 
is a significant obstacle in the use of low-temperature renewable heat sources; 
• lack of suitable locations for large-scale RES heat storage facilities and the lack of 
a sufficiently developed infrastructure of heating networks for the integration of 
RES to be used beyond the boundaries of urban agglomerations; 
• lack of large industrial plants or data centres in vicinity of district heating systems, 
allowing for further deploying waste heat potential; 
• significant uncertainty  on the large -scale availab ility of hydrogen and future 
conditions for using the current gas infrastructure for transporting hydrogen in 
such a short time frame; 
• the need to significantly modernise and expand the electricity infrastructure in 
order to increase the volume of RES electricity that could be used in power-to-
heat plants. 
High-efficiency natural gas or biomass cogeneration are the most preferred technologies for 
the Polish  district heating systems, given the atmospheric conditions, temperature 
parameters of district heating networks, and above all, required large in stalled capacity , 
which cannot be provided today by low-temperature RES installations. It is worth noting that 
in the future, to reduce carbon dioxide emissions  high-efficiency gas solutions will be ready 
to be adjusted to be fuelled with hydrogen or biomethane blends, or fully hydrogen to achieve 
zero emissions. However, this is closely linked to the prospects for developing the market for 
zero-carbon gaseous fuels and establishing the necessary infrastructure. 
Currently in Poland, key energy utilities, including PGE  Group, are undertaking investment 
activities concerning the replacement of coal -fired heating plants with high -efficiency 
combined heat and power plants fuelled with natural gas. Given that a typical construction 
period for a high-capacity cogeneration unit based on gaseous fuels, from the planning stage 
to final commissioning, takes around eight years, it will not be possible to fulfil the proposed 
criteria if the new requirements for zero-emission buildings come into force. 
Adopting the requirements for zero-emission buildings proposed in the draft EBPD will result 
in a degradation of existing district heating systems using heat generated from transition fuels 
such as natural gas. Consequently, by not being able to connect new buildings to efficient 
systems, the development of district heating in the broader sense will be hampered.

In the opinion of PGE S.A., the key to maintaining coherence is to combine the requirements 
for zero -emission buildings under the EBPD proposal  with the definition of an efficient 
heating and cooling system  contained in the currently discussed legislative proposal for a 
new Energy Efficiency Directive 5 (hereinafter: "EED proposal"). Article 24 par. 1  of the EED 
proposal puts forward that in 2030 an efficient heating and cooling system could still be based 
on the share of heat produced from fossil fuels, as long as the requirements in Annex III of 
the EED proposal for the qualification of cogeneration as high-efficiency are met, including a 
direct CO2 emission limit of 270 gCO 2/kWh. The proposed definition of efficient district 
heating systems assumes that energy only from RES and waste heat will be used from 1 
January 2050, in line with the climate neutrality objective. Therefore, restricting the possibility 
of meeting primary energy demand solely from RES or waste heat for efficient district heating 
systems as of 2030 is clearly ahead of the ambitions set out in the EED proposal. Under Polish 
conditions, if the proposed legislation comes into force, it will not be possible to connect new 
buildings to large district heating systems, which are key suppliers of heat to households in 
urban agglomerations.  
In the view of PGE S.A., it is crucial to extend the requirement to supply new and renovated 
zero-emission buildings broadly to heat from efficient district heating and cooling systems.

In terms of inconsistencies in the EBPD proposal , it is also worth pointing out that the 
Regulatory Scrutiny Board has twice given a negative opinion on the draft . The supporting 
documents of the EBPD proposal confirm that the Board maintained its negative opinion, due 
to the fact that the impact assessment did not clearly identify the gap to be filled by the EBPD 
proposal. Moreover,  the necessity of introducing harmonised rules at EU level given the 
heterogeneity of the building stock across Member States was not convincingly demonstrated 
and the reasons for the selection o f the preferred policy measures were not sufficiently 
explained. PGE S.A. counts on these comments being addressed by European co-legislators 
during further works on the EPBD proposal. 
Annex III in the EBPD proposal contains maximal values of total annual primary energy use of 
zero-emission buildings . An important element impacting this parameter is building’s 
geographical location, in particular the climatic zone in which it is located. Bearing in mind the 
values proposed in Annex III and the classification of individual Member States into the four 
EU climate zones, it should be no ted that the division into climate zones is of too general 
nature and lacks any indication on uniform criteria on which it is based. 
It is worth noting that Poland, for the purposes of calculating heat demand for buildings, in 
accordance with PN-EN 12831:20066, is divided into as many as five climate zones. Buildings 
located in the east ern and southern parts of the country are characterised by significantly 
higher energy con sumption than those located in Central and W estern Poland. In addition, 
the establishment of thresholds for energy consumption in buildings should take into account 
the analysis of heating degree days for individual Member States over a number of years, 
which is the main tool for reflecting the scale and variability of demand for district heating. In 
the opinion of PGE S.A., the requirements included in Annex III of the EBPD proposal should 
reflect the differences in basic calculation parameters and temperature conditions in a way 
that guarantees an objective and transparent manner of determining the value of total annual 
primary energy consumption for individual Member States. 
Annex III further proposes that the total annual primary energy consumption can be covered 
by, among others, energy from RES produced on-site. This eliminates electricity from national 
electricity grids and the exceptions provided for in the EBPD proposal  are significantly 
restrictive. In the opinion of PGE S.A., this method of covering a building's primary energy 
demand with electricity from electricity grids, which are not dedicated distribution grids, 
should be allowed as meeting the definition of a zero-emission building.

4. Financial incentives and investment barriers (Article 15) 
In the EBPD proposal, the European Commission has set out that from January 1, 2027 at the 
latest, Member States, with the exception of investments before 2027 in the European 
Regional Development Fund, the Cohesion Fund and the C ommon Agricultural Policy  
Strategic Plans, should not provide any financial incentives for the purchase and installation 
of boilers using fossil fuels , including natural gas. In the wording proposed by the 
Commission, this provision may also apply to high -efficiency gas boilers in district heating 
systems. 
It should be pointed out that high-efficiency cogeneration is the most feasible technology for 
the Polish district heating systems. In the future, in order to reduce carbon dioxide emissions, 
gas-fired high -efficiency cogeneration units will be ready for retrofit ting to burn admixed 
hydrogen or biomethane or exclusively hydrogen for zero-carbon generation. With a view to 
2027, however, there is a lack of RES technologies that can provide a sufficiently high 
temperature heat fed into heating networks on a larger scale. 
In the opinion of PGE S.A., in order to take into account different starting points of Member 
States, it should be possible to finance heat sources based on natural gas in district heating 
systems due to efficient heat production  and lower emission of  greenhouse gases  
comparing to solid fossil fuels. On the other hand, Member States should be able to support 
the installation of individual heat sources in residential buildings, including those using natural 
gas. This is particularly important where cons umers do not have access to district heating 
systems and where the potential for RES installations is limited. These provisions in the EBPD 
proposal should be consistent with the new guidelines on state aid for climate, energy and 
environmental protection9, and any restrictions on financial support for the purchase of heat 
sources should primarily apply to other fossil fuels such as coal and oil.

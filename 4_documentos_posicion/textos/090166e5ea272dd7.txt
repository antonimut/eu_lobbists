The revision of the Energy Performance of  Buildings 
Directive (EPBD) started on 15th December 2021 
with the publication of the proposition by 
 the European  Commission. The ITRE committee 
is leading, via its Rapporteur Ciarán Cuffe, to 
draft the EU Parliament’s position. The ENVI 
committee will provide an opinion. 
The recast of the EPBD is an unmissable 
 opportunity to consider building renovation in a 
holistic way, placing the ﬁre safety of EU citizens 
at its heart. The Renovation Wave initiated by the 
European Commission to reach the climate 
 objectives set by the European Green Deal will 
massively boost the renovation of the EU 
 building stock and has the opportunity to keep 
citizens ﬁre safe. After the tragedy of Grenfell in 
the UK in 2017, Members of the European 
 Parliament took action to include ﬁre safety in 
the previous EPBD revision, and now we have the 
opportunity to build on that by  integrating bett
 er 
ﬁre safety provisions into the EPBD recast.

Building Systems should include electrical in-

should ensure that electrical safety checks are

ARTICLE 8 on Existing Buildings should 
require inspections of electrical installations 
every ﬁve years and also when important

ARTICLE 16 and ANNEX V on Energy Perform-

ADDRESSING FIRE SAFETY HOLISTICALLY
RECITAL (4a) NEW: The intensiﬁcation of renovation efforts at a national level,  
aligned with the European Renovation Wave Strategy and supported by the  
Multiannual Financial Framework for 2021–2027 and the Recovery and Resilience Facility, creates  
a huge opportunity to transform our building stock to a safer and more sustainable one. 
RECITAL (33a) NEW: Fire safety aspects shall be  considered during the design, construction, 
 renovation and operation of buildings to ensure and promote prevention, detection, early 
 suppression, evacuation, compartmentation, structural resistance and ﬁreﬁghting. Since 30% of 
 domestic 
ﬁres and 50% of domestic ac cidental ﬁres have an  electrical source, Member States 
should develop an electrical inspection regime, and the European building stock renovation  
should integrate  electr ical safety checks and upgrades. 
ARTICLE 3 and ANNEX II on the National Renovation Plans should request Member States to 
 holistically address ﬁre saf ety and to exchange best practices within the Fire Information Exchange 
Platform (FIEP) organised by the European  Commission.

The European Fire Safety Week  conference, held on 1st December 2021, addressed the 
 importance of ﬁre safet y in the context of the energy transition. Eighteen speakers with

This report summarises their  policy  recommendations for the revised EPBD.

FIRE SAFETY IN THE PROPOSED 
EPBD RECAST 
■ Article 7 on New Buildings requests Member
States to address ﬁre and seismic safety.
■ Article 8 on Existing Buildings requests
Member States to address ﬁre and seismic
safety in relation to buildings undergoing
major renovations.
■ Annex II, referred to in Article 3, suggests as
optional indicator in the National Building
Renovation Plans, the overview of policies and
measures with regard to the increase of ﬁre
safety
Existing buildings and smaller renovations are 
not covered.
WHAT’S THE EU COMMISSION 
DOING ABOUT FIRE SAFETY? 
■ Following the tragic Grenfell ﬁre in 2017 in the
UK, the EU Commission established the EU
FIEP. Under the leadership of the Commis-
sion’s DG GROW, the FIEP gathers EU national
regulators and stakeholders from civil society
and industry. Fire safety of buildings remains
a national competence (in line with the
 subsidiarity principle). The FIEP facilitates
 discussions and exchanges on common
 challenges, lessons learned and best practices
regarding ﬁre safety.
■ The European Commission Joint Research
Centre (JRC) established the Fire Engineering
Expert Network in 2019. This group aims to
propose to the European Commission the
 development of a ﬁre engineering approach
that can be incorporated into the ﬁre safety
regulations of EU Member States similarly to
existing Eurocodes.
■ The Construction Products Regulation
 outlines harmonised rules for the marketing of
construction products in the EU. The regulation
provides a European framework to assess the
performance of construction products. Out of
the seven basic work requirements, from which
essential characteristics are established, the
 following four have a strong safety component:
mechanical safety, ﬁre safety, safety in use and
emission of substances.
■ Following the adoption of a pilot project by
the EU Parliament, DG GROW is running the
EU FireStat Project, which maps the existing
ﬁre data collected across the Member States
and generates meaningful data sets to help
facilitate decisions on ﬁre safety at the
Member State and EU levels.

FIRE SAFETY IS ALREADY IN THE 
 CURRENT EPBD, BUT THERE IS  
ROOM FOR IMPROVEMENT 
■ Since its last revision in 2018, the EPBD has
 included two references to ﬁre safety. 
Member States are encouraged to consider 
ﬁre and seismic safety within their national 
Long-
Term Renovation Strategies and to 
 ensure ﬁre and seismic safety are considered 
during major r
enovations. Nevertheless, 
 electrical safety checks are not foreseen for 
existing buildings. 
■ Following the adoption of the revised EPBD in
2018, and to support its implementation by
the Member States, the Commission pub-
lished some recommendations on building
renovations. It includes a chapter on elements
of safety, highlighting the importance of
 prevention measures and inspection of
 electrical installations, among other issues.
■ According to the Commission Analysis of
Long-Term Renovation Strategies, almost half
of the Member States are trying to combine
energy renovations with measures that can
increase ﬁre and seismic safety.
THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT 
HAD SIMILAR REQUESTS AND 
TOOK ACTION 
On 17th September 2020, the European 
 Parliament adopted the Own Initiative Report 
Maximising the Energy Efﬁciency P otential of 
the EU Building Stock, drafted by the 
 rapporteur, Ciarán Cuffe. 
■ Paragraph 19a refers to the EPBD content
 regarding seismic activity and ﬁre safety.
■ Paragraph 19aa describes a holistic approach
to ﬁre safety and the importance of the
 competency of professionals: ‘Recalls that ﬁre
safety aspects should be considered during the
design, selection of materials, construction,
renovation and operation of buildings in
 prevention, detection, early suppression,
evacuation, compartmentation, structural
 resistance and ﬁre-ﬁghting, as well as the rel-
evant competencies of professionals involved
during design, construction and renovation.’
■ Paragraph 25 goes further and refers to [Fire]
Safety Competency, which ‘underlines that
ensuring quality, compliance and safety
requires adequate competencies and skills of
professionals involved during the design and
construction/renovation.’

European Media Freedom Act – EMMA ENPA Key Priorities                                                 Version – 25 March 2022 
I. No EU media regulation beyond the AVMSD 
➔ The EU should refrain from attempting to regulate media at EU level. Existing issues in certain Member States 
should be addressed through political pressure and recommendations.  
• The EU has no competence to regulate the press and media, including the print ed press, aspects of media 
concentration etc.  
• There is also no such thing as a single “EU media market”. Media markets are characterized by historical, cultural, 
demographical and democratic characteristics on national and regional level.  
 
II. The Media Freedom Act promotes media regulation rather than media freedom 
➔ We do not see how a Media Freedom Act would increase media freedom across the EU. Single issues should be 
tackled through political pressure.   
• EU Member States like the Scandinavian ones are global blueprints for press freedom precisely because there is 
the least amount of media regulation or state intervention. An EU regulation on Media Freedom would not 
improve this situation.  
• EU media concentration regulation would not support, but instead hamper press freedom in the EU. Particular in 
many smaller member states, consolidation of media companies is not a problem but necessary to counteract 
economic downturns, to keep editorial teams and therefore to preserve media pluralism. Competition law on 
national and EU level has been and still is suitable and effe ctive with regard to media concentrations and 
consolidations.  
 
III. EMMA-ENPA strictly oppose any editorial regulation and regulation of “internal press freedom” 
➔ Safeguarding editorial freedom is a pre requisite to ensure a healthy democratic system where the press is 
enabled to support a diverse and critical public opinion. 
• EMMA-ENPA have always opposed a ny interference with editorial freedom, including in editorial composition, 
editorial work etc. Such initiatives are simply incompatible with the concept of free press.  
• This freedom includes on the one hand the freedom vis-à-vis the state or other external bodies to determine the 
editorial line, including the freedom to publish more or less biased articles and content, as long as it is within the 
boundaries of the laws. On the other hand, this freedom of the publisher also exists vis-à-vis- the editorial teams, 
including the composition of editorial teams. Any EU regulation interfering in the relations between the publisher 
and the editorial team would be a violation of the so called “internal press freedom”.  
• In addition, it also includes  regulatory or co -regulatory involvement of the EU in press councils . Press councils 
must have the right to remain truly voluntary and truly self -regulatory, and in particular neither directly or 
indirectly regulated or supervised by laws, regulators or other authorities.  
 
IV. EMMA ENPA oppose selective subsidies and interferences in the distribution of legal press 
➔ The principle of non -discrimination in possible subsidies as well as in the distribution of the online and printed 
press must be upheld to ensure plurality. 
• All legal editorial content must be distributed online and offline on the basis of non-discriminatory parameters. 
Promoting or favouring certain content is incompatible with a free and pluralistic press.  
• The same applies to media subsidies. Any subsidy must be done on the basis of non -discriminatory parameters 
and made available to all newspapers and magazines, such as reduced VAT rates.  
 
V. The EU must ensure that the free press and media can finance its content. 
➔ Ensuring economic sustainability is an essential tool to support press freedom. The EU must support it in ongoing 
negotiations on e-Privacy and DSA, as well as in the implementation of the Publishers’ Right. 
• A free press and media are dependent on economic sustainability in the market. 
• This must be recognized in data protection laws , while avoiding further advertising restrictions  that limit an 
important source of revenues for publishers. 
• Equally, the Publishers’ Right must be enforced against gatekeepers.  
 
VI. The EU must defend press freedom in existing initiatives impacting the economic and editorial freedom, as 
well as the distribution of the media 
➔ Rather than introducing new legislation on media freedom, the EU should instead focus on fixing issues in current 
proposals that undermine press freedom, such as in the DMA and DSA.   
• The DMA and DSA do not sufficiently take into consideration press and media freedom.   
• For example, the following  shortcomings with a direct impact on the free press must be resolved: The missing 
protection of legal editorial content against T&C censorship in the DSA, as well as the missing right for press and 
media content regarding fair access conditions to gatekeeper services beyond AppStores in the DMA.

Introduction
Let us focus on the end goal – carbon neutral building stock by 2050.
Buildings are at the heart of decarbonization. The European Green Deal recognizes buildings as
important infrastructure in our strive for 2050 carbon neutrality. The decade 2020-2030 must
witness an unprecedented wave of renovations resulting in building emissions reduction by
60%.
Siemens welcomes the work of the Commission in revising the Energy Performance of Buildings
Directive (EPBD) as part of the ‘Fit for 55 Package’ and coherently connecting it with both the
Renovation Wave, the Digital Decade and the Energy System Integration Strategy. The proposed
recast contains numerous clarifications and new provisions, notably the introduction of Zero-
Emission buildings, the setting of national building renovation plans, renovation passports,
data exchange and databases for energy performance of buildings, together with a significant
effort to bring more consistency in European energy & climate laws. Altogether, the proposal
has potential for fostering a more energy efficient and climate friendly building stock, however
the acceleration in the digital transition of the building sector should be improved.
Siemens is looking forward to contributing further to the discussions over the coming months
and to the successful approval of the ‘Fit for 55 Package’ by the end of this year.
Siemens Key Recommendations
1. Base the data exchange  on defined international standards and a common EU
building data management framework in line with the EU strategy for data.
2. Mandate the use of digital twins in certain new construction and deep renovation
projects to facilitate integrated building design and operation
3. Require the use of real performance data  in energy management and digital
certificates, passports, reports and logbooks for simplified exchange and update.
4. Include relevant provisions to apply adistrict approach related to new and “deeply”
renovated buildings aligned with the first building renovation plans timeline
5. Promote buildings as demand-side flexibility providers and active contributors for
energy system integration and system efficiency

Article 2 Definitions
In general, we commend the introduction of many new definitions related to the new
provisions in the EPBD. Along with the definitions for Energy Performance Certificate (EPC),
Building Renovation Passport and Digital Building Logbook it would be useful to add a definition
for the “Smart Readiness Indicator” Art. 2 (58)
To avoid carbon lock-in and ensure a decarbonised building stock by 2050, all major
renovations must be considered as a step on the way to a zero-carbon building and documented
accordingly in the renovation passport scheme. Define “major renovation” as a step within the
"staged deep renovation" in addition to the current definition Art. 2 (21c)
With the increasing electrification of the energy system, electrical installations are the
backbone of decarbonized buildings. Therefore, we recommend including the technical
equipment for electrical supply and distribution in the definition for ‘technical building system’.
Art. 2 (6)
Article 7 New buildings
To reach the 2050 carbon neutrality goal all sectors need to contribute towards it. We applaud
the focus on “zero-emission buildings” and the important consideration of the issues for healthy
indoor climate conditions, adaptation to climate change and fire safety.
Article 8 Existing buildings
To reach the 2050 carbon neutrality goal for the building stock all existing buildings need to
undergo this transition. The decarbonisation of the existing building stock should be addressed
with a holistic approach, to consider sustainability of buildings in a comprehensive way from
CO2 emissions to inclusiveness or healthy indoor environment. We therefore commend the
inclusion of the issues for healthy indoor climate, adaptation to climate change, fire safety, and
risks related to intense seismic activity, the removal of hazardous substances, including
asbestos and accessibility for persons with disabilities.
The active control of energy usage through Technical Building Systems is often overlooked
during energy renovations with the emphasis more often placed on the building envelope (as
exhibited in par. 2 of this article). Any improvements in the building envelop affects the energy
and comfort needs of the building and will require evaluation of the respective adjustment or
replacement of the associated technical building systems. Furthermore, the ambitious
upgrading of technical building systems would futureproof buildings and enable smart grid
integration. We advise the addition of these considerations in Art. 8 (2)
With the increase of the electrification rate in buildings, fire risks need to be scrutinized and
properly addressed in the Directive. The Commission foresees 40% of all residential and 65% of
all commercial buildings will be heated with electricity by 20301. We recommend that measures
that address fire safety, such as inspection and test of all equipment provided for fire safety,
become mandatory elements in major renovations. Art. 8 (3)
The expression “in so far as that is technically, functionally and economically feasible” appears
in every paragraph in this extremely important article. Feasibility should be defined at EU level
taking into consideration the revised cost-optimal levels including the costs to planet and
society. Art. 8 (1, 2, 3)
It is important to treat every major renovation as a step towards transforming the building on
the path to 2050 carbon neutrality.  We strongly believe that every major renovation should be

We believe that reducing the energy demand first will ultimately enable a swifter and more

Article 9 Minimum energy performance standards
We support the introduction of Minimum Energy Performance Standards with focus on worst
performing buildings, with a differentiated approach per type of buildings. While this is a step
in the right direction, there is a risk of missing valuable opportunities to accelerate the
transition. We question if it is feasible, efficient, or effective to bring all buildings one class up
at a time, e.g. from G to F in 2027. Next step would be to bring these “just renovated”
buildings to class E in 2030 plus all the other class F buildings.
Instead of prescribing a specific energy performance class at individual building level we
suggest prescribing a renovation to attain the best energy performance class that is cost-
optimal for the present moment (including the risk management for climate change) and
according to the roadmap in the building renovation passport. In this case it might be feasible
to renovate a worst performing building to a higher class than the currently prescribed
minimum class, especially if “deep renovation” is possible Art. 9 (1)
Also, the proposed Energy Efficiency Directive (EED) Art. 5. requires 3% of the total floor area
of buildings owned by public bodies to be renovated into nearly zero-energy buildings. This
EPBD article should align with the ambition in the EED. Art. 9 (1)
It is proposed that the Member States submit the first building renovation plan to the
Commission by 30 June 2025. This would be the time when the timelines for Minimum Energy
Performance Standards for the renovation of all existing buildings, in line with the pathway for
transforming the national building stock into zero-emission buildings by 2050, should be widely
communicated to all stakeholders. The harmonized EPC scale by 31.12.2025 will further
facilitate comparability of the expectations for assets portfolio managers with buildings in
different Member States. Currently, it is hard to gain predictability of the required classes
roadmap after 2033. A clear roadmap should be set out to achieve carbon neutrality in all
building types by 2050. The MEPS roadmap should be at least indicatively extended from 2033
to 2050 with the main reason to enable reliable longer-term planning for building owners and
investors. Art. 9 (2)
Article 11 Technical building systems
We commend the important provisions in setting system requirements for the proper
installation, and the appropriate dimensioning, adjustment, and control of the technical
building systems which are installed in new or existing buildings.

At the same time, we regret the unclear feasibility clause which would complicate issues,
particularly in terms of implementation in the member states. This offers a lot of space for
interpretation, which creates an unnecessarily high level of uncertainty among market
participants and regulators. As a result, we argue that the feasibility should be defined while
specifying system requirements. Once defined these requirements shall be applied without
exceptions. Art. 11 (1)
For the optimal use of technical building systems throughout their entire life cycle the
continuous monitoring and optimisation of their operation is paramount. The existing
requirements for non-residential buildings to be equipped with BACS capabilities are currently
placed in Article 20 on “inspections”. This framing leads to misunderstandings. The requirement
in Art. 20 (7 and 8) is self-contained and not conditional to the inspections. Therefore, we
recommend this requirement to be moved to the section on “Technical Building Systems”.  Art.
11 (2a)
The requirements for zero-emission buildings to be equipped with measuring and control
devices for the monitoring and regulation of indoor air quality should be extended to include
other indoor environmental quality (IEQ) parameters such as air temperature and humidity to
better reflect comfort conditions for enhanced health and quality of life of building occupants.
Art. 11 (3)
We believe that technical equipment must be planned in the context of the building as a holistic
system and not as a stand-alone element. Therefore, when a technical building system or part
of it is installed, the energy performance of the complete system and its impact on the overall
building energy performance should be assessed. Art. 11 (4)
With the increasing degree of electrification in buildings and building integration with grid and
e-mobility electrical installations/systems become the backbone of decarbonized buildings.
Therefore, we recommend that special attention is paid to ensure that electrical installations
are dimensioned and fit for the challenge. Art. 11 (5)
Taking a holistic approach to building performance should also integrate monitoring and
management of water use in building automation. Art. 11 (6)
Article 12 Infrastructure for sustainable mobility
We applaud the Commission's efforts to strengthen the provisions for sustainable mobility and
in particular the requirements for recharging points to be capable of smart charging and
bidirectional charging which would support the wider energy system integration.
To avoid unmanaged peaks on the building power supply we recommend that EV infrastructure
is linked to the whole building load management, e.g. through a building automation and
control system.
Article 13 Smart readiness of buildings
We welcome the proposed requirement to apply the Smart Readiness Indicator rating scheme
to non-residential buildings with an effective rated output for space heating, or combined space
heating and ventilation systems over 290 kW as linked to the deadline for the implementation
of the BACS requirements by 31 December 2024. In parallel, the same provisions should be
added for space cooling, or combined cooling and ventilation to match the current EPBD BACS
requirements. Art. 13 (2 and 4)
In the recent paper “ Digital building transformations for Europe’s green renovations ”, Digital
Europe show that applying digital technologies that foster collaboration across the value chain
and align participants on common metrics and goals, can significantly cut the sector’s current

emissions. Currently 30% of projects do not meet the original objectives, 92% of planners say
that not all information is available when plans are made, and 37% of materials used in
construction become waste.
Having a digital representation (Digital Twin) of the building at everybody’s disposal could well
be the game changer that lifts the entire industry to a new level of productivity. This will in turn
increase the global competitiveness of the European building industry and thus safeguard jobs.
Currently, the EU construction market ranks 5th  in the use of building information modelling
(BIM) globally. Digital twins link data from operational processes (usage data of the building),
infrastructure data (asset management) and data dedicated to energy and performance
management for the building end-users and city or grid operators. Ultimately, BIM is the entry
ticket to a truly lean building design, construction and maintenance approach that allows you
to “get it right” from the start and continue to improve, much like what we see happening in
manufacturing.
The use of digital twins enables buildings, districts and cities to be designed more efficiently
from the beginning of planning through to operation. An ongoing validation during the
operation should be mandatory. Therefore, we recommend mandating the use of Digital Twins
for buildings over 2000 m 2 in all new construction and deep renovation projects to facilitate
integrated building design, increase efficiency in the construction process and support the
introduction of the life-cycle Global Warming Potential (GWP) in the EPC as of 1 January 2027.
Art. 13 (5)
Digital certificates, passports and data repositories, such as the Digital Building Logbook, are of
great importance to boost data transparency, stakeholder engagement and support further
digitalisation of the building sector. We suggest that smart readiness should be utilized to
enable synergies in the various building inspections based on common data usage and to
ensure interconnection and compatibility between the various digital certificates, passports and
data repositories Art. 13 (6)
Buildings are the nexus between energy supply and demand and strengthening their smart
readiness is a driver for energy system integration and system efficiency. We recommend the
Commission to promote buildings as flexibility- and storage providers and to introduce metrics
for quantification of the demand-side flexibility at building and/or district level. Art. 13 (7)
Article 14 Data exchange
We support the introduction of this important topic in the EPBD proposal. By establishing truly
open platforms and an ecosystem of customers, partners, and cross-industry players, the full
potential of digital transformation could be harnessed by adapting an open model of shared
values. This means bringing all stakeholders together into an ecosystem sharing resources and
common technologies.
For direct access to building systems' data the scope needs clear specification per user group
(building owners, tenants, managers, third party) according to their expertise, legal rights, and
purpose of data use. Data ownership, business models, governance of data, quality and safety
of data are essential factors. Even the ownership of the hardware devices that provide meter
readings, as well as their maintenance, calibration, and validation need to be considered. There
could be an enormous amount of data transactions that would increase related energy use. We
suggest that the scope of data exchange should be limited to the quantity and quality that is fit
for the purpose of the user and be covered with a legal agreement. The data exchange should
be based on defined international standards. Art. 14 (1)
There should be a common EU building data management framework in line with the applicable
EU legal framework. This would facilitate delivery of products and services across borders in the

Single Market. Given the variety of stakeholders in the building sector with their mainly
temporary involvement in a building’s lifecycle and their diverse needs we recommend that the
roles and rules for data sharing should be clearly defined according to the EU strategy for data.
The Commission should orchestrate the efforts to create a data framework that is acceptable

Usage rights and relevant charges for access to data shall ensure fairness in the allocation of

The work on the implementing acts with new rules on data interoperability and access to data

Real building performance data is indispensable for the ongoing optimization of building
performance, the interaction with the grid and the building users. This data might be used in
digital twins for model predictive control, but also for energy consulting and calculated energy
demand might be correlated with existing energy monitoring. Furthermore, real performance
data might be utilized in the simplified update of various building digital documents. We believe
in the use of real building operational data and recommend for it to be required at least in
energy management and monitoring, and for simplified exchange and update of digital
certificates, reports and logbooks. Art. 14 (6)
Article 15 Financial incentives and market barriers
We commend the attention to appropriate financing and support measures, enacted to address
market barriers and stimulate investments in energy renovations.
However, we express concern about labour shortages of a qualified construction workforce. To
achieve the 2050 carbon neutrality goal and solve the lack of skilled labour the proposed
renovation deadlines need to be accompanied with the build-up of a qualified workforce,
including tradespeople and energy experts. A coordinated European approach, like thePact for
Skills in Construction officially launched during the recentEU Industry Days should be promoted
and the progress should be followed up. Art. 15 (7)
To improve financing in support of the implementation of this Directive and taking due account
of the principle of subsidiarity, the Commission shall, preferably by 31 December 2026, present
an analysis on the effectiveness of use of Union and national funds for achieving Union
objectives related to buildings. Art. 15 (8)

Member States shall link their financial measures for energy performance efficiency

Article 16 Energy performance certificates
We acknowledge the Commission's efforts to improve the quality, usability, and reliability of
energy performance certificates. At the same time, we notice that real measured performance
metrics are not mandatory in the Energy Performance Certificate template (Annex V).
To ensure comparability between calculated and metered values we urge including real
measured performance metrics as a mandatory element in the EPC template. The energy
performance of an existing building shall always be determined based on metered energy use.
The requirement for metered final energy consumption metrics would indicate the extent to
which the Energy Efficiency First principle is applied. Art. 16 (2) and Annex V
The validity of the energy performance certificate shall be 5 years for all buildings. High-
performance buildings should also prove they maintain the level of performance. Simplified
procedure for EPC update should be used through data import of real building performance
indicators provided by the building energy management system. Art. 16 (10)
Simplified procedure for update of energy performance certificates should be used in all cases
through data import of real performance values associated with individual elements or
standalone measures. Art. 16 (11)
Article 19 Databases for energy performance of buildings
We applaud the introduction of this article dedicated to setting up national databases, as
knowing your building stock is essential in managing its transition. It is important to have
reliable data to monitor and to steer the renovation wave.
We further commend the introduction of planned digital building logbooks which should be
interoperable and integrated with other building-related databases.
We encourage the Commission to indicate a timeline for the definition and introduction of
digital building logbooks aligned with the building data management framework. The adoption
of an implementing act with a common template for the transfer of the information to the
Building Stock Observatory by 30 June 2024 could be the start for gradually feeding digital
building logbooks with information from the national databases enriched with data from the
Building Renovation Passports from 2024 on. Art. 19 (7)
Article 20 Inspections
We support the necessity of having regular inspections of technical building systems. However,
paragraph 6 still leaves room for exceptions to inspection obligations. As a result, in member
states where no inspection obligations are implemented, heating, ventilation and air
conditioning systems are not optimally adjusted, and energy waste cannot be prevented. We
suggest removing this paragraph from the proposal. Art. 20 (6)
We argue that regular inspections should cover all technical building systems in buildings within
scope including safety inspections of electrical and fire safety installations and propose the
inclusion of a new paragraph. Art. 20 (12)
Additional provisions on District Approach
We recognize the mention of integrated district and neighbourhood approaches in the proposal
as they are an important part of the energy future, where a broader, holistic approach is needed
to make full use of all potential synergies a) between individual buildings, b) between buildings
and a flexible, smart grid and c) between buildings and variable options for the use of
heating/cooling and e-mobility.

Building energy renovations and new construction should be aligned with district-level strategic
planning to realize economies of scale and utilize new financing and business models allowed
by the possibility to bundle otherwise fragmented renovation projects. A district, campus or
neighbourhood approach offers potential to gain synergies at local energy system level
(heating, cooling, microgrid, distributed energy sources).
Local Energy Markets as an extension to the existing central markets are a promising concept
to combine various measures and activate untapped flexibility potential of the demand- and
generation-side as well as that of storage. Furthermore, they offer market-based incentives for
potential investors to build generation and storage facilities nearby load centres and thus to
reduce the necessity of time-consuming and cost-intensive expansions of the distribution and
even the transmission grids. Buildings as demand-side flexibility providers and in some cases
even virtual power plants have an important role in the energy system.
Article 25 mentions that in the next review period by the end of 2027 integrated district or
neighbourhood approaches would be examined. We propose to include relevant provisions to
apply this approach related to new and “deeply” renovated buildings as soon as 2025 (Art. 7
New buildings and Art. 8 Existing buildings), aligned with the first building renovation plans
timeline.
Conclusion
We believe that in the upcoming negotiations of the EPBD recast it is important to preserve
consistency across all building-related provisions in the Fit for 55 package and to agree on a
coherent and robust framework for buildings to meet the EU’s 2030 and 2050 targets. Hence,
all elements of the Fit for 55 package should be discussed and approved as a whole.
Siemens is committed to the climate neutrality goals and expects from the policymakers to
create the enabling regulatory environment for the industry to contribute to the full.

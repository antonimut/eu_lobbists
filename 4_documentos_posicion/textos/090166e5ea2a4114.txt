Audience Measurement Coalition detailed response to section 2.3 on audience measurement 
 
2.3. Audience measurement 
 
1) Do you think that audience measurement with respect to the following media is carried 
out in a transparent, objective and inclusive way in your Member State (or, if you are 
replying on behalf of an EU organisation), in the EU as a whole?  
 
 
 
*Note by the AMC : T he lack of transparency in measurement is caused by an increasingly 
fragmented media within the digital sphere which has encouraged new entrants to self-measure. 
This situation has (to some extent) constrained the established “independent” third party 
measurement systems set up by the traditional media ecosystem over several decades  to 
balance the market. However, we do not think that it makes sense to point towards a lack of 
transparency of any “type of actors” or “types of media”. The actors that do not publish their 
audience numbers in a transparent manner are usually those that currently lack sufficient 
incentive to do so because they enjoy  substantial market dominance on the advertising and 
media market. It may be more appropriate to look at the issue from the perspective of business 
power balances and fair competition rather than the structure of the outlet that conveys the 
media content. In our opinion this justifies that all actors that trade on the media and 
advertisement market are transparent and that their audience measurement numbers can be 
verified and compared to the rest of the market.

Other issues, please specify: 
 
Independent third party audience measurement systems can only be effective if the players that 
agree to submit themselves to the measurement system are themselves transparent. Audience 
measurement provided by external specialised companie s or supervised by Joint Industry 
Committees is a self -regulatory system that  relies on the information that is willingly given to 
them by the relevant market players and clients. For decades, a relatively balanced media 
market ensured that the different s takeholders had an incentive to be open with the information 
pertaining to their audiences. It could even be said that the market made independent audience 
measurement into a “soft law” within the business: any outlet that wished to enter into major 
business agreements on the advertising market would have to submit to external audience 
measurement which would be trusted by the market, more specifically by both buy-side and sell-
side players. Why is the market “soft law” not as effective anymore? Certain pla yers have 
become influential to the extent that they are uncircumventable for companies and advertisers 
that wish to reach their potential customers online. Some global digital players claim billions of 
end-users and even if these numbers are not independe ntly verified, the high approximate  
ballpark number of users they reach makes it impossible for companies to exclude advertising 
on such platforms. This is especially the case when it comes to reaching younger end-users that 
primarily consume information o n social media platforms and other digital intermediaries. In 
addition, in recent years, transparency has also not always been a priority for  buy -side actors 
(buyers of advertising space) of the market, as the price of advertising on social media platforms 
contrasts to other competing channels. Social media or advertisements placed in User 
Generated Content is often  viewed as being cheaper than any form of advertising available 
today and is offering an exposure of up to a thousand end -users for only a few euros. 
Nevertheless, the advantageous prices on certain digital platforms can come at the cost of 
transparency and an increased risk of advertising waste (In some instances there may be an 
“ad fraud” risk too and  so program mes such as TAG’s Anti -Fraud cer tification 
(https://www.tagtoday.net/fraud) or similar should be considered in parallel) . Generally 
speaking, “measurement specific issues” can be traced back to a refusal to both align with an 
official (di sclosed) methodology  that ensures comparability with other market players and a 
verification of audience numbers  by an independent third party on the basis of data that is 
granular enough to be verified. These issues can be found on a local, European and 
international level.  
 
A separate issue is also the optimisation of reported data by maximising the potential access to 
useful information. By this we mean that in some audience measurement projects access to 
Cable/Satellite Operator data has been made poss ible as Telco’s and Operators allow a data 
transfer to take place. In Europe the number of cases is small but the discussions are ongoing. 
Elsewhere, in Canada, for example, legislation has been passed to ensure that such operator 
data is provided for audience measurement purposes. It is seen as a public utility data set.

-Provide a forum for discussion and exchange of best practices Set out principles to 
enhance transparency, objectivity and inclusiveness and of audience measurement  
 
A forum for discussion and exchange of best practices could be useful as a post-legislative 
initiative if the Commission chooses to enshrine a principle of market transparency via a system 
of third party audience measurement for all players that are part of the media ecosystem 
receivers of revenues from the advertising market. It  could serve as a forum for all the digital 
players to discuss how to concretely align their standards according to the principles set forward 
in the legislation. Nevertheless, a priority should be given to legislative means first in order to 
ensure that t he measurement standards that  are indispensable to guarantee  market 
transparency are not in themselves an object of the debate.  
 
-Ensure independent auditing of audience measurement  
 
Independent auditing of audience measurement providers already exists f or the most reliable 
players in the industry. It is in fact enshrined in i nternational industry standards  and is a 
requirement of the International Guidelines for Television Audience Measurement (Global 
Guidelines for Television Audience Measurement “GGTAM”AM, ESOMAR international 
Code, guidelines of the International Federation of Audit Bureaux of Certification 
“IFABC” etc). For instanc e, ESOMAR which is the official membership organization for 
market, social, and opinion researchers founded in 1947 clearly states in its ICC/ESOMAR 
international Code under article 7 on “transparency” that “Researchers must on request allow 
clients to arrange for independent checks on the quality of data collection and data preparation.” 
All respectable audience measurement providers are regularly audited and are fully transparent 
with their clients. Notable national self -regulatory media audit institutes that have official 
recognition exist in countries like the USA with the Media Rating Council (MRC) and in France  
with the Centre détude des supports de publicité (CESP) 
 
 
Examples (Austrian case study) 
System Auditing in Austria’s TV audience measurement system 
TV audience measureme nt in Austria is provided by AGTT ( Arbeitsgemeinschaft Teletest,  
https://agtt.at/show_content.php?hid=2 ), an association according to Austrian association law. 
The members are the Austrian Broadcasting Corporation (ORF, public broadcast service), 
ORF’s subsidiary that handles sales and the 5 companies that constitute the federal segment of 
the privately owned TV sector ( https://agtt.at/show_content.php?hid=1 ). 
AGTT measures the size and composition of audiences for the programs of their members, the 
market share of programs an d channels for days, months and years. AGTT also collects and 
publishes usage for all Video streaming programming of its members and aligns these 
measurements with TV results. 
The main technical and measurement service provider is GFK (Gesellschaft für 
Konsumforschung). Auditing the measuring system and its components is done in several steps 
and processes: 
-Auditing and full access to collected and processed data for 3rd party auditor is ensured through 
the contracts that AGTT has with GFK 
-Every member has the same access as auditors to all data in full detail 
-Members have a full set of household equipment at their premises with access to signals and 
test usage of this equipment, its signals and the collection and processing side effects of 
interaction with every version change in software and hardware. 
-A scientific board of AGTT regularly commissions 3 rd party auditors to check on thematically 
changing aspects of the measuring system. 
As an example: AGTT uses audio matching for a number of purposes, amon gst them a control 
mechanism for the correctness of context data sent with the measuring systems. An auditor with 
full access to finely granulated data is commissioned to investigate and evaluate every aspect of 
this component in relation to the overall system. 
System Auditing in Austria’s online audience measurement system 
ÖWA measures the size and composition of audiences and their usage of single services such 
as for example classifieds markets, business information services, sports results offerings, news 
from publishing houses or VOD -Services from broadcasters and others for months, weeks and 
days. It also aggregates data on publisher properties and marketing communities 
The main technical and measurement service providers are INFOnline GmbH (technica l census 
data collection and processing) and Reppublika Data Analytics & Technologies GmbH (online 
access panel and modeled computation of audience sizes). Auditing the measuring system and 
its components is done in the following layers and processes: 
-The managing office of ÖWA controls the implementation of the system by every member 
according to the defined set of rules (for example definition of user interactions that are legally 
registered) twice a year. Results are presented and evaluated by the board  of ÖWA. Members 
with large walled sectors contractually agree to grant the ÖWA access to these sectors. 
 
-The managing office regularly performs data signal plausibility and consistency checks in 
cooperation with INFOnline GmbH, as provided for in Service Level Agreements. 
 
-Members verify and correct against the reports they are delivered from the former checks. 
 
-Members have access to the raw data on request and regularly for a small handling fee, if they 
want to process and check themselves. 
 
-ÖWA runs  regular media research expert commissions that check the plausibility and 
consistency of result data for all members quarterly. Every member can nominate an expert for 
participation. The board of ÖWA , vote yearly on the members of the commission that elec t a 
speaker and deputy independently from themselves. 
 
-ÖWA has signed a contract with an experienced consulting and auditing expert company that 
reviews processes, algorithms, input- and result-data consistency. Reports of these reviews are 
open to all members of ÖWA. 
 
The standard of openness to scrutiny towards clients, set by the international guidelines, could 
be strengthened and reiterated in the future European Media Freedom Act. All independent third 
party measurement providers should at the request of the stakeholders that they measure agree 
to submit themselves to an audit procedure. The audit would ex amine and evaluate the correct 
observance of the study methodology alongside with the quality of the establishment survey, 
quality of the panel recru itment and data processing and control. The audit should however 
exclude any measures that would allow a specific client to interfere with the established 
methodology or gain access to confidential information (such as panel contact information and 
identity) in view of   influencing the results of the measurement.  
 
-Introduce common EU standards for audience measurement  
 
Internationally and on given European markets, standards, and calculation methodologies have 
been outlined to provide various metrics wh ich uniformly determine the true weight, influence 
and size of audiences of various players. Key standards that are considered as the minimum 
common standards by the industry are namely outlined in the Interactive Advertising Bureau’s 
“Audience Reach Measurement” Guidelines V. 2.0” 1 which is addressed to Internet Publishers, 
Ad-serving organizations, Syndicated Measurement Organizations and auditors.

crucial across EU markets and could be promoted as EU standards in the context of the 
European Media Freedom Act: 
In view of market transparency on the media market  third-party independent 
measurement should be compulsory for all players that are involved in  the buying and 
selling process of advertising on the media market. The third parties should apply hybrid models 
for the audience measurement. They are needed beca use of the multiple different “signals” that 
can cause “noise” in the data. For example Computers and Smartphones deliver subtly different 
log or census data given that the underlying operating systems and browser implementations 
are different. Online acti vity information from Browsers differs from App activity on mobile 
devices2. Extensive proxy and caching infrastructure (mobile carriers, content delivery networks) 
often hide signals and data structure.

-Entrust an independent EU body with a competence to monitor the methodologies of 
audience measurement in Member States Other (please specify) 
 
The market players that generally conform to market standards on audience measurement and 
submit themselves to independent third party audience measurement are already participating 
in measurement structures where methodologies are scrutinized, publicized and verifiable. The 
AMC does not deem it necessary to create an EU body which would monitor measurement 
methodologies in various Member States as that would unnecessarily add an additional layer of 
bureaucracy to welfunctionning and transparent systems put  in place by European markets. It 
also wouldn’t make sense to put up such a structure for the purpose of complete harmonization 
measurement methodologies. Media markets in Europe are very diverse and differing 
methodologies are sometimes a necessity to truly capture the size and reach of an audience on 
a given market.  There may be an opportunity for the EU to consider financial support to the 
smaller media markets that need seed funding in establishing a system, however, since the set -
up of a programme ca n require capital investment upfront.  In addition, it would require a high 
amount of financial resources to set up such a body, including the hiring of a high number of 
specialized experts with the necessary technological means to monitor the different 
methodologies. Finally, it would not address the core problem: the increasing lack of 
transparency on the market caused by self -measurement which creates black boxes on the 
digital media market. Unmonitored sections of the market will remain the primary cause  of 
advertising misplacement, potential for inflation of advertising prices, limitation of competition to 
the detriment of smaller traditional media players and even downstream the  possible spread of 
disinformation (without proper verification of audiences  a “follow the money” approach cannot 
be taken by authorities as quantification of the problem is not viable).   We would rather 
encourage the Commission to put in place instruments that would incentivise the players that 
currently resort to self -measurement to enter into to structures that would allow their audience 
numbers to be scrutinized by the market or help existing structures to evolve: this would imply a 
structure that is transparent with its methodology, a system that includes independent 3 third 
party audience measurement using independent audience measurement data, including 
industry-agreed panel data results to ensure that corrections and validation of the stakeholders 
data is valid, comparable and accurate.

European Media Freedom Act  
 
About the Audience Measurement Coalition (AMC) 
AMC is a business coalition that consists of  like-minded research companies and Joint Industry 
Committees (JICs); it includes research organisations such as FINNPANEL (FIN), IVW (DE), ÖWA, 
Médiamétrie (FR), ATO (CZ), SPIR (CZ), Nielsen (US), Kantar (UK) and Gemius (PL). 
 
Introduction 
In connection with the European Commission’s consultation on the European Media Freedom Act, 
the AMC would like to stress the importance of the role played by third party audience measurement 
in ensuring better competition and transparency for various actors in the European Digital market. 
Aside from the detailed response submitted in the Commission’s public consultation section 2.3, this 
paper is a presentation of our sector and its role on the media market as well as our general 
positions on digital policy.  
 
Audience Measurement 
The prima ry function of Audience Measurement is to establish “a currency” upon which media 
organisations and their business partners can trade. Just like  monetary currency, audience 
measurement metrics serve as a common reference that the entire market can trust. However, the 
role of Audience Measurement extends beyond currency for the planning, buying and reconciliation 
of advertising media. Data processing as part of  the discipline includes standardised, market -wide 
and enforced error and fraud mitigation stages, such as the removal of invalid or non -human traffic 
which is not guaranteed by Information Service Providers’ first party data. 
Furthermore, Audience Measurement provides the market with impartial benchmarks on the use, 
effectiveness and reach of media pla tforms, websites and apps for matters such as fair -competition 
assessments and activities such as corporate investment, mergers and acquisitions. 
For this reason, the market will usually select a trusted third party accepted by all stakeholders , as 
the audience numbers must be provided by an organisation which cannot be suspected of serving 
one particular commercial interest. Media organisations commonly have to prove via an 
independent third party that their audiences are of a certain size , but usually provide more granular 
information about the demographics they reach. The use cases for the data are diverse. For 
instance, news publishers and rights -holders rely on Audience Measurement to determine precise 
amounts for royalty payments , collective rights man agement and other payments; advertisers plan

All in all, Audience Measurement is essential in providing transparency. For decades , different 
stakeholders in the media market (media companies, advertisers, agencies) have required that 
independent third parties establish the trusted numbers that determine market shares and the value 
of a media outlet ’s inventory . Furthermore, “Joint Industry Committees” (JICs) have been 
established in several European countries to organise and consolidate the current and future needs 
of measurement in their market. 
These organisations represent key stakeholders: while the composition of the JIC varies by market, 
they include a combination of television/radio broadcasters, publishers, advertisers and media 
agencies. Typically, they regulate themselves by committee-based governance; many are not -for-
profit and some are even State-monitored. 
To sum marise, audience measurement by third parties and JICs is not an exception, but an 
indispensable system for ensuring diversity, competition and collaboration within the media market. 
For this reason, we hope that the final ePrivacy regulation acknowledges the specificity of the 
Audience Measurement sector’s role as an impartial referee for the media market.  
 
Audience Measurement and Privacy  
Audience Measurement establishes the size and general composition of audiences for a given 
market on a very broad scale (age, gender, geographical area). The results are published by 
measurement providers through reports of aggregat ed data derived from both Internet users’ data 
and panel data (users who agreed to participate in a research sample). The purpose, granularity 
and structure of m easurement data is distinct from the type of data that is used for data -driven 
targeted advertising. In addition, user information collected from a website is pseudonymised during 
processing and then aggregated when made public to prevent identification of the individuals. Also, 
measurement providers deploy best practice privacy safeguards as standa rd, such as 
pseudonymisation, anonymisation, minimi sation and limitation of data retention period , as well as 
right to opt-out of measurement. 
 
A closer look: Media convergence, Markets and methodologies 
Audience Measurement for TV traditionally  involves the recruitment of households (in the industry 
they are known as panels ). These panels are  provided with equipment installed on TV-sets and 
other devices in the home . Through the  equipment audience measurement providers receive  data 
signals that allow them to collect and process viewing data. This gives researchers a thorough 
insight into the consumption habits of the different segments of society.  
 
TV and Internet panels differ insofar as TV -panels often are smaller than Internet panels but 
nevertheless are more expensive to recruit and manage since they do not benefit from the census 
data that is  collected through bidire ctional Internet protocols and therefore  need to be more 
representative of the actual population than internet panels do. 
 
There is however a trend for TV and Internet audience measurement to converge, as streaming 
markets continue to grow, and TV continues to get more interactive. 
 
 
Other traditional media sectors such as radio, print and out -of-homei use different sets of 
methodologies to assess the performance of their respective media. 
 
In most European countries , Joint Industry Committees  bring together members from the media - 
and advertising industries to define the values, KPIs, periods, methodology and ways of sharing and 
publishing the results. These self -regulation mechanisms ensure correctness, fairness and 
transparency across suppliers and customers in markets that are worth billions and have a high 
level of public and political awareness. 
 
Results and details of audience  measurement are presented as reports used by ad vertisers and 
media industries, s ome of them as an open publication but, as a ru le, a complete set of results 
constitute a service to members and customers through a paid service with restricted access . The 
data sets are regularly integrated within booking and controlling systems the industry uses. Details 
of availability, cost and access differ from country to country and media platform to media platform, 
but the principles stay the same. 
 
Third-party Audience Measurement and its current and potential contribution to the 
transparency of the digital market could be promoted in the European Media Freedom Act.  
Third-party Audience Measurement providers deliver unbiased, reliable data that enable  media 
companies and their business partners to grow and trust each other. A system of third -party 
verification, which is no different to an  independent audit of audiences, is a staple of the  traditional 
media ecosystem and has allowed the market to develop in a sustainable manner. Nevertheless, 
the development of the digital economy has seen the rise of new platforms that are unavoidable . 
Thus, i ntroducing truly independent  third-party Audience Measurement for all players that draw 
revenues from the advertising market remains vital. This ensures that we continue to evolve our 
measurement to serve the broader industry and provide agencies, advertisers, publishers and 
broadcasters with independent reporting on the audiences reached by both their content and ad 
campaigns. In fact, the development of a “single currency” for all advertisers is strongly 
recommended by the World Federation of Advertisers and has received significant support from the 
media ecosystem.

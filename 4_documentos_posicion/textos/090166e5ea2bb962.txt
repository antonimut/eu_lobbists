Introduction 
MFE welcomes the opportunity to submit its response to the call for evidence launched by the European 
Commission on the European Media Freedom Act (MFA) and supports the Commission's objective to ensure 
the proper functioning of the EU media market in the digital era. In particular, the scale and scope of 
European media companies should continue to uphold and embody the European values enshrined in the 
Treaty. The regulatory framework envisaged at the turn of the millennium needs to be fine -tuned with 
reference to both technological development and geopolitical evolution.  
Directive 89/552/EC,  updated by the recently reviewed AVMSD 1 has laid the foundatio ns for the 
development of audio -visual media services in the Member States which have contributed to information 
pluralism in a dual public and private system and to the financing of European original works.  
As underlined by the Commission, the regulatory framework will have to integrate competition safeguards: 
while consolidation of European groups is essential to compete with  global OTT players, it is equally 
important to ensure that European citizens have direct, non -intermediated access to legacy linear and on-
demand (VOD) services which have created the backbone of a fully regulated ecosystem. A pro-competitive 
framework, respecting the complementary role of public service and commercial operators, is the best 
safeguard for independence and pluralism. The MFA proposal could codify a sector-specific framework with 
reference to the horizontal ex ante obligations in the Digital Markets Act.

Considering the harmonisation intervention that the Commission intends to propose, under Article 114 
TFEU, three priorities could support the objective of strengthening European media within a system of 
checks and balances that would ensure both their independence and their economic sustainability: 
1. Regulatory benchmarking to protect pluralism (external and internal) 
2. Adequate prominence of general interest services 
3. Fair and certified audience measurement 
 
1. Regulatory standards to protect pluralism.  
 
In Italy, the rules protecting  external pluralism are provided in Article 51 of Legislative Decree 208/21 
amended in accordance with the findings of the Court of Justice of the EU outlined in its Ruling of 3 September 
2020 (C-719/18 - Vivendi). This is an innovative and dynamic concept that provides for a specific,  case-by-
case assessment of the potentially ha rmful situations through well -identified parameters, moving " from a 
system of ex-ante thresholds rigidly determined, to a system that provides for adequate concrete verifications 
(through market analyses conducted by the Communications Authority AGCOM) of possible violations of 
pluralism, leaving aside a purely "automatic" application of the predetermined concentration thresholds"2.  
In concrete terms, the law prohibits the creation of a position of significant market power detrimental to 
pluralism and confers to AGCOM the power to monitor and to control the progress and evolution of the 
integrated communications system (SIC), also with reference to the revenues generated in Italy by entities 
established abroad. In order to assess whether a company or a group of companies is in a situation of 
significant market power which may be detrimental to pluralism, the Authority will have to take into account, 
among other things, not only revenues but also: the level of static and dynamic competition within the 
system, barriers to entry, convergence between sectors and markets, synergies arising from activities carried 
out in different but contigu ous markets, vertical and conglomerate integration, availability and control of 
data, direct or indirect control of necessary and scarce resources (such as broadcasting frequencies ), the 
economic efficiency of the enterprise, also in terms of economies of scale, range and network, as well as of 
the audience share of radio and television programmes, including news programmes, cinematographic 
works, editorial and online products and services. If, following the investigation, the Authority identifies 
positions of significant market power detrimental to pluralism, it intervenes to ensure that they are promptly 
dealt with. If some operations are likely to lead to dominance, they are duly inhibited and orders their effects 
removed. 
The Spanish Audiovisual Law (Ley  General de la Comunicación Audiovisual "LGCA" 3) recognises the right of 
all citizens to receive information that respects political, social and cultural pluralism (Article 4.5). Likewise, 
it recognises the right of all audiovisual communication service p roviders to set their own editorial line 
through the selection of content and the definition of their programming schedules, and to organise their 
content in a channel or catalogue (Article 10 LGCA). Furthermore, the national regulatory body, the Comisión 
Nacional de los Mercados y la Competencia ("CNMC"), is in charge of ensuring transparency and pluralism in 
the audiovisual sector and the independence and impartiality of the public media.

2. Adequate prominence of general interest content 
 
The Audiovisual Media Services Directive (AVMS Directive) and its transposition provide a solid legal 
framework for linear audiovisual media services. However, this framework does not ensure fair competition 
with large online platforms active in the same markets where the provision of content, audience 
measurement and advertising sales are increasingly dependent on the availability and use of data. 
Alongside the well-established delivery modes for linear TV channels - digital terrestrial (DTT), cable and 
satellite, and subscriptions from Internet Service Providers (ISPs) – over the past ten years, direct access via 
the Internet using connected TV sets (smart -TV) a nd OTT (over -the-top) keys or equipment offered by 
companies such as Google or Apple has developed, making it increasingly difficult for users to find DTT 
channels. ISPs and cable and satellite operators are subject to rules protecting national publishers:  must-
carry provisions (i.e. the obligation to distribute public service channels) and the obligation to comply with 
logical channel numbering (LCN) for DTT channels. However, these rules do not apply to manufacturers of 
connected TV sets nor to OTT operators. The revision of the Audiovisual Media Services Directive (AVMS) laid 
the foundations for regulation with Article 7a, which provides that " Member States may take measures to 
ensure the appropriate prominence of audiovisual media services of general interest.".  
The Italian National Regulatory Authority, AGCOM, has suggested including a provision that would give to 
news provided on audiovisual  media a general interest value, in order to strengthen the protection of 
information pluralism, regardless of the delivery mode and of the reception devices, in compliance with the 
principle of technological neutrality4.  
The rapid and effective implementation of these provisions could also be relevant to the objectives that the 
Commission aims to promote with the MFA proposal. Channels subject to a national authorisation must 
comply with a comprehensive regulatory framework which defines their general in terest relevance and 
ensures they contribute significantly to media and information pluralism. 
At present, the TV-set still holds a central place in the consumption of audiovisual content. Linear services 
represent by far the vast majority of such consumpt ion compared to the new OTT services: linear services 
account for 91% of daily viewing time in Italy and Germany and for 89% in Spain (see below). In fact, free 
national and local broadcast TV is the most democratic means of consumption, followed by all th e free (or 
mostly free) proprietary IP services which, unlike OTT services, do not discriminate viewers on the basis of 
their income. Viewers therefore prefer this medium , which allows free access to quality content and 
continues to be a key driver of audiovisual media consumption on a large scale. However, new smart -TVs 
make the access to national broadcasters' channels very cumbersome, by drowning their offer in the midst 
of OTT services, whose audience is by no means proportionate to their visibility.

In order not to lose the legacy of the linear offer, which is fully compliant with the qualitative and quantitative 
standards laid down in the AVMS Directive, it is necessary to promote direct and non -intermediated access 
to linear and non-linear content services catered by national broadcasters, in compliance with the neutrality 
and non-discriminatory conditions that all means of access to editorial services are required to ensure.   
The Digital Markets Act introduces a self-preferencing prohibition and a FRAND obligation, providing for fair 
and non-discriminatory conditions of access to business users of a gatekeeper’s core platform service. From 
a media perspective, these obligations could set the basis for the MFA to ensure that all European 
broadcasters are granted  fair access to connected -TV receivers, voice assistants, and OTT services (e.g.: 
aggregator of third parties’ TV services).  
A report by the French National Assembly 5 notes that broadcasters easily lose 70% of their traffic if users 
have to scroll on a screen to find them. This has severe consequences for European content providers who 
support free offers through advertising revenues: reduced advertising revenues risk undermining their ability 
to invest in the production of new audiovisual content, thus increasing the risk of being less prominent on 
interfaces. 
MFE welcomes the Commission's ambition, expressed in the “European Media and Audiovisual Action Plan”,6 
to support media recovery and transformation in order to encourage common approaches that would give 
prominence to general interest content, as well as the study it recently launched on the prominence and 
findability/discoverability of content and services of general interest. Moreover, a specific subgroup of ERGA 
was established in 2020 to facilitate a common understanding of the scope of article 7a and poten tial 
regulatory approaches by Member State s. In fact, ERGA’s  2022 work programme is meant to explore how 
algorithms / recommendation systems could ensure the appropriate prominence of audiovisual media 
services of general interest 7. Concrete measures should be proposed to promote the development of 
European standards such as HbbTV (hybrid broadcast broadband TV). The mandation of the HbbTV standard 
- already provided for in Spanish law - is a provision that could be included within the framework of the MFA 
to ensure that all users have direct and free access to the interactive services offered by authorised 
national broadcasters, on all tel evision sets and receivers , regardless of the transmission or reception 
means. 
Another significant initiative in this respect is the open DVB-I standard with ETSI certification8.  The adoption 
of European standards in connected devices marketed in the European Union would ensure interoperability 
and access to the services delivered by European content providers. There is a need to ensure that access, 
previously safeguarded by regulation, does not end up being managed by private negotiations with 
manufacturers of reception devices and operating system providers.

3. Audience measurement 
 
Reliable and transparent audience measurement is crucial for the independence and sustainability of 
advertising-funded broadcasters and, ultimately, for the diversity of audiovisual media services.  
Broadcasters must have access to the data generated by th e consumption of their own content. It is crucial 
for product development (with important insights into how audiences consume content), to improve 
services, and to enable strategic and business decisions regarding content production. 
The problem of audienc e measurement especially rises when media content is accessible on social media 
platforms and gatekeeper services. It mostly boils down to risks of bots and fraud , with money shifting to 
social media, hence impacting broadcasters’ ad budgets and revenue. 
In this context, building on the provisions set in the DMA, it is absolutely necessary to include in the MFA a 
few key principles guiding audience measurement, such as transparency, verifiable data, and audits of results 
by the broadcaster and/or a third p arty authorised by the latter, along with the guarantee of access by 
broadcasters to data related to their services consumption when such services are intermediated by digital 
platforms.   
We consider defining audience measurement principles (independence, transparency, verifiable data, audits 
of results, fair representation of all stakeholders involved) at EU level should set the basis for a pro -
competitive framework where digital audiences on different services can be gauged via comparable metrics. 
*** 
It is necessary to promptly re -establish fair competition between broadcasters and large online platforms 
that have taken advantage from an unprecedented information asymmetry and regulatory vacuum to impose 
their own rules. They are set to take control of t he different modes of audiovisual content consumption, to 
the detriment of legacy audiovisual media services that have been subject to severe regulatory scrutiny from 
inception. Access to audiovisual media services, traditionally subject to public regulation, has become subject 
to private rules.  
A sector -specific European legislative instrument should ensure European media service providers 
continue to be granted fair access to their audiences. To this end, the adoption of an open and interoperable 
European standard across all television sets and receivers, regardless of the transmission or reception means, 
could be envisaged with in the MFA and implemented by national regulators in consultation with relevant 
stakeholders. 
Whilst the DMA has set significant ex ante obligations that apply to the whole internet ecosystem, the MFA 
could pave the way to re -establishing fair market conditions for the existence of truly democratic, open, 
transparent and accountable European digital media.

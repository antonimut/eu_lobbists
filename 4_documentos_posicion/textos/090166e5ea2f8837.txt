 
 
Position to the European Commission’s public consultation for the 
European Media Freedom Act 
 
The European Partnership for Democracy (EPD) is a not-for-profit organisation with a global 
remit to support democracy, that brings together a network of 18 organisations specialising 
in the different parts of a democratic system. We are the only democracy support 
organisation covering such a wide variety of specialisations anywhere in the world. 
 
We welcome the opportunity to contribute our comments and inputs to the Public 
Consultation for the European Media Freedom Act and the very fact that the Commission will 
propose a legislative initiative on this policy area, and we make the recommendations on the 
following aspects: 
 
Going beyond the focus on the internal market: According to the inception impact assessment 
of the initiative, the legal basis of the legislative proposal will be Art. 114 TFEU. The 
consequence of this is that the focus of the initiative will be on internal market measures. We 
would like to stress that this should not be an obstacle to propose an ambitious proposal if 
the social impacts and political dimensions of the media market are taken into account  to 
put forward an initiative that will be a holistic instrument. Furthermore, the European Media 
Freedom Act should take be drafted in a way that aims to exploit synergies with, at least, the 
AVMSD, the DMS, DSA, and Regulation on political advertising. In relation to the DSA, we 
emphasise that the objective of tackling the issue of diversity of views in media and achieve 
balanced media coverage requires the unbundling of hosting and content curation for very 
large online platforms (VLOPs) in the DSA . This vertical disintegration would also have 
positive impacts in terms of competition in the digital economy. 
 
European Regulators Group for Audiovisual Media Services (ERGA): Further regulation of the 
media market in Europe should go hand in hand with a reinforcement of the role and remit 
of ERGA, which is called  to play a substantial role in the promotion of common approaches 
and standards, the exchange of best practices, and capacity and consensus building  in the 
sector. As the role of ERGA is likely to be enhanced, it becomes especially important that ERGA 
redoubles efforts to ensure that it is genuinely transparent, inclusive, and democratic ; 
including by conducting consultations with broad groups of stakeholders and by ensuring that 
the functional independence of national regulatory bodies is guaranteed. We stress that this 
term must be interpreted broadly to encompass financial, operational and administrative 
matters and not only the regulation. 
 
Allocation of public resources:  We advocate for including in the scope of this Act not only 
state advertising, which has been identified by the Centre for Media Pluralism and Media 
Freedom as an acute problem in multi ple Member States, but also to regulate the provision

of public subsidies  to the media. Public subsidies can both enhance media pluralism and 
independence and curtail it, particularly when transparency and democratic criteria for its 
allocation are lacking. To ensure that public subsidies impact media pluralism positively, we 
recommend that the EU refers to recently approved ‘Recommendations on quality journalism 
in the digital age ’ by the Council of Europe in relation to the provision of subsidies. Last, we 
recommend the EU to establish a fund to support journalistic partnerships either under the 
Media Action Plan or as part of the Creative Europe MEDIA programme. 
 
Type of EU intervention: Considering the different media landscapes across the Union and the 
specificities of different national media markets, we recommend a combination of the 
establishment of h armonised procedures, harmonized standards, common criteria  that 
would best ensure the success of the foreseen internal market initiative  as asked in Section 
1.8 of the Public Consultation questionnaire , subject to the principles of subsidiarity, 
proportionality and conferral. Harmonised procedures could be most a dequate for media 
transactions and ownership and authorisation requirements; harmonised standards could be 
most proportionate to measure audiences and ensure PSM genuine independence; and 
common criteria could be developed for restrictions on (ultimate) ownership, administrative 
decisions on media outlets, and a series of definitions such as public interest content, content 
curation or content promotion.

France Assureurs Comments on the proposal to establish a European single access point for 
company information 
 
France Assureurs welcomes the proposal of the European Commission (EC) establishing the European Single 
Access Point (ESAP). It will allow French insurers to access robust, comparable and reliable financial and ESG 
data in an electronic machine -readable format, and thus he lp to steer even more effectively their investment 
portfolios towards sustainability objectives and comply with their disclosure requirements. 
 
We particularly support that: 
• The timeline requires ESMA to establish ESAP by 31 December 2024 which is consistent with the 
Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive requirements 
• The ESAP does not create new reporting requirements and includes ESG data relating to the 
(CSRD), Sustainable Finance Disclosure Regulation and Taxonomy Regulation  
• The ESAP proposal respects the ‘file only once principle’ to avoid redundant reporting channels 
 
However, France Assureurs would like to stress that: 
• Priority given to ESG data: the EC should develop an implementation roadmap specifying 
a phased approach with ESG data needed under the CSRD, SFDR and TR available as of phase 
1. In general, ESG data should be prioritized over f inancial and other types of information  which 
can be incorporated in later phases  (see Annex 1). It is essential that the implementation timeline 
of ESAP remains consistent with CSRD. To this extent, the European supervisory authorities  
should develop and submit to the EC their draft implementing technical standards in time to ensure 
alignment with the entry into application of new disclosure obligations. In addition, we believe that 
inclusion of prudential information shouldn’t be of first concern as such information is already 
included in financial statements. Investors and analysts are used to treat such data and are familiar 
with those reports. Finally, inclusion of PRIIP and PEPP product information in the scope is 
premature. Other initiatives as the PRIIPs framework review or EIOPA PEPP register should be 
finalized before considering the appropriateness and suitability of including such information within 
ESAP.  
• Data Format and ITS: shifting progressively from an extractible format (ie  pdf) to a machine -
readable format will allow entities to scale up their capacities to comply with ESAP requirements. 
Yet leaving too much flexibility might later lead to a need of “re -formatting” data to enable 
comparability. In any case we believe that technical specifications should be made rapidly available 
so that entities can perform the relevant IT developments and be ready to comply with ESAP 
requirements as soon as possible. It might be worth considering adding information (metadata) to 
contextualise the data which will be made available on the platform. For instance, adding 
information on the type of data and whether it has been audited may be relevant from an investor’s 
perspective. Finally, while developing the European Sustainability Reporting S tandards, EFRAG

will also provide guidance on digitalisation of such standards. Consistency with ESAP requirements 
will be crucial.    
• Access to data included in the ESAP . Ideally access for users such as insurers who need the 
ESAP data to make sustainable  investment decisions and to comply to regulatory reporting 
requirements should be free. However, a small fee to access and use ESAP data might be charged 
if it ensures rapid development and a well -functioning, reliable and user -friendly platform. In any 
case, it is important to clearly distinguish between different categories of ESAP users. The fee for 
investors shall be significantly lower than for data providers who will use the data to sell analytics 
and data as part of their business model. A key criterion may refer to the volume of data extracted 
through ESAP. It might also be relevant to allow differentiated access to stakeholders outside of 
the EU. 
• Strong governance, stakeholder and expert input is key for the development of ESAP to be a 
success. Insurers support the establishment of an advisory steering board, composed of users, 
preparers, national and European competent authorities to advise ESMA on the set up and running 
of ESAP.  
• Voluntary data submissions from companies outside the EU should be possible  for EU 
companies with investments outside the EU to fulfil their reporting obligations. Further clarity is 
needed (including with respect to relevant equivalence mechanism) to encourage voluntary 
reporting.

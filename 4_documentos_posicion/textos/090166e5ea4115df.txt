Recast of Energy Performance of Buildings Directive: Leveraging digital design 
technologies for new and existing buildings across the EU 
24 March 2022 
Autodesk welcomes the European Commission’s proposal for  the Energy Performance of Buildings 
Directive (EPBD) recast and the opportunity for stakeholders to share their views on it. While we 
appreciate the recognition for the enabling role of digitalisatio n during the operation of a  building, we 
call on  the EU institutions to make the revision of the EPBD a  transformative moment for the 
decarbonisation of buildings across their full life-cycle, starting in the design phase , by taking 
advantage of the potential of digital technologies. 
Using digital technologies is the best way to accurately record, assess, simulate, measure and track 
carbon emissions over a building’s life-cycle. To deliver on the high ambitions of the Fit-for-55 package, 
strong incentives to use digital technologies are paramount. The potential of digitali sation for the 
greening of the EU building stock remains under -exploited in the EU today . The EPBD is a unique 
opportunity to leverage digital technologies to drive a more energy efficient built environment in the EU. 
1. Digital design technologies are an enabler for future high-performance buildings 
The EPBD recast is currently focused mainly on the building’s operation, with requirements to install 
building automation and control systems.  Yet, some of the most impactful choices to reduce carbon 
emissions and improve building sustainability occur during the design phase of a building.  
The choices made early on by owners, architects, engineers etc. can reduce the amount of high carbon 
materials used in buildings, while still meeting performance standards. In the design phase they can also 
take into consideration daylighting, heating and cooling load, whole building energy, and carbon 
emissions of materials and prepare for a more sustainable decommissioning or renovation process. 
Digitalisation – especially simulation and 3D modeling  - enables these professionals  to efficiently 
design and plan high -performance buildings, conduct energy analysis at key  project stages, reduce 
material waste and rework during construction . It provides clash detection 1 during design, reduc es 
embodied carbon through design and material specification,  optimises HVAC system design, improves 
structural material efficiency, optimises site planning, etc.  
Digital technologies also support factor screening 2 across distinct areas that contribute to the energy 
performance of buildings, helping teams uncover the interventions or areas for investment with the 
highest impact. 
We believe that the methodology for calculating the energy performance of buildings (Article 4) and the 
provisions on new buildings ( Article 7) should include recommendations to use technologies such as 
digital simulation and 3D modeling (BIM)3 to anticipate, analyse and optimise a building’s energy use.

2. Digital design technologies are an enabler for better and deeper renovations  
Using digital technologies all along the renovation process can contribute significantly to its cost -
efficiency and provide a  more sustainable outcome. In addition to the  physical evaluations of existing 
buildings, t hey provide advanced levels of assessment and are therefore critical to enable deep 
renovation by analysing the comparative benefits of  retrofit options such as HVAC systems, 
windows, and insulation among other s. Project stakeholders are empowered  to adopt a circular 
approach and make better choices in terms of material reuse4, choice of material and processes to avoid 
mistakes that lead to rework.  
The EPBD recast is an opportunity  to encourage the use of digital technologies for buildings retrofits, 
thereby enabling a truly “deep renovation”.  Modeling and simulation also enable streamlined 
reporting and calculations to meet new requirements under the Energy Performance Certificates.  
We believe that the requirements for existing buildings undergoing major renovations (Article 8) as well 
as the indicators required in the buildings’ renovation plans to be developed by Member States (Article 
4) should include recommendations to use technologies such as digital simulation and 3D modeling 
(BIM) to analyse renovation options and optimise deep renovation.  
3. Digital design technologies are the cornerstone of a n EU database on  energy 
performance of buildings 
The great potential of digital technologies lay s in the enhanced cooperation between stakeholders and 
in the possibility to connect and transfer knowledge across countries and sectors. 
The collection and sharing of data play an essential role in the optimisation of the energy efficiency of 
buildings over their life cycle. The structuration of  this data is key to ensuring  interoperability and an 
efficient use of the information collected. It also allows a better  integration into a future Digital Building 
Logbook. 
3D models are not only a critical tool for planning and optimising high performance building 
solutions, but they are also a valuable data source for the implementation of Article 14 to ensure that 
owners, tenants, and managers fully understand the building, its design, and its system performance 
throughout the life of the asset. Following a renovation or construction of a new building, 3D models can 
be used to build a descriptive digital twin, bring ing together all the data created about the project’s 
design, construction, and intended operational performance.  
Linking digital technologies such as BIM with life -cycle assessment databases and passpo rts such as 
the Buildings Digital Logbook allows stakeholders to analyse and evaluate energy and resource 
consumption throughout all phases of a building's life cycle (design, construction, use, deconstruction). 
In the current proposal, the structuring of databases and the link with Buildings Digital Logbook is left to 
Member States. We need more harmonisation at the EU level to ensure that this is efficient.  
The EPBD recast should empower the European Commission to adopt a delegated act on Digital Building 
Logbooks and foresee a future connection with digital technologies such as BIM.  This would also help 
drive the data goals in Article 14 and the optimization of  technical building systems and building 
automation and control systems referenced in Articles 11 and 13.

4. Digital technologies are a driver for total carbon reduction 
An ambitious approach to buildings’ decarbonisation needs to look at total carbon, including the carbon 
associated with building materials ( i.e., embodied carbon). The current EPBD proposal focuses mostly 
on operational carbon. It includes a requirement to calculate the life -cycle Global Warming Potential of 
new buildings, which constitutes a first step in its r eference to embodied carbon , h owever this 
requirement is limited in ambition and only addresses new buildings.  
The renovation of large existing buildings will generate  embodied carbon . Incentivising the 
measurement of the carbon footprint generated by the building materials selected for deep 
renovations is a contributing driver to greater environmental responsibility . Autodesk, with other 
industry leaders, has developed a free open-source tool to measure and track the embodied carbon in 
building material, called the EC3 (Embodied Carbon in Construction Calculator) 5. 
The European Commission recently published its Scenarios for a transition pathway for a resilient, 
greener and more digital construction ecosystem , in which it recommend s developing a “regulatory 
framework to calculate embodied emissions of construction activity” and creating a “roadmap to address 
whole life-cycle emissions from construction across EU policies”. 6  
We believe that Member States should be further encouraged to report on their efforts to measure and 
reduce embodied carbon in their National Building Renovation Plans (Article 3). 
Conclusion 
Autodesk believes that the EPBD is an opportunity to leverage the development of innovative and digital 
solutions to create a more sustainable Built Environment in the EU , in line with the objectives set in the 
European Commission’s Communication on a Renovation Wave for Europe and the upcoming Strategic 
Foresight report on the twinning of the green and the digital transitions 7. 
Democratizing the use of technologies to analyse and simulate energy performance is essential to reach 
these objectives. The role of public authorities as a public procurer, owner, supporter, policymaker, and 
regulator will be key to set the path forward and the pace of adoption of these technologies.

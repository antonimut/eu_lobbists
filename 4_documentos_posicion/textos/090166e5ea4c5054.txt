AEGIS Europe’s feedback on the Proposal for protection from economic 
coercion by third countries 
 
AEGIS Europe is an industry alliance that brings together more than 20 European manufacturing 
associations committed to manufacturing in the EU on a truly level playing field ensured through rules-
based, free and fair international trade. Our members accou nt for more than €500 billion in annual 
turnover, as well as for millions of jobs across the EU. 
There is a fear that third countries might decide to take economic measures against the EU through 
retaliatory measures affecting trade or investment in order have EU actions modified or terminated. 
Whether these measures can be addressed through existing instruments, in particular WTO dispute 
settlement is not always obvious. Third countries can and do act in ways that coercively interfere with 
the EU’s internal processes that do not amount to an actual breach of the international agreement in 
question. The introduction of this instrument would be particularly important given that the EU 
currently lacks the tools necessary for acting against economic coercion. I f adopted, the Proposal 
would allow the EU to dissuade or offset these actions which are ultimately designed to hurt EU 
interests whether on the internal market or on the export side.  
Thus, we welcome the proposal to establish an “anti-coercion” instrument which, properly designed, 
could act  as a powerful deterrence tool to contrast the increasing weaponisation of trade . The 
instrument should cover existing legislative gap s. Some of our members export up to 50% of  their 
production amounting to more than  €135 billion a year in direct exports. Our industry sectors have 
been exposed to coercive actions in the past, for instance, when additional tariffs have been imposed 
in the context of international trade disputes or when third countries have acted to interfere with the 
internal EU decision-making processes to prevent the adoption of a specific act or obtain its 
modification through blackmail actions vis à vis the Commission or the Member States. 
The scope of Article 2 (1) of the Proposal should be widened so that “measures of economic coercion” 
cover those that target EU economic operators, in addition to those actions directly aimed at 
government authorities.  Thus, actions targeting major and/or otherwise strategic EU economic 
interests1, including value chains, should be considered " measures of economic coercion " whether 
directed at Member States and/or EU authorities or enterprises.  
AEGIS Europe considers that it is essential for the Commission to be able to act in a timely and effective 
manner under the instrument, with due regard to the gravity of the subject matter, the degree of 
pressure being exerted and the possibility of irreparable harm from inacti on.  In this respect, AEGIS 
Europe supports the provisions of Articles 7 (5) and (6) which give to the Commission the appropriate 
means to react rapidly to interferences of third countries regarding the cessation, modification or 
adoption of a particular act.

Intergovernmental consultations and all other avenues should be explored in parallel to the use of the 
instrument, in order to avoid , to the extent possible , a needless escalation of actions and 
counteractions that would harm global trade and give rise to more protectionist policies.   
It is important that stakeholders, including industry associations, be consulted before the adoption , 
amendment, suspension or termination  of anti-coercion measures, as provided by Article 11 of the 
Proposal. This would allow due consideration of, inter alia, the impact of these measures on EU 
operators and business partners, and the administrative burden which may be occasioned by the 
measures. 
AEGIS Europe respects the fact that the European Commission is solely responsible for its own internal 
organisation. H owever, bearing  in mind the complexity of coercion, AEGIS Europe considers it 
necessary for the Commission to establish an integrated internal system that has sufficient 
competence to examine and evaluate the acti ons being taken by trade partners and to respond 
accordingly. 
AEGIS Europe considers that t hird country coercion may occur when the EU is in the process of 
adopting rules aimed at fighting climate change, given the different climate ambitions among 
countries. Thus, AEGIS Europe welcomes the fact that the illustrative example s mentioned in the 
Impact Assessment of the Proposal2 specifically refer to rules protecting the environment such as the 
Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism3 which is currently being discussed by the EU legislators. AEGIS 
Europe believes that the new anti-coercion instrument could be employed by the EU to counteract any 
measures or actions taken by third countries with a view to interfere in EU’s climate ambiti ons and 
which may have severe negative impact on EU industries. 
The scope of the envisaged instrument needs to be clearly specified since it is not clear whether it 
covers extraterritorial sanctions  which are already covered by the Blocking Statute (Regula tion 
2271/96). It appears necessary to clearly spell out how these two instruments will interact – if one 
instrument overrides the other or if they apply in tandem.  
Finally, AEGIS Europe welcomes the anti-coercion instrument as a key tool for the achievement of the 
EU’s open strategic autonomy. As a signal to international partners that the EU is not willing to accept 
economic coercion, the tool would, ultimately, help defend an open and multilateral global trade 
environment.

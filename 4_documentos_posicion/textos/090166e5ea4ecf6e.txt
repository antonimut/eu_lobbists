1) The EBF welcomes the establishment of a European single access 
point (ESAP) for companies’ financial and sustainable investment -
related information made public pursuant to EU legislation 
The EBF welcomes and supports the proposal to establish a European single access 
point (ESAP) to provide investors with a common database to financial and sustainability 
information that EU companies are required to publish . ESAP allows financial analysists, 
market intermediaries, investors and lenders and other users easy access to standardized 
and machine-readable public information disclosed by companies.

European Banks share the European Commission's views that current  flows of financial 
and non-financial information publicly disclosed pursuant to EU financial legislation should 
be systematized. Fragmentation, as well as the lack of comparability, standardization, and 
varying quality of data, constitute a barrier whose removal remains essential for the 
realization of the true potential of a single market for capital in Europe. 
In this respect, European banks share the European Commission’s expectations that ESAP 
will not only directly benefit users but will substantially contribute to the achievement of 
the Capital Markets Union (CMU)  in its objective of improving companies’ direct and 
indirect access to capital markets by giving them greater visibility and increasing business 
comparability. 
In general, ESAP is seen as having  the potential to lay solid foundations for  the 
development and integration of EU financial markets  and data strategy as part of the 
financial common European data space , as well as to support the objectives of the 
Strategy for Financing the Transition to a Sustainable Economy and the European 
Green Deal. 
Notably, EBF Members believe that the proposal for a voluntary contribution scheme open 
to all companies, alongside with public management of the initiative  entrusted to ESMA  
and coupled with free and widespread access to the information, will greatly contribute to 
proceed in the direction of producing substantial incentives to private investments. 
Against this background, EBF members emphasize that ESAP, in its current design, scope 
and timeline to be established by 31 December 2024, is a highly ambitious project. It 
envisages reporting obligations from a total of 37 EU legislative acts that are to be 
introduced gradually, having data and information transferred to collecting bodies from 1 
January 2024 and from there to ESAP, where it will be made accessible. In 30 of these 37 
legislative acts, the European Commission or another body will be empowered to adopt 
implementing measures with specifications regarding the data format. 
Specifically, the start date  for ESAP as envisaged by the current proposal remains 
challenging, not least in  consideration of the time that will be needed to  implement the 
relevant format specifications  following the publication of the final implementing 
measures.  
Therefore, to foster a timely, smooth and efficient implementation of ESAP, EBF Members 
would like to offer  further consideration s on a number of issues and open points to be 
addressed in the course of the ongoing legislative process. 
 
2) ESAP should “start small, think big” , with sustainability 
information at the forefront 
EBF Members anticipate that the technical work needed to fully implement the correct 
collection and transmission protocols of data that is “machine readable” and “extractable” 
is set to place heavy and substantial material operational impacts on data preparers. Under 
the current setup, EBF Members emphasize the risk that the foreseen start date for ESAP 
will be difficult to meet. Hence, EBF Members reiterate that the implementation strategy 
for ESAP should be carefully assessed as to avoid placing unnecessary or disproportionate 
costs and burdens on data prepares. 
The current policy objective to  include from the get -go almost the entirety of  publicly 
disclosed information pursuant to EU legislation o n financial markets  in the mandatory 
3 
 
regime, including prudential requirements of banks, accounting and financial information 
and sustainability, makes it difficult for  those who are subject to such legislation  to be 
ready within the timeframe indicated by the Commission (31 December 2024).  Such 
regulated information is currently available in different formats and not necessarily 
machine-readable, as it is not necessarily sub ject to any market standard. This implies 
that further standardization will require a time  and resource consuming process for the 
authorities and the industry to develop and implement new ad-hoc data gathering 
standards. At the same time, to produce the least impact on financial intermediaries (FIs) 
and data preparers, any further standardization exercise will need to be compatible with 
market-standard software solutions. Otherwise, it will be very difficult to establish ESAP. 
EBF Members stress that the need for non-financial (sustainability) public data is critical, 
as we currently lack any system for its EU-wide distribution. Banks rely on the disclosures 
of companies for risk and portfolio management, investment and financing decision 
making as well as their own reporting under the disclosure requirement of sustain able 
finance regulation.  Sustainability information disclosed and required for disclosures under 
the upcoming Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive, Taxonomy Regulation 
and Sustainable Finance Disclosure Regulation  should be made available through 
ESAP as soon as it is reported by companies. As the reporting standards are currently 
being built, it is paramount that they are designed in an extractable format , required by 
ESAP from the start and prioritized in the setting -up of ESAP. It is particularly important 
that the implementation timeline of ESAP remains consistent with the CSRD. 
Therefore, EBF Members emphasize that EU regulators should rethink the all-in approach 
by “starting small, thinking big” . Specifically, to ensure that ESAP is effectively and 
timely deployed where it is needed the most, European banks believe that the building of 
ESAP should  “start small ” by proceeding gradually, giving priority to sustainability, 
accounting, and financial information. The information covered by the go-live date should 
be that immediately relevant for companies to become more visible  on the market , for 
investors to form better -informed decisions  and for FIs and companies to build an 
accessible ESG data set. 
Once established, ESAP should then “think big” by going beyond its primary objectives. 
Its scope should grow as its functionalities are ensured and data become s machine 
readable. However, to do so, ESAP should avoid two risks: 
i. It should not include items of information for which there is little rationale to use 
the ESAP infrastructure. 
ii. It should be technically possible for ESAP to include new disclosure obligations that 
may arise from future EU  sectoral legislation. ESAP itself should not impose n ew 
obligations. Therefore, it is key that ESAP is designed based on an open, future-
proof architecture that allows the incremental connection to new data feeds and 
extensions at a later stage.  
In addition, to manage the risk that ESAP and the technical standards will not be ready by 
the envisioned start date, EBF Members suggest that  the entry into force of reporting to 
ESAP should be tied to the publication of the technical standards. Entry into force should 
not happen earlier than at least one year after the publication of the standards. 
Specifically, i n line with the priority-oriented approach outlined above , EBF Members  
emphasize that: 
4 
 
i. The phasing-in should also consider priority needs both in terms of data access 
and in terms operational impact on contributors. Access to sustainability information 
is most acutely needed. 
ii. There is no need to include real time or daily flows / trade transactions at launch. 
iii. In case of highly technical and frequently updated data points  such as data 
resulting from EMIR or MIFIR transaction reporting, for which detailed technical 
specifications have already been implemented at great cost to industry, ESAP should 
adopt “look through” approach for legislation in scope to consider whether such 
data-points should be further limited in the initial phase ( e.g., key ratios or KPIs 
from NFRD or other regulations). 
iv. ESAP should only cover “essentially” public information . Some transactional 
data could remain private for a first period and then made public at a second stage 
and/or they could be sub ject to fees for access . Sometimes both  options are 
contained in a unique regulation and unique reporting. 
v. For PRIIPS regulation, many key information documents are related to private 
placements and should not be uploaded in ESAP (see Annex). 
 
3) ESAP should include comprehensive sustainability disclosures  
In line with the above, EBF Members emphasize the need to make sure that the Omnibus 
Regulation and Directive cover the incoming ESG-related legislative texts: EU Green Bond 
Standard, Corporate Sustainable Reporting Directive (that will include Transition Plans and 
a list of indicators, to be precisely defined by EFRAG), Sustainable Finance Disclosure 
Regulation, Taxonomy regulation, Benchmark Regulation  and CRR-CRD with Pillar 3 
requirements for credit institutions. 
In this context, European banks welcome the possibility offered by the European 
Commission for companies excluded from NFRD and then CSRD scopes to provide their 
ESG information on a voluntary basis. 
Moreover, EBF Members are of the view that to meet the requirements of the Taxonomy 
Regulation and the planned CSRD, it would be necessary that data on the energy efficiency 
of buildings are also accessible in ESAP. In addition to the directives listed in the draft 
annex, the "DIRECTIVE 2010/31/EU OF THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND OF THE 
COUNCIL of 19 May 2010 on the energy performance of buildings" should also be included 
in the current version. 
 
4) ESAP should respect the ‘file only once’ principle and avoid the 
creation of new burdens or duplication of regulated disclosures 
Regarding data collection, EBF Members  appreciate the intention of the European 
Commission to build on existing channels. However, European banks are of the view that 
further work is needed to ensure that data exchange protocols are determined in a way 
that avoids excessive administrative burden  for FI s or additional costs  due to 
revamping of existing reporting chains. 
In this context , EBF Members reiterate the importance of maintaining a strong focus on 
minimizing the burden on companies  throughout the process, not least through 
ESMA's development of formats and requirements, and that existing w ell-functioning 
digital solutions should be used as far as possible. 
5 
 
The duty of submitting the information to ESAP through the “collecting bodies” by 
regulated institutions should not turn into a burden for regulated entities that are already 
required to publish or transmit information to reporting mechanisms, national or European 
authorities, market infrastructures or any other existing information storage bodies, 
system or mechanisms already delegated to receive such information and according to the 
different pieces of legislation in scope.  
Therefore, in order not to duplicate reporting activities and to avoid an increase in 
reporting costs, ESAP maintains the “file only once” principle by collecting information 
directly through existing reporting entities or mechanisms . ESAP should also make sure 
that costs and resources collected by authorities are not directly or indirectly passed on to 
the companies. 
Against this background, European banks would suggest that: 
i. When still to be identified (for PRIIPS for instance, there is no collection body at 
this stage), existing arrangements/ channels/ collection bodies  should be 
leveraged when possible. 
ii. An expert group should be established to define additional protocols. 
At the same time, EBF Members welcome: 
iii. The proposal that access to the information will take place via the web or API and 
will be in most cases free of charge. 
iv. The Commission’s objective to give friendly access conditions: 
a. For data user credit institutions (for instance, for those that particip ate to 
NZBA), accessibility and centralization of data will be crucial as well as the 
normalization of methodologies and data to support comparability needs. 
b. Data should be made available as a workable consolidated database 
(Bloomberg-like) as ability to exploit it will be crucial for scaling-up. 
 
5) EBF’s observations on the voluntary information regime 
EBF Members  support the voluntary information regime as drafted in the legislative 
proposal. However, it is worth underlying that the definition of formats and technical 
requirements, delegated to ESMA, will be crucial for the setting up of such regime. A 
balance between the standardization of the information and the reduction of burdens 
for companies who submit themselves to the voluntary regime, should be found. This 
would be an important incentive. 
In addition , EBF Members believe that voluntary data submissions fr om non-EU 
companies could also be encouraged subject to further assessment, including of relevant 
equivalence mechanisms. This would further support the adoption of ESAP and allow EU 
users with exposures to non-EU clients to better fulfill their reporting obligations. 
 
 
6 
 
6) Accessibility 
While EBF Members emphasize that ESAP should be  machine-readable and accessible 
through APIs for the meeting the needs of advanced market participants, they believe that 
ESAP should be made accessible to individuals, in line with the aim to encourage the 
participation of European households in financial markets . This entails that an easy -to-
navigate interface should be made available to enable simple queries from retail investors 
willing to identify companies that meet certain criteria, or to retrieve data or reports for a 
given company. 
 
7) Cost of use, budgetary and responsibility observations 
Finally, albeit the ESAP proposal stipulates that, as a rule, information should be accessible 
free of charge, it also allows ESMA to impose fees for users that require very large volumes 
of data or frequently updated information.  
EBF Members consider that, as recommended by High-Level Forum (HLF) on the CMU, the 
ESAP should provide access to information “ freely accessible to the public and free of 
fees or license use.” European banks expect that the financial industry will be the main 
user of ESAP, especially with regard to sustainability information. Therefore, EBF Members 
believe that the access to data should be made free regardless of the volume , even 
more so in the case of information that is required to fulfill regulatory obligations and 
would be retrievable via ESAP. 
At the same time, should this prove to  be instrumental t owards ensuring the rapid 
development and deployment of a well -functioning and reliable user -friendly platform, 
some EBF Members anticipate that an appropriate and proportionate fee may be charged 
to frequent users extracting large volumes of data, subject to further assessment following 
the inception phase of ESAP.  
The funding provided to ESMA (for both administrative and operational costs related to 
ESAP) should be solely co -funded by the Union and National Competent Authorities (in 
accordance with ESMA’s founding Regulation 1095/2010, i.e. 40% from union funds and 
60% through contributions from Member State National Competent Authorities).  
Regarding the budgetary implications , the proposed regulation envisages that ESAP will 
be staffed with three full-time employees (FTEs). EBF Members consider it highly ambitious 
to operate such a substantial database with three employees only.  
Finally, EBF Members are of the view that the responsibility regime should be clarified, 
considering the specificities of financial institutions who rely on their clients’ disclosures  
and should therefore not be considered responsible for the accuracy of such data.

The information to be provided by clearing members concerning the level of protection

The proposed amendment sets information requirements to be met by CSDs. In some 
cases, however, this will not be practicable as the CSDR requires the information to be 
published not by CSDs, but by competent authorities (e.g., Article 12(2) and Article 62 of 
the CSDR) or other entities (cf. Article 59(4)(j) of the CSDR). 
 
We also believe it is an open question whether the specific information provided by CSDs 
and required by ESAP will be sufficiently anonymized and really fit to contribute  to 
investment decisions made by  investors, since it does not normally relate to the capital 
market products themselves or their issuers, but to the settlemen t of trades and the 
supervision of CSDs. 
 
Especially when it comes to information that is made public in aggregated form on an 
annual basis, we would recommend dispensing with regular/monthly reporting aimed 
primarily at supervisors of financial market inf rastructure to avoid information overload 
(e.g., Article 7(1) and (9) of the CSDR). Information aimed at ensuring the stability of 
CSD services (such as the measurement/monitoring/management of liquidity risks of the 
banking service provider pursuant to Articles 54(3), 54(4) and 59(4)) is also likely to play 
only a minor role when making investment decisions.  
 
Moreover, in case of frequent settlement fails, the CSD publish a temporary suspension 
notice. EBF Members emphasize that further consideration shou ld be given on the 
possibility to update ESAP with the appropriate frequency, including when the suspension 
ends. 
 
Information on CSDs or participants which are not issuers might also be superfluous (see, 
for example, Articles 12(2), 26(4), 27(4) – information on promoting the gender under -
represented on the management body, 27(7), 28(2), 33(1) and 33(2)). 
 
With respect to Article 38(6) of the CSDR, information from the CSD is indeed sufficient 
since CSDs already must publicly disclose this information today. The question that arises 
here is to what extent the text (now in PDF form) should have to be delivered in a data 
extractable format. As to the data format requirements, please see our general remarks.  
 
Regarding Article 62 of the CSDR: compete nt authorities must publish details of 
administrative sanctions or measures on their website. In this case, too, the idea of the 
CSD reporting this information to ESMA seems impracticable. In addition, the proposed 
new Article 74a (3) of the CSDR envisages that the information should contain the name 
and LEI of the CSD. In our view, it would only make sense to include these details as 
supplementary information unless the sanctions are imposed on the CSD.

And with respect to the proposed new Article 74a(1)(b) of the CSDR: (i) should read “the 
name of the CSD” instead of “all the names of the CSD”. Furthermore, it is not clear to us 
what classification types of the information referred to in (iv) should be assigned to, or 
what specific publication periods are meant under (v).

On the submission of KIDs: EBF Members highlight the following observations concerning

o Some KIDs are not disseminated to the public as the placement of securities benefits is an

o Specificities regarding data should be clarified: what type of meta data or data format

o Unique collecting body: a unique collecting body should be designated for all public KIDs

o Submission by external vendors: the PRIIPs manufacturers should be able to delegate the

o It should be clarified if the submission to ESAP will be triggered by local transposition of

3. Directive 2004/109/EC on the harmonisation  of transparency requirements in

The European Singe Electronic Format (ESEF) has not, or only to a very limited degree, 
achieved the aims of greater compa rability and of easier access. In particular, the 
application of the prescribed standardized IFRS taxonomy in the annual financial 
statements of banks has not led to any additional value in the information provided to 
users of those annual financial statements. The balance sheets and profit and loss accounts 
of banks have a different structure and rationale to those of industrial companies. The 
compulsory application of a taxonomy designed for the real economy means that the 
special features of banking cann ot be adequately represented. This problem is overcome 
in the ESEF with the option of using extensions. However, this does not make the reports 
any more comparable. The lack of informative value and usefulness of the ESEF bank 
balance sheets is shown, among other things, by the fact that, to our knowledge, no bank 
has ever received any response or other reaction to the ESEF from users .. 
 
There have also been technical problems with ESEF reporting. As far as we are aware, 
there are currently no software solutions on the market that can properly meet the ESEF. 
This also applies to the major software companies.  
 
From the point of view of the software developers, the problems mainly concern the XHTML 
and not the iXBRL publication. It must be technically possib le to implement the 
specifications, otherwise it makes no sense to establish an ESAP.

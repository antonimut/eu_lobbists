AREA position on the Energy Performance of Buildings Directive revision 
proposal 
 
30 March 2022 
 
 
AREA members, as the architects of RACHP systems, responsible for their design, installation, 
maintenance and repair, welcome the revision of the Energy Performance of Buildings Directive. The 
decarbonisation of the buildings sector is vital to achieve the EU’s climate and energy objectives, given 
that buildings are responsible for 40% of total energy consumption. With cooling needs expected to 
increase in the years to come, the refrigeration, air conditioning and heat pump (RACHP) sector has 
an important role to play in delivering on the energy efficiency and decarbonisation targets for 2030. 
 
Without proper design of the cooling system, energy savings of efficient equipment can be totally 
negated. Indeed, proper system design, effective installation, as we ll as regular maintenance and 
servicing are of the utmost importance to ensure that expected energy savings become a reality. 
Periodic inspections of heating and cooling systems are necessary to ensure the efficient functioning 
of the system, even in the presence of automated monitoring systems. 
 
RACHP contractors have in -depth knowledge on the design of systems, proper  maintenance and 
operation and can provide tailor -made solutions to consumers based on users’ specific needs. They 
use every available solution with complete technology neutrality and with the sole aim of ensuring 
the highest level of reliability, energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness. 
 
We would like to take this opportunity to bring attention to the following key aspects of the proposed 
revision: 
 
1. Definitions 
We welcome the new definitions for ‘non-renewable primary energy factor’ and ‘renewable primary 
energy factor ’ because the primary energy factors will be  no further politically assigned , as it is 
oftentimes the case today. 
However, we strongly believe that a definition on ‘heat pumps’ should be present in the Directive and 
expand it  to include their capability to transfer both heat and cold  from not only their natural 
surroundings, but also other sources like thermal grids. 
2. Indoor environmental quality (IEQ) and climate conditions 
It is a positive development to see new indoor climate provisions for new buildings and having them 
included in the methodology for calculating the energy performance of buildings in Articles 4 and 7.  
However, the draft Directive has much more potential to address indoor environmental quality. 
Although the E uropean Commission touches upon the issues with a provision to support the

integration of measuring and control devices for the monitoring and regulation of indoor air quality  
(IAQ), we believe that mandatory minimum requirements are a must.  There should b e mandatory 
IAQ/IEQ references and use of European harmonised  standards to ensure a consistent 
implementation at Member States level. 
3. Financial incentives and market barriers and certification of building professionals 
During the COVID-19 crisis, the shortages of qualified staff and investment in skills became even more 
evident. With the evolving ambition and targets of the EU, and the high -level technicality of RACHP 
systems, it is highly necessary to have a corresponding support for the role of installers. Therefore, we 
fully support the proposal aimed  at requiring Member States to provide measures and financing to 
promote education and training to ensure that there is a sufficient workforce with the appropriate 
level of skills corresponding to the needs in the building, construction, and energy efficiency sector. 
We also support the obligation on Member States to ensure the appropriate level of competence for 
building professionals and ensure that certification or equivalent qualification schemes are available 
for providers of integrated renovation works. 
 
4. Issuing and assessments of Energy performance certificates (EPCs)  
We strongly support having Energy Performance Certificates (EPCs) issued by independent experts 
following an on-site visit and the shorter period for their update. However, assessments on adaptation 
of air-conditioning systems might introduce higher costs and complexity for installers and assessors.  
5. Inspections and building automation and control systems (BACS) 
We welcome t he Commission’s push for more frequent and encompassing inspections of heating, 
ventilation and air-conditioning systems performed by qualified and certified experts  to ensure that 
building systems operate efficiently and allow for achieving the Union’s goals. 
 
However, we are worried that there are crucial unclarities regarding the threshold definition of the 
effective output. The counting methodology is not clear and could lead to issues like double counting. 
Furthermore, if only systems above 70 kW are inspected, small installations will be at risk of remaining 
inefficient.  
We also stress that the requirements for the installation of building automation and control systems 
(BACS) are uncertain. Also, lowering the threshold for the effective rated o utput to 70  kW by 31 
December 2029 is unrealistic and could be only feasible in around 15 years. 
 
6. Independent experts 
We strongly support that the energy performance certification of buildings, the establishment of 
renovation passports, the smart readiness assessment and the inspections of heating and AC systems 
are carried out by qualified and /or certified, rather than accredited experts. 
However, making available to the public t he information on training and certifications at Member 
State level might bring  additional administrative burden on the assessors and building 
managers/owners.

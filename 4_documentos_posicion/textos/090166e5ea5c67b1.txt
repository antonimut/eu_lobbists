  
 
Position on the revision of the Energy Performance of 
Buildings Directive 
Brussels, 30 March 2022  
 
Introduction 
 
The European Infrared Heating Alliance (EIHA) and the Electric Underfloor Heating Alliance (EUHA) 
welcome the Commission’s ambition to achieve zero-emission buildings (ZEBs) by 2050. We strongly 
believe that local space heaters (LSH), especially infrared and electric underfloor heaters, can 
contribute to these objectives as a result of their characteristics and relatively easy installation. As a 
result, LSH can directly foster the uptake of modernised heating networks as part of the currently 
ongoing energy transition. 
 
We believe that the revision of the Energy Performance of Buildings Directive (EPBD) is 
indispensable for the achievement of Europe’s climate objectives, fostering economic growth, 
supporting comfort and wellbeing of building users, while simultaneously decoupling from the use of 
fossil fuels. As such, we have the following main messages: 
1) Support for the complete electrification of heating 
2) Recommendation for additional strengthening of zero-emission buildings by lowering the PEF 
coefficient for local space heaters and by accepting all types of renewable energy sources 
3) Support for the proposed Minimum Energy Performance Standards 
4) Recommendation to include Indoor Environmental Quality in the Energy Performance Certificates 
5) Support for the smartness and digitalisation of heating 
 
Recommendations 
 
1. Support for a full electrification of heating 
Buildings are responsible for roughly 36 % of EU’s greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions, most of which 
originates from heating and cooling. Heating systems based on fossil fuels still dominate in the 
European building stock: two-thirds of heating demand is covered by fossil-based technologies. 
 
Therefore, the EIHA and EUHA support a complete electrification of the European heating sector. 
This enables the heating of our buildings, both residential and non-residential, to be fully powered 
by renewables. Aside from the energy efficiency savings, this also fosters better Indoor 
Environmental Quality (IEQ), contributing to the comfort of wellbeing of building users. As such, a 
ban on financial support for fossil-based heating will further enable consumers to make the switch to 
electric heating. We are aware that such an electrification will require a significant increase in the 
renewable energy production, which we support locally and centrally.  
 
2. Additional strengthening of zero-emission buildings by lowering PEF coefficient for local space 
heaters and by accepting all types of renewable energy sources 
We believe that the introduction of ZEBs is a vital step to decarbonising the European building stock.

across the EU, and infrared and electric underfloor heating can immediately switch to electricity 
sources from renewables. From this perspective, we believe that the default coefficient of the 
Primary Energy Factor (PEF) for LSH, set in Annex IV of the Energy Efficiency Directive, should reflect 
this transition to renewables by including a significantly lower coefficient value.  
 
Moreover, we are concerned about the limiting ZEB definition in draft Article 2(2), as it restricts 
renewable energy to the power that is generated on-site, ignoring the possibility of using centrally 
generated electricity from renewable sources. Infrared and electric underfloor heating work on both, 
but it would be against the principle of technology neutrality to limit the definition of ZEBs by 
including only renewable generated on-site, instead of accepting all renewable-powered energy. 
 
3. Support for Minimum Energy Performance Standards 
We strongly support the introduction of minimum energy performance standards (MEPS) at EU level 
for the worst performing sections of the building stock. Requiring an EPC Class E as the minimum 
from 2030 onwards will drastically accelerate the renovation of the existing building stock. 
 
Nevertheless, as energy renovations require long-term planning and predictability for market actors 
and citizens, we would like to encourage any further improvements to the defined scope and 
timeframe of the proposed MEPS to ensure that the multiple benefits of ambitious energy 
renovations are achieved, and a carbon lock-in effect is avoided. 
 
4. Include Indoor Environmental Quality in the Energy Performance Certificates 
In order not to compromise the health and wellbeing of building occupants, recommendations for 
cost-effective or cost-optimal upgrading of energy performance should also incorporate IEQ aspects. 
Considering that thermal comfort, indoor air quality, adequate levels of natural lighting, and 
acoustics are among the most important drivers and benefits of renovation, including these factors 
in the EPC can further drive renovation. 
 
5. Support for the smartness and digitalisation of heating 
The EIHA and EUHA welcome the boost that the proposal will give to the deployment of smart 
Technical Building Systems (TBS), particularly heating. In particular, we support the improved 
requirements for building automation and control systems (BACS) in draft Article 20, which in 
essence will make smartness mandatory for new and renovated buildings from 2030 onwards. We 
also appreciate the fact that the Smart Readiness Indicator (SRI) will become mandatory for non-
residential buildings from 2026 onwards, although this will require an update or the SRI calculation 
methodology to ensure harmonisation and improve the health dimensions of buildings. 
 
However, conflicting and double regulation on TBS should be avoided in light of the Ecodesign 
Framework, especially considering ENER Lots 20 (LSH), 33 (demand-side flexibility), and 38 (BACS). It 
would be prudent to focus the centre of the weight of smartness and digitalisation requirements 
under the dedicated product-specific Ecodesign regulations, especially concerning interoperability, 
so that the requirements are suitable for the specific product group characteristics.

About the signatories 
 
About the European Infrared Heating Alliance (EIHA) 
The European Infrared Heating Alliance (EIHA) is a network of national associations that represents 
infrared heating manufacturers who have a strong European footprint. Together, we develop 
environmentally friendly heating that is powered by renewables. Our products are fully repairable, 
sustainable, and improve the comfort and wellbeing of users. More information: www.ig-
infrared.com/en/home.html 
 
About the Electric Underfloor Heating Alliance (EUHA) 
The Electric Underfloor Heating Alliance (EUHA) represents European manufacturers of electrical 
underfloor heating and their immediate supply chain. Our members foster energy savings through 
the adoption of renewable-powered smart grid infrastructure, and continuously improve the 
comfort and wellbeing of users. See also: www.euha-alliance.eu.

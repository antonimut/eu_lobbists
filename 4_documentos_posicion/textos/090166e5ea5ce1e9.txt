ANDIMAT Comments to EPBD recast proposal 
 
Introduction 
 
ANDIMAT is Spain´s national association for thermal insulation manufacturers and it is formed by firms and associations that represent the vast majority of 
manufacturers and organizations that operate in the Spanish Market. Andimat´s goal is to promote and communicate the benefits of thermal insulation by 
providing  thermal comfort to users and by reducing the energy needs of buildings, thus decarbonising heating and cooling, improving the energy 
performance of buildings and fighting energy poverty. 
Aspects of EPBD Recast we fully support 
 
ANDIMAT fully supports the incorporation of new definitions like: Zero-emission buildings, national building renovation plans, minimum energy 
performance standards (MEPS), Renovation passport and deep renovation. Andimat also fully supports the harmonization of Energy Performance 
Certificates and the creation of a database for Energy Performance Certificates. 
Areas for improvement of EPBD recast 
 
However, ANDIMAT finds room for improvement in three main areas: 
1. Targets for MEPs and definition of zero emission building should be more ambitious 
2. A higher relevance of the minimum energy needs of a building, thus applying in a more effective way the “energy efficiency first” principle. 
3. Making Energy Performance Certificates easier to understand for end users, in particular, by sharing information on the minimum energy needs of a 
building.  
Besides these three areas, for which we propose changes in  the text that can be found in the table below, we would like to reinforce the importance of 
assuring that the Global Warming Potential (GWP) is calculated correctly and taking into account the entire life cycle of the building. The reason for this

Rationale for change 
Article 2 
Definitions 
2. ‘zero-emission 
building’   
‘zero-emission building’ means a building with a very high energy 
performance as determined in accordance with Annex I and 
minimum energy needs of 15 kWh/m2.y , where the very low 
amount of energy still required is fully covered by energy from 
renewable sources generated on-site, from a renewable energy 
community within the meaning of Directive (EU) 2018/2001 
[amended RED] or from a district heating and cooling system, in 
accordance with the requirements set out in Annex III; 
 
The highest class of energy performance of a building is reached 
through a combination of three factors: reducing energy needs, 
using most efficient heating and cooling systems and introducing 
renewable energies. It is therefore a team effort supported by 
three different industrial sectors. However, the current definition 
is missing a reference to one of the three factors, to reducing 
energy needs, which itself is the consequence of the “energy 
efficiency first” principle. It is a mistake not to explicitly consider 
the benefit of the reduction of energy needs by the thermal 
envelope, and to only take into account the decarbonisation of 
the energy production.  
 
The cleanest energy is the energy that is not used, and this basic 
principle is missing in the most important definition of the EPBD. 
At the same time, energy cost is rising within the EU and energy 
poverty and energy independence of the EU becoming of a 
pressing issue, of high political importance.  
 
At the same time, reducing the energy needs of a building to a 
minimum is reached through the work of many small and 
medium local enterprises, thus helping local economies.

On the other hand, it is possible to set a common value of 
minimum energy needs across Europe, applicable to all EU 
member states, independent of the regions.  
 
For all of these reasons, we propose to add the reference to 
minimum energy needs within the definition, and to set it at 15 
kWh/m2.y, which is a well known and used limit used in the 
Passivehaus standard. 
 
Article 9 
 
Minimum energy 
performance 
standards 
2. In addition to the minimum energy performance standards 
established pursuant to paragraph 1, each Member State may 
shall establish minimum energy performance standards for 
the renovation of all other existing buildings. 
 
Where established, the minimum energy performance 
standards shall be designed with a view to the national 
roadmap and the 2030, 2040 and 2050 targets contained in 
the Member State’s building renovation plan and to the 
transformation of the national building stock into zero-
emission buildings by 2050. 
The goals set for buildings with energy performance F and G in 
paragraph 1 are considered adequate, however they are not 
ambitious enough for the rest of building stock in EU. The reason 
is that any renovation that takes place on these buildings under 
any national building code of any member state, will very likely 
end up in a higher energy performance class than F or G.  In the 
particular case of Spain, any renovation that affects more than 
25 % of the thermal envelope will need to comply with certain 
aspects of the Spanish Technical Code, and will then easily end 
up in a class D or C. Therefore, it is feasible to be more ambitious 
in these goals on paper, because in many cases, they will be met 
automatically. 
 
The best way be more ambitious is to oblige (using “shall”, 
instead of “may”) all member states to prepare minimum energy 
performance standards for renovation of all existing buildings 
(not only F and G), thus allowing flexibility to reach higher goals 
by simply applying national building codes to renovations. Some 
countries are already going a step further and are applying 
criteria of MEPS to rentals and sales of buildings. This would be a 
flexible obligation, allowing the EPBD to have a stronger impact,

and at the same time allowing flexibility to member states on the 
means to reach the same final goal. 
 
Article 16 
Energy 
performance 
certificates 
1.Member States shall lay down the necessary measures to 
establish a system of certification of the energy performance of 
buildings.  
The energy performance certificate shall include the energy 
performance of a building     expressed by a numeric indicator of 
primary energy use in kWh/(m2.y),     and reference values such 
as minimum energy performance requirements   , minimum 
energy performance standards, nearly zero-energy building 
requirements, minimum energy needs, and zero-emission 
building requirements,       in order to make it possible for owners 
or tenants of the building or building unit to compare and assess 
its energy performance.  
Following the same rationale used in Article 2 for including 
minimum energy needs, this requirement should also be applied 
in the Energy Performance Certificates. This is also a parameter 
that is easily understood by the end user.

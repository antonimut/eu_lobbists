 
 
Feedback to Public Consultation “Energy efficiency – Revision of the 
Energy Performance of Buildings Directive” 
Date: 31 March 2022 
 
The European PVC Profiles and related Building Products Association EPPA represents the 
manufacturers of PVC window systems and related building products in Europe. About 
25,000 employees process about 1,4 million tonnes of PVC creating a turnover of €4 billion 
with profile systems and building products. Based in Brussels, EPPA provides a common 
platform for bundling national activities in the fields of PVC window technology, recycling, 
environment and public affairs.  
We welcome the opportunity to provide feedback in the course of the revision of the 
Energy Performance of Buildings Directive, which we consider a key pillar of the work 
towards climate neutrality by 2050.  
 
We would like to draw attention to our recently published window stakeholder position 
paper that addresses the key elements of the Directive and how windows can support, and 
are essential to the success of energy efficient refurbishment in Europe: 
https://tinyurl.com/yfrkyapd  
 
Beyond this horizontal window position, we would like you to consider the following points 
in the revision process. 
 
1. Need for an aligned legislative framework  
The Green Deal is affecting all major legislative frameworks and therefore the PVC profile 
system providers within scope of these frameworks. We therefore call on the Commission 
to bring in line sustainability requirements for our products as (to be) covered in: the 
EPBD, the Construction Products Regulation, the Ecodesign Directive. Concretely, we call 
on the EU legislator to: 
 Remove requirements or references to fire safety from the EPBD but to keep them 
in the framework of the Construction Products Regulation (basic work requirement 
2)  
 Any product (“building element”) related sustainability requirements from the EPBD 
must refer to the applicable product legislation. Depending on the on-going 
legislative initiatives, we understand that for windows this may be the Construction 
Products Regulation (CPR) or the Ecodesign Directive (Sustainable Products 
Initiative – SPI). This is particularly important in the context of Article 5 & 8 where 
Member States are asked to “take necessary measures” and Article 6 where the 
Commission is to adopt delegated acts “on a methodology framework”. It is 
important to see energy-efficiency assessment in the wider context of sustainability 
performance. Accordingly, requirements on energy-efficiency must be set in line 
with existing sustainability assessments and requirements as they will be set in by 
the CPR and SPI in the future. 
 
 
2. Consider entire life cycle performance for new buildings: Level(s) 
The methodological framework level(s) has been developed and tested over the past years 
and now reached a maturity that warrants market implementation across Europe. We 
welcome the introduction of the level(s) methodology in the context of Global Warming 
Potential calculation in the context of article 7. However, we call for making the use of 
level(s) with regard to all Level(s) LCA indicators and for the assessment of all new 
buildings, ideally before 2030.

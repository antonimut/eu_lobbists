Warsaw, 31st March 2022 
PGNiG POSITION ON THE DRAFT REVISION OF THE ENERGY PERFORMANCE OF BUILDINGS 
DIRECTIVE (COM(2021 802 final) 
 
PGNiG welcomes the opportunity to comment on the draft revision of the Energy Performance of 
Buildings Directive (EPBD). 
 
Increasing the energy performance  of buildings is one of  key challenges in the context of the 
implementation of the EU "Fit for 55" package allowing to achieve decarbonization goals for 2030 and 
2050. According to estimates of the European Commission  (EC), buildings are responsible for 40 
percent of the energy consumed in the European Union (EU) and 36 percent of GHG emissions, while 
heating, cooling and hot water are responsible for 80 percent of the energy consumed by households. 
At the same time, about 75 percent of buildings in the EU are currently not energy efficient. 
 
GENERAL COMMENTS 
 Reducing emissions in buildings should be carried out in the most cost -effective and technically 
feasible way possible, without excessive socio-economic burden. It should be ensured that the 
process of buildings’ renovation takes into account the needs and interests of groups vulnerable 
to energy poverty, each Member State’s  (MS) development level as well as existing 
infrastructure. This is even more important as the EU economy still struggles with the effects of 
the COVID-19 pandemic, just aggravated by turmoil resulting from Russian invasion of Ukraine.  
 Well-developed and widely available solutions, such as natural gas from credible sources should 
be considered as key in the transition to a more efficient and low -carbon building stock in the 
EU. In the environment of tight energy supplies,  natural gas has an essential role in the energy 
mix as a flexible and non -intermittent source of energy supplying continuously and  reliably 
households, industry  and the power generation.  Member States should be able to take 
advantage of the opportunities for green transition offered by this low emission resource. 
 National policy frameworks should enable Member States to plan renovations of their building 
infrastructure according to their specific needs and in line with the overall climate neutrality 
objective. Given the varying nature of obstacles across Member States, a largely one-size-fits-all 
approach based solely on renewable energy  sources would be too prescriptive.  Even if zero 
emission solutions are binding for 2050, MS should be free to implement appropriate measures 
to reach this goal, taking into account their starting points and local conditions. 
 
1. PHASE-OUT OF FOSSIL FUELS BY 2040 [Annex II, point c, subpoint f]  
 The draft revised Directive obliges Member States to prepare indicative National Building 
Renovation Plans from 2025 and update them every 5 years. They have to include roadmaps with 
proposed caps on the emission from the use of heating fuels in the building sector, necessary to 
achieve the EU's plan for net zero greenhouse gas emissions by 2050. 
 Under the draft Directive, Member States may only support the installation of fossil-fuel boilers 
by 2027, until the end of the current 2021-2027 Multiannual Financial Framework (MFF).

 Annex II ( point c, subpoint f ) of the discussed draft mentions decarbonisation of heating and 
cooling, including through district heating and cooling networks, and the phase out of fossil fuels 
in heating and cooling with a view to a complete phase-out by 2040 at the latest.  
 The goal of phasing out of fossil fuels f rom heating by 2040 does not  fit binding targets for 
effective district heating modernisation schedule provided in  the already over-restrictive draft 
revision of Energy Efficiency Directive  (EED) (COM/2021/558 final)1 and can jeopardise district 
heating modernisation plans both at MS and company level. Shortening by as much as 10 years 
the possibility  to use fossil fuels in heating and cooling systems including high -efficiency 
cogeneration signi ficantly undermines investments  incentives for transformation of existing 
heating systems, worsening especially the situation of MS which currently have well-developed 
heating networks in large cities.  
 Given that reaching net-zero emissions by 2050 is the goal for heating sector as well, each MS 
should individually set its own path towards attaining this objective, choosing measures which fit 
best its conditions. Gas is a low emission resource serving to achieve climate targets  and can 
efficiently and temporarily replace more emitting fuels like coal. As the draft of RED III Directive 
(COM/2021/557 final) sets goals for increasing share of renewable energy in heating and the EED 
draft already sets goals for development of district heating systems , there is no justification for 
phasing out natural gas from heating by 2040. 
 The strategic document “Energy Policy of Poland until 2040” (PEP 2040) assumes increased use 
of natural gas allowing to significantly reduce emissions in the Polish economy. In September 
2018 Polish government launched “Clean Air” program. It financially supports installation of new 
boilers and aims to reduce  emissions resulting from heating single -family houses  with poor 
quality fuel in outdated stoves. This program, supported by the European Commission as part of 
the “Catching Up Regions” in itiative, will last until 2027. By the end of November 2021,  
approximately 45 percent of newly installed boilers were gas-fired, in line with the assumptions 
of PEP 2040. Consequently, a great chunk of new gas boilers will be installed in 20’s and they will 
run on natural gas. The average lifespan of new boilers is around 15-20 years, therefore forcing 
households to  phase out all  operational boilers  until 2040  would not only entail significant 
financial costs for households but also generate environmental costs contrary to the assumptions 
of a "circular economy”.  
 In view of the argumentation above it is reasonable to  delete 2040 as a deadline for fossil fuels 
phase out in heating and cooling as it is not cost and environmentally effective . The binding EU 
net-zero emission goal is to be reached until  2050 and Member States should be able to 
implement most cost-effective and technically means to achieve the EU level ambition.  
 
2. ZERO EMISSION BUILDINGS [Annex III] and EFFICIENT DISTRICT HEATING SYSTEMS 
 
 The definition of a zero -emission building in the Annex III of  revised EPBD requires for all new 
public buildings by 2027 and all other new buildings by 2030 to be powered solely from RES or 
waste energy.

 Such definition  and dates stand  in contradiction with  the revised EED as they constitute  a 
significant limitation in relation to its Art. 24 which temporarily allows for use of various sources 
of heat, not only from RES. If this approach were to be adopted, it would significantly limit the 
development of district heating which is one of the most cost-effective method of heat supply.  
 Proposed definition of zero emission building should not eliminate possibilities  to provide 
heating, especially in district heating with technologies other that RES and waste heat. Natural 
gas with CCUS and nuclear energy should be also eligible as they could serve as non GHG emitting 
solutions. Furthermore, classification of RES has significant limitations as it might be also contrary 
to some other drafts of EU legislation such as complementary Delegated Act of the EU Taxonomy 
covering gas and nuclear activities. 
 Amendments that would be introduced in the legislative process to EED and EPBD draft need not 
only to provide for natural gas to fulfil its role of transitional fuel, but also be coherent between 
the two acts. Energy efficiency requirements regarding heat supply should be set out consistently 
across all acts of Fit for 55 Package and they should take into account the importance of providing 
uninterrupted heat supply to end customers. More realistic timeframe for increasing the RES 
share in district heating is needed  and r equirements for both zero -emission and near -zero-
energy buildings should enable the use of efficient district heating systems. 
 In order to sustain incentives for the development of different types of technologies and at the 
same time provide more flexibility for utilities to decarbonise, requirements in the draft Directive 
(particularly in the definition of a zero -emission building and  clarifying annexes) should  not 
explicitly settle on the source of energy but instead refer to requirements related to greenhouse 
gas emissions. Such a solution would provide a coherent and holistic approach to sectoral 
changes, emissions reductions and energy efficiency improvements.  
 
3. IMPACT ASSESMENT AND FINANCING [Art. 15] 
 
 According to the draft Directiv e, Member States can finance renovations  from numerous 
instruments such as ETS auctions, MFF, Social Climate Fund, Invest EU  and National Recovery 
Plans. 
 In spite of funding listed above,  the EC does not estimate the tota l costs regarding  
implementation of new regulations.  In fact, it is impossible to carry out  such calculations as 
Member States have to classify buildings according to their energy performance  until 2025. 
Only then it might be possible to prepare more precise estimates of the proposed legislation. 
Without them, it is impossible to determine the cost and real scale of needed renovations. As 
a consequence, the Commission comes up with a proposal  having far reaching implications, 
without providing sufficient financial impact assessment for it. 
 As no dedicated financing  and no more detailed cost calculation  is provided under the  draft 
Directive, it is essential to monitor whether the framework and tools included in the  draft 
Directive are sufficient , especially for individual cu stomers. For them, the cost  of investment 
with return expected only in a very long run is particularly elevated. At present, EU funding and 
grants can usually only be paid out once the renovation is completed, making it difficult for 
consumers (especially the less well-off) to launch it. It would be worth considering to provide 
at least part of funding upfront , so that funding  would be more available , nudging  more

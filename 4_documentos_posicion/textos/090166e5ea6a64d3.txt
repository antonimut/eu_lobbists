An increase in operational and housing costs could 
lead to a worsening of the housing affordability crisis 
across the EU, especially for citizens looking to 
access affordable decent, energy-efficient, and 
environmentally friendly  housing (such as NZEB) . 
Housebuilders could also face unfair competition 
with the existing housing stock, as renovation will 
face a limited increase of energy -efficiency 
requirements (until 2027-2030). 
Furthermore, there is no logical nor proven causality 
between high minimum requirements for energy 
performance and the reduction of energy costs. 
Consumption savings do not necessarily lead to 
lower costs . On the contrary, raising the minimum 
standards for new buildings and for existing 
buildings could jeopardise the successful and

According to cost-optimality principles, energy 
savings must be able to be refinanced from the 
energy costs saved, and the selected specifications 
must ensure that the greatest savings potential is 
achieved at the lowest cost for the household.

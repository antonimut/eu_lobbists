The European Commission’s proposed Fit -for-55 package has set an ambitious greenhouse gas emissions  
reduction target for 2030 to achieve climate neutrality by 2050.  
 
Improving the energy efficiency of the building sector will be key to achieving these objectives.  Buildings 
represent roughly 40% of total greenhouse gas emissions, with heating and cooling accounting for 80% of 
the energy used in households.  
 
In this context, we welcome the revision of the Energy Performance of Buildings Directive (EPBD) , as an 
opportunity to accelerate progress in this  sector, which would also generate significant cost savings, 
improved well-being and indoor comfort, as well as better air quality in cities.    
 
To unlock the significant energy saving potential of both new and existing buildings, we need an even more 
ambitious, legislative framework that incentivizes and, where appropriate, mandates the uptake of energy 
efficiency measures and solutions. 
 
Three quarters of the aforementioned figure concerning heating and cooling in buildings is still supplied 
from fossil fuels. This means that increasing the ambition for decarbonizing the building stock with efficient 
and renewable heating and cooling solutions such as heat pumps, district energy and building automation 
and controls that optimize the performance of the systems in buildings will have to sit right at the heart of 
the recast. 
 
Danfoss stands ready to support and engage in discussions over the coming months to ensure the successful 
adoption of an ambitious EPBD.

Detailed comments 
 
Article 2: ZEB: Introduce carbon metrics accelerates decarbonization 
 
• Danfoss supports the introduction of Zero Emission Building (ZEB) as a mandatory requirement for 
new buildings and deep renovation and supports the logic that the very low amount of energy needed 
is provided by renewables and/or any available district energy network, including those based on 
waste heat. BACS represent the best technical building system to integrate such different sources. 
Both primary and final energy c onsumption indicators should be used in the ZEB definition to 
adequately consider how to reduce energy needs when comparing different solutions.  
 
Article 3: Building Renovation Plans are crucial to make change happen on the ground. They 
need to be harmonized, transparent and comparable at EU level 
 
• Roadmaps should include targets for 2030, 2040 and 2050 renovation rate, both primary and final 
energy consumption of the building stock and clear greenhouse gas emission reduction targets. MEPS 
should be in line with the above targets. All information collected and exchanged should be available 
and comparable at local and European level via a uniform approach. 
• More ambitious targets should be set for short term (2027-2030). Quicker energy independence has 
to be achieved without further delay. All buildings should be involved with  a primary role for public 
buildings. 
 
Article 7 - 8: Introduce staged renovation as holistic approach to increase building energy 
efficiency 
 
• New buildings: Danfoss supports the introduction of the life cycle Global Warming Potential with the 
EPC of the buildings and welcomes the pioneer role of public buildings in meeting ZEB requirements. 
• Renovation:  Danfoss strongly supports and encourages minimum energy performance requirements 
on buildings or building units undergoing renovation. Danfoss welcomes the Renovation passport 
scheme with clear renovation steps to eventually turn the building into ZEB. Such a staged approach 
• Reinforce the mandatory requirement on BACS in all types of buildings with explicit 
mention of room controls (TRVs and ERCs ) as simple, effective and easy to implement BACS, 
as recommended by IEA in its recent proposal on energy independence. 
• Ensure mandatory requirements on hydronic balancing on all building types to manage 
part load and variable energy flows to adapt changing needs. Ensuring optimal energy performance 
at all heat load conditions is essential to ensure the least energy use and operating costs. Balancing 
will also deliver best ‘low temperature heating’ which is a must for elevated heat pump performance 
(COP). 
• Introduce carbon metrics to ensure real effect on climate goals and properly compare different 
technologies and technical solutions, i.e. electrification of heating. 
• Extend the requirements on digital solutions , Buildings need ongoing intelligence, delivered 
digitally through mo dern technologies already available on the market such as BACS. Energy 
Performance Inspections should be encouraged to be conducted using digital tools. 
• Increase ambition on MEPS, EPCs to fully tap into the potential of energy efficiency and ensure 
renovation rate meets the expected speed. The energy performance of an existing building shall 
always be determined based on metered energy use. The metered final energy consumption metrics 
would indicate the extent to which the Energy Efficiency First principle is applied.   
• Ensure the energy saving potential for all complementary technologies, such as variable 
speed drives, are properly accounted for in the EPBD.

Article 5 - 9: Minimum Efficiency Performance Standards are an essential tool to speed up the 
renovation rate but need to be further strengthened 
 
• MEPS could be strengthened. The yearly energy renovation rate in the European Union remains well 
under 1% of the total building stock. In this context the introduction of mandatory Minimum Energy 
Performance Standards (MEPS) can speed up the annual energy renovation rate. MEPS together with 
the mapping and roadmaps provided by national Long Term Renovation Strategies will enable 
monitoring of measures effectiveness, integration capability of renewables and demand side 
flexibility.  
• Holistic deep renovations (one shot or staged) need to be enshrined in a more ambitious and forward 
looking way. 
 
Article 11: Introduce the mandatory and systematic installation of self-regulating devices and 
hydronic balancing  
 
• As confirmed by the suggestions provided by the Eur opean Commission in its guidelines to the 
current EPBD in force, in new buildings the installation of self -regulating devices should always be 
considered as starting point, both from a technical and economical perspective.  Self-regulating 
devices are fast payback investments (1-3 years) with a low upfront cost (1.5 EUR/m2) which can 
considerably improve the health and comfort of the occupants and tackle energy poverty. In line 
with the increased ambition of this revision and taking into account the benefits  that these devices 
can deliver at such a low cost, their installation should be systematically required in all existing 
buildings when heat and cold generators are replaced and at the latest by 2027.  
• Introduce a new requirement on dynamic balancing under art.11 for new buildings and existing 
buildings when replacing heat or cold generators. Despite it being a capital -light investment with a 
short pay-back time, system balancing is often neglected in renovations. The total potential savings 
from optimizing hydronic distribution in domestic EU heating systems would amount to 2 2.6 Mtoe. 
Hydronic imbalances and the lack of individual room temperature controls in buildings are the main 
causes for energy waste, unnecessary heating/cooling costs, occupant complai nts, and the 
performance gap between expected and actual energy consumption after renovation. Ensuring 
optimal energy performance at all heat load conditions is essential to provide comfort for occupants 
with the least energy use and operating costs. Balan cing will ensure best ‘low temperature heating’ 
which is a must for elevated heat pump performance (COP). 
• IEQ (indoor Environmental quality) should be promoted at unit level, not only building level. Danfoss 
supports monitoring of Indoor Environmental Qual ity that should start with a minimum set of 
meaningful parameters. Such parameters should be made visible to building occupants to inform 
them about deviations between actual and target values. This would incentivise intervention and 
optimization. Each relevant unit (dwelling or independent portion) of the building should be able to 
measure the IEQ, in order to properly identify failures or localize inefficiencies and enable an easy 
follow up. 
 
Article 13: Include the SRI in EPCs 
 
• The SRI should be directly linked to EPCs and rely on the same database: the harmonized common 
EU scheme for Smart Readiness Indicator is of crucial importance during the design, construction, 
operation and maintenance of buildings across their whole life. The Smart Readiness Indicator (SRI) 
will drive improvements across three key smart functionalities in the context of buildings: 
responsiveness to the needs of the building occupants, predictive maintenance capabilities, and grid-
flexibility.

• The use of the SRI will enable end user s to understand the direct link between active technical 
systems and the energy performance of the building in its environment, the city and the grid. Danfoss 
welcomes the boost that this measure will give to the deployment of smart Technical Building 
Systems (TBS), particularly heating, ventilation, and air -conditioning (HVAC) systems and Building

Article 14: Make Information and data visible to all relevant stakeholders  
 
• We recommend that the roles and rules for data sharing should be clearly defined according to the EU 
strategy for data. 
• Danfoss believes that all stakeholders involved in the energy renovation value chain should have direct 
and easy access to the building systems’ relevant data, ideally in digital format Member States should 
be encouraged to use and promote building automation and control systems, smart meters etc. to 
support the usage of real -time metered data when assessing the energy performance of a building.  
 
Article 15: Ensure that financial measures for energy performance improvements are linked to 
the targeted and achieved energy savings. 
 
• Danfoss supports comparing achievements with targets when renovating. Design performance often 
does not match real performance. BACS enable the measuring of savings and real consumption before 
and after each stage of renovation and can deliver such informa tion in a digitalised way. This fits very 
well with Eco-design measures proposed for Heat pumps relating to real condition performance  
 
Article 16: Danfoss supports EPCs as the most suitable instrument to verify BACS requirements 
in buildings 
 
• Include in EPCs the control and verification of mandatory technical building systems. Danfoss welcomes 
the introduction of a harmonised European template, to ensure comparability between member states. 
The same is true for the harmonization of energy classes. Such a uniform framework should support the 
renovation process especially in light of specific triggers that will require issuing an EPC: construction, 
renovation, sale and all rent events. For the above reasons EPCs are a perfect instrument to validate 
compliance with mandatory requirements.   
• New metrics should be integrated into the Energy Performance Certificates to trigger buildings 
decarbonisation beyond energy performance only, alongside a digitalisation of EPCs to make them more 
dynamic and informative. 
 
Article 20: Move BACS requirements to art. 11. 
 
• The paragraph related to BACS should be moved to the section on Technical Building Systems. Danfoss 
welcomes the inclusion of inspections of ventilation and cooling systems and the additional part on 
recommendations of improvements to be included into the report.  However, the article on inspections 
is also describing BACS requirements. The requirements for buildings to be equipped with BACS 
capabilities are currently placed in Article 20. Danfoss believes that  this framing is leading to 
misunderstandings as the requirement is not directly related to inspections per se. It should therefore 
be moved to the section on “Technical Building Systems”.  
• For residential buildings Danfoss recommends distinguishing betwee n requirements for small and 
requirement for large buildings with a threshold of 70 kW. 
• Danfoss recommends a stronger focus on demand side flexibility. Demand -side flexibility must be 
harnessed to ensure the smooth integration of large shares of variable r enewable energy and at the 
same time minimizes the investments in the energy infrastructure

Call for evidence for an impact assessment on ‘Right to Repair’ – EVA Comments 
 
Dear EU Commission, 
The European Vending & Coffee Service Association (EVA) is the trade body for the unattended 
retail sector covering vending and office coffee provision. There are 4.4 million vending machines 
in Europe, with annual revenue of €12.3 billion, significantly reduced from a peak of €16 billion as 
a direct result of the pandemic.  The EVA counts 12 National vending association s and over 100 
member companies, including the vast majority of vending manufacturers in Europe. 
The EVA would like to state its support for the overall goals of any initiative that can help reduce 
unsustainable consumption, build a circular economy and ensure goods are designed to last as long 
as possible. We note likewise that the circular economy action plan aims at promoting repair and 
encouraging more sustainable products.   
We would like to make the following comment s in relation the call for evidence  for an impact 
assessment on the initiative.  
Problem the initiative aims to tackle 
First of all, t he EVA would  like to  refute the blanket statement that businesses have limited 
economic interest to produce or supply more environmentally sustainable goods.  In our view, this 
is a statement which would not be applicable with relation to vending machine manufacturers, who 
are required as a result of a long -established business model, to supply parts and modular 
components to their professional customers (i.e. the vending machine operators) and ensure their 
products can have their lifespan extended. 
In addition, a number of vending machine manufacturers are indeed specialists in refurbishing of 
older machines and these refurbished machines play a n important role in the machines placed in 
the market every year. 
Who should have the right to repair?  
The call for evidence talks frequently about the ‘consumers’ right to repair’ but yet does not define 
who the consumer is. This is a crucial point for the EVA as the customer in vending is not the final 
consumer but rather the machine operator. In the vending industry, machines are already designed 
on a modular bas is, allowing many components – including the cooling unit and stacks – to be 
easily replaced and changed when needed. This significantly prolongs the lifespan of the machines 
and has been standard industry practice for many years. These updates, repairs an d reuse are 
undertaken by the professional vending operator who is not the final ‘consumer’ and indeed the 
final consumer would have no interest nor ability to influence repairs on a vending machine. 
Any potential initiative should provide clarity as to whether any potential right to repair would apply 
to professional B2B products or if it would be intended for only B2C products.  The EVA believes it 
should only apply to the later.

However, there is sometimes a ‘gray area’ between a professional coffee/vendi ng machine and a 
domestic coffee machine, depending on where it is used. For example, on occasion a domestic -
targeted coffee machine may be bought for use in a professional environment, such as an office, 
where professional vending and coffee machines are usually located. Therefore, if a right to repair 
would be introduced for small household appliances for example, it is possible that this could  also 
impact coffee machines in a professional environment. Further to this, some machine 
manufacturers produce also smaller machines for the office as well as bigger free standing vending 
machines. We acknowledge the difficulties this situation implies, but this reality should nevertheless 
be understood when determining the product categories impacted by a right to repair.   
Interaction with other legislation 
We note in the call for evidence that t he approach taken by the  right to repair initiative will also 
seek to achieve synergies with other ongoing Ecodesign initiatives.  The EVA would query how 
exactly a potential right of repair would interact, overlap or duplicate certain measures from other 
pieces of legislation, and would therefore ask the EU Commission to ensure that a right to repair is 
absolutely coherent with other legislation and in particular the Ecodesign Directive. 
Since March 2021, Ecodesign and Energy Labelling legislation has applied to refrigerated vending 
machines. In EU 2019/2024 there are already a series of requirements on ease of repair and end-
of-life measures for these machines. The availability and accessibility of spare parts – along with a 
specific timeframe to make these available – as well as detailed information on repairs /parts for 
professional repairers are all required to be provided. In the EVA’s view, these requirements help 
ensure repair and reusability are easily available but we struggle to see what additional elements 
would improve these aspects in our industry. Indeed, further requirements  through a ‘right to 
repair’ could actually hinder the business model in vending as a result of a cumbersome and 
bureaucratic system which may do little to achieve its objectives in reality, which includes ensuring 
repairing a product can be the cost-effective option. 
  
Of course, we are available and are happy to discuss any aspects of t he letter further and thank 
you for your consideration of them.

ETUC reply to the EC consultation on the proposal for a Regulation amending Regulation 
(EU) No 1025/2012  
 
The ETUC does not question the European standardisation system as governed by Regulation 
(EU) No 1025/2012, nor the process by which the European Commission, in line with specific 
procedures, can mandate the development of standards and standardisation del iverables to 
support Union legislation and policies.   
The ETUC further agrees that when the European standardisation organisations, as defined in 
Article 2 of the Standardisation Regulation, develop standards to support Union legislation and 
policies, their internal governance must take into account the views and interests of all European 
stakeholders.  
The ETUC therefore welcomes that the proposed amendment to the Regulation aims to safeguard 
good governance principles in the delivery of harmonised standards, and to strengthen the role of 
National standardisation bodies within the system.   
However, the ETUC stresses the need to ensure a balanced representation of interests within the 
National standardisation bodies. It is not enough to limit the decision-making power to NSBs if the 
objective is to ensure sufficient checks and balances. National standardisation bodies must 
facilitate the appropriate representation of all national interests, such as worker representatives or 
trade unions when the standards have an impact on workers and workers’ conditions. National 
delegations from the European standardisation bodies cannot only represent industrial interests if 
the standards are going to be used to show compliance with rules imposed in the wider public 
interest.  
The ETUC proposes the European Commission, Parliament and Council: 
• To complement the wording of the new paragraph 2a of article 10 of EU Regulation 
1025/2012 as follows:  
2a. Each European standardisation organisation shall ensure that the following decisions 
concerning European standards and European standardisation deli verables referred to in 
paragraph 1 are taken exclusively by representatives of the national standardisation bodies, which 
include a balanced representation of all stakeholders that have an interest , notably trade 
unions, within the competent decision-making body of that organisation:

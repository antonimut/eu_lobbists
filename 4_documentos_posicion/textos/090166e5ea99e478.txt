Response to the public consultation on the European Standardisation Strategy  We thank the commission for being able to respond to the public consultation on the European standards strategy and wish to submit the following contribution. The Internet Society works to keep the Internet a force for good: open, globally connected, secure, and trustworthy. We send our contribution to this public consultation because of our association with the Internet Engineering Task Force (IETF), the leading Internet standards body. Composed by a large, open, and international community of network designers, operators, vendors, and researchers, the IETF focuses on the evolution of the Internet architecture and the smooth operation of the Internet. It develops open standards through open processes with one goal in mind: to make the Internet work better. The Internet Society supports the work of the IETF through a variety of programs, and provides a corporate home for the IETF LLC, its administrative entity.  Global Open standards are a cornerstone of the Internet. They are key to allowing devices, services, and applications to interoperate across a wide and dispersed network of networks. In addition to the standards themselves, the open standard development processes and principles on which it is based, ensure the evolution of Internet technologies that meet the need of the growing number of devices and uses that empower people around the world. IETF work focuses on developing and maintaining open standards and key protocols for data communications, routing, security and network operations and management, aiming at making the Internet an infrastructure that works better for everybody. It is relevant to add that these open standards are available at no charge, are chosen according to their technical merits of the proposals and are deployed in a voluntary manner. The fact that these standards are open, is at the core of the success of the Internet we can enjoy today. Also, it is important to highlight that this standard setting process is transparent, all drafts, reviews and outcomes are publicly available. Finally, another factor that adds to the openness of the IETF is that everyone is invited to participate in the process, with no membership requirements.  The IETF is a prime example of a model of standardisation that has the widest possible global impact.

The Internet Society notes that the Communication COM(2022)31 from last February 2nd, mentions explicitly the importance of global standards-setting in section IV. With the objective of ensuring "EU’s global competitiveness, security and open strategic autonomy, as well as the ability of the EU to promote its values" the Commission calls the EU and its Member States to "promote a more strategic approach to international standardisation activities", including the IETF. Also, the Communication declares that "international standardisation on internet protocols has become increasingly politicised, at the risk of limiting the evolution of the global open internet and hampering the digitisation process across the world." We recognise that the coordination role of the EU excellence hub on standards can help to raise awareness among Europeans and increase relevant European participation in global fora and consortia. But it is important to avoid political interference in processes that must be led by technical expertise rather than decision-making power. The development of open standards in the IETF and similar global, open standards organisations is based on a set of guiding principles: development through due process, broad consensus, technical merit, transparent operation, balanced input, and open participation. Impeding any of these principles would jeopardize ability to identify the best solutions in a global context, lead to fragmentation, and result in only a few companies being able to serve the fragmented market. In this way a tighter control of the Internet and Internet protocol standardisation can endanger the Internet and the values it is built on. We are concerned that some aspects of the strategy encourage the pursuit of political goals through standards setting in a way that displaces the importance of technical goals and merit. Specifically, the strategy allows that the Commission could be empowered to develop common specifications where harmonised standards are absent and insufficient.  The Commission should be careful not to position itself as the provider of technical judgement. Because global interoperability and voluntary adoption is crucial for the Internet Standard, we call the Commission to explicitly carve out Internet Standard making processes from this proposal. Otherwise, other governments in the world may find this argument as excuse to take similar action, with unwanted consequences.  We believe that regional or even national fragmentation of ICT related standardisation is in contrast with the global and open nature of the Internet and we urge the commission to focus on active participation in global fora and consortia and the recognition of specifications like IETF RFC instead of pushing decision power to the national standards bodies.  We also call the Commission to carefully monitor the excellence hub to prevent that it evolves into a de-facto SDO where specifications are developed.

Global standards development remains in need of further diversification, therefore we applaud the initiative to fund standardisation projects in selected African countries. Our final comment is to support the European Commission efforts to "foster a free, open, accessible and secure global Internet". These attributes complement and match those supported by the Internet Society worldwide and our vision of an Internet that is for everyone.

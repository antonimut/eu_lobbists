 Ref. Ares (2022)175084 - 11/01/2022  
FFE comments: Sustainable consumption of goods - promoting repair 
and reuse  
European Commission has published call for evidence for an impact as-
sessment (Ref. Ares (2022)175084 - 11/01/2022). The initiative promotes a 
more sustainable use of goods throughout their useful life. The aim is to en-
courage consumers to make more sustainable choices by providing incen-
tives and tools to use goods for a longer time, including by repairing defec-
tive goods. The proposal on the right to repair is planned for third quarter 
2022. 
The FFE would like to express its views regarding to the right to repair initi-
ative.  
 
General 
The promotion of the circular economy and sustainable consumption are 
important goals. Properly implemented, the promotion of the reuse and the 
right to repair can also create new business opportunities for SMEs, for ex-
ample as providers of repair services. As demand grows it is likely that new 
players will emerge to the market, and this will increase options and lower 
repair costs for consumers. 
Promoting sustainable consumption requires measures to encourage con-
sumers to repair goods but also measures to encourage companies to pro-
vide repair services and to sell second-hand/refurbished goods. In this re-
spect, it should be noted that an unreasonable increase in the obligations of 
companies may in fact hamper activities that are important for the circular 
economy. Heavy obligations do not encourage companies to invest in these 
functions. 
The Sale of Goods Directive has only become applicable in early 2022 and 
therefore it would be unreasonable to further tighten the requirements on 
companies within very short term. Instead, it is justified to view and modify 
the Sale of Goods Directive in a way that improves the goal of sustainable

Policy options 
 
Options for extending legal guarantee period 
Regarding the options that would extend the legal guarantee period (Sub 
option 2A and Sub option 3B), it should be noted that the duration of the 
legal guarantee period has not been harmonized in the EU. Therefore, op-
tions that would extend the duration of legal guarantee period are not feasi-
ble. 
The Sale of Goods Directive requires a legal guarantee period of at least 
two years, but the Directive has also allowed Member States to provide for 
a longer guarantee period. In Finland, for example, there is no explicit time 
limit for the legal guarantee period. The legal guarantee period is deter-
mined by the expected period of durability for the good and is ultimately lim-
ited by the general limitation period. The options of extending the legal 
guarantee period are not suitable for such regulatory regimes where the 
specific duration of the legal guarantee period has not been defined. 
We consider changes to the legal guarantee period to be appropriate only if 
it aims to harmonize regulatory framework for legal guarantee period in the 
EU. The harmonization would remove the administrative burden on compa-
nies that offer their goods in several Member States and currently have to 
take into account the differences in legislation. 
The consumer's right to choose between repair and replacement must be restricted 
 
We consider that the best approach is to limit the consumer’s right to 
choose the way that the remedy is fulfilled (Sub option 3A). However, it 
should be noted that repair of the product is not always possible or may be 
prohibitively expensive. Therefore, the obligation to prioritize repairment in 
every situation is too inflexible. 
We also consider appropriate to take measures to make the repair of goods 
a more popular option (Sub Option 2B, first option) in situations where re-
pair is cheaper or at the same cost as replacement.

The seller usually has the best expertise of the goods and in which cases 
repairing the goods or alternatively replacement of the goods is more sensi-
ble considering the costs and environmental aspects. It would be justified to 
restrict the consumer's right to choose between repair and replacement to 
promote sustainability objectives. 
Challenges to the achievement of the objectives of circular economy are 
caused by the fact that the consumer, instead of the seller, has the right to 
choose whether to repair or replace the defective product. It is likely that 
many consumers choose to replace the defective product instead of repair-
ing it, as this is often more effortless and faster. Consumer’s right to choose 
the way of the remedy does not in fact provide an incentive to repair goods. 
We support the option that would allow the seller to replace defective prod-
ucts with refurbished goods instead of new goods (Sub option 3C). Espe-
cially in situations where the product is replaced after a long period of use, 
it is unreasonable to demand the seller to provide completely new product.  
Obligations on companies should not be increased 
It is important to recognize that progress for circular economy cannot be 
achieved by increasing the responsibilities of companies alone. The invest-
ments to repair/ refurbished services requires that these activities are profit-
able business also from the point of view of entrepreneurs. An unreasona-
ble increase in the regulatory burden may hamper investments to these im-
portant functions. 
New possible measures that would be introduced to promote sustainable 
consumption must be carefully considered to ensure that the burden on 
SMEs does not become disproportionately heavy. It is important to maintain 
a balance between consumer rights and companies’ responsibilities and 
focus on finding solutions that do not increase the burden on SMEs. 
We find that the option where producers or sellers would be obliged to re-
pair products at a reasonable price after the legal guarantee period (second 
option in Sub Option 2B) and option where producers or sellers would be 
obliged to repair products even free of charge after the legal guarantee pe-
riod (second option in Sub Option 3A) to be particularly problematic. These 
options would be unreasonable for producers and sellers of goods, espe-
cially for SMEs. 
The legal certainty of sellers and producers requires that they will not be 
subject to claims after the legal guarantee period. If producers and sellers 
of goods were obliged to provide repair services indefinitely after the end of 
the legal guarantee period, they would not be able to anticipate or prepare

for the consumer claims. Such obligations would significantly increase the 
risks of sellers and producers. 
Unexpected claims imposed on sellers and producers would force them to 
include the increased risk in the prices of consumer products. An increase 
in the price of consumer products would be very likely, especially if produc-
ers or sellers would be obliged to provide repair services free of charge af-
ter the end of the legal guarantee period. 
Even if a product is designed to be durable and long-lasting, it should be 
recognized that products also have a certain life cycle. The indefinitely long 
right of repair the product does not recognize the limitations of the real 
world, where in some cases repair is simply no longer possible option.

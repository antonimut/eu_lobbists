Sustainable consumption of goods – promoting repair and reuse  
(EC Consultation) 
 
We, Sony Interactive Entertainment (SIE) would like to share our position on the upcoming   legislative 
proposal for an amendment of the Sale of Goods Directive and possibly a separate new legislative 
proposal on the right to repair. 
Ensuring access to repair of games consoles has been a commitment for the video games sector for 
many years. In cooperation with the European Commission, the console manufacturers signed the 
Games Consoles Voluntary Agreement  (GCVA) which includes a requirement to provide an out -of-
warranty repair service to consumers.   
Sony Interactive Entertainment – Ensuring Access to Repair 
For over 2 5 years Sony Interactive Entertainment has provided affordable repair and reuse of 
PlayStation consoles outside of the manufacturer’s warranty period in the European Union, avoiding 
the creation of electronic waste and unnecessary consumption of new materials.  
To return a console for repair consumers can take their items back to the point-of-sale across the EU 
i.e. the retailer, or alternatively, can use a website or phone a dedicated call centre to troubleshoot 
their issue and organise the product to be collected for repair if needed.  
SIE partners with a specialist fourth party logistics (4PL) company (Cycleon B.V.) who manage and 
organise local carriers to collect consoles from retailers and customers to be taken to dedicated service 
centres. As of 2022, SIE have taken part in Cycleon’s ‘Return to Zero’ programme to offset the carbon 
footprint of reverse logistics.  
For a return within the manufacturer's warranty period , consumers are in some cases  offered a 
replacement at the point-of-sale to ensure a quick resolution. The returned consoles are sent to the 
same service centres for assessment, and the skilled workforce employed at these centres carry out 
diagnostic and refurbishment operations. Refurbished products are kept for consumer replacements 
for a console beyond economic repair or for resale. Alternatively, if the console is sent directly to the 
service centre, repair is attempted on the same unit and returned to the consumer. If repair is not 
possible, the pool of refurbished stock ensures there is a resolution for every consumer.    
Any consoles beyond economic repair are harvested for reusable spare parts before sending to secure 
recycling facilities for disposal. The 4PL also organise carriers to ensure the repaired or replacement 
console is returned to the customer, via retailers and direct courier.  
SIE’s reverse logistics and repair operations are successful for several reasons. Firstly, the out -of-
warranty repair serv ice is made viable as the cost to the consumer  is less than the cost of a new 
product. Second, the complex nature of the product requires skilled technicians and access to highly 
confidential technologies, so this process is only made possible by ensuring products are repaired by 
qualified and suitably insured technicians . Third, the overall process of console repair  and

Key Considerations 
SIE can provide the following feedback on the key concerns highlighted by the European Commission’s 
Call for Evidence. 
• SIE provide an out -of-warranty repair and refurbishment service for games consoles which 
assists consumers in using their goods for as long as they could be and avoid excessive waste 
creation. 
• Although there are concerns that under the Sale of Goods Directive, consumers often choose 
a repl acement over a repair , in the case of SIE most of these returns are repaired or 
refurbished to be provided to a consumer in the future. Thus, ensuring a quick, but still 
environmentally friendly resolution for the consumer. 
• The cost of these repair and refurbishment out-of-warranty services is always less than the 
purchase of a new product which ensures repair /refurbishment is a viable option for 
consumers. 
In light of the success of the example above  on manufacturer-led repair operations  in meeting the 
objective of the initiative, which is to encourage consumers to use consumer goods for a longer time, 
SIE supports Option 1 (Low intervention – voluntary commitments) of the policy options being 
considered by the Commission.  
Alternatively, we also support Option 2B (Moderate Intervention) depending on the provisions to 
determine what constitutes a ‘reasonable’ price and time period beyond the legal guarantee period. 
In the corresponding questionnaire to this consultation, several options are presented related to the 
legal guarantee framework (Sale of Goods Directive). We support options 2 and 6, as already proven 
successful by the voluntary repair and refurbishment activities of SIE. 
In addition, SIE believe the following points should be taken into consideration whilst proposals to 
encourage the sustainable consumption of goods and promote repair and reuse, as well as to make 
products more sustainable, are developed.

As for any repair service, the introduction of a Right to Repair would raise theissue of liability. It seems fair that the liability is that of the entity performing the repair. A Right toRepair would most likely require setting legal obligations on a specific operator, which would createissues interms of theindividual liabilityof therepairingeconomic operator andpromotinganeffectiverepair market.
Looking into other aspects tackled by the consultation, applying the proposed Right to Repair tosituations wheretheconsumerisatfaultwouldbedifficulttoachieve,especiallyunderstandingwhetherthe product became defective due to mishandling. This Right to Repair couldinsteadcover situationswhere the defect comes as the result of wear and tear or a failure in the product itself or a part of it.
ThetimeframeduringwhichaRight toRepair couldbeassureddependsonthetypeofgoodsitwouldapplyto. Likewise, theRight toRepair wouldhavetoberestrictedtofunctional failuresthatrenderthegood unusable. Conversely, minor cosmetic defects ought tobeexempted. This is duetothefact thatover time the spare parts availability decreases and e.g. certain colour variations may be out of stock.
Finally, we want to stress that the promotion of repair is not a stand-alone initiative and must becarefully thought and developed in conjunction with connected policies such as product safety andenergy efficiency in the case of home appliances.

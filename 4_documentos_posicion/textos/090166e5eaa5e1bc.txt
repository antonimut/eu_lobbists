ETNO strongly welcomes the initiative of the European Commission to adopt an EU Standardisation 
Strategy. Standards are of supreme importance for the ICT sector, not only for the Single Market, but 
also – crucially – for the competitiveness of Europe in the global economy.  
 
European Standardisation Development Organisations  
European Standards Organisations (ESOs) have long played an successful and influential role in 
developing high quality standards, adopted by other regions around the world, especially ETSI, to 
which ETNO and our members participate. These achievements are due to the fact that ETSI’s technical 
work is based on a large membership, allowing a global participation (incl. non -European) and direct 
participation of industry. This specific entity for the ICT domain allows for the development of global 
standards that avoid fragmentation of the market and ensure interoperability of services for 
customers. Further, the very success of international standards depends on wide, voluntary adoption.  
 
It must also be noted that such ESOs, while successful in developing and encouraging uptake of 
standards around the world, also count among their contributors non -European members and 
participant experts and companies. To guarantee European leadership in standard-setting and in global 
digital markets, European governments, companies and academics must be supported to participate 
in, and contribute to, standard-setting processes. This could include direct promotion of experts in the 
industry through project-independent basic funding.  
 
European participation and leadership in standardisation also requires greater coordination among 
European actors within European and global SDOs, in order to optimise and maximise European 
influence. This could be a role for the European Commission. However, Commission oversight on the 
alignment of standardisation activities with EU strategic interests, and the implementing of acts under 
sectoral legislation, should not lead to a fragmentation by ignoring market deman d due to political 
objectives. Otherwise, outside of the adjusted market no provider will follow these standards. 
 
Furthermore, closer collaboration on standardisation with like-minded partners in strategic areas, and 
coordinated positions in international  standardisation bodies is welcomed. For the ICT -industry in 
particular, this could include South Korea and Japan, besides the US. 
 
Proposed reforms of ESOs and new bodies  
ETNO supports the EC proposal for a change in Article 10 of the Standardisation Regulation (Regulation 
(EU) No 1025/2012), relating to a European -based decision process in reply to EC standardisation 
requests (SR) within the 3 ESOs.  
 
Concerning the “policy” process, main decisions/votes on different steps (acceptation, approval, etc.) 
should be managed by NSOs solely, with possible advice or consultations of technical experts (e.g., 
relevant ETSI TBs and the ETSI Board) as it is important to ensure that acceptance of an SR also has the 
‘buy-in’ of experts and industry players, who would provide resources to develop the ENs. Each 
country sets up each official NSO, having in mind that all relevant stakeholders should be associated.  
This could be considered as an “inner” European process specific to the development of EN. 
 
This process should also be managed with a reasonable flexibility/dialogue between EC & ESOs, with 
the possibility to propose a modification to an SR even after its approval by the COS: such amendments 
could be achieved by asking for a second reading by the COS or by developing an iterative process or 
discussion between the EC and concerned ESOs, within a limited timeframe.

Where reforms are introduced in ESOs, these should not have the effect of discouraging or limiting the 
collaboration of non -European countries, and restricting it to institutions within their world region. 
Indeed, The planned administrative and good governance principles to limit decision-making power to 
national standardisation bodies (also in ETSI) must be limited to requests from the Commission, 
without damaging other standardisation processes. 
 
We welcome the initiative to establish a European High Level Forum and an Excellence Hub, but the 
tasks and responsibilities of t hese bodies should be clearly defined and carefully delineated from 
existing bodies: the overlap of responsibilities risks duplicating standardisation work, or new bodies 
competing with ESOs. Confusion on roles and competences would weaken the entire Europ ean 
standardisation system, at a time when it must be strengthened. 
 
Furthermore, requiring decision-making by national bodies could cause delays in the process due to 
high heterogeneity of the national institutions  particularly in certain countries  (in t erms of 
stakeholders represented, dimension, focus, etc.) not necessarily holding the right competence, and 
resources and naturally representing different positions, difficult to merge into one. 
 
ICT Standardisation 
ICT standardisation plays an important p art in the EU’s twin transition strategy: “The EU’s ambitions 
towards a climate neutral, resilient and circular economy cannot be delivered without European 
standards.” Digitalisation does not begin at the application layer, but must have a stable foundation in 
digital infrastructure. 
 
To this end, while focusing on ‘ standardisation priorities ’ outlined in the Strategy is of great 
importance for the European digital economy (Green Deal, healthcare etc.), this must not come at the 
expense of ICT-standardisation: ICTs must remain a priority of the standardisation policy. In our view, 
the primary focus of the Commission ’s communication is on traditional products, but there is no 
mention of ICTs or digital activities. Telecommunications infrastructure is a baseline technology, which 
is continuously evolving and innovating. As such, standards on essential technical interfaces should not 
be left to a few non -European stakeholders, also with regard to developing environmental 
technologies that depend on ICT. The str ategic importance of the ICT sector should also be reflected 
in the opportunity including ICT stakeholders representatives in the High Level Forum and the 
Excellency Hub: this could include a body specifically dealing with ICT standardisation, e.g. at the level 
of CTO, to advise the High Level Forum. For this reason, we also support the continuation of the 
Commission’s Multistakeholder Platform for ICT Standardisation, as an important forum for exchange, 
and in an advisory capacity to the High Level Forum. 
 
The Commission ’s proposals, specifically on the Commission oversight on the alignment of 
standardisation activities with EU policy objectives and strategic interests should not lead to delays 
that risk undermining the competitiveness of the European market and its industry.

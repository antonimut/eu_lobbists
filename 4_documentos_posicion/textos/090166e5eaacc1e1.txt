BDI Statement Regulation (EU) No 1025/2012 
 
 
 
 
 
European Standardisation System internationally competitive 
and appreciated by industry  
 
Within the framework of the Standardisation Strategy presented by the 
European Commission, the publication of a proposal to amend Regulation 
(EU) No. 1025/2012 was announced.  
In the Commission’s view, the special status accorded to the European 
Standardisation Organisations (ESOs) must be based on a national delegation 
principle, which the Commission does not see fulfilled by ETSI.  
European Standardisation System internationally competitive 
and appreciated by industry  
        
The European Standardisation System (ESS) is internationally competitive 
and appreciated by industry. Standardisation is a tried and tested tool for 
relieving the legislator and p romotes practical, lean and thus innovation -
friendly regulation. With the organisation of structures and processes in the 
background, the ESOs do a very good job from the point of view of German 
industry and their processes are well adapted to the correspo nding sectoral 
needs. Furthermore, the European standardisation processes have efficient 
structures for international and global cooperation. European requirements 
and values are successfully incorporated into international standards. A 
divergence between european and international standardisation can thus be 
ruled out. This is of central importance for the global competitiveness of the 
european economy. 
Negative representation of ETSI surprises 
 
ETSI has been a successful building block of the ESS for more than 30 years 
and is characterised by the development of high -quality and globally 
successful standards. ETSI is highly inclusive and gives all interested 
stakeholders direct access to participation in technical standardisation. This 
is reflected not least in t he high number of SMEs (25 percent) that 
successfully collaborate with large companies in standardisation. Only 
companies based in the EU can become ESTI members; companies not based 
in Europe can become associate members.  
ETSI processes are characterised by the fact that national governments decide 
on the statutes and processes. Government representatives are members of 
the democratically elected ETSI Board, which has the task of overseeing the 
technical work programme. This includes the decision to accept  or reject 
standardisation requests, which the Board takes after intensive consultation 
with the technical experts and in close consultation with the Commission. In

Avoid European decoupling in the ICT sector  
 
In the context of industrial and trade policy shifts of global dimension, the 
political and strategic importance of standardisation is clearly increasing. 
Against this background, we advocate for fair and open access and balanced 
participation in stan dardisation. Fragmentation of standardisation and 
isolation of the ESS from international stakeholders is not expedient. It is not 
only the application of standards that brings benefits, but also participation 
in their development - all countries benefit f rom common technological 
guidelines. We advocate active participation in the development of 
international standards and their subsequent adoption. Efforts to exclude run 
the risk of provoking corresponding counter-reactions and counteracting the 
mutual opening of markets.  
Recommendations of the German industry  
 
A revision of the Regulation (EU) No 1025/2012 should be seen strictly as a 
last resort solution. Against the background of current global shifts, potential 
reactions in the international context should be included in the decision -
making process.  
We recommend the European Commission to jointly evaluate the ETSI 
governance with ETSI. This should ensure "good governance" - in agreement 
with all stakeholders involved - and guarantee ETSI's connection  to the 
international ICT standardisation system.

About BDI 
 
The Federation of German Industries  (BDI) communicates German 
industries’ interests to the political authorities concerned. She offers strong 
support for companies in global competition. The BDI has access to a wide-
spread network both within Germany and Europe, to all the important 
markets and to international organizations. The BDI accompanies the 
capturing of international markets politically. Also, she offers information 
and politico-economic guidance on all issues relevant to industries. The BDI 
is the leading organization of German industries  and related service 
providers. She represents 40 inter-trade organizations and more than 100.000 
companies with their approximately 8 million employees. Membership is 
optional. 15 federal representations are advocating industries’ interests on a 
regional level.

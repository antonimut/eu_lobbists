 
 
 
Additional remarks for the consultation  on Sustainable consumption of goods – 
promoting the right to repair and reuse 
 
METRO AG is an international wholesaler with headquarters in Düsseldorf, Germany. 
With our brands METRO and MAKRO, we serve professional customers in the hotel, 
restaurants and catering (HoReCa) industry as well as independent merchants (Traders). 
We have more than 95,000 employees in 15 EU member states and in total 34 countries 
worldwide. As a multichannel supplier, we combine a wide network of modern wholesale 
stores with an extensive delivery service (Food Service Distribution/FSD delivery 
companies Classic Fine Foods, Pro à Pro, Rungis Express, Aviludo and Pro a Pro Spain) 
and an online marketplace (METRO Markets) all supported digitally. 
 
We would like to catch your attention to the following points in addition to the questionnaire 
we have submitted: 
 
- We welcome the foundation and enfo rcement of repair and re use norms, 
especially by strengthening the sustainable and durable design and 
manufacture of products with incentives to use secondary raw materials . We 
need a holistic approach in improving consumer preferences and right to repair 
is one of the options. Extending legal guarantee period will not change 
consumer preferences.  Repair is not always the e conomically and 
environmentally best answer. 
 
- The choice on type of remedies should be left to the trader / manufacturer. 
Especially our B2B professional customers opt for refund or replacement 
instead of repair due to long waiting times and spare part availability in cases 
of repair. 
 
 
- Delivery of spare parts from overseas is also timely, costly and comes at an 
environmental footprint.  
 
- Labor cost variations in different countries affect the extend to which a product 
can be repaired or discarded. If the repairing of the defect product costs more 
than a new product, this is economically not feasible.  Some companies also 
divert all repair business to a single country, which again comes at an 
environmental cost; i.e. to send the defect items there and get them back. 
- 3D printing for spare parts is at its first footsteps and accurate data sharing on 
repair and product information between different actors (especially between 
manufacturers and repair points) is key for a smooth implementation. 
 
- We need a fair level playing field especially concerning the sale of low-price 
products via online channels, which creates an abundance of low-quality items 
on the market, and which mostly need to be discarded when they have defects.

- We have internal customer feedback on their preferences of return or repair 
and which items were mostly repaired. In general, we can say many B2B 
customers opt for return / refund / new product instead of repair, since the 
defect item needs to be quickly replaced by a working one, as B2B customers 
are working with these products on a daily basis to run their businesses. On 
average for the EU, repair of products that have a sale price of less than 50 
EUR is not plausible.

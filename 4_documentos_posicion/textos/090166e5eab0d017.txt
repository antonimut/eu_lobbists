Background and method
Background
HUI has been commissioned by Svensk Handel/Swedish Trade Federation to examine the attitudes by consumers and companies to threecurrent 
issues:
• Accessibility adaptation of digital commerce
• The right to repair
• Consumer credits
The project aims to give Svensk Handel/Swedish Trade Federation a stable foundation on which they can base their communication with politicians, 
officials, and citizens. In particular, the focus is on developing insights that can form the basis for communication and that can also serve as inputs for 
future investigations of the current issues.
Method
In light of the project's purpose and goals, HUI and Svensk Handel/Swedish Trade Federation have produced two surveys, one with Svensk Handel's 
member companies, and one with a representative sample of consumers. This presentation contains insights from the responses to the survey that 
was addressed to consumers.
The survey includes a sample of 1,010 respondents. The sample is nationally representative regarding the respondents’ age, gender, and region.
Limitations
The survey was extensive, and the insights presented had to be limited. Further insights can be presented upon request by Svensk Handel/Swedish 
Trade Federation.

Behavioral patterns and inconvenience may stand in the way of 
repairs
Repairs appear relatively rare
• Only sixteen percent of those who had an item that needed to be 
repaired or replaced in the past year chose to repair it.
• More than forty percent chose to buy a new item.
• Only twenty percent say that they always or often buy spare parts and 
repair themselves.
Low willingness to pay for repairs
• A majority of consumers say they would not be willing to pay more than 
SEK 800 to repair a one-year-old smartphone that they bought for SEK 
8,000.
• This is true across all age groups.
• It is also true for all income groups except those with an annual income 
of more than SEK 500,000.
Regulations are unlikely to be sufficient for behavioral change
• The most common reasons for choosing to buy a new product, instead 
of repairing the broken one, are difficult to target by rule changes.
• The main reasons given by consumers are that: ‘the product was not 
repairable’, ‘that it was not cheap enough to repair the product 
compared to buying a new’ or ‘they got a new or updated model if they 
bought a new one’.
Practical barriers and behavioral patterns may interfere with 
repairs
• Less than twenty percent chose to repair an item that needed to be 
replaced or repaired in the past year.
• Barriers to repairing goods appear to be that:
• it is not possible to repair the item, 
• it is too expensive, 
• or simply that the consumer wants to change to a newer model.

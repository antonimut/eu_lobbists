ERGaR’s Feedback on Common Rules for the Internal Markets in Renewable 
and Natural Gases and in Hydrogen 
  
06/04/2022 
  
  
The European Renewable Gas Registry (ERGaR) is a Brussels-based association that aims at enabling 
cross-border transfers of certificates for renewable gases. Today we represent 32 members from 13 
European countries and from a wide range of activities in the gas sector, such as renewable gas 
registries and associations, energy commodity traders and gas DSOs/TSOs. ERGaR operates the ERGaR 
CoO Scheme, which facilitates cross-border transfers of gas Guarantees of Origin as well as other types 
of renewable gas certificates. We also strive for ERGaR to be come a Europe -wide recognised 
organisation for administering the mass balancing of biomethane distributed along the European gas 
network.   
ERGaR appreciates the European Commission´s intention to provide a clear and comprehensive 
guidance on the common ru les for the internal markets in renewable and natural gases and in 
hydrogen. We may suggest to further elaborate on the following points of the proposal to fully reflect 
the existing practice:   
  
1. Article 8 (1) Gas Directive (COM 2021 /803): “Certification of Renewable and Low 
Carbon Gases” 
ERGaR welcomes the acknowledgement of sustainability certification and mass balancing of 
renewable and low carbon gases according to Directive Article 29 and 30 of (EU) 2018/2001, as 
requested in article 8. 
However, the administrative and financial burden of a full sustainability certification of small 
installations could exceed the benefits.  
Thus, sustainability certification shall not be a mandatory requirement for biomethane from 
installations below the capacity threshold set out in Article 29 (1) of Directive (EU) 2018/2001 (RED II) 
to be eligible for target compliance, e.g., to a get a discount as proposed in Article 16 of the draft 
revision of the Gas Regulation.

2. Article 16 Gas Regulation (COM 2021/804): Tariff Discounts for renewable and low 
carbon gases  
According to Article 16 (5) of the draft Gas Regulation (COM 2021/804), a discount of 100% on the 
regulated tariffs from the transmission system operators at all interconnection points shall be granted 
to renewable and low -carbon gases for which a Proof of Sustainability (PoS) pursuant to Articles 29 
and 30 of Directive 2018/2001 can be provided.  
There is a risk for claiming a discount on the interconnection tariffs by means of PoS belonging to 
domestically consumed renewable and low -carbon gases. TSOs may not unambiguously verify 
whether a PoS belongs to the nominated and allocated capacity at the interconnection points and 
entry points from third countries without support from Voluntary Schemes or ot her competent 
bodies, which monitor and document the issuance of PoS. Implementing and maintaining the 
necessary settlement processes may lead to exorbitant administrative and financial burdens.  
The cross-border tariffs make up only a small part of the co sts for renewable and low carbon gases 
and will not provide significant leverage in supporting green gases.  
It is questionable whether this additional administrative burden could justify the intended support for 
renewable and low-carbon gases. Other support mechanisms / incentives are considered to reach a 
higher impact. 
Thus, ERGaR recommends deleting Article 16 (5). 
  
3. Article 15 Gas Directive (COM 2021 /803): Bills and billing information  
Member States shall ensure that bills and billing information fulfil the requirements set out in Annex 
I, which includes the disclosure of the share of renewable and low carbon gases by means of 
guarantees of origin (GOs).  
According to Article 8 of the Gas Directive, renewable and low carbon gases shall be certified pursuant 
to Articles 28 -30 of Directive EU 2018/2001. This creates a couple of challenges related to the 
interaction between the issuance and cancellation of GOs by issuing bodies according to Article 19 of 
Directive EU 2018/2001 and the sustainability certification and reporting obligations of renewable and 
low carbon gases: 
1. In the event GOs exists for renewable and low carbon gases, which shall be reported to the 
Union Database in accordance with Article 31a 4) of the draft recast of Directive EU 
2018/2001, those GOs shall be cancelled before they are reported to the Union Database.

The cancelled GOs cannot be used to disclose the origin of the renewable and low carbon 
gases to final customers anymore, which is in contradiction with the billing requirements set 
out in Annex I 5 of the Gas Directive. Not cancelling GOs before the renewable or low carbon 
gas is reported to the Union Database would, however, create the risk of double claiming of 
the renewable value for different purposes (3.2). 
2. The verification and documentation of the renewable origin and sustainability characteristics 
of renewable gases follow different principles and requirements depending on whether a 
GO or a PoS is issued for the same renewable gas consignment. The competent authorities 
and Voluntary Schemes, which are responsible for the issuance of either GOs or PoS, have 
no legal framework to cooperate with each other for preventing the potential risk of double 
claiming the renewable value with GOs and PoS.  
3. In this regard ERGaR suggests the following actions: 
• Clear provisions are needed on the purpose of GOs and on the purpose of the 
sustainability certification of renewable and low carbon gases.  
• A framework needs to be established enabling the interaction between GO issuing 
bodies and national and Voluntary Schemes aiming at preventing double claims and 
synchronising both systems ensuring reliability and increasing efficiency.

EU hydrogen and gas market decarbonization package  
 
RAG Austria AG is a pioneer in the development of hydrogen technologies, large -volume 
hydrogen storage and the integration of hydrogen into the existing natural gas infrastructure. 
Numerous projects already implemented demonstrate the corresponding feasibility of a low-
carbon energy transition with gaseous energy carriers.  
 
We therefore welcome the inclusion of hydrogen in the EU's climate strategy and the creation 
of appropriate framework conditions for the related infrastructure. Nevertheless, we see 
considerable need for improvement in the current draft of the gas package: 
 
• The role of large-volume seasonal storage is still undervalued and has regulatory 
gaps: In principle, the introduction of tariff regulation for hydrogen storage makes 
sense, since considerable investments are required, which the market itself would 
probably not be ready to cover in full. In the ramp -up phase, there should be the 
possibility of so-called "regulatory sandboxes" similar to those for grids. The principles 
of Art 48 (Directive) should therefore apply not only to networks, but also to the 
storage sector. 
 
• We also miss regulatory framework conditions for temporary storage of CO2 (not 
its permanent sequestration) with subsequent use as a valuable raw material (CCU), 
including the methanation of H 2. This will be a key function for the planned circular 
economy and CO2 sinks. Compared to hydrogen, there is even already an assessable 
market price of CO 2, which can make the temporary storage economically feasible. 
Intermediate CO2 storage should therefore also be included. 
 
• It is striking that in the coordinated EU-wide infrastructure planning within the TYNDP, 
DSOs are now to play an important role, but at the same time storage companies are 
only stakeholders - among many others - in the consultation process. A binding and 
formal inclusion of storage companies as a party in the TYNDP process should 
therefore be included. 
 
• Beside a possible tariff regulation, a strong long -term commitment to large -scale 
hydrogen storage is needed. Above all measures, funding for H2 is key to a quick 
market development . In addition to support programs for scaling up  and further 
developing the technology readiness level of H 2 storage as well as H2 production 
technologies and H2 infrastructure (in line with recently published European Hydrogen 
Backbone 3.0) in general, there is also a need for substantial investment support 
for the repurposing of existing natural gas underground storage facilities into 
hydrogen storage facilities to ensure the rapid implementation of these cost-intensive 
projects.  
 
• At the same time, the current market situation shows that the applicable framework 
conditions for natural gas storage facilities - which will continue to play an important 
role in the future through the  addition of hydrogen  in order to guarantee Europe's 
security of supply and, above all, price hedging - are not sufficient to (i) force and 
motivate storage bookings or injections and (ii) generate storage fees that even come 
close to covering costs in the case of negotiated storage tariffs (negative summer-

winter spreads). The review of the competitive situation in the storage market by 
national regulators has proven to be inappropriate, as it does not take into account the

The importance of large-volume gas storages as well as the necessity for developing 
future hydrogen storage urgently needs to be strengthened. In this context, the so-
called storage paradox  should be pointed out, which shows that especially at low 
filling levels the market has difficulties to sufficiently remunerate the true value of large-
volume cross-seasonal storage.  Accordingly, a low filling level at the end of the heating 
season leads to high demand/high prices in summer, which in turn squeezes the price 
margins for winter contracts (the true value of storage or benchmark for security of 
supply). The lower the filling levels or the higher the filling targets relative to the starting 
point, the more present this phenomenon is. However, sufficient remuneration must 
be ensured for the storage operators , especially because, in addition to cost -
covering operation, the maintenance of the storage facilities and the repurposing of 
natural gas to hydrogen storages requires considerable investment.  
 
There is a need for meaningful market-based incentives for storage management 
(tightening of the SOS Regulation incl. obligation to physically hold gas quantities, load 
flow commitments, etc.). Only then will it be possible to finance storage investments in 
existing infrastructure that enable hydrogen to be added to gas. 
 
Furthermore, it can be observed that gas prices are extremely volatile, and that 
exchange trading causes price fluctuations of about 50% within one day. This is 
driven by large international investment funds that bring purely speculative price 
elements into the energy supply. This makes a meaningful long-term evaluation of the 
storage systems based on market prices almost impossible. We see the marketability 
of negotiated storages at risk through this uncontrolled exchange trading and suggest 
a review of the exchange trading in gas, as well as the introduction of fair rules to 
stabilize the exchange, as it is common on other exchanges ( e.g., by "suspension of 
exchange trading" to calm the market) and to protect the others. 
 
• A functioning European storage market  with member states’ filling levels of 80 
percent should take into consideration the technical characteristics and conside r the 
respective domestic demands. Since technical capacities are not equally available in 
all member states, the burden sharing mechanism should provide for capacity bookings 
from countries without or too little capacities to cover a reasonable share of the 
domestic demand [EU-27 average of 30%]. This also strengthens Europe's political 
independence. The REPowerEU targets for diversification of natural gas using LNG 
sources and significantly higher targets for biomethane and hydrogen increase 
Europe's storage needs.  This is the case because LNG is purchased as a wave -
delivered product, unlike pipeline gas, which is delivered flat. In this context, 
underground gas storage or hydrogen storage is an important buffer option but urgently 
needs to be strengthened as regards clear regulatory framework conditions.  
 
• With regard to the definition of low -carbon gases or low -carbon hydrogen, we 
argue for setting  a realistic CO2 reduction target of  60 percent. Regardless of this, 
however, in our opinion the determination of the assessment method by means of a

• Standardized certification will play a key role in the tradability and thus the liquidity 
of gaseous energy carriers of different quality. Therefore, we also see the approach 
of mass balancing as too restrictive. The "book and claim " approach  is already 
standard in balancing today and would support marketability. It is also important that 
the import of hydrogen is also taken into account in the certification system. Here 
in particular, mass balancing seems unfeasible. 
 
• The currently insufficient specifications for vertical unbundling - and thus the shift of 
the hydrogen sector into the unregulated area - leave the design of the framework 
conditions for the roll -out of hydrogen production and its economic viability to an 
unclear fate. Although the question of where the hydrogen should come from is 
generally still open, the framework conditions to be taken from RED III and the 
delegated act on the addition ality principle for green hydrogen already contain 
unobjective and unrealistic preconditions that demotivate investments and project 
developers. In particular, the sharp distinction between grid -connected plants and 
directly supplied plants for hydrogen pr oduction, as well as the associated maximum 
time period between the construction of renewable electricity production and 
electrolysis, contradicts the typical project process, which is based on a  gradual 
expansion of electricity production and electrolysis.  
 
We kindly ask you to consider our arguments in the future legislative proposals and are 
available for discussion at any time.

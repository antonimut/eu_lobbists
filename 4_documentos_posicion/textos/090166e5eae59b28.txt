On behalf of Save the Rivers Coalition and Time for the Oder Coalition, in relation to the document ‘Proposal 
for a REGULATION OF THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND OF THE COUNCIL on Union guidelines for the 
development of the trans-European transport network, amending Regulation (EU)’, we would like to present 
our comments to the above said regulation.  
 
Our comments are related to the part s of the document that refer to waterways , and which significance the 
revision of the TEN-T would have for the Oder, Vistula and Elbe rivers.  
Above all, we request a clear separation in the document between the development of waterway transport on 
the existing and functioning waterways that form part of TEN -T network of international importance (i.e. with 
the parameters of navigability class V or not less than IV) from the remaining watercourses, both channels and 
rivers. It should be determined precisely and specified without any doubt tha t in the time of climate change , 
crisis of biodiversity and the objectives adopted in the Water Framework Directive , the  extension of inland 
navigation within the European Union beyond the existing TEN-T network is unacceptable when developing the 
EU’s transport network. For the same reasons, rivers with natural character – such as the Elbe – must be deleted 
from TEN-T. Supporting inland cargo navigation at the cost of river ecosystems with high environmental values 
and of the ecosystem services they provide, while not implementing the law of the European Union at the same 
time (the Birds Directive, the Habitats Directive, the Water Framework Directive, the SEA Directive) is contrary 
to the ‘responsible development’ or ‘sustainable transport’ as referred to in the revision of TEN-T. 
In this context, it is significant that in the case of Poland, the available economic analyses indicate that the 
construction of waterways will be several times more expensive than an extension of the railroad in Poland to 
reach standards competitive to road transport.  In the case of the extension of waterways, the benefits to the 
transport network in Poland will be disproportionately small in comparison to the amounts being spent. 1 
The correct planning of railway investments would allow freight trains to move with the speed of 60 km/h 
(current average speed is 27 km/h and even 14 km/h when delays are taken into account), while eliminating the 
delays that exceed 7–8 hours; the river transport would have no chance of achieving a higher share in transport. 
A look at the Elbe corridor and the modal split of freight transports shows how important a reasonably 
functioning rail connection is. Between the Port of Hamburg and the Czech Republic, 80 percent of goods are 
transported by rail and only 20 percent by truck. Almost nothing is transported on the Elbe itself, one of the 
reasons being that the river lacks reliable fairway depths. 
 
Environment: 
We would like to stress that the extension of the TEN –T network into any new sections of the rivers in Poland 
and in the Czech Republic is unacceptable. The construction of waterways that the Polish government and partly 
also the Czech one have been pursuing would entail a drastic and destructive interference in the ecosystems of 
the rivers and of their valleys, including  areas protected under  national and EU law. 
Equally unacceptable is that the inland Elbe is part of TEN-T, and it should be removed from the network. Almost 
the entire length of the inland Elbe in Germany is protected under Natura 2000, and it is also designated as a 
natural water body under the European WFD.  
The situation is similar on the Vistula. For example, within the section of the Vistula River from its estuary to 
Dęblin only (on the path of the proposed E40 waterway) ten Natura 2000 sites and nineteen natural reserves 
are located. Against this background , we would like to stress that the extension of the existing waterways of 
local importance and the increase of their navigability class to waterway parameters of international importance 
are inconsistent with the requirements for the protection of Natura 2000  areas that are located in the river

Limited water resources: 
The threat of limited water resources has been identified  in Poland, among others, in the paper/publication 
prepared for the polish Ministry of Marine Economy and Inland Navigation: ‘A risk for the implementation of 
this investment [canal between the Bug and the Vistula River ], however, may be posed by limited water 
resources’. ‘In the coming years, a more frequent occurrence of both drought and high water, both having a 
negative impact among other things on inland navigation, must be taken into account’. 
The situation on the Elbe is similar. The consequences of climate change are intensifying the extremes and 
precipitation is increasingly unevenly distributed. Large parts of the German Elbe catchment area are already 
suffering from a water deficit.3 In addition, due to the closure of open-cast mines, the amount of water pumped 
into the river is decreasing. This is particularly noticeable in the case of lower discharges.  
We would like to stress that with such limited water resources and in the light of  climate change, an extension 
of waterways and the financial support for inland navigation should not take place on rivers with natural 
character and limited water resources - such as the Elbe - or beyond the already existing TEN-T network. Making 
rivers navigable fundamentally represents a significant eco logical impairment and is one of the main reasons 
why the targeted good hydromorphological status according to the WFD has not been achieved. So far for the 
Elbe, for example, no robust concepts have been presented to demonstrate that the objectives – improving 
navigability and achieving the environmental and nature conservation goals – could be realized at the same 
time. 
It is incorrect to link the  beyond the already existing TEN -T network. It is incorrect to link measures related to  
inland navigation to an increase of retention for the purpose  of fighting drought and improving flood 
protection. It needs to be emphasized that different types of infrastructure are required to support retention 
and to allow for cargo navigation.  For the purposes  of water way navigation, barrages and multi -functional 
dammed reservoirs need to  be constructed  – measures which offer either limited or no flood protection 
functions at all.   Given the Polish climatic conditions, they may even be the cause of ice-jam flood in winter. In 
addition, for waterway navigation, river regulation is required, which accelerates the water outflow, thus being 
inconsistent with the objectives of flood protection. To conclude, an explicit division should hence be made 
between the targets pertaining to waterway navigation and the remaining ones.  Within the evaluation of water 
resources, it must be strictly observed that there are alternatives to waterway shipping – preferably rail, but 
also heavy goods vehicle (HGV) ; in addition, a fundamental reduction of transports is necessary. In contrast, 
the protection of ecosystems and the resource water is mandatory and therefore a priority.  
This problem does not only apply to Poland. The investments planned on the Czech Elbe River  aim at increasing 
the depth of the waterway to 230 cm with the ultimate goal to reach the north see ports. This will have, adverse 
effects on the natural character of the river as well as on its natural values. The planned investments, however,

Economy: 
In the course of 20 years  from 1998 to 2018 , 600 million Euros have been spent on the inland Elbe waterway 
and its ports in Germany. But instead of the intended shift of goods  from roads to cargo ships - up to 23 million 
tonnes were forecasted for 2010 – transports on the Elbe have plummeted by 90 percent to less than 0.2 million 
tonnes per year, and the downward trend continues. A cost-benefit-analysis for the German inland Elbe has not 
been made. 
We would like to note the unprofitability of the extension of waterways in Poland as well,  which is also shown 
by the analyses prepared for the Polish government. The document under the title ‘Analyses of the inland 
waterway transport sector within the scope resulting from the modernization of the Oder Waterway [Odrzańska 
Droga Wodna] and the Vistula River Waterway [Droga Wodna Rzeki Wisły]’ (Stage IV, full final report, Version 2, 
Warszawa, December 2018) performed by WYG International Sp. z o.o. for the Ministry of Marine Economy and 
Inland Navigation makes it clear that in the case of the waterways of the Vistula and the Oder, which are crucial 
in terms of the National Waterway Navigation Programme 2030, the W1 variant, recommended for 
implementation, is unfavourable to the society. In the analysis, it was explicitly stated that ‘the transfer of cargo 
transport from road and rail to inland waterway transport  will lead to an increase of economic costs for the 
society. This increase will be significant enough to outstrip the potential e conomic benefits such as savings in 
connection with the reduction of negative impacts of the transport on the environment. ’ 
Whereas, a WWF report indicates that the investments (planned as part of the extension of waterway navigation 
in Poland) involving costs on such a scale, will not produce any gains within the next 30 -50 years in comparison 
to the investment outlays incurred, environmental costs, costs of other sectors of the economy and costs borne 
by residents.  Moreover, the protracted expa nsion of waterways does not create speedy solutions to current 
transportation problems. 
Already now, water resources are not sufficient to cover the needs of many of the investments in waterways 
that are planned in Poland as well as at the inland Elbe in Germany and Czech Republic. . At the same time,  there 
are many indications that the problem of water scarcity will be aggravated by the present climate change. Even 
if a huge financial effort is made in the attempt to implement these investments, the costs of their maintenance, 
including the costs of the necessary provision of water resources, will be extremely high. Consequently, the costs 
of such investments, both financial and external, will be covered by the taxpayer. It also needs to be emphasized 
that (with very high probability)  the cases of competition between various sectors of the economy for water 
resources will occur.  
 
Modernization and development of rail network instead of waterways : 
Inland navigation cannot guarantee reliable transport conceived as transport available 24 /7 all year , which is 
perceived as  standard for transport in the conditions of the modern economy  and also a goal of TEN -T. As 
experience shows, especially in the recent years, this is not possible even on the largest and fully canalised rivers 
of Europe. Consequently it will be even more unlikely on  rivers with much lower average flows such as the Oder, 
Vistula or Elbe. At the same time, inland navigation causes a significant destruction of water resources and other 
natural resources in river valleys  - resources which are necessary for the protection of biological diversity , 
ecosystem services and the adaptation to the effects of the changing climat

If development and modernization of railways are further supported, this means of transportation can provide 
reliable and timely delivery of goods . With much lower financial and environmental  costs, both improving the 
transport performances could be obtained and a better quality and speed of passenger connections c ould be 
ensured. Hence, the implementation of the targets and challenges in connection with the passenger traffic could 
be achieved at the same time.

Conflict between the targets of waterway development and flood & drought protection:  
This is to stress that the navigation targets presented in the Polish governmental strategic document relating to 
the development of inland navigation in Poland  (Polish National Waterway Navigation Programme 2030) are to 
a significant extent in conflict with the flood protection objectives. At Border Odra Polish plans may have a effect 
on Gemany. The argument that the interference intended for the purposes of waterway navigation will result in 
the ‘protection against the effects of flood’ cannot be accepted. Flood protection functions of the barrages and 
dammed reservoirs that serve for navigation are  limited. Additionally, as it has already been mentioned before, 
such barrages and reservoirs may even contribute to the creation of ice-jam floods in winter. 
To conclude, in a country like Poland, where the share of inland transport in the overall cargo shipment in the 
years 2000 -2019 decreased from 0.8% to 0.2%  4 supporting an extension of the infrastructure for cargo 
navigation is, in our view, financially inefficient, socially harmful and inconsistent with the national and EU law. 
This also applies to the Elbe. Despite substantial spending on ports and river works, navigability has not been 
improved in the last 30 years. Due to the lack of reliability, freight transports on the river have fallen to a historic 
low. Therefore, the Elb e must be removed from TEN -T. It must be said , that the mandatory European 
environmental goals are not being met. The “overall concept for the Elbe” does not bring about a solution here 
either, but rather reinforces the blockade.

EHI’s position on the Hydrogen and decarbonised Gas 
package 
 
 
Dear all,  
 
 
The European Heating Industry, EHI, welcomes the publication of the Hydrogen and Decarbonised Gas 
Package that sets the rules for creating a competitive hydrogen market and better includes renewable 
and low-carbon gases in the legislation. 
 
In the current international context, and following the REPowerEU communication,  it is more important 
than ever to reduce our energy dependence, especially from Russian gas . This  includes phasing in 
modern and efficient heating systems in Europe’s buildings , including electric heat pumps and hybrids, 
but also efficient appliances using gaseous fuels. For example: hybrid heaters, thermally driven heat 
pumps and microcogeneration units, including fuel cells , as well as condensing boilers  provide huge 
energy savings. Other efficient technologies such as solar thermal  installations, biomass boilers and 
bioliquid appliances, used in efficient heating systems, can also contribute to cutting energy consumption 
and CO2 emissions. 
 
Crucially, our production and use of renewable and low-carbon gases, including hydrogen, must increase, 
as set out by the European Commission in the REPowerEU communication , with the aim of fully 
decarbonising all energy vectors . EHI is fully committed to supporting the new targets and developed 
already technologies that can work with these new gases . Our heating technologies can run with 
biomethane and e -methane without any required adaptation of the appliances. This possibility to use 
immediately green gases in the heating sector is an enormous advantage to make quick gains as set in 
the REPowerEU communication. Moreover, modern condensing boilers can run with up to 20% hydrogen, 
thus reducing natural gas consumption while creating a large and immediate demand for renewable and 
low-carbon hydrogen that provide a security of investments for hydrogen producers. Heating 
manufacturers have also developed ‘hydrogen ready’ products, i.e. : working with methane-based gases 
today, but convertible into 100% hydrogen thanks to an easy to install kit . These appliances have been 
tested successfully on field in the last two years and are designed to replace old and inefficient heating 
systems based on natural gas in the coming years.  
 
Together with network planning, the bottom-up approach is essential to make the hydrogen economy a 
reality as local communities are those who know the best where hydrogen and green gases are relevant 
and be the most efficient solution for decarbonisation . The European heating industry  is working in 
cooperation with citizens and local authorities in many places in Europe such as in Stad aan 't Haringvliet 
in the Netherlands, in Chateauneuf in France, or in Kaisersesch in Germany  to create synergies  in the 
energy systems with all the relevant actors, from the production, transmission and distribution and end-
use sectors. 
 
For these reasons, it is important that the Hydrogen and Decarbonised Gas Package clearly allows and 
promotes the use of hydrogen and biomethane in the building sectors, as a necessary complement to 
electrification. A multi -technology, multi -vector approach is key to decarbonise buildings in a fast, 
sustainable and affordable way and to support our objective of cutting energy dependence. The package 
is key to support the switch from natural gas to renewable and low-carbon gases. 
This paper presents the views of the European Heating Industry on the Hydrogen and Decarbonised 
Gas Package, published in December 2021.

1. Yet, the Directive on common rules for the internal markets in renewable and natural gases and in 
hydrogen focuses on transmission of hydrogen and pure hydrogen infrastructure and not on 
distribution. Cutting emissions from buildings is one of the most important challenges of 
decarbonisation as the sector represents 40% of Europe’s final energy consumption and 36% of 
CO2 emissions. Given the role the hydrogen can play in emission’s reduction in the residential 
sector together with electrification, it is important that the legislation guarantees that the 
hydrogen will be accessible to customers at the distribution level. 
 
2. Despite some flexibility introduced in the legislation, we worry that the decentralised production 
of hydrogen is not enough promoted by the package, although we know that hydrogen valleys  
are developing across Europe , via off-grid systems for many of them . The concept of ‘Citizen 
Energy Community’ is introduced in article 14 of  the Directive but it foresees a role only in the 
natural gas market, without mentioning hydrogen.  Such communities should have the right to 
produce hydrogen locally and have access to the infrastructure to export any surplus they might 
produce as it is the case for methane.  With the development of hydrogen valleys , we observe 
citizen initiatives emerging across Europe and testing hydrogen with the aim of replacing natural 
gas in their energy systems . We have to make sure that the legislation does not restrict such 
initiatives, otherwise it would discourage the efforts of citizens to find their own pathways - the 
most adapted to their needs - to decarbonisation. The bottom-up approach must be encouraged 
as it will play a key role to make the hydrogen transition a success in the EU.  
 
3. The Regulation on the internal markets for renewable and natural gases and for hydrogen sets out 
rules on the European hydrogen infrastructure with a new entity to supervise its implementation: 
the European Network of Networks Operator for Hydrogen (ENNOH). Network planning between 
gas, hydrogen and electricity is key to optimise the European energy system  and reach our 
carbon-neutrality target by 2050. To this aim, it is crucial that all the relevant stakeholders of the 
hydrogen and the gas value chain are involved in the ENNOH to ensure customers certainty, both 
for the use of blends and pure hydrogen.  
 
4. To accelerate the transition from natural gas to renewable gases, the Directive should introduce 
EU-wide binding targets to translate into the legislation the increased ambition announced in the 
REPowerEU communication. Such targets are key to provide a predictable and stable framework, 
for the decarbonisation of the gas system. It will help to kick-start a European industry in the field 
of synthetic methane and h ydrogen production (e.g.: electrolysers) and foster circular and 
sustainable agriculture and farming by spurring investment in biogas and bio-methane.  
 
5. We support the transparency and information on renewable and decarbonised gases across the 
value chain and to end -users, as the final consumers must be able to use decarbonised and 
renewable gases to heat buildings and be informed of the fuels they are using: natural gas, bio 
or synthetic methane, and hydrogen. However, while the Annex I to the Directive introduces the 
concept of guarantees of origin , which we support,  it is unclear at this stage where the actual 
measuring of gas quality at the point of consumption  will take place. The Directive should also 
clarify in the Article 16  that it is up to natural  gas meters to measure blends with hydrogen to 
avoid confusion with the Article 17 on  smart metering systems in the hydrogen systems that 
focuses on pure hydrogen only, to our understanding.  Moreover, real-time information on gas 
quality (e.g.: in case of blending) and fair technical rules defining the interaction between new 
gases and heaters are relevant for the appliances, the grid and supply communication. This is 
essential, as heating appliances cannot run on mixtures of gases for which they have not been 
certified; on the contrary, it is important that there is a common understanding across the value

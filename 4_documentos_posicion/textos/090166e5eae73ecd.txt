Alliander, Enexis & Stedin response to the consultation on the proposal for 
a Decarbonised Gas and Hydrogen Package  
 
 
 
The Dutch distribution system operators (DSOs) Alliander, Enexis and Stedin welcome the European 
Commission's proposal for the "Decarbonised Gas and Hydrogen Package". This package aims to 
significantly modernise the current regulatory framework for gas, bring it into line with the current 
climate objectives and determine the market organisation for hydrogen. This package also focuses on 
security of supply by diversifying the energy sources. These are directions that we, as Dutch DSOs are 
willing to support. It is important to note that there are no all-fit solutions, no silver bullets to 
achieve the above goals. The energy transition develops unpredictably, which means the puzzle is 
always different. With that in mind, we advocate that as few options as possible should be excluded 
from the regulatory framework in advance, allowing this package to provide the flexibility to adapt to 
national and changing circumstances.  
 
We would like to present our views on the following topics:  
• Role of DSOs in the distribution of hydrogen and renewable and low carbon gas  
• The EU DSO Entity  
• Third Party Access to the hydrogen system  
• Consumer rights  
• Renewable and low carbon gases: Definition, tariff discounts, cooperation and gas quality  
 
Role of DSOs in the distribution of hydrogen and renewable and low carbon gas  
 
The decarbonised gas and hydrogen package lacks clarity on the distribution of hydrogen and the 
role of DSOs, and seems to have the assumption that hydrogen production and demand will only 
occur at transmission (TSO) level. This while DSOs can have an important role in connecting supply 
and demand and accelerating the uptake of renewable and low-carbon gases (like hydrogen). 
  
The existing gas grids of DSOs can be used to distribute a large proportion of renewable and low 
carbon gases such as hydrogen after retrofitting. This is a cost-efficient way of making our energy 
mix more sustainable by using an already existing infrastructure and thus accelerate the energy 
transition. Also, large parts of end-users, including industry, are connected to the distribution grid 
and DSOs can give these groups the opportunity to decarbonise their gas use.  Specific rules for DSOs 
are needed at this stage to involve them in this transition in a timely manner and to provide 
investment security.  
 
In order to clarify this role, we advocate adjusting the definitions of Hydrogen Network Operator 
(HNO) & natural gas in such a way that HNO only applies to the transmission of hydrogen. Natural 
gas will have to be adapted to “gas” including all forms of gas and therefore also hydrogen. Since 
DSOs will then be responsible for the distribution of gas, it also includes the possibility of distributing 
hydrogen. In this way we create clarity about the role of DSOs in the distribution of hydrogen and 
align the regulatory framework for hydrogen with the methane & electricity system.   
 
We welcome a transition period for the Regulated Asses Base (RAB) in which financial transfers 
between methane and hydrogen are possible in relation to the high operating costs and small 
customer bases at the start of hydrogen grid operating. To finance this start-up phase without 
slowing down the transition, limited financial transfers are needed.

The EU DSO Entity  
 
In our opinion, the electricity, methane and hydrogen system should be seen as one energy system 
in which the subsystems are dependent on each other, therefore we support the initiative of the 
commission to create only one EU DSO Entity for electricity and gas. In the current proposal, this 
merging of systems at the entity level is limited to methane only. In line with our vision, this would 
also include the hydrogen system at distribution level. This enhances sector integration.   
 
DSOs with electricity customers vote for only electricity issues and DSOs with gas customers vote for 
only gas issues. Issues that impact both the electricity and gas system will be voted on by electricity 
DSOs and gas DSOs. Each gas customer and electricity customer should lead to one vote.  
  
Third Party Access to the hydrogen system  
 
We support the Commission in their reasoning that in immature hydrogen grids negotiated access 
should be possible as a TPA system, but that this should eventually develop into a system of 
regulated access. In this way, customer interests are well protected.  
 
In line with the EU Hydrogen Strategy, the third phase of hydrogen market development aims to 
ensure that from 2030 renewable hydrogen technologies reach maturity and are widely deployed, 
reaching all sectors that are difficult to decarbonise. In this phase, the involvement of DSOs in the 
hydrogen grid is foreseen, which means that even after 2030, negotiated access should be possible 
for local isolated grids for a limited period of time. We will explain this position:  
 
In the ramp-up phase of hydrogen distribution by DSOs (which will continue for DSOs after 2030), 
isolated distribution networks and distribution networks connected to the hydrogen backbone can 
coexist. Isolated networks will independently have to guarantee the availability of hydrogen, the 
balance on the grid and security of supply (both security of supply of hydrogen and technical 
safety/integrity). Such isolated networks are complex and require specific powers to guarantee the 
integrity of the grid by allowing the network operator to consider whether, and if so when, it is 
possible to connect new producers and customers. In the case of isolated networks, the 
responsibility for security of supply lies primarily with the hydrogen supplier, but in some cases it 
may be necessary for the network operator to also be given a responsibility in this regard.  
  
Consumer rights 
 
We support the Commission’s proposal to establish consumer rights for the hydrogen system. 
Although in the current proposal hydrogen consumers have fewer rights than natural gas 
consumers.   
 
There is a difference between consumer rights for natural gas consumers and hydrogen consumers. 
Since this package lays down the rules for perhaps the next 10 – 15 years, we prefer that consumer 
rights are better safeguarded for hydrogen consumers as well. We prefer that consumer rights 
between natural gas consumers and hydrogen consumers are equal. After all, in practice consumers 
on the distribution level often cannot choose between natural gas or hydrogen. This choice is often 
made for them.  
  
Renewable and low carbon gases: Definition, tariff discounts, cooperation and gas quality   
 
We support the Commission's desire to promote the production of renewable and low-carbon gases. 
In order to speed up the energy transition and to diversify our energy sources it is of the utmost

importance that there is an increase in the production of both sustainable hydrogen and renewable 
methane-containing gas.   
 
Definition  
The proposal could gain clarity and to avoid selective quotes from RED, it would be recommendable 
to refer to all renewable gaseous fuels in RED III. With the current definitions included in the 
directive, the definition of green hydrogen (produced with green electricity) seems to be missing.   
 
Tariff discount  
The promotion of production of renewable and low-carbon gases should not be achieved with 
incentives through network tariffs. Network tariffs should incentivize efficient network usage, but 
network tariffs are not related to the production itself. We believe that targeted stimulation of 
production through specific subsidies aimed at production installations of renewable and low-carbon 
gases (CAPEX or OPEX) would be more effective.  
  
If, all things considered, the view is still that the production of renewable and low-carbon gases 
should be stimulated (also) through network tariffs, we see two attention points. Firstly, the tariff 
discount should only be applicable to gases injected in the methane network. For the hydrogen 
distribution network, our starting point is that we will only distribute green hydrogen. Therefore, a 
tariff discount on the hydrogen distribution network does not seem appropriate to us. Secondly, the 
proposed tariff discount seems to be applicable to transmission level only and not to distribution 
tariffs. The choice for a connection to a transmission network or distribution network should be 
based on technical system characteristics and should not be influenced by such a discount.   
 
In the end it is important that the producer also has an incentive to work as efficiently as possible. It 
will also have to be taken into account that the total costs incurred by the network operator will have 
to be reimbursed. With increasing injection of renewable and low-carbon gases, either the discount 
percentage will have to be reduced or the standard rate will have to be increased. We therefore 
consider it would be sensible that the proposed rate discount is regularly evaluated. It should also be 
possible to differentiate per Member State, because developments will not keep pace in all Member 
States.  
 
Cooperation  
Given the foreseen increase in the production of renewable gases like biogas and biomethane, we 
support the Commission's proposal that the distribution system operators and the transmission 
system operator should draw up plans in consultation on how the system can most efficiently 
facilitate market participants. With the expected sharp increase, the gas system will change from a 
top-down system to a decentralized, dual system, in which the gas can flow both downstream and 
upstream (bi-directional). This transition from a few carbon-based producers to a huge amount of 
smaller renewable production units is already unfolding in the electricity grid. Cooperation between 
the network operators is indispensable for the proper functioning of that system.  
 
Gas quality  
The Commission proposal gives the option to the regulatory authorities in the Member States to 
make DSOs responsible for ensuring efficient gas quality management on the DSO network. If this is 
necessary for system management as a result of the feed-in of renewable gases, the position of the 
Dutch DSOs Alliander, Enexis and Stedin is that this is a task for the TSO and the producers on the 
DSO network. This places the costs on the parties that can influence the quality and thus guarantees 
the most cost-efficient system.

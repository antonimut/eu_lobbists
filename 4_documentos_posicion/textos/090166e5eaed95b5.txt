Subject: Consultation response of VGN to the revision of EU rules on market access 
Vereniging Gasopslag Nederland (hereinafter: “VGN”), an association representing gas storage system 
operators active in the Dutch gas market,  welcomes the opportunity to comment on the European 
Commission’s proposals for a revision of Regulation (EC) No. 715/2009 on conditions for access to the 
natural gas transmission networks  (the “Gas Regulation ”) and a revision of Directive 2009/73/EC 
concerning common rules for the internal market of natural gas (2009/73) ( the “Gas Directive”), as 
published by DG Energy on the 15 December 2021 (the “Proposal”). The comments provided in this 
consultation response are based on the text of the proposed amendments, other related publications, 
and our recent discussions with local authorities in the Netherlands, including the regulatory authority 
for the Dutch gas market and the Ministry of Economic Affairs and Climate. 
1. General remarks on the Proposal 
Natural gas storages are set to play an important role in facilitating an efficient energy transition and 
supporting the EU’s decarbonisation goals. They are crucial in providing flexibility  to the energy 
system, both on a short -term and on a seasonal basis, while the EU’s energy market will become  
increasingly dominated by intermittent renewable energy supply. Natural gas storages also pl ay an 
important role in ensuring security of supply, as highlighted by recent developments and as recognised 
by the European Commission in the communication dated 8 March 2022 calling for joint European 
action for more affordable, secure and sustainable en ergy, as well as  the subsequent legislative 
proposal addressing security of supply concerns. 
VGN has the view that the Proposal is overall successful in building the right way forward for the EU’s 
natural gas and hydrogen markets. In particular, the Proposal contains a set of clear-cut rules for the 
development of the hydrogen market from the start, thus avoiding the pitfalls of a lenghty and drawn-
out process similar to the liberalisation of  the EU’s internal natural gas market. In addition, VGN 
supports the introduction of stricter rules with r espect to TSO benchmarks via ACER and the 
obligations on regulatory authorities to forecast transportation tariffs up to 2050. 
However, VGN has concerns in relation to a number of elements in the Proposal. Firstly, VGN does not 
support the possibility to allow for cross-subsidies between natural gas and hydrogen systems, which 
would not be in line with the principle  of cost-reflectivity as set out in the joint white paper by ACER 
and CEER titled “When and How to Regulate Hydrogen Networks?” as published on 21 February 2021.

2. Additional points for consideration 
In addition to the above, VGN would also like to raise attention to the challenging market conditions 
for storage system operators, which are expected to continue in the coming years. A key concern is 
the persistence of low  summer-winter spreads for a number of years and most recently  the 
development of negative spreads. Another concern is high level of transmission costs, which make up 
a large share of the operational costs of a storage facility, as they are expected to increase significantly 
further due to the effects of the energy transition. Although the European Commission’s most recent 
legislative proposals targeting security of supply are beyond the scope of this consultation response, 
VGN welcomes the proposal to introduce a 100% discount for transportation tariffs at network points 
connecting to storages. We strongly believe that this can lead to a more effective use of natural gas 
storage facilities, with beneficial effects for the EU’s security of supply and flexibility in the gas market. 
In order to have a well-functioning natural gas market for all market participants, we believe that the 
Proposal should also address the negative cost effects of stranded assets of a natural gas TSO. In the 
Netherlands, natural gas transport has declined by over 30% since 2013 and is expected to decline 
even more over the coming years. Given the long depreciation periods of TSO assets, this leads to 
stranded assets. Although this trend can be partially compensated by transfers of assets to a hydrogen 
TSO, the effect of such transfers will be much smaller and currently estimated at 10% of the regulated 
asset base. A similar trend is likely to occur in other Member States in the coming years. Therefore, it 
is important that the Proposal includes the possibility for the regulatory authorities to identify 
stranded assets on the basis of objective criteria and the principle that these stranded assets should 
not be charged to network users. 
3. Conclusion and suggestions for improvement 
Based on the foregoing considerations, VGN would like to suggest four amendments to improve the 
Proposal, with the aim of ensuring  the continued availability of natural gas storages to facilitate the 
energy transition and improving market access rules for all market participants. Our amendments and 
their individual justifications are attached to this letter.

Amendment No. 1 
Article 8 – Market assessment for renewable and low -carbon gases by LNG and storage system 
operators 
Justification 
LNG and storage system operators cannot bear responsibility for the security of supply of a Member 
State, or a region as indirectly implied by this Article. The role of LNG and storage operators lies in 
providing their services in a transparent and non -discriminatory way with the intention to meet the 
demand of the market in both volume of capacity and variety of services offered.

Amendment No. 2 
Article 16a – Discount of transmission tariffs for storage system operators 
Justification 
VGN proposes a new Article 16a of the revised Gas Regulation , stipulating a discount of 100% of the 
regulated tariff from the transmission system operator for storage system operators in order  to 
incentivise gas traders to store gas before the winter season, which will increase security of supply for 
the EU.

Amendment No. 3 
Article 17 – Revenues of gas transmission system operators 
Justification 
Long-term planning of the natural gas network is the responsibility of the TSO and any investments in 
the network are approved by the Member State. The users of the network are charged the costs of 
network without having any influence on this process . If the process has led to over -investments in 
the network, or if demand for natural gas declines due to policy decisions of the Member State, then 
any resulting stranded assets of the TSO should not be charged to network users, who had no influence 
on such process or policy decisions and who in most cases have no alternative to natural gas. As such, 
it is fair that the costs associated with stranded assets should be borne by the Member State that has 
approved the investments in the first place.

Amendment No. 4 
Article 20 – Hydrogen blends at interconnection points between Union Member States in the natural 
gas system 
Justification 
In case of hydrogen blending, all consequences of the hydrogen admixture especially on the integrity 
of a storage facility (seals and components installed, compatibility of identified materials, r eduction 
of marketable capacity, etc.) are to be carefully assessed on a case-by-case basis before injection. This 
is done to ensure that there will be no migration out of the reservoir and alteration of the rock. The 
analysis of storage assets shows that the injection and storage of hydrogen/natural gas admixtures of 
more than 2% may have significant technical and commercial impacts on surface and subsurface 
storage facilities for all types of storages assets 1. Additional costs for retrofitting systems as well as 
any commercial losses that serve to create a higher hydrogen computability must be subsidized by the 
state or compensated for in the regulatory framework. Considering the time needed to adapt storage 
facilities for hydrogen admixtures, more flexibility should be given regarding the proposed deadline.

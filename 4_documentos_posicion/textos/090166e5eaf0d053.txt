GEODE position on the European Commission’s proposals for a Directive on 
common rules for the internal market in renewable and natural gases and in 
hydrogen & a Regulation on the internal markets for renewable and natural 
gases and for hydrogen 
 
Summary and key points 
• GEODE believes the hydrogen and renewable gases market decarboni sation package 
represents a unique opportunity to promote system integration in the EU energy sector and 
scale up the share of renewable and low carbon gases as key drivers for decarboni sation. 
Hydrogen plays a central role in this context and the package can dramatically enable its  
growth 
• Advancing these goals is even more crucial in the current geopolitical landscape especially 
with the EU’s new objective to phase out dependency on Russian gas as set out in the 
Commission’s REPowerEU initiative. To this end, t he latter identifies a series of measures, 
including a dramatic ramp up of hydrogen and biomethane  
• To transform the REPowerEU’s wishlist into reality, regulation must allow every actor in the 
energy system to work to its full potential to reduce emissions 
• While acknowledging the importance of hydrogen, this approach is only partially reflected in 
the Commission’s proposal s. The latter  introduces a very strict unbundling regime limi ting 
DSOs’ role in the hydroge n economy, and in general their ability to drive decarbonisation 
across sectors 
• This barrier can be addressed  by creating a more flexible framework  where network 
operators at all levels are entitled to adapt their assets to support the growth of a hydrogen 
economy across Europe 
• The separation of natural gas  ownership and hydrogen networks as proposed means that 
vertically integrated local energy undertakings and municipalities are prevented f rom 
establishing and operating new hydrogen pipelines. The transmission system operators will  
also not be able to tend to the hydrogen distribution network infrastructure in either the short 
or medium term. This means that millions of industrial and commercial consumers that are 
connected to European distribution systems and that do not have access to transmission 
systems are excluded from the supply of hydrogen 
• The production of green hydrogen, which offers considerable potential, is also significantly 
hindered by this proposed regulatory framework . It would be more efficient and technically 
sensible to feed hydrogen into local gas grids , which can be then transformed to hydrogen 
grids 
• Furthermore, this approach would deprive municipalities  of a decisive element of municipal 
heat planning 
To ensure that the role of DSOs and their decarbonisation potential is adequately reflected in the 
proposals, GEODE would like to share a series of initial considerations below.

Directive on common rules for the internal markets in renewable and natural gases and in 
hydrogen 
 
Definitions (Article 2) 
The definitions of ‘distribution’ (18) and ‘distribution system operator’ (19) must be broadened to 
ensure that the role of DSOs is not limited only to the distribution of natural gas but rather is 
expanded to a multiple range of molecules. 
Market access for renewable and low carbon gases (Article 26) 
The approach set out in the proposal is welcomed as it acknowledges the role of distribution networks 
in integrating low carbon and renewable gases in the system. 
Tasks of DSOs (Article 40) 
GEODE welcomes the new provisions included in the article which reflect increasing co nsiderations 
for the role of DSOs in the energy transition. 
Unbundling of distribution system operators (Article 42) 
The proposal precludes the management of DSOs which are part of a vertically integrated undertaking 
to participate in company structures in volved with the operation of hydrogen networks. Such an 
approach leads to an inefficient framework practically preventing the combined operation of gas and 
hydrogen networks at distribution level. Combined grid operators will contribute to build up at the 
needed speed the hydrogen network while supporting sector integration, accelerating the energy 
transition. To avoid this shortcoming, the reference to “transportation ” (of gases) in paragraph 2a 
shall be removed. 
Unbundling of dedicated hydrogen network operators (Articles 62-64) 
The unbundling requirements are very strict and risk creating unnecessary obstacles to the 
development of a hydrogen market. The ownership unbundling requirements proposed in Article 62 
imply a separation of hydrogen networks from other activities of a vertically integrated energy 
company. These rules directly refer to the unbundling provisions for transmission system operators in 
Article 54 (and following articles)  and should be limited to them. While the text provides for some 
room for adaptation  through the application of ISO or ITO models, the proposed rules would be 
extremely burdensome for vertically integrated local energy distributors . Ownership unbundling – 
which excludes local energy undertakings from owning and operating hydrogen networks – prevents 
value creation at local level and risks shifting added value to the shareholders of transmission system 
operators (e.g. global insurance groups and pension funds). 
The legal unbundling requirements introduced by Article 63 are also problematic as they make the 
operation of natural gas and hydrogen networks by the same company practically impossible. 
In addition, the derogations from the application of unbundling rules for hydrogen networks (Articles 
47-48) are extremely limited in scope and don’t really create an alternative for DSOs.

GEODE finds the approach proposed by the Commission as detrimental to the creation o f a fit for 
purpose hydrogen market in Europe due to the following reasons:  
• The creation of parallel structures within the same company would be extremely complicated 
to i mplement for local companies involved in energy distribution. As our members often 
operate both electricity and gas networks, their strength s lie in harnessing the synergies 
between different carriers to create an integrated energy system. The same synergies will take 
place if hydrogen networks are added to the picture. Introducing artificial barriers would 
therefore undermine an efficient operation of the networks they operate 
• Beyond taking several years, the creation of parallel structures for the operation of hydrogen 
and natural gas networks will result in internal inefficiencies and administrative hurdles. It’s 
therefore economically and practically  smarter to use pre-existing competencies and 
expertise within DSOs 
• The proposed requirements are also contrary to the urgent need – further made evident by 
the EU’s decision to phase out dependency from Russia – to decarbonise the energy system. 
Less stringent unbundling requirements would serve this purpose better by facilitating the 
development of much needed hydrogen networks at local level   
In addition, the unbundling proposals are not consistent with other provisions of the Directive, namely 
Article 26 (see above), which requires natural gas network operators to integrate renewable and low 
carbon gases into the natural gas system.  
 
Regulation on the internal markets for renewable and natural gases and hydrogen 
 
Representation of DSOs at the EU level (Articles 36-38; 40-42) 
The text introduces new rules on the representation of network operators at EU level through: 
• The inclusion of DSOs operating natural gas networks into the existing EU DSO Entity which 
currently represents electricity distribution operators (Articles 36-38) 
• The creation of a new entity, the European Network of Network Operators for Hydrogen 
(ENNOH) in Articles 40-42 
The creation of ENNOH reflects the Commission’s approach set out in the Gas Directive proposal to 
develop a separate hydrogen network (“transport of hydrogen”) independent, to the most possible 
extent, from the existing distribution and transmission systems. GEODE finds this choice problematic 
as it would lead to uncertainties related to DSO representation at EU level. According to the current 
proposal, for instance, DSOs operating pure hydrogen networks would not have a role in the EU DSO 
Entity, diluting DSO representation across different organisations.  
In our view, it would be critical to create a coherent representation  framework for DSOs, reflecting 
their ability to manage different types of gases in an increasing integration of the energy system.

MEMO
HYDROGEN AND DECARBONISED GAS PACKAGE (GAS
MARKET DIRECTIVE 2021/0425 (COD)) –
RESPONSE TO EC CONSULTATION
General remarks
Energinet generally welcomes the package of proposalsfrom the European Commission (EC),
noting that the market-based approach is maintained, and that there is a focus on promoting
green gasses and establishing regulation for the future hydrogen market.
Energinet welcomes that the general principles from the gas (and the electricity) market, such
as unbundling and regulated third-party access are transferred to the hydrogen market. Addi-
tionally, Energinet finds it positive that gas system operators may also operate hydrogen sys-
temsand that the endeavors for more integrated network planning arepursued.
Detailed comments on the proposed directive(2021/0425 (COD))
Please see comments for the proposed gas market regulationin a separate document
Articles 31-33 and 62-64(General rules for the hydrogen-and gas markets)
Energinet welcomes that the general competition rules from the gas market are introduced to
the hydrogen regulation. It is important that unbundling will also apply to the hydrogen mar-
ket, to ensure full separation between production and transport of hydrogen. Energinet also
supports the Commission’s suggestion on regulated third-party access for dedicated hydrogen
networks and storage to ensure a transparent market and non-discrimination between market
operators.
Energinet supports that gas system operators may also operate hydrogen systems, given that
there will be horizontal unbundling. Energinet also supports that ACER will be tasked with
providing guidelines for the valuation of assets that are transferred from the gas grid to the hy-
drogen grid. The valuation should be based on the market value of the assets toavoid indirect
cross-subsidies. Cross-subsidies should not be an acceptable way to finance future hydrogen
networks.

Articles 10-25 (Consumer protection and retail market)  
Energinet supports a general roll-out of smart meters for natural gas customers only if the 
cost-benefit analysis provides a positive result. For hydrogen Energinet believes that it is ac-
ceptable to require roll-out of smart meters for all consumers, as it is expected to only affect a 
small number of large consumers that in any case will need new meters. 
 
Regarding certification of green gasses and low carbon fuels there are no rules regarding dis-
closure of the origin of green gases to final consumers. This should be changed to ensure a 
similar approach across green energy markets.  
 
Articles 46 -50 (Rules applicable to the dedicated hydrogen networks) 
It is important to ensure both efficient long-term network planning (including integrated cross-
sectoral planning between hydrogen network operators and electricity and natural gas TSOs) 
and efficient operation of a hydrogen network. Therefore, the responsibilities and the tasks 
must be clearly defined. In Member States with more than one designated hydrogen network 
operator, it might be most efficient to transfer certain tasks from one operator to another in 
order to ensure overall efficiency. Therefore, it is proposed to give Member States the possibil-
ity to transfer one or more of the tasks mentioned in Article 46 to one of the designated opera-
tors if they consider it relevant to ensure efficient long-term cross-sectoral planning and opera-
tion. Such a provision could be laid down in a new article 46 (4).  
 
Articles 51-53 (Integrated planning)  
Energinet supports the requirement for gas TSOs to update national 10-year network develop-
ment plans for the gas system every two years. However, it is unclear if a single plan per mem-
ber state is required, or if multiple plans are allowed. The latter case would be inefficient. It is 
also unclear what the connection is between the 10-year network development plans and the 
reports that hydrogen network operators must send to the national regulator. 
 
 
Energinet will be happy to provide further elaboration on the above-mentioned issues, but also 
to engage in the discussions of other aspects of the proposed directive if that would be relevant 
for the European Commission or other stakeholders.

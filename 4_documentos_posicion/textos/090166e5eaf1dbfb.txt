HYDROGEN AND DECARBONISED GAS PACKAGE (GAS
MARKET REGULATION 2021/0424 (COD)) –
RESPONSE TO EC CONSULTATION
General remarks
Energinet welcomes the proposal from the European Commission (EC), noting that the market-
based approach is largely maintained, thatthere is focus on establishing regulation for the fu-
ture hydrogen market and that some efforts have been pointed towardsgreen gasses.
Energinet warmly welcomes that the general principles from the gas (and the electricity) market,
such as unbundling andregulated third-party access are-for most parts-transferred to the hy-
drogen market. Additionally, Energinet finds it positive that it is clarified that gas system opera-
tors may also operate hydrogen systems.
Still Energinet is also concerned about some aspects of the proposal. Most prominently Ener-
ginet finds,that the ECmisses the opportunity to remove technical barriers for free flows of bio-
methaneacross member state borders. In 2021, biomethane already accounted for 21 percent
of the Danish gas consumption (and on top of this 5%points ofbiogaswas consumed “off grid”
i.e. in direct consumption). This trend is expected to increase significantly over the coming
years. As first movers in the area we already experience that we need to divertcross-border
flows due to national technical gas qualitystandardsfrom other member states.
To solve this, the updatedregulation should include a strong political mandate to demolish na-
tionally induced barriers for cross border-flows with high biomethaneshares. Not at least in
the contrast to the EC effort to promote hydrogen blending, it is regrettable that equal political
courage and effort has notbeen mobilized in the legislative proposal, when it comes to pro-
moting blending of biomethane into the existing natural gas system.
As clearly presented in thesubsequently published RePowerEU initiative, biomethane repre-
sents a unique and readily available opportunity to deliver on climate, security and supply and
toreduce import dependencies already in a 2030-timehorizon. Hence, we strongly recom-
mend amending the draft regulation to pave the way for a smooth and cost-effective integra-
tion of rapidly growing amounts of biomethane flowing across the borders of the EU member
states.

Detailed comments on various articles of the draft regulation 2021/0424 (COD) 
Please see our comments for the proposed directive in a separate note 
 
Articles 3-14: General rules for the hydrogen- and gas markets) 
Energinet welcomes that the general competition rules from the gas market are introduced to 
the hydrogen regulation. It is important that unbundling will also apply to the hydrogen mar-
ket, to ensure full separation between production and transport of hydrogen. Energinet also 
supports the Commission’s suggestion on regulated third-party access for dedicated hydrogen 
networks and storage to ensure a transparent market and non-discrimination between market 
operators. 
 
Energinet supports that gas system operators may also operate hydrogen systems, given that 
there will be horizontal unbundling. Energinet also supports that ACER will be tasked with 
providing guidelines for the valuation of assets that are transferred from the gas grid to the hy-
drogen grid. The valuation should be based on the market value of the assets to avoid indirect 
cross-subsidies. Cross-subsidies should not be an acceptable way to finance future hydrogen 
networks. 
 
Energinet note that long-term capacity contracts were necessary for the development of the 
gas grid to ensure a sustainable business model. Similarly, it might be necessary to guarantee a 
certain level of security for the investment for the infrastructure owner during t he develop-
ment of a hydrogen system. Yet, caution should be given to the dilemma that if the infrastruc-
ture owner demands long-term capacity contracts for the first operators that wish to connect 
to the hydrogen system, it may in itself become a barrier for the development of hydrogen in-
frastructure, given the uncertainties lack of maturity in the hydrogen market. If opting for long-
term capacity contracts it should – based on experience from the natural gas market - be safe-
guarded that they will not distort the competition in the market.  
 
Article 16: Tariff discounts for renewable and low carbon gases 
Energinet generally supports promoting green gasses in the existing gas system, however the 
current principles for cross-border tariffs should be maintained. 
 
While it is operationally possible to provide discounts when green gas enters the system from 
the production side, it is impossible in practice to do the same when it crosses borders, as the 
green gas has been mixed physically with natural gas. Energinet also notes that that the EC sug-
gest that shippers or other relevant parties must provide the respective TSO with a proof of 
sustainability, based on a valid sustainability certificate, to receive such a discount. However, 
such a certificate is not related to the physical cross-border flows in the gas system for which 
the tariff is charged.  
 
Also, a 100 % tariff discount at all interconnection points for renewable and low-carbon gases, 
will result in higher tariffs at exit points. To justify that Member State A should provide free ac-
cess to green gases from Member State B, an accounting system must be established. This sys-
tem cannot be designed objectively, and it will be very difficult for TSOs to agree on a financial 
compensation system - which naturally will be a zero-sum game.  
 
Under the assumption that the proposals in this article have been created to indirectly subsi-
dize biomethane, we suggest exploring more transparent and direct ways of doing so.

Article 17: Benchmarking of gas-TSOs 
Energinet supports the development of comparable benchmarks between European gas TSOs. 
Today, national regulators and ENTSOG already develop benchmarks. To avoid double work it 
is important that the new benchmark regime replaces the existing regimes instead of adding 
additional layers. It is also important that the methodology developed for benchmarks can op-
erate independently of the different corporate structures for TSOs that exist in different mem-
ber states. 
 
Articles 19 and 20: Gas quality and hydrogen blending 
As voiced in the introduction, Energinet is concerned regarding the proposed rules governing 
gas quality and specifically the framework around biomethane induced oxygen content ac-
cepted at cross border interconnection points. The proposed article 19 largely repeats existing 
procedures and although increased involvement from national regulators and ACER is positive, 
it is problematic that the specific procedures are only set in network codes at a later stage. This 
leads to a high degree of uncertainty in combination with long timelines where all national 
technical considerations could be weighted equally important.   
 
The potential supply crisis and the climate targets call for a new approach which – as a matter 
of priority - prepares the EU natural gas system for rapid integration of biomethane and facili-
tates unhindered cross-border gas flows. Action is needed on three levels and can be ensured 
by a new dedicated article i.e., an article 20(a) covering the following aspects:  
 
1. Overarching political mandate 
 Prohibit Member States from restricting cross-border flows of biomethane and other 
green gases. 
 Clear wording that local sensitivity issues must be handled nationally and not be 
pushed to the borders.  
2. Facilitative operational framework  
 Oblige TSO’s to – as a minimum - accept gas flows at interconnection points with oxy-
gen contents equal to the highest threshold allowed within their national high-pres-
sure networks.  
3.  Update of technical standards  
 Mandate the European Commission to task the European Committee for Standardiza-
tion to assess and update the quality standard for cross-border gas to ensure that it 
supports a significant green transition of the gas system.   
 
In general, Energinet is concerned and somewhat puzzled regarding the distorted focus and 
political prioritization between biomethane and hydrogen in terms of gas quality related as-
pects of blending and ultimately decarbonization of the natural gas system. The requirement 
to make the gas system ready to receive flows of 5% hydrogen across borders in 2025 is as-
sessed to be disproportional, as there is expected to only be a very small amount of hydrogen 
in the system in the near future. A premature acceptance threshold will require upgrading of 
measurement and management systems before it is operationally relevant and will conse-
quently lead to undue investments, which will be financed by the gas consumers. 
 
Article 33: Firm capacity for renewable and low carbon gases to the distribution system

concerned that the specific wording in the Commission’s proposal is unnecessarily rigid. We 
suggest making a direct reference to socioeconomic assessments as a guiding criterion and to

Articles 40-47 - European Network of Network Operators for Hydrogen 
The European hydrogen market will likely not be mature by 2024/25, and most member states 
will not have built hydrogen infrastructure yet, nor have assigned a hydrogen system operator. 
Due to this, establishing ENNOH within this time frame seems premature. 
 
Energinet recommends that a separate European association for hydrogen operators should 
only be established when there is a critical mass of operators, to ensure that the association 
will be able to represent broader European interests. Until then the responsibilities should be 
placed with ENTSOG in a separate unit, that can be formed into its own association at later 
point. Objective criteria should be established for this and could be enshrined in the regulation 
as relevant.  
 
In this context, it is also noted that hydrogen network planning with the revised TEN-E regula-
tion is already an integrated part of the TYNDP process for gas, which ENTSOG is responsible 
for. Modelling of hydrogen supply and demand is already included in the TYNDP 2022 scenar-
ios, developed by ENTSOG and ENTSOE. 
 
Article 54: Network codes for hydrogen 
Energinet assesses that it is premature to decide that network codes for gas can be adapted to 
hydrogen. The dynamics of the hydrogen system is more dependent on the dynamics of the 
electrical grid, where there is a great need for flexibility. Energinet expects the use of the hy-
drogen system to be significantly different than that of the gas system, where there are consid-
erable seasonal changes in consumption, a large retail market, and a stable gas supply with a 
few, central sources. The hydrogen market is expected to primarily become a B2B -market with 
stable demand over the year, with fluctuations in production over shorter intervals, and pro-
duction from central and decentral sources. The process of setting European network codes 
should not be forced, but instead developed along with the development of the market, and 
the practical experiences of operating a cross-border hydrogen network. 
 
Article 60: Exemption rules 
Energinet believes caution should be exercised for time-limited exceptions in general, and they 
should be avoided entirely for unbundling. 
 
Article 67: Changes regarding gas security of supply 
Energinet in general supports focusing on gas storage and securing more transparency on this 
area. Energinet also suggests involving market actors more actively to ensure that gas storages 
are sufficiently supplied. Article 7b can be used as a leverage for a general discussion of how to 
secure adequate storage via a balance between regulation and market-based incentives. 
 
Energinet finds it highly important that joint procurement for strategic stocks remain voluntary, 
and that the regulation does not limit the individual Member State’s ability to procure strategic 
storage capacity, even if the regional risk assessment does not indicate a need for it. Energinet 
notes that the solidarity requests require a very comprehensive and detailed process, and it is 
difficult to understand the complete scope, relevance, and proportionality in practice. 
 
Regarding cyber security, Energinet appreciate that the proposal strengthens the prioritization 
of the issue and support better cooperation across borders.

Energinet generally supports that the scope of the regulation has been expanded to include re-
newable and low carbon gases, as particularly biomethane production will be an important 
component to ensure security of supply in the short and medium timeframe. Yet, when looking 
to wording and the background of the current SoS regime a notable change is that most bio-
methane is injected into the distribution network, hence creating a bottom -up flow working 
within the boundaries created by the technical constraints of reverse flow capacity between 
TSO and DSO level. To this end Energinet suggests ensuring that the SoS-regulation more explic-
itly reflects the changed dynamics of a greener gas system.  
 
An example where explicit clarification would be helpful is in article 13. In a crisis situation 
where Denmark is asked for solidarity by another Member State, the rather clear and straight 
forward operation in the “conventional gas system” would be to shut off all or a part of the 
customers, who are not solidarity protected. Yet in a gas system with biomethane injections at 
DSO-level there can be situations where biomethane could be available for consumption in a 
specific DSO area but cannot be transferred to the TSO-gas system due to the socioeconomi-
cally balanced technical capacity of the reverse flow infrastructure 1. Therefore, the SoS-regime 
need to take into account that the gas system will include so-called “biomethane pockets”, 
where also costumers who are not solidarity protected in specific DSO-areas could be supplied 
with residual gas unavailable for cross border solidarity purposes as a result of the reverse flow 
capacity not being dimensioned for supply crises. In the same manner there could be scenarios 
where Member states with “biomethane pockets” would need solidarity to supply solidarity 
protected costumers in one DSO-area, although there would still be gas available for all or a 
part of the customers who are not solidarity protected in other separate parts of the DSO-
system due to a limited reverse flow capacity. 
While it must surely be the overall intention of the regulation that all gas should be utilized in a 
crisis situation, we suggest clarifying the text further to avoid unnecessary legal unclarity and 
possible scope for disputes between member states.  
 
On a more general note, Energinet suggests that the regulation could leave the rules regarding 
protected, solidarity protected, and unprotected consumers less detailed, giving member states 
more freedom to choose which consumers to protect. This means that in countries where e.g., 
gas for household heating is less important, it would be possible to protect e.g., industries in-
stead.

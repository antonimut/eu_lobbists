GIE feedback to public consultation on the recast of the Gas 
Directive 
Introduction 
 
Gas Infrastructure Europe (GIE), representing almost 70 European companies operating transmission 
pipelines, storage facilities and LNG terminals, shares the European Commission’s (EC) objectives of 
improving the gas market framework and to decarbonise the gas system. The future integrated energy 
system fulfilling the European Green Deal’s objectives will consist of renewable electricity a nd 
renewable or low -carbon gases, supported by an extensive infrastructure ensuring effective 
competition and security of supply, hence a higher level of independency from natural gas delivering 
countries.  
 
Replacing natural gas by biomethane or synthetic methane is one of the solutions for gas markets to 
decarbonise as the existing midstream and downstream infrastructure can be used without changes. 
However the uptake of biomethane requires an effective pan -European certification framework  in 
order for customers to fully decarbonise their consumption. The package is expected to improve the 
current framework, but some further changes could make it even more effective. 
 
Hydrogen appears more and more as a key decarbonised energy vector for the future. The current gas 
infrastructure – long-distance transmission and regional distribution transportation, short-term and 
seasonal storage, import facilities – can be repurposed to transport, store or import hydrogen at a 
lower cost than building brand -new infrastructure. This is an opportunity for Europe: as hydrogen 
markets develop from industrial clusters, natural gas infrastructure could be repurposed progressively 
to transport, store and import hydrogen, converging towards a pan-European hydrogen backbone. 
The Package proposes a comprehensive framework for hydrogen markets, access to hydrogen 
infrastructure, and the repurposing of natural gas asse ts to ensure  a fast deployment. Using its 
experience in natural gas, GIE proposes improvements to the package that could deliver hydrogen 
markets even faster and cheaper , among others by fully using the synergies with the natural gas 
infrastructure. 
 
 
1. Towards an integrated energy system 
 
As stated in the “EU strategy on energy system integration” published by the EC in July 2020 1, sector 
integration will make it easier to optimise and modernise the EU’s energy system as a whole. The gas 
directive states that “the joint operation of hydrogen networks and gas or electricity grids can create 
synergies and should thus be allowed”. Even though  the package contains several measures that 
promote synergies, other provisions could make them more effective. 
 
Numerous studies and initiatives of gas infrastructure operators in the last few years have 
demonstrated that natural gas infrastructure could be repurposed to hydrogen at a lower cost than 
building brand-new infrastructure. It would be efficient to recognise that the activities of gas TSOs and 
hydrogen network operators (HNOs) are very similar and that synergies should be promoted.  
Therefore, the additional horizontal unbundling provision on the legal form preventing gas TSOs to

Furthermore, the concept of combined operator should allow one single entity to carry out several 
natural gas and hydrogen activities: gas transmission, gas distribution, LNG regasification, gas storage,

The package proposes two separate  market organisation s for hydrogen and natural gas : The 
hydrogen network operator has similar role s and responsibilities as the gas transmission system 
operator. However, the proposed requirements applicable to one system are in many aspects 
different from those applicable to the other and, unfortunately, the differences are of such nature as 
to prevent the two systems from benefitting from their natural synergies and hence, increase the costs 
to the public. Furthermore, the distinction between transmission and distribution level and their 
established commercial relationships has worked well in the elect ricity and natural gas market and 
should therefore also be applied to the hydrogen system, espacially since a large share of SME 
industrial customers are connected to the distribution grid. GIE is of the opinion that many elements 
of  the natural gas frame work serve as a good basis, after 20 years of improvements towards an 
efficient liberalised pan-European gas market. 
 
Both the EC and EU regulators have confirmed that the current unbundling models for energy 
infrastructure have prove n to function well. Therefore, all three vertical unbundling models  
(ownership unbundling, independent system operator and independent transmission operator) 
should be extended to hydrogen network operators, guaranteeing a level playing field across Member 
States. The restriction to ownership unbundling would prevent gas TSOs under the ITO model in 
numerous Member States from owning and operating a hydrogen network after 2030. This would 
have the immediate practical effect of disincentivising their investments into hydrogen and undermine 
the creation of a pan-European hydrogen backbone.  
 
 
2. Fostering renewable and low-carbon gases and hydrogen in infrastructure and markets 
 
GIE welcomes the inclusion of low -carbon gases and low-carbon hydrogen within the future gas and 
hydrogen market framework. However the methodology to calculate the 70 % greenhouse gas saving 
threshold would not be determined before the end of 2024. This u ncertainty would prevent market 
players to deploy technologies that decarbonise gases or hydrogen, because they are not certain they 
would qualify as “low-carbon” gases or hydrogen. GIE asks to clarify the methodology to calculate the 
70 % greenhouse gas saving threshold embedded within the definitions for low-carbon gases and low-
carbon hydrogen in the Directive.  
 
The certification system should be well-adapted to the functioning of energy markets. For the gas and 
hydrogen markets, it means that the information on the origin and sustainability characteristics of 
renewable and low-carbon gases and hydrogen should not be physically assigned to gas mixes flowing 
in the European gas infrastructure and could be documented in certificates traded separately from 
the gas commodities. The Guarantees of Origin system has been in use for more than ten years, which 
means that its application to low -carbon gases would require minimal administrative or financial 
resources. 
In this context, and also in the context of the introduction of the Union Database, it is important to 
consider the gas infrastructure as a single logistical facility, composing of transmission and distribution 
networks as well as underground gas storage sites and LNG terminals.

3. Delivering hydrogen infrastructure and markets 
 
As hydrogen is to become a key decarbonised energy carrier of the future EU energy system, the 
development of hydrogen infrastructure and markets is the cornerstone of the package. GIE welcomes 
the measures proposed to incentivise transport, storage and import infrastructure along with market 
developments, in order to support the growing demand, from unconnected industrial clusters to a full 
pan-European hydrogen system and market. 
 
Some definitions should be clarified to match the exact scope of the proposals. For instance,  
“hydrogen storage facility” should refer to large hydrogen storage facilities but not those gas storage 
facilities receiving only minor amounts of hydrogen blending . Terminals re ceiving hydrogen  and 
“hydrogen supply” should include  relevant hydrogen carriers  for the storage and transport of 
hydrogen over long distances. 
   
In order to foster the repurposing of natural gas storage into hydrogen storage facilities , existing 
authorisation procedures for storage facilities should also apply to the storage of hydrogen , as is 
proposed for transmission and import terminals. 
 
In principle, the repurposing of existing storage facilities and the building of new hydrogen stor age 
infrastructure can be classified as a natural monopoly. A regulated Third-Party Access (TPA) regime 
is therefore justified to trigger the necessary investments and provide operators with a stable 
regulatory environment. At the same time, additional tools are needed to exceptionally deviate from 
the default rule (regulated TPA) for innovative business models or cross-border projects. 
  
With regards to the TPA regime for hydrogen networks, GIE supports the Commission’s pr oposal to 
establish a system of rTPA, while giving Member States the flexibility to implement a system of nTPA 
by 2030.   
 
 
GIE remains committed to working together with all stakeholders to improve the Hydrogen and Gas 
Market Decarbonisation Package in order to better fulfill the European Union’ objectives.

2 Detailed position on the G as and Hydrogen 
Decarbonisation Package 
This section contains Enagás detailed positions on the main aspects of the proposed 
Recast Directive and Regulation, summarised in the previous section: 
1. Vertical unbundling : the ownership unbundling  model should be 
favoured 
The OU model should be favoured in the long-term for both HNOs and gas TSOs since 
it’s the most effective one and entails less monitoring efforts by regulators.  
Enagás supports the proposal on certification models for HNOs. 
As regards natural gas, Enagás encourages to also foresee a transition for gas TSOs 
under the “Independent Transmission Operator” (ITO) model to OU by 31 Dec 2030, 
thus allowing all TSOs to combine both activities in the long-term and better aligning 
incentives in the context of repurposing.  
As explained in the Horizontal unbundling section below, allowing for combined TSOs 
and HNOs would enable the full attainment of synergies between the two grids 
acknowledged in Recital 68 of the proposed recast Directive, and  would set correct 
incentives for TSOs when deciding if and whether to repurpose gas pipelines into 
hydrogen pipelines.  This is particularly relevant given that most of the future 
hydrogen network would consist of repurposed assets. 
However, given that the temporary restriction placed on Integrated HNOs  would 
prevent gas network operators under the ITO model from owning and operating 
hydrogen networks after 2030, the investment in hydrogen networks of gas ITOs 
might be disincentivised, undermining the creation of a European hydrogen backbone 
system. Rather than relaxing the unbundling requites for HNOs, this problem would 
be solved by applying to gas TSOs the same rules envisaged for HNOs. 
Member States should also be able to allow the use of the independent system 
operator model for gas (ISO) and hydrogen (Independent HNO), allowing existing 
vertically integrated owners of hydrogen networks  to retain ownership of their 
networks while ensuring the non -discriminatory operation of such networks after 
2030. 
2. Tolling scheme s for operating renewable gases facilities should be 
compatible with the OU model 
The ownership and operation by HNOs and TSOs under tolling schemes of conversion 
facilities contributing to decarbonisation and enabling sectoral integration, such as 
P2G (converting electricity to hydrogen), biogas and biomethane (biogas upgrading) 
plants, should be fully compatible with unbundling as long as operators do not engage 
in trading or supply of energy (electricity, gas, hydrogen). 
This possibility should be included  in the proposed Directive  to facilitate 
decarbonisation. This sharing of the investment risks between the users and the 
operator will allow smaller market participants to enter this new market. 
Under a tolling scheme the owner and operator of the facility  provides energy 
conversion or other services and is only responsible for operating such facilities. Rules 
for gas and for H2, would be respected as the TSO or HNO, owning and operating 
such a facility, does not engage in commodity ownership or commercialisation.

Tolling schemes are a proven concept which has been used across different economic 
systems and industries. This sharing of the investment risks between the users and 
the operator will allow smaller market participants to enter this new market.  
These facilities should be regarded as sector -coupling infrastructure offering 
conversion or decarbonisation services, since power-to-gas is transforming electricity 
from a RES electricity producer, or any other electricity sources, into gas (i.e. 
hydrogen) for further use in the energy system. Power to Gas facilities will operate 
at the interface of the electricity and gas sector and there is no good reason to 
exclude gas TSOs from only owning and operating such facilities. 
While proposed amendment to Article 2 of the Gas Directive currently in force 
properly defines a tolling model, amendments to Article 54 of the proposed recast 
Directive should give TSOs the right to engage in decarbonisation related activities 
(corresponding amendments to Article 62 proposed recast Directive for HNOs) under 
the tolling model concept to provide access to conversion facilities such as 
electrolysers. 
3. Horizontal unbundling : no legal unbundling r equites should be 
established between TSOs and HNOs 
Legal unbundling between TSOs and HNOs is unnecessary, disproportionate and 
counterproductive, and may create unnecessary costs and administrative burdens 
and uncertainties. Combined operators (network, storage and terminal activities for 
gas and H2) sh ould be explicitly allowed, enabling the full attainment of synergies 
between the two grids acknowledged in Recital 68 of the proposal. 
Repurposing existing natural gas pipelines is widely accepted to be a quicker and 
more economical way to develop a hydrogen network, compared to the option of 
building new hydrogen assets. Repurposing costs of typical transmission pipelines 
are expected to  be between 0.4 and 0.5 €/kg/1,000km, vs. 2.2 and 4.8 
€/kg/1,000km for new lines. 1  Other sources estimate a repurposing cost of around 
25% the deployment of a fully-new network.2. 
It is therefore expected that a significant proportion of the future hydrogen network 
will be composed of repurposed gas assets. The 53,000 km European Hydrogen 
Backbone for 2040 requires an estimated total investment of €80 -143 billion, based 
on using 60% of repurposed natural gas pipelines and 40% new pipeline stretches. 1 
Given this inevitable interdependence between the assets of these two energy 
vectors, and in view of the similarities in terms of the skills, knowledge and resources 
that are required to develop and safely operate the gas and future hydrogen pipeline 
networks, Enagás considers that gas network operators should be able to also 
become hydrogen network operators.  This would allow the full attainment of 
synergies between the two grids, acknowledged in Recital 68 of the proposal: “the

The separation of Regulatory Asset Bases (RABs) (“accounting unbundling”) for 
natural gas and hydrogen networks as stipulated in Art. 64 of the proposed Directive 
ensures sufficient transparency to trace any financial transfer between natural g as 
and hydrogen under the close scrutiny of regulatory authorities and subject to their 
approval. An additional legal separation between HNOs and TSOs is therefore not 
proportionate and would only create unnecessary costs and administrative burden 
(e.g. complications regarding land -use authorisations in the process of transferring 
assets between the two legal entities).  
As such, Enagás proposes to delete horizontal unbundling of HNOs in Article 63 which 
would allow the creation of synergies between gas and hydrogen transportation 
activities in order to lead to cost savings for both gas and hydrogen to the benefit of 
the whole society. By removing legal separation, the following issues would be 
minimised: 
• Rising costs coming from having to change the structure of the company. For 
example, a maintenance team set in the gas company that would provide 
maintenance services to the hydrogen company.  
• Lengthy discussions in agreeing the transfer of assets from the gas to the 
hydrogen TSO, including issues regarding the transfer of rights of way and land -
use authorisations.  
Restricting the speed of development o f the hydrogen pipeline system because of a 
constant process to perform since it is envisaged to have a progressive transfer of 
asset as the hydrogen backbone will deployed step by step. 
4. ENTSOG role should be expanded to H2 activities 
ENTSOG scope should b e expanded to H2 activities (TYNDP, Network Codes, …) 
rather than creating the ENNOH. The latter would be up and running no sooner than 
2025, and would increase costs and complexity in the EU dialogue, introducing further 
fragmentation in the energy system, including the disintegration of gas and hydrogen 
modelling and planning activities . ENTSOG is ideally placed to assess investment 
needs at EU level since most of the future H2 network will be comprised of repurposed 
gas pipelines. Access rules and management will also be very similar to those of the 
gas network. 
Enagás calls for the full integration of hydrogen and gas network activities  allocated 
to ENTSOG and ENNOH in the recast Regulation in a single European entity , such as 
network planning, network code development and security of supply, especially while 
the hydrogen market remains immature. 
The extension of ENTSOG activities to hydrogen is particularly relevant in the context 
of energy system integration. Future TYNDPs should build on the synergies between 
gas and hydrogen systems to allow the EC and Regional Groups to select the PCI 
projects based on the total social -economic welfare of those projects on both 
systems.  
Separated TYNDPs will oblige ENTSOG to dismantle its fully integrated processes and 
its flow modelling to create silos between methane and hydrogen TYNDPs or Security 
of Supply outlooks, whereas those tasks can be kept fully integrated but supervised 
by both associations. The tasks of infrastructure planning assessment and security of 
supply should be fully integrated and performed by ENTSOG, reflecting the intrinsic

5. rTPA should be the default regime for H2 networks, while the possibility 
of nTPA should be limited in  time and subject to further predictability 
requisites at the time of opting for it 
Regulated TPA should be the default regime for H2 networks, limiting the possibility 
of negotiated TPA in time to 2030. Exemptions should be granted only in limited and 
duly justified cases, and should be  ceased as soon as the relevant criteria are no 
longer met or at a sunset date. 
The application of two different regimes, negotiated and regulated, to a single asset 
during its useful life create s investment barrier s if e nough visibility on the 
methodology to move from one regime to the other is not provided.  
Either the promoter will have to take an investment decision not backed by contracts 
for the duration of the asset life, or the users will have to sign a contract wi thout 
visibility on tariffs from 2031 onwards. The regulatory asset life will be of particular 
relevance to determine the tariff level to be paid by users. 
The most adequate situation would have to maintain the same regime during the 
whole life of the asset. Given the desirability to have in the mid -term a regulated 
regime with common rules for all infrastructures in place, it is of the utmost 
importance that if negotiated access is allowed until 2030, enough visibility on 
regulated conditions is provided at the time of opting for negotiated access. 
6. Financing H2 networks: temporary financial transfers between natural 
gas and H2 networks should be allowed for a longer period 
Temporary financial transfers between natural gas and H2 networks should be 
allowed, collecting the  dedicated charge mentioned in art . 4.2 of the proposed 
Regulation only at exit points to final customers located within the same Member 
State. The completion of the internal gas market has been hampered by the allocation 
of excessive costs to intra-EU IPs exits. 
The duration foreseen for capacity contracts is abnormally low for newly -built 
infrastructures. Unless infrastructure operators operate on a fully -regulated 
environment where cost recovery is guaranteed, they expect to be able to sign 
contracts with a sufficient duration as to guarantee costs recovery. Durations around 
20 years are common for energy infrastructures taking into account the level of 
investments involved. For an emergent industry as the transport of hydrogen, where 
costs are still high, a period of 15 years could be insufficient for promoters to adopt 
a final investment decision. 
Two possible solutions could be adopted: 
1. A guarantee by the regulator that the infrastructure cost will be recovered once 
the infrastructure becomes regulated in 2031. This option has practical difficulties 
and could entail delays in investment decisions until the regulator is ready to

7. Retrofitting and repurposing costs should be allowed by NRAs 
EU-level principles should be established to ensure that retrofitting and repurposing  
and costs are allowed by NRAs. 
In particular, the mandatory acceptance of gas flows with a H2 content of up to 5% 
by volume at IPs between EU Members from 1 October  2025 in some cases it might 
entail operational or investment costs for different infrastructure operators, 
depending on the chosen solution for its implementation. Since this requisite is 
essential to support the hydrogen market uptake in peripheral regions and to avoid 
market fragmentatio n, these costs, when borne by regulated infrastructure 
operators, should be allowed by regulatory authorities insofar as they correspond to 
those of an efficient and structurally comparable regulated operator. 
8. Tariff discounts  for renewable and low -carbon gases are useful and 
should not privilege imports from outside the EU vs. EU production 
Tariff discounts for renewable and low -carbon gases will support the uptake of the 
hydrogen market.  
Discounts should not privilege import s from outside the EU vs. EU production. A 
100% discount should be applied at entry points from renewable and low carbon 
production facilities. Introducing a discount lower than 100% for national entry points 
would discriminate these flows vs. the flows at entry points from third countries. This 
could disincentivize production within the European Union. 
Finally, zero-tariffs at IPs in pure-hydrogen networks support the development of an 
internal market for hydrogen, minimising price differentials between EU Members. 
9. A rapid development of an EU H2 network and infrastructures is key to 
meet REPowerEU goals 
The REPowerEU Communication, aimed at phasing out Europe’s dependence on fossil 
fuels from Russia well before 2030 and to increase the resilience of the EU -wide 
energy system, introduces an ambition to reach around 20 Mt of renewable hydrogen 
by 2030 (10 Mt of EU produ ction and 10 Mt coming from imports), vs. the 5.6 Mt 
foreseen under Fit for 55.  
The rapid development of an EU core hydrogen network (backbone) , together with 
hydrogen storage and port infrastructure, is key for escalating hydrogen production 
and achievin g an internal market for hydrogen, ensuring competitiveness and 
security of supply.

10.Integrated and harmonised planning requirements should be established 
The European Commission legislative proposal fall short of its stated ambition to 
better integrate  whole energy system , and to achieve a more interconnected  
infrastructure, based on a more holistic and inclusive approach. 
When it comes to network planning, it is effectively limited to common scenarios and 
some specific elements. ENTSOs are already devel oping joint scenarios at EU level. 
An integrated network planning approach would facilitate the cost -optimal 
development of the electricity , gas and hydrogen  (and potentially other)  systems, 
since it would capture the value generated by all infrastructures. Without coordinated 
planning of key infrastructures across electricity , gas and hydrogen , there is a 
substantial risk of inefficient investments. 
Similar planning requirements on all TSOs should be established to ensure 
consistency at national and EU level. 
11. Anchor in EU legislation quotas for renewable gases 
The REPowerEU  Communication introduces an ambition to reach around 20 Mt of 
renewable hydrogen by 2030 (10 Mt of EU production and 10 Mt coming from 
imports), vs. the 5.6 Mt foreseen under Fit for 55.  
The EC should fix policy actions aimed at arriving to renewable H2 and biomethane 
competitive prices as soon as possible to be able to reach the production targets set 
in the REPowerEU Communication. 
The introduction of EU quotas for renewable gases  in the present Package or in the 
RED II review , underpinned by adequate p olicy support mechanisms, deserve s 
further consideration.  
12. Gas and hydrogen quality management responsibilities of network 
operators should be defined in accordance with the tools at their disposal 
Enagás welcomes the inclusion of provisions on gas quality  (GQ) and hydrogen 
quality (HQ) handling, which reflect the need for TSOs or, of Member States so 
decide, HNOs, to manage the quality of gases to provide a smooth functioning of 
dedicated networks for methane and for hydrogen as well as the blending pathwa y, 
as relevant and necessary for users. 
The responsibility of network operators should be defined in accordance with the tools 
at their disposal for the management of GQ and HQ. 
For the blending pathway, TSOs are ready to accept the task of ensuring efficient GQ 
management in their facilities, in line with applicable gas quality standards, provided 
their investments and related costs are allowed by the regulatory framework.  
Enagás welcomes the European Commission view to establish a harmonised approach 
at Union level to hydrogen quality management at cross -border interconnectors, 
which should lead to the cross-border flow of hydrogen and to market integration.  
EASEE-gas has pioneered the discussion on HQ and has recently published its quality 
specification recommendation for industrial grade hydrogen running through 
repurposed natural gas pipelines or new dedicated systems (Common Business 
Practice 2022-001/01 (CBP)). Its recommendation of a hydrogen concentration equal 
or higher than 98 mol-% is a good basis for discussion.

13.Blending: the acceptance of gas flows with a 5% H2 content at IPs is 
essential to support the hydrogen market uptake , and coordination 
procedures leadtimes should be halved 
The mandatory acceptance of gas flows with a H2 content of up to 5% by volume at 
IPs between EU Members from 1 October 2025 is essential to support the hydrogen 
market uptake in peripheral regions and to avoid market fragmentation.  
However, the procedure described in article 19 may delay the acceptance of gas flows 
with a H2 content of up to 5% by volume at IPs between EU Members for around 3 
or 4 years just by showing a discrepancy. In order to avoid the undesirable effect on 
the hydrogen m arket uptake and on market integration that this would have, a 
reduction of leadtimes to a half is required. 
In some cases, it might entail operational or investment costs for different 
infrastructure operators, depending on the chosen solution for its imp lementation. 
These costs, when borne by regulated infrastructure operators, should be allowed by 
regulatory authorities insofar as they correspond to those of an efficient and 
structurally comparable regulated operator. 
The legislation should be more speci fic regarding the rights of TSOs to operate 
facilities for the blending and de -blending of gases and to provide the respective 
services, to ensure gas quality management.

Article 7d 
Joint procurement for strategic stocks 
1. Member States may set up a mechanism for the joint procurement 
of strategic stocks by transmission system operators as part of the 
preventive measures to ensure security of supply.  
The mechanism shall be designed in compliance with EU law, and 
competition rules, and in a way so that the strategic stocks can be 
used as part of the actions coordinated by the Commission in case of 
regional or Union emergency, as referred to in Article 12(3).  
The mechanism shall be open to participation of all transmission  
system operators within the Union who wish to join after its 
establishment. 
2. The participating Member States shall notify their intention to 
establish such mechanism to the Commission. The notification shall 
include the information necessary to assess t he compliance with this 
Regulation, such as the volume of gas to be purchased, the duration 
of the measure, the participating transmission system operators, the 
governance arrangements, the operating procedures and conditions 
for activation in an emergency situation. It shall also specify the costs 
and benefits expected.

Justification 
Enagás supports the proposed new SoS measures and tools as foreseen in the new regulation. However, Enagás considers potential 
challenges for TSOs being obliged to purchase the strategic stocks. Existing short-term bookings and tariff models could limit the financial 
capability and available free cash flows after paying TSOs´ operating expenses, capital expenditures, taxes, etc. This has to be taken into 
account when defining and implementing the new rules. To safeguard the TSOs’ financial stability and thus capability to fulfi l its tasks, a 
levy in combination with a pre -financing by member states could be app lied. A mechanism must be foreseen that enables TSOs to have 
enough resources to buy the gas and related infrastructure services (e.g., storage and transmission capacities), so that to the transmission 
system operator remuneration for any costs borne there in is ensured and no financial risk would arise from these obligations. These 
provisions would ensure that the measure is implemented and available on short notice if needed.  
Also, purchasing and accounting of strategic stocks, and the usage of which in ca se of emergencies, should be aligned with existing 
national and EU legislation.

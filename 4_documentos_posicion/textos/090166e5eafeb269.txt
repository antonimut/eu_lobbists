       Storengy 
 
 
Methane emissions reduction in the energy sector 
High-level recommendations by an underground (French and German) gas 
storage operator 
 
Storengy operates 20 underground gas storage facilities within the EU (France and Germany). 
Storengy is committed to pursuing and intensifying their contribution to reducing emissions. Our 
three companies in France, Germany and UK joined OGMP2.0 in 2021. 
We understand the emergency and support the deployment of an appropriate and efficient roadmap.  
Storage o perators have significantly decreased methane emissions since 1990 . In France this 
corresponds to 34% of methane emissions reduction between 2015 and 2021 . To go further,  
behavioural changes and substantial investments are needed. 
Here are our recommendations for a successful proposal: 
1. Principle of proportionality should be considered.  
Regulation should strike a careful balance between the measures and the expected benefits. The 
general mitigation obligation should be limited by rationality. Mitigation efforts shall not directly 
or indirectly lead to a higher warming effect than the corresponding saved methane emissions.  
This principle of proportionality between the efforts and the abatement of methane emissions 
should be considered in different articles. 
 
2. Costs of storage operators:  
Gas companies should have a multi-year methane emissions mitigation plan, validated by Competent 
Authorities, to allow prioritization of the most cost-effective mitigation measures. 
We welcome the Regulation proposal about recognition of investments and operating costs incurred 
by regulated infrastructure operators. Nevertheless, gas storage is not under cost regulation in every 
country of Europe. The efforts to monitor and reduce methane emissions will exceed potential 
savings. The compensation of investments and efforts of non-regulated operators should be also 
guaranteed through European and national incentives.  
 
3. Monitoring, Reporting and Verification (MRV):  
We support the implementation of a transparent and robust MRV system. OGMP 2.0 is currently a 
clear and solid reference. In the future, European Standards, in line with this first reference, will 
define precisely the framework of this reporting. 
The Regulation should consider not only “measurements”  to report methane emissions, but also 
engineering calculations, simulation tools and emission factors. In many cases, direct measurements 
are not technically or safely feasible or don’t lead to a higher accuracy. 
With regards to top-down/site-level measurements and data reconciliation, these methodologies are 
not mature enough for quantifying methane emissions to a sufficient level of certainty. Therefore, we

do not support  the inclusion of obligations on top-down/site-level measurements and data 
reconciliation in the Regulation  at this stage . They are to be performed on  a voluntary basis until 
standardized methodologies are available. 
 
4. Inspection/ verifiers and International Methane Emissions Observatory (IMEO)  
The industry acknowledges the importance of inspection and verification to ensure the quality of the 
methane emission reporting system.   
Considering the high quality of technical standards and skills of some operators, it may be reasonable 
to consider an accreditation scheme for qualified teams of operators , if the independence of their 
organization is insured and regularly controlled.  
The national authorities and the verifiers represent two levels of control  on the data provided by 
operators.  
IMEO´s role in conveying the global dimension of methane emissions and ensuring a level playing field 
for all stakeholders is of high importance.  
Nevertheless, we recommend not to include a verification role for the IMEO , as a third level of 
control. More over the legal status of this organization does not seem very well adapted to be an 
accountable verifier. 
 
5. Leak Detection and Repair (LDAR) 
We support LDAR as one of the main instruments for methane emission reduction.  
We recommend not to set strictly defined intervals for LDAR survey s, but define them in the 
methane emissions mitigation plan , which also should include adequate detection thresholds . This 
approach will guarantee reliable performance by identifying the main emissions to prioritize the 
action. 
 
6. Venting and flaring 
We clearly support the prioritization to recover the gas instead of venting or flaring and the 
prioritization of flaring over venting. 
 
7. Reporting/Timing for  implementation 
The reporting associated with LDAR and venting / flaring should be the subject of annual reports, and 
double reporting should be avoided.  
Timing for the implementation of technical solutions must be considered as well as the definition of 
thresholds allowing the prioritization of methane emission sources.

Input Methane Regulation Proposal 
 
Emerson supports an EU Methane Regulation, with common rules for the EU market that facilitate a 
framework for measurement and reduction of emissions.  
Emerson believes that the technology to recover  methane emissions and repurpose it into energy  
shall have separate consideration for the oil, gas and coal sectors , including a reference t o the 
infrastructure required for realization .  Legislation is the starting point to drive, to focus and to 
implement operational actions. Prescriptive mitigation requirements aren’t sufficient ; economic 
incentives are necessary to promote faster and more widespread adoption. Methane recovery should 
be appropriately incentivized to ensure emissions mitigation is accelerated and serves as an additional 
and useful source of energy and/or revenue. The recovery of emissions is also the most efficient way 
to ensure that methane emissions are not ultimately vented to atmosphere.  
In line with these comments, Emerson proposes the following to be added to the regulation. 
Inclusion of Methane Recovery and Repurposing in Oil, Gas and Coal establishments 
The proposed regulation covers methane monitoring, mitigation, leak detection and capturing across 
oil, gas, and coal sectors. Emerson recommends adding a section on recovering methane emissions 
and converting these otherwise emitted gases into value added forms of energy such as Renewable 
Natural Gas derived from agricultural digesters and landfill waste gas, Compressed Natural Gas, and 
Hydrogen.  
In the Annex to the regulation , more details could be provided on Reco very and repurposing 
applications such as:  
• Recovery and utilization of vented gas: capture of associated gas from oil wells; blowdown 
capture; recovery and utilization of vented gas with vapor recovery units and well plungers.  
• Production of Compression N atural Gas  (CNG) and L iquefied Natural Gas (LNG)  for 
transportation purposes 
• Greening the gas transmission and distribution system by injection of low carbon fuels like 
renewable natural gas and hydrogen 
• Transformation of methane to hydrogen or other low carbon biofuels like methanol

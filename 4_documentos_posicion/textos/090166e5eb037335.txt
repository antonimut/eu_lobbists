ENGIE position - 
Regulation on methane emissions reduction in the energy sector 
 
1. ENGIE’s contribution to the reduction of methane emissions 
ENGIE group is committed to pursue and intensify its contribution to the reduction of methane emissions. 
• Gas infrastructure operators of ENGIE group (namely Elengy, GRDF, GRTgaz, Storengy) are targeting 
an overall reduction of 45% in methane emissions from infrastructure  between 2016 and 2025 , 
increasing to 60-75% by 2030, compared with 2016. Methane emissions have already been reduced 
by 15% between 2016 and 2019.  
• GRTgaz cut its methane emissions by 67% between 2016 and 2020 . Meanwhile, GRDF is continuing 
its ambitious project to modernize the distribution network, including a regulator maintenance plan 
leading to decrease methane emissions.  
• In 2016, methane emissions related to all gas infrastructures in France accounted for 0.26% of total 
national greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions and less than 0.2% of gas consumption. 
• Elengy, GRDF, GRTgaz, Storengy  joined in November 2020 the « Oil & Gas Metha ne Partnership 
(OGMP) 2.0 ». 
• The gas infrastructure operators also contribute to the development of renewable gases  which 
makes possible to reduce methane emissions from other sectors of activity such as agriculture or 
waste. 
In its first annual report on the International Methane Emissions Observatory (IMEO) in November 2021, the 
United Nations Environment Programme acknowledged the credibility of all ENGIE gas operator’s programmes 
to reduce methane emissions  ranking them as “gold standard” companies; GRTgaz therefore proposed a 
reporting system classed at mainly level 4 on a 5-point scale. 
 
2. ENGIE position on the Commission’s proposal 
Methane emissions occurring within the European Union 
• ENGIE welcomes the Commission’s proposal as we support the implementation of a transparent and 
robust initiative in order to reduce methane emissions . However, it should be aligned with the 
ambitious reporting standard OGMP 2.0, without redefining key conc epts. A strong misalignment 
with the OGMP 2.0 approach would lead to additional workload of local authorities, which would need 
to handle the additional requirements from EU legislation and confuse the global and comparable 
approach of OGMP. 
• Regulation should strike a careful balance between the measures and related costs foreseen in this 
regulation and the expected benefits.  
• One-size-fits-all actions will not be able to produce an effective methane emission reduction , as 
different assets, system configurations and operational conditions require quite different approaches 
to ensure the optimal cost efficiency.

• ENGIE welcomes the Regulation proposal about recognition of investments and operating costs 
incurred by regulated infrastructure operators. The compensation of investments and efforts of non-
regulated operators should be also guaranteed through European and national incentives.   
• Concerning Leak Detection and Repair (LDAR): the Regulation should take into account the different 
practices successfully used by the operators and especially: 
o not define fixed interval for the surveys to be performed, 
o give flexibility for the delay to fix the leaks when immediate repairs is not possible because of 
operational issues including technical, safety, environmental and administrative constraints.  
• Concerning Monitoring, Reporting and Verification (MRV): 
o The Regulation should consider not only “measurements”, but also engineering calculations, 
simulation tools and specific emission factors . In many cases, direct measurements are 
neither feasible nor lead to a higher data accuracy in comparison with those methods.  
o Site-level quantification has a comparatively (with source level) low technology readiness level 
(TRL). Therefore, we do not support the inclusion of obligations on top -down/site-level 
measurements and data reconciliation in the Regulation. 
 
Methane emissions occurring outside the EU 
• It may be difficult for importers to oblige exporters to provide certain types of information, especially 
within existing contractual arrangements , and for them to verify the qua lity of the information 
provided. In certain cases, it may not even be possible for an EU importer to identify a single exporting 
producer. For that reason, EU importers cannot be held liable for elements outside their control or 
outside the EU’s jurisdiction. The responsibility for the data quality should remain with the exporter.

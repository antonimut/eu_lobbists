Edison’s response to the consultation on the Proposal for a Regulation to recast the Regulation No 
715/2009 as regards the internal markets for renewable and natural gases and for hydrogen 
 
As a leading energy company with more than 130 years of history and an ambitious and forward -looking 
strategy centered around innovation and sustainability, Edison welcomes the new EU climate ambition 
reflected in the Green Deal proposals, being aware that a profound rethinking of some of the main features 
of the European economy will be required. Edison plans to increase its installed renewable capacity from the 
current 2 GW to 5 GW by 2030 through investments of 3 billion euros and aims to boost investment in storage 
solutions to accompany electrification . Edison is also consolidating its presence in the area of energy 
efficiency and environmental services.  Furthermore, Edison recognizes the central role of gas es in the 
energy transition, both in balancing  intermittent renewable energy in the power sector and in 
decarbonizing hard-to-abate sectors such as heavy-duty vehicles and maritime transport.  
 
There is no doubt that gas needs to be progressively decarbonized . Hydrogen and biogases will have a 
pivotal role in decarbonizing the gas sector to achieve  carbon neutrality by 2050 . In this context,  Edison 
welcomes and supports the European Commission’s initiative to revise the gas market regulatory 
framework to accelerate the growth in the production and use of hydrogen, biogas and other decarbonized 
gases. At the same time, the unprecedented situation in natural gas markets – with record high prices and 
efforts to minimize dependency on Russian gas as a result of the crisis in Ukraine – calls for a fresh assessment 
of Europe’s approach to security of supply and affordability of gas procurement, while preserving market 
principles. 
 
In the field of decarbonized gases, Edison has been the first operator enabled to off-take biomethane from 
the Italian national network and is developing an integrated value chain for the production and sale of 
biomethane in Italy. Edison is also in an excellent position to become an important player in the emerging 
hydrogen market thanks to its presence across the future hydrogen value chain , from the production of 
renewables to the sale of molecules to customers. The company is developing several integrated projects. In 
2021, an agreement was signed with T enaris and Snam to launch a project aimed at decarbonizing the 
Tenaris steel plant in Dalmine (Northern Italy) . The company is also part of the joint development of the 
Puglia Green Hydrogen Valley in Southern Italy, one of the first large-scale green hydrogen production and 
transportation projects in Italy. Besides, Edison’s new Combined Cycle Gas Turbines (CCGTs) have been built 
to be able to burn blends of hydrogen and a feasibility study has been launched in February 2022 to use 
green hydrogen at Marghe ra Levante . Furthermore, the EastMed-Poseidon pipeline  (included in the 
European Clean Hydrogen Alliance project database) – which will provide much-needed diversification from 
Russian gas – is ‘hydrogen-ready’ and could transport blends of hydrogen from the European Neighbourhood 
to the EU when clean molecules will become available in future. Finally, in 2021, Edison has inaugurated the 
first coastal LNG deposit in Italy as part of an integrated small scale LNG value chain aimed at significantly 
reducing emissions in heavy duty vehicles and in the maritime sector. This type of investment is an enabler 
of the uptake of biogases (and bioLNG in particular) , favouring the transition away from a status quo 
dominated by heavy fuel oil. 
 
Overall, Edison welcomes the general approach and policy direction of the Hydrogen and Decarbonised 
Gas Markets Package which will be a fundamental tool to accelerate the decarbonization of the gas system. 
In the upcoming legislative process, the company will fully support the role of this legislative initiative in 
preserving and enhancing market competition while ensuring security of supply over the lo ng term. As a 
contribution to the ongoing legislative process, a series of considerations on the main provisions proposed 
in the recast of the gas regulation are listed below.

Edison welcomes the fact that, along the principles of unbundling  and third-party access, the Gas Package 
ensures the separation of RABs between the electricity, gas and hydrogen asset bases. Indeed, the financing 
of the new hydrogen network should respect the overarching cost reflectivity and beneficiary -pays 
principles so that network costs and investments are paid by the consumers who actually benefit from the 
transport services offered by the network.  
 
Even if the Regulation states that cross-subsidies from a regulated asset to another are incompatible with 
cost-reflective tariffs, Article 4 (2) asserts that financial transfers between regulated assets are allowed for a 
transition period  if approved by the regulator . Edison believes that there should be n o cross -subsidies 
among different types of network users . Consumers  of hydrogen will be mainly industrial users , and 
household or industrial  consumers who will not shift to hydrogen consumption should not bear 
disproportionate costs. Furthermore, the fact that national regulatory authorities have discretion in 
approving financial transfers risks creating fragmentation in the EU market.  
 
Edison supports the intention of the Commission to provide measures that incentivize the promotion of 
renewable and low -carbon gases. However, t here are considerations to be mad e on the proposed tariff 
discounts, especially with regard to the potential impacts on the bills of final consumer s. Discounts to 
network access tariffs will in fact require network operators to allocate somewhere else the costs that are 
not charged to decarbonized gases at the injection point. Such costs will be transferred to the exit point of 
the network, and hence paid by final gas consumers , who will not necessarily benefit from the use of 
decarbonized gases. The same argument is valid for the proposed 100% discounts at interconnection points, 
where it is not clear who will bear the cost of this measure and if certain categories of consumers will be 
disproportionately charged. Edison believes that the development of clean gases should be  incentivized 
through ad-hoc financial measures and that access to networks should respect the principle of technology 
neutrality. In case a measure such as tariff discounts would be implemented, full transparency on who bears 
direct costs must be ensured. 
 
The revision of the gas regulation should also assess if there are any potential indirect costs that could arise 
from incentivizing decarbonized gases (similarly to what had happened with high  grid balancing costs 
resulting from the growth of incentivized solar PV plants). It is hence necessary to weight such indirect costs 
as they could also be transferred disproportionally to certain categories of final consumers. Overall, the 
revision of the Gas Package should provide measures that guarantee full transparency on the direct and 
indirect costs of incentives, providing a clear assessment on who bears the costs of decarbonization.  
 
Given the nascent state of the hydrogen market, Edison is prioritizing investment in the production of green 
hydrogen near centers of consumption . As opposed to large investments in the repurposing of 
infrastructure, these types of projects would effectively meet the potential demand of key (industrial) 
consumers while avoiding the technical issues posed by  blending and large -scale repurposing of 
infrastructure.  
 
Another element of attention is related to the techni cal feasibility of r equiring TSOs to accept up to 5% 
hydrogen-blended gas from 2025 at interconnection points. This measure indirectly leads to percentages of 
blending in the gas system of Member States, and this will result in complications and costs for the rest of 
the system . Indeed, even percentages of blending lower than 5% c ould potentially cause problems for 
appliances, storage facilities and industrial consumers . Therefore, Edison encourages the implementation 
of measures that ensure the protection of consumers and infrastructural counterparts. It is also important 
to underline that non-regulated market players will not be able to charge infrastructural adaptation costs in

their tariff structures. In this sense it wi ll be necessary to ensure that also regulated operators internalize 
the adaptation costs derived from blending, or to establish a framework that compensates non-regulated 
operators for such investments.  
 
In addition to posing technical challenges,  blending does not incentivize end consumers nor industries to 
develop specific hydrogen uses. 
 
Edison agrees with the Commission on the need to ensure that gas storage is adequately filled and 
welcomes the measures which are based on market mechanisms ( i.e., tendering schemes). On the other 
hand, the impacts of non-competitive measures that would allow regulated operators (TSOs) to purchase 
and manage gas reserves should be avoided. Measures such as  the joint procurement of gas and the 
possibility for Member States to oblige TSOs to purchase gas directly from suppliers would create significant 
market distortions. A direct role for TSOs in the purchase of gas would lead to an increase in supply costs . 
Edison believes it would be useful to consider a mechanism that provides - through incentive formulas - a 
role for market players in the filling of stocks up to minimum levels established at Community or Member 
State level. 
 
In addition, it is important to emphasize that ensuring the filling of stocks does not solve import shortages. It 
is therefore important to enhance the role of interconnections within the EU market and new supply routes 
so that the various Member States ca n benefit from a truly coordinated European approach. In this 
framework, Edison believes it is important to enhance strategic diversification options such as the Eastmed 
- Poseidon system that exploits the route with Greece to connect Italy to the new discoveries of the Eastern 
Mediterranean.

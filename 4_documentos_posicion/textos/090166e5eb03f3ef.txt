   
ENI POSITION PAPER | HYDROGEN & GAS MARKETS DECARBONISATION PACKAGE 
Eni welcomes the proposal for a hydrogen and decarbonised gas market package . We regard it as a 
key framework for decarbonization of the gas sector in line with the EU Green Deal energy and climate 
goals, by including renewable and low carbon gases in the gas market rules.  
Eni fully supports the objective of promoting full access of renewable and low -carbon gases to the 
wholesale market, LNG terminals, transmission network and cross-border trade. However, we believe 
that the proposed discounts on the  tariffs for the access to natural gas transmission networks 
represent a cross-subsidy with distorting impacts on the market. Furthermore, they do not contribute 
to effectively promoting the development of renewable and low-carbon gases. Other direct incentives 
for both production and consumption  (such as contracts for difference , grants, etc.) and/or public 
funding for network construction, are more suitable to achieve this purpose. We further highlight that 
the 100% transportation tariff reduction on Interconnection Points between Member States and third 
countries favours foreign production, as the EU production is only eligible for a 75% reduction. Finally, 
we find that the compensation mechanisms envisaged between adjacent TSOs for the loss of revenue 
associated with these discounts are not clear. 
To effectively promote the development of renewable and low-carbon gases, the measures proposed 
in the Gas & Hydrogen Package should be aligned with those in the ongoing revision of the Renewable 
Energy Directive (RED III). A common certification scheme for renewable and low-carbon gases, which 
should be adopted as soon as possible , is crucial , as well as  a common framework  – such as the 
Guarantees of Origin  (GOs) as per  Art.19 in  the RED III  – to avoid the proliferation of al ternative 
certification schemes developed at Member State level.  
In particular, we believe that the  GOs’ framework should be strengthened, specially by making their 
full utilization easier in order to alleviate ETS -related burdens. While respecting the approach of the 
RED, which is proposed as a key element of an approach oriented towards sector integration and 
which already contains provisions regarding energy of non-renewable origin, we believe that it will be 
necessary to ensure appropriate inter-textual references in the text of the RED and the Gas Directive 
that define an integrated and equivalent certification system and related incentives aimed at creating 
a level-playing field for decarbonised low-carbon products with a comparable emission profile. 
Moreover, separation of environmental characteristics within the certificates as tradable 
independently from the underlying physical commodity would be a significant step forward. 
With reference to Article 27, paragraph 1 (d)  in the RED III, GHG emission savings should include 
Renewable Fuels of Non-Biological Origin (RFNBOs) and Recycled Carbon Fuels (RCF s) which are not 
explicitly included in the proposed formula for estimating GHG reduction intensity. In addition, 
RFNBOs and RCFs should be explicitly recognised as suitable options for reaching the target in other 
parts of the proposed Directive (e.g. Article 25), to ensure a harmonised regulatory framework at 
European level and to avoid enforcement discretion that would undermine the integ rity of the EU 
internal market.    
With regards to the provision set at Article 8 of the Gas Directive recast on the deadline foreseen for 
the adoption of the Delegated Act (DA)  that will detail the methodology for calculating GHG savings 
of low-carbon fuels (December 2024), we believe that it seems illogical and inconsistent with the EU 
ambitions on hydrogen development. We therefore suggest that the DA is adopted as soon as possible 
and ideally by 2022. 
Finally, we consider it necessary to count in gross final consumption of energy from renewable sources 
all biofuels, biogas, RCFs and RFNBOs consumed in the transport sector even when used as 
intermediate products to produce fuels (transport fossil fuels or biofuels).

Concerning hydrogen network, its development  will necessitate public support and should not be 
subsidized by gas customers. We support the concept of distinct regulatory accounts for hydrogen 
and natural gas inf rastructure, but we believe that cross -subsidization of energy carriers should be 
avoided.   
With regard to unbundling rules for hydrogen networks, we agree with the adopted approach, which 
provides for less stringent obligations for hydrogen networks in the initial phase, with the possibility 
for MS s to grant existing networks and geographically confined networks an exemption from 
unbundling obligations. We consider, however, that the transitional regime should last no less than 
10 years. We therefore propose postponing to 31 December 2040 the deadline of 31 December 2030 
set for existing networks in Article 47 and for geographically confined networks in Article 48 of the 
proposed Gas Directive.  In principle, the unbundling rules that will be define d must in any case be 
such as to guarantee an adequate level of independence of network operators, as well as transparent 
and non-discriminatory access conditions. 
Eni welcomes the extension to gas consumers of the provisions in place for the electricity market, 
which aim to provide customers with more information and flexibility, to reflect the digitization of 
energy markets, and to allow consumers to play a more active role. In particular, the increased access 
to consumption data allowed by the massive deployment of smart meters  is critical to allow final 
customers to participate in both the flexibility market (demand side response) and the  promotion 
of energy efficiency. We also consider that eliminating any regulated supply tariff would incr ease 
consumer empowerment. 
Eventually, while we agree that it is desirable to shorten the switching process for consumers, we 
would like to point out that the proposal to reduce the time required to perform the switch to a 
maximum of 24 hours by 2026 does not adequately account for the heavy burden in terms of 
operational management of logistical activities that it would entail. 
In the light of the recent proposals from the European Commission, the proposed amendments to the 
Regulation (EU) 2017/1938 on security of gas supply will be treated separately.

JSW Position Paper on 
 
Regulation on methane emissions reduction in the energy sector 
 
The Paris Agreement set the plan to limit global warming below 2  degrees Celsius, to eliminate the 
threat of climate change. During COP26 in Glasgow, the US and the European Union presented “Global 
Methane Pledge”, an initiative which aims at limiting methane emissions by 30% by 2030 and was 
signed by over 100 countries. The largest source of anthro pogenic methane emissions is agriculture, 
followed by energy sector responsible for 19% of total EU methane emissions. 
 
Jastrzębska Spółka Węglowa S.A. (JSW) is the largest European producer of coking coal . Since 2014 
coking coal has been considered a critical raw material , thus one of strategic importance for 
development of the European economy, with high supply risk. Coking coal is key ingredient for steel 
production, with no economically viable and available in industry scale alternative steel technology. As 
JSW we understand the need to reduce methane emissions and support the targets set by “ Global 
Methane Pledge”. However, we are also aware of many difficulties and threats resulting from technical 
aspects that are not reflected in the provisions of the proposed Regulation, to which we would like to 
refer below. 
 
The exploitation of coal deposits is always associated with methane emissions. In deep mines, during 
operation, it is possible to partially capture the emitted methane by the methane drainage system. In 
the global mining industry, it is assumed that about 20 % to 30% of the total emitted methane can be 
captured. At JSW, this level is now at around 30 % – 40%. The rest of the emitted methane must, for 
safety reasons, be diluted to safe values and removed with the ventilation air to the atmosphere. 
 
Methane emissions from ventilation shafts 
Release of methane from ventilation shafts is critical to ensure the safety for mine workers. According 
to Polish regulations the maximum concentration of methane in ventilation shafts is 0,75%, while in 
JSW mines the level of concentration varies between 0,06% – 0,45%. Captured methane can be further 
used for combustion in thermal reactors, which is the only globally known technology of economic use 
of methane. However it can be applied with the minimum threshold 0,5% of methane in the ventilation 
air, while its profitability is ensured with the concentration above 1%. Hence, in order to meet the 
technical requirements additional amount of methane would have to be injected into the ventilation 
air to reach the threshold.  
 
Cost estimation of technology for a ventilation shaft with  the airflow of 8  000 m3/min and methane 
concentration of 0,6% would be around €60M based on received pricing. However, the airflow of single 
airshaft in JSW mines varies between 6 000 m3/min to 25 000 m3/min. Therefore, the cost estimation 
for an airshaft with airflow of 14 000 m3/min has to include two installations, which would double the 
final cost. In JSW mines there are currently 13 ventilation shafts with average airflow of                               
215 300 m3/min., which would require about 27 installations to capture such high volumes methane, 
with estimated cost of about €1 620M (according to the cost estimate from 2018). However, this does 
not include operating costs e.g. cost of methane which would have to be added to the ventilation air 
to reach necessary concentration. Moreover, s uch installations have a very low efficiency , thus they 
are not economically nor technically viable.

Specific comments  
 
Recital, par. 65 
Coking coal is considered a critical raw material for the EU, which confirms its strategic importance for 
European economy and its transformation. Any regulatory changes which could lead to the reduction 
of domestic production and thus increase dependence on imports from third countries should be 
introduced after prior market analysis, while taking into account the volume and sources of import and 
the degree of dependence on suppliers. 
 
Chapter 4, section I, art. 19, par. 2 c): Scope of monitoring and reporting in operating mines 
Processes defined as post -mining activities should not be in the volume of methane emission s from 
coal mines, as there might be different entities managing of transport or storage of extracted material. 
Hence the operator of the coal mine is not necessarily  responsible for length of transport nor for the 
length of the storage. Moreover, it will be impossible to calculate occuring emissions and indicate the 
responsible entity. 
 
Chapter 4, section II, art. 22, par. 1: Methane emissions from methane drainage stations 
It is impossible to completely reduce methane emissions from drainage stations. One of the elements 
of the methane drainage station technology is the technological exhaust. As the name suggests, it is 
not an emergency venting, as its is a normal and anticipated process necessary from technological 
point of view. The gas captured by th e drainage system is characterized by the amount and 
concentration of methane that vary in time. Moreover, the gas pressure in the pipeline to the recipient 
(e.g. cogeneration system) is regulated by releasing the gas into the atmosphere. The quantities 
discharged are small but unavoidable. The introduction of any restrictions should be related to the 
available technologies, and the goals should not only be ambitious but also realistic. 
Chapter 4, section II, art. 22, par. 2: Methane emissions from ventilation shafts 
The proposed limits are impossible to be met for deep mines, even for non-methane mines. Currently, 
there are no technologies available that would allow the economic use of methane from ventilation 
shafts if its concentration in the air is less than 0.5%. On the other hand, the existing technologies 
indicate the concentration of methane in the air at the level of 1.0 - 1.2% as the threshold of economic 
and energy profitability. Ventilation is the primary method of diluting hazardous gases in underground 
mine works. The mining ventilation system has been designed in such a way as to: provide miners with 
fresh air, regulate the temperature and humidity of the air in the mine, and effectively dilute or remove 
hazardous gases and respirable dust suspended in the air. For this reason, imposing a requirement to 
limit methane emissions from ventilation shafts will pose a direct threat to the health and life of 
working miners and will increase work safety risk in in the mining plants. Given the uncertainty about 
the effectiveness of the VAM capture technology, reduction of methane emissions from ventilation 
shafts, similarly to that from methane drainage stations, will not be feasible. 
 
Chapter 4, section II, art. 22, par. 3: Methane emissions from ventilation shafts 
Coking coal is listed as one of the critical raw materials, thus is a raw material of strategic importance 
for the european economy. All initiatives leading to the reduction of domestic production of critical 
raw materials are in contrad iction with the assumptions of the European Commission's 
Communication " Critical Raw Materials Resilience: Charting a Path towards greater Security and 
Sustainability." Moreover, introduction of restrictions regarding methane emissions from ventilation

Chapter 5, Methane emissions occurring outside the Union 
The provisions proposed in this chapter will not ensure level playing field. In case of EU-based mines it 
is proposed to introduce penalties for not complying with the regulation, while the provisions of in 
Chapter 5 and Annex VIII only encourage non -EU producers to reduce emissions. Cur rent measures 
will eventually lead to the transfer of methane emissions to third countries and will not result actual 
reduction of methane emissions. 
 
Chapter 6, art. 30: Penalties 
The imposed penalties should take into account funds allocated by enterprises for investments aimed 
at reducing methane emissions from mines. In this way, the provisions of the Regulation will be an 
incentive to increase investments in methane capture and economic use and thus will contribute to 
reduction of methane emis sions. When imposing penalties and determining their amount, Member 
States should take into account measures already taken by the company to reduce methane emissions 
and any fees or charges already imposed on methane emissions, regulated in accordance with national 
law and be able to exempt the entrepreneur from additional fees. Justified derogations would provide 
security for the European production of raw materials necessary for transformation.  
 
Summary 
Measures proposed in the regulation aimed at reducing methane emissions from hard coal mines 
(including coking coal  mines) are impossible to implement due to  technical reasons. In addition, 
Regulation proposal does not provide appropriate provisions encouraging the economic use of coal 
mine methane, which would actually contribute to emissions reduction. It is also worth emphasizing 
that the production of energy, heat and cold from the captured methane is also subject to fees 
resulting from the EU -ETS system, which is an additional burden for entrepre neurs and significantly 
reduces the profitability of this type of investment. The European Commission’s proposal will de facto 
lead to phase out of coking coal mines in the European Union and will further increase dependence on 
imports from third countries . In addition, the proposed provisions relating to methane emissions 
outside the EU, which are limited to providing information on the possible measurement and reporting 
of the counterparty's methane emissions outside the EU, are disproportionate to the measures 
imposed on EU companies. The Regulation as it stands will not lead to reduction in emissions from coal 
mines, as any coal shortages will be covered by its import, and thus the emissions avoided in the EU 
will be imported. In the current geopolitical  situation, as well as after economic crises caused by the 
COVID pandemic, further increasing dependence on imported raw materials, and in particular critical 
raw materials, from third countries poses a significant risk to the security of European supply c hains. 
In 2021, European coking coal production amounted to 14 million ton nes, compared to 40 million 
tonnes of imports (including over 10 million ton nes from Russia). The extraction of coking coal in the 
European Union is far from meeting the demand of the steel market, but at least it constitutes a stable 
supply base, and the introduction of more and more restrictions will have a negative impact on the 
safety of the European steel industry, without bringing the intended environ mental effects. The aim 
of t he R egulation should be to oblige operators and companies to monitor and report methane 
emissions, while at the same time maximally support  efforts to reduce emissions, based on real 
technical possibilities, ensuring sustainable extraction of the critical raw material, that coking coal is 
for the steel industry- a pillar of the European economy and transformation.

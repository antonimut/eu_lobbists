MOL Group's recommendations regarding the European Commission's proposal for a regulation to 
reduce methane emissions in the energy sector (COM (2021) 805 final): 
MOL Group welcomes the European Commission's proposal for the regulation on methane emissions 
reduction in the energy sector. Monitoring and preventing methane leakage in the energy sector is an 
important step towards achieving climate neutrality, however, some of the Commission's proposals 
would place a disproportionate burden on operators and force companies to apply not cost -effective 
solutions. The proposed  time frame  for the introduction of the detection and repair system is 
unrealistically short, and the excessive administrative burden and human resource require ments 
associated with the obligation of quarterly measurement and reporting of methane emissions would 
also represent a serious  challenge to companies . The proposed sensitivity of 500 ppm for leak 
detection is also considered to be too low to implement cost-effectively in practice. Therefore, please 
find below our detailed recommendation  which aim to help creating a consistent regulatory 
environment for methane emission reduction. 
General comment on the proposal: 
One type of solution does not fit all cases . The principle of proportionality should be considered and 
differentiated between Upstream, Downstream and Midstream.  
Regarding article 12 (reporting): 
• In point 8., whereas mitigation action for significant discrepancies between source-level 
quantification methods and those resulting from site -level measurement , additional 
measurements are requested. Based on current measurement technologies and testing 
experiences, significant discrepancies were identified. We recommend the obligation of repeated 
measurements in case o f discrepancies to be deleted, or at least set to a timeframe/condition, 
when site-level methodologies and technologies  have reached a maturity  to be able to perform  
more precise measurements. Unrealistic amount of measurements will not lead to emission 
reduction, however results in high costs.  
 
• In point 12.4 -5, the definition of “non -operated assets” needs to be clarified . (In industry, the 
term “non-operated asset” is used for assets where the company is present as the owner but the 
operator (legally) is another company.) It is also recommended that after surveying inactive wells 
by the operator, the operator should be able to exempt those inactive wells from the obligation 
where there is no physical risk of methane leakage  (e.g. in the case of unsuccessful drilling). 
Where there is a physical possibility of leakage  in principle (but in practice the well is sealed, 
plugged), it is recommended that the operator be able to verify the absence of leakage by a 
technical inspection. 
 
Regarding article 14 (“Leak detection and repair”): 
• According to points 1-2, within 6 months of the entry into force of the Regulation, operators must 
implement a "leak detection and repair" system. The 6 -month period is considered too short for 
both the national authorities and the operators to implement such system. Hence, the time limit 
of 12 months  is recommended  for the introduction of the system  and the authorization 
procedure by the authority. 
 
• According to point 2, after the entry into force of the regulation, the operator is obliged to carry 
out a survey and report every 3 months, which is consider to be unrealistically frequent, and would

impose unduly administrative burdens and human resource needs on companies. Besides avoiding 
excessive burdens, the repetition of the surveys every 12 months and the introduction of a 12-
monthly reporting obli gation are recommended given that annual reporting process has been 
used within the framework of the OGMP (Oil and Gas Methane Partnership), as an annual cycle 
can provide comprehensive information on emission trends, quarterly reports are not comparable, 
thus, they can be misleading. 
 
• Point 3 requires the operator to use a measuring device that is able to detect a methane leakage 
from the concentration of “500 ppm” . The industry does not currently use such sensitive 
measuring instruments and only very few devices are available on the market that are capable of 
detecting loss from “500 ppm”. We do not consider it necessary or justified to introduce such a 
low threshold for detection, given that such sensitive measuring instruments are not applied to 
measure other VOC components either. In addition, the introduction of the 500 ppm detection 
value would significantly limit the market for applicable devices, thus would result in higher costs. 
Therefore, as option a) omission of the instrument sensitivity requirement  is proposed , or as 
option b) the use of an instrument with a sensitivity of “1000 ppm” is recommended. 
 
• Point 4 sets a repair threshold for components emitting “500 ppm” or more. This criteria is stricter 
than the currently known  – and considered to be strict and environmentally conscious –  
requirements in system operator practice. With setting this low threshold, very small losses would 
be covered by the obligation concerning repairs, which would require a disproportionate amount 
of human resources and costs compared to the emission reductions achieved by the repair.  In 
connection with point 3, as option a.), a gradual reduction of methane emissions based on risk-
based consideration (which is also in line with the basic principle of LDAR) is recommended, i.e. 
the operator should move from the treatment of the largest emissions to the treatment of minor 
leaks. It is recommended to introduce a decreasing limit value, starting from 10,000 ppm to 1000 
ppm, within 3 years or when 75% of the measured leak ages fall below 10,000 ppm (whichever 
occurs first).  The introduction of a phased limit value will allow significant emissions to be 
effectively eliminated in “less advanced” systems first, based on the principle of risk -based 
assessment. As option b) , it is proposed to introduce a threshold of "1000 ppm"  for repair.  In 
point 4, i t is also necessary to specify the time interval for the repair or replacement of the 
component that may be covered by the "immediate repair after detection" requirement. In case 
of a major malfunction, it is recommended that the operator be able to ask for delay and not be 
forced to shut down immediately. Furthermore, if a leakage cannot be fixed by repairing, but only 
by the replacement of a  component, the operator may recommend to the authority the timing 
of the replacement, taking into account cost-effectiveness and risk-based assessment. Member 
State authorities are proposed to have more power to assess the repair schedule prepared and 
submitted by the operator, using cost -effectiveness and risk-based considerations. In the case 
of a complex leak (leak that cannot be repaired immediately), it is recommended to change the 
five-day deadline to the deadline for submitting the repair / replacement plan schedule to the 
national authority. It should be up to the national authority to set a deadline for repair / 
replacement. 
 
• Additional proposal for Article 14: It is proposed that the European Commission develop and 
introduce an incentive mechanism (for example in the form of a tax reduction) that contributes 
to the detection and immediate repair of methane losses . An incentive scheme to reduce 
methane emissions would not only speed up operator intervention, but also reduce the potential 
for possible breaches. 
Regarding reporting obligations stated in articles 14 and 16: 
• It is essential to harmonize the various reporting obligations (Article 14 (2) and (7), Article 16 (2)), 
given that the proposed legislation imposes unduly administrative burden to operators . It is 
proposed to introduce an annual reporting obligation in order to avoid excessive human resource 
requirements and administrative burdens, and taking into account that annual reporting process 
has been used within the framework of the OGMP (Oil and Gas Methane Partnership), as an annual 
cycle can provide comprehensive information on emission trends, quarterly reports are not 
comparable, thus, they can be misleading. 
Regarding article 17 ("Requirements for flaring standards"): 
• Point 3 requires – and Annex III. details –  weekly inspection of flare stacks . This requirement 
imposes a disproportionately high demand on human resources compared to amount of emissions 
that can be detected and prevented by weekly monitoring. It is therefore proposed to introduce 
"monthly" inspection. 
Regarding article 18 ("Inactive wells"): 
• According to point 2 , the installation of devices for the detection of methane emissions is also 
mandatory for all inactive wells.  It is proposed to delete or amend the regulation so that only the 
site-level measurement is mandatory for inactive wells  once a year  (for wells where there is a 
physical risk of leakage). We do not consider it feasible to install a measuring instrument on each 
inactive well (thousands), given that it would be extremely costly, which we cannot estimate yet, 
because we do not use or know such complex, online instruments. The physical feasibility is also 
questionable how a single sensor could cover at least 8 gate valves at a plugged well. In addition, 
the risk of materi al damage is very high due to theft of the instruments when it is placed at an 
abandoned site, unguarded. 
Regarding article 27 (“Importer information requirement”) and Annex VIII: 
• The reporting obligation for oil and gas importers set out in Annex VIII may encounter difficulties 
in case of import sources where the regulatory environment is different from the EU regulat ion, 
and the importer is therefore unable to access the necessary information on the exporter and the 
producer. Therefore, it is recommended that the Union shall obtain the necessary data through 
bilateral contacts.

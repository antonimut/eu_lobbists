Edison’s response to the consultation on the Proposal for a Regulation on methane emissions reduction 
in the energy sector and amending Regulation (EU) 2019/942 
 
Considering its active role in the gas value chain and its commitment to the EU emission targets, Edison fully 
supports the European Commission’s initiative to adopt a n EU-wide regulation addressing methane 
emissions. The company agrees that it is necessary to improve the accuracy of information about sou rces 
of emissions and that operators should maximize their efforts in reducing emissions. As a contribution to 
the ongoing legislative process, a series of considerations on the main provisions proposed in the methane 
regulation are listed below. 
 
Edison welcomes the objective of the Commission to increase measurement, reporting and verification 
(MRV) of methane emissions in the EU territory. Designing an action plan based on updated data is crucial 
to accelerate the transitional process towards  climate neutrality. It is important to take into account that 
technologies to detect and directly measure methane emissions are still under development and measured 
data on emissions is therefore not always readily available. The Regulation should hence consider estimates 
of methane emissions through engineering calculations, simulation tools and emission factors  which are 
valuable alternatives when direct measurement is not feasible.  
 
To ensure accurate reporting, the MRV obligations envisioned in the document should be requested to the 
actors that are directly involved in emitting activities. This because it is who operates and manages the asset 
that has the knowledge and information to provide the most accurate data. More specifically, the reporting 
responsibilities for non-operated assets should instead be assigned to the asset operator.  
 
MRV activities, as well as all the other obligations proposed in the regulation, have cost implications which 
market operators will have to internalize . In Article 3, t he proposal allows regulated operators to recover 
such costs and investments in their tariff structure. However, non-regulated market operators do not have 
the option of transferring such costs into tariffs and will have to internalize them, disincentivizing mitigation 
efforts. In this regard, relevant institutions should ensure the establishment of a framework that 
incentivizes such investments and the related adaptation costs for non-regulated operators.  
 
Edison fully supports the prioritization of recovery and re -injection of gas with respect to venting and 
flaring and welcomes the fact that the Commission acknowledges that in some specific circumstances it is 
not possible to avoid venting and flaring. A key guiding principle should be to prioritize interventions that 
have the highest emissions reduction potential in the shortest time and at the lowest cost. In this regard the 
Regulation should ensure that operators do not undergo disproportionate efforts to mitigate non-material 
emissions. To ensure an efficient allocation of costs and effort s, the introduction of  a mechanism that 
determines emission thresholds above which the prohibition of specific venting and flaring activities 
applies should be considered. This to allow operators to focus their efforts and investments on interventions 
with the highest mitigation potential. 
 
Following the same principle of weighting costs and emission reduction potential, when it comes to the 
obligation for operators to undergo leak detection and repair surveys every three months, the Commission 
should take into account the feasibility and effectiveness of undergoing these assessments so frequently. 
An efficient approach would be to require more frequent surveying on spe cific at-risk components rather 
than on the whole infrastructure.

Article 27 of the Regulation requires fossil fuel importers to submit specific information on the measuring 
and emissions abatement efforts of producers and exporters with whom the import contract has been signed 
with. While the new Regulation does not extend any compliance obligation to exporters  and producers in 
third countries, there are several considerations to be made on this article. Considering the limited ability for 
importers to influence producers’ MRV activities, setting obligations to EU energy importing companies 
might not be effective to reduce external emissions. The regulation should not impose a  disproportionate 
burden on importers by requiring them to submit specific information on players who operate outside of EU 
territory and jurisdiction. The Commission should instead consider an  EU climate diplomacy approach to 
encourage third countries and foreign energy companies to increase MRV and mitigation measures . 
Furthermore, when it is important to differentiate between existing supply contracts and new contracts . 
Since these obligations were not discussed nor negotiated, they should not be applied to existing contracts. 
Finally, importers sho uld not be made responsible for the figures and information provided (or not 
provided) by the foreign counterpart.

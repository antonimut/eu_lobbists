 
 
   TRAFFIC’s recommendations for the legislative procedure on the new ECD 
 
TRAFFIC’S RECOMMENDATIONS TO THE EUROPEAN COMMISSION ON THE 
PROPOSAL FOR A NEW ENVIRONMENTAL CRIME DIRECTIVE    
 
TRAFFIC warmly welcomes the Commission’s (EC’s) proposal  for the Directive on the 
protection of the environment through cri minal law and replacing Directive 2008/99/EC. This 
position paper outlines the main recommendations from TRAFFIC for the proposed new 
Environmental Crime Directive.  
 
1. Scope of the Directive 
 
TRAFFIC strongly supports the proposed broadening of the Directive’s scope. As environmental 
crime is one of the most lucrative crimes globally , it would be essential to take the scale of 
financial benefits gained by committing the offence into account when assessing whether the 
quantity of affected species is negligible or not, as well as whether the offence was committed 
in the framework of a criminal organisation, or linked to corruption/fraud. Such crimes seriously 
affect the ecosystem as well as societies and undermin e public confidence . Accordingly, 
environmental crime has been selected as  one of the EMPACT 2022-2025 priorities. 
 
2. Sanctions and aggravating circumstances 
 
Environmental crimes, including wildlife trafficking, are often viewed as having relatively low-
risk and high profit due primarily to low chances of being prosecuted  and the lack of severe 
penalties imposed. TRAFFIC applauds that the proposed penalties and measures for natural 
and legal persons would be more dissuasive. However, the financial aspects of the crimes could 
be targeted more effectively to ensure that any organised crime groups involved are affected 
negatively where it is most disruptive  (i.e., illicit profits). As additional sanctions , it would be 
beneficial to include an obligation to compensate for the damage caused, having fines 
proportionate to the financial benefits accrued by committing the offence, and payment for 
damages (civil liability), particularly when environmental restoration is no longer possible. The 
financial benefits gained by committing the offence are not reflected in the sanctions for natural 
persons, even though the offences are often committed outside of a traditional company 
structure. Additionally, it would be important to add as an aggravating circumstance the 
irreversible or long -lasting substantial damage to the status of wild animal or plant species 
populations covered by the EU Wildlife Trade Regulations. Furthermore, we suggest that the EC 
develops sentencing guidelines to a ssist the Member States (MS) and judges in defining and 
applying effective, proportionate, and dissuasive sanctions, including the economic valuation of 
environmental damage. 
 
3. Specialisation and training  
 
Given the complex nature of environmental crime, in-depth knowledge and specialisation are 
needed to handle such cases successfully . All MSs must have  sufficient resources to tackle 
environmental crime and provide specialised training along the entire enforcement chain. The 
Directive also has to put further emphasis on  efficient cross-border and inter -agency 
cooperation and information sharing. TRAFFIC would strongly support specialised investigation 
units, further contributing to the efficiency of law enforcement  in environmental matters . 
Specialised prosecutors would also have a key role in bringing environmental crimes before the

4. Other issues  
 
Furthermore, jurisdiction should be extended: MS should be obliged to establish jurisdiction 
over offences committed for the benefit of a legal person established on their territory because 
legal persons established in the EU should be bound by laws in force in the EU.  Besides, we 
welcome the provisions on  national strategies , reporting requirements , and minimum 
requirements for resources, training, and investigative tools. We would like to see the Directive 
provide more extensive protection for environmental defenders:  plus, provide appropriate rights 
to participate in proceedings for the members of the public concerned , including civil society 
organisations. It is also crucial that raw statistical data from the MS on environmental crimes 
be made public. 
 
About TRAFFIC 
 
TRAFFIC is a non-governmental organisation working on trade in wild animals and plants in the 
context of biodiversity conservation and sustainable development. We work to achieve a 
sustained reduction in illegal wildlife trade by increasing the risks and reducing the rewards 
associated with trading in wildlife contraband, reducing criminal motivation and engagement in 
the illicit trade. TRAFFIC offers its assistance to the EC to support their efforts to tackle  
environmental crime, in particular wildlife trafficking.

MAIF contribution on the proposal for a regulation establishing 
harmonised rules for fair access and use of data 
 
April 2022 
 
On 23 February 2022, the European Commission presented its proposal for a regulation establishing 
harmonised rules for fair access and use of data, known as the Data Act, which MAIF warmly 
welcomes. 
 
Since 2020, MAIF has been working on the development of a European Personal Information 
Management Systems (PIMS) that will allow users to keep control of their data through t heir 
consensual and secure use within an ecosystem of trusted players. This work has been carried out in 
partnership with other players (technology partners, start-ups, and large French companies) from 
different sectors in order to have the largest impact for our approach.  
 
This platform should contribute, not only to place the citizen at the heart of the management of his 
or her data when using digital technology (website, mobile app or the IoT), but also to further develop 
a more secure environment for their real-time circulation and storage. 
 
In the light of this work, MAIF welcomes the objectives pursued by the Data Act, notably:  
- Facilitate access to data and their use by consumers and businesses 
- Facilitate switching between cloud and data processing services, at equivalent standards  
- Implement a more level playing field for data sharing 
- Remove barriers to 'hot data portability' and develop interoperability standards 
 
Within the regulatory ecosystem under discussion at European level, the Data Act will be an additional 
tool for European digital sovereignty, allowing: 
- To promote a fair distribution of the wealth linked to the exploitation of data along the value 
chain 
- To place the citizen at the heart of this data economy 
- To build a "Data as a Service" vision in which the consented sharing of data will allow access 
to tailor-made services requested by the citizen 
 
MAIF has already shared with the European Commission, during various public consultations in 2020 
and 2021, its proposals for placing the citizen at the heart of the data economy. Today, we would like 
to send our comments on the proposed Data Act.  
1) Scope of the text (Art. 1) 
 
In order to build a data economy based on the sharing and circulation of data, the regulation must 
cover the widest possible scope, including the key players in the daily lives of citizens.  
In this respect, we welcome the fact that the following are included: 
 
- Manufacturers of related products and service providers placed on the EU market. The notion 
of linked services seems particularly impactful due to the development of the Internet of 
Things 
- The users of these products and services 
- Data holders who make data available 
- Recipients of these data 
- Data processing service providers

2) Business-to-consumer and business-to-business data sharing provisions 
 
The platform that MAIF wishes to implement offers a vision in which citizens could generate service 
requests on the basis of a consenting exploitation of their shared data.  
It relies on a simple paradigm: 
 
- For citizens: increased access to data and easier sharing of their data, especially for real-time 
data. 
o The citizen user and owner of his data must be able to decide with whom, how and 
when he shares his data, through easy -to-use interfaces allowing him to take full 
advantage of the potential generated by his data thanks to tailored services. 
 
- For companies:  development of tailor -made services, responding to citizens' expectations, 
based on the data shared by users.  
o This will enable them to accelerate their digital transition with the benefit of a clear 
regulatory framework. 
 
The provisions of the Data Act strongly support the development of such a platform, thanks to three 
major and interrelated principles: 
 
- Obligation for manufacturers to make data generated by the use of related products or 
services accessible to the user (Art. 3) 
 
- Users' rights to access data generated by the use of a linked product or service (Art. 4)  
 
- Possibility for the user to ask the data holder to make available to a third party the data 
generated by the use of a linked product or service (art. 5) 
 
These three principles must be accompanied by the following guarantees: 
 
- Regarding access to data:  
o It is based on the manufacturer of the linked product or service, for the benefit of the 
user 
o It must be accompanied by clear and comprehensible prior information to th e 
consumer 
o It must be possible to access it securely 
o To be fully relevant and impactful for the user of these services on a daily basis, it must 
be applied to all data, including continuous and real time data 
 
- Regarding the provision of data: 
o It is based on the holder of the data, for the benefit of the user of related products and 
services  
o To be fully relevant and impactful for the user of these services on a daily basis, it must 
be applied to all data, including continuous and real time data 
 
- Regarding the sharing of data with a third party: 
o Il must be free of charge

3) Changing of data processing services (Chapter VI) and interoperability (Chapter VIII)   
 
a. Portability 
 
The Data Act provides the ideal framework to achieve full data portability, not only from connected 
objects but also from consumers' daily consumption (public services, retail, mobility, etc.). 
 
Also, we welcome the fact that the proposed Regulation introduces minimum contractual, commercial 
and technical regulatory requirements for cloud computing service providers to enable  the transition 
from one service to another. In particular, we welcome: 
- The retention of functional equivalence of service (Art. 24)   
- The phasing out of switching fees (Art. 25) 
 
Nevertheless, we draw the Commission's attention to the need to take account of real -time data in 
order to make this portability fully effective. 
 
b. Interoperability 
 
Beyond portability, the data as a service approach requires the development of a user -friendly API 
system based on simple user navigation, where the services offered are based on voluntary and 
consented data sharing. It would allow private and public entit ies to create value, from legally 
obtained and processed data, through the implementation of tailor-made everyday services with user-
friendly interfaces. For example: better management of household expenses, simplified access to 
public aid, better assessment of risk coverage, assistance with tax returns, etc.  
 
Thus, t he provisions of the proposed Regulation go in the right direction by providing essential 
requirements to meet with regard to interoperability for data space operators and data processing 
service providers. These provisions will support a seamless multi-vendor cloud environment.  
 
In conjunction with these provisions and in order to strengthen European sovereignty, we propose, 
finally, that the Commission financially support, in the framework o f the European recovery plan, EU 
companies ready to develop services based on data freely shared by the citizen.

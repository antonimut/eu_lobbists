Fortum promotes the next generation  
of circular economy
As a strategic programme of Fortum’s Recycling and Waste 
business, Carbon2x can help the company reduce the CO 2 emissions 
of its waste treatment operations significantly and produce high-
quality raw materials for new products. Based on carbon capture 
and utilization (CCU), the Carbon2x concept represents the next 
generation of circular economy solutions.
Fortum’s objective is to reuse, recycle and utilize as much of the 
waste stream as possible. According to our estimate, the Carbon2x 
programme could help improve the recycling rate of plastic 
packaging significantly. By adopting the concept at a large scale, 
the recycling rate of plastic packaging in Finland can be doubled 
compared to the current level by 2030 – simultaneously, 90% of the 
carbon dioxide released into the atmosphere can be captured and 
absorbed into products.
The spread of recycling methods such as the Carbon2x programme 
is curbed by the fact that the Finnish and the EU legislation clearly 
do not recognize such a recycling technology.
Benefits of CCU technology
Carbon capture and utilization will have a significant role in mitigating 
climate change and promoting circular economy. Carbon2x enables 
the use of waste as energy and molecular level recycling without 
emissions. 
In the Carbon2x programme, non-recyclable or non-reusable waste 
is processed at Fortum’s waste incineration plant in Riihimäki. At the 
first stage of the Carbon2x programme, the carbon dioxide captured 
from the flue gases of waste incineration is combined with hydrogen 
to produce methane and methanol. From Fortum’s perspective, this is 
an intermediate phase, as our deeper interest lies in the production of 
products of higher degree of processing such as specialty plastics.
Recycling obligations must be set for the side 
streams of waste incineration
After the recovery of energy and heat, waste incineration produces 
ash and slag, which are composed of the non-combustible materials 
in waste. Ash and slag as well as the metals and minerals in them 
can be recycled quite efficiently already with the current methods. 
• The Waste-to-Energy sector should be incorporated into the 
EU Emissions Trading Scheme during the current period of 
examination, starting from 2026. Incorporating the sector 
into the Scheme would provide incentives to invest in ways 
to reduce emissions at Waste-to-Energy plants, including 
carbon capture, storage and utilization. 
• A Waste-to-Energy plant that captures CO2 and utilizes it 
in, for example, the production of plastics – thus storing the 
recycled carbon in products – should be released from the 
obligation to surrender emission allowances. 
• EU’s waste legislation should be technology-neutral and 
adapt to the opportunities created by new recycling 
methods. Products that have been produced from waste- 
based CO2 using CCU should be deemed recycled by waste 
legislation. 
• The recycling of ash and slag should be increased both with 
tax control measures and by setting mandatory recycling 
targets for the related waste streams. 
Legislation to promote the transition towards wider circular economy
POLICY PAPER
Ref. Ares(2022)3328441 - 29/04/2022
• CCU refers to capturing carbon from CO2 and utilizing it in new 
products. 
• Recycled carbon can be used as a raw material in the production of 
fuels, chemicals, polymers and carbonates, among others.
• Fortum has launched the Carbon2x programme, in which the 
company explores the production of recycled materials of high 
degree of processing, such as specialty plastics, from the CO2 
emissions of waste incineration. 
• Based on CCU, the Carbon2x concept reduces the climate change-
boosting impacts of waste incineration. 
• The global demand for plastics is expected to grow threefold by 
2050. Today, 90% of plastics is produced from fossil feedstock 
such as oil.
• Carbon capture and storage is not a solution that promotes 
circular economy, because the carbon dioxide is permanently 
stored in the ground.
• To combat climate change, reducing the use of virgin fossil 
feedstock is of vital importance. 
• It has been estimated that many companies will invest in CCU 
technology and market products that have been produced by 
using carbon that is recycled from carbon dioxide.
However, their recycling could be significantly increased with 
legislative and tax measures. 
In fact, mandatory recycling obligations should be imposed on the 
side streams of waste incineration. Additionally, recycling should be 
controlled with waste tax to prevent some of the usable materials 
from being dumped in landfills, which happens today.
Plastic is a sustainable material when  
recycled correctly
Plastic is needed for various applications now and in the future. 
Abandoning fossil raw materials requires that the raw materials used 
in the production of plastic also come from sustainable sources. 
Plastic raw material that is waste-based and recycled in various ways 
provides an answer to this challenge.
In terms of recyclability, plastic is an excellent material. Nowadays, 
recycled plastic can be used to produce plastic products such as dish 
brushes, flowerpots, pipes and plastic bags. However, mechanically 
recycled plastic that is manufactured from consumer packaging may 
not be used in food or pharmaceutical packaging or toys.
Instead, the Carbon2x concept allows recycled plastic to be used in 
the production of products in which recycled materials could not be 
used previously, like the packaging used in pharmaceutical and food 
industries.
Thus, Carbon2x reduces the need for fossil plastic and alleviates the 
burden on the environment and climate caused by the production of 
virgin plastic.

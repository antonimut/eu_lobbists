The role of the EU in the certification of carbon removals   
To secure in the long run the possibility for landowners to sell their carbon credits also internationally 
and outside the EU we need credible, transparent, and market -based solutions. There are already 
several carbon market services, platforms and standar ds, like Puro.earth, frontierclimate.com, 
Verra/VCS, and Gold Standard and significant progress in ISO/EN-standardization. The quality of these 
services is getting better all the time with the pressure of market competition. This means that there 
is no nee d for the EU to establish a new market platform or a new standard for carbon offsets. 
Establishment of certification methodologies, establishment of the system for accreditation of 
certification bodies, validation of the carbon removal project, and verific ation of removals made 
should be all carried out by independent private entities. The EU should only establish minimum 
standard requirements on reporting transparency for carbon removals. The EU certification 
framework should define only the minimum criter ia for the certification and should not 
comprehensively define the certificates.  
The role of the EU in the certification should be to guarantee that the EU supply has access to 
international market through EU ITMO’s, and the compatibility with LULUCF/AFOL U. The EU should 
also point out a transparent methodology how double counting is handled in the national GHG -
inventories/registry so that producer of net-removal (credit) has the right to sell it to the international 
markets. For this, the transparency registries described in the Picture 1 (page 3), are needed.  
Main objectives for the certification of carbon removals are to increase the transparency and level 
playing field of voluntary carbon markets and allow comparability and competition between different 
carbon removal solutions.   
Market guarantee arrangement is needed to facilitate commitment  of farmers to take the first step 
towards entering the market. Upfront costs of implementing climate actions are significant, and if 
demand for the credit units produced cannot be guaranteed, supply does not arise. Producers should 
receive pre-financing immediately when entering the system.  
Financing carbon removals with the CAP is not feasible as there is not enough money in the CAP for 
this purpose. Relying to CAP funding would mean that financing would be possible approximately 2030 
the earliest. From climate perspective we don’t have this time to wait anymore.  
It is important to establish a system, where net -removals are credited. However , in the view of 
scalability, producers’ commitment, and to keep the spectrum of measures as wide as possible, also 
ex-ante procedures should be certifiable in certain cases. The baseline should be the measured 
starting level where a landowner joins the crediting system or tech nical net -removal appliance is 
applied.  If net -removals are achieved, they should be credited despite of the methods used; the 
markets should have freedom to choose the methods.  Only thing that matters for climate is the net-
removals. It is useless to consider any modelled behaviour of people or possible natural phenomena 
happening in the future as the baseline. Only achieved net-removals matter for climate. In this respect 
the certification system should be result-based.  
 
Information carbon certification should disclose  
To indicate the information related to the type of carbon removals, quantity of carbon removed, 
information on the carbon removal provider, information on the certificate owner, duration of carbon 
storage, price if the certificate has bee n traded, should be made compulsory for market registry 
providers. Also, the name of the standard applied should be mentioned. Voluntary market schemes

Price statistics should be carried out in accordance with the normal principles followed by the

pointed out from the statistics.  
 
The roles of the different parties should be clear  
The role of the EU certification scheme is very unclear in the overall climate architecture of the EU. 
The COM should focus on making this role clear. The main task is to guarantee EU originated ITMO 's 
a market access to international markets by the Paris article 6 rules.  
The content of the questionnaire related to this consultation indicates that there is a clear 
misunderstanding of the roles of different parties in the process. In this process it is important to make 
clear the roles of different parties.  
For example, a voluntary carbon market platform can only establish an open registry of the credits 
what they have credited. It is public authorities’ role to keep the national GHG -inventories and t he 
corresponding registry where the market platforms can inject the needed information of traded 
credits. The only parties who can describe the credits ’ contribution to the Paris Agreement are the 
Parties of the Paris Agreement. It is not a job for voluntary carbon market players, even they may wish 
to do so. So, in the EU case the member states and the EU need to figure out how inavoidable double 
counting is solved with the transparency registry bookkeeping rules. The rules how to do 
corresponding adjustments need to be decided by the EU law and at least on the EU -level. UNFCCC 
level would be the best to establish a level playing field for parties of the Paris agreement. EU member 
states can't do these rules by their own. If they do so by their own, there i s a risk that those national 
decisions lead to counterproductive policy incentives in the member states’ LULUCF/AFOLU targets. 
The double counting is inavoidable, if national GHG-inventories are economywide. In this respect the 
issue is an issue for all the Parties of the Paris agreement.

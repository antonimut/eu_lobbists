 
 
CALL FOR EVIDENCE  
 
Tetra Pak’s feedbacks to the call for evidence for an impact assessment on the new 
EU rules for the certification of carbon removals  
 
May 2022  
 
We need to collectively optimi se the contribution of forests to achieve the Green Deal (climate, 
biodiversity) while minimizing the trade -offs on its current benefits (social, economic and 
environmental). Hence, Tetra Pak welcomes the European Commission’s proposal to develop a carbon 
removal certification mechanism for activities that sequester carbon. This will support the restoration, 
protection and resilience of natural ecosystems while enhancing climate action.  
 
A strong bioeconomy as a key driver of decarbonisation  
 
As a global leader in plant -based food packaging and food processing technologies, Tetra Pak is 
exploring the most efficient ways to achieve net zero GHG emissions along its value chain. For that 
reason, we support the development of a st rong sustainable circular bioeconomy as a basis to reach 
the Green Deal’s ambitions. The proposed initiative on  a carbon removal certification mechanism can 
help toward achieving a carbon positive bioeconomy.  
 
When following the cascading principle, the sustainable circular bioeconomy has the potential to remove 
large volume of carbon: the full climate change mitigation potential of biotechnology processes and 
biobased products ranges from between 1 billion and 2.5 billion tons CO2 equivalent per year by 2030.1 
While healthy and well-managed forests capture and sequester carbon dioxide in growing trees, wood 
products, in addition to substituting fossil-based material, also contribute to carbon storage. 2 
 
Being a highly recyclable material with a recycling rate for fiber-based packaging amounting to 85.5% 
in Europe3, the wood fibers used in pulp and paper can be recycled at least 5 -7 times and sometimes 
over 20 times.4 Furthermore, the use of wood fibres can also change over time. For instance, the carbon 
stored in wood fibers, after being used in packaging, can be used in new applications like construction 
material (e.g., building insulation), turning parts of the construction sector into carbon storage. 
 
In a well-functioning circular bioeconomy, the lifetime of carbon stored in wood fibers is not set in stone, 
as the use of the wood fibres can be prolonged, and their  carbon removal period can be significantly 
extended depending on the use of the recycled material.   
 
Ensuring a life cycle approach for forest-based materials 
 
In addition to the forestry sector, this carbon removal potential should take into consideration the role 
of the whole downstream value chain and involve all stakeholders operating in the sustainable circular 
bioeconomy. Biomass is a valuable resource, and many parameters need to be considered when 
assessing its sustainable use.  
 
When developing its methodology for carbon removal, the European Commission should assess the 
carbon removal performances of forests against the sustainable downs tream uses of woody biomass.

Bringing clarity on the impacts of this initiative on biomass use  
 
We welcome the European Commission’s call for evidence to develop a detailed science-based impact 
assessment. As a major user of paperboard, Tetra Pak, along with the forest-based industry, needs 
clarity on how this carbon removal certification mechanism will affect the u se of biomass and how the 
biobased market will be impacted. Furthermore, existing internationally recognised carbon certification 
schemes like Verra VCS (Verified Carbon Standard) and CCB (Climate, Community and Biodiversity) 
standards should be cons idered when creating an EU certification scheme. We encourage the 
European Commission to take the same approach as with EU Renewable Energy Directive (RED) 
having approved voluntary schemes.

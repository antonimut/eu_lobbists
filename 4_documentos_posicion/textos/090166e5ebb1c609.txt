Reply to the Public Consultation 
Carbon Dioxide Removals are necessary, alongside carbon reductions, to achieve the EU’s 2050 target. 
The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change’s (IPCC) findings underscore this assumption: within 
their 116 scenarios that limited global warming to 1.5°C, negative emission technologies were included 
in 101 scenarios.  One key obser vation on the recent IPCC AR6WG3 report  is that Carbon Dioxide 
Removals (CDR) methods are valuable and even essential in the 2030 ’s to prevent any target 
overshoot. 
URGENCY OF ACTION 
The scientific research that forms the basis  of the IPCC report also points out that postponing CDR 
beyond mid-century would substantially reduce the removal potential to almost half  by end of the 
century. Timely action is needed as soon as possible, and a focus should be placed on implementation 
now and scaling up in the 2040’s. Delaying the carbon dioxide removal pathway in the European Union 
puts our climate targets at risk. 
NEED FOR A ROBUST FRAMEWORK 
We encourage the EC to develop a robust certification mechanism for negative emissions which 
should provide economic incentives for the application of negative emissions technologies ( NET) 
that would rely on a robust monitoring, reporting, and verification system crucial for the transparent 
quantification of achieved abatements and their monetization.  Careful consideration must also be 
given to determining a solid definition of permanence so that the durability of carbon removals is 
guaranteed. Moreover, t he framework should be technology neutral and enabling rather than 
restricting. Different CDR have their strengths and  weaknesses depending on their  applications and 
different circumstances. It is important not to restrict the full spectrum of solutions from the market 
at the start but to allow competition as long as the transparency and accuracy of real carbon removals 
accounting is respected.  Thus, we encourage the Commission to make sure that multiple 
methodologies are approved so that a full range of solutions can be certified and assessed.  
INCORPORATE SUSTAINABILITY  
Sustainability must be a core principle of any carbon removal technology or policy framework; this 
means CDR will be done in a way that supports both social and environmental objectives. Generating 
high quality durable removals can and should be done in a sustainable way.  The EU should ensure 
consistency across sustainability frameworks where they already exist. For example, the biomass 
criteria included in the RED is also suitable for use with Bioenergy Carbon Capture and Storage projects. 
New certification mechanisms should also promote the EU’s progress towards a circular, biobased, and 
resource-efficient economy and encourage cost-effective projects entailing high energy efficiency and 
high carbon capture rates.  
NECESSARY INCENTIVIZATION 
Any framework should be done in a way that fosters a market able to generate revenue to support and 
upscale the sector and further technology developme nt. At a minimum, this will require valorisation 
of removed carbon or a framework which ensures stable operational support for the deployment of 
these technologies. We call on the EC to assess different viable policy options to implements such 
mechanisms, whether in the frame of the EU ETS or as a parallel system. The EU is a market leader and

Comments of EchoStar Mobile Limited on the European Commission Proposal for a 
Regulation of the Union Secure Connectivity Programme for the Period 2023–2027 
5 May 2022 
Introduction 
EchoStar Mobile Limited (EML) hereby submits these comments in response to the European Commission 
Proposal for a Regulation of the Union Secure Connectivity Programme for the Period 2023 –2027 (the 
“Programme”). The Programme is broad in its ambition. If successful, it can bring significant benefits to the EU, 
ensuring secure connectivity for government and commercial users. However, as discussed herein, there are a 
number of improvements that need to be made to ensure the Programme’s success. 
EML, an Irish company providing services across Europe using the EchoStar® XXI satellite, holds one of two 
pan-European authorisations for Mobile Satellite Serviced (MSS) with a Complementary Ground Component 
(CGC) in the 1980–2010 MHz/2170–2200 MHz bands (2 GHz band). In addition, EML, working with its affiliates, 
is developing a global 5G NTN-NR MSS ecosystem for use in Europe (and globally) as part of its current MSS 
network and planned Non-Geostationary Orbit (NGSO) network. 
EML urges the Commission to ensure that the draft regulation is robust and addresses a number of key areas that 
are necessary for the Programme’s financial and long-term success. EML urges the Commission to amend the 
draft regulation to require public comment on a number of areas, including governance structure, the design of the 
network, how spectrum will be shared between the government and private sector, and  how to ensure the 
economic sustainability of the Programme. By amending the draft legislation to address these and other critical 
issues, the EU can help the Programme succeed, which will, in turn, help Europe achieve its critically important 
goals. 
Ensuring the Draft Regulation Leads to a Successful Programme 
EML fully supports the EU’s effort to create the Programme. It will bring a number of important benefits to Europe, 
including secure communications and widespread connectivity. However, EML has concerns that the draft 
regulation fails to address several key areas. This includes the need to obtain public input on a number of specific 
areas that must be decided on before issuing a competitive tender for the network. 
To best address these areas, EML proposes that the Commission amend the draft legislation to require the 
Commission to seek comment on several key topics within 90 days of the effective date of the regulation. These 
topics include: 
 Governance of the Programme 
 Network design 
 How spectrum and capacity will be shared between government and private user s (as well as defining 
government usage) 
 How the Programme will be economically sustainable, including addressing financial issues such as how 
the private sector will be compensated and how the Programme will meet ongoing operational costs 
 How to divide the payloads/network capacity between commercial and government use  
 How to make commercial opportunities open to all companies (not just the current concessionaires) and 
prevent disruptions to the commercial market.

Further, it is critical that all stages of the tender process be public. This includes seeking comment on draft tender 
documents. Failure to receive wide-ranging public comment could lead to disruptions in the market and 
unnecessarily foreclose qualified companies from participating.  
Accordingly, EML urges the Commission to amend the draft regulation to add a provision that would require the 
Commission to seek public comment on the preceding areas within 90 days of the effective date of the regulation 
and to consider these comments when developing the tender documents. Further, the Commission must also 
ensure that its draft tender documents are available for comment by the public and not limi ted to a handful of 
companies. In doing so, the Commission can receive critical input to ensure that the Programme is economically 
sustainable and meets the needs of Europe.

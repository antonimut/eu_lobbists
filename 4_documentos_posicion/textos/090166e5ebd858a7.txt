ACT | The App Association’s feedback to the European Commission’s proposal for a regulation establishing a framework of measures for strengthening Europe's semiconductor ecosystem I. Introduction ACT | The App Association welcomes the opportunity to provide its comments to the European Commission regarding its proposal for a regulation establishing a framework of measures for strengthening Europe's semiconductor ecosystem (Chips Act). We believe that semiconductors are a key component of the European Union’s (EU) strategic autonomy and technological capacity across all sectors.   The App Association is a not-for-profit trade association located in Brussels, Belgium. We represent more than 5,000 small and medium-sized application developers and connected device companies located across the EU and around the globe. Today, the ecosystem the App Association represents— which we call the app economy—is valued at approximately €830 billion globally and is responsible for over 1.3 million jobs in the EU.1 Alongside the world’s rapid embrace of mobile technology, our members have been developing innovative hardware and software solutions that power the growth of the internet of things (IoT) across various modalities and segments of the economy. Their innovations and inventions have spawned solutions across consumer and enterprise contexts that manage our physical and financial health, improve workplace productivity, accelerate academic achievement, and more. Often, their inventions integrate and build on semiconductors because they are an essential component of all kinds of electronic devices. Chips have enabled advances in communications, computing, healthcare, defence, transportation, clean energy, and countless other applications.   Our small tech business members thrive in legal environments in which they can contribute as well as utilise technologies to grow and innovate. Improved access to semiconductors across Europe will help technology-focused businesses of all sizes and boost European leadership in innovation, competition, and transparency. We, thus, support the goals of the European Chips Act and appreciate the opportunity to submit our feedback to the European Commission.   II. The proposed European Chips Act package A. General comments Semiconductors’ role as the engine of Europe’s twin digital and green transitions continues to grow. By 2026, their value in automotive applications will reach €100 billion. Already, over 65 per cent of health tech companies utilise chips in their devices, a percentage that will only grow in the next couple of years. Given these statistics, we believe it is highly important for the European economy for the Chips Act to succeed.   We appreciate this important initiative and emphasise that regulatory coherence is essential to ensure its success. Industry players, especially small and medium-sized enterprises like our members, thrive in environments with legal certainty and predictability that allows them to plan and make manufacturing investment decisions. Therefore, the Commission should align the investment goals of its recent Communication2 with those in the Chips Act, particularly in Pillar II of the proposed Regulation.  European chip manufacturing faces other challenges, such as high energy prices, attracting expertise from third countries, and the location of suppliers of components and raw materials. Chapter II Pillar One: Chips for Europe Initiative  The App Association, overall, welcomes the direction of Pillar One. Particularly, we appreciate the design capacities approach, pilot lines development, and integration of quantum chips. Further, a network of competence centres is hugely important as a resource for skills development, especially for end-user SMEs and startups. We endorse the Commission’s planned promotion of such centres. As for the proposed Chips Fund, as stated above, we would encourage the Commission to clarify its proposed investment. We note and welcome the explicit mention of startups, scaleups, and SMEs as beneficiaries of the Chips Fund. The establishment of a private-public European Chips Infrastructure Consortium (‘ECIC’) could be useful, and we appreciate the inclusion of private sector stakeholders. However, we emphasise that the implementation of the ECIC should follow general EU competition law to ensure a level playing field and maximum operational efficiency of this body.  Concerning the development of native design capabilities, we appreciate the Commission’s emphasis on promoting instruction set architectures like RISC-V. 5G and 6G, for example, rely on one of the most complex products on the market: System-on-a-Chip (SoC). Thus, accessing chip prototypes will spur European innovation, and, thus, funding is of crucial importance in this context.  The pilot lines for new production capabilities should include experimentation, testing, and manufacturing of advanced and mature nodes. We also encourage the Commission to take advantage of the strengths of the European semiconductor industry, such as larger power semiconductors and sensors. All kinds of industrial applications will need a broad range of chips to function in the future, and, thus, the focus of the pilot lines’ structure sizes should align with the needs of industry. Further, the Commission should clarify the terms and conditions of pilot lines. In terms of third parties, it would be helpful to understand better which third parties can access pilot lines and the software and hardware content of the proposed EU virtual design platform. As an SME representative, the App Association particularly appreciates the promised clear rules for intellectual property and the use of SMEs for the virtual design platform. We emphasise that the design of IP rules must involve industry stakeholders of all sizes.  Regarding the Chips for Europe Initiative, and more specifically the Chips Joint Undertaking (CJU), we urge the Commission to consider industry needs as much as possible and to address the existing gap between basic research and the commercial deployment of chips in applications like manufacturing, artificial intelligence, telecommunications infrastructure, automotive, health, and many others. The Commission should, thus, clarify that the CJU needs to consider the whole value chain, including materials, design, manufacturing, assembly, and testing.  C. Chapter III Pillar Two: Security of supply  The App Association welcomes the introduction of the instrument for first-of-a-kind facilities. We further appreciate the Commission’s clarification of the possibilities for state aid for large semiconductor investments (Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU) Art. 107 (3) (c) 7). These provisions will help to create a framework for new semiconductor facilities, potentially allowing for broad investments that are not bound to technologies or node sizes. While this will surely generate new investments and manufacturing projects in Europe, we emphasise that the economics of semiconductor manufacturing are particular and do not apply to other industries. The Commission should ensure its use of TFEU Art. 107 (3) (c) 7 continues to apply only in cases of market failures and does not set a legal basis for ‘picking winners’ in other industries. Additionally, we believe the Commission should expand on the eligibility criteria for first-of-a-kind facilities, particularly concerning

the international government cooperation and the recognition of ongoing research, development, and innovation activities at the EU level.   Concerning the selection and deselection of facilities, we encourage the Commission to conduct an open consultation process that includes the operator of the facility as well as the European Semiconductor Board. The Commission should further explicitly state the role of the Board in the decision-making process, whether appeals are possible, and if a loss of rights includes repayment obligations for state aid.  The App Association fully supports the proposed national fast-tracking of permit granting procedures. This provision represents a concrete step towards improving doing business in the EU. In terms of funding, we believe the Commission should provide clearer guidance on how entities can demonstrate a funding gap to receive state aid. Concerning the proposed certification of chips, we believe any initiative to establish the certification of chips should be voluntary. Further, it should be based on international and market-driven standards and involve industry in developing such standards.  D. Chapter IV Pillar Three: Monitoring and crisis response  Overall, the App Association has some concerns relating to Pillar Three regarding regulatory and competition policy. While we generally strongly support transparency in the value chain, the crisis monitoring mechanism seems disproportionate and ineffective. The extent of market intervention remains unclear as well as what constitutes a ‘critical state’. Additionally, it seems counterintuitive to have 27 national authorities across Europe to report relevant information to, rather than having a single EU-level body. The proposed approach may lead to procedural inconsistencies and duplicative reporting requirements. We thus encourage the Commission to revisit this proposal and consider the establishment of a single monitoring body.  Further clarification concerning the mandatory prioritisation, mandatory release of information, joint procurement, and export controls would be welcome. Concerning information gathering and mandatory release of information, the Commission should reconcile the goals of greater supply chain transparency and minimising the risk of exposing sensitive data. Cyber attacks, data breaches, or other security incidents impact a company’s bottom line and its future success. Thus, further safeguards to protect a company from the unintended exposure of data would be welcome.   Similarly, the Commission should work with industry to develop the proposed requirements for priority rated orders and to determine essential sectors that need chips. Fair chips access in all sectors is a key goal for the EU and while priority orders could be useful to achieve this goal, they are only one part of a broader solution to address upstream supply chain disruptions. Additionally, we urge the Commission to assess common purchasing, state allocation, or monopolised chips purchases very carefully.   In Pillar Three, the Commission proposes export restrictions as a solution to occurrences of ‘significant shortages’ but does not provide details on what those controls would look like. Since export controls are a significant measure, we urge the Commission to clarify what type of measures it envisions and how it would implement them. With global supply chains, export controls require careful design, as they often trigger unintended consequences.  E. Chapter V: Governance  In light of the existing broad committee landscape, the Commission must clarify the EU Semiconductor Board’s specific mission and powers. Further, the Board should have an institutionalised exchange

with industry stakeholders concerning a variety of topics including funding, chip certification, technology deployment, and identification of critical shortages.  III. Conclusion The App Association welcomes the opportunity to submit feedback to the European Commission’s proposal for a Chips Act. The Chips Act is a crucial step to establishing Europe as a global chip producer, and we support a robust and cohesive legal framework to achieve this goal. Other Commission goals, like the successful green and digital transformations as well as a resilient industrial policy, depend on semiconductors, and we commend the Commission for launching this initiative. We further encourage the Commission to consider semiconductor production and its geopolitical aspects holistically, especially in the context of global value chains and how the Commission can work to establish a level playing field. To strengthen the value chain, we encourage the Commission to also focus on other relevant areas such as expertise in cybersecurity, materials, production processes, and equipment. Considering current semiconductor shortages, we appreciate the speed with which the Commission has acted and would welcome a quick implementation and distribution of funds.   We thank the Commission for its consideration of our comments and look forward to future engagement and discussion on the topic of standardisation.  Sincerely,

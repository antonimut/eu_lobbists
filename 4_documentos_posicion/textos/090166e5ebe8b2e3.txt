IQM FEEDBACK ON EUROPEAN CHIPS ACT PACKAGE 
 
About IQM Quantum Computers 
 
IQM Finland Oy (“IQM”) is the European category leader in building quantum computers with over €50 
million signed contracts in the last year. IQM is the only quantum start-up in the world to have delivered 
an on-premises quantum computer with full access to a customer – VTT in Finland and is preparing to 
deliver a second one in Germany. IQM is made in Europe and delivers full-stack quantum computers 
from QPU design to manufacturing and  system integration, done in Europe. IQM also creates all its 
intellectual property (“IP”) in Europe from its hubs in Finland, France, Germany, and Spain. In 2022, 
the company will continue to accelerate its development and commercialization of quantum 
processors, thanks to a €35 million support from the European Guarantee Fund of the European 
Investment Bank. 
 
Feedback 
 
IQM welcomes the opportunity to share its comments on the European Chips Act package. IQM has 
provided a set of recommendations to raise the level of ambition of the Chips Act package in relation 
to the technologies at the core of the digital and green transitions, quantum computing. The European 
Chips Act Package should take a clear stance on supporting quantum. The following measures should 
be considered:  
 
• Ensure that the Chips Fund incentivizes European start -ups to manufacture quantum 
technologies in Europe 
o IQM fully supports the objective of the Chips Act’s Pillar One: Chips for Europe 
Initiative, of building advanced technology and engineering capacities for accelerating 
the innovative development of quantum chips. 
  
o As part of the Pillar One, the proposed Chips Fund instrument has the potential to 
quickly spur both public and private sector investments in state-of-the-art technologies 
in the semicondu ctor value chain, including next -generation quantum chips, and 
restore Europe’s self-reliance in key industrial sectors. 
 
o The Chips Fund appears to be the main financing instrument for start -ups, scaleups 
and SMEs under the Chips Act, to bu ild sustainable E U supply chains. As for the 
largest players, they would benefit from th e financing instruments under Pillar Two – 
Security of Supply to address semiconductor crisis shortages as they occur. To 
achieve both objectives, the financial breakdo wn betwe en the different Chips Act

• Provide support to quantum design tools and pilot fabrication lines for the prototyping, testing,

o The Covid-19 pandemic has revealed the gaps in the European value chain, but it has

o To ensure that the Chips Act delivers its p romise and that a certain part of the global 
production is made in Europe, it will be cru cial for the EU to bet on the players 
developing breakthrough products with a proven ability to scale them up. Hence, it will 
be crucial that the funds available under the Chips Fund and the pilot lines are directed

tomorrow. 
 
o The Chips Act should guarantee that these players can contribute to the pilot lines for 
testing and experimentation and design foreseen under Pillar One, at the same level 
as all other value chain players. 
 
• Ensure the European start-up ecosystem contributes to the governance of the Chips Act 
o The Commission proposes to establish a multistakeholder governance framework for 
the Chips Act, that involves the Commission itself and EU Member States as part of 
the future Semiconductor Board, as well as the future Chips Joint Undertaking which 
should be convening the whole ecosystem. Smaller actors se eking funding under the 
various instruments defined by the Chips Act will greatly benefit from a clearer breakout 
of the roles and responsibilities between each governance stakeholder.

• Foster skills, talent, and innovation in quantum technologies 
o IQM welcomes the plans set out in the Chips Act to create a network of competence 
centers across the EU that would foster s kills and talent and facilitate access to pilot 
lines and design facilities. IQM has qui ckly grasped the importance of setting 
excellence hubs in several key locations in Europe, building a network of quantum 
researchers that has the effect of attracting other top researchers and engineers across 
the world in Europe.  
 
o The company now has the l argest industrial hardware team of world -class quantum 
experts in Europe, with over 640+ scientific publications and over 27000 citations. The 
future EU network of competence centers needs to embrace the diversity of European 
actors and practices that lead to forming and attracting talent in quantum, and not just 
the largest organizations. Considering the global competition to attract talent from a 
small pool, the EU network of competence centers should forge synergies between the 
private, public, and academic communities and raise the level of in terest for next -
generation technologies among researchers and scientists. 
 
• Implement clear quantum policy and agreements to build international partnerships with like-
minded countries 
o Concluding partnerships with like-minded countries across the world will be crucial for 
the EU to assert its leadership on the global stage. The partnership between the EU 
Quantum Flagship and its US counterparts has already shown some benefits in areas 
where the EU and US share common interest. This partnership, which has been so far 
limited to basic scientific research, should explore new lines of cooperation, in the fields 
of standardization or funding for instance. 
 
It is important that start ups like IQM are given the chance to scale up  the quantum enabling 
infrastructure. Only with enough quantum enabling infrastructure can we guarantee that the EU will 
be a serious contender in the quantum race. Furthermore, it is important that SMEs get sup port to 
develop Design Automation tools which are crucial for the IP generation of  next generation 
processors.

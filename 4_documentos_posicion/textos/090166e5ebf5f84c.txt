 EU Chips Act Proposal for a Regulation 
 Position Paper & Recommendations 
 Air  Liquide  welcomes  and  supports  the  EU  Chips  Act  Package  released  on  February  8,  2022  to  ensure  European 
 strategic  autonomy  and  leadership  in  semiconductor  manufacturing  by  doubling  Europe’s  share  in  semiconductor 
 production capacity to 20% by 2030 (that means to increase by 4 times the manufacturing capacity vs today). 
 Air Liquide’s three key recommendations: 
 1.  Create an EU integrated ecosystem for microelectronics 
 ●  This  would  allow  having  a  transversal  view  on  the  manufacturing  capacity,  including  all  required  raw 
 materials for the manufacturing in Europe, and its supply chain and anticipate shortages. 
 2.  Ensure a resilient raw materials supply chain 
 ●  Integrate  in  the  Chips  Act  the  concept  of  “products  &  materials”  supply  chain  to  manufacture  the  chips  in 
 Europe,  explicitly  including  the  necessary  high-purity  gases,  materials  and  related  products  in  the  “raw 
 material deﬁnition”. 
 ●  EU  semiconductor  manufacturers  must  have  an  open,  secured  access  to  critical  materials  from 
 manufacturers in Asia and in the USA that include the shipping of these critical materials to Europe. 
 3.  Ensure  that  the  supply  chain  of  gases  &  chemicals  is  supported  and  sized  for  the  production 
 demand of semiconductors in Europe 
 ●  The  EU  Chips  Act  proposal  should  aim  at  restoring  the  manufacturing  capacity  in  Europe  of  critical 
 products  and  materials  necessary  for  the  manufacturing  of  semiconductors  in  Europe  -  at  the  quality  levels 
 and  compliant  with  the  requirements  of  the  semiconductor  industry.  This  should  be  done  through  agile, 
 easily  accessible  and  adequately  sized  funding  schemes.  These  funding  should  be  subject  to  simpliﬁed 
 procedures  to  get  subsidies  so  as  to  bolster  and  extend  the  raw  materials  and  specialty  gases  supply-chain 
 and their industrial manufacturing capability in Europe. 
 ●  The  funding  programs  shall  boost  innovation  but  also  help  economical  actors  to  invest  in  new  or 
 refurbished  manufacturing  facilities  located  in  Europe,  and  devoted  to  the  production  of  molecules  or 
 products needed for semiconductors’ manufacturing. 
 A  world  leader  in  gases,  technologies  and  services  for  Industry  and  Health,  Air  Liquide  is  present  in  75  countries  with 
 approximately  66,400  employees  and  serves  more  than  3.8  million  customers  and  patients.  Oxygen,  nitrogen  and 
 hydrogen  are  essential  small  molecules  for  life,  matter  and  energy.  They  embody  Air  Liquide’s  scientiﬁc  territory  and  have 
 been at the core of the company’s activities since its creation in 1902. 
 Among  its  core  activities,  Air  Liquide  manufactures,  imports  and  supplies  products,  molecules  and  materials  -  including 
 ultra-high  purity  gases,  manufacturing  units,  gas  &  chemicals  distribution  equipment  and  related  metrology  and  services  - 
 that are essential to semiconductor manufacturers. 
 Being  the  leading  industrial  gas  company  in  the  European  semiconductor  environnement,  Air  Liquide  aims  at  securing  the 
 supplies  of  products,  molecules  and  materials  for  the  manufacturing  of  semiconductors  at  European  level  in  a  viable, 
 innovative and competitive manner.

 PROPOSED COMMENTS TO THE EU CHIPS ACT REGULATION PROPOSAL 
 CHAPTER I - General Provisions 
 General considerations 
 Air  Liquide  welcomes  the  deﬁnition  proposed  in  the  regulation  that  covers  extensively  all  the  actors  involved  in 
 the  semiconductor  industry  and  associated  supply  chains.  Some  deﬁnitions  should  however  provide  more 
 clarity  and  certainty.  On  art.  2  (4),  to  be  fully  comprehensive,  this  deﬁnition  should  explicitly  include  under  the 
 “raw  materials”  terminology  gases  and  related  products.  Ensuring  the  security  of  supply  for  chips  for  the 
 internal  market  would  be  in  vain  if  foremost  the  supply  of  gases  &  chemicals  is  not  securised  upstream. 
 Indeed,  that  would  be  unwise  to  invest  public  money  in  new  manufacturing  capacities  in  Europe  if  these 
 essential  raw  materials  and  molecules  (i.e.  NF3,  CF4,  HFC  gases  ,  Silane)  are  missing.  Moreover,  the  proposal 
 for  the  EU  Chips  Act  should  include  the  concept  of  “products  &  materials”  supply  chain  to  be  able  to 
 manufacture the chips in Europe  . 
 CHAPTER II - Chips For Europe Initiative 
 Focus  on  the  investments  that  will  increase  manufacturing  capabilities  to  hit  the  proposal’s  goal  of  20%  of 
 global chip production by 2030 
 Art.  3  foresees  that  the  Initiative  is  expected  to  be  supported  by  funding  from  the  Horizon  Europe  and  the 
 Digital  Europe  programmes.  Increasing  industry’s  ability  to  manufacture  chips  in  Europe  is  instrumental  to 
 build  supply  chain  resilience  to  current  and  future  shortages.  The  EU  Chips  Act  should  further  strengthen 
 Europe’s  capabilities  in  supporting  larger  manufacturing  capacity  on  the  continent,  including  securing  the 
 availability  of  necessary  resources  and  materials  for  their  production.  Therefore,  Air  Liquide  calls  for 
 mechanisms  also  earmarking  fundings  to  new  or  refurbished  manufacturing  facilities  located  in  Europe 
 dedicated  to  the  production  of  molecules  or  products  needed  for  semiconductors’  manufacturing.  To  this 
 end,  public  co-funding  of  CAPEX  (for  ex.  land  costs  acquisition,  building  costs  etc.)  should  be  considered  as 
 well  in  order  to  ensure  that  production  sites  for  raw  materials  suppliers’  facilities  will  be  set  up  and/  or 
 expanded  in  Europe.  Additionally,  in  the  light  of  energy  price  surges  that  the  industry  has  been  experiencing 
 the  last  few  months  and  exacerbated  due  to  the  geopolitical  crisis,  the  EU  should  also  address  the  OPEX 
 dimension  as it will have direct repercussions on the supply chain robustness and competitiveness. 
 Simpliﬁed procedures for swift public co-funding at European level 
 Indeed,  agile  funding  schemes  and  simpliﬁed  state  aid  procedures  are  needed  to  accompany  the  required 
 investments  and  keep  up  the  production  pace  in  comparison  with  non-EU  regions.  It  is  to  be  noted  that  the 
 IPCEI  is  not  the  most  suitable  tool  with  regards  to  its  complex  process  and  timelines  to  match  the  context  of 
 the  electronic  supply  chain.  Accelerating  approval  and  planning  processes  is  key  and  the  EU  and  Member 
 States  should  speed  up  planning  and  approval  processes  to  ensure  that  European  semiconductor  projects 
 are ready for implementation within months  .

 CHAPTER III - Security Of Supply 
 Coordinating policy actions with global partners in the US and Asia 
 Despite  Europe  homes  world-leading  suppliers  for  specialty  gases  such  as  Air  Liquide,  Europe  remains  highly 
 dependent  on  third  countries  on  certain  materials.  In  that  view  and  considering  the  global  and  complex  nature 
 of  the  semiconductors  supply  chain,  the  EU  must  remain  open  with  non-EU  partners  in  Asia  and  with  the  US. 
 For  the  time  being,  agreements  with  third  countries  located  in  Asia  or  the  United  States  to  secure  the 
 availability  of  selected/critical  products  required  by  semiconductor  manufacturing.  In  particular  chemical 
 gases (advanced materials) and chemicals’ imports need to be secured. 
 Facilitate shipping and customs from key regions 
 The  EU  Chips  Acts  should  include  in  its  proposal  provisions  to  secure  priority  in  products  shipping  from  Asia 
 and  the  US  (i.e.  priority  treatment  on  container  ships,  customs  clearance  and  export  GHG  quota  notably)  as 
 this  aspect  is  not  covered  in  the  proposal.  Delays  in  shipping  have  caused  acute  shortages  of  raw  materials 
 and  other  essential  materials  used  in  the  manufacturing  process,  increasing  supply  uncertainties  about 
 whether these materials are delivered on schedule. 
 Create a framework to ensure security of supply 
 Air  Liquide  calls  for  the  development  of  a  fully  integrated  ecosystem  (“end-to-end  ﬁliere”)  in  Europe  to 
 secure  the  supply  of  raw  materials  and  manufacturing  capacity  so  as  to  ensure  a  better  forecast  for  future 
 industry and consumer demands. 
 CHAPTER IV - Monitoring And Crisis Response 
 Priority-rated and scope should be further clariﬁed 
 On  common  purchasing,  Air  Liquide  calls  for  clarity  on  the  speciﬁc  functioning  of  the  mechanism  in  art.  22  for 
 the  Commission  to  act  as  a  central  purchasing  body  for  semiconductor-related  products,  if  and  when  applied. 
 We  identify  two  problems  for  the  set-up  of  this  mechanism:  (i)  the  purchase  of  semiconductor-related 
 products  should  go  through  a  complex  speciﬁcation  qualiﬁcation  process,  making  this  common  purchase 
 inapplicable  as  such  as  every  semiconductor  manufacturer  has  its  own  speciﬁcation  and  qualiﬁcation 
 mechanisms,  (ii)  conﬁdentiality  issues  may  arise  as  the  safeguards  in  art.  27  may  put  at  risk  to  further 
 exacerbate  data  exposure  concerns,  as  they  entail  the  possibility  of  further  transmission  and  exchange  of 
 data between competent authorities, potentially at global scale. 
 Last  but  not  least,  we  call  for  more  clarity  about  the  way  the  EU-wide  coordinated  procurement  will  be  set  up, 
 and  the  way  stakeholders  or  actors  in  the  supply  chain  would  beneﬁt  from  it  in  case  the  crisis  stage  was 
 activated. 
 CHAPTER V - Governance 
 Role of industry in the European Semiconductor Board 
 Air  Liquide  welcomes  the  fact  that  industries  can  be  invited  to  participate  on  an  ad-hoc  basis  in  the  European 
 Semiconductor  Board  as  stated  in  art.  24.  However,  the  Board  should  also  have  permanent  industrial

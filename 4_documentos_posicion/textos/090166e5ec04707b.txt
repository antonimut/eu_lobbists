EU Data Act
PFA position paper – short version
CSTA31 “Extended Vehicle and data use” 
Ref. Ares(2022)3567506 - 10/05/2022
2
Key takeaways
• The scope of the proposed regulation is very broad and the definitions (data, products, …) are very open.
• The ‘user’ (natural or legal person that owns, rents or leases a product or receives a services) is at the center of the Data 
Act proposal.
• B2B/B2C data sharing :
Art 3.1 : ‘Products shall be designed and manufactured, and related services shall be provided, in such a manner
that data generated by their use are, by default, easily, securely and, where relevant and appropriate, directly
accessible to the user.’
Art 4, 5 : ‘free of charge to the user’ : a legal person (any stakeholders) can use its “free of charge user access right"
to provide services under unfair conditions (e.g. commercial use of data by user without compensation for data
holder, risk of reverse-engineering).
• Privacy / cyber : Art 3.1 seems to be contrary to the principle of proportionality of the GDPR (Privacy-by-design) asking
the collection of data to be limited to what is necessary for the purposes it is collected for. The cyber security principles
(Cyber act, UN R155) also require to expose as less data as possible.
3
Key takeaways
• IP / Trade secrets : No clear safeguards to protect manufacturers' intellectual property (included in the recitals but not in 
the articles). The data holder should always give its consent to share trade secrets.
Regarding exclusive license, same concerns considering that countries outside Europe are not suffering the constraints 
of the Data Act.
• Intellectual property right on Database (Sui generis right – Article 35): 
➔ exclusion of the IP rights of investors and creators of databases whose data is obtained or generated by the 
use of a related product or service. 
➔ limitation of IP rights of stakeholders. A compensatory fee is planned but can be dismissed by law (by member 
state).
➔ contradiction with current French law (IP code).
• Application period (12 months after its entry into force):
➔ too short period about the life cycle of the vehicle and in particular its development time.
➔ longer time for adoption of standard (e.g. smart contracts) and safeguard than for adoption of regulation.

11 May 2022
Position Paper on Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive
The Korea Business Association Europe (KBA Europe), representing over 360 Korean companies in
Europe, appreciates the feedback opportunity regarding the European Commission’s proposal on
Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive (hereinafter referred to as the “proposed directive”).
We welcome the Commission’s initiative for an EU-harmonized regulatory approach to due diligence
on human rights and the environment. At the same time, we respectfully request the Commission to
take into account the following remarks when finalizing the proposed directive.
First, KBA Europe expresses our concern that the scope of the due diligence across the supply
chain is too broad, as the scope of the supply chain under the proposed directive covers the entire value
chain. For multinational corporations that have a complex web of supply chains around the world, it is
not practical, if not impossible, to implement due diligence obligations in a meaningful way that
contributes to the objective of the proposed directive. In particular, the proposed directive requires
companies to conduct due diligence on their suppliers, which also includes “indirectly” established
business relationships. In our view, the broad coverage of the due diligence on the supply chain, as well
as the lack of clarity on key issues (as explained below), imposes unrealistic expectations on companies.
We recommend the Commission to limit the scope of the due diligence on the supply chain to directly
established business relationships.
Second, KBA Europe is concerned that many key issues on how the proposed directive would
work in practice remain undecided. This includes: examples of what constitutes “established business
relationships” (Article 3(g)); the scope of “companies” falling under the proposed directive (Article
3(a)) (e.g., whether it includes a holding company, which owns the asset of a subsidiary company); and
practicability of “contractual assurances” (Articles 7(2) and 8(3)); examples of documents that can be
considered as means of compliance with due diligence rules (other than contracts between companies
and their partners); and the definition of “wholesale trade” (Articles 1(b) and 2(b)). In particular,
although the term “contractual assurances” is mentioned several times and described as a mechanism
to ensure compliance with companies’ code of conduct from their partners with whom they have an
established business relationship, specific details on what needs to be covered in “contractual assurances”
are missing.
These ambiguities increase legal uncertainties and, thus, make it difficult for companies to prepare for
the implementation of the proposed directive. We urge the Commission to issue guidelines on practical
issues, as identified above, and adopt guidance on model contract clauses as soon as possible to provide
clarifications.
Third, KBA Europe emphasizes that financial and technical support envisaged under the
proposed directive must be available to both EU and non-EU companies on an equal basis. We
note that Article 14 requires the EU Member States and the Commission to provide accompanying
measures, such as financial and technical support, to companies, in particular SMEs, and their partners
along the global value chain that are indirectly affected by the obligations of the proposed directive.
The EU Member States and the Commission must ensure that the non-EU companies and their partners
impacted by the proposed directive enjoy the same level of support as the EU companies and their
partners.

Fourth, KBA Europe expresses our concern regarding the increased administrative burden for
companies that may stem from the patchwork of different national laws on due diligence. During
the transposition of the proposed directive, each EU Member State is likely to adopt different rules on
due diligence obligations. This means that companies will face fragmentation of national laws, in
particular, with respect to sanctions and civil liability and thus bear the increased administrative burden
for compliance with different national rules. We suggest the Commission to provide a consolidated list
of Member States’ national laws on due diligence and regularly update the list for the public1 to help
companies understand different rules in the EU Member States.
Fifth, KBA Europe stresses the importance of coherent enforcement of due diligence rules by the
EU Member States. Given that national supervisory authorities will be primarily responsible for the
enforcement of due diligence rules, this risks inconsistent enforcement among the EU Member States.
We note that, according to Article 21, the Commission will set up a European Network of Supervisory
Authorities (“ENSA”), composed of representatives from national supervisory authorities, to facilitate
cooperation among authorities and coordinated investigation, sanctions, and supervisory practices. We
suggest the ENSA to ensure consistent enforcement among the EU Member States and to speak
directly with companies when necessary.
Lastly, KBA Europe urges the Commission to establish a reporting requirement under the
proposed directive that is workable and not burdensome for companies.Article 11 requires the EU
Member States to oblige companies to report their implementation of due diligence and publish annual
statements on their website. From this point of view, the Commission must adopt delegated acts
regarding the content and criteria of such reporting. In so doing, we suggest the Commission to ensure
that the contents and standards are closely aligned with those under the Commission’s proposal on
Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive.
We thank you for your kind consideration of the aforementioned suggestions. We look forward to
having regular contact with decision-makers in the EU, including the Commission, and discussing the
proposed directive as it develops further.
Yours sincerely,

 
DI response to the European Commission’s public consultation 
on the Single Market Emergency Instrument (SMEI) 
General comments 
 
The Confederation of Danish Industry (DI) supports the European Commission’s ambi-
tion to enhance the resilience of the Single Market’s four freedoms in future times of cri-
sis. We agree that crisis-mitigating measures should be readily available – under strict 
criteria – and swiftly applied. This requires a clear legal framework at EU level. In this 
regard, we welcome the upcoming proposal for a Single Market Emergency Instrument 
(SMEI). 
 
The scope of SMEI should be narrow and focus on the protection of the Single Market 
freedoms under specific crisis conditions. SMEI should also facilitate an open dialogue 
among the European Commission, Member States and key stakeholders in order to ad-
dress unforeseen crisis-related challenges in a swift and coordinated manner. 
 
Conversely, the scope of SMEI should not be broadened to also include objectives related 
to the EU’s industrial, trade or health policies. In DI’s opinion, it is important to avoid 
duplication. Enhancing the strategic independence of Europe’s economy is better ad-
dressed with dedicated legislative and non-legislative tools in their relevant policy do-
mains. Consequently, SMEI should be sharply focused on short-term, crisis-related resil-
ience of the Single Market. 
 
The provisions of SMEI should be designed with proportionality in mind, e.g. with re-
gards to crisis-related obligations for companies. Furthermore, the instrument’s defini-
tion of “crisis” must be clear and precise, even as it encompasses different types of crises. 
European companies have faced persistent burdens brought about by “temporary” crisis 
measures becoming de facto permanent – both in Europe and abroad. Examples include 
trade restrictions imposed as a response to the global financial crisis in 2008 and per-
petual suspension of border-free travel within parts of the Schengen Area. SMEI should 
be carefully designed so as not to impose such long-lasting barriers to any of the four 
freedoms once a particular crisis has passed. 
 
Finally, DI would like to emphasize the central importance of pan-European and inter-
national value chains for European businesses and their employees. SMEI should do as 
much as possible to maintain the conditions for open and flexible flow of goods, compo-
nents, and employees across borders. Any tools that seek to respond to a crisis by limit-
ing the availability of such value chains must be proportional, thoroughly justified, and 
short-lived in nature.

Specific comments – company experiences from the COVID-19 pandemic 
 
DI considers the COVID-19 pandemic as the prime example of a “crisis” that a future 
SMEI toolbox can help to alleviate. Below, we have summarized some our member com-
panies’ most pertinent experiences with the EU’s crisis response in that context. 
 
Companies’ positive experiences with the EU’s crisis response: 
 
• The introduction on 23 March 2020 of the so-called “green lanes” alleviated many 
costly bottlenecks related to intra-EU flow of especially goods but also services. It 
should be noted, however, that there were some issues with the implementation of 
the green lanes – not least due to disagreement on which products were deemed 
“critical”. Concrete examples include the labelling of certain machinery as “critical” 
but that spare parts and components for such machinery was not critical. This para-
doxical situation lead to serious issues in cases of manufacturing and maintenance.  
• The relatively swift adoption of a common EU Digital COVID Certificate helped 
to restore much-missed mobility within the Single Market for cross-border service 
providers and business travellers. 
• While problems of mobility persisted, the (voluntary) coordinated guidelines for 
travel restrictions adopted by the Council on 13 October 2020 was a positive step 
to increase the transparency and predictability of travel restrictions within the Single 
Market. 
 
Areas where the EU response could have been better: 
 
• The exchange of and access to timely information, as well as the coordination 
of crisis-mitigation actions between Member States and the Commission should be 
reinforced. Improving the provision of information in times of crisis must be one of 
the core tasks of SMEI. A common dedicated online information interface (such as 
the webpage of DG MOVE during the first wave of COVID-19 in Europe, later the Re-
OpenEU application) should be immediately operational when a crisis strikes, up-
dated regularly, and include easily understandable and reliable information. A fully 
operational Single Digital Gateway could serve as the basis with some of the 
measures being: 
➢ Reliable and timely (minimum 48 hours in advance), widely accessible infor-
mation for companies about changes in crisis-related travel or other re-
strictions/conditions; 
➢ Extensive and regularly updated overviews of measures in various Member 
States accessible to internationally operating businesses; 
➢ Hotline for companies and citizens for the 24/7 identification of Single Mar-
ket disruptions in times of crisis, allowing quick exchange to be made be-
tween various parties (business federations/member states/EC) to solve the 
problems. The SOLVIT network could serve as inspiration. 
 
• Naturally, free movement of workers in general and free movement of service-pro-
viders in particular, were heavily impacted by the crisis. One area where companies 
experienced varying degrees of difficulties across Member States related to the ques-
tion of social security contribution for service-providers who were “stuck” in 
another Member State. Some Member States waved the regular rules that require 
companies to pay social security contributions to the “host Member States”. There 
was however no EU coordination or common approach, leading to uncertainty 
3 
 
regarding potential extra social security expenses for many companies who were al-
ready in financial difficulties. 
 
• DI companies experienced difficulties regarding service provision due to disparate 
quarantine rules across the Single Market. A concrete example: A member company 
that works with the servicing of industrial wastewater systems faced issues when at-
tempting to send their technicians to a physical site. Contractually, they were obliged 
to do so within 48 hours. When their technicians crossed the borders of either Po-
land or Germany, however, they were put in quarantine for 14 days and in turn were 
not able to provide their essential service. This showcased potential gaps in the Com-
mission guidelines on quarantine application in critical occupations. A more 
robust framework would be a benefit of SMEI. 
 
• In general, the European Commission was quick to realize the necessity of some 
short-term flexibility regarding the EU’s state aid rules in relation to government 
compensation schemes for the hardest-hit companies and sectors. However, in prac-
tice, there were still issues with the authorization of certain types of emer-
gency state aid. A concrete example was the lack of EU approval for elements of 
the Danish government’s support scheme for companies that had experienced a loss 
of revenue caused by restrictions, border closures and travel guidelines. One of the 
issues was linked to DG COMP demanding the schemes to be notified and approved 
based on the “temporary framework” for state aid. However, this framework had a 
support ceiling set at 3 million EUR per company in the entire period covered by the 
scheme. This caused problems for some Danish companies, because the aid ceiling 
was calculated on company group level and not on individual company level, hereby 
creating a big financial disadvantage for companies structured in groups and clearly 
showed the need for a more flexible approval in the specific crisis period.  
Specific comments – potential provisions of SMEI 
 
DI has the following comments to the potential provisions of SMEI as listed in the two 
pillars of the consultation. 
 
I. The CRISIS PREPAREDNESS pillar: 
 
• DI is not in favour of a “module for targeted monitoring of identified strategic sup-
ply chains to anticipate shortages”. The same applies to “mitigating measures, in-
cluding possible strategic storage or stockpiling system(s), for increasing the avail-
ability of goods of key strategic importance such as critical raw materials”. This 
set of issues is already being addressed by initiatives such as the Chips Act, the EU 
institutions’ work on strategic dependencies as well as by the Industrial Forum. DI 
reiterates that SMEI should not duplicate the EU’s industrial policy tools. 
 
• Regarding “regular assessment of risks and preparedness” and “emergency train-
ings and drills for relevant national and EU experts”, DI does not yet have a final 
position. While it could improve the EU’s latent crisis response capabilities, any such 
measures should be designed carefully with cost-effectiveness and proportionality in 
mind. 
II. The CRISIS RESPONSE pillar: 
4 
 
• DI is in favour of a special procedure for accelerating the setting of require-
ments for products that are deemed “critical” in the crisis at hand. Such a 
procedure should build upon the following principles: 
➢ Both product requirements and technical specifications should be approved 
quickly.  
➢ Ideally, the technical specifications in this exceptional procedure should be de-
veloped as harmonised standards.  
➢ The special procedure should only be triggered for truly “critical” products. 
SMEI should set out a set of criteria that clearly set out when and how a product 
qualifies as “critical”. 
➢ SMEI should provide for earmarked funds that can be used to pay for the devel-
opment of the necessary standards so that they are available to all users. This 
will strengthen the dissemination of concrete information on which require-
ments the products in question must live up to.  
➢ Market surveillance authorities need additional resources and guidelines in or-
der to deal with “critical” products coming from outside the Single Market. This 
includes information campaigns by the Commission to the market surveillance 
authorities as well as towards businesses and consumers. Such provisions are 
necessary if the EU is to have safeguards against ineffective/dangerous illegal 
“critical” products in a time of crisis and/or shortages.  
 
• Regarding “additional clarifications and possibilities for joint action in the area of 
public procurement”, it is unclear at this point which circumstances qualify for 
activating the use of the instrument. DI recommends a narrow scope of the applica-
tion that is limited to international crisis situations such as COVID-19. This is im-
portant in order to safeguard that public procurement is opened up to competition 
and to avoid a variety of domestic situations that can lead to protectionism. 
➢ DI is concerned with a situation of rash decision making and direct award with-
out prior publication. We experienced situations like these in Denmark during 
2020 and 2021, where contracting authorities avoided public tenders due to 
time sensitivity, when they procured COVID-19 Quick-tests exceeding the EU 
threshold value. This had led to formal complaints being filed at the Danish 
Complaints Board for Public Procurement.  
➢ DI suggests, that the creation of the SMEI initiative considers the Commissions’ 
guidance on public procurement during the COVID-19 crisis from April 2020. 
Here the Commission clearly states that “European public procurement rules 
provide all the necessary tools to satisfy those needs, under the provisions of the 
Directive”.  
 
• It is not clear what a “module for targeted information requests to economic opera-
tors” would entail. DI notes that the formulation indicates “information requests to” 
and not “information requests by” economic operators, i.e. companies. DI would like 
to stress that businesses should not be burdened by excessive and disproportionate 
additional information requirements – especially in a time of disruption and/or cri-
sis. This can especially hamper SMEs that would need the ability to receive timely 
and precise information from the authorities (see the section above for our proposal 
on increased information and transparency for companies). 
 
• DI understands the intention with the so-called “measures of last resort”, such as 
the “ramping up of production capacity for products relevant for a certain type of 
crisis, possibly encouraged by targeted and necessary financial support or 
5 
 
speeding up permitting procedures at national level”. It is of course paramount that 
the conditions for such provisions are clear, well-defined and proportionate. How-
ever, DI is not convinced of the need for such provisions at EU level. They are not re-
lated to safeguarding the functioning of the Single Market in a time of crisis, which 
should remain the narrow focus of SMEI. As such, DI would prefer that such 
“measures of last resort” be taken at national level – with appropriate channels for 
information-sharing among Member States. 
➢ On a related note: The COVID-19 crisis led to some companies shifting their 
manufacturing activities from their ordinary product to crisis-related products 
such as disinfectant and personal protective equipment. Companies did this to 
play their part in the acute crisis. However, some of them were subsequently 
forced to pull their “crisis products” from the market due to potential label-
ling/packaging confusion by consumers (e.g. disinfectant packaged in “juice 
box” shaped packaging). As a component of SMEI, the Commission could pro-
vide guidance for such “dual use” products and potential labelling/warnings to 
make it easier for companies to shift production to “crisis products”. 
 
• DI would support an additional “notification mechanism for national crisis 
measures including fast-track procedures” as a more swift system could be benefi-
cial in times of crisis. However, further details are of course needed to see how this 
could be done in practice. 
 
DI looks forward to the Commission’s final proposal and we stand ready and available for 
further dialogue and engagement related to SMEI.

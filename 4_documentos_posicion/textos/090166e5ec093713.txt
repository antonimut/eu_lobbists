HOTREC1 reply to the Commission consultation on the EU Package Travel Directive (PTD) 
10 May 2022 
Summary  
HOTREC is committed to enhance consumer protection and  improve hotel guest experience. HOTREC 
supports a targeted revision of the E U Directive 2015/2302  to better reflect the specificities of  
hospitality business es in Europe  from which 90% are micro -enterprises and 99% are SMEs . The 
hospitality sector was hit hard by the COVID-19 pandemic and is now severely affected by rising energy 
and food prices. Measures tailored to small hospitality businesses are detrimental to a swift and solid 
recovery. HOTREC calls for a simplification of the scope and definitions of the PTD. 
 
While the paramount objective of the PTD was to enhance travellers ’ protection, the current 
framework constitutes hurdles for hoteliers and in some cases jeopardise the serene delivery of hotel 
services and impact on travellers’ experience.  
I.1 Market developments 
90% of HOTREC members are micro-enterprises and 99% SMEs. Since the entry into application of the 
PTD, some establishments have stopped providing a selection of hotel services combined with 
accommodation fearing they would fall under the scope of the PTD and be considered as liable package 
organisers. Others avoid partnering with local providers by fear of constituting Linked Travel 
Arrangements (LTAs). 
This impacts upon hotel guest s experience and total revenues generated by hotels. This comes  in 
addition to two hard years for the sector since the outbreak of the COVID19 pandemic, increased 
energy and food prices, severe financial difficulties and struggle to recruit. 
I.2 Definition and scope of application 
The scope and definitions are too broad while pre-contractual information required is prescriptive. 
It causes legal uncertainty and administrative burdens2 for establishments and misunderstandings for 
travellers. HOTREC recommends to: 
• clarify the definition of package to cover less travel services (Art. 3) 
• question the definition of  Click-through booking  (Art. 3(2)(b)(v)) as it has currently limited 
practical value. Click-through bookings are qualified as packages but are difficult  to handle in 
the day-to-day booking process as their definition is not easy to understand for both customers 
and organisers. This has an impact upon an acute application of the rules in various 
establishments and Member States.

• simplify the concept of LTAs to ensure that local cooperation between two service providers 
without remuneration, are exempt (Art. 3(5)).  
• extend the list of services intrinsically part of hotel services detailed in recital (17).  For 
instance, we suggest adding spo rts and cultural activities, e.g. ski pass, opera tickets, golf 
courses, spa treatments. Hotels often sell combined services falling under the scope of the PTD 
when the value of the other tourism service exceeds 25% of the total price even if not 
representing an essential feature of the combination, e.g. a wine tasting (50€) sold together 
with a hotel room (150€) exceeds 25% of the total value.  Recital 17 lists services intrinsically 
part of hotel services that should not be considered as travel services in their own right and do 
not constitute a package if combined with accommodation. It is to note that the Italian law 
provides an extended list of tourist services that are considered intrinsically part of 
accommodation, including : entertainment; sports acti vities, the use of bicycles, skiing 
equipment and other facilities, access to the beach, and any other typical service according to 
local habits. Extending the list of examples of services in recital 17 would contribute to 
harmonise practices in the EU27. 
• raise the 25% threshold to 30% (Recital (18)).  
• clarify ‘business travel’ liability  (Art 2(2)(c). As this stage, all business travels are covered by 
the PTD unless ‘purchased on the basis of a general agreement for the arrangement’. It doesn’t 
protect smal l businesses and small hotels who fail to impose a general agreement or a 
framework contract. Small travel businesses contracts are therefore not covered by the PTD 
and small hotels end up being liable for big corporates.  
• simplify pre-contractual information requirements (Art. 5), i.e. A5.1(viii) & (f) in particular on 
visa requirements and suitability of persons with reduced mobility.  
A mere overnight stay combined with tourism services offered & provided by the same service 
provider should never constitute a package. 
 
I.3 Cancellations, liquidity and reimbursements 
1. Vouchers should be specified in the PTD as viable alternatives to a refund within 14 days. We 
are however concerned by the requirement to have these vouchers protected against 
insolvency.  
2. According to Art 12 (2), the traveller has a right of withdrawal free of charge in case of 
unavoidable and extraordinary circumstances . The definition of una voidable and 
extraordinary circumstances (Art 3 (12)) must be clarified and understood in a way that only 
unforeseeable events are covered.  The PTD should however not regulate cancellation rights 
that remain subject to national contract law.

I.4 Insolvency protection and pre-payments 
1. Article 17 in its current form generates high costs for small hospitality businesses. Since the 
end of 2020 and the COVID19 pandemic, insolvency protection insurance or bank guarantees 
for hotels are difficult to find on the European market. In Austria, for example, insurance 
companies with corresponding insurance products have withdrawn from the market (an 
exception remains for the accommodation sector). As a result, many establishments have no 
other choice than ignoring these PTD requirements.  For hotels and intermediaries of 
associated travel services, insolvency insurance appears disproportionate with limited effect 
on travellers' protection. In Austria, due to corresponding case law, it is guaranteed that in the 
case of the insolvency of the intermediary, the payments made to him do not have to be paid 
a second time (to the tour operator or service provider). Insolvency protection standards shall 
remain subject to national law. 
2. State guarantee funds are needed under ‘un avoidable and extraordinary circumstances’.  
Without it, hotels won’t be insured and consequently won’t be able to offer packages.  
3. Limiting or prohibiting pre-payments for packages  won’t solve the issue of refunds  as 
experienced during the pandemic. Pre-payments are also a means to manage ‘now shows’ in 
hotels. 
4. Non-conformity issues with the PTD  within EU member states create legal uncertainty and 
unfair competition between establishments  in Europe . While some Member States strictly 
apply PTD requirements on what combinations of services are considered as packages, others 
perform a less stringent application of the rules. 
 
To conclude, HOTREC would like to thank the European Commission for the opportunity to provide 
input to the consultation and hope that our contribution will be taken into account. We'll contribute 
to a study run by ICF to support the preparation of an evaluation of the Package Travel Directive 
back-to-back with an impact assessment on its potential revision.  
Background 
The European Commission  (EC) is considering a revision of the  PTD Directive (EU) 2015/2302 on 
package travel and Linked Travel Arrangements (LTAs). Replacing the EU Directive (90/314/EEC), the 
PTD broadened the definition of pa ckage and introduced the concept of LTAs  to reflect the 
development of online combined bookings.  
HOTREC is a member of the exper t group advising the EC on the application of the current PTD. The 
March 2021 EC's report considers the main challenges of the application of the PTD  in relation to the 
Thomas bank ruptcy and the  COVID19 pandemic . On 15 February 2022,  the EC launched a public 
consulation (open until 10 May) to inform the revision of the Directive.

 
 
Response to Consultation on the Package Travel Directive 
 
We welcome the European Commission’s (Commission) intention to review the Packaged Travel Directive, as 
the travel industry represents a clear example of how the pandemic has radically altered the landscape and 
expectations of all stakeholders of the ecosystem.  
Mastercard is interested in ensuring that all participants are properly protected, placing special focus on the 
most vulnerable part of the value chain: European consumers. European legislation in combination with the 
rules of payment networks, are central in maintaining the liquidity of market players in the travel industry, 
while ensuring a high-level consumer protection. 
The review of the Directive can clarify or introduce rules that would protect the travel intermediar y providing 
reimbursements to the consumer , as well as  the consumer. , which would be impacted by the potential 
insolvency of the travel intermediary. 
i. Aligning with New Market Realities  
Due to the pandemic, many traders are changing their existing business model/booking process in order not to 
be considered as packaged organisers and be liable in situations where a service disruption occurs (e.g., airlines). 
More specifically, organizers of packaged travel are not comfortable to offer packages for airlines as they had 
difficulties to receive back funds from airlines in the case of cancellations. 
In parallel, there are many new services such as excursions and ground transportations that are not currently 
included in the package and should be included to provide maximum consumer protection.  
In this context, the existing legal -framework should remain fit-for-purpose and aligned with emerging realities 
in the sector to safeguard that consumers are adequately protected when they book their travel package. 
Suggestion: 
• The Packaged Travel Directive should cover more combinations of travel services, where certain criteria, 
indicating a close link between the services are met. 
• Ensure that rules provide clarity regarding the liable entity acting as the organiser of packaged services. 
ii. Cancellations & Reimbursements  
An important issue that became apparent during the pandemic was not only protecting refunds to the 
consumers, but also securing the payment from travel intermediaries, as packaged travel organizers, (e.g., travel 
agents) to travel service providers (e.g., airline, hotel).  This is because the current rules on cancellation rights 
are not sufficiently clear. 
It is critical for the health of the entire ecosystem that package organisers can get their funds back (that were 
paid in advance) from service providers so that organizers can fulfil consumers’ obligations. The Packaged Travel 
Directive should, therefore, be amended to clarify the refund mechanism from the service providers to the 
packaged organiser to secure overall liquidity in the value chain and refund consumers.  
We would also like to point out that while a crisis fund could be viewed as a potential solution, the willingness 
of merchants to pay into such a fund needs to also be considered. Requiring merchants to p ay into a crisis fund 
could put unnecessary strain on merchants or push merchants to only accept payment methods that ultimately 
bring less protection to consumers. Moreover, currently, certain flows today such as BSP cash and close loop 
cards for airlines  payment flows are not guaranteeing money back to organizers and can cause issues such as 
delayed refunds to consumers or even the bankruptcy of organizers. 
Suggestion: 
• Organisers should have the right to a refund against service providers, such as airlines or hotels, within a 
specific deadline, to enable them to reimburse travellers. This aspect can be regulated under the Packaged 
Travel Directive. 
• A clear timeline and protection for funds  paid in advance from organizers to service providers  are 
needed.

iii. Harmonizing the Insolvency Framework across the EU 
In many situations there is a lack of harmonization on rules across EU jurisdictions  concerning national 
insolvency protection systems. National bonding schemes purport to protect consumers, but due to the lengthy 
procedures for reimbursement it pushes consumers towards other types of refund processes, such as 
chargebacks from card schemes. 
This leads to an array of other problematic outcomes such as unfair competition within the internal market, and 
ultimately influence the decision of companies on where to conduct their business or organise their insolvency 
protection. These divergences are detrimental to the consumer who faces insufficient protection as travellers. 
Furthermore, in extraordinary circumstances, such as the pandemic, this may lead to liquidity issues to the 
acquirer of a packaged organiser transferring the liquidity issue to a separate point in the value chain . This is 
because the card schemes will hold acquirers liable for valid chargebacks regardless of merchant solvency.  
Suggestion: 
• To avoid a different national interpretation or implementation of rules, and EU harmonized approach may 
need to be deployed to safeguard consumer protection. Based on this, refund rights against an organiser 
should be protected in case of insolvency.  Vouchers issued by organisers should also be protected in case 
of insolvency. 
iv. Clarifying Liabilities for Linked Travel Arrangements (LTAs) 
Overall, the category of LTAs in the Packaged Travel Directive is needed to differentiate between packages and 
stand-alone travel services is necessary. However, market-driven changes to the travel ecosystem and the range 
options consumers have at their di sposal to book their travel plans, indicated that further clarifications are 
needed in regard to LTAs. 
In practice it has become harder to verify this distinction, making it difficult for travellers to be informed on the 
type of protection they have under their booking, and whether they are protected in the case of insolvency of 
the organiser/service provider. It is also not clear whether the booking of a second travel service was facilitated 
in a targeted manner.  
While there are many ways in which a consumer can purchase a hotel/flight/cruise – the primary scenario which 
can have a significant impact on consumer protection, liquidity risk and payment, is when the consumer reserves 
the hotel/flight/cruise indirectly via a travel intermediary (e.g., a travel agency). This implies one of the following 
scenarios in the value chain: 
a) The travel intermediary acts as a ‘booking intermediary’, the booking is made with the travel intermediary, 
but the payment goes directly from the consumer to the travel service provider. 
b) The travel intermediary acts as the ‘merchant of record’, the booking is made with the travel intermediary 
(e.g., travel agency) and the payment goes to the travel intermediary who in a second payment pays the 
travel service provider (hotel, airline, cruise company). 
The second scenario in the value chain (b), effectively leads to the risk towards the travel intermediary (e.g., 
travel agency) as it is liable for the refund to the consumer.  Hence, by ensuring more clarity in the Packaged 
Travel Directive, liability and procedures can be clearly defined to ensure maximum consumer protection, and 
the liquidity of the package organiser. 
Suggestion: 
• The Packaged Travel Directive should further clarify which entity in the value chain is the merchant of 
record, and if the merchant of record is the retailer/trader/organizer. This should be detailed to make 
clear to the consumer what each LTA is covering in each separate case. 
• There should be a definition or official guidance on the criterion ‘single visit or contact ’, to better 
distinguish LTAs from mere stand -alone services, informing the consumer if they stand to benefit from 
insolvency protection before entering a contract with an organiser or service provider. 
 
 
 
 
v. Conclusion 
To mitigate all these risks, Mastercard is of the view that the proposed review of the Packaged Travel Directive 
represents a great opportunity to introduce provisions or rules that would ensure a fairer distribution of the 
burden for refunds between travel service provi ders and travel intermediaries, which will as a consequence 
increase consumer protection and legal certainty.

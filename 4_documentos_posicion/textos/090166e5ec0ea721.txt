INTRODUCTION 
 
FEBIS stands for Federation of Business Information Services, the specialized and 
recognized industry body of providers of global B2B business intelligence services for 
managing trade risks.  
 
Today´s 140 plus members of our federation are present in nearly 60 countries throughout Europe, 
Asia, Africa, and the Americas. 
 
FEBIS members facilitate B2B trade relations and transactions with a focus on credit and risk 
intelligence services. These services require updated, accurate and relevant business information 
to produce beneficial, tailor-made solutions as well as scorings to assess the risk of default of 
businesses. By interacting between different business users and/or institutions of all sectors and 
sizes and helping them to take relevant decisions on their cashflow management, FEBIS members 
can be seen as trustworthy partners providing data and relevant financial information on trade 
counterparts. They are instrumental in helping companies manage their trade credit (amounting to 
*4 times the bank lending in several EU Member States), their onboarding and their KYC processes. 
 
FEBIS members welcome the European Commission’s will to better promote data flows and 
exchanges with the EU Data Act proposal and the will to foster a true European market for data. 
Nonetheless, it seems important to have clarifications on some key issues of the Data Act which 
may impact the business information sector, such as definitions, scope, voluntary vs mandatory data 
sharing, B2G data sharing obligations, incentives, the database right and moreover the interaction 
between the Data Act and other EU legislation. FEBIS members are willing to getting involved in the 
full debate during the legislative process.  
 
Here are a few comments on some points of the Data Act where we believe it would be great to get 
clarifications on:  
 
• Definition of enterprise (art 2.8):  article 2 (8) defines that ‘enterprise’ means a natural or 
legal person, which in relation to contracts and practices covered by this Regulation is acting 
for purposes, which are related to that person’s trade, business, craft or profession; FEBIS 
supports this definition which clarifies that individual entrepreneurs and sole traders are 
enterprises and not considered as consumers, and we believe this definition should be 
promoted in all relevant EU legislation. To this respect, the promotion of the term “enterprise” 
should be present in instances when it justifies data holding, data treatment, and carrying out 
data searches to pursue transparency with the only purpose of promoting good business 
conduct and preventing the overall economy from business failures (that cause defaults’ 
cascade effect), fraud, money laundering and other crimes. The term “Enterprise” is different 
from individual and, thus, data disclosures will need to be more flexible specially, in the 
above-mentioned cases. 
 
• Scope of data ownership and portability: The text seems to suggest that it applies to IOT-
generated data too, by putting in place a legal certainty for consumers and businesses to 
access data generated by the products or related services they own, rent or lease. There is 
an obligation to the data holder to make such data available to third parties upon the request 
of the user (excluding very large platforms under the meaning of DSA/DMA). FEBIS

questions regards the scope of IOT data that is considered and the question whether 
business information or credit scoring information could fall under this scope.  
 
• Incentives or compensation for making data available (art 9): Any compensation agreed 
between a data holder and a data recipient for making data available shall be reasonable. 
FEBIS members think that a clearer definition of what constitutes a “reasonable” 
compensation should be defined, and that difference should be made between B2B and B2G 
incentives for data sharing in this respect. To this end, in order to make all new data platforms 
useful, traditional business data added value handlers should qualify to be the vehicle to align 
uniformity, coherency and practicality to get to the general public  
• B2G obligation to data sharing under exceptional circumstances (art 14): the Data Act 
proposal authorises the use by public sector bodies and Union institutions, agencies or 
bodies of data held by enterprises in certain situations where there is an exceptional data 
need. This primarily concerns public emergencies, but also other exceptional situations 
where compulsory business-to-government data sharing is justified, in order to support 
evidence-based, effective, efficient, and performance-driven public policies and services. 
These B2G data sharing under exceptional circumstances will have to be done without 
financial incentives or compensation. FEBIS thinks that a much clearer definition of the 
meaning of “exceptional circumstances“ is needed, to ensure there are clear boundaries that 
can’t be bypassed. Furthermore, a clearer definition of the scope of B2G data that is 
considered would be good, and incentives shouldn’t be completely ruled out but more 
decided on a base by case or at sector level. In our opinion, cases as pursuing transparency, 
should prevail in exceptional circumstances as: preventing the overall economy from 
business failures (that cause defaults’ cascade effect), combating fraud, money laundering 
and other crimes. 
 
• Database directive & sui generis right (art 35): it doesn’t change the general scope of the 
DB directive, but the Data Act states that the sui generis right provided for in Article 7 of 
Directive 96/9/EC does not apply to databases containing data obtained from or generated 
by the use of a product or a related service. Here also the clarification of the scope of what 
data obtained from or generated by the use of a product or related service means would be 
helpful, as the database directive and its sui generis right are an important tool for business 
information providers.  
 
• Cloud-services portability right (chapter VI): the proposal pushes for access to 
competitive and interoperable data processing services within and across sector 
ecosystems. FEBIS members don’t think they fall under the scope of cloud services, but any 
clarification would be welcomed.  
 
• Interoperability (chapter VII): It provides for the development of interoperability standards 
for data to be reused between sectors, in a bid to remove barriers to data sharing across 
domain-specific common European data spaces, in consistency with sectoral interoperability 
requirements, and between other data that are not within the scope of a specific common 
European data space. The chapter refers to “operators of data spaces”:   
FEBIS members would also like to get clarification on this and to understand if reusers of 
public sector information are qualified as operators of data spaces or not. As data spaces 
are intended to percolate to the public and an optimal way to accede to the public, in business

information, are the existing networks, operational in the whole EU, FEBIS membership is a 
good potential driver. 
 
 
• National authorities and supervision: The text proposed that each Member States shall 
designate one competent authority and also that many of the technical issues will be dealt 
with through delegated or implementing acts. It would be great to clearly identify who the 
national supervisory authorities will be and how they will coordinate with other national 
authorities (DPAs, Financial…) and also what the scope of delegated or implementing acts 
will be.  
 
• Coordination with other EU legislation: it seems essential to FEBIS that provisions of the 
Data Act are coherent and aligned with other relevant horizontal or sector specific legislation 
such as the Open Data and PSI directive and its forthcoming Implementing Act on High-
Value Datasets or the Data Governance Act.

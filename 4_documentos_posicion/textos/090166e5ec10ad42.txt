Section I – Questions for all Respondents 
5.  What are your views on the current definition of package?   
The current definition of package does not go far enough to ens ure that increased consumer 
protection is achieved.  PEOPIL agrees that different types of travel service should be a 
requirement, but most travellers do still seem to be purchasing  combinations of flights with 
accommodation, as envisaged by the 1990 Directive.  Therefore, adding to the types of travel 
services that need to be combined is not going to lead to signi ficantly greater consumer 
protection.   
Reference is also made to the response to questions 3 and 3a above regarding the ease with 
which organisers can organise their business models to avoid fa lling within the scope of the 
definition of “package”.   
The reference to the need for an inclusive or total price is confusing.  It is the view of PEOPIL 
that a total price should be sufficient.   
The introduction of LTAs is also likely to be confusing to trav ellers who may assume, 
incorrectly, unless clearly notified, that the protection offered by that arrangement would allow 
them to sue the organiser of their holiday in the event that anything goes wrong while they are 
away rather than simply providing insolvency protection.   
The view of PEOPIL is that the definition of “package” should b e amended to include the 
elements of an LTA to ensure that maximum consumer protection is achieved.

15.  Currently, where package travel contracts are terminated b ecause of unavoidable and

The current 14-day period in the PTD should be maintained for a ll packages to ensure that

32.  Should there be EU rules on professional liability insuran c e  f o r  p a c k a g e  o r g a n i s e r s  
covering, for instance, compensation claims and the risk of hav ing to replace travel service 
providers? 
The lack of a mandatory requirement for organisers of package h olidays to have liability 
insurance militates against the very purpose of the PTD, i.e. to provide increased consumer 
and insolvency protection.  As a result, a traveller is only tr uly protected in the event of 
insolvency that occurs while they are in the course of the package or before it has taken place.  
This means that, in the event that an organiser becomes insolvent before any personal injury 
claim arising from the package holiday has been concluded, seri ously injured travellers and 
the families of fatally injured travellers are left wit hout a remedy even if they can establish a 
lack of conformity.  Where liability coverage is obtai ned, self-insured excess levels can be at 
an unreasonably high threshold.  Against the backdrop of the Covid 19 pandemic and the rising 
cost of holidays due to external economic factors, insolvency of organisers is likely to become 
even more prevalent.  Legislation should be introduced to ensure that any organiser of package 
holidays operating within the EU (including the selling of packages to travellers in the EU even 
when the organiser is based outside of the EU) is required to have liability insurance to cover 
these sorts of issues.   
 
33.  Do you see any other aspects not mentioned above which should be regulated in the PTD 
to enhance consumer protection and/or the functioning of the internal market.  Please explain. 
Under the PTD travellers who have suffered injury on a package holiday need to establish that 
there has been a lack of conformity by a provider of services c ontracted for as part of the 
package.  Whether or not a lack of conformity has occurred is to be assessed by reference to

the local standards in place at the time and location of the accident.  A disparity has arisen due 
to the different procedural and evidential rules that operate in the different jurisdictions in which 
the PTD is applied. This disparity means that travellers in dif ferent jurisdictions face very 
different issues when bringing claims against organisers under the terms of the PTD. 
This disparity is best illustrated by the different ways in whi ch national courts deal with the 
burden of proof.  By way of example, the burden of proof before  courts in the UK is on the 
traveller.  This means that the traveller has to obtain evidenc e from experts in the country 
where the accident occurred to establish what the local standar d is, and then has to provide 
evidence to establish that the relevant standard has not been met. By contrast, in Spain, once 
a traveller has established that an accident occurred during th e course of a service provided 
under a package holiday contract, the organiser is liable for all damage and loss sustained by 
the traveller unless it can show that it acted diligently, comp lied with local statutory 
requirements and put adequate measures in place to prevent trav ellers from being injured – 
Article 147 of Act 1/2007 of 16th November 2007 which regulates consumer protection in Spain.  
This provision - which has been nuanced by case law - applies generally to liability for damage 
caused by goods or services to consumers and users, since the s ection of the law relating to 
package travel does little more than translate the directive literally. 
PEOPIL is concerned that, as a result, nationals of the different countries in which the PTD is 
applied are treated differently.  As the organiser is meant to be providing a holiday that 
conforms with the local standard, it should already know what the local standard is and should 
be more readily able to provide evidence of whether or not it h as been complied with.  It is 
therefore the view of PEOPIL that the reverse burden of proof, such as that utilised in Spain, 
should be applied to package holiday claims in all nations in which the PTD is applied to ensure 
that all citizens of those jurisdictions are on an equal footing.

37.  In your experience to what extent do traders comply with t he obligation to provide one of 
the standard information forms contained in Annex 1 to the PTD when they are offering 
packages as defined in Article 3(2) PTD?   
Generally, traders do comply with this obligation when offering packages and so on that basis 
there is a high degree of compliance.  However, often this information is contained in a section 
headed “Important information” and so the traveller has to scroll through the information to get

to the standard information required to be provided by the PTD.  It is the view of PEOPIL that 
the standard information should be more prominently displayed, and the traveller should have 
to confirm that they have read the information before they can proceed to book.    
 
38.  In relation to LTAs as defined in Article 3(5)(a) do trade rs generally comply with the 
information requirements for potential LTAs under Article 19(2)  and Annex II (Parts A, B and 
C) to the PTD? 
PEOPIL does not know whether traders offering LTAs of this nature generally comply with the 
information requirements.  However, PEOPIL is of the view that the information requirements 
should be changed so that there is an obligation to clearly exp lain the additional rights a 
traveller would have if a package was booked instead so that th e traveller can make an 
informed choice about the type of arrangement they wish to book.  In addition, there should be 
an obligation for this information to be prominently displayed and the traveller should have to 
confirm that they have read the information before they can proceed to book.

42.  Have there been a significant number of bankruptcies of organisers and/or retailers since 
February 2020? 
PEOPIL does not know the exact number of travel company failure s there have been since 
February 2020, but on 14 October 2020 the Daily Mail in the UK reported that the ABTA chief

March 2020.  Given the trading difficulties caused by the Covid  19 pandemic it would not be 
unreasonable to suspect that a significant number of organisers  and/or retailers would fail or 
be very close to failing in the months to come.

EC proposal for a Data Act regulation (May 2022) 
UNIFE Feedback to the proposal 
UNIFE - the association of the European rail supply industry - supports the European Commission ’s 
objective to increase access to and further  use of data, in both the B2B and the B2G context s. We 
firmly believe in the added value of data sharing and the development of an inclusive data ecosystem 
to maximize the benefits of data for the mobility ecosystem.  
UNIFE, therefore, supports the EU initiatives aiming to improve the  access and sharing of more data 
in the rail sector . In this respect, it is of utmost importance to foster EU rules on access to data and 
sharing of data.  However, it must be ensured that data is shared safely, appropriately, in line with the 
applicable EU legislation and taking into consideration the varieties of the different businesses models. 
Data types and business models are significantly different across sectors, such  as B2C significantly 
differ from B2B, where the usage and sharing of data is predominantly stipulated in bilateral contracts. 
Hence, we do not agree that a horizontal regulation will achieve the European Commission’s objective 
to enhance and foster data sharing. By contrast, we believe that a one-size-fits-all approach will bring 
about negative impacts which outweigh the potential benefits o f the EU Data Act , especially in the 
field of critical infrastructure, including the railway sector.  
Overall, we therefore suggest to opt for sector specific regulations which are better suited for the rail 
sector and take into consideration the specific market conditions.  
The European Rail suppliers have identified the following critical issues in the EU Data Act proposal. 
1) Scope and definition of data (art. 1-2): It is necessary to clearly define what is meant by data 
and clarify which data fall within the regulation scope.  Thus, a distinction between raw data 
and derivative data that, for example, has been processed, transformed or valorized  as a 
result of any software process  must be introduced . Any derivative data resulting from 
software processes – especially proprietary ones – should not fall under the scope of  the EU 
data act  as such software processes may contain trade secrets and be  subject to IP rights . 
Generally, IP and trade secret protect ion is insufficient in the data a ct proposal  and data 
containing trade secrets should not be covered by the obligations under the proposal.  
Proposals: 
o a clear definition of “derivative data” shall be included in the proposal in order to ensure 
the exclusion of derivative data from the regulation  obligations. Data containing trade 
secrets should be excluded from the proposal. 
o a clear definition of  connected object  is missing . In the railway sector, this notion 
corresponds to any product with a sensor or measurability tool which could (potentially) 
fall within the scope of the regulation. 
o a clear definition of which data fall within the regulation scope as well as better distinction 
between the different stages of data processing.  
 
2) Data sharing obligations (art. 3-5):  
Access by design for the user:  the Commission proposal sets out far -reaching obligations for 
the data holder to make data generated by the use of products or related services accessible . 
While it is not yet fully clear what this obligation would entail and whether they would fully 
pertain to manufacturers as potential data holders, it can lead to the undesired side-effect that 
manufacturers try to refrain from developing products that produce data. Overall, t here 
should be no far-reaching data access requirements for machine data. A better balance needs 
to be found that still encourages the development and monetization of data-based services.

Access for third parties: the general obligation of the data holder to make available the data 
to a third party (upon  request of the data user) raises a huge concern  of protection of 
confidentiality, trade secret, intellectual property and competition, especially when the user 
is a company (B2B) and not an end consumer.  
Proposal: data sharing arrangements involving third parties should be  rather based on 
contractual arrangements  between the data holder and data user , instead of relying on 
horizontal obligation.    
3) B2G provisions  (chapter V) : being active in public procurement  markets, UNIFE members’  
customers are often public authorities  which might request access to data, after 
demonstrating an exceptional need to use the data requested. 
Proposal: it is necessary to better define to what extent these authorities can request access 
to data (both in B2B and B2G context). In particular the concept of exceptional need must be 
clarified and strictly defined so as to be consistent with the notion of public emergency (art. 2)  
In addition, the articulation of the text with existing national fr ameworks (e.g. Loi LOM in 
France) should be further clarified (see art. 16). 
4) Provisions on switching of provider (Chapter VI): a clear distinction between data processing 
service and application service  is to be introduced. The latter  calculates/generates derivate 
data through a software process by proprietary software hosted on computer resources 
(Software as a service applications). If this is subject to the obligations relating to facilitating 
the switching between data service providers , manufacturers are likely to refrain from 
developing these application services  in future, as digital assets and applications provided by 
manufacturers may include IP relevant know-how which would have to be shared with other 
providers of data processing services to be considered as potential direct competitors. 
Proposal: Software as a service (SaaS) shall not be captured by the regulation , in particular 
under the obligations relating to facilitating the switching between data service providers. 
5) Interoperability (Chapter VIII) : the issue of standardization and consistency with sectoral 
development (specific to the rail sector) is perceived as critical for UNIFE members.  
Proposal: to protect industry-driven investments, the EU Data Act should mandatorily build on 
existing technological developments and also use existing, proven and industry-driven / sector-
specific standards for operational capability. 
 
One general point to highligh t is that the presumed market asymmetry between the data holder as 
the one controlling the data vis-à-vis the data user does not exist in the railway industry. In the railway 
industry, the product manufacturer produces field assets, which are sold to the user who then owns 
the product. The data, which is produced by the product  generally  pertains to the user of that asset  
only. Thus, data is extracted by the product manufacturer only if and to the extent as stipulated by 
the contract between the manufacturer and the user and then provided to the user.  For this reason, 
horizontal rules are not fit for the railway sector as they do not take into consideration the complexity 
and nuances of the factual and contractual relationship between the different players and  creates 
substantial uncertainty concerning the applicability of the EU Data Act and the extent of its obligations.

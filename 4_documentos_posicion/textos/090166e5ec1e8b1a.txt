EUTELSAT CONTRIBUTION 
 
 
Eutelsat welcomes the issuance of the Proposal for a Regulation establishing the Union Secure 
Connectivity Programme (the “Regulation”) and is pleased to hereafter provide its views.  
In the past, the European Union has already implemented significant European spatial program, such as 
Galileo. The European Union is aware of the success and disadvantage of Galileo. Among the 
disadvantages, there was the financing of the project. That is the reason why for the Secure Connectivity 
Programme (the “Programme”), the European Commission leans towards a public-private partnership.  
Such implementation model provides agility and flexibility.  
In this contribution, Eutelsat offers some views on the Regulation and would like to see them reflected 
in amendments to the Regulation to be discussed in the relevant EU bodies. The thoughts and assessment 
conducted by Eutelsat took into account the objectives to ensure a proper use of  public funds, in 
compliance with competition rules , and guarantee a  flexible implementation of the Programme, 
which shall favour the independence and resilience of EU spatial infrastructure. 
The key points put forward by Eutelsat in the paper below are the following: 
- Clarifying the definition of governmental services and commercial services (services portfolio); 
 
- Improving the programme implementation: budget, timeframe, existing assets and ownership;  
 
- Developing a successful PPP: clarifying the role of private operators and its remuneration; 
 
- Foster the innovation and competitiveness of the EU Space Sector without distorting the market; 
and 
 
- Associating third countries to optimize outside -EU opportunities and international development 
goals.

1. CLARIFYING THE DEFINITION OF GOVERNMENTAL SERVICES AND 
COMMERCIAL SERVICES (SERVICES PORTFOLIO) 
1. The definition of Governmental Services should ensure a better protection of competition and EU 
financial interest and be more cost/effective conscious.  
European satellite communication is a mature commercial, governmental and military markets. There is 
effective competition among several companies. The current proposed segregation of governmental and 
commercial markets does not reflect the current state of the market and would constitute an undue 
obstacle for a significant number of private satcom operators and service providers already supplying to 
the governmental market , secure communications for low -critical governmental needs, in frequency 
bands where large availability and affordability of both space and ground segment exist. 
It is commonly agreed that Low-Critical Governmental Services are currently procured by commercial 
bandwidth and could continue to be procured by commercial bandwidth under the Secure Connectivity 
Programme1. High-governmental services are provided by the governmental infrastructure. 
2. The strict separation between Commercial and [Low-Critical] Governmental Services prevents 
synergies (such as backup) thus affecting the reliability of the infrastructure and sustainability of 
the PPP. 
A more inclusive approach to Governmental Services, in line with the one proposed in the EU Space 
Programme for GOVSATCOM, is needed to ensure (i) sustainability of the PPP model on existing 
“market services” and (ii) greater availability and resilience because of the large pool of resources and 
assets, and (iii) provision of a greater array of services. The Regulation of the EU Space Programme 
clarifies (point 74) that economies of scale can only be achieved for a sufficiently large pool of capacities 
and services and user base. 
3. Ensure an appropriate level of security for each category of services.  
As it stands, the Regulation does not clarify whether Article 33 of Regulation (EU) 2021/696 applies as 
well to the commercial component of the Programme, which would lead to disproportionate and 
unnecessary constraints for private operators addressing non-High-Critical Governmental Services. 
The definition of the security requirements of the Programme should be based on a pragmatic, stringent 
and cost-effective approach consisting in assessing the appropriate level of security for each category of 
services ( High-Critical Governmental Services, Low-Critical Governmental Services, Commercial 
Services).  
The strictest security regime, as prescribed by the applicability of Article 33 of Regulation (EU) 
2021/696, should be applied to the governmental infrastructure addressing high -critical governmental 
needs. However, when it comes to Low-Critical Governmental and Commercial Services that are per se 
less sensitive, cost and time efficiency should also be taken into account. The EUSPA accreditation 
requirements would impose disproportionate constraints on private operators that will need time to 
comply. Such high security requirements will also remarkably impact the equipment cost for the end -
users2.

Eutelsat Proposal: 
 Replace the distinction between “Governmental Services” and “Commercial Services” by the 
following categories: High -Critical Governmental Services, Low -Critical Services and 
Commercial Services.  
 Low-Critical Governmental Services and Commercial Services could be procured by commercial 
bandwidth, i.e. under the “Commercial Services” provided by the private operator  on the private 
infrastructure.  
 Commercial Services and Low-Critical Governmental Services should be broadly described in the 
Regulation. According to the Commission, Commercial Services should be defined by the 
“concessionaire”3. However, it is unclear if “Commercial Services” shall comprise the operation 
of the satellite infrastructure to provide a seamless d igital communication in order to bridge the 
digital divide. Indeed, to many extents, such an objective shall be pursued by the Member States 
with the European Commission and cannot, thus, be defined by the private contractor4. In any case, 
such general interest objectives should be partly financed by public funds. 
 The Regulation should be more specific as regards the commercial components of the Programme 
(catalogue of services, service characteristics, service supply and provision, target customers, 
priority areas, ...) and the potential synergies with the management of the infrastructure (including 
the governmental infrastructure) in order to attract private companies to participate to the project.  
 Low-Critical Services do not require the same  stringent level of security services as the High -
Critical Services (Art. 26)5.  
 
4. The set-up of a structurally and legally separate entity for the provision of Governmental Services 
and for the provision of Commercial Services need s to be clarified  (Art. 7.4). The strict legal  
separation of the new entities goes beyond what is needed (accounting and separate business unit) 
and brings significant overhead affecting sustainability of the PPP. 
5. Indeed, if the obligation of accounting separation ( i.e., analytical accounting) is essential, a structural 
and legal separation might bring excessive constraints.  
Legal unbundling of the various entities in charge of these subjects is disproportionate and affects the 
sustainability of the PPP model: it implies an expendable time to set up and additional costs. 
Furthermore, it hinders synergies and operational agility. More importantly, this could be addressed by 
other well-established measures (accounting/organisation of separate business unit/staff accreditation).

2. IMPROVING THE PROGRAMME IMPLEMENTATION: BUDGET, TIMEFRAME, 
EXISTING ASSETS AND OWNERSHIP 
6. Ownership (Art. 4, 16). For a better use of public budget, to increase synergies with the private 
investments and to improve the efficiency of the Programme, an optimal utilization of existing assets 
should be envisaged.  
7. The strategic objectives of the Programme call for a clearer articulation with Regulation 2021/696 
and a broader and more comprehensive approach than the sole construction of the initial space 
and ground infrastructure. 
The integration of GOVSATCOM assets into the Programme should be more specific. Indeed, such 
assets already exist, may fulfil part of the governmental needs (in particular High-Critical Governmental 
Services) and their integration within the Programme is clearly envisaged in the draft Regulation6. Thus, 
it would be wise to be more specific about their effective use into the Programme.  
In doing so, t he Programme will be consistent with the Regulation 2021/696 on the EU Space 
Programme adopted less than a year ago (April 2021), This programme establishes (point 76) the stepped 
approach of GOVSATCOM, where existing capacity from private actors and Member States is used, 
then completed with additional bespoke space infrastructure or capabilities to cover the evolving 
demand. 
On both the government and commercial sides of the system, the Regulation should better clarify the 
potential role of in-kind contributions from Member States that have recently invested in governmental 
space programs, and from private companies that have heavily invested in the latest satcom technologies. 
8. Introduction of additional flexibility regarding the use of existing (non-EU owned) infrastructure.  
The provision of required services does not necessarily have to go through a fully EU-owned 
infrastructure, unless for well-identified, High-Critical Governmental Services. 
If it has been considered that no current EU provider can serve all the increasing and evolving 
governmental needs ( Regulation, p. 7), there are assets currently available from EU services 
providers which could be used in the early days of the Programme until the effective functioning 
of the new EU satellites constellation, and/or to provide Low-Critical Governmental Services and 
Commercial Services.  
9. In overall, the use of some existing or planned a ssets and services would be an appropriate solution 
given the timeframe and budgetary constraints; the exploitation activities should begin by 2024 and any 
delay in implementation would jeopardise the window of opportunity and would be likely to generate 
cost over-runs.

Eutelsat’s Proposal: 
In the end,  
➢ High-Critical Governmental Services shall be delivered by assets belonging to the EU, so-called 
Governmental Infrastructure which include satellites or satellite subcomponents, space and ground 
subcomponents ensuring the distribution of cryptographic keys, infrastructure for monitoring the 
security of the Prog ramme infrastructure and services, infrastructure for the provision of the 
services to the governmental users, GOVSATCOM ground infrastructure; 
➢ Low-Critical Governmental Services  and Commercial Services  which can be procured by 
assets and services belongin g to private contractor and which are not part of the Governmental 
Infrastructure; such asset may include existing assets and services; 
➢ being specified that some assets shall be common to serve High-Critical Governmental 
Services, Low-Critical Governmental Services and Commercial Services, which shall be the 
“Common Ground Infrastructure” that operates the constellation(s). 
Alternatively, a similar approach for Galileo and EGNOS could be envisaged: 
➢ Since the Union is responsible, in principle, for financing the Programme in full, provision should 
be made for the Union to own all tangible and intangible assets created or developed under the 
Programme. 
➢ However, in order to optimize the use of the services provided, the systems, networks and services 
emerging from the Programme should be compatible and interoperable with existing systems, 
without prejudice to the objective of strategic autonomy. 
➢ In order to comply fully with any fundamental rights relating to ownership, the necessary 
arrangements should be made with existing owners, particularly with respect to essential elements 
of the infrastructures and their security. 
➢ Such ownership by the European Union should be without prejudice to the possibility for the 
European Union where it is deemed appropri ate on the basis of a case -by-case assessment, to 
make the assets available to third parties  or to dispose of them . In particular, the European 
Union should be able to transfer the ownership of or license the intellectual property rights arising 
from work under the Programme to third parties.  
 
3. DEVELOPING A SUCCESSFUL PPP: CLARIFYING THE ROLE OF PRIVATE 
OPERATORS AND ITS REMUNERATION 
10. Article 7. Conditions set do not allow to secure a sustainable PPP consistent with EU rules. 
The implementation model must rely, from its beginning on fair, balanced and market-based principles 
consistent with EU ex-ante regulation in other sectors.  
First, to be sustainable , PPP needs to rely on a profitable business case to commit to the secure 
connectivity initiative. The Regulation gives no evidence that mutualisation of capabilities with a 
governmental system will generate the anticipated synergies on commercial services. 
 
 
6 
Second, there is no general open access / neutrality of telecom infrastr uctures. EU rules relies on the 
possibility to impose ex-ante FRAND conditions, subject that the manager of the infrastructure is found 
to have a significant market power either because of its specific rights or because of the lack of 
alternatives and/or in cases when subsidies are granted to develop and deploy an infrastructure. 
Thus, such public interventions intended to secure general interest services on the commercial market 
(e.g., through a long-term commitment of bulk purchase of commercial services to serve public policies 
or through vouchers for households and SMEs in rural areas) shall be foreseen and conditioned 
accordingly. 
11. Articles 8 and 23 – Pricing. Unrestrained free -of-charge provision of services would create a 
distortion of competition, a misallocation of public priorities and would not protect EU financial 
interests. 
Since Governmental Services are currently procured on a competitive market, the provision of such 
services free -of-charge does not ensure the best “value for money” nor does it ensure “sound and 
efficient” management of EU financial interests. 
The access to the Governmental Services , although free of charge, should not be priceless. The set-up 
of the satellite constellation and the operation of Governmental Services are supposed to be financed by 
the EU budget. However, operating the Governmental Services will necessarily have additional cost 
which cannot be compensated by the mere pricing of Commercial Services (given the obligation of 
accounting separation). As a consequence, it would be preferable to provide a distinct and specific 
remuneration / tariff for the provision of Governmental and Commercial Services. Otherwise, the 
Programme and the PPP model will not be financially sustainable. 
This would constitute a better guarantee of EU financial interest/public priority allocation. Indeed, the 
use of assets for High -Critical Governmental Services are particularly expensive. Thus, the needs for 
such services are very specific and should not lead to excessive and irrational use of capacities. 
Free-of-charge Governmental Services should be allocated on the condition that the genuine market 
value of services procured under the Programme are exposed when making them available to the eligible 
users, and that usage is valued on the basis of actual consumption. 
Governmental Services could be provided to eligible users through a financial envelope, made available 
via e.g., a system of vouchers that the EU would give in advance to the Member States, and in fine to 
the eligible users in order for them to  procure the services. From an accounting point of view, this 
acquisition would be a zero -sum operation for users, and so equivalent to a genuine free -of-charge 
provision of services. In addition, the overall amount of the financial envelope would help limit possible 
abuse in case of shortage of capacities  and protect EU financial interest and sound/cost -effective 
management.

Eutelsat Proposal: 
 Services procured from Programme shall be promoted and maximised at conditions that remain cost 
effective, protecting the sustainability of the PPP and of the financial interest of the EU. 
 High-Critical Governmental Services, although free of charge, should not be priceless as explained 
above. 
 Low-Critical Governmental Services and Commercial Services should be priced at market practice. 
 
4. FOSTER THE INNOVATION AND THE COMPETITIVENESS OF THE UNION SPACE 
SECTOR WITHOUT DISTORTING THE MARKET 
12. Article 6. Enabling the contractor to ensure an innovative and competitive implementation of the 
Programme, including contribution of all actors of the satcom market. 
The specific roles assigned to start-ups and SMEs by the contractor along the whole relevant value chain 
should be based on their added value in terms of innovation and introduction of disruptive technologies, 
as well as on criteria of cost-effectiveness and reliability. 
As it stands, the regulation does not foresee any criteria and this open the door for SME free riding; it 
does not cl ear whether Article 6 applies as well to the commerci al component, then impacting the 
contractor’s commercial policy. 
The principles of procurement should foster, among others, the adoption of innovative and disruptive 
solutions. While SMEs and start-ups are by nature usually focused on these aspects, being a new entrant 
is not per se a guarantee of bringing in either innovative and disruptive solutions, or cost -effectiveness 
and reliability. The criteria for the award of the contracts and the procurement should therefore be based 
on criteria allowing a neutral evaluation of the added value of each component. 
Eutelsat Proposal: 
 Specify the Services to which Article 6 would be applicable to (High Critical Governmental 
Services, Low-Critical Governmental Services and/or Commercial Services). 
 Clarify that the main criteria for the award of the contracts and the procurement should be based 
on criteria allowing a neutral evaluation of the added value of each component. 
 
13. Articles 15 and 17. According to the Working Document, Articles 15 and 17 of the Regulation would 
clearly allow to offer several contracts and suppliers. However, in the same response, the EU 
Commission seems to consider that “ the decision should be taken at the launch of the procurement 
process”7.  
Eutelsat Proposal: 
 In order to  have all options open, Articles 15 and 17 should be more specific as to the possible 
allocation of the procurement process so that several private operators may intervene and be 
awarded part of the Programme.

14. A better description and inclusion of the Commercial Services and the financing of such activities should

5. ASSOCIATING THIRD COUNTRIES TO OPTIMISE OUTSIDE-EU OPPORTUNITIES 
AND INTERNATIONAL DEVELOPMENT GOALS 
15. The legitimate constraints imposed for third countries to access the Programme should be limited 
to High-Critical Governmental Services to preserve  the contribution of the Programme in 
strategic areas (e.g., Africa) and the sustainability of the PPP model on an open market. 
Commercial and Low-Critical Governmental Services should not be hampered by constraints related to 
the applicability of Article 218 TFEU when dealing with outside the  EU partners. The participating 
private companies will pay a close attention to the sustainability and profitability of their financial 
contribution to the Programme, while such constraints are likely to negatively imp act the initiative 
business model by reducing market opportunities and add administrative burden where agility and 
swiftness are key success drivers. 
Eutelsat Proposal: 
 Introduce in Article 36 that it applies to Governmental Services (only), so that it is clear that 
Commercial Services can freely be procured to third countries. 
 Introduce in Article 37 that it applies to High -Critical Governmental Services (only), so that it is 
clear that Low -Critical Governmental and Commercial Services can freely be procured to third 
countries.

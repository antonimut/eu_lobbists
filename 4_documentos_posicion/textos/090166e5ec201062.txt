OVHCLOUD’S POSITION ON THE EUROPEAN COMMISSION’S PROPOSAL FOR A DATA ACT 
 
OVHcloud, the leading European cloud provider, supports the European Commission’s ambition to 
promote more fairness and legal certainty for  data sharing  and processing  in a B2G and B2B 
environment. 
 
Ensuring consistency with its long-term engagements and current activities, OVHcloud encourages 
the establishment of a fair European data economy, based on three pillars: fair competition, fair 
protection and fair sharing.  
 
Only a comprehensive and innovative framework built at European level can lead to more fairness 
and be the architect of a trusted digital economy, particularly for the cloud sector . Acting quickly 
and having a comprehensive set of frameworks for the cloud sector is expected by two third s of 
business decision-makers, who are confident about a strengthening of the European regulation in 
the near future1. 
 
In this respect, OVHcloud welcomes the European Commission’s proposal for a Data Act . It is a 
fundamental building block for European data sovereignty and fairer competition in the European 
cloud market. OVHcloud appreciates the opportunity to share the following comments on the text.  
 
 
Fair competition: Ensure switching opportunities by removing concrete obstacles (Chap. VI) 
 
 
A successful digital transformation needs to enable users to have access to reliable, secure and 
trustworthy choices regarding their needs and suppliers' choices for both digital services and 
infrastructures. Lock-in situations, where customers and users are prevented from switching to 
solutions or services that better fit their needs and aspirations, are one of the cloud market’s biggest 
issues.. Facilitating data portability, interoperability and reversibility allows organizations to have 
tailor-made cloud solutions that reflect their diverse requirements. In that context, we welcome that 
the Commission identified several practices that impede the fair ness and openness of the  cloud 
market. 
 
The Commission takes the view that self-regulatory initiatives envi saged in the free flow of non -
personal data regulation are unlikely to effectively improve switchability, further reinforcing the 
case for regulatory action. The persistence of lock-in situations indeed shows the need to take extra 
steps towards fairer competition in the cloud market. While the DMA is a positive step towards 
restoring fair competition in the European cloud market, the Data Act has an even more important 
role to play and should aim at smoothly and quickly abolishing all switching barriers to the benefit 
of users and the European digital economy. 
 
Switchability is often hampered by the lack of common data standards, which makes the process 
more difficult . The promotion of open -source standar ds can reduce these barriers, ensuring a 
smoother data porting and switching process. OVHcloud has for years supported the conversion to 
open-source solutions and software, for instance within the Open Cloud Foundation or as an Open 
Invention Network member2. It increases not only trust and collaboration between the parties, but 
also contributes to data interoperability and reversibility.

OVHcloud welcomes and supports the following objectives and provisions of the Data Act related to 
switching between data services: 
➔ The promotion of an approach combining levers that would prevent lock-in situations and a 
lack of data control. 
➔ The inclusion of a mandatory switchability clause in cloud services contracts and the need 
to ensure a degree of functional equivalence when making the transition.  
➔ The obligation on providers to support the customer throughout the switching process and 
the inclusion of clear timelines to do so. 
 
In addition and to further make sure that the Data Act reaches its objective of fairer competition in 
the European cloud market, OVHcloud calls on the following improvements to be made to the text 
by the co-legislators: 
➔ While the gradual abolition of switching fees is a positive development, as the introduction 
of a switching monitoring mechanism  (art. 25) , we  encourage such mechanism to be 
operational as quickly as possible to guard against fees that effectively discourage provider 
switching during the transition period.  
➔ Shorten the transition period for the full withdrawal of switching charges (art. 25) ,. The 
sooner the abolition of switching charges, the quicker fairer competition can take place  as 
well as making European users’ freedom of choice even more concrete.  
➔ Introduce a reversibility requirement for the provisions related to switching between 
providers of data processing services, as this is a necessary component to make porting and 
switching achievable. 
 
 
Fair protection: Provide strong safeguards for non -personal data in an international context  
(Chap. VII) 
 
 
As global competition intensifies and the volume of sensitive data shared and processed, 
particularly in the cloud, increases exponentially, more and more European public and private 
organizations are becoming aware of the importance of having control of their IT systems and the 
data they handle and, of the possibility to choose a cloud provider that guarantees data security and 
sovereignty. 
 
According to a recent survey3, the most important criteria for organizations to select a cloud provider 
are data security and data sovereignty, with almost 90% of respondents considering data 
sovereignty as an important driver during the decision-making process. In addition, the Commission 
notes in its impact assessment that the vulnerability of cloud service providers to unlawful data 
access by non-EU authorities is a factor that diminishes trust in cloud services. It is also noteworthy 
that the data sovereignty comprehension is mainly driven by two dimensions: GDPR compliance and 
data centre location.  
 
Increasing competition for access to data strongly suggests that data  sharing between the EU and 
third countries requires efficient safeguards to protect Europeans’ non-personal data while ensuring 
that the value derived from EU data remains in Europe . National certifications, like France’s 
SecNumCloud, improve the security , performance and sustainability of data. At the EU level, the 
ongoing work of ENISA on cybersecurity certification for cloud services is a concrete example of a 
measure that would protect sensitive data from any request for access coming from a third country.

The Data Act should be complementary to such initiatives and aim at the same objective: ensuring 
European data sovereignty for all the concerned organizations. 
 
OVHcloud welcomes and supports the following objectives and provisions of the Data Act related to 
the international context of safeguards of non-personal data: 
➔ Address the issue of safeguards for non -personal data in international contexts. According 
to a recent study 4, only a few non -EU hyperscalers control more than two thirds of the EU 
IaaS market, exposing user data to potential legal and industrial risks. 
➔ Impose “technical, legal and organisational measures to prevent access or transfer based on 
requests from third country jurisdictions in relation to EU customer data”. 
 
However, we strongly believe that the text must also consider third country laws that expose 
companies to data access requests based on the location of their headquarters. The Data Act text 
could also go into more detail s on the safeguards that companies must put in place to protect 
European data against unauthorized access. We suggest the following safeguards: 
➔ Refrain from data transfers that are not compliant with EU or Member State laws , as 
mandated in the Data Act. 
➔ Exclusive EU jurisdiction should be mandatory for sensitive data (for instance relating to 
national security and defence, key infrastructures such as energy, digital networks, 
communications, hospitals, etc.) 
➔ References should be made to current cloud security certification schemes , as key 
instruments to protect European data against unauthorized access. 
 
Fair sharing: Guarantee interoperability standards to ensure a multi -cloud approach (Chap. 
VIII) 
 
 
Interoperability is an essential pillar to guarantee users a concrete freedom of choice regarding their 
data usage , based on their current and future needs and capacities, i.e. to ensure a multi -cloud 
approach through tailor-made cloud solutions. 
To achieve this, having a set of technical standards that are easily understandable and approachable 
is key as well as considering the specific needs of certain sectors. 
 
OVHcloud welcomes and supports the following objectives and provisions of the Data Act related to 
facilitate interoperability of data, data sharing mechanisms and services: 
➔ The establishment of essential requirements that would apply to all operators. 
➔ The principle of a presumption of conformity for interoperability solutions that meet 
harmonised standards (recital 79). 
➔ The possibility to adopt, at a later stage, common specifications in the different sectors.

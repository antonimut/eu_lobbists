 
 
Visa response to EU Data Act proposal  
• Visa welcomes the opportunity to provide feedback on the Data Act proposal  and to support the 
overall objective of the European Commission to facilitate data access and data use, and to 
promote data sharing while preserving incentives to invest in data innovation  as a fundamental 
condition to establish the European Single Market for Data and to enhance  the development of 
the data economy in Europe. 
• We agree with the European Commission assessment  that to enhance consumer trust in the data 
economy and promote data sharing, users must be in control of their data with clear mechanisms 
made available to them to consent, track and monitor the use, re -use, and sharing of their data 
with third parties. At the same time, industry stakeholders  should be given the confidence of a 
stable and secure legal and regulatory environment around data sharing.  
• While the proposed Data Act offers a comprehensive set of policies targeting various elements of 
the data sharing economy, Visa’s commentary on the Data Act proposal will focus on the 
provisions inscribed in Chapter 5, with regards to making data available for the public good, and to 
Chapter 7, concerning proposed safeguards for international data transfers, reserving further 
contributions to the design of a framework for open data sharing in the payments and financial 
services sector to the upcoming review of the Payment Services Directive and the expected 
European Commission proposal for a new Open Finance Framework.  
The societal benefits from the strong and focused application of “data for good” concepts are 
potentially enormous. 
• Business-to-Government Data Sharing is an important aspect of Visa’s ambitions to be an 
impactful, purpose-driven company and we have been actively exploring our potential in this 
area.  
• The COVID-19 pandemic clearly illustrated how data-driven insights can enhance the abilities of 
governments, central banks, and public authorities to prevent, respond , and assist in the recovery 
from a public emergency. Visa partnered extensively with governments, central banks, and 
intergovernmental organizations to share data-driven insights about the shifts in economic 
activity resulting from shutdowns, social distancing, and travel restrictions in order to help them 
make informed decisions and design more effective policies for relief and recovery.  For example, 
in partnership with the World Bank, Visa shared 10 months (January 2020 through October 2020) 
of historical, aggregated transaction data for nine countries, enabling the World Bank to assess 
changes in travel and tourism related spending in the early stages of the pandemic. The World 
Bank levered the data to analyze how governments could have better modeled reopening

 strategies, informed timing and targeting of local restrictions and incentives, and nudged travelers

• We welcome the proposed provisions related to Business-to-Government data sharing based on 
an exceptional need as an opportunity to harmonize the rules for data access and data use at the 
European level and to resolve the inconsistent approaches between Member States, and to 
provide legal certainty for all stakeholders.  
• As with any activity related to data sharing, Visa supports sharing “ data for good” when there is a 
clear case for public benefit, and where appropriate data security, data privacy, and financial 
disclosure protections and safeguards are in place.   
• Visa welcomes the European Commission acknowledgment that safeguards are necessary to 
ensure that the requests for data are proportionate and limited to what is strictly necessary to 
respond to exceptional circumstances. Visa also welcomes the provisions allowing businesses to 
refuse to comply or to seek changes on the data requests, when the conditions set on articles 15 
and 16 are not met.  
• We would welcome further guidance from the European Commission and relevant European 
authorities with regards to the proposed procedures for compliance with requests for data , under 
article 18, and the interplay with the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) , as the proposed 
regulation seems to indicate that any data, even personal data, can be disclosed (alt hough 
recommended to be psedonymised). 
Principles-based regulatory frameworks should be underpinned by robust, globally interoperable 
and internationally recognized technical standards.  
• Visa welcomes the European Commission’s open, rules-based approach to international data 
flows, with its strong emphasis on international cooperation and global standards to promote the 
free and safe flow of data not just within the EU, but also to and from third countries, and the 
Commission’s recognition that international data flows are “indispensable” for global businesses.  
• As a global company, Visa advocates for the free flow of data across borders, based on 
international cooperation and standards, based on a premise of EU personal data being afforded 
equivalent level of protection anywhere in the world. 
• Visa facilitates global commerce, enabling individuals, businesses, and governments to pay and 
get paid, by operating our global payment network, VisaNet, and conducting important related 
activities such as authorizing a transaction, fraud monitoring and prevention, and secur ity. Visa’s 
use of data to achieve these objectives is very focused and explicitly limited through adherence to 
 
 
 
 legislation regarding primary and secondary uses of data, through contractual provisions with 
clients, and through the Visa Data Use Principles 1. 
• Restricting cross-border data flows can produce unintended and negative consequences on the 
productivity of SMEs2, often having unintended consequences and contradicting many of the 
goals they are trying to achieve. In particular, regulations that lead to the restriction of data 
analysis, or visibility into real time transactions, have the potential to undermine the ability of 
companies like Visa to apply advanced capabilities that help protect the ecosystem. This is 
because electronic payments and internet-based services work at optimal levels when data is able 
to move freely and securely across borders. By severing connections with primary data centers 
around the world, localization policies can also prevent companies from adequately ensuring data 
resilience, data recovery, and business continuity, introducing significant operational risk.  
• Visa supports the Data Act’s proposed article 27 efforts to ensuring that any data sharing with 
foreign authorities is done with appropriate consideration of legal standards and safeguards  and 
mandating companies providing data processing services to take all prescribed measures to 
prevent international transfer or governmental access to non-personal data when there is a 
conflict with EU law or national law of a Member State. We would however welcome further 
guidance from the European authorities, namely the European Data Protection Board, to develop  
a standardized process streamlining compliance with the regulation, avoiding burdening 
businesses with added costs in assessing data transfer conditions.

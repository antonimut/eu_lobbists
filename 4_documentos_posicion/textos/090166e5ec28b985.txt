 
 
 
Wärtsilä feedback on the European Comission’s 
Data Act & amended rules on the legal protection 
of databases 
13 May 2022 
 
As the global maritime and energy sectors are moving towards a data-driven and connected future, 
the upcoming Data Act has been studied within the different business units at  Wärtsilä. Generally 
speaking, we understand and support the Commission’s ambition to strengthen the European Union 
as a fair data economy by fostering the access to and use of data. We also believe that the proposed 
Data Act will help European businesses to innovate and create new services that are valuable for 
our societies, environment and economy. However, we have also identified some open questions 
related to the applicability of the Data Act in our business that we would like to see to be clarified 
during the upcoming negotiation phase.    
   
Firstly, we noticed some inconsistency between the recital 14 and article 2 that creates uncertainty 
regarding the scope of the regulation. In order to ensure proper incentives also in the future for the 
development of new, smart, data-based services, we would like to see that the exclusion of derived 
data from the scope of the regulation (as expressed in recital 14) would be confirmed with a strong 
articulation at the respective article(s) . We believe that this is a common viewpoint presented by 
many industrial partners.   
   
Also, some questions were identified related to the  definition of “user ” as presented in article 2. 
The contractual environment in the marine business is very complex with several parallel parties 
sharing responsibilities in ship building and op eration phases, including ship owners, ship 
management companies and shipyards. With the current given definition of the “user”, there may 
be situations where companies like us might need to provide access to data to more than one “user”, 
also in cases whe re no contractual relationship exists between the “producer” and “user”. In our 
understanding, this can be tricky from a contractual and commercial point -of-view and would 
expand the original idea of the act. It would be good to clarify, if for the purpose of this regulation, 
the user equals to customer.   
   
Another question relates to the storing of data. Is it enough that the seller of the product provides 
the user a possibility to access the data or does the product seller, as the data holder, need to store 
all collected data for the user? For example, could the user (or third party) approach the data holder 
and request all data collected from its engines from the past 5 years?   
   
Finally, the international nature and complex operational set -up of global shipping establishes one 
more question when it comes to territorial scope and implementation of the proposed act, and its 
impacts to the competitiveness of European players.  In inte rnational shipping, vessels sail in

international waters under different EU or 3rd country flags and visit various ports. The ownership 
and operation responsibilities can be divided to different parties. At this point, we don’t have a full 
picture of the potential complexity of the issue, but we would appreciate if the Commission could 
pay additional attention to the topic relating to the implementation of the Data Act in the global 
maritime business during the next month s, assisted by maritime sector stake holders. How would 
the Data Act apply in the following examples:  Wärtsilä has sold an engine to an EU based shipyard 
who has sold the ship to a Chinese shipowner – does Wärtsilä need to give data free of charge to 
the Chinese shipowner (who is the “user”)? What if there is an EU based third party requesting data 
via the Chinese shipowner – does Wärtsilä need to give the data to the third party when the user is 
not in the EU? What about a situation where the shipowner is an EU based company but the third 
party who requests the data via the user, is from the US – does Wärtsilä need to give the data to a 
non-EU-based third party? This could lead to situations where the US third party gains a competitive 
advantage over EU based competitors.

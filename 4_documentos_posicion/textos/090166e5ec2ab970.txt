TIM comments to the Public Consultation on the Data Act 
13 May 2022 
 
TIM welcomes the opportunity to comment on the European Commission proposal on the Data 
Act, a pivotal piece of legislation to reestablish the role of Europe in the global data economy.   
TIM supports the overarching objective of promoting and facilitating data sharing, pursued by the 
proposed Regulation. Measures aiming at removing  current obstacles to data sharing within and 
across sectors, as well as incentivizing the reuse of data, while avoiding dominant players in the 
data market to further strengthen their position , are a welcome step toward the achievement of 
a striving European data market and the development of strong European market players.  
However, given the breadth of the proposed measures and the importance of a correct 
implementation, TIM believes that a particular attention should be put to some aspects of the 
regulation which might entail unintended adverse effects on competition and innovation. 
In particular, data exchanges should continue to be primarily based on voluntary agreements that 
address the specific needs of contractual parties,  both private and public, subject to general 
contract and competition law. Proposed rules should avoid crowding-out commercial solutions 
available in the market; mandating access may very  well hinder market players from investing in 
innovative data analytics solutions and services, which in the long term would be detrimental for 
all parties. 
Moreover, the proposal may benefit from further clarifications on the definitions and on the scope 
of the different provisions as to provide more legal certainty. 
 
Access to data generated by the use of products or related service (IoT) 
Actions that facilitate data access by users of connected products and that enhance competition 
in the aftermarket are welcome.  
Nevertheless, clarifications on the definition of pr oducts and related services as well as on the 
scope of certain provisions are necessary. In particular: 
 the definition of related services should explicitly exclude connectivity services; 
 the exclusion of devices that require human intervention (such as smartphones) from the 
definition of product should be further stressed, as to clarify that data originated by the use 
of mobile devices (e.g. location data) are out of scope of this regulation; 
 since an IoT service comprises two componen ts, the IoT device/sensor and the attached 
service, it should be clarified if the prohibition “to use the data obtained to develop a product 
that competes with the product from which the data originate ”, is limited to the physical 
product (i.e. the sensor) or also includes the service provided, as to avoid undermining the 
investment incentives and disrupt business models of the IoT service offered by the original 
provider.       
TIM appreciates that undertakings designed as gatekeepers under the Digital Markets Act are not 
an eligible recipient of the data generated duri ng the use of IoT products, as to avoid further 
strengthening the position of already dominant players in the market.

Business to Government data sharing 
Telecommunications operators have a long history of cooperation with public administrations and 
a variety of data-based services is currently available in the market to meet their needs (e.g. “smart 
city” services for municipalities).  The provision of such services is generally based on voluntary 
contractual agreements. 
The Data Act should therefore avoid to crowd-ou t commercial solutions, clearly defining and 
circumscribing the exceptional circumstances in  which data access from Government can be 
mandated and always ensuring fair compensation on investments made. 
In times of crisis, as the Covid pandemic, operators have been very responsive to the Government 
demands, providing insights based on aggregated and anonymized network data. 
However, given the impossibility to foresee the probability and extent of the request, the 
regulation should in principle ensure at least that the costs incurred for providing data are 
recovered. 
 
Switching between data processing services (Cloud) 
TIM supports the Data Act’s propositions regardin g switching and interoperability between data 
processing services, as they could create oppo rtunities for a more competitive cloud and edge 
market in Europe. 
TIM would like to point out that the proposal may benefit from some clarification as to the 
obligations, taking into account the different business models for the provision of data processing 
services (i.e. managed services vs pure resale or cloud broker), in order to correctly allocate the 
responsibilities along the value chain, consid ering that the party which has the contractual 
relationship with the customer is not always the data holder.

Feedback on a proposal for a Directive on 
combating violence against women and 
domestic violence 
 
 
Our ambition 
 
The IKEA vision is to create a better everyday life for the many people, and we believe 
that an equal everyday is a better everyday. At Ingka Group, we recognise our responsibility 
and the expectations on leading companies to have a positive societal impact. Together with 
policymakers, civil society, employ ees, customers and so many others , we can rise to the 
challenge of moving from words to actions, supporting victims of Violence Against 
Women and Girls (VAWG) and domestic violence in all its forms.  
 
We take a holistic and long-term approach to gender equality.  We believe that gender 
equality, both at home and at work, lies at the core of gender equality in society.  Equal 
treatment and a shared responsibility for both people and planet are deeply rooted in our 
culture and values.  We want to create a futureproof IKEA, integrating sustainability into 
everything we do and achieving true inclusivity based on equality. This is not only the right 
thing to do – it also supports progress toward achieving  many of the United Nations ( UN) 
Sustainable Development Goals. 
 
 
Our position  
 
Ingka Group welcomes the proposal of a Directive on combating violence against 
women and domestic violence . We believe leading companies must take responsibility 
and utilise their resources to raise awareness and tackle issues that affect employees and 
customers al ike. It is time for companies to publicly support the European Commission  
proposal that aims to strengthen the support, protection and rights of women and girls who 
are victims of violence. 
 
We also support the EU Gender Equality Strategy that includes measures to prevent and 
combat gender-based violence, protect victims and hold perpetrators accountable. Gender-
based violence includes sex -based harassment, online violence, forced marriage, and 
female genital mutilation.  Research shows that women, and specifically women from 
disadvantaged groups – such as women with disabilities, refugee women, migrant and 
undocumented migrant women and LGBT women – face difficulties in obtaining adequate 
support services, legal help and com pensation. We therefore welcome the intersectional 
approach of the proposal.

Our recommendation 
 
We welcome  the provisions highlighting the role of the employers and a safe 
workplace.1 We would like to make the following recommendations for a hollistic approach 
to address VAWG:  
 Guidelines on how the private sector can proactively get engaged in the fight 
against VAWG  - in line with article 17 of the Istanbul Convention  and the ILO 
Convention No. 190 - should be put forward to secure impactful compliance going 
beyond a simple tick-boxing exercise.  
 We would welcome exploring together with EU Institutions how businesses could 
better integrate engagement towards protecting and improving women’s rights in  
Environmental, Social and Governance (ESG) reporting frameworks. 
 Including VAWG in the list of Eurocri mes should be a priority to secure a 
comprehensive and impact-driven approach to violence against women and girls.  
 Ratifying the Istanbul Convention should be a priority for the European Union to 
improve its credbility in the fight against VAWG.  
 
 
Our commitment  
 
At IKEA, we  have a zero -tolerance policy on gender -based violence, including sexual 
harassment at the workplace, and we have a mandatory cod e of conduct training which 
clearly states that all employees have the right to work in a place free from any form of 
abuse or harassment.  We want everyone to have equal opportunities and to feel included, 
but this is only possible if women and girls can  enjoy an everyday free from gender -based 
violence in the countries where we operate. 
 
Within our own company, all leaders are responsible for identifying and minimising barriers 
to gender equality while driving positive systemic changes to support long -term progress. 
We are reviewing and strengthening our workplace response to combat domestic violence, 
addressing the needs of our employees who suffer from violence. We have committed to 
this journey for some time but more still needs to be done.  In Czech Republic, Hungary 
and Slovakia, we offer employees 10 days of paid leave from work in the event of domestic 
abuse. In Spain, a special protocol against gender-based violence supports employees from 
the beginning with the expert help of social workers. 
 
Our markets also work with local experts and Non-Governmental Organisations to 
make change. For example, in Czech Republic, we have been using the strength of the IKEA 
brand to support local NGOs with legislative changes, increasing the rights of the victims of 
domestic violence. In many countries  – including Germany, France, Italy, Belgium  and 
Portugal, we support victims, transforming women’s shelters into places they can call home

Conclusion 
 
Despite the best efforts of many actors, women are still not getting the protection they 
need. Unfortunately, forces in the EU are still opposing this proposal against gender-based 
violence. Some argue that addressing VAWG imposes ideas and values onto other cultures. 
However, gender-based violence exists in every country. We do not believe it is a legitimate 
part of any culture. Others say that domestic violence is a private issue. That is false – it is a 
crime. It is irrelevant if the crime is committed in your home or outside of it. 
 
The adoption of the Directive on combating violence against women and domestic 
violence will strengthen the prevention of these crimes, the protection of the victims 
and the prosecution of perpetrators. This should be a joint effort and companies have a 
unique role to play. We are serious about our responsibility towards the world in which we 
operate, recognisin g the expectations falling on  companies like ours to have a positive 
impact on society. Companies must come together and use their size and leverage, joining 
forces with civil society, employees and customers  to call on governments to step up  their 
work against violence against women and girls and domestic violence in all its forms.

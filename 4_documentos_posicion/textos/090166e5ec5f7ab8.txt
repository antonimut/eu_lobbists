 
 
Input into 18 May EC submission - 
VAW/DV directive 
Contact: francesca@tgeu.org 
TGEU welcomes the legislative initiative to combat violence against women and 
domestic violence. Such violence, especially against marginalised communities 
including trans people is widespread and devastating and increased during the 
pandemic. 
TGEU welcomes the comprehensive approach to providing specialised support to 
victims of violence, including training of professionals and staff providing support 
services. Trans people are at high risk of repeat victimisation and re-traumatisation 
following a violent attack, through misgendering, transphobic treatment by 
authorities or health personnel, risk of being deported in the case of undocumented 
migrant trans people, among other issues, and specialised support is essential. TGEU 
also welcomes the acknowledgement that many different groups of people, including 
LGBTIQ people, can be victims of domestic violence. This is important for ensuring 
access to support services and protection.  
TGEU further welcomes the inclusion of undocumented migrant women and women 
sex workers among victims with specific needs and groups at risk in Article 35 (1). We 
know that further marginalised groups, including those with migration experience, 
racialised women, and young people, suffer even more from violence. Listing those 
groups at greater risk explicitly in the Directive can have a significant positive effect 
on access to justice and holding authorities accountable for facilitating access to 
justice. 
However, TGEU considers the text can be strengthened so as to best protect the 
most marginalised women against violence, including trans women, who are not 
mentioned in the articles of the directive. Such a specific reference is important in 
ensuring access to support services and access to justice, as mentioned above. 
The below feedback will be supplemented by a more detailed policy paper, currently 
in development with ILGA Europe, OII Europe, IGLYO and EL*C.

Key demands from TGEU: 
1. Explicit inclusion of LGBTI women and violence inflicted due to a person’s 
gender identity and gender expression in the text of the directive, 
complementing the Victims’ Rights Directive and the EU Gender Equality 
Strategy; 
2. Greater emphasis placed on intersectionality and the unique experience of 
violence that further marginalised groups like trans women, racialised women, 
women with disabilities, and women sex workers, experience due to 
intersecting elements of their identity, reflecting the definition and approach in 
the Pay Transparency Directive as voted on by the European Parliament; and 
3. Collection of gender identity-segregated data on gender-based violence and 
domestic violence to have a complete picture of the true extent of such 
violence and to support in designing policy responses. 
 
1. Explicit inclusion of trans women and violence inflicted due to a person’s 
gender identity and gender expression in the text of the directive.  
Explanation: 
● Trans women suffer greatly from gender -based violence and to ensure that 
those at the margins are covered by this directive, they are mentioned explicitly 
in the directive as vulnerable groups and groups at risk, in line with and 
complementary to the language of the Victims’ Rights Directive. 
● LGBTI persons, including LBTI women, are at particular risk of violence that is 
directed against them because of that pers on's gender, gender identity or 
gender expression, that is, gender-based violence. As identified by the Victims’ 
Rights Directive, they are particularly vulnerable and therefore need particular 
support and protection. 
● Violence against trans people in the EU occurs at an alarming rate and 
threatens trans people at home. 36% of trans women, 36% of non-binary 
people, and 33% of trans men reported to the FRA 2019 LGBTI equality 
survey that they had experienced physical or sexual violence in the preceding 
five years. An extraordinary 60% of trans people experienced in-person 
harassment in the same period. TGEU’s COVID-19 impact assessment1 
 
1 Boglarka Fedorko, Anwar Ogrm, and Sanjar Kurmanov (2021) Impact assessment: COVID-19 and 
trans people in Europe and Central Asia. TGEU. p7.  
 
highlighted how, “many women and trans people have been forced to 
‘lockdown’ at their residence with their abusers, while services to support 
survivors have been downsized or made inaccessible.” The 2021 update of the 
Trans Murder Monitoring project showed that 24% of the reported murders of 
trans people occurred in the victims’ own homes. 
● In its Resolution on identifying gender-based violence as a new area of crime 
listed in Article 83(1) TFEU the European Parliament highlights that “LGBTIQ+ 
persons are also victims of gender -based violence because of their gender, 
gender identity, gender expression and sex characteristic s”; recognises that 
“gender-based violence targets women and girls in all their diversity and 
LGBTIQ+ persons, driven by a desire to punish those seen as transgressing 
societal norms of gender hierarchies, gender expression and binary gender 
systems”. 2 
 
2. Greater emphasis placed on intersectionality and the unique experience of 
violence that further marginalised groups like trans women, racialised women, 
women with disabilities, and women sex workers, experience due to 
intersecting elements of their identity, reflecting the definition and approach 
in the Pay Transparency Directive as voted on by the European Parliament. 
 
Explanation: 
● At outlined by the European Parliament Committee on Women’s Rights and 
Gender Equality in its 4 April 2022 draft report on Intersectional discrimination 
in the European Union: the socio-economic situation of women of African, 
Middle-Eastern, Latin-American, and Asian descent, which “[h]ighlights the 
need for a comprehensive directive on gender-based violence with an 
intersectional approach, covering all women and girls in all their diversity and 
LGBTIQ people on the grounds of gender identity, gender expression or sex 
characteristics; calls on Member States to ensure that instances of gender-
based violence are appropriately investigated, prosecuted and sanctioned and 
that victim-support services are comprehensive enough to address all forms of 
violence with a victim-centred perspective, particularly that which results from 
intersectional discrimination.” 
 
2 European Parliament, Resolution of 16 September 2021 with recommendations to the Commission

● The 2020-2025 EU Gender Equality Strategy indicated that “The Commission 
will enhance gender mainstreaming by systematically including a gender 
perspective in all stages of policy design in all EU policy areas, internal and 
external. The strategy will be implemented using intersectionality – the 
combination of gender with other personal characteristics or identities, and 
how these intersections contribute to unique experiences of discrimination – 
as a cross-cutting principle.” It also states that the importance of gender 
mainstreaming in EU policies that “ensures that policies and programmes 
maximise the potential of all – women and men, girls and boys, in all their 
diversity.” 
● A strong reference to intersectionality and to violence inflicted on a person 
because of their gender identity in this Directive will support the aims of the 
EU Gender Equality Strategy and will better ensure that women and girls in all 
their diversity are protected from violence.  
● The Pay Transparency Directive is the first ever piece of EU legislation to 
include a definition of intersectional discrimination; any new directive must 
also reflect this standard. Intersectional discrimination is defined in the Pay 
Transparency Directive in this way: “’intersectional discrimination’ means a 
situation in which grounds of discrimination prohibited under Directive 
2006/54/EC and one or more grounds of discrimination prohibited under 
Directive 2000/43/EC or 2000/78/EC - interact with each other at the same 
time in such a way as to be inseparable, producing distinct and specific forms 
of discrimination…” 
 
3. Collection of gender identity-segregated data on gender-based violence and 
domestic violence, including bias motivation, for the violence to have a 
complete picture of the true extent of such violence. 
 
Explanation: 
● Disaggregated data and research are needed to identify the root causes of 
violence and monitoring progress in preventing and combating violence 
against women and domestic violence.  
● Indeed, the EU’s LGBTIQ Equality Strategy 2020-2025 states that “reliable and 
comparable equality data will be crucial for assessing the situation of LGBTIQ 
people and to effectively tackling inequalities”. The EU has committed itself to 
being at the forefront of efforts to better protect LGBTI people’s rights. 
 
● As acknowledged in the proposal text itself, violence against women can be 
exacerbated by the compounding of the ground of sex with other grounds. “In 
particular, lesbian, bisexual, trans, non -binary, intersex and queer (LBTIQ) 
women, women with disabilities and women with a minority racial or ethnic 
background are at a heightened risk of experiencing gender -based violence” 
(recital 11). Additionally, the explanatory memorandum highlights that domestic 
violence can affect LGBTIQ persons. This must be reflected in Article 44 on data 
collection, to ensure that data about the bias motive(s) of the episodes of 
violence, provided that this information is available, are collected and given 
visibility too.

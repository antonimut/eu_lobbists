Proposal for a Directive on Corporate Sustainability Due 
Diligence 
 
Background 
Henkel welcomes the Commission’s proposal for a Directive on Corporate Sustainability Due 
Diligence and supports the Commission in its efforts to establish a harmonized framework for human 
rights and environmental due diligence.  
As a globally active company with leading positions in both industrial and consumer businesses, the 
creation of strong brands, innovations, and technologies across our three business units  — Adhesive 
Technologies, Beauty Care, and Laundry & Home Care  — is inseparable from our commitment to 
respecting internationally acknowledged human rights along the entire value chain.1 
We believe that a harmonized EU -wide regulatory framework makes a crucial contribution to  
eliminating adverse impact s on human rights and the environment in corporate value chains. 
Nonetheless, to avoid fragmentation of due diligence obligations, sanctions, and civil liability regimes, 
the Directive must adopt a maximum harmonization approach to both due diligence obligations and 
enforcement rules . This will create a level playing field  across the single market and ensure legal 
certainty for industry.  
Furthermore, in accordance with international standards, due diligence should be understood as the 
continuous improvement of processes and follow a risk-based approach. Accordingly, the Directive 
should encourage companies to identify and address actual or potential risks in their supply chain s 
without the fear of legal claims against them. Hence, civil liability should exclude third parties such as 
suppliers or customers and be limited to human rights and environmental impacts caused by a 
company’s own activities or the activities of its subsidiaries. 
Ultimately, we welcome that the Commission recognizes the invaluable role of industry and multi -
stakeholder initiatives in conducting effective human rights and environmental due diligence.  It is 
through our long-standing collaborations and engagement in multi-stakeholder initiatives such as the 
chemical industry’s Together for Sustainability (TfS) initiative or the Action for Sustainable Derivatives

Call for action 
Adopt a maximum harmonization to create a level playing field and ensure legal certainty for 
industry 
Henkel welcomes the Commission ’s intention to create a level playing field by harmonizing national 
approaches to due diligence. Similarly, Henkel appreciates that the due diligence obligations set out 
in the Directive are to a large degree aligned with the OECD Due Diligence Guidance for Responsible 
Business Conduct, which is crucial to support the global harmonization of due diligence standards.  
Nonetheless, we are concerned that the proposed Directive takes a minimum harmonization 
approach to both the due diligence obligations and the set of enforcement rules . We fear that by 
allowing Member States to define their own due diligence obligations and sanctions, the Directive will 
result in an uneven playing field across the Single Market, incentivizing less responsible companies to 
establish their operations in Member Sta tes with less stringent due diligence obligations, sanctions, 
and civil liability regimes.  
Therefore, Henkel supports a maximum harmonization approach to both due diligence obligations 
and the set of enforcement rules  to avoid fragmentation across the single market, ensure legal 
certainty for industry, and create a level playing field. 
Understand due diligence as the continuous improvement of processes based on actual and 
potential risks in alignment with international standards 
In line with the OECD Due Dilig ence Guidance for Responsible Business Conduct, we call on EU 
lawmakers that due diligence should be  risk-based and focus on the continuous improvement of 
processes. Although Henkel recognizes that the concept of “established business relationships” can lift 
some administrative burdens for companies, particularly SMEs, we fear that it may lead to an

overreliance on contractual assurances and enhanced due diligence efforts on activities that  pose a 
negligible risk to people and the plane t — risks are oftentimes the greatest where relationships are 
not established. 
Consequently, Henkel believes that companies  should focus their due diligence on those activities 
where the potential harm to people and the planet is the greatest and where they have the greatest 
leverage to induce change in supplier’s behavior. As such, the scope of the due diligence obligations 
should cover a company’s operations, those of its subsidiaries, and its business relationships based 
on a prioritization of due diligence efforts on the severity of the actual or potential harm of an 
activity to people and the planet.  
Clearly distinguish between a company’s responsibility to conduct due diligence and civil 
liability 
Effective due diligence is due diligence that actively seeks to identify  actual or potential adverse 
impacts, addresses the se, and contributes to the continuous improvement of sourcing processes . 
Accordingly, the Directive should encourage companies to identify and address actual or potential risks 
in their supply chain, without fearing legal claims against them. Therefore,  the respon sibility of 
companies to conduct due diligence, as reflected in the scope of due diligence, should not equal the 
scope of civil liability. 
Instead, civil liability should exclude third parties such as suppliers or customers and be limited to 
human rights and environmental impacts caused by a company’s own activities or the activities of 
its subsidiaries. The primary function of civil liability should be to hold companies liable for the failure 
to establish and maintain a reasonable due diligence process that could have prevented adverse 
impacts on human rights or the environment.  
Clearly link director’s duty of care to the fulfilment of due diligence obligations  
Unfortunately, director’s duties are ill-defined under the current proposal as they refer to undefined 
and ambiguous terms such as “sustainability. ” Henkel believes director’s duties should be clearly 
linked to the fulfilment of the human rights and environmental due diligence obligations set out in 
Articles 5 to 11 of the proposal.  
Furthermore, we believe that  a director’s duty of care should lie where the company strategy is 
created, and its execution is organized. Thus, directors of a company’s subsidiaries should be entitled 
to fulfil their duty of care obligations by adhering to the policies of the parent company.  
 
  
 
Recognize the valuable role of industry initiatives in conducting due diligence 
Henkel welcomes that the Commission recognizes the invaluable role that multi -stakeholder 
initiatives can play in identifying  and addressing adverse impacts on human rights and the 
environment. We believe these initiatives are essential to facilitate the implementation for companies 
and their business relationships (e.g., through the sharing of audit results within industry schemes) 
and simplify controls by competent authorities . Simultaneously, multi-stakeholder initiatives and 
industry schemes enable businesses to effectively use their leverage vis-à-vis suppliers and allow for 
collaborative learning to bundle industry know -how, ultimately stimulating continuous 
improvement on the ground.  
Consequently, Henkel encourages  the Commission to issue concrete  guidance for assessing the 
fitness of industry and multi-stakeholder initiatives as part of a company’s overall due diligence.

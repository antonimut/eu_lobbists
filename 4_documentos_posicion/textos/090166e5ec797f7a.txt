Comments to the Proposal for a Directive on Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence 
AB Electrolux would like to give the following comments to the Proposal for a Directive of the European 
Parliament and of the Council on Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence (COM (2022) 71) amending 
Directive (EU) 2019/1937.  
Electrolux is in favor of a corporate due diligence legislation at the EU level . It has the potential to 
reduce differences between EU Member States requirements as some Member States are considering 
own legislation in this area. A common EU legislation would help to prevent a potential patchwork of 
national legislation.  
We would like to highlight the following main points and concerns as input and suggestions ahead of 
the upcoming procedure in the European Parliament and the European Council. 
1. Many multinational companies, including Electrolux, have  thousands of direct suppliers around 
the world who in turn have sub-suppliers in several layers. Exercising proper due diligence beyond 
immediate suppliers will pose great challenges for most companies, both in terms of access and 
required resources. Companies must be able to prioritize the focus of their due diligence efforts 
and be able to show a systematic and risk -based approach when arriving at their prioritization. 
The legislation therefore needs to clarify  that focus of the due diligence duties should be o n 
direct suppliers in combination with a risk-based assessment scope for the entire supplier base, 
and more clearly refer to the UN Guiding Principles and the OECD Guidelines  when it comes to 
prioritizing potential impact.  
 
2. The definition of “stakeholders” in the propos al is very broad. While we support stakeholder 
consultations as such, the wide scope of the definition could  lead to lengthy and complicated 
consultation processes and opens for the need to consider views from a potentially vast number 
of organizations, companies and individuals not directly affected by the company’s operations . 
This imposes unproportionate administrative burdens for companies and risk s hampering 
decision-making. We therefore propose that the  Directive includes a  clearer definition of 
stakeholders, which  only include s parties directly affected by the company operations  or 
deemed relevant by the company. 
 
3. The proposed possibility for natural or legal persons to file substantiated concerns to an authority 
opens for complaints from a large variety of organizations, companies and individuals.  While we 
support the introduction of the procedure as such, we beli eve that it should be limited to the 
parties who are direct stakeholders in the matter, and at least limited to the three listed groups 
in Article 9 (2).  
 
4. A related matter when filing substantiated concerns to an authority regards confidentiality. Since 
information related to the reporting to authorities in many cases could include personal and 
business sensitive information , confidentiality must be adhered to as part of the reporting, 
investigation, and supervision by the authorities. We propose that a confidentiality requirement 
is added to the legislation.

5. The proposal is unclear regarding the due diligence obligations of a Group. In parts of the proposal 
the focus is on the company level, which in a Group structure would mean the local subsidiaries 
across several or most member states. This means that one Group potentially will be governed by 
the rules of several different national supervisory authorities. We suggest that the  legislation 
clearly states that companies within a Group are regarded as one legal entity , with a reporting 
obligation only to the supervisory authority of the country where the parent company has  its 
registered office, and that substantiated concerns sho uld fall under the competence of the 
authority in this country. 
 
6. The pro posed directive leaves severa l aspects of the due diligence obligations open for 
interpretation by the supervisory authori ties and creates legal uncertainty  and an  uneven 
competitive environment. It risks being further amplified when the directive is implemented in 
national legislation. To reach a desirable harmonization and further clarity the obligations , we 
would like to stress the importance of guidelines , to be jointly developed by the industry and 
the Commission, as foreseen by the proposal for the Directive. Electrolux is available to support 
in the development of the guidelines. 
 
7. Finally, we note that due diligence obligations are also included in the proposal for an update of 
the Battery Legislation and that the chosen legal instrument (a Regulation) and legal base would 
result in a more stringent level of harmonization.

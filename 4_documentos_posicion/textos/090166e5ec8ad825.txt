Proposal for a Directive of the European Parliament and of the Council on 
Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence and amending Directive (EU) 2019/1937 – 
Comments by Technology Industries of Finland 
 
 
The Finnish Technology Industries organization promotes competitiveness and operational 
preconditions for technology industry c ompanies, the  largest and most important export 
sector in Finland. Technology industry comprises five sub -sectors: electronics and electro 
technical industry, mechanical engineering, metals industry, consulting engineering and 
information technology industries. The sector accounts for 50% of total Finnish exports and 
70% of Finnish R&D -investments. There are 300 000 directly employed in the sector, total 
employment effect is around 700 000. Constantly progressing technology industry creates the 
basis for the Finnish welfare conditions. 
 
 
Key Messages 
 
• Technology Industries of Finland supports the goal of strengthening the protection of 
human rights and the environment through EU-wide harmonised due diligence 
regulation.  
• Regulatory approach should be risk-based and provide for predictable, clear and 
proportionate operating environment for companies – especially taking into account 
administrative burden.  
• Although the proposed regulation would directly apply to companies of bigger size, 
the obligations will run through contracts to companies of all sizes. 
• Special care must be taken to guarantee coherence of EU sustainability and 
responsibility acquis. Provisions of Digital Product Passports and data transferred 
through those must form the basis for reporting. 
• Several legal proposals have been made, mostly under the Green Deal to guide 
Europe’s development to carbon neutrality. The European model is built on emission 
trading system, taxation and avoiding carbon leakage. These regulatory approaches 
are based on price-driven market mechanism, which is the most effective way to 
catalyse the green transformation. Europe does not need yet another layer of 
Member State level regulation to that end, it would add solely administrative burden 
of companies.  
• Proposed Civil Liability provisions threat to mangle the internal market and are 
problematic vis-à-vis the foundations of European states’ civil law and it is hard to 
identify the added value. We would recommend concentrating on due diligence on 
this proposal.

Due Diligence 
 
We support the due diligence approach and suggest that it would be developed further 
by adding a risk-based approach so that the obligations would start from risk analysis 
to set an adequate level for actual measures. A broad and vague scope of due diligence 
obligations requiring companies to avoid any adverse impact on human rights,  
environment and good governance would be practically impossible to manage for 
companies. The risk-based approach would allow companies to focus their efforts and 
make the due diligence requirements more effective. 
 
The proposed scope, linked to actual human rights and environmental agreements, 
provides a certain legal basis for companies’ actions. Some key concepts of the 
proposal have been concretised by referring directly to international human rights and 
environmental agreements and conventions . Yet , it is to be noted that  such 
international agreements cannot be directly binding on companies.  These agreements 
do not contain well -defined and clear obligations which  would set a predictable legal 
framework for companies. 
 
Also, the proposal includes vague concepts and insufficiently concrete definitions, such 
as the definition on ‘established business relationship’ and ‘value chain’, which leave 
too much room for inte rpretation and will inevitably cause legal uncertainty on 
companies.  
 
The directive should aim for lean and meaningful requirements and policies, keeping 
in mind that the actual requirements must be such that they are possible also for SME 
companies to follow. Although the proposal seeks to take account of the impact on 
SMEs, it is  questionable whether measures to support SMEs are sufficient. We also 
suggest that the EU framework shall only apply to those parts of the value chain with 
which companies have a direct contractual relationship, i.e., the tier 1 suppliers of the 
upstream supply chain. 
 
The regulatory approach needs to take into account the reality that our economy has 
been and will also be hit in the future by different shocks, which may require forming 
of new business relations swiftly. Regulation should not unnecessarily stifle the change 
of partners when it is needed.  
 
Complaints procedure of the proposal should be built upon the whistleblowing channel 
as to the directive (EU) 2019/1937  in order to avoid double requirements on 
consecutive data protection impact analysis and other requirements. 
 
When the Commission develops the Model Clauses meant in the article 12, it should 
involve industries and companies of all sizes to give their views.

Supervision 
 
Networks of supervisory authorities should be developed further taking into account 
coordination of supervisory activities so that companies are only dealt by one 
supervisory authority for one issue. Inspiration can be sought from the mechanism on 
the GDPR.  
 
The role of the governments and public authorities in the E U and the third countries 
will need to be a core part of the framework. They should provide companies with the 
data required for managing sustainable supply chains, such as relevant and reliabl e 
data on specific human rights risks. Otherwise, companies will struggle to comply with 
due diligence obligations. 
 
Civil Liability  
 
The proposed civil liability article is problematic because it is hard to fit with 
foundations of European civil law on third party damages. Different solutions in 
member states would not necessarily build for common single market, favourable for 
companies to scale up their activities.   
 
We are concerned that the proposal will in practice create a completely new regulatory 
framework for civil liability, rejecting the basic principles such as the requirement for 
a causal link and the principle that only the  legal entity that caused the damage is 
liable. The proposed civil liability article  does not adequately reflect such 
considerations, as it provides that a company is under certain circumstances liable for 
damages caused by an adverse impact arising as a result of the activities of an indirect 
partner with whom it has an established business relationship. 
 
 
Directors’ duties 
 
The proposal includes controversial elements going beyond due diligence, e.g. , 
directors’ general duty of care and executive remuneration. These intruding corporate 
governance elements are unnecessary to attain the objective of the proposal and they 
were rejected twice by the Regulatory Scrutiny Board. They will add to the legal 
uncertainty and interfere with longstanding, well -functioning corporate governance 
models of the member states.  Therefore, we strongly recommend not to include the 
proposed articles on directors’ general duty of care and executive remuneration into 
this directive.

Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive – Intesa Sanpaolo position 
The Intesa Sanpaolo Group  is one of the top banking groups in Europe and is engine for 
sustainable and inclusive growth, with a significant ESG commitment, a world-class position in 
Social Impact and strong focus on climate.  It is the leading Italian banking Group, offering its 
services to 1 3.5 million customers through a network of over 3.700 branches well distributed 
throughout the country. The Group has a strategic international presence - approximately 1,000 
branches and 7 million customers  - with subsidiaries operating in commercial banking in  12 
countries in Central Eastern Europe and Middle Eastern and North African areas , and an 
international network of specialists in support of corporate customers across 25 countries . 
Intesa Sanpaolo is aware of the responsibilities tha t are connected from its position and of 
having a significant impact on the social and environmental space in which it carries out its 
business. Thus, it has chosen not to seek only profits, but also to create long-term value for the 
Bank, its employees, customers, community and the environment. For this reason, Intesa 
Sanpaolo wants to be a responsible financial intermediary that generates collective value , 
being mindful that innovation, development of new pro ducts and services and corporate 
responsibility can contribute to reducing the impact on society of phenomena such as climate 
change a nd social inequality.  Our presence in the more relevant international sustainability 
indexes attests to the effectiveness of our approach. 
Intesa Sanpaolo recognises the urgency to act to achieve the EU social and environmental 
goals. These objectives are vital for successfully coping with the ongoing global environmental 
crisis and for supporting the respect of human rights, which are increasingly endangered also 
in Europe. 
Introduction 
On the 23th of February 2022, the European Commission adopted the proposal for a Directive 
on Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence 1 (CSDDD) (hereinafter the Proposal) , aimed at 
encouraging corporate responsibility related to the sustainability impacts of its operations and 
of the value chain, ensuring that human rights and processes of environmental risk 
management and mitigation are part of business strategies. 
Intesa Sanpaolo welcomes the Proposal and believes it is a necessary milestone in order to 
contribute to the achievement of European Union’s sustainability objectives. Intesa Sanpaolo 
is a responsible player and believes that integrating sustainability considerations into decision -
making processes is not only functional to risks ’ reduction but that has positive effects for the 
achievement of corporate long-term objectives and resilience. 
Level playing field 
Intesa Sanpaolo supports the goal of the Proposal to create a level playing field for entities 
operating within the European Union and to consolidate the transparency elements for 
customers and investors. The extra-territorial reach of the Proposal, with the inclusion in the 
scope of companies operating in the EU  but based in third countries, is considered very 
positively. Such an element would avoid competitive imbalances between entities operating 
in the same market. 
However, we want to draw the attention also to potential fragmentations within the EU market. 
In our opinion, the transposition phase could lead to a risk of gold-plating by Member States. 
To mitigate this risk, it will be extremely important for the European Commission to timely publish 
clear guidelines that can simplify and harmonise the transposition of the contents of the 
Directive, ensuring a high level of harmonisation within the European Union.

Moreover, a clarification on the Directive’s scope is needed. Specifically, the Directive sets 
obligations for companies regarding actual and potential adverse impact on human rights 
and environment arising from their own operations or those of their subsidiaries . It is not clear  
whether all subsidiaries are therefore included in the scope or only those exceeding the 
thresholds set by the Directive. 
Implementation of the Directive in the financial sector 
Intesa Sanpaolo welcomes the consideration of financial sector’ s specificities in the CSDDD 
proposal. Financial undertakings are, in fact, already supervised and subject to obligations in 
terms of internal conduct and specific governance requirements. We support the definition of 
value chain for financial firms : limiting it to clients that are large undertakings  is necessary in 
order to ensure a realistic applicability of the due diligence obligations .  The exclusion of small 
and medium-sized enterprises is consistent with the difficult balance that the Directive aims to 
achieve, and it is important that it is preserved during the legislative process. 
We note that the exclusion of “SMEs receiving loan, credit, financing, insurance or reinsurance” 
is insufficient to cover the breadth of financial services that are provided to SMEs  (e.g. 
payments services ). A new wording introducing  a general exclusion of SMEs from the value 
chain of regulated financial undertakings is needed. 
Moreover, the proposed definition of value chain includes client’s subsidiaries “whose activities 
are linked to the contract in question ”. In order t o gauge the impact on internal operation al 
processes, it is important to explain clearly what it entails. In the case of undertakings with highly 
complex corporate chains, we note that this impact could be significant. 
In order t o properly assess the effect of the obligations of the Directive on financial 
undertakings’ operations , a clear definition of  customer relationship processes impacted  is 
needed. In view of the objectives of the Directive, we assume that the due diligence obligation 
for financial undertakings concerns only the phase of onboarding of new clients , and not 
already established  relationships (e.g. the opening of further contracts  or the provisions of 
financial services following the first). Clarification in this respect is essential. 
Still related to  this aspect, further details are required  regarding the type of financial services 
that will fall within the regulatory scope . Intesa Sanpaolo recognises the relevance of lending 
and credit services, which are already characterised by internal evaluation activities  of the 
client in the social and environmental perspectives. The extension to other financial services, 
on the other hand, should be adequately assessed and defined, in order to understand (and 
evaluate) its potential benefits with respect to its higher operational burden. 
We acknowledge the attention paid in the Proposal to the essentiality of the services and 
products offered by the financial sector, including the derogation from the termination of the 
relationship with a client  as a means of remedying its adverse impacts on  environmental 
matters and human rights. In this sense, however, it is necessary to better detail what is meant 
specifically by "substantial prejudice", at the bas e of the aforementioned derogation  in the 
Proposal. The nature of the services offered by the banking sector makes them vital for clients’ 
financial balance . It is therefore difficult to understand which occasions are not considered 
essential, and then insufficient for triggering the derogation. 
Directors’ duties 
A more precise definition of directors is required in order to correctly identify impacted roles by 
the Proposal. Specific reference to executive directors and/or non -executive directors would 
be desirable. 
The CSDDD proposal provides a broad definition of stakeholders . Their identification  should 
remain firmly among the directors’ duties; therefore we ask to specify this point in order to avoid 
fragmentation in the transposition.  We recall that the directors of banks and financial 
intermediaries, supervised by the competent authorities, must comply with the laws, regulations 
and supervisory provisions that already incorporate the interests of stakeholders.

Adverse impact and code of conduct 
In order to maintain a high degree of harmonisation within the borders of the European Union 
market, also in determining the underlying principles, further details in the definition of "adverse 
impact" with regard to the environment and human rights are needed. 
As regards the prevention of adverse impact, we acknowledge potential criticalities in the due 
diligence requirement of seeking contractual assurances throughout a bank’s value chain. This 
will represent a heavy burden and will increase the potential risk of litigation.  
On the basis of international best practices and the European and national regulatory 
environment in the sector,  Intesa Sanpaolo  already applies internal codes (code of ethics, 
codes of conduct) aimed at developing an ethical corporate culture in the operation 
performed by different functions.  
A greater detail of the contents and main elements of the code of conduct proposed in the 
Directive is required, in order to understand which adjustments are necessary to the documents 
and processes currently implemented. 
Moreover, we highlight that the Proposal provides only a short description of the elements 
characterising the due diligence required. More details are desirable to evaluate actions to 
comply. We note that the obligation to carry out a due diligence on clients represents a 
meaningful impact on the internal operational process and on the customer relationship. 
Intesa Sanpaolo already leans on several ESG providers in order to produce internal ESG scores 
on clients. It would be desirable to obtain a clarification on the role that ESG ratings may have 
in the due diligence process on clients provided by the CSDDD Proposal. 
It is also important to specify potential links between the financial undertaking’s code of 
conduct and its value chain. Considering the current CSDDD proposal, such codes of conduct 
apply to clients covered by the definition of value chain. The application of this principle would 
require the client to  contractually agree to comply  with a multitude of codes of conduct, in 
the light of the potential business relationships with different financial undertakings. The latter, 
in turn, should comply with each client’s code of conduct  (e.g. the credit institution could be 
seen as a supplier for certain services such as consulting ). The feasibility of such elements is 
highly unlikely, especially because codes of conduct will be updated annually. 
Coherence and alignment with other European initiatives 
The scope and some elements of obligation may clash with existing European legislative 
initiatives, such as SFDR and the Sustainable Taxonomy of the European Union, or which will 
come into force, as CSRD, or in the analysis phase for a possible future implementation, such 
as the Social Taxonomy.  
For example, the obligation to adopt a transition plan, provided for in the Proposal, coinc ides 
with the disclosure requirement contained in the Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive. 
The latter shall delegate the Commission to define  the information to be contained in the 
transition plan. In order to maintain consistency between the two initiatives on this element, a 
specific reference in the CSDD proposal to CSRD is required.  
We recommend that the harmonisation of all legislative initiatives  be taken into account to 
ensure the effective and correct implementation of the requirements and  obligations 
contained therein.

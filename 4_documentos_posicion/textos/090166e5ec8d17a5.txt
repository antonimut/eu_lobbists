on Commission adoption 
for DIRECTIVE OF THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND OF THE COUNCIL 
on Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence and amending Directive (EU) 
2019/1937 
 
 
 
23.05.2022 
 
Avocats Sans Frontières (ASF) is an International Non -Governmental Organization created in 
1992 in Brussels (Belgium), whose mission is to contribute to the establishment of institutions 
and mechanisms which allow independent and impartial access to justice, and which are able to 
guarantee the protection of fundamental rights (civil and political as well as economic and social), 
including the right to a fair trial. 
From Kinshasa to Tunis, from Kam pala to Bangui, our teams inform people about their rights, 
strengthen civil society and lawyers to better support litigants, and encourage legislative reforms 
for better respect for human rights. 
ASF estimates the European Commission (EC)’s Corporate Sust ainability Due Diligence (CSDD) 
proposal is an important step toward meaningful legislative actions to address the threat of 
corporate activities to human rights and the environment and to ensure sustainable growth.  
However, ASF joins the relevant number of civil society representatives, activists, and human 
rights defenders in calling on EU institutions and Member States to substantially improve the 
text to trigger significant changes and set a global example. 
Despite the Commission’s initial ambitions, A SF warns the new law will risk being limited in its 
scope. 
The proposed legislation will only apply to a limited number of large companies. Such an 
approach is not aligned with the UNGPs and the OECD standards, which clearly state that all 
businesses, regardless of size, have a responsibility to respect human rights and that States 
shall ensure they respect such responsibility. In that regard, the definition of high-impact sectors 
appears as a restrictive understanding of business sectors, and also of busin ess areas. On this 
matter, the proposal should include also conflict and post-conflict areas.  
According to the text, companies could be held liable for harms committed at home or abroad 
by their subsidiaries, contractors and suppliers, and their victims will have the opportunity to file 
lawsuits before EU courts. However, the definition of companies and business relations is not in 
line with OECD nor the UNGPs standards and could make it easy to avoid obligations. In fact, 
this approach fails to provide le gal clarity and risks reducing a company’s due diligence

obligations towards its business relationships further down the global value chains to mere 
contractual clauses.  
Moreover, the EU proposal should be able to clarify the scope of due diligence obliga tions and 
provide rules ensuring that social and environmental objectives are taken into account following 
a human rights-based approach. For this, some improvements are needed in the material scope 
of the proposal.  The first one consists in enlarging the list of Human Rights instruments referred 
to in Annex I. The second one concerns the notion of violation to be replaced with the one of 
impact endorsed by existing international standards. A third aspect regards enriching the list of 
environmental conventions and principles of international and European environmental law that 
companies should respect.  
Besides, such a framework is deeply intertwined with the effective and meaningful participation 
of all interested and potentially affected gro ups in situations of vulnerability and structurally 
disadvantaged, including special protection environmental and human rights defenders.  
For this reason, ASF calls to strengthen the text by endorsing stakeholders’ engagement at every 
step of the due diligence process. 
One of ASF’s key pillars for action concerns the empowerment of civil society and potential 
victims promoting access to justice. In this regard, we welcomed the introduction of an obligation 
for companies to remedy the harm they might have ca used or contributed. However, the 
proposal limits it to financial compensation and fails to recognize, as per the UNGPs, a company’s 
responsibility to provide an effective remedy. Often, the remedy goes beyond financial 
compensation and may include reparat ion or restoration, for example in the case of 
environmental damage. Moreover, recalling UNGPs, complaints procedures should be legitimate, 
accessible, predictable, safe, equitable, and transparent as stated in UNGPS.  
A key issue ASF pleaded for is the need for civil liability in due diligence legislation. The proposal 
establishes the possibility for companies to be held liable in civil courts for damages committed 
in their own operations or value chains allowing national authorities to sanction companies who 
fail to respect their obligations. However, the civil liability regime results limited in scope. If EU 
companies have secured contractual assurances from their business partners that they complied 
with the company’s code of conduct, EU companies can be  safe from civil liability claims. In 
addition, an important aspect to point out is that the proposal does not specify on whom the 
burden of proving that the harm was indeed caused by a failure to comply with the CSDD’s 
obligations falls. In transnational cases of corporate violations of human and environmental 
rights, the imbalance of power and information between the parties makes it clear that the 
burden of proof should be reversed and always fall on the company, also when it comes to lifting 
the corporate veil and establishing parent companies' liability for their subsidiaries and value 
chain partners located in third countries. 
Furthermore, ASF stresses the need for the proposal to address key obstacles to victims’ access 
to remedy: high costs of litiga tion, disproportionately high burden of proof, opacity, lack of 
access to information, restricted legal standing, and limited statutes of limitations.  
ASF wishes the legislation on Due Diligence will establish an effective framework for sustainable 
corporate governance promoting fundamental rights, respect for the rule of law, and 
preservation of environmental resources.

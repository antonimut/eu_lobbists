Eramet contribution to the EC proposition of a 
Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive  
 May 2022  
Eramet, a European mining and metallurgical group, is a key player in the extraction and refining of 
base metals necessary for the overall growth of our economy (manganese, titanium mineral sands) 
and those critical for the transition to a climate neutral economy (nickel, lithium, cobalt). 
As a European company and currently chairman of the European Raw Materials Alliance (ERMA) 
Battery task force, we are working towards becoming a major contributor to fulfil the European 
demand in base metals by 2030. We have been developing activities with high growth potential, from 
our own mines of Lithium in Argentina and Nickel/Cobalt in Indonesia and New Caledonia to 
metallurgical transformation activities in Europe, combined with batteries recycling development 
projects, with the aim to become a one-stop shopping place for raw materials supplying the European 
electrical vehicles value chain.  
Eramet supports the E uropean Commission’s (EC) proposal, presented in February 2022, for a  
Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive (CSDDD).  
 
The EC ambition to impose due diligence requirements on companies regarding risks that may arise 
on their value chain in the areas of human rights (child labor for instance) and environment (pollution, 
biodiversity for example) is essential to improve the industry transformation and transparency.  
 
However, Eramet is concerned about the Commission’s recommendation relative to the 
denomination of the extractive industry as a high-impact sector (article 2.1.b.iii).  
 
Eramet will continue to contribute actively in the discussions to improve reporting methodologies on 
environmental and social issues, as a complement to the new  Corporate Sustainability Reporting 
Directive (CSRD) requirements and the taxonomy regulation. 
 
 
CONSIDERING THE EXTRACTIVE INDUSTRY AS HIGH -IMPACT S ECTOR WILL UNDERMINE OUR 
ENERGY TRANSITION   
 
The development of a European mining, refining, and recycling industry is one of the EC ambitions 
to secure our critical raw materials supplies and develop our strategic autonomy. The European Raw 
Materials Alliance (ERMA) was created in 2020 to support the development of this goal.   
 
Mining activities have a key role in the success of the ecological and energy transition by providing 
the raw materials needed to develop low -carbon energy and mobility and by supporting the shift of 
our economy to new fossil-free value chains, for which minerals and metals become critical inputs. 
 
Nickel, Cobalt, and Lithium are the main critical raw materials needed to achieve the goal to be climate 
neutral by 2050 as they are increasingly used in batteries for new mobility, energy storage and mobile 
devices. For instance, in a full electric vehicle, a single battery needs approximately 50kg of nickel, 
7kg of cobalt and 45kg of lithium and it is expected that electric vehicles by 2030 will represent more

> Eramet strongly questions the EC suggestion to label in the future CSDDD the extractive 
sector as “high -impact” (article 2.1.b.iii) , while due diligence commitments are expected 
from all business actors (according to the OECD and the UNGP) and while the European union 
has stated on multiple occasions (for example in the September 2020, action plan on raw 
materials) the European objective to develop an European mining, refining and recycling 
industry to secure responsible sourcing of raw materials in Europe.  
 
> Eramet is convinced that such a denomination , that can also be found in the CSRD,  will 
reinforce the lack of confidence of the European consumer in our industry. At the opposite, 
the level playing field required for being competitive against non -European players shall be 
supported by the development and the implantation within the EU of the highest 
responsible mining standards available in the world. 
 
o Eramet recalls that adopting a responsible mining certification at European level 
remains key and is a recommendation that was made in Philippe Varin's January 2022 
report in France but also in Europe, along the lines of the "responsible mining" 
principles published by DG Grow in July 2021. 
 
> Today, the alignment with these standards is not recognized by the European legislator as a 
prerequisite to access to the European market. Eramet deeply regrets this situation. 
 
o Eramet ask for the appropriate regulatory support that will set out criteria to achieve 
a sustainable mining industry and the financial support to carry out the investments 
made necessary by the energy transition. 
 
> Therefore, Eramet calls for the removal of our sector from that list.  
 
 
The absence of a European consensus on a responsible mining standard puts us in a situation of unfair 
competition with other players who do not respect our rules, to the detriment of environmental  
protection and human rights. 
 
Eramet calls for a real launch at Commission level of a roadmap on CSDD standards for responsible 
mining, by instance through the endorsement of  the Initiative for Responsible Mining Assurance 
(IRMA) standards, and their adoption in the body of European legislations.  
 
Taxonomy is currently the best tool to secure and develop a responsible supply in critical raw 
materials: listing our sector as “high-impact” in the CSDDD and CSRD will undermine current efforts 
of European mining companies to make their activities eligible and align with the taxonomy.

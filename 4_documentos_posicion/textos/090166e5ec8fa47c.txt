CFA Institute feedback on the European Commission proposal for a Directive on Corporate 
Sustainability Due Diligence 
 
CFA Institute welcomes the opportunity to provide its views  on the European Commission 
proposal for a Directive on Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence. We support the objectives of 
such an initiative, which are to put in place a horizontal legal framework to foster businesses to 
increasingly contribute to the respect of the human rights  and environment in their own  
operations and through their value chains . The proposed Directive would set out minimum 
standards at EU level on the identification, prevention, mitigation and accounting of companies’ 
adverse human rights, and environmental  impacts, and for the development of adequ ate 
governance practices.  
In September 2021, Carbon Tracker published the report “ Flying blind: The glaring absence of 
climate risks in financial reporting ”, which underlines that over 70% of the world’s biggest 
organisations, including EU companies, do n ot fully account for climate -related risks. The 
proposed requirements on due diligence might push EU companies to address adverse climate 
impacts, and inform investors on firms’ actions on the prevention and mitigation of climate -
related risks.  
The legislative proposal excludes small and medium enterprises (SMEs) from the scope of the 
due diligence obligations. CFA Institute argues that the same standards should be applied to all 
firms, irrespective of their size. Nevertheless, since the proposed requireme nts are expected to 
be particularly onerous for SMEs (particularly  for micro-companies), standards could be initially 
made voluntary for these businesses, and then be required at a later stage. 
Since the proposed EU requirements for due diligence duties for companies cover a great extent 
of activities, the level of corporate directors’ responsibility would be extremely broad. To allow 
for a smooth implementation of the rules across member states,  a clear EU guidance explaining 
how organisations are expected to fulfil their duties would be essential , along with a detailed 
cost-benefit analysis as such a regulatory development will have a significant impact on the EU’s 
competitivity and competition framework, both for industry and financial services, which should 
not be underestimated or disregarded. 
In the 2021 CFA Institute report “Corporate Governance and ESG Disclosure in the EU”, we 
emphasised that it is often in businesses’ interest to carry ou t a thorough due diligence and 
disclose detailed information concerning perceived risks. Investors could play a big role in 
engaging with firms in order to encourage the provision of high-level due diligence practices, and 
consider any adverse human rights , social an d environmental impacts and risks  related to 
environmental and social issues. 
CFA Institute also advocates for the presence of a significant proportion of directors on company 
boards to have expertise on ESG issues.  The 2018 CFA Institute report  “The Corporate

Governance of Listed Companies ” underlines that board members should have the knowledge, 
skills, and experience required to advise management in light of the particularities of the 
company, its businesses, and the competitive environment.  The proposed EU rules on corporate 
due diligence would require boards to have appropriate knowledge and expertise on health and 
environmental matters, including climate change and respect of human rights. Moreover, such a 
requirement also would enhance boa rd diversity. The CFA Institute report “Investment Firm of 
the Future” highlights that diversity is a big contributor to companies’ better decisions, and that 
firms looking to get positive benefits of diversity should diversify their people among multiple 
dimensions, including ideas, gender, ethnicity, national origin, knowledge, and educational 
institution. 
Finally, we would like to stress that the  legislative initiative  on Corporate Sustainability Due 
Diligence, and the future governance policy work should be addressed jointly by DG JUST 
(Directorate General for Justice and Consumers) and DG FISMA (Directorate-General for Financial 
Stability, Financial Services, and Capital Markets Union ) of the European Commission . Allowing 
DG FISMA to participate in the co-legislators’ table and discuss these reforms would facilitate 
coherence and better coordination of the Commission work on governance reforms with the 
development of the Capital Markets Union agenda. In addition, Economy and Finance Ministers 
should be involved in the negotiations of corporate governance rules in Council meetings for the 
same reason.

EDFI’s Comments on the Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence (CSDD) Proposal 
 
Promoting Corporate Sustainability 
As long-standing impact investors and ESG pioneers, European Development Finance Institutions ( DFIs) 
very much support the introduction of a 'due diligence obligation' that is binding on companies and makes 
companies more aware of the risks and consequences in terms of the environment and human rights. 
Extensive d ue diligence of all business relationships, particul arly focusing on human rights and 
environmental risks, is a key pillar of our already stringent ESG risk management processes. We agree that 
promoting corporate sustainability is a “shared responsibility” which cannot be achieved by one actor or 
piece of legislation alone.  As close partners of the EU, we congratulate the initiative to support sustainable 
value chains, and emphasise the good cooperation in Team Europe Initiatives  (TEI) such as the one on  
Sustainability in Global Supply Chains. We look forwa rd to contributing to this TEI with our responsible 
investment activities and with the proposed  EFSD+ guarantee programmes , which will primarily target 
Sub-Saharan Africa and low-income countries in other regions . Harmonised regulation at the EU level is 
desirable as this is an international challenge,  and as the due diligence obligation concerns international 
value chains. An international approach also increases the impact and allows for a level playing field 
between companies.  
 
Consideration of impacts on Low- and Middle-Income Countries (LMICs) 
• We appreciate the work of the European Commission to mitigate potential undesired effects of the 
proposed Directive on LMICs with the preparation of accompanying support to the Directive. We 
concur on the need for a comprehensive range of support measures for companies, civil society and 
partner countries to meet the future requirements of the proposed Directive and comm end the 
Directorate-General for International Partnerships on preparing guidance to design effective and 
inclusive accompanying support to the legislation. 
• From our perspective, it is critical  that private sector companies in developing countries 
understand and implement effectively due diligence and contractual requirements with regards to 
human rights and environmental considerations in a suitable manner.

• We are pleased that t he Commission acknowledges the need to take systemic issues 1 in partner 
countries into account , also to prevent  that companies disengage from high -risk countries or 
sectors in an attempt to de -risk their supply chains. Further to the work of the European 
Commission on this topic so far, we would welcome more clarity in the proposed Dir ective about 
how to deal with persistent systemic issues . For example, additional research on ways to mitigate 
undesired effects of the systemic issues  could be helpful, also to ensure the continued inclusion of 
investments in LMICs’ counterparts in EU companies’ value chains. 
• Moreover, we would appreciate more clarity on the application of the proposed Directive to 
countries outside Europe. In our view, we should ensure that the EU law is effectively aligned with 
international standards as well as the partner countries’ national laws. From the experience with 
our committed counterparts in LMICs, the improvement and alignment of standards can take some 
time. Therefore, we would favour an adapted and progressive application of the Directive, allowing 
to address the specificities of LMICs.  
 
Clarifying the Definition of Civil Liability 
European DFIs embrace the OECD  Guidelines and the UNGPs and use them as a standard. From our 
perspective, t he proposal could be improved by aligning  further with the OECD Guidelines approach  
whereby different 'liability' cases are defined: a distinction is made between causing, contributing, or being 
linked to non-compliance with the requirements outlined in the proposed Directive.  
 
To conclude, we would like to re-emphasise our support for the CSDD. Moving forward, we think it is critical 
for private sector companies that the above-outlined aspects are taken into account and clarified to ensure 
effective and straightforward implementation of the proposed Directive on the ground, especially in LMICs.  
 
 
 
EDFI – the Association of European Development Finance Institutions – was established in 1992 to 
support and promote the work of bilateral Development Finance Institutions (DFIs)  which are state -
controlled development organisations . With a combined portfolio of €48 billion, includi ng over € 20 
billion of investments in Africa, EDFI’s 15 member institutions share a vision of a world where the private 
sector offers people in low  and middle -income countries opportunities for decent work and improved 
lives, and where private investment flows are aligned with the Sustainable Development Goals and the 
Paris Climate Agreement. EDFI members apply stringent investment criteria aimed at s afeguarding 
financial sustainability, transparency, and environmental and social accountability. EDFI’s mission is to 
promote the joint interests of its members, inform policy, and drive innovation in industry standards. 
EDFI’s membership includes BIO (Belgium), Cofides (Spain), DEG (Germany), Finnfund (Finland), FMO 
(The Netherlands), IFU (Denmark), BII (UK), Norfund (Norway), OeEB (Austria), Proparco (France), 
SIFEM (Switzerland), Simest and CDP Development Finance (Italy), SOFID (Portugal), Swedfund 
(Sweden).

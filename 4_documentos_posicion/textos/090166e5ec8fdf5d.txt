 
 
Policy Hub’s Response to the Consultation on the Corporate Sustainability Directive – Additional points 
Amsterdam, May 
 
 
Additional to the Policy Hub’s consultation answer, we would like to emphasise the following points that should be 
also addressed:  
• More guidance is needed: Compared to human rights due diligence, measuring environmental impacts and 
carrying out environmental due diligence is more difficult and still relatively new. Further guidance on 
environmental due diligence would therefore be useful, including adverse impacts generated throughout 
the life-cycle of production, use and disposal etc., as well as guidance on the required climate plan. Also, 
guidance for organisations to complement the contractual clauses is welcome.  
• Guidelines: When it comes to the garment and footwear sector, we urge the Commission not to diverge 
from the relevant OECD Guidelines that already exists and that companies have already aligned with. 
Furthermore, stakeholders need to be appropriately consulted when any EU guidelines will be developed.   
• Smart mix of measures: The due diligence should be part of a toolbox of complementing policy measures 
and EU should address these issues through multilateral cooperation, and bilateral and unilateral actions, 
for example creating incentives and address underlying root causes of structural issues. Responsible 
business conducts should be encouraged and supported.  Supporting investments, innovation and existing 
leading industry and multi-stakeholder initiatives to promote harmonization in implementation will be 
important. 
• Inclusion of SMEs in the scope of the Directive. As recognised in international standards, a due diligence 
process is relevant for all companies, regardless of the size and sector. From the scope defined in Art. 2, the 
Directive covers only a significantly limited part of companies operating in EU markets, casting doubts on 
the legislation’s effectiveness to create the intended level playing field and meaningful impact. We 
recommend the extension of the scope of the Directive to also cover small and medium-sized companies. 
With the right guidance and design allowing for the prioritization of risks and proportionality applied to 
SMEs, due diligence can be conducted by all. In this way all companies are part of the solutions needed. 
Moreover, EU Member States should support SMEs in the implementation of the Directive instead of 
companies. 
• Civil liability: As foreseen in international standards, the responsibility of business enterprises to conduct 
due diligence should be distinct from issues of legal liability. We recommend separating the scope of due 
diligence requirements from the one where civil liability applies. Furthermore, companies shall not be held 
liable if they acted with due care in line with the due diligence obligations of means. 
• Suspending a contractual relationship should be the ultimate last resort. The current language of the 
proposal suggests that companies have a legal obligation to suspend or terminate a business relationship in 
several circumstances, which is not fully aligned with the intentions mentioned in paragraphs recitals 32-36 
of the Proposed Directive. While the suspension or termination of a business relationship may be the best 
option in rare cases, the proposal does not allow for enough room for constructive engagement with the 
supplier to address and remediate the issue at hand.

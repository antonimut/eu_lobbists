 
Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence 
Public consultation 
23.05.2022 
 
 
Founded in 1973, the Fédération de la Haute Couture et de la Mode (“the Federation”) 
represents over a hundred French and international brands and is the voice of the creative and 
luxury fashion industry. The Federation manages commissions aimed at raising  active 
awareness in relation to emerging issues in the legal, social, sustainable development and 
innovation fields.  
 
The Federation and its members are already active players in fostering sustainable and 
responsible behaviour and anchoring human rights and environmental considerations in their 
operations and corporate governance.  
 
At the French level, the 2017 French law on “ devoir de vigilance des sociétés mères et des 
entreprises donneuses d’ordre”1 already provides for “vigilance plans” that the members of the 
Federation subject to this text  have implemented to prevent violations to human rights and to 
the environment in France and abroad, that could stem from their activites as well as from those 
of their direct or indirect subsidiaries. The Federation has also taken an active part in the debates 
linked to the “Anti-Waste and Circular Economy”2 and “Climate & Resilience”3 laws and fully 
supports the objectives pursued, in particular to provide more information to consumers on the 
environmental and social footprint of fashion. 
It is thus developing, within the framework of the experimentation for an environmental display 
of textile clothing products implemented at the request of the French Ministry of Ecological 
Transition, a method for evaluating the environmental performance of these products.  One of 
the particularities of the experimentation conducted by the Federation is that it aims to evaluate 
not only the environmental performance of products but also their social performance, in line 
with key issues for our sector and therefore  anticipating the future requirements of pub lic 
authorities.  
The Federation is also actively participating in the project "Enhancing traceability and 
transparency of sustainable value chains in the garment and footwear sector" of the United 
Nations Economic Commission for Europe (UNECE) developing standards and experimenting 
with the use of blockchain to improve traceability in the industry.

Besides the legal requirements, many members of the Federation have for their part and for 
several decades implemented various actions to raise social standard s both within their 
companies and with their suppliers and subcontractors (production sites, control of the 
industrial value chain). They are particularly committed to respecting international standards 
on responsible business conduct, including on workers’ rights. 
The Federation and several of its members are also part of treacability pilots operated by the 
Strategic Committee for the Fashion & Luxury Goods Sector ( Comité Stratégique de Filière 
Mode & Luxe), a collective experimentation aimed at testing traceability and accelerating its 
implementation in the sector. 
 
The Federation and its member s therefore fully support the European Commission’s 
objectives. 
However, the Federation would like to draw the Commission’s attention to the opportunity t o 
further consider the following issues and key points raised by stakeholders.  
 
In particular, the Federation would like to provide comments on the following key points 
considered in the proposal for a Directive on corporate sustainability due diligence:  
 
Use of the product in the Value Chain – Article 3(g) 
 
While the inclusion of downstream players in the scope of the directive is, in principle , 
welcomed by our members, we would suggest a clarification of the notion of "use of the 
product" in the "value chain" as defined in Article 3(g). Indeed, the current wording could imply 
that a company subject to due diligence requirements could be held responsible for the wrongful 
or fraudulent use  of one of its products by a consumer, which  we believe goes beyond the 
purpose of the text and could not be implemented from a practical perspective.  
 
Indirect business relationship – Article 3(f) 
 
Many Federation’s members already embrace the due diligence approach, and ensure that their 
suppliers commit to do so as well , through charters aimed at guaranteeing high social and 
environmental standards. Many members have also implemented monitoring and alert systems 
and produce  periodic reports to account for the effectiveness of these commitments.  In 
particular, compliance with the above-mentioned charters is considered by our members as a 
prerequisite before entering into any commercial relationships with potential business partners. 
The control of  such compliance is monitored  by Group or Company representatives or an  
external partner. 
 
In addition, in order to mitigate the remaining potential risks, especially when dealing with the 
lower end of the industrial value chain, our members i nvest in obtaining labels (e.g. GOTS 
certification) and are engaged in adopting codes of ethics or joining collective initiatives such 
as the "Fashion Pact". 
 
Our members are therefore committed to continuously improving the control and monitoring 
of their industrial value chain on social and environmental issues. In this context, a clarification 
of the notion of "indirect business relationship or partner", as referred to in Articles 7(3) and 
8(4), would be welcome in order to establish a more precise  scope of responsibility of 
companies subject to the due diligence requirements.

Reporting  
 
At national level, members of the Federation that would not, individually, be directly subject to 
the obligations resulting from the French law on “devoir de vigilance” may fall within its scope 
as they belong to larger groups which have a wide range of activities (fashion, leather goods, 
jewelry, perfumes, spirits, art of living, travel, communications, media, etc.). 
  
In order to ensure consistency between French and European law  as well as to allow for a 
consistent group approach applying to subsid iaries that may be subject to the Directive we 
believe that the reporting obligations set out in the proposed Directive should be consolidated 
at group level.  
This is also in line with the reporting obligations  currently provided for in the Corporate 
Sustainability Repoting Directive (CSRD). 
 
Implementation of the Directive for SMEs 
 
The Federation wishes to draw the Commission's attention to the impact of the future text on 
SMEs, which, for those belonging to the textile industry, will be subject to the due diligence 
requirements at lower thresholds than those usually set for SMEs at the European level.  
At the French level, the law on “devoir de vigilance” applies to companies with more than 5,000 
employees, regardless of their annual turnover.  
 
Considering the gap between the thresholds currently submitted to due diligence requirements 
and the scope provided for by the Proposal, t he Federation therefore ca lls for a pragmatic 
approach and a s trong support for SMEs  in the ir implementation of the new obligations 
resulting from the future Directive.

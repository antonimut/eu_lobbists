INTRODUCTION 
 
FEBIS stands for Federation of Business Information Services, the specialized and 
recognized industry body of providers of global B2B business intelligence services for 
managing trade risks.  
 
Today´s 140 plus members of our federation are present in nearly 60 countries throughout Europe, 
Asia, Africa, and the Americas. 
 
FEBIS members facilitate B2B trade relations and transactions with a focus on credit and risk 
intelligence services. These services require updated, accurate and relevant business information 
to produce beneficial, tailor-made solutions as well as scorings to assess the risk of default of 
businesses. By interacting between different business users and/or institutions of all sectors and 
sizes and helping them to take relevant decisions on their cashflow management, FEBIS members 
can be seen as trustworthy partners providing data and relevant financial information on trade 
counterparts. They are instrumental in helping companies manage their trade credit (amounting to 
*4 times the bank lending in several EU Member States), their onboarding and their KYC processes. 
 
FEBIS members welcome the European Commission’s proposal to revise corporate due diligence 
and add more sustainability items, following therefore the recommendations from the EP report from 
MEP Lara Wolters and from the OECD report. We also welcome the fact that the proposal on Due 
Diligence is to be seen in conjunction with the Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD) 
proposal which will pave the way for a better reporting of ESG items in Europe.  
 
The proposal aims at enhancing a better global framework for due diligence and at clarifying the 
regulatory landscape which is most welcomed. FEBIS members however would like to draw your 
attention on the 4 following points on which clarification may be needed in order to ensure a fully 
workable and compatible regulatory framework: 
o Clarification of the definitions and scope and of the type of enterprises concerned  
o Value chain approach and SME exemption 
o Sanctions 
o Definition of business relationship  
 
 
1. Definitions and scope  
 
The proposal gives a very large definition of Due diligence as “ actual and potential human rights 
adverse impacts and environmental adverse impacts for their own operation, the ones of their 
subsidiaries and of the whole value chain with whom they have established business relationship.” 
 
The definition encompasses a lot of companies and articles 1 and 2 stress out which companies are 
drawn into the scope depending on turnover and number of employees, making differences between 
large companies, small companies and non-EU companies which are nevertheless in scope 
because of the territoriality principle.  
 
The biggest question for FEBIS members relates once again to the definition of a company: since 
it seems from definitions that SMEs and sole trader would be excluded from the scope, but

how will that work in practice when a value chain approach is required and when the CSRD 
mandates some reporting also for certain SMES?  
 
Furthermore, the scope of the Due Diligence proposal may differ from the one of the CSRD but also 
from the one of the ESAP which is focusing on listed companies only. How could we bridge these 
differences in scope and make sure reporting is made easy also for companies and for data users?  
 
As outlined in several FEBIS papers, all EU regulations should align and consider the capacity in 
which an individual person interacts. Therefore, natural persons referred to should in our view be 
understood as consumers in the sense of EU Consumer Credit Directive and in other related EU 
consumer laws ie as natural person, who is acting for purposes which are outside his trade, business, 
or profession and thus in a purely private capacity. Therefore, sole entrepreneurs, as well as 
companies’ legal representatives or ultimate beneficial owners should be seen as “natural 
persons acting in their trade or business capacity” in all EU legislations, including the present 
one, to ensure that business transparency, trade credit checks and management and fight against 
fraud can continue to be performed with business reporting services. 
 
Furthermore, some clarification on the way article 11 will be applied would be welcomed, as article 
11 states that companies that are exempted from corporate reporting under 2013/34 must publish 
an annual statement on matters covered by the due diligence directive once a year.  
 
2. Value chain approach and SME exemption 
 
The proposal wants to put in place a value chain approach but at the same time excludes SMEs 
from its scope, so it seems to us a bit contradictory.  The value chain approach would work only 
for some sectors as per article 1, and the due diligence obligation is only at the beginning and for 
finance and credit related activities only. It seems to us that this should prompt more access to 
beneficial owner information and an ongoing duty of care and responsibility to ensure better fight 
against fraud.  
 
On the SME exemption, SMEs are defined as anything that is not in the scope of article 3 (1, 2 or 3) 
of the accounting directive 2013/34 which could again be problematic to handle with the value chain 
approach. FEBIS members would recommend sticking to the value chain approach and 
encompass all companies, as duties of care for clients and suppliers are also needed in business 
information.  
 
Furthermore, on directors’ duties of care compliance, it would be better to clarify clearly that directors 
and all managers concerned are viewed as business persons acting in their business capacity and 
therefore not having a natural person exemption.  
 
3. Sanctions 
 
The sanctions considered for non-compliance will be decided by Member States who will be able to 
choose if they put in place financial or non-financial sanctions. How will coordination work and what

EU or national authorities will be responsible for supervision, for compliance monitoring and for 
issuing sanctions? If there are financial sanctions, will they be capped as in other EU regulations, or 
will Member States have all leeway to determine them?  And which coordination will be put in place 
with the bodies responsible for CSRD compliance? These all seem questions that would need to be 
addressed.  
 
4. Definition of business relationship 
 
Last but not least, the proposal puts in place a definition of business relationship in article 3 and 
even goes deeper in differentiating between business relationship and an established business 
relationship. But what happens when a business relationship is indirect, e.g., with a supplier, a client 
or another relevant trade partner?  
 
Furthermore, in business information, the whole purpose of getting a credit scoring or a business 
report overview is precisely to get information on existing but also on envisaged business 
relationship for example with prospects or leads. How will that be considered in the due diligence 
proposal, and will it affect the way business relationship are defined? For example, in trade credit 
management, it is very important to be able to assess business relationship for the whole value 
chain.

       
 
We welcome the upcoming Cyber Resilience Act as horizontal legislation addressing all relevant 
aspects of the cybersecurity of digital products and services. We support the introduction of 
mandatory horizontal cyber security requirements for all forms of ICT products, services and 
processes, regardless of whether they are linked to a physical product and regardless of the target 
group. Security by default and by design should be guiding principle.  
Obviously, the introduction of cyber security requirements does not relieve the user of his own 
responsibility to ensure the security of his systems. The user must be aware of the sensitivities and 
required protection of the purchased systems and services and take the actions required on his part, 
such as the timely implementation of security updates. As such, it is a shared responsibility. 
We believe the horizontal cybersecurity requirements should be based on the New Legislative 
Framework (NLF), independently of products group. The technical specifications to meet the 
cybersecurity requirements under the CRA should be laid down in harmonised European or 
international standards, which are to be applied as part of conformity assessment. 
Functional and non-functional requirements must be tailored to the intended use in relation to the 
reasonably foreseeable risk and already existing standards and rules within the sector in which the 
digital products or services are used. 
The Cyber Resilience Act must bring legal clarity. It is important that the proposal for a CRA cleans up 
the regulatory environment around cybersecurity and doesn’t add an extra layer of complexity. 
Multiple or even conflicting requirements for the same products or economic operators should be 
avoided.  
Software by its very nature needs to be updated regularly. The manufacturer/developer should be 
responsible for providing updates for vulnerabilities for a certain period, to be determined in a 
European context. But this requires differentiation by type of hardware product and type of 
software. It is not possible to have a one-size-fits-all period that applies to all types of hardware and 
software. The costs must be in proportion to the security obtained, user responsibilities and a 
reasonable end of life after which such obligations no longer apply.  
Hardware manufacturers and software developers must be able to demonstrate their compliance 
with cyber security requirements. In principle, a self-assessment of conformity by a hardware 
manufacturer or software developer should provide sufficient confidence that the security 
requirements are met.  
A third-party conformity assessment is more cumbersome and less flexible, but may be desirable for 
certain categories of products (to be established on a European level), to demonstrate compliance 
with high-risk hardware/software.  
Effective market surveillance is a crucial element in the framework of this regulation. The CRA must 
ensure that authorities have the necessary competences, to be able to competently commission 
and/or carry out tests, so only products that meet the relevant requirements are on the market.

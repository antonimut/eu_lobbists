as regards empowering consumers for the green transition through better protection

May 2022  
 
 
AIM, the European Brands Association, welcome s the European Commission’s proposal on consumer 
empowerment for the green transition. It is broadly aligned with AIM’s position on green claims and 
sustainability information outlined in its Manifesto “No Data, No Claim”. Eliminating greenwashing is key to 
create a level playing field for businesses and truly empower consumers.  
 
AIM brand manufacturers strongly support the paradigm “No Data, No Claim”:  
➢ Environmental/‘Green’ claims should be voluntary, relevant, based on clear rules for businesses who 
decide to m ake them, and use robust, commonly recognised and science -based 
criteria/methodologies and definitions , and, where applicable,  per existing and recogni sed 
international standards, such as ISO standards. 
➢ Any environmental/‘green’ claim made by manufacturers regarding their products should be 
substantiated with scientific data : In the absence of credible, commonly recognised 
criteria/methodologies, transparent data  and the opportunity for appropriate scrutiny, a claim 
cannot be made. 
 
In this contribution, we outline a number of points in the Commission’s consumer empowerment proposal  
that need further clarification and adjustments. Our focus lies solely on environmental claim issues that are 
tackled through amendments to the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive (UCPD) 2005/29/EC. 
 
In summary:  
➢ AIM supports that this Directive requires that targets and commitments  for environmental 
performance of future claims are tracked as required by other EU legislation,  but requiring an 
additional independent third party monitoring scheme creates needless duplication. 
➢ AIM supports that the  definitions in the proposal (Article 2 (o) and (q)) should be aligned more 
closely with the definitions of ‘environmental claim’ and ‘non -specific environmental claim’ as 
outlined under ISO 14021. 
➢ AIM believes that  environmental claims/ terms that are already defined and qualified in 
international standards should be deleted from the list of ‘generic environmental claims’ outlined 
in Recital 9.

1. Environmental performance of future claims  & monitoring system – Article 6  paragraph 2 (d) /          
Recital 4 
• Since there is no definition of the “independent monitoring system”, we are assuming that this could 
mean contracting a third party to track every single future environmental claim. For companies that 
are required to comply with the EU Non-Financial Reporting Directive, EU Corporate Sustainability 
Reporting Directive, EU Deforestation Regulation and EU Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence 
Directive, or that have voluntarily made commitments to the EU Code of Conduct on Responsible 
Business and Marketing Practices 1, and therefore have in place robust internal processes (including 
internal requirements, audits and transparent reporting), this request creates unnecessary 
duplication that is  excessively burdensome. 
 
➢ AIM supports that this Directive requires that targets and commitments for environmental 
performance of future claims are tracked as required by other EU legislation,  but requiring an 
additional independent third party monitoring scheme creates needless duplication. 
 
2. Generic environmental claims – Article 2 (o), (q) / Recital 9 
• Currently, recognised international standards are establishing principles for the development and 
use of environmental labels and declarations. We believe that the International Standards (ISO 14020 
series) should be used as a basis for environmental claims and their requirements when available, as 
is the case today for existing EU regulations/guidelines (e.g. (EC) No 66/2010 – based on EN ISO 14024 
type I).

specific req uirements, as already outlined in the ISO Standards  (table below for illustration

➢ AIM supports that the  definitions in the proposal (Article 2 (o) and (q)) should be aligned more 
closely with the definitions of ‘environmental claim’ and ‘non-specific environmental claim’ as 
outlined under ISO 14021. 
 
• The examples that are listed as “generic green claims” in Recital 9 need to  be more differentiated: 
vague claims such as ‘environmentally friendly’, ‘eco -friendly’, ‘eco’, ‘green’, etc. need to be 
distinguished from environmental claims/ terms that are already defined and qualified through 
international Standards (e.g. ISO 14021) such as ‘carbon neutral’ or ‘biodegradable’.  
 
➢ AIM believes that  environmental claims/ terms that are already defined and qualified in 
international standards should be deleted from the list of ‘generic environmental claims’ outlined 
in Recital 9. 
 
• According to today’s standards, all self-declared claims already need to be accompanied by a specific 
qualification. As AIM, we support such specification of the claim to be  provided in clear and 
prominent terms on the same medium or through digital means.  Due to the size of packaging and 
the available space on-pack, and since we do not aim to overload the consumer with information or 
to increase the pack size to be able to fit all information requirements, digital means are an important 
tool to communicate any specification and details around an environmental claim to consumers.  
 
➢ AIM advocates to add the  use of digital means (e.g. through a QR code) to communicate any 
specification and details around an environmental claim to consumers.

of that company’s sustainability strategy with or without icons. As these types of corporate ESG logos

• Additionally, the problem for consumers with a product-related sustainability label arises when a 
label is used that suggests certification of that product or process by a third-party verification 
scheme, but when this is not the case. 
• Certifications based on religious beliefs  such as k osher and halal labels are not considered , in our

vegetarian or plant-based. 
 
➢ AIM emphasises that a sustainability label means any product-related voluntary trust mark, quality 
mark or equivalent, the use of which is likely to suggest certification of that product or process by 
a third-party verification scheme . Vegan, vegetarian, plant -based, kosher and halal labels are not 
considered, in our view, to be sustainability labels. 
3.2. Display of sustainability labels – Annex I 4a 
• We understand and support the objective of the Commission to tackle and decrease the amount of 
sustainability labels. However, we  believe that a s long as traders have a robust substantiation for 
environmental claims, they should be able to portray sustainability labels and the regulation should 
require substantiation and qualification  for these labels  that need to be  specified in clear and 
prominent terms on the same medium or through digital means.  
• Furthermore, as the text stands today, the traders would for example not be allowed to use the 
Mobius loop symbol in recyclable packaging.  
 
➢ AIM therefore supports a broadening of the approach to allow the display of sustainability labels 
under the conditions that it is: 
o defined in a certification scheme (see definition in Article 2(s)); or 
o defined as specific symbol under international standards; or 
o established by public authorities; or 
o substantiated in clear and prominent terms on the same medium or through digital 
means. 
 
4. Advertising a common practice – Article 6 paragraph 2(e) 
• Although Recital 5 includes an example, a clear definition of what a common practice constitutes is 
missing. This lack of clarity will create uncertainty. 
 
➢ Further guidance on  what constitutes a common practice  in the relevant market  would be  
important.  
 
 
About AIM – European Brands Association 
AIM (Association des Industries de Marque)  is the European Brands Association , which represents 
manufacturers of branded consumer goods in Europe on key issues that affect their ability to design, 
distribute and market their  brands. AIM’s membership comprises 2500 businesses ranging from SMEs to 
multinationals, directly or indirectly through its corporate and national association members.

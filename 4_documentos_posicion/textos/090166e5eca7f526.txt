EFAMA Feedback on CSDR Refit Proposal. 
General comments 
EFAMA warmly welcomes the publication of the CSDR Refit proposal .  The EC has clearly taken onboard 
the feedback from market participants on the structural flaws in the design of the mandatory buy -in (MBI) 
regime and its potential for unintended consequences on capital market efficiency and resilience . 
It is surprising therefore that the CSDR Refit should contain any provisions for MBIs at all, given the negative 
impacts that are widely known and understood on bid- offer spreads, on market liquidity  and on driving 
settlement onto non-EU CSDs.   
At the same time, we recognise and commend the efforts by the EC to address some of the provisions that 
lacked clarity  (buy-in agent) or  were simply lacking  (pass-on mechanism, exclusion of certain types of  
transactions, payment symmetry).  Despite these improvements,  we firmly believe that MBIs, used in a 
more limited scope and under determined circumstances, are still a tool whose net effect will be negative 
for European capital markets.   
We are more apt to support a further recalibration of cash penalties to address any shortfall in settlement 
efficiency gains than to introduce mandatory buy-ins in Europe.  Further increases in penalty rates should 
be the required next step in finetuning the settlement discipline regime. An increase in penalties would best 
be accompanied by an in- depth analysis of the root causes of continuing settlement fails.  Such analysis , 
based on qualitative and quantitative data should help guide the next round of measures.   
Furthermore, there is the inclusion of a new provision (para 13a) empowering the EC to suspend MBIs for 
a certain category of instruments if there is a threat to financial stability or the proper functioning of financial 
markets.  Firstly, it is questionable that the EC would access appropriate data quickly enough and put in 
place a suspension before significant damage to financial markets.    The very need for such a suspensive 
provision points to the inherent problems associated with mandatory buy-ins.   
CSDR Refit Provisions on Mandatory Buy-Ins 
The CSDR Refit proposal seeks to refine the scope of mandatory buy -ins by allowing the EC to determine 
the financial instruments or categories of transactions within those instruments that should be subject to 
MBIs.  The MBIs should apply, it is foreseen, where such measures are seen as a proportionate means to 
address settlement fails . This remains a vague provision, it is unclear what metrics would be used to

determine ‘proportionate means’, nor do we understand what would drive the decision on which financial 
instruments or categories of transactions to include. 
The 2-step approach , in which the implementation of MBIs would be deferred and only triggered if 
necessary, is equally fraught with questions.  Most pressing is the question of how long the cash penalties 
regime will be allowed to take effect before an assessment is carried out to determine if any of the 3 
triggering conditions are met.   
Cash penalties have only been operational since February 2022, and as market participants we have 
witnessed first hand that this process is still being streamlined with the various actors along the chain.  On 
the whole, implementation across the settlement chain has been more burdensome than perhaps first 
anticipated.  This is in part attributable to the lack of readiness of market actors, including CSDs and 
custodians to implement cash penalties  at go-live.  But even when full reporting cycles  are established, a 
lack of standardized approach means that at asset manager level, we are faced with monthly reports 
arriving on different dates, inconsistent approaches to applying a credit or a debit, and differences between 
daily and monthly amounts.  This creates a major problem for reconciling the data at client/fund level, 
compounded by the fact that we can not isolate the level at which the issues are arising. 
In this regard, we welcome the creation of three cross -industry task forces to develop more standardized 
approaches to cash penalties. 
 It is fair to say then that it will be some time before we can consider that the cash penalties regime is stable 
and functioning smoothly, and longer after that before we can hope to see changes in market behaviours 
and improvements in IT capacity.  What is the envisaged timeframe in which to assess settlement efficiency 
improvement?  
Related to the above are the changes to article 2(a), with the proposed triggering conditions for MBI s 
including a) long-term continuous reduction in settlement fails and b) appropriate levels considering the 
situation in third countries, it is unclear which quantitative metrics will be applied to determine that one of 
these conditions has been met.  The existing text leaves a wide margin of interpretation on what constitutes 
‘ long-term continuous reduction’ and ‘ appropriate levels considering..’.  We are keen to better understand 
the ambition level put forward by the EC in terms of settlement/fail rate per asset class, the reasoning 
behind it as well as the peer markets to compare to. 
After careful deliberation, we have come to the conclusion that revisions to the SDR are simply premature 
at this stage, not having the necessary visibility on the effect of cash penalties.  A more targeted and 
relevant regime could be developed recalibrating cash penalties, and invoking mandatory buy -ins in 
extremis, only after the cash penalties regime has been effective for a significant period of time and 
necessary structural changes within participant firms have been observed. 
  
Transparency on ESMA data 
It would be very  helpful if the data on settlement fail evolution were made public such that market 
participants are also aware of the emerging trends and better able to react. 
Primary market transactions/SFTs 
Here we are pleased that there will be a carve- out for settlement fails caused by factors not attributable to 
the participants to the transaction or for operations that do not involve two trading parties.   This should 
exempt ETF primary market orders and corporate actions which should not be in-scope for mandatory buy-
ins as indicated under Recital 4 .

The scope of exemptions should be clarified in the level 1 text, recital and amendments, to include securities 
financing transactions (repo, reverse repo and stock lending), as well as provision/receipt of collateral.  The 
current text suggests t hese operations will be excluded if their timeframe is sufficiently short to render a 
buy-in ineffective.  Greater clarity would be welcome here. 
Definitive list of exempt instruments and transactions 
The amended proposal should therefore provide a definitive list of financial instruments and transactions to 
which the MBI regime would not apply.   
Buy-in agent 
While the impact assessment heavily referenced the issues with the current provisions around the 
appointment of a buy-in agent, namely high/unjustifiable costs and lack of choice, it was not clear how the 
legislative proposal would introduce more flexibility in the use of buy-in agents. There is a lack of clarity on 
how more choice could be brought in, with a larger range of actors and the possibility for firms to execute 
their own buy-ins.  We note the proposed amendment to Article 7 (15) of CSDR to allow ESMA to revise 
the RTS also with respect to the buy-in agent.     
Payment symmetry  
The new paragraph 6 introduces payment symmetry requiring prices difference to be paid out to participants 
in such a way that the buy-in is restorative to what the economics of the trade would have been had there 
not been a settlement fail  .  However, Article 7, paragraph 7 is not amended consequently f or cash 
compensation, which implies a remaining asymmetrical treatment in the case of the cash compensation 
differential. We recommend revising this article to harmonize the symmetric payments for both buy-ins and 
cash compensation. 
Pass-on mechanism 
This amendment is clearly welcomed  in as far as it will avoid multiple buy-ins for a single failed transaction 
across a long chain of transactions. 
Suspension of MBIs 
In the new paragraph 13a, on ESMA’s recommendation the Commission would be empowered to suspend 
MBIs for certain financial instruments if these present a threat to financial stability.  
CSDR Refit Provisions on Cash Penalties 
We welcome the proposal to further define the scope of cash penalties, excluding participants ‘..where 
those settlement fails are caused by factors not attributable to the participants to the transaction or for 
operations that do not involve two trading parties.

ABOUT EFAMA 
 
EFAMA, the voice of the European investment management industry, represents 28 member 
associations, 58 corporate members and 24 associate members. At end Q1 2021, total net assets 
of European investment funds reached EUR 19.6 trillion. These assets were managed by more 
than 34,600 UCITS (Undertakings for Collective Inve stments in Transferable Securities) and 
almost 29,600 AIFs (Alternative Investment Funds). At the end of 2020, assets managed by 
European asset managers as investment funds and discretionary mandates amounted to an 
estimated EUR 27 trillion.

Orange’s views on the EC public consultation on cybersecurity of digital 
products and ancillary services (CRA)  
 
 
Orange welcomes the opportunity to provide its views in conjunction with  the 
European Commission’s public consultation on the proposal for a Cyber Resilience 
Act (CRA). Orange  shares and  supports the CRA overall objective of introducing 
additional cyber resiliency requirements directly addressing obligations to 
manufacturers of digital products. This approach helps filling the gap caused by the 
revision of the NIS Directive that , unlike the CRA,  follows an unbalanced approach 
that gives telecoms providers cybersecurity liability for their whole ICT supply chain  
instead of addressing obligations directly to digital product manufacturers. 
 
The technical evolutions in  the telecommunication sector  are bringing more  
interdependence of providers and third parties in the 5G architecture . Such changes 
need a clear sharing of liabilities to ensure network and service resilience through an 
efficient allocation of responsibility for cybersecurity. Vendors of digital products that 
become an integral part of the critical services delivered to end -users are often best 
placed to manage their own vulnerabilities, and thus to address cyber threats related 
to their own products in the first place.  Moreover, policy frameworks that directly 
addresses liabilities to manufacturers avoid overlaps and incoherencies. 
 
Furthermore, building products and services secure -by-design and protecting them 
throughout the entire life cycle  is equally  essential for the security of the digital 
ecosystem. It is for example critical for hardware manufacture rs and software 
developers t o provide regular security updates, to prevent attacks and provide 
immediate remedies to mitigate any attacks. Moreover, the new requirements should 
be proportionate to the level of risk of a given digital product when used by businesses 
operating in critical economic sectors.

Proposed amendments to the Consumer Rights Directive
Thedefinitionof durabilityintheempoweringconsumers proposal wouldbeconsistent withtheoneintheSalesofGoodsDirectiveEU)2019/771:“‘durability’ meanstheabilityofthegoodstomaintaintheirrequired functions and performance through normal use”.It however differs fromthe definition of durabilityas proposedinarticle2of theESPRproposal: “theability of aproduct tofunctionas required, under specifiedconditions of use, maintenanceandrepair,until a limiting event prevents its functioning”.
We doubt that introducingsuchadifferenceis beneficial. It is keytousedefinitions andconcepts inaconsistent manner, to foster proper understanding and especially as information about a product's“durability” andtheexistenceof a“commercial guaranteeof durability”canbeexpectedtobeincludedon the Digital Product Passport (DPP) at some stage.
The current Consumer Rights Directive (CRD) foresees informingconsumers about “theexistenceandthe conditions of after-sale customer assistance, after-sales services and commercial guarantees”, ifsuch guarantees are provided. Commercial guarantees of durability, where proposed by marketoperators, are thus already covered by that very provision.The only new element of the Commission proposal is a suggested requirement to also inform theconsumer in case no “commercial guarantee of durability” is provided. We question the value of thisminor modification of the CRD.

EU GUIDELINES FOR THE TEN-T 
Achieving the objectives of the European Green Deal: the importance of 
sustainable urban nodes for European citizens’ journeys 
 
The decarbonisation of the Trans-European Transport Network (TEN-T) appears as a strategic axis to reduce 
emissions from the transport sector, which remains one of the most polluting in Europe. The revision of the 
EU guidelines for the development of the T EN-T stands as a real opportunity to translate European climate 
ambitions into concrete projects for greener and smarter mobility.  
The TEN-T appears to be even more strategic as it constitutes a key base for the development of various 
European policies. T ransdev supports the general priorities proposed by the European Commission in the 
context of the revision of the TEN -T network guidelines, particularly those encouraging greater use of 
sustainable transport. 
Encouraging key investments in sustainable mobility for European citizens 
Everyday mobility has a crucial role to play in decarbonising the TEN -T, especially within urban nodes. With 
the average length of journeys in private vehicles by European citizens being only 20km 1, public transport 
solutions represent a valid alternative considering the variety of means of transport available. The carbon 
footprint of public transport, considering the number of passengers carried, is much lower than that of the 
private car2. Beyond its environmental benefits, public transport reduces congestion in urban areas and offers 
social and economic benefits for the most vulnerable households by providing affordable mobility services 3. 
In this respect, better integration of urban nodes in the network development strategy, as  proposed by the 
Commission, is essential.  
However, some provisions need to be further developed, in particular to  clarify the measures needed to 
decarbonise the TEN -T. This clarification will be key to propose investments aligned with the EU's climate 
objectives and the real needs of European citizens. This is all the more important considering that in 2020 
the European Court of Auditors noted that the Connecting Europe Facility allocated only 1% of its budget to 
urban nodes. Going forward, the role of public transport will have to be fully recognized, especially within the 
provisions on the requirements for urban nodes. 
Ensuring a sustainable transport system adapted to the needs of citizens living 
in urban nodes 
Strengthening the role of peripheries in the definition of urban nodes 
Urban areas are home to 75% of the European population and are centres of economic and social activity. Of 
the total emissions from daily travel, 56% are generated in urban areas 4. Most of these emissions are 
generated using private cars and mainly for journeys between the peripheries and the city centres. As such, 
peripheries should be fully integrated into the definition of “urban nodes”, and the infrastructure necessary 
to alternatives to the private car should be deployed in these nodes. The inclusion of bus and coach terminals 
in the definition of urban nodes as proposed by the Commission is, in this sense, a major step forward.

However, other types of infrastructure have a crucial role to play in the deployment of sustainable mobility 
solutions along the TEN-T. In this sense, public transport should be included in the defined priorities. 
 
Accelerating the energy transition of urban nodes by supporting public transport 
Public transport remains one of the most effective solutions to ensure both seamless travel between modes 
and convenient travel planning within urban nodes, especially in multimodal passenger transport hubs. 
Public transport operators must be able to contribute to the development of sustainable forms of mobility in 
the future. This must be done with alternative energie s that will allow them to manage increasing mobility 
flows, while reducing their carbon footprint. To support the development of shared mobility solutions, the 
TEN-T will have to provide the necessary infrastructure for the deployment of public transport solutions within 
these nodes. For instance, dedicated lanes for public transport on arterial roads  is crucial to ensure the 
competitiveness of this mode of transport. 
The TEN-T should also direct investments towards alternative fuel infrastructure for public transport vehicles, 
including in private depots. This would encourage the uptake of zero -and low-emission buses in urban and 
suburban areas. A technology-neutral approach should be maintained, recognising the role of natural gas and 
biogas to grant a tra nsition phase for heavy -duty vehicles such as buses. This is even more necessary as 
hydrogen is not yet mature enough and electricity remains too expensive. 
 
Strengthening the role of local authorities in the governance of Sustainable Urban Mobility Plans 
Involving local urban transport authorities in the designing of SUMPs (Sustainable Urban Mobility Plans) is 
crucial, it would enable developing an end-to-end mobility policy that is virtuous and adapted to the needs of 
citizens while meeting the EU’s climate objectives. Annex V on the requirements for SUMPs should therefore 
clearly underline the necessary role that local and regional authorities must play in the managing of transport 
policy in urban areas. In this sense, it would also be beneficial if these  authorities set up local or regional 
policy frameworks defining the priorities they wish to see included in the SUMPs.

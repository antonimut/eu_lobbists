Brussels, 1 June 2022  Subject: Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation – EVA Comments  Dear Sir/Madam, Thank you for the opportunity to comment on the proposed Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation (ESPR). I am writing to you from the European Vending & Coffee Service Association (EVA) which represents the interests of the European-wide industry. This includes vending and hot drinks coffee machine manufacturers, vending operators, and product suppliers. As of 2020, the number of vending machines in Europe was 4.4 million, dispensing 25 billion products annually and with an annual revenue of €12.3 billion. The latter two figures have reduced by almost a third since reaching a peak before the pandemic. We consider that the vending industry is quite unique in terms of its business model, through which it ensures that the lifespan of a machine is extended considerably through inbuilt design features. Machines are built on a modular basis, allowing easy repair and replacement of various key components. Typically, the vending operator purchases a machine from a manufacturer and he ensures that the product is maintained and when necessary, he can replace parts in the machine himself to effectively and significantly extend this product lifecycle. Overall we consider that in vending the ‘repairability score’ is exceptional as most parts and components can be removed and replaced. The EVA notes that several of the overall objectives of the ESPR aim to achieve what the vending industry already does as its standard business model.  As a general point, we therefore ask the EU Commission when introducing product group-specific requirements under the ESPR to acknowledge and consider the good practices and sustainable policies already implemented in certain sectors, namely the vending industry, prior to the introduction of any targeted measures. In addition, the difference between B2B products of which vending is part, and B2C products must be recognised. For B2B products, it is our view that end consumers/general public do not need to understand in detail a sustainability score or details about the product lifespan when they interact with a product they have no say on or influence over. On the other hand, customers of vending machine manufacturers (i.e. the vending operator) and for professional clients (i.e. procurement) may find this information useful and desirable, but the differences between the professional and consumer environments must be noted. The EVA notes and supports the overall objectives of the policy, including to improve the sustainability of products and the incentivisation to purchase more sustainable products. We would however like to highlight one general area of potential concern in that we are not clear how and when exactly the new policy would interact and or replace the measures already in place with regards to product specific Ecodesign and Energy Labelling Regulation. Regarding the impact assessment, it concludes that “overall costs for economic actors involved in product manufacturing are likely to increase, with some costs passed on to consumers. The experience of the Ecodesign Directive is, however, that increased costs are more than offset by financial savings for consumers over the long term.” We do not agree particularly with the second

We would like to make some more specific comments below in relation to certain aspects of the draft Regulation. Article 2: Definitions We believe that the definitions of ‘remanufacturing’ (Article 2 (16)) and ‘refurbishment’ (Article 2 (18)) are perhaps too focused on waste. The machines or objects that are to be remanufactured or refurbished in our view are not technically waste as they are not intended to be discarded as per Article 3 of Directive 2008/98/EC.   Article 5  This outlines the types of criteria that will be regulated over the full lifespan of a product, including information on environmental footprint of a product. The EVA is unsure at this stage how the EU Commission proposes to calculate the environmental or CO2 footprint of a product and believes there could be serious difficulties in trying to determine this accurately. This is particularly relevant for our sector where – as described earlier – the business model ensures machines are designed on a modular basis, with parts regularly changed, and as such any meaningful calculation over the lifespan of the product could prove challenging. Articles 8,9 and 12 We note the that the ESPR will introduce a requirement for a ‘Digital Product Passport’ and will require this to be registered. While we understand the rationale for this, the EVA is concerned as to the additional workload and resource requirements for manufacturers to fulfil the requirements. More so when a ‘sustainability label’ could be introduced. As a B2B sector, we are not totally convinced in practice that such a step would bring much added value to the typical customer in vending who purchases a machine e.g. the vending operator. Article 15 We would ask the EU Commission to clarify what exactly would be considered ‘mimicking a label.’ The EVA developed more than 15 years ago an energy consumption calculation standard which is voluntary but widely used within our industry and has helped drive significant improvements to the energy efficiency of vending machines. The test result is typically used and promoted by manufacturers and vending operators to demonstrate how efficient their machines are and is furthermore used in procurement as a criteria and comparison tool. We would therefore have concerns over whether displaying information such as the test result, which may be provided as a ‘energy score’ or ‘energy rating’ would potentially be considered as something which could confuse consumers or customers, and hence fall foul of the intent of this Article. Article 58  We note the policy to make green public procurement criteria mandatory. The EVA provided a lot of technical feedback and statistical input into the current voluntary EU GPP criteria for food procurement, catering services and vending machines published in 2019 in order that they are appropriate for our sector, despite clearly being challenging.

Additional comments for consultation on International freight and passenger transport – increasing 
the share of rail traffic 
 
It is our position that once again there seems to be an overly unambiguous focus on the modal 
shift of goods from road to rail with the EC.  
 
This strategy has been pursued under the auspices of the EU since the early 1990s, where 
several € 100 billion has been invested in strengthening the railway and in ensuring a modal 
shift. It is happened under the Marco Polo program, Shift2Rail just as the vast majority of CEF 
funds have been allocated to the railways. 
 
It has probably been the most expensive and least efficient solution to ensure more sustainable 
transport in the EU, as the result has simply been that road transport today accounts for a larger 
share of transport in the EU. Today, more than three quarters of all transport within the EU takes 
place by road. 
 
One of the main reasons why it has gone so wrong is that for far too long the different modes of 
transport have been seen as competing instead of focusing on them as parts of an overall system 
that must play together. 
 
To this must be added an inability and unwillingness to acknowledge that road transport is in 
many cases the best, and that it is especially on the long international trips that there is a 
potential for moving transport away from the roads. 
 
Basically, EU transport policy should be about creating a well-functioning transport system. We 
are in a supply crisis where there is an urgent need for truck drivers and more capacity in road 
transport. 
 
In a well-functioning transport system, the transport will automatically move to where it makes 
the most sense, and a well-functioning transport system will be able to strengthen both rail, sea 
and road transport while increasing efficiency. 
 
As it is not possible to build railways at all ports, terminals, and companies, it is necessary to 
understand that a strengthening of rail transport also presupposes a strengthening of road 
infrastructure, so that lorries can easily access ports and terminals. 
 
Had the EU invested just a fraction of the funds invested in the railways in a conversion of road 
freight transport, the EU would be much closer to meeting its climate targets. 
 
Here it is important to note that despite a lack of investment, road freight transport is already 
well under way in the green transition, and transport companies are now able to transport goods 
with a lower climate footprint through efficiencies and new technology.

Thus, it is seriously time to put an end to the one-sided understanding of rail transport as 
greener than, for example, road transport. This is supported by the special report of the 
European Court of Auditors "EU transport infrastructures: more speed needed in megaproject 
implementation to deliver network effects on time". The special report points out that railway 
projects are not in fact as green as they are intended to be, because the calculations 
underestimate the CO2 emissions during the construction phase and overestimate the potential 
for modal shifts.

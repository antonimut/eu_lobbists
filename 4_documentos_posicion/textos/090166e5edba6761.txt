Ecodesign for Sustainable products: how to ensure it makes 
sustainable products the norm 
 
1) Provisions to keep  
1.1 Ambition 
• Wide scope of the initiative, includes intermediate products 
• Aims to cover sustainability issues across the entire value chain, including when chemicals 
pose a problem  
• Has the potential to step in should product legislation fail to 'sufficiently’ cover 
environmental aspects  
• Can introduce mandatory Green Public Procurement (GPP) criteria, as well as ban the 
destruction of unsold goods, or use information requirements on both a mandatory and 
informative basis to foster product sustainability 
1.2. Enforcement 
• All necessary references to the market surveillance regulation have been made, including 
very relevant links to avoiding circumvention, enabling market surveillance authorities to 
withdraw products from the market in case of non-compliance, including  based on 
consumer testing programmes etc. Could even be further improved with stronger penalties 
in case of non-compliance, but that is mostly in the hands of MS 
1.3 Implementation 
• Welcome the establishment of a working plan and Ecodesign Forum, and the consultative 
process foreseen which includes civil society 
 
2) Provisions to strengthen  
2.1 Implementation 
• Procedure for setting up the working plan: product regulations that have failed to address 
sustainability hotspots should become part of ESPR 
• Prioritization of sectors: respective share in contributing to EU consumption footprint  
• Prioritisation of measures: there is a hierarchy in what measures need to be considered to 
decouple resource use from growth, the higher tiers circular economy need to be prioritised 
for the measures. 
• Schedule for implementing measures: all of this needs to be further detailed in the articles 
themselves, Including setting up a transitional working plan into an article to avoid delays.  
2.2 Scope & Definitions 
• Make ESPR applicable to digital services like online gaming or steaming etc.

2.3 Classes of performance & labels 
• Should not be based on current average performance but should be forward-looking with 
regards to net-zero compatible pathways/calculating product footprint against planetary 
boundaries. 
• Incentives for MS should in this space only apply to the top class, not the top two as 
currently foreseen in the regulation. Same rationale for mandatory GPP criteria.  
2.4 Information requirements 
• Mainstream the no data/no market principle and introduce the Digital Product Passport 
(DPP) by default, adding full bill of material & chemicals, as well as the product assembly 
scheme into the passport. The no data/no market principle is especially important for it to 
generate data so that the EC has the basis for future requirements 
• Include a proper implementation plan for the DPP as well as a plan for information 
requirements to receive a similar level of availability and transparency in the absence or 
delay of a DPP 
• Ensure that the needs to know principle does not hamper access to information for 
authorities (including EC) and civil society.  
2.5 Chemicals  
• Chemicals: chemicals used throughout the value chain, as well as present in the final 
product, should be declared. Although chemical safety is not to be regulated under ESPR, 
existing chemical safety limits have a direct impact on whether a product will be reusable or 
recyclable.  
2.6 Use of standards 
• Should not enable for environmental ambition to be watered down. 
• Common specifications are a great alternative to maintain when standards are not available 
or their establishment is delayed, however it is uncertain what triggers the development of 
such a common specification. 
• Values, limits and classes are not to be part of harmonized standards. 
• When standards are to be used for conformity assessment, the regulation needs to specify 
the procedure to do so very clearly and must guarantee maximum independence of the 
conformity assessment procedure referred to.  
 
3) Provisions to get rid off   
• Industry self-regulation measures – have consistently failed to deliver better than BAU 
• Exemptions for SMEs, especially when it comes to unsold goods 
• LCCC/ assessment methodology without systemic circular economy approach/ appropriate 
functional units, weighting, and forward-looking assessment, e.g. elements from 
consequential LCA?

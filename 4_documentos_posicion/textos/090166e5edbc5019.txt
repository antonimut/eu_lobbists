ROCKWOOL position  
 Revision of the Industrial Emissions Directive   
 
The revision of the Industrial Emissions Directive (IED) comes at a crucial time for the EU’s transformation 
towards a low-carbon economy. ROCKWOOL supports the need to strengthen and improve the IED and 
appreciates the Commission’s proposal particularly regarding energy efficiency and circular economy 
aspects. While we support full transparency towards administrative authorities, we are concerned the high 
level of public reporting proposed will require revealing business sensitive information and therefore a 
balanced approach needs to be found.   
The IED also needs to find a balance between an ambitious approach for emissions limit values while still 
being achievable. In addition, a transparent permitting procedure is needed with a clear governance and 
timeline for consultations that will allow accelerating new production capacity for products like insulation 
that are vital to the EU’s clean energy transition.  
In this context, ROCKWOOL underlines several key considerations for the revision of the IED: 
• Including reasonable permitting timelines and streamlining the governance of the permitting process 
for both environmental, utility and construction permits should be a priority. This would enable a 
rapid scale-up of manufacturing capacity for key technologies and components that are needed to 
meet the EU Green Deal objectives, such as insulation products. Article 25 on access to justice in 
particular should include clear timelines to avoid drawn-out legal procedures that pose a significant risk 
to investment certainty and could postpone needed investments in manufacturing capacity.  
• Rather than using the lowest possible emissions limit values (ELVs) in the BAT-AELs, the IED should 
set narrow limits in BAT documents. The ELVs should be ambitious but achievable and must take into 
account that lowering emissions in one area may lead to a trade-off in another area. Thus, setting the 
lowest possible ELVs across a range of substances may not be possible or realistic.  
• The development of transformation plans by IED installations will help set an ambitious long-term 
sustainability pathway for operators. However, a robust transformation plan would likely include 
information about proprietary manufacturing procedures and innovations. Making the full 
transformation plans and audit results public, as proposed in article 27d, would risk unveiling 
proprietary business information. Instead, a summary of the transformation plan could be shared with 
the public, with high-level targets and measures rather than detailed and potentially proprietary 
information.  
• The implementation of the environmental management system (EMS) in article 14a must be better 
defined to give clarity to industry and protect sensitive information. Requiring installations covered by 
the IED to share all EMS information, including progress on benchmarks and detailed life-cycle 
environmental performance, on a public platform would risk exposing proprietary innovative 
techniques and competition information. Instead of publishing EMS information, the EMS should be 
third party certified. Furthermore, more detail is needed on how benchmarks will be used in the 
context of the EMS.

• Strengthening energy efficiency aspects would further accelerate industrial energy efficiency efforts, 
which can deliver important results in terms of reducing CO2 emissions.  According to a recent study, 
industrial insulation has significant energy efficiency potential, representing approximately 37 Mt of 
annual cost-effective CO₂ savings, or around 4% of total annual CO₂ emissions of the EU industry1. The 
energy efficiency potential in industry stays largely untapped. Therefore, ROCKWOOL welcomes the 
Commission’s proposal to make energy efficiency requirements mandatory via an amendment to article 
9. However, the text should explicitly make the horizontal energy efficiency BREF mandatory for all 
sectors. This would align the IED better with the Energy Efficiency Directive and support a more 
ambitious energy efficiency effort as outlined in the new EU Save Energy Communication
2.  
• The inclusion of circular economy aspects and the formal involvement of ECHA in developing BREFs is 
a welcome addition to the IED. However, circular economy provisions could be emphasised more 
clearly, as they are currently only mentioned in conjunction with industrial transformation plans (article 
27d). The IED has the potential to support circular economy and resource efficiency, bringing it more in 
line with the EU’s Circular Economy Action Plan. ECHA’s role is essential to ensure a full risk assessment 
related to chemicals, in particular substances of concern, in the permits for IED installations. This would 
help reduce the presence of harmful chemicals in emissions from installations, as well as in waste and 
by-products.

*** About ROCKWOOL *** 
 
ROCKWOOL, headquartered in Denmark, is a world leader in stone wool solutions for applications such as building 
insulation, industrial and technical insulation for process industry, wall and facade systems, and noise and vibration control. 
Insulation products play a key role in reducing the CO2 emissions of the built environment and delivering on the EU’s 
building renovation objectives. Stone wool in particular has many advantages ranging from sustainability and circularity to 
fire safety.

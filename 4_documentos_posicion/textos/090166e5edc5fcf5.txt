 
 
 
Eaton recommendations for a rapid phase-out of SF 6 in electrical 
switchgear in Europe 
 
Comments to the Proposal for a Revision of the F-Ga s Regulation (COD 2022/0099) 
7 June 2022  
 
Eaton supports the ambition set-out in the proposal  for a Revision of the F-Gas Regulation (“the 
Proposal”; COM (2022) 150 final). Eaton sets out be low its general comments and suggestions to the 
European Parliament and Council of the European Union on provisions related to sulfur hexafluoride 
(SF 
6) and its use in electrical switchgear.  
As a forward-looking power management company, Eaton is pleased to see that the Commission 
acknowledges in its proposal the need to eliminate the use of SF 6 in electrical switchgear 1. Such 
a prohibition responds to the fact that this F-Gas tops the list of the United Nations’ Intergovernmen tal 
Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) as the most harmful greenhouse gas (GHG) known to date 2. As the 
Commission notes in its impact assessment, even tho ugh SF 6 only accounted for 1% of the EU F-gas 
supply (in tonnes) in 2019, it represented approxim ately 18 % in terms of CO 2 equivalents. This is a 
serious climate issue that can only get worse as SF6 remains in the atmosphere for thousands of years 3.  
Today, SF 6-free technologies that are cost-effective, technic ally feasible, energy-efficient and reliable 
exist for the electrical sector. However, market up take of these solutions is not as fast as could be 
expected or as needed considering our climate emerg ency. SF 6-free solutions are rarely privileged in 
tenders and industry and utilities overall appear t o be unconcerned by an expanding SF 6 market. The 
former F-Gas Regulation has regrettably largely sup ported the status quo  through an approach that 
seeks to contain and manage SF 6 gas instead of actively encouraging SF 6-free alternatives. With this 
proposal for a revised Regulation, we have the oppo rtunity to correct this path and finally eliminate the 
most harmful gas on earth.   
For the purpose of this public consultation, Eaton recommends to: 
•  Clarify Annex IV, point 23 (a) to include switchgea r up to and including 24kV  – 
Decentralised energy generation and the uptake of r enewables and their inclusion in the grid 
will primarily impact the lower voltage ranges, spe cifically up to and including 24kV. This will 
result in significant growth in the market for switchgear, which is already underway and can be 
expected to continue for many years to come. Many manufacturers sell products at 24kV, which 
is both a recognised IEC standard level and a level  used often in specifications and tenders. It 
is therefore critical to change the language in t Annex IV to clarify ‘up to and including 24kV’. 
 
•  Seek an immediate phase out for MV switchgear up to  and including 24kV in Annex IV, 
point 23 (a) – The commercial lifetime of SF 6 switchgear is between 40-60 years which means 
that any new switchgear sold prior to the placing o n the market prohibition (POM) date will 
remain in use well beyond 2050, the climate neutral ity target set in the European Climate law. 
The EU should act decisively to avoid further emiss ions wherever possible. SF 6-free 
alternatives – based on air, natural gas and other technologies – are mature and widely

•  Avoid repeating errors of the past –  The 2014 Regulation did not represent a strong 
regulatory push to phaseout SF 
6 from electrical switchgear in medium voltage appli cations; in 
fact it created a large loophole for business as us ual. Market players had little incentive to 
demonstrate SF 6-free applications were cost-effective, technically feasible, energy-efficient and 
reliable under the test provided in Article 21(4) o f the 2014 Regulation. Looking ahead, while 
the specific mention of these test criteria have be en removed in the Proposal, the 
conditionalities to assess SF 6 alternatives are still present, e.g. technical fea sibility and costs, 
among others. Eaton recommends to clarify these con ditionalities by using a single ‘no other 
suitable alternatives exist’ test, as employed for example in Annex IV (2).  
 
•  Implement a comprehensive tracking and tracing system from production and import of 
SF6 to decommissioning to account for the real emis sions of SF 
6 (Article 5(1))  – Eaton 
welcomes the proposed provisions on leak checks for  electrical switchgear. When it comes to 
F-Gasses in general, and to SF 6 specifically, control of all emissions are critical . Industry 
standards are often cited for leakage rates but these standards reference equipment in use and 
refer to state of the art equipment, which is not representative of most equipment installed and 
in use today. To understand actual leakage rates and thus emissions, it is critical that any track 
and trace system cover the full life cycle of the SF 6, from production through disposal.  
 
•  Tighten the provisions on checks for imports and ex ports of F-Gasses (Articles 12 (8) 
and 23 (10))  – EU’s efforts to control the release of F-Gasses in the EU should not result in 
exporting the problems to other parts of the world,  both in terms of the emissions from 
production and from use of SF 6 switchgear. As such, we call for tighter wording a round trade 
related provisions to ensure that all switchgear be ing imported in the EU market is compliant 
with the POM prohibitions set in Annex IV (23) and that products containing F-Gasses listed in 
Annex I aimed for direct export are not exported if they do not comply with the POM prohibitions 
set out in Annex IV (23). SF 6, whether installed in Europe or elsewhere, will ha ve the same 
impact on our climate. Finally, we strongly support the obligation for importers and exporters to 
be registered in the F-Gas Portal as well as the requirement to present a valid licence to custom 
authorities. 
 
 
Eaton has pioneered the production and commercialis ation of SF 
6-free switchgear. The Green Deal 
vision of the European Commission is clear and the revision of the F-Gas Regulation must be a key 
building block toward the realization of that vision. The Commission’s proposal is a big step in the right 
direction. Our recommendations are aimed to clarify , tighten and secure an easy win for the EU in its 
climate policies. As the expansion of renewable ene rgy generation will grow exponentially, 
decarbonisation of Europe’s grids must be a priorit y and we must not allow SF 6 to accompany this 
expansion. We urge legislators to act now and seek an early and effective phase out of SF 6, the most 
harmful greenhouse gas on earth.

FSC’S FEEDBACK ON THE PROPOSED REGULATION ON ECO-
DESIGN FOR SUSTAINABLE PRODUCTS (ESPR) 
The Forest Stewardship Council (FSC), the pioneer of forest certification with nearly +50 million 
hectares of certified forests in Europe , supports the EU Commission objective of improving the 
overall sustainability and circularity aspects of product s. Below five recommendations aiming at 
further strengthening the proposal:   
  
1. Including “responsible sourcing” as a new eco-design requirement in article 5.1  
Sustainable products start with sustainable raw materials. Responsible sourcing makes sure that 
resources are managed in a way that respects people, animals and the environment. Responsible 
sourcing is therefore the  precondition for an effective sustainable circular econom y because it 
helps eliminating sustainability risks in the supply chain by considering social and environmental 
aspects already at the sourcing level.  Moreover, incentivising products from renewable  and 
responsibly managed sources would facilitate shifting from today’s linear, fossil -based economy 
to a  bio-based circular one . For all these reasons, FSC recommends adding “responsible 
sourcing” among the eco-design requirements in article 5.1.  
  
2. Employing type I and type I like ecola bels in articles 14 and 15 to demonstrate 
compliance with selected eco-design requirements   
Type 1 and type 1 like ecolabels embedding both environmental and social criteria are “third party 
verified” and hence are  ready-made tools to support the proposal implementatio n. In fact, they 
allow EU Member States Competent Authoritie s checking product compliance with  selected 
ecodisegn criteria and assessing environmental footprint by ensuring that the verification process 
is carried out in a consistent, neutral and reliable manner by a party independent from the operator 
being verified, based on international, European or national standards and procedures. This is an 
option already adopted in numerous EU legislation,  for instanc e the EU Ecolabe l Regulation 
(66/2010) and the EU Taxonomy Regulation  (852/2020). As such, FSC suggests amending 
articles 14 and 15 to include type I and type I like ecolabels.

3. Further specifying criteria for employing type I and type I like ecolabels – in line with 
the empowering consumers towards the green transition proposal – and including 
them in article 34(3)  
There are many standards on the market – yet, some are more credible than others. Type I and 
Type I-like ecolabels as defined by ISO 14024 are independently assessed by a third party and 
are therefore the most reliable labels to underpin statements regarding the environmental 
performance of relevant products . FSC thus encourages the EU Commission to set strict 
sustainability criteria for labels – in line with the ISEAL framework - as pre-condition for employing 
employ them as a proxy to  demonstrate compliance with EU product sustainability principles. In 
addition, type I and type I like ecolabels complying with the  “strengthening consumers towards 
the green transition” proposed Directive should be deemed eligible to be included in article 34(3) 
of the proposal. Specific text suggestions are highlighted in the annex below in green.  
  
4. Employing type I and type I like ecolabels to feed the digital product passport   
FSC supports the development of a digital product passport. Operators should be allowed to 
employ type I and type I like ecolabel s, such as those providing high-quality data via blockchain 
and geographic information system (GIS ) technologies, to upload data in the digital product 
passport. This is a cost-effective solution which will allow economic operators not to report twice 
on the same informatio n. Type I and type I like ecolabels have a third -party verification system 
ensuring reliable information from the suppliers.  
  
5. Including responsible sourcing as one of the mandatory Green Public Procurement 
(GPP) criteria as per article 58  
FSC strongly encourages the development of mandatory criteria for GPP, which are instrumental 
for addressing sustainability challenges. To foster sustainability already at the sourcing level, new 
mandatory GPP rules shall include  “responsible sourcing” as one of the  eco-design criteria in 
public supply contracts, public works and/or public services contracts for relevant products as per 
article 58 of the proposa l. Article 58 should also specify that type I and type I like ecolabels 
complying with the  “strengthening consumers towards the green transition” proposed Directiv e 
can be used as a proxy to demonstrate compliance with the GPP criteria.

ANNEX  
  
Article 34(3)  
Products covered by a delegated act adopted pursuant to Article 4, which have been awarded 
the EU Ecolabel pursuant to Regulation (EC) No 66/2010 or other public authority commissioned 
type I or type 1 like ecolabels complying with the EU Directive amending Directives 2005/29/EC 
and 2011/83/EU (Empowering consumers for the green transition)  shall be presumed to comply 
with the ecodesign requirements set out in that delegated act in so far as those requirements are 
covered by the EU Ecolabel criteria established according to Article 16(2) of Regulation (EC) No 
66/2010.   
  
Article 57   
Member State incentives Section 1 Member States incent ives relating to products covered by a 
delegated act adopted pursuant to Article 4 that determines classes of performance in accordance 
with Article 7(4), in relation to a product parameter referred to in Annex I, shall concern the highest 
two classes of performance that are populated at Union level or, where relevant, products with an 
EU Ecolabel or other type I or type 1 like ecolabels, unless otherwise specified in that delegated 
act.

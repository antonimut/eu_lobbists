Tarkett position paper on the European Commission proposal on 
Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation 
 
Tarkett, a global leader in innovative and sustainable solutions for flooring, welcomes the 
European Commission’s proposal on Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation (ESPR), 
aiming at creating an EU-wide Single Market for sustainable products across the European 
Union, while improving transparency through the Digital Product Passport.   
 
Tarkett supports the proposed introduction of a framework aiming at providing horizontal 
rules for products placed on the EU market for two reasons: 
 
• Firstly, we believe that it is essential that ecodesign requirements are tailored 
to the unique characteristics of each product group while targeting the 
overall climate and environmental impacts associated with production and 
consumption patterns.  
• Second, the choice of delegating the implementation to secondary 
legislation will enable the European Commission to conduct proper impact 
assessments on each product category. This is key to design product-specific 
ecodesign requirements. 
 
Tarkett calls on the European policymakers to further improve the proposal and continue 
to foster the  competitiveness of the  EU Single Market. We, indeed, believe that 
harmonisation of requirements at the EU level is a key driver for the correct functioning of 
the EU Sing le Market. We also call on Member States to avoid putting in place national 
measures on sustainable products that may impair the correct implementation of the ESPR. 
 
This position paper highlights priorities we believe are key to the successful development 
of this ambitious policy framework. 
 
Coherence with other legislation 
As a construction materials company, Tarkett’s products are governed by several different 
regulations, including the Constructions Product Regulation (CPR) and the REACH 
Regulation. We urge the Commission and other policymakers to ensure consistency and 
alignment between the ESPR and existing legislation, to avoid duplication of efforts for both 
policymakers and the industry.

An effective Digital Product Passport  
The ESPR introduces, through Delegated Acts, a Digital Product Passports (DPP). Tarkett is 
a long-term supporter of increased transparency. Although DPPs carry a significant number 
of benefits, including providing consumers with clear information and enabling responsible 
actors to reap the benefits, they must be carefully set up to preserve corporate proprietary 
information and intellectual property rights. Asking companies to publicly disclose the 
exact composition of their products will hurt competition, hinder innovation and ultimately 
jeopardise the future of the European design. It is also important to avoid duplicating 
information that is already publicly available.  
 
Tarkett calls on the European Commission to design a DPP on a “need to know basis” 
and carefully select the criteria for information disclosure.  
 
Tarkett recommends that the European Commission takes a technology-neutral approach 
when designing the DPP and considers industry -led voluntary schemes. Tarkett’s Material 
Health Statement (MHS) system provides an efficient example of a third -party verification 
mechanism that protects sensitive information, while allowing consumers to bene fit from 
greater transparency regarding health and safety of materials and their ingredients in the 
intended product use.  
 
We have implemented our MHS system on several products, including our PVC flooring. 
Through this third -party verification system, we can communicate to our consumers the 
product’s chemical composition and associated risk assessment , thus informing on its 
sustainability and safety.  
 
Tarkett would be keen to contribute to the development of the DPP and share its best 
practices with the European Commission.  
 
Supporting reverse logistics business models 
Tarkett believes that the reverse logistics business model, whereby products are collected 
post-use to be reused and recycled, is key to an effective circular economy. We urge the 
 
European Commission to provide mechanisms  such as a circularity scoring system to  
support the reverse logistics business model. Tarkett’s own ReStart® take-back programme, 
which collects post-installation and post-consumer flooring, is a prime example of reverse 
logistics that should be better incentivised. Through a previous partnership with large 
brand owner, the ReStart® programme enabled us  to avoid close to 100 tonnes of CO2 
emissions by recapturing 10,000 m2 of used vinyl flooring and recycling it in new vinyl floor 
for the customer’s stores. 
 
About Tarkett  
With a 140 -year history, Tarkett is a worldwide leader in innovating flooring and sports 
surface solutions, with revenue of 2.8 billion euros in 2021. Offering a wide range of 
products, including vinyl, linoleum, rubber, carpet, parquet and laminates, artificial grass 
and athletic tracks, the Group serves its customers in over 100 countries worldwide. Tarkett 
has more than 12,000 employees and 34 industrial sites, and sells 1.3 million square metres 
of flooring every day for hospitals, schools, housing, hotels, offices or shops and sports 
fields. Committed to changing the game with circular economy and to reducing its carbon 
footprint, the Group has implemented an eco -innovation strategy based on Cradle to 
Cradle® principles, aligned with its Tarkett Human-Conscious Design® approach. Tarkett is

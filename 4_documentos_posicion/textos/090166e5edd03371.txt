POSITION PAPER 
EUROPEN Feedback on the Ecodesign for 
Sustainable Products Regulation proposal  
EUROPEN, the European Organization for Packaging and the Environment, supports the overall objectives of the 
European Commission’s Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation (ESPR) proposal to reduce the negative 
life cycle environmental impacts of products and improve the functioning of the internal market. 
The packaging value chain is constantly innovating to ensure th e uptake of  sustainable packaging solutions, 
supporting the EU climate and circular economy goals . This is done working together with product manufacturers 
to identify new solutions aimed at advancing the sustainability of both the packaging and packaged products, while 
continuing to protect products and ensuring their safe delivery to consumers and businesses.  
 
EUROPEN supports the following principles: EUROPEN would like to stress several aspects 
that require specific attention: 
- The ESPR’s potential to contribute to the EU 
circular economy ; and ensuring effective 
enforcement and market surveillance; 
- The ESPR approach  to ensure a functioning 
internal market (supported by the internal market 
legal basis),  and therefore a  move towards 
harmonised EU requirements, using existing 
European and international  standards and 
industry involvement in the process; 
- The potential benefits of the Digital Product 
Passport to reduce the space needed on 
packaging to provide certain product -related 
information. 
- Possible d uplication of efforts and double 
regulation, in particular of  product-specific 
legislations like the Packaging and Packaging 
Waste Directive (PPWD); 
- Need to protect confidential business data ; need 
for the  data provided via  the Digital Product 
Passport to be tailored to the relevant audience; 
and need to consider the interplay with other 
mandatory information requirements. 
 
 
 
EUROPEN would like to share some more specific comments in relation to certain aspects of the draft Regulation.  
Packaging sustainability should not be addressed in the 
ESPR  
Packaging sustainability should be solely addressed in the Packaging and Packaging Waste Directive 
- The Packaging and Packaging Waste Directive is the EU regulatory framework that determines the conditions 
for placing packaging on the EU market. These include sustainability criteria, which play a key role in enabling 
the uptake of climate neutral and circular packaging solutions in line with the Green Deal and CEAP 2.0 policy

goals. The ongoing re view of the PPWD is focused on translating these goals into concrete regulatory 
measures.  
- The ESPR proposal’s explanatory memorandum acknowledges the existence of  EU product-specific 
legislations, including for packaging . Although EUROPEN supports the poi nt made that “As a matter of 
principle, this Regulation will only apply to products not covered by existing legislation, or when legislation does 
not sufficiently address the sustainability of those products ”, we would like to stress that the Packaging and  
Packaging Waste Directive already addresses the sustainability of packaging . The Ecodesign requirements 
mentioned in Article 1 of the ESPR proposal are already being assessed by DG Environment as part of the 
revision of the PPWD, including: reusability, recycling, waste prevention, recycled content and the presence of 
substances of concern. 
- To avoid policy and/or regulatory inconsistency, ensure compliance with the EU Better Regulation agenda, and 
given the peculiar role of packaging in relation to the packaged product, it is important to exclude packaging 
from the scope of the ESPR: both as a possible product in scope, as well as  any reference to packaging 
sustainability in relation to the products in scope of the ESPR. While the objectives of the ESPR are naturally 
interlinked with those of the PPWD, because of the intrinsic relationship between packaging and the packaged 
product, sustainability measures specific to packaging need to continue to be governed by the PPWD. 
Sustainable product legislation should safeguard packaging functionalities and prevent environmental 
trade-offs  
- It is important to recall packaging’s reasons to exist, which are to  protect products’ integrity and consumers’ 
health and safety, to increase products’ shelf -life, to reduce product losses, to facilitate transport, efficient 
handling and distribution, to promote the packaged product and to provide information and convenience to 
consumers. Any implications of product specific legislation on packaging should be duly assessed to avoid risk 
of undermining packaging functionalities.  
- As packaging plays an important role to prevent product and resource waste, a thorough case -by-case 
assessment of potential trade -offs between product and packaging sustainability is key to avoid or minimise 
overall negative impacts (environmental, economic and social impacts). 
Proposed amendments  
Recital 21 
In light of the above comments, EUROPEN supports the deletion of Recital 21 of the propo sal, which states that 
the ESPR may complement European Parliament and Council Directive 94/62/EC57  “by setting product -based 
requirements focussing on the packaging of specific products when placed on the market ” to , wh ere relevant, 
“contribute in particular to minimising the amount of packaging used, in turn contributing to the prevention of waste 
generation in the Union.” 
Article 5 (1) – Annex I 
Article 5 defines that the European Commission shall establish ecodesign requirements to improve several aspects 
of the products in scope of the Regulation. Annex I of the ESPR details the parameters that “may, as appropriate, 
and where necessary supplemented by others, be used as a basis for improving the product aspects referred to in 
Article 5(1)”. The parameters listed in Annex I include:  
- “(i) weight and volume of the product and its packaging, and the product-to-packaging ratio; 
- (p) amounts of waste generated, including plastic waste and packaging waste and their ease of re -use, and 
amounts of hazardous waste generated;” 
In light of the above comments, EUROPEN supports the deletion from Annex I of these two parameters.

Key considerations on the Digital Product Passport  
- With regard to mandatory information  to be submitted as part of the Digital Product Passport , a tailored 
approach is needed per product category, based on the relevant environmental characteristics of the different 
products addressed.  
- Article 8 (3) of the ESPR proposal foresees that “consumers, economic operators and competent national 
authorities, can access product information relevant to them” via the product passport. EUROPEN would like 
to stress the importan ce to give  particular attention to the rights to access the products’ informatio n. T he 
information made available should be tailored to each audience (to ensure for example that consumers only 
have access to information they can understand and use), and confidential business data should be protected. 
- The Digital Products Passport should also avoid creating any further burden (administrative, financial, etc.) on 
operators entering information in the database . The data requirements must be based on harmonised 
legislation within the EU, and must not duplicate other existing information requirements.  
- A Digital Product Passport  should not apply to packaging but only to the product it contains. The 
dematerialisation of information currently placed on packaging could be a positive aspect of the Digital Product 
Passport, i.e. to reduce the space needed on packaging and for accompanying instructions that provide certain 
product-related information. A Digital Product P assport could for example include information on products’ 
collection and sorting requirements. We welcome more transparent information sharing across the value chain. 
Alignment with existing legislations will however be key to avoid the duplication of mar king requirements next 
to the Digital Product Passport’s data carrier on packaging.

Comments of the VDMA Compressed air technology  
department and the Air-Handling Technology Department for the  
Proposal for a regulation on fluorinated  
greenhouse gases, amending Directive (EU) 2019/1937 and  
repealing Regulation (EU) No 517/2014 (2022/0099 (COD)) 
 
 
Date: 22.06.2022 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Our compressed air industry as well as the Air-Handling industry welcomes the proposal for 
a F-Gas Regulation and supports the European Commission’s climate ambition and promo-
tion of climate-friendly technologies.  
Compressed air technology as enabler technology/cross-sectional technology enables many 
processes and is used above all in many other industrial sectors. The compressed air refrig-
eration dryer extracts the water vapour (and to some extent oil vapour) as contaminant from 
the compressed air. The compressed air is cooled in a heat exchanger system with a closed 
refrigerant cycle.  
The Air-Handling industry represents the manufacturers in the fields of refrigeration and heat 
pump technology, air conditioning and ventilation technology, et.al., also for commercial re-
frigeration. 
 
In the ongoing revision of the F-Gases Regulation we have following concerns regarding the 
current draft of the F-Gas regulation which should be considered: 
- Regarding proposal for categorisation in Annex IV: 
o We as producers of  compressed air refrigerant dryers comply regarding the 
current valid F-Gas regulation 517/2014 with prohibitions regarding “Stationary 
refrigeration equipment” (art. 12 in Annex III of current F -gas regulation). We 
assume that we need to continue to comply with this same category “Stationary 
refrigeration equipment” regarding art 13&14 in Annex IV of the new F-gas pro-
posal under which we see the industrial refrigeration equipment.  
To be certain of this conclusion, we would like clarification on definition of “any 
self-contained refrigeration equipment“ (Annex IV, new No. 12), which could not 
be found in the draft regulation text or elsewhere. 
o We assume that prohibition of No. 12 of Annex IV only concerns "refrigeration 
equipment" that is not already affected by another prohibition in this Annex. 
For example, stationary refrigeration equipment, which may also contain all 
the essential components of a refrigeration cycle, is regulated by prohibitions 
in Nos. 13 and 14 and is therefore not affected by No. 12. Is this a correct un-
derstanding? 
o If further equipment which did not previously fall under this regulation shall be 
tackled by this definition “any self-contained refrigeration equipment“ (new No. 
12) then a proper assessment and justification shall be done what equipment 
would fall under this definition and what consequences would arise. Further-
more, a longer time frame for entry into force regarding the new tackled equip-
ment should be considered.

o We assume that this definition of new No. 12 “any self-contained refrigeration 
equipment“ was introduced because of A9.2 in the Commission staff working 
document impact assessment report part 2 regarding new small hermetic 
commercial refrigeration and heat pump appliances. We therefore propose to 
name the above-mentioned term any commercial or house-hold self-contained 
refrigeration equipment. 
- Furthermore regarding No. 11 Refrigerators and freezers for commercial use (self-
contained equipment) in Annex IV of the new F-gas proposal: 
o We see an issue with retroactively changing regulations which are already in 
force since before 2022. Specifically, Annex IV (11) used to address “hermeti-
cally sealed equipment” in the previous regulation and has now been changed 
to “self-contained refrigeration equipment”. In case the definitions of these de-
scriptions are not identical, the regulation draft would introduce immediate 
changes with no grace period for producers. 
- Unclarities will arise regarding refrigerant blends in Article 5 leak checks of the new F-
gas proposal: 
o In this article 5, respective requirements are connected to two different sub-
stance groups of Annex I and Annex II. What if the substances of a refrigerant 
blends belong to Annex I and Annex II Section 1? It would be good to clarify 
how refrigerants which consist of a blend of substances shall be treated.  
▪ More precise following question arises: At what mass of refrigerant 
blend e.g. R-513A (minimum limit) must leak checks for non-hermetic 
or hermetic equipment be performed in accordance with Art. 5 No. 1? 
▪ For example:  
• R513a consists of HFC-134a and HFC-1234yf. HFC-134a is 
mentioned in Annex I, Section I and HFC-1234yf is mentioned 
in Annex II, Section I. How does these refrigerants comply to 
the respective thresholds regarding the tonnes of CO2 equiva-
lent of fluorinated greenhouse gases listed in Annex I or 1 kilo-
gram or more of fluorinated greenhouse gases listed in Annex 
II, Section I? 
 
We would be delighted if our concerns would be considered.

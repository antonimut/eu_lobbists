Comments on Document „Proposal for a Regulation of the European Parliament and of the Council 
laying down harmonized conditions for the marketing of construction products, amending 
Regulation (EU) 2019/1020 and repealing Regulation (EU) 305/2011” 2022/0094 (COD) 
 
1. In recital no (66) “common deficient practice of accreditation bodies” are mentioned as a reason 
for multiple changes and new requirements in the regulation. These “deficient practice” are in 
no way explained or proven on the basis of objective evidence. The recital thus contains 
unproven allegations and could well be understood as defamation. It is required to be deleted. 
2. Article 6 paragraph 3 empowers the Commission to amend Annex V and to introduce additional 
assessment or verification steps. This provision is understood to be disproportional. The change 
of assessment or verification steps is considered to be such a fundamental part of the CPR that 
changes to them concern the whole system and all market participants. It should be subject to a 
full change of the regulation only. 
3. Article 13 introduces an additional (new) declaration of conformity in addition to the declaration 
of performance. This two-steps approach is considered to be overcomplicated and not 
necessary. A single declaration is considered to be sufficient for all aspects. 
4. Article 28 paragraph 2 and 3 introduces new obligations for 3D-printing service providers that 
should present a certain number of produced sample items to the notified body. However it is 
not clearly defined which assessment procedure should be followed by the notified body. 
5. Article 45 paragraph 1 requires TABs to have at least haft of the staff of the TAB be located in 
the designating Member State. However it is not clear enough what the meaning of “be located” 
is defined exactly. In times of mobile working the exact fulfillment of this requirement cannot be 
guaranteed and not enforced. 
6. Article 45 paragraph 2 introduced “presumption of conformity” for TABs via making reference to 
article 51. However for this concept it is not clear how this should be applied in practice as long 
as the concept of accreditation is not applied for TABs as well. 
7. Article 48 paragraph 2 requires Member States to instruct their national accreditation bodies 
(NAB) to take as a basis for accreditation only the precise legal body applying for accreditation 
and to assess that body against the relevant requirements and tasks laid down in this regulation. 
It is unclear whether this is required for NAB only in case they are doing the assessment and 
monitoring referred to in paragraph 1 of this article or to all NABs (see first sentence of 
paragraph 2). Beside this the wording “take as a basis” of this requirement is unclear whether it 
completely forbids the legal body to take recourse to resources of other (contractual) partners 
or not. Thus the requirement will not contribute to a more harmonized implementation. 
8. Article 49 paragraph 6 empowers the Commission to define minimum staffing resources for 
monitoring notified bodies. It is unclear whether this applies to accreditation bodies only in case 
the national accreditation body (NAB) also has the assessment and monitoring function referred 
to in article 48 paragraph 1 or for all NABs. Beside this the requirement is in contradiction to the 
subsidiary principle as the Commission is not in a position to determine staffing resources in 
practice.

9. Article 50 paragraph 3 requires conformity assessment bodies to “be independent of any and all 
business ties … with other notified bodies and their … parent companies”. This requirement is in 
no way justified and would in practice require to withdraw notification of the majority of 
presently notified bodies. The requirement need to be deleted. 
10. Article 50 paragraph 6 requires the personnel of the notified body that is taking assessment 
decisions has to be employed under the national law of the notifying Member State. This 
requirement will lead to problems in case a notified body as subsidiaries (locations) in other 
(Member) States in which the personnel will normally be working on a contractual basis of the 
local law. For this reason the requirement will lead to a factual (and unnecessary) limitation of 
the options of notified bodies to use (own) locations in other (Member) States. The 
requirements is thus not helpful and will (in addition) not help to guarantee to improve factual 
availability of staff as the requirement could also be fulfilled by staff having contracts with a very 
limited availability. 
11. Article 55 paragraph 2 requires that the “accreditation certificate shall … not take account of the 
capacities or personnel of parent or sister companies”. It is unclear whether this requirement 
also forbids the usage of other resources on a proper contractual basis. In case proper 
contractual requirements are established between the notified body and its contractual partner 
companies (no matter whether they are parent or sister companies or not), the usage of these 
capacities and personnel are fully at the disposal of the notified body as required by article 50 
paragraph 6 subparagraph (a). This is therefore fully in line with the requirements of article 50 
and cannot be prohibited. Any deviating expectation would be in clear contradiction of present 
practice, which did not lead to known problems if established properly. 
12. Article 55 paragraph 2 last sentence takes reference to “the specific requirements and 
assessment tasks”. For clarification it should be added that the requirements of “this regulation” 
are referred to. 
13. Article 60 paragraph 7 requires notified bodies to “ensure rotation between the personnel 
carrying out different assessment tasks”. This requirement is not clear enough to determine 
exact fulfillment by notified bodies. Beside this, SME might not be in a position to introduce 
regular rotation. 
14. Article 62 empowers the Commission to amend article 60 and 61 and to introduce details on 
how notified bodies have to execute their obligations. This provision is understood to be 
disproportional. Laying down details of how the work of the notified bodies has to be executed 
has a very deep impact on all market participants. It should therefore be subject to a full change 
of the regulation only. 
15. Articles 64, 65 and 66 are laying down a “simplified procedure” while the exact assessment 
procedures and the kind of activity they constitute (e.g. “testing”, “certification of products”) 
are unclear. Especially the conditions laid down in article 67 paragraph 1 might be in 
contradiction with the corresponding international standards on conformity assessment 
(ISO/IEC 17025, ISO/IEC 17020 and ISO/IEC 17065). 
16. Article 80 introduces new provisions on training of notified bodies’ staff. It is unclear whether 
these trainings are compulsory for notified bodies or not.

17. Annex V introduces minimum numbers of checks to be undertaken by notified bodies under the 
assessment and verification systems for each of the systems. These fixed numbers (50 – 40 – 30 
– 20) are not appropriate to address the required certainty of spot-checks as the number of 
products and the number produced items could vary significantly. Either a calculation method is 
required or the requirement should be withdrawn from the proposed regulation. 
18. Annex V number 4 introduces a new system called “System 3+”. The description of this system is 
not clear in regard to the question whether it constitutes verification, validation or management 
system certification. Further elaboration of this system 3+ in line with international standards 
and the present state of the art is required.

Comments and observations on the revision of the Industrial 
Emissions Directive 
 
 
BAT-AEL 
Setting emission limit values  complying with the lower limit of the emissions range  
associated with the best available techniques (BAT -AEL), according to art. 15(3), will lead 
to a reduction in flexibility in dialogue with the competent authority and a reduced possibility 
of assessing specific technical conditions in the choice of BAT to be applied. There is a risk 
of not taking into account the overall  balance between abatement and consumption 
in order to obtain it. 
 
It is unclear whether this provision should also apply to the BAT -AELs already developed 
under the current IED, or only to the BAT conclusions that will be drawn up after the entry 
into force of the new IED. It should be clarified that the provision will apply to new 
installations and that it will not lead to changes either to existing BREFs or to 
authorizations already granted. 
 
BAT-AEPL 
Article 15 (3a), provides that the competent  authority also sets binding environmental 
performance levels (BAT-AEPL), referring to the efficient use of resources (e.g. energy and 
water consumption, material consumption, waste production, etc.). 
To date, BAT-AEPLs are not mandatory, but they are used as a reference in the approval 
process. 
 
It should be considered that AEL and AEPL are in parts  mutually exclusive ( enhanced 
abatement can lead to higher energy or water consumption); it is therefore not clear which 
parameter will take precedence when the overall optimal solution is to be found. 
 
In order to consider AEL and AEPL at the same time, the “lowest cross-media effects” 
should therefore be assessed and identified and this should be clearly stated in the 
directive. 
 
Adaptation of the permit - SQA 
The proposal seems to suggest (Art 14(1) and Art 18) that whenever a change in other 
legislation, such as the one on “ Environmental Quality Standards ”, leads towards even 
stricter limits than those possible when implementing BAT, permits are subject to change. 
Given the long investment cycles , it is not feasible for the industrial sector  that abatement 
techniques, that have been installed under a given permit , are rendered outdated after a 
short time. Transition periods, related  to the risks involved, are needed, just as they 
apply when a new BREF is derived. 
 
Environmental Management Systems, emission inventory and Transformation plans 
Article 11 includes the introduction of an environmental  management system (EMS) as an 
operator obligation; Article 14 defines that an environmental management system (EMS) 
must be established as a permit condition, while its requirements are listed in a new Article 
14a.

It should be clarified that  existing env ironmental management systems fulfilling 
internationally accepted standards (e.g. ISO 14001, EMAS) are sufficient to fulfill this 
obligation. 
 
In addition, the requirements of the EMS include an inventory of hazardous substances 
in the plant , a risk assessment of the impact of these substances on human health and 
environment and an analysis of the possibilities of replacing them with safer alternatives. 
The inventory of chemicals - as described by the proposal - is extremely large; it should be 
clarified that this inventory should focus on the substances that are "relevant" for the plant 
and which potentially lead to "significant" emissions. In the specific Italian case, Legislative 
Decree 102/2020 has inserted paragraph 7bis in art. 271 in Part Five of Legislative Decree 
152/2006, which introduces the obligation for the operator of the plants , from which 
emissions originate from substances of particular danger used in the production cycles, to 
send to the competent Authority  a report, which analyzes the availability of alternatives, 
considers the risks and examines the technical and economic feasibility of their replacement. 
Amendments to the directive should be proposed on the basis of approaches already 
used at national level , providi ng that the inventory of hazardous substances (and 
their possibility of substitution) refers only to substances/mixtures classified as 
carcinogenic or toxic for reproduction or mutagenic (H340, H350, H360) and 
substances of very high concern (SVHC) pursuan t to Regulation (EC) No. 1907/2006 
(REACH). 
 
Regarding the transformation plan , this should not be necessary for every single 
ainstallation as the transformation is not focused on single units but on a site , or on the 
company in general; the plan should also focus on relevant information and should not 
contain confidential business information. 
 
Since the transformation plan becomes part of the permit, which is publicly available, only 
an extract or an overview of the transformation plan should be included in the EMS (also for 
CBI reasons). In general, it should be clarified who the stakeholders will be and what 
information from the EMS should be made public: it should be sufficient to 
demonstrate that an EMS has been adopted, by publishing a corresponding 
certificate/attestation/declaration that shows the existence of the EMS. 
 
It is suggested to delete the EMS requirement from Article 14 as a permit requirement, since 
an EMS as an operator obligation in Article 11 is entirely sufficient. 
 
Human health protection 
The revision proposal provides for the introduction of the concept of “protection of human 
health”. So far, key environmental issues, which means key aspects and substances to be 
considered and for the abatement of which BATs and AELs were derived , were selected 
based on the impact on the environment , that is, in case of significant emissions, whether 
the involved sector contributes a relevant share of overall emissions and there is a good 
chance of a further and substantial reduction of such emissions. The aspe ct of ‘human 
health’ opens a completely different avenue as now aspects like ‘toxicity’ and other impacts 
on human health are to be considered, unrelated to the question whether the su bstance at 
issue is even released into the environment. It risks a duplication of REACH or 
Occupational Exposure regulations. Furthermore, a s there is a large number of 
substances identified as “of concern”, this will significantly prolong the BREF making 
process.

ECHA involvement 
In art. 13, relating to BAT reference documents and information exchange, the involvement 
of ECHA (European Chemicals Agency) has been added. It should be clarified what its role 
will be and in what contexts it will operate. 
 
Compensation 
The Commission proposes to allow NGOs to bring collective actions for compensation for 
health damage before national courts in case of breach of IED permit against operators and 
competent authorities, based on a reversed burden of proof.  
 
In principle, the provision for  collective action for health damage caused by emissions in 
breach of EU law  is acceptable, but the regulatory approach elected by the Commission  
does not appear adequate . The proposal does not include the  essential safeguards and 
rules to ensure a balanced and predictable regime for the resolution of collective claims by 
civil law courts in the EU (e.g. criteria for qualified entities, procedure, evidence, information 
duties). In particular, mechanisms and  rules to avoid abusive litigation should be 
established, similar to the directive on collective redress and consistent with the principles 
set out in the Commission Recommendation of 11 June 2013 (2013/396/EU). 
Importantly, the burden of proof should remain with the claimant, subject to the freedom 
of the national judge to reduce or reverse it on a case-by-case basis, in accordance with the 
national rules of evidence and civil procedure. This is an important safeguard against 
abusive litigation. Especially in cases of health damage allegedly caused by air pollution, if 
the burden of proof is reversed, operators would have to prove a negative (i.e. that the 
emissions did not cause the damage), and thus would have to demonstrate beyond 
reasonable doubt that other causes for the health damage are present. As a practical matter, 
given that many diseases are correlated with a large number of putative causal factors, 
providing such proof would be impossible. It would also require that the operator has access 
to sensitive personal information on the claimants. 
 
Amendment of Annex I - Scope 
Annex I proposes the modification of point 3.5, relating to the “manufacture of ceramic 
products by firing, in particular roofing tiles, bricks, refractory bricks, tiles, stoneware 
or porcelain with a production capacity exceeding 75 tonnes per day and/or a kiln 
capacity exceeding 4 m3 and a setting density per kiln exceeding 300 kg/m3”. 
The current directive provides that each Member State can decide whether to apply both 
criteria (production capacity and kiln capacity) or only one of them. 
In the proposal, with a view to ensuring a more consistent implementation across the Union, 
the criterion "and/or" has been replaced by a pure “or”, therefore such installations will 
always be included within the scope of the IED whenever any one of th ose two criteria is 
met. 
The purpose of the IED is to legislate on large industrial plants and on their consequent 
significant emissions; this modification would imply that even the smallest plants, such as 
those for the production of inorganic bonded abrasives , could fall within the field of 
application of the IED. The impact of the new definition would be huge for these installations, 
mostly belonging to small to medium-sized companies, and with a very low environmental 
impact. 
 
In particular, in Italy, coated abrasives (cloths, papers and fibers) and bonded abrasives 
(inorganic or organic bonded abrasives) represent the two main areas of the Italian industrial 
production of traditional abrasives. The offer is made up in Italy by some large multinationals 
and by many important national companies.

Federchimica’s gruppo Abrasivi has about 25 companies, mainly concentrated in Piemonte, 
Lombardia and Veneto, for a total of about 1500 employees and with an estimated turnover 
of over 350M €, representing approximately 80% of the Italian market. 
The Italian industry is among the first in Europe in this sector along with Great Britain and 
France, immediately behind Germany. 
On the basis of the current formulation, throughout the national territory the 
production plants of vitrified inorganic bonded abrasives have been excluded from 
the scope of the IED permit  regulations. This exclusion is based on the  fact that the 
production of this particular type of abrasives is characterized by medium and small size 
plants, with completely negligible emissions compared to the large plants that the IED 
legislation regulates. 
 
In fact, compared to large plants, the CO2 emission from the production of vitrified inorganic 
bonded abrasives is negligible: for example in Germany (the largest European producer) the 
total sum of CO2 emissions of all the productions of this type of abrasives is approximately 
9,000 tons/year, equal to 0.002% of the total CO2 quotas. 
To give a comparison parameter between the plants currently subject to IED permit and the 
“typical” company in the Abrasives sector, it should be noted that the maximum daily 
production of an Itali an producer of vitrified inorganic bonded abrasives  is less than 7 
tons/day, that is more than 10 times lower than the threshold value of 75 tons/day of 
production capacity. 
In order to ensure a more coherent implementation of this Directive and a level playing field 
throughout the Union and to guarantee that those (large) industrial installations for which 
the IED is designed are covered but not very small installations, the definition of installations 
for the man ufacture of ceramic products by firing should be amended deleting completely 
the second criteria, leaving only the one about the daily production capacity. This would 
fulfill the Commission's wishes while preventing the smallest installations from falling under 
the IED regime. 
Alternatively, it would be advisable not to change the scope  of the Directive, leaving 
the Member States, on the basis of the characteristics of the national industrial sector, to 
decide whether to apply both the criteria, production capacity and kiln capacity, or just one 
of them. 
 
Replacement of Annex II 
It is proposed to replace the list of substances in Annex II of the IED directive with the list 
established in Annex II of the E -PRTR register. In this way, the scope will be significantly 
expanded. This will prolong not only Kick Off meeting and the subsequent collection, 
validating (by Member States) and evaluation (by the JRC) of data, but the BREF process 
as a whole.

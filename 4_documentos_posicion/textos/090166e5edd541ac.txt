 
General Information 
H&M Group’s view on EU Commission’s proposal for 
Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation 
June 2022  
 
H&M Group welcomes the European Commission’s proposal for a Regulation on Ecodesign for Sustainable 
Products and the development of product specific requirements. In our view, this will provide the needed 
granularity to drive change towards more circular, durable, and recyclable products and as such is a major 
milestone in the EU’s transition towards a circular economy. 
 
 
About us 
 
H&M Group is on a journey towards circular fashion. To enable our own teams to create products fit for a 
circular economy, H&M Group developed an innovative design tool, The Circulator, aligned with the Ellen 
MacArthur Foundation’s vision for a circular economy for fashion. The Circulator is a tool that enables our 
teams to create products that are fit for a circular system. It optimises the use of resources and balances 
existing trade-offs, by taking the purpose of products into account to increase their circular potential when 
selecting materials, processes and design strategies.  We have set ourselves the ambition to have all our 
products designed for circularity using the Circulator by 2025. This means products which are made to last, 
come from recycled and more sustainably sourced materials and that can be recirculated (e.g., be reused 
and recycled) multiple times. With the Circulator, H&M Group also aims to raise awareness both within and 
outside our organisation about the complexity of design decisions and provide innovative solutions.  
 
As part of our work on circular products, we also have set ourselves a bold target on our material choices. 
By 2030, we aim for 100% of our materials to be either recycled or sourced in a more sustainable way, 
including delivering 30% recycled content in materials used by 2025.  
 
In addition, we prioritise prolonging the life of garments by keeping pre -loved garments in use for as long 
as possible through investing in scaling new circular business models.  We are currently selling pre-loved 
products on h&m.com in Sweden and we are expanding to more markets within this year. 
 
 
Our views on the EU Commission proposal 
 
After an initial review of the Commission’s proposa l, H&M Group  key reflections on the draft ESPR 
published in March 2022 are as follows:   
 
1. H&M Group welcomes that ecodesign requirements will be set per product group.  
2. ‘Design for purpose’ should be integrated as a fundamental and overarching principle in the 
EU ecodesign and shall be carefully considered when setting ecodesign requirements. 
3. Substances of Concern , including substances that hamper recycling,  shall be restricted under the 
REACH Regulation. 
4. Policy coherence with relevant other EU initiatives , notably  under the EU Green Deal , shall be 
guaranteed to ensure a well-functioning policy framework for more sustainable products. 
5. The Digital Product Passport shall foster a true shift to circular economy and appropriate transition 
periods shall be foreseen to allow the industry to set up the needed infrastructures and collect and 
process the requested data. 
6. It is crucial to ensure that the whole value chain for circularity is directly represented in the Ecodesign 
Forum, including technical experts from the industry.  
7. Unnecessary administrative burden on Declarations of conformity and markings  for apparel and 
footwear shall be minimised. 
8. Ecodesign requirements should be clear , measurable and enforceable by market surveillance 
authorities, who shall be fully equipped to ensure a robust enforcement of ecodesign rules.  
9. H&M Group welcomes the transparency obligations on the destruction of unsold goods but regrets that 
recycling is considered as destruction.

This paper will elaborate further on these priority points: 
 
1. Ecodesign requirements shall be set per product group 
H&M Group welcomes that ecodesign requirements will be set per product group . This will ensure 
the right level of granularity required to drive the change towards sustainable products. We welcome that, 
as defined in Art. 2, product groups should only include products that serve similar purposes.  We also call 
for the Commission to consider the context of use (for instance: formal wear, casual wear, workwear, etc.) 
when adopting ecodesign requirements for textile products, in order to optimise resource use and balance 
trade-offs that exist today between product aspects like durability and recyclability . Horizontal criteria 
should be strictly used only when technical similarities are clearly proven. 
 
2. Design for Purpose 
The ‘design for purpose’ principle should be integrated as a fundamental and overarching principle in the 
EU ecodesign, supporting companies to implement circular design.  When looking at product design  we 
know that product parameters such as durability and recyclability sometimes come with trade-offs. One of 
our key learnings in the development of the Circulator is that the product’s purpose (i.e. how it will be used, 
for how long and how often) is a crucial element in circular design: it enables us to decide what to prioritise, 
whether it is durability, repairability or recyclability based on the expected product use.  
 
Design choices must be better recognised in line with durability requirements, but also future recycling 
technology developments. Design for durability requires much more resources (materials, CO2 emissions), 
which also brings additional environmental impact s. Designing for purpose would allow to optimise 
resource use in a resource constrained world , as not all products require the same resource intensity to 
function according to their intended purpose. 
 
Hence, to optimise resource use, it is fundamental to design products for circularity by taking their purpose 
into account early on in the design and development process. The intended product purpose will influence 
the choice of materials, processes and the design strategies. Designing for intended product purpose will 
be influenced by how long and how often the product will be used, how much wear and tear it is exposed 
to and depending on the product access models (rental, resell or others).  
 
H&M Group  recommends strengthening the principle of ‘design for purpose’ in Art. 5 of the 
proposed Regulation, by requiring the Commission to take it into account when drafting ecodesign 
requirements for the various product groups.   
 
3. Substances of Concern shall be restricted under REACH and be coherent with the Safe and 
Sustainable by Design methodology 
The ESPR should drive companies to make safe chemical and material choices compatible with the circular 
economy, enabling recirculation of materials without creating hazardous legacies. Tackling the presence of 
chemical legacy and hazardous chemicals in recycled materials will be a key part of realising circularity. In 
line with the EU Chemical Strategy for Sustainability, H&M Group has a vision of working towards toxic -
free fashion by 2030.  
 
H&M Group agrees in principle with restricting  hazardous chemicals, as well as any chemicals 
hampering recycling and material recovery. Nonetheless, Substances of Concern, as noted under Art. 2 
of the ESPR, should be defined  under the REACH Regulation  and not in the ESPR . This would enable  
REACH to stay the only prevailing chemicals legislation in the EU . Furthermore, it is crucial that such 
definition is aligned with the Safe and Sustainable by Design methodology  that the EU is currently 
developing.

4. Policy coherence to ensure a well -functioning policy framework for more sustainable 
products 
The ESPR is providing a fantastic opportunity to drive systemic change, should the coherence with  many 
other initiatives under the EU Green Deal be guaranteed:  
• EU common definitions on circularity principles should be applied in product related initiatives: 
Ecodesign criteria , Product Environmental Footprint methodology , Extended Producer 
Responsibility (EPR) eco-modulation definitions, Safe and Sustainable by Design methodology, the 
Taxonomy technical screening criteria  and the revision of the Packaging and Packaging Waste 
Directive;  
• One clear and consolidated framework addressing customer information, with the Green Claims  
initiative, the Empowering consumers  proposal and the revision of the Textile Labelling 
Regulation;  
• Alignment with legislation on end-of-life, in addition to EPR schemes and the packaging legislation: 
end-of-waste criteria and waste shipment rules; 
• Coherence with other chemicals legislation s, such as the revision of REACH and the Safe and 
Sustainable by Design methodology. 
Policy coherence will be crucial to ensure a well-functioning policy framework for more sustainable 
products. We believe it is a fundamental responsibility of the European institutions to guarantee 
that these policies are complementary and function in harmony. 
 
5. Digital Product Passport 
H&M Group is a key advocate of transparency and traceability. We believe that transparency is key to 
driving sustainable change across the industry as it creates both comparability and accountability and this 
will ultimately lead to positive change. In this spirit, we believe the Digital Product Passport constitutes a 
key enabler of a circular economy.  
 
Furthermore, we welcome the possibility of having digital labels and providing information digitally. 
Optimally we should try hav ing all product related information provided through digital means. 
Considering the technical challenges and the resources needed for the implementation of the Digital 
Product Passport, we recommend foreseeing appropriate transition periods to allow the industry to 
set up the needed infrastructures and collect and process the requested data . The relevance and 
reliability of the information i ncluded in the passport should be a corner stone of the digital passport 
approach to ensure that the right information reaches the right audience.  
 
6. Engagement with the industry and broader value chain 
H&M Group  looks forward to further engaging with policy makers and the broader value chain on the 
development of ecodesign criteria. We are convinced that it is  only by involving the appropriate set of 
stakeholders when product requirements and implementing measures are developed that will guarantee 
the creation of impactful policies. To ensure that new requirements are fit for purpose, any proposals for 
new requirements should be preceded by detailed impact assessments. It is crucial to ensure that the 
whole value chain for circularity is represented in the Ecodesign Forum  (Art. 17). This includes 
brands and manufacturers as well as re -circulators of products and materials (i.e., repair, resell, rental, 
recycling, sorting, etc) as their expertise will be key to the policy making process.  
 
The involvement of relevant stakeholders, including technical experts from the industry (from large to 
small companies) will be fundamental to ensure that the requirements developed will be relevant, realistic 
and will drive impact across the entire industry . We encourage the Commission to provide clarity as 
soon as possible on the composition and the governance of the Ecodesign Forum.

7. Declaration of Conformity (DoC) and CE marking 
Articles 36 to 39 on conformity assessment, declaration of conformity and CE marking set very burdensome 
administrative requirements  with little impact for apparel and footwear products’ environmental 
performance. H&M Group welcomes the possibility foreseen in Art. 40  to adopt alternative rules for 
conformity declarations and markings  and we strongly encourage the Commission to make use of this 
possibility when setting ecodesign criteria for apparel and footwear products. Administrative burden on 
Declarations of conformity and markings for apparel and footwear shall be minimised. 
 
8. Guarantee robust market enforcement 
To ensure a true level playing field, a robust market enforcement of the ecodesign requirements is needed. 
We understand that this might place further administrative burden on  market operators and will have 
implications across the supply chain,  which is why enforcement measures will need to be adequately 
assessed by the European authorities . However, p roducts that do not comply with the minimum level of 
environmental performance set in ESPR delegated acts should not have access to the EU single market. 
 
Ecodesign requirements should be clear and need to be measurable and enforceable  by market 
surveillance authorities to monitor implementation and ensure a level playing field. Furthermore, Member 
States surveillance authorities shall be fully equipped  and have increased capacity  to ensure the 
proper enforcement of ecodesign requirements.  
 
9. Destruction of unsold goods 
H&M Group welcomes the transparency obligations on companies on the destruction of unsold 
goods. Incineration of goods should only be limited to those few garments not suitable for reuse or 
recycling, e.g. , due to health and safety risks. Textiles should never be sent to landfill. Circular business 
models will give a second life (and many more) to these unsold sustainable goods through reuse, remake, 
refurbishment, etc.  
 
Nonetheless, H&M Group regrets that recycling is considered as destruction. When there is still recoverable 
value in an unsold product, recovering the value through recycling should always be considered from an 
environmental impact point of view. Recycling should therefore not be considered as destruction in 
Art. 20 of the ESPR, as long as other uses or destinations have been e xplored and recycling is the 
only available end of life solution for said product.

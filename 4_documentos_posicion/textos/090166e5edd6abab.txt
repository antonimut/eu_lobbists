 
Corbion’s input to the EU Commission proposal for a Regulation 
establishing a framework for setting Ecodesign requirements for 
sustainable products  
 
Corbion welcomes the Commission’s initiatives to address unnecessary adverse environmental 
impacts of products placed on the EU market, while harmonising industry requirements . The 
objectives set in the COM proposal for the Ecodesign for sustainable products Regulation (ESPR) are 
core to Corbion’s priorities of sustainability, circularity, and resource efficiency. For the EU to become 
carbon neutral by 2050, as outlined in the EU Green Deal objectives, a balanced and science -based 
Regulation will need to consider the important role played by bio -based, bi odegradable, and 
compostable plastics in decarbonizing the EU economy and in improving end-of-life options for many 
products placed on the EU market.  
 
Corbion believes the following points need further consideration:  
 
Evaluating products’ sustainability through the use of PEF and LCA methods  
 
With the ESPR, PEF is put forward as  the preferred method for evaluating the environmental 
performance of products. The definition of “environmental footprint”, as outlined in the Commission 
proposal, is based on the Product Environmental Footprint method – “the life cycle assessment 
method to quantify the environmental impacts of products established by Recommendation (EU) 
2021/2279”. The challenge with the current PEF methodology is that the key advantage of bio-based 
products, which is to remove carbon from the atmosphere and sequester it into products  so it does 
not negatively contribute to climate change, is not taken into account. Biogenic carbon uptake should 
be accounted for in the assessment of PEF for bio-based plastics.  
 
In Corbion’s view, the current PEF methodology is not incenti vising the use of biogenic carbon. 
Introducing CO2 removals credits to biomass when it is produced, and CO2 penalty to all released CO2 
back into the atmosphere (both for biobased and fossil based)  could be a solution . This procedure 
would be aligned with the EN 16760 standard “Bio-based products – Life Cycle Assessment”. Next to 
that, Corbion considers that with regards to end-of-life options, all recycling options, including organic 
recycling need to be treated equally and must correctly represent existing and potential future waste 
infrastructure. The LCA method should be performed on waste stream levels rather than on product 
level to capture the real value of industrial composting in comparison to other EOL options.  
 
To avoid unfair comparison between products, Corbion calls on the Commission to include pertinent 
existing standards for comparing data in a transparent and meaningful way. W e suggest to use the 
standard EN 16760 Bio -based products – Life cycle assessm ent for the calculation of the GHG 
emissions. 
 
Digital Product Passport (DPP) 
 
Corbion agrees that a digital product passport could enhance transparency along the supply chain and 
easily inform consumers on the product composition and characteristics, in an environmentally 
friendly manner. This could indeed pave the way for stimulating demand for sustainable products on 
the EU market. Nevertheless, the issue around Intellectual Property (IP) is a matter of concern. 
Corbion supports information at product level when it is relevant and possible. At the same time, the

information provided must respect confidentiality related to trade secrets and secure IP protection 
for companies.  
 
Corbion proposes to involve industry at all stages of the development of the digital products passport, 
and to ensure the burden put on companies is proportionate. At the same time, as there is no one size 
fits all solution, we suggest that the requirements for the DPP are established on a sector- by-sector 
and on a product-by-product basis, taking into account the differences in products and in the relevant 
information to be disclosed.  
 
In addition, Corbion recommends to design DPP in a way that promotes the principles of the circular 
economy, without hindering innovation by the requirements. In order to promote innovative products 
placed on the EU market, we disagree with the EC proposal to include in the DPP the following:  
 
- List of materials and substances present in the product  
- Quantities of materials and substances present in the product  
- Information on the origin of product components  
- Recycled content of each material present in the product 
 
Recycling  
 
There is a strong focus on recyclability among the ecodesign  requirements which will be further 
elaborated through Delegated Acts by the Commission. In this regard, Corbion is calling on the EC to 
consider bio -based and compostable plastics as equal to the recycled content. Bio -based, 
biodegradable and compostable plastics' production is following the same reasoning as the 
Commission’s ambition with regards to ecodesign for products.  
 
Certified biodegradable plastic products can be industrially and home composted, digested or 
biodegraded on agricultural land. Indus trial composting which is considered recycling is a cost -
effective way of processing municipal organics kitchen waste and garden waste. Therefore, 
compostable bags for instance help in avoiding incineration. 
 
PLA bioplastics is also chemically and mechanic ally recyclable. Bio -based plastics, as opposed to 
conventional plastics, have the advantage of closing the carbon loop and uses nature as its primary 
source for production. Therefore, bio-based, biodegradable, and compostable plastics do not increase 
the complexity of the recycling process they should be given equal status as recycled content in the 
ESPR.    
 
Sustainable non-fossil sources 
 
Corbion believes that there should be a minimum target set for the use of carbon from sustainable 
non-fossil sources in chemical and plastic products . This percentage should be at least 20%, but it is 
Corbion’s belief that this could and should be higher than 20% in 2030. Polylactic Acid ( PLA), a 
biobased, recyclable and biodegradable polymer made from annually renewable resources, would  be 
a great source in reaching this target.

EuRIC Position Paper on EU COM proposal for a Regulation on 
substances that deplete the ozone layer  
23 June 2022  
 
The European Recycling Industries’ Confederation (EuRIC) strongly welcomes the proposal -
published by the EU Commission on April 8, 2022 - for a Regulation on substances that 
deplete the ozone layer. Ozone depleting substances (ODS) have depleted throughout the 
years the stratospheric ozone layer which has led to an increased solar UV-B radiation at the 
surface of the Earth (Norval et. all, 2011). This has had as a consequence a huge negative 
impact on the environment and human health and hence reducing emissions is crucial – now 
more than ever.  
 
EuRIC is the umbrella organisation that encompasses a network of European Member State 
and National recycling associations. It is the link between the recycling industry and the 
European Union, acting as the platform for co-operation and the exchange of best practices 
across the industry. EuRIC represents key companies included in the collection, processing, 
recycling, transport, and trade of various recyclables (metals, paper, plastics, tyres, 
construction & demolition waste from household or industrial waste, WEEE, ELV, Packaging, 
etc.) across Europe. 
 
In EuRIC’s view, although the aforementioned proposal is seen as a huge step forward to 
reducing greenhouse gas emission (GHG) and therefore reaching the ambitious but necessary 
targets of the EU Green Deal, it is still unclear in terms of which installations are the most 
suitable to treat certain materials - used in the construction sector - at the end of their useful 
life. More precisely, in Paragraph 2 of Article 20, the proposal states that: 
 
‘…Building owners and contractors shall ensure that during renovation, refurbishing or 
demolition activities implying the removal of metal -faced panels that contain foams 
with ozone depleting substances  listed in Annex I are avoided to the extent possible by 
recovery for reuse or destruction of the foams and the substa nces contained 
therein …’. 
 
In regards to the above paragraph, EuRIC would like to draw attention to the Impact 
Assessment (IA) accompanying the proposal which explicitly states that, ‘refurbished recycling 
facilities that have so far been treating old refrigeration equipment can treat metal-faced 
panels.’. Although EuRIC finds the above statement to be correct, it  would like to reiterate 
the importance of the constrains pointed out in point D of Annex 61. These include: 
 
➢ For metal-faced panels, the capacity of nearby recycling facilities may be too limited 
to process all metal-faced panels. For example, the actual annual treatment capacity 
of the facilities treating old refrigeration equipment is limited to the volume of 
fridges manufactured in the EU.

➢ National capacity of waste recycling plants is not evenly distributed across Member

➢ Metal faced panel may be produced with different material than the fridge (rubber,

It should also be stated that today, the recycling facilities referred above are equipped with 
machinery that only allows them to treat large quantities of WEEE, and in particular of 
fridges, and not of other waste flows. This is strongly linked and connected with the inability 
of those facilities to process expanded polystyrene (XPS)  – a material that is not used at all in 
the example of products provided above. Besides XPS, some metal-faced panels also contain 
Polyisocyanurate foam (PIR)  and Phenolic foam (PF), materials on which research is today 
very limited. EuRIC, therefore, sees the need of more research on these foams, especially 
considering the limited knowledge in terms of how they act during the degassing process.   
 
Furthermore, EuRIC - from inputs gathered from its member recycling federations and 
companies – would like to stress that there is no currently available treatment capacity on 
the market to treat  large quantities of metal-faced panels and laminated boards reaching 
end -of life. Facilities that are able to treat very low volumes of metal-faced panels are forced 
to separate manually the metal components from the foam, thing that would be extremely 
time consuming and not feasible today from an economic and technical standpoint, for large 
volumes.  It is noteworthy to mention, that once the foam is separated from the metal 
components, then the recycling facility would need to cut/pre-treat the panels into smaller 
pieces for further processing.  
  
In addition to what mentioned above, EuRIC would also like to also draw attention to the EU 
Waste Framework Directive and more precisely to the EU List of Waste  which provides a 
common terminology for classifying waste across the EU. This helps manage waste, as well as 
national authorities, local authorities and businesses deal with permitting related issues. In 
that regard, it is of an utmost importance to highlight that, facilities where metal-faced 
panels are supposed to be treated – according to the EU COM proposal - are mainly 
authorised to treat waste which are under waste codes 16 02 11*(discarded equipment 
containing chlorofluorocarbons HCFC, HFC) and 20 01 23* (discarded equipment containing 
chlorofluorocarbons). Thus, as the waste stream in question is under a different waste code 
from the ones referred above  (chapter 17 06, insulation materials and asbestos-containing 
construction materials ), recycling facilities will have to apply for an additional permit which 
may take a long period of time before granted by the local competent authority .  
 
Besides the lengthy process of applying for a permit, EuRIC would also like to express its 
concerns on the lack of Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) schemes for metal-faced 
panels and laminated boards. EPR is a mechanism where producers are responsible for the 
recovery and recycling of products that they place on the market after they have been used. 
Typically, this is through providing financial support to facilitate the collection, sorting and 
recycling. Some Producer Responsibility Organisations (PROs) may also get operationally 
involved, in particular in the sorting and recycling elements of the supply chain. Therefore,  
given  the lack of necessary funding to ensure collection and recycling takes place, it can be 
deduced that sufficient time is needed before entry into force to avoid fully placing the cost

of collection, sorting and recycling of metal-faced panels on building owners, contractors and

The European Recycling Industries’ Confederation therefore calls for:  
 
❖ Placing the burden of extracting/treating the foam included in metal-faced panels on actors 
who possess the technology which can efficiently process the materials in question. 
 
❖ Additional time before implementation: This will allow recycling plants to scale up and 
optimise recycling capacity which will in turn increase recycling rates of the products in 
question. 
 
 
❖ A level playing field: For a successful implementation of this Regulation, facilities in all 
Member States must abide by the same rules. Should that not be the case, EuRIC fears that it 
will results in some Member States benefiting more than others, and waste flows transiting 
from one country to another. 
 
❖ Ensuring that materials are treated in authorised treatment facilities: EuRIC is of the strong 
opinion that building materials containing ozone depleting substances should only be treated 
by authorised treatment facilities. This will help ensure that these materials are treated in 
accordance with European law and do not end up in undocumented streams or illegal 
landfills. 
 
❖ More in  depths analysis: for a strong legislative framework a proper analysis on the different 
options and alternatives must be conducted. This is strongly linked and connected with the 
impact assessment of the proposed Regulation which fails to identify the significant barriers -
faced today - related to the treatment of metal-faced panels and laminated boards.

Position of PGE Polska Grup a Energetyczna S.A. on the review of the Directive of the 
European Parliament and of the Council amending Directive 2010/75/EU of the European 
Parliament and of the Council of 24 November 2010 on industrial emissions (integrated 
pollution prevention and control) and Council Directive 1999/31/EC of 26 April 1999 on the 
landfill of waste  
 
Key messages 
 The guiding principle for the revision of the IED should be to maximise environmental 
and human health benefits, while minimising the investment and administrat ive 
burden on the sector. 
 The existing solutions based on achieving emission limits falling within the ranges of 
BAT-AELs should be retained, as they have enabled considerable and cost-effective 
success in the area of emission reductions adjusted to the technical  specificity of 
installations. In our opinion, the intention of the European Commission was to 
propose a change in the approach to determining  permissible emission levels - 
however, in the current wording of the proposal, the provisions suggest that the only 
permissible value is the lowest of the range. Such an approach would be wrong from 
the point of view of the ne cessity to link the emission value to the best available 
technique applicable in a given installation.  
 Radical solutions that would force operators to incur large capital expenditures with 
little environmental benefits should be avoided. In particular, the rules should not be 
changed with respect to installations that are already operating, as meeting the 
strictest emission standards sometimes requires far -reaching structural changes. In 
addition, the Commission's predictions regarding the financial consequences of the 
proposed changes, for both operators of installations and Member States, should be 
regarded as grossly underestimated.  
 It is necessary to clarify the manner in which the achievable emission level for a given 
installation and the means to be used by the operator for that purpose would be 
determined.  Risks related to imprecise provisions will cause doubts among 
authorities and an increased number of appeals to courts against decisio ns granting 
integrated permits. We propose that the operator should  indicate which emission 
level is achievable and optimal - taking into account the specificity of a given 
installation. Therefore, the provision requiring the setting of the strictest emission 
limit values consistent with the lowest emissions achievable through the application 
of BAT in the installa tion (Article 15(3), first sentence, of the proposed amendment 
to the Directive) should be changed  to better reflect the specifics of particular

Full version of the position 
The draft amendments to the IED presented by the European Commission create risks for 
business activities in the field of electricity and heat generation. They result from several 
different modifications  proposed in the existing Directive. These are mainly due to the 
tightening of the requirements that are presumably feasible to meet, however, this will entail 
an increase in the already considerable expenses on ensuring compliance with the existing 
rules determining the permissible standards of industrial emissions. 
1. Environmental performance levels (Article 3, Article 11, Article 14, Article 15) 
The draft directive introduces an additional element of BAT conclusions and permits, i.e. 
environmental performance levels. 
According to the definition contained in the draft revision to the directive, environmental 
performance levels  means range of environmental performance  levels except emission 
levels. This way of defining a concept used in a legal act is a circular definition and does not 
allow for a precise understanding of the Commission’s intentions. The way of formulating the 
definition of this new element of BAT conclusions, which is to be reflected in integrated 
permits, is incorrect.

 significant risk of the need to review the existing permits after the entry into force of the 
revised IED; 
 possible need to amend or supplement the current BAT conclusions with environmental 
performance levels; 
 additional costs for installation operators; 
 similar effect will be generated by the introduction, in Article 11, of additional elements

use of raw materials in an efficient manner; 
 introduction of an additional area of requirements - as of today, the scope of which is 
difficult to estimate, which makes it harder to prepare applications for issuing or changing 
integrated permits and the risk of additional consultations with stakeholders. 
2. Incidents and accidents (Article 7) 
This provision regulating the principles of dealing with incidents and accidents, introduces the 
obligation to cooperate across borders, i.e. to immediately notify other Member States if 
there are accidents or incidents with significant effects on human health or the environment 
in another Member State.  
Clarification of the notions of accident or incident and significant effects on human health 
or the environment in another Member State  is required so as to eliminate any possible 
doubt as to whether the criteria for such an information obligation are met  in a particular 
case. This could be done by clarifying these in the Directive or by referring to other provisions 
in EU law governing similar matters. 
In particular, excess emissions over the requirements of the integrated permit should not be 
classified as an accident or incident.  
Impact of the proposed change: 
 lack of notification of the occurrence of an accident; 
 potential mismatch between resources and incidents , resulting in involvement and 
coordination of emergency services, in the case of even minor incidents; 
 unnecessary use of diplomacy in minor cases and increased administrative burden, 
including outside the business sector. 
3. Greenhouse gas emissions (Article 9) 
The draft removes the current Article 9(2) of the IED, which gave Member States the option 
not to impose requirements relating to energy efficiency, in respect of combustion units or 
other units emitting carbon dioxide on the site, for activities listed in Annex I of  the EU ETS 
Directive.  
The previous provision allowed the authorities issuing integrated permits to set energy 
efficiency requirements as optional. This allowed the energy efficiency and industrial

4. Additional substances to be analysed (Article 14(1a)) 
The replacement of the list o f pollutants contained in Annex II of the Directive by the list 
contained in Annex II of Regulation No 166/2006 has the effect of extending the list from 13 
substances to 91 substances.  
Many of the substances listed in the Annex of the PRTR Regulation are not related to energy 
generation and their inclusion in BAT conclusions and integrated permits will create a 
significant administrative burden without any positive environmental effects. The preferred 
solution would be to define separate lists of pollutants for the different sectors in the current 
Annex II of the IED. If necessary, the Annex could be updated with new substances. 
Impact of the proposed change: 
 significant increase in the time and costs of preparing applications to issue or amend 
integrated permits; 
 scopes of applications are also increased by the environmental performance levels; 
 additional costs on the operator's side - annual measurements, fees related to the 
measured pollutants. 
5. Environmental management system (Article 14a and Article 27d) 
A new obligation is proposed, relating to Environmental Management Systems (EMS) 
established for each installation. The new provisions precisely define the requirements that 
such an environmental management system would have to meet. This will create 
administrative obligations and costs of fulfilling these obligations for the operators of the 
installations without bringing any tangible benefits to the environment. If this provision were 
to remain in the draft, it would be reasonable, in our opinion:  
1) to introduce installation capacity thresholds from which particular installations would be 
covered by the obligation to maintain an EMS, 
2) to relax the requirements concerning the necessary system components. 
Particularly significant risks seem to arise from the  obligation to provide each EMS with a 
transformation plan for each installation and the obligation to make all data contained in the 
ESM available on the Internet, without registration.

6. Emission limit values and derogations (Article 15, Article 16(3), Annex II)   
The proposed changes to this provision (Article 15(3)) relate to new rules for determining 
emission limit values in integrated permits. It states that the authority sets emission limit 
values that are the strictest and consistent with the lowest emissions achievable through the 
application of the best available techniques (BAT) at the installation and ensures that 
emissions do not exceed the emission levels associated with the best available techniques 
(BAT-AEL) under normal operating conditions. At the same t ime, it states that the emission 
limit values are based on the operator's assessment . It may therefore be unclear whether 
the operator's assessment or the strictest emissions resulting from the application of BAT are 
binding.  
It is not clear how the opera tor is supposed to demonstrate the inability to meet the lower 
levels of the emission limit range, which increases discretion, uncertainty and will affect the 
timing of the issuance of permits.  In connection with the possibility of appealing against the 
issued decisions by any entity, as indicated in the draft (Article 25), this may cause a complete 
paralysis of the authorities issuing permits, appeal bodies and courts.

The BAT conclusions and the emission ranges provided therein for individual industries were 
not designed in such a way that the lower level would constitute the basis for setting permit 
conditions. If the solution proposed by the Commission were to be adopted, transitional 
provisions are needed to indicate that it would only apply to BAT conclusions adopted under 
the new rules, i.e. after the entry into force of the revised Directive. In addition, it would be 
necessary to clarify the criteria for the analysis to be carried out when applying for a specific 
emission level higher than the lowes t emissions, so that this does not become a matter of 
dispute in administrative procedures.  
It should be noted that the upper and lower limits of BAT-AELs were established as a result of 
specific statistics (questionnaires – even from non -EU third countri es) at the stage of 
developing the BREF documents for the LCP. The BAT -AELs ranges represent reference 
conditions showing where the "performance" of an installation should be located, while only 
the emission standard should remain binding.  The BAT conclusi ons cannot be specific to a 
technique and therefore it is wrong to expect that each installation should comply with the 
lower end of the range. N o BAT technique should be preferred as the techniques listed and 
described in the BAT conclusions are neither prescriptive nor exhaustive. 
In our opinion, taking into account the specifics of a given installation, it is the operator that 
should indicate the optimum emission level  to be set in the permit. At the same time, the 
current discretion of administrative bodies in determining the optimum emission level should 
be maintained. The lower emission limit may not be optimum for many installations covered 
by the IED, inter alia due to the fact that the lower emission limits may have been determined 
on the basis of data concerning reference installations from outside the European Union or 
even installations which have not been commissioned (on the basis of designs). The 
introduction of the best available techniques was based on the adaptation of r equirements 
to the possibilities of a given installation, i.e. a technique that is both possible and available 
for a given installation.  
It should be borne in mind that investments in emission reduction require technological 
changes that are particularly burdensome and costly at the time when the installation is 
already in operation. For this reason, stricter requirements should not be imposed on the 
installations that are already in operation due to the risk of the necessity to make significant 
changes (i ncluding structural ones). Imposing the revised requirements only on new 
installations would allow achieving a beneficial result with smaller capital expenditures since 
high capital expenditures lower the competitiveness of the European Union economy. 
As explained in the impact assessment presented by the Commission, the proposed solution 
aims to encourage the tightening of the emission limits in the terms of permits for 
installations across the EU, using a harmonised approach.  According to the document, the 
introduction of the lower limits of BAT -AELs is not intended to be mandatory . Competent 
authorities would be expected to make decisions on a case -by-case basis, still taking into 
account local environmental conditions and technical characteri stics of the installation, i.e.

7. Compliance assessment (Article 15a) 
It is proposed to publish an implementing act establishing the measuring method for assessing 
compliance with emission limit values  set out in the permit with regard to emissions to air 
and water.  However, the obligations arising from these pr ovisions will affect not only the 
Commission but also the installation operators. The unification of the way in which 
measurements are carried out will result in a completely different recognition of the limit 
values, approval periods, measurement uncertai nties and frequency of exceedance which 
exist in the legislation systems of Member States and which are reflected in the operation of 
the measuring systems.  
Impact of the proposed change: 
 risk of having to bear the high financial costs necessary to rebuild  the systems that will 
not comply with the requirements of the Commission’s delegated act at the effective date 
of the legislation. 
8. Compliance with plans and programmes set under Union legislation (Article 
21(5)(c)) 
The reference to plans and programmes established under Union legislation is too vague to 
provide a basis for a revision of the integrated permit. It is necessary to remove or clarify this 
condition in order to avoid the risk of onerous administrative burdens for the authorities and 
operators.  
Impact of the proposed change: 
 lack of clarity and risk of diversified application of the legislation; 
 initiation of unnecessary processes of revision of integrated permits; 
 additional financial and administrative burden. 
9. Access to the justice system (Article 25(1)) 
The Directive introduces the principle that participation in the review procedure cannot be 
made conditional on the role played by the concerned member of the public during a 
participatory phase of the decision-making procedures. In other words, any individual will be 
entitled to challenge an administrative decision, even if they have not previously participated 
in the integrated permit procedure and have not demonstrated an interest in the case.

10. Penalties (Article 79) 
This provision regulates the maximum amount of a fine imposed on operators for violation of 
national provisions adopted pursuant to this Directive. However, it contains an editorial error 
since it states that  “the maximum amount of such fines shall be at least 8% of the operator’s 
annual turnover in the Member State concerned”. The phrase “at least” should be deleted. 
Impact of the proposed change: 
 the current wording makes it impossible to apply the provision, which is self -
contradictory. 
11. Compensation (Article 79a) 
This regulation provides for rules on compensation in the event of damage to human health 
resulting from violations of national measures adopted pursuant to the Directive. It lays the 
onus on the person responsible for the violation to prove that the violation did not cause or 
contribute to the damage. Proving this will not usually be possible because diseases can be 
caused by a variety of factors that can affect human health at the same time. It will be 
impossible to completely rule out the health effects of emissions into the environment, even 
if the likelihood that the emissions caused the harm will be remote. This will lead to numerous 
and often unjustified compensation claims against the operators of installations.

12. Annex V 
The entry into force of the BAT conclusions causes the functioning of two emission 
assessment systems in the energy sector: emission levels (BAT AELs) and emission standards 
(with higher values and different averaging periods). The functioning of both of these systems 
in the energy sector is unjustified – demonstrating compliance with them constitutes an 
unnecessary obligation and cost for the operators of combustion installations and 
administration bodies.  
Taking into account the different types of industries covered by the IED, whose individual 
situations may be different, we suggest supplementing the Annex with a provision stating that 
“in the case of the energy sector, compliance with the BAT conclusions will be regarded as 
the fulfilment of the emission standards”. 
We also propose that Annex V include an unambiguous definition (meaning) of the concept 
of "indicative character" showing its dependence on other parameters and unambiguously 
specifying that exceeding it does not imply non-compliance with emission levels (this concept 
appears, for example, in Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2021/2326 of 30 November 
2021 establishing conclusions on best available techniques (BAT) for large combustion plants).

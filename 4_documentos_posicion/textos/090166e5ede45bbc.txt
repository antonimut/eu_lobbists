 Veolia  designs  and  deploys  tailor-made  solutions  to  help  cities  and  industries  optimise  their 
 resource  management,  directly  contributing  to  their  sustainability  performance.  With 
 220,000  employees  worldwide,  the  Group  designs  and  provides  water,  waste  and  energy 
 management  solutions  that  contribute  to  the  sustainable  development  of  communities  and 
 industries  .  Around  75%  of  Veolia’s  activities  are  performed  in  the  EU.  In  2021,  Veolia 
 supplied  95  million  people  with  drinking  water  and  62  million  people  with  waste  water 
 service,  produced  nearly  43  million  megawatt  hours  of  energy  and  converted  47  million 
 metric  tons  of  waste  into  new  materials  and  energy.  In  June  2019,  the  Group  announced 
 the   development   of   a   new   business   activity   focusing   on   improving   air   quality. 
 Veolia  welcomes  the  Commission’s  proposal  to  revise  the  Industrial  Emission  Directive  in  an 
 effort  to  support  the  EU  Green  Deal’s  objectives  and  stands  ready  to  support  its 
 implementation   as   the   newly   created   champion   of   ecological   transformation. 
 Definition 
 There  is  a  clear  necessity  to  update  the  Industrial  Emission  Directive  to  pave  the  way  for  the 
 circular  economy,  and  better  address  the  new  challenges  that  resource  scarcities  bring  to 
 light.  For  this  reason,  Veolia  strongly  supports  the  revision  of  the  Directive’s  impactful 
 definitions.  We  think  the  revised  definition  of  ‘  Installation  ’  and  ‘  BAT  Conclusions  ’  in  Article  3 
 and  the  introduction  of  the  new  definitions  ‘  BAT-AEPL  ’,  ‘  emission  levels  associated  with 
 emerging  techniques  ’,  ‘  compliance  assurance  ’  and  ‘  benchmarks  ’  will  help  significantly 
 stakeholders  to  improve  their  installations  toward  more  circularity  and  a  better  and 
 consistent   application   of   the   text. 
 Enlarged   scope 
 We  welcome  the  fact  that  the  revised  Directive  maintains  the  size  of  installations,  that  is  for 
 “Combustion  of  fuels  in  installations  with  a  total  rated  thermal  input  of  50  MW  or  more”. 
 Keeping  this  threshold  is  indeed  the  most  optimal  solution  for  treating  polluting  substances, 
 with   the   complementary   contribution   of   the   Medium   Combustion   Plants   Directive. 
 Also,  to  avoid  overlapping  with  existing  legislation,  we  recommend  not  to  include  in  Article  9 
 (1)  on  GHG  emissions  installations  already  covered  by  ETS  (unless  a  Member  State 
 estimates  that  there  is  significant  local  pollution).  This  will  avoid  unnecessary  administrative 
 burden  for  ETS  installations  as  well  as  ensure  optimal  emissions  reduction  path  (provided 
 through  carbon  pricing  that  keeps  on  increasing).  We  note  a  review  of  the  synergies  between

 Operator’s   responsibilities  (Article   14a) 
 The  issues  of  water  scarcity  and  energy  shortage  are  increasing  across  many  European 
 countries.  In  consequence,  we  are  convinced  that  the  updated  obligations  on  waste 
 generation,  optimisation  of  resource  use  and  water  reuse  can  contribute  to  strengthening  the 
 circular  economy,  in  a  constructive  way.  The  prevention  or  reduction  risks  associated  with 
 the  use  of  hazardous  substances  can  equally  contribute  to  achieving  this  circular  future. 
 Moreover,  the  generalisation  of  the  Environmental  Management  System  corresponds  to  the 
 state   of   the   art. 
 Emission   Limit   Values 
 Regarding  “  the  strictest  possible  emission  values  that  are  consistent  with  the  lowest 
 emissions  achievable  by  applying  BAT  in  the  installation  ”,  Veolia  acknowledges  the  need  to 
 use  the  full  range  of  the  BATAELs  and  not  simply  its  least  stringent  end.  We  however  insist 
 that  potential  cross-media  effects  should  be  taken  into  account  by  the  competent  authority 
 when  setting  emission  limits:  the  global  environmental  performance  in  the  local  context  of  the 
 facility  was  at  the  core  of  the  original  IPPC  Directive  and  this  approach  remains  paramount. 
 We  also  anticipate  that  specific  BREFs  would  need  to  be  revised:  for  instance,  the 
 Incineration  BREF  was  established  on  a  ‘high  and  low  value’  logic  covering  several 
 techniques  that  have  different  performances.  If  the  lowest  value  is  to  be  applied  then  the 
 BREF   should   be   reviewed   and   each   technique   differentiated   as   in   the   LCP   BREF. 
 Compliance   assessment 
 The  Directive  can  achieve  its  full  potential  in  terms  of  sustainability  and  environmental 
 protection,  only  when  effectively  and  consistently  implemented  across  all  Member  States. 
 Therefore,  Veolia  fully  supports  the  setting  of  common  rules  for  assessing  compliance  with 
 emission  limit  values  and  validation  of  measured  levels  for  both  air  and  water  emission 
 based  on  BAT.  This  clarifies  that  the  maximum  correction  made  to  measurements  to 
 determine  a  validated  average  emission  value  shall  not  exceed  the  measurement 
 uncertainty   of   the   measuring   method. 
 Environmental   Quality   Standards  (Article   18) 
 The  proposal  highlights  the  need  to  take  full  account  of  the  environmental  quality  standards 
 including  new  standards,  for  surface  water  and  groundwater.  Veolia  also  endorses  the 
 precisions   in   the   case   of   discharge   via   the   municipal   sewage   system.

 Conclusion 
 The  high  energy  prices,  the  disrupted  supply  chains  and  high  inflation  pose  existential 
 difficulties  to  companies  who  have  already  been  under  pressure  in  the  last  years  due  to  the 
 COVID  crisis.  This  dramatic  situation  calls  for  a  clear  and  robust  EU  policy  framework  which 
 mitigates  these  short-term  challenges  while  supporting  the  necessary  industrial 
 transformation   required   for   the   long-term   by   the   Green   Deal’s   objectives. 
 To  conclude,  the  EU  is  best  placed  to  set  the  rules  promoting  a  sustainable  and  circular 
 society  through  the  regulation  of  industrial  installations  and  livestock  farms’  emissions.  A 
 number  of  measures  presented  in  this  Directive  tackle  the  issue  of  consistent 
 implementation  and  help  promote  environmental  protection  and  the  correction  of  distortions 
 to  competition  between  Member  States.  The  Commission's  proposals  are  largely  in  line  with 
 Veolia's  vision  in  terms  of  control  of  pollution  at  source,  resource  efficiency,  water  reuse  and 
 circular   economy.

Hitachi Energy’s position on the European Commission’s proposal for the revision of the F-gas 
regulation 
Hitachi Energy (formerly ABB Power Grids), a joint venture between Hitachi and ABB, is a global technology 
leader with roots in Europe and a history of innovation spanning more than 130 years. The company is com-
mitted to advancing a sustainable energy future for all. It serves customers in the utility, industry, and infra-
structure sectors with leading technologies to help accelerate the energy transition and facilitate a carbon-
neutral future. Hitachi Energy has a proven track record and an unparalleled installed base in more than 140 
countries. Headquartered in Switzerland, the company employs more than 38,000 people in 90 countries and 
generates a business volume of over $10 billion USD. 
Hitachi Energy is a technology and market leader in high-voltage switchgear including GIS (Gas Insulated 
Switchgear), a technology it pioneered. This business has a significant presence in Europe, with engineering 
centers and manufacturing units in several countries including Germany, Italy, the Czech Republic, Sweden, 
Bulgaria, and Switzerland. 
Facilitating the energy transition is at the core of Hitachi Energy’s purpose. The company is actively enabling 
EU policy initiatives like the EU Green Deal, Fit for 55 and Repower EU. Hitachi Energy firmly supports the 
Commission’s objective of mitigating the impact of fluorinated greenhouse gases and replacing SF6 in switch-
gear as swiftly as possible while ensuring the grid reliability that is essential to the energy transition. 
The European Commission’s proposal announced on April 5, 2022, however, contains provisions for 
high-voltage switchgear which would derail the timely phase-out of SF6 and would hence be adverse 
in climate terms and counter-productive to achieve its stated objectives. 
 By restricting the use of products with gases having a Global Warming Potential (GWP) more 
than 10, it restricts the most practical and scalable SF6 replacement solutions available today. 
The enforcement of such a proposal could significantly slow down the replacement of SF6 and jeop-
ardize the EU climate change mitigation targets, for which companies need to be investing today. 
 The proposal focuses solely on the GWP of the gas and does not take into account the overall 
environmental footprint of the entire switchgear and the substation. This is a simplistic approach 
that can be misleading. Solutions with GWP <10 of the insulation gas have currently an overall envi-
ronmental impact which is actually higher when considering the full product life-cycle due to additional 
material used in the manufacturing of the switchgear and due to the additional space and land use 
required. 
 The proposal does not factor in the additional costs for the industry, eventually affecting con-
sumers in an already challenging energy environment. Even if the additional material for achiev-
ing GWP <10 would be produced in a fully carbon-neutral way, it would result in excessive abatement 
costs being passed on to consumers. 
 The proposal does not differentiate between voltage levels of the switchgear in setting the 
GWP threshold. This would have adverse consequences for switchgear above 52 kV since technol-
ogy for high-voltage switchgear is different compared to medium voltage switchgear and require-
ments for grid resilience and reliability are more critical at the high-voltage level. 
 The proposal would create uncertainty due to multiple thresholds and uncertain exemption 
criteria. The suggested criteria would require for high-voltage switchgear a case-by-case analysis of 
technology alternatives for each substation project and could be continuously challenged before, dur-
ing and after the project tendering process. Limiting the technology options for grid operators would 
slow down the SF6 replacement process. 
For high-voltage switchgear (52 kV and higher), the Commission’s proposal should therefore replace 
the two thresholds at ‘GWP = 10’ and ‘GWP=2000’, as well as the exemption wording, by a single 
threshold of ‘GWP = 1000’ enabling all SF6 replacement technologies, leading to a faster phase-out 
of SF6 usage. Hitachi Energy looks forward to substantiating the above points with technical studies 
and independent assessments, as well as engaging with all relevant government, institutional and 
industry stakeholders during the legislative process.

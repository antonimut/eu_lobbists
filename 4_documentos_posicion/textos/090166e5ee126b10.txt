Copa-Cogeca position paper on the Commission’s 
proposal for the revision of the GI system 
Considering the Commission’s proposal for a Regulation of the European Parliament and of the 
Council on European Union geographical indications for wine, spirit drinks and agricultural 
products, and quality schemes for agricultural products, amending Regulations (EU) No 
1308/2013, (EU) 2017/1001 and (EU) 2019/787 and repealing Regulation (EU) No 1151/2012, 
Copa and Cogeca believe that the following elements should be taken into account: 
1. Delegation of power to EUIPO 
• Copa and Cogeca believe that shifting competencies away from DG AGRI and towards an 
agency specialised in intellectual property rights and without the necessary knowledge of 
the specificities of the agricultural sector and the nature of GIs puts at risk this successful 
policy. Before involving EUIPO in GI management, the added value of such an 
involvement should be demonstrated, as well as the agency’s ability and expertise when it 
comes to conducting such work. The recent introduction of national level processing for 
certain amendments to GI specifications should simplify management of GIs. This 
simplification should mean there is no need to involve another agency. 
• To accelerate the processing of GI registration and new amendments, we believe that we 
should rather delegate more competencies to Member States (like with amendments) 
instead of delegating competencies to an external agency like EUIPO 
• If EUIPO are to play a role in GI management, their involvement should be strictly 
defined and limited directly in the Regulation and not through delegated acts. 
Furthermore, all decisions regarding the application, amendment, and cancellation of GIs 
should remain strictly within DG AGRI’s remit. EUIPO’s activities should preferably 
remain restricted to GI protection online, management of the GI register, and 
administrative tasks. In no way should EUIPO be involved in opposition and infringement 
procedures for GIs. 
• The monitoring of EUIPO’s performance on the GI management tasks that could be 
delegated, as laid down in the proposal, does not seem sufficient to ensure proper 
management of GIs. To ensure appropriate monitoring, precise evaluation criteria should 
be directly laid down in the proposal. We also consider that the European Parliament and 
the Council should be entitled to reattribute the delegated tasks back to DG AGRI in case 
the monitoring reveals shortcomings in EUIPO’s management. 
2. Powers and functioning of producer groups 
• Division of producer groups into “producer groups” and “recognised 
producer groups” (Art. 32 and 33 of the proposal): 
Copa and Cogeca believe that the division into “producer groups” and “recognised 
producer groups”, with the latter being endowed with some additional rights, is worth 
considering as it could contribute to empowering producer groups. However, we believe 
that in order to work efficiently this structure should take the following elements into 
account:

o The interaction between “producer groups” and “recognised producer groups” need 
to be clarified. We believe that the existence of a “recognised producer group” for a 
GI should not prevent a “producer group” for the same GI to exercise its 
prerogatives. 
o The power to launch legal actions and liaise with intellectual property enforcement 
and anti-counterfeit bodies to protect GIs should be part of the powers of “producer 
groups” and not only accessible to “recognised producer groups”. 
o The condition of representing at least 2/3 of producers and accounting for at least 
2/3 of the production of a GI to be entitled to be designated as the “recognised 
producer group” of that GI is acceptable, but the other conditions to be fulfilled for 
this designation should be left to the discretion of Member States, which most of the 
time already have well-functioning procedures in place. 
o Specific powers and responsibilities reserved for “recognised producer groups” (Art. 
33 §2 of the proposal) should not be subject to a prior agreement concluded 
between at least 2/3 of the producers of the product designated by a geographical 
indication, accounting for at least 2/3 of the production of that product in the 
geographical area referred to in the product specification. The procedure to take 
decisions on those specific powers and responsibilities should be left to the 
discretion of the “recognised producer groups”. 
o We believe that no actor other than producers should be involved in the internal 
functioning of "producer groups" and "recognised producer groups", unless these 
groups decide otherwise. 
• “Producer group” definition (Art.2 §1 point a) of the proposal): 
For the producer groups for PDOs, producers of the raw materials required to produce the 
PDO should be included as producers and thus be entitled to be part of the producer 
groups. This would require amending the definition of “producer group” as referred in 
Art.2 §1 point a) of the proposal. 
• Management of the intellectual property rights of a GI: 
The name of a GI is the property of a group of producers, of a collective. It is owned by 
them and they alone have ownership of the rights. We thus appreciate that producer 
groups are entitled to manage the intellectual property rights associated with a GI. 
However, we believe that the responsibility for the management of a GI’s property rights 
should be shared between the producer group for that GI and the Member State that has 
filed the registration. This would ensure that Member State authorities are also 
responsible for the protection of GIs, while preventing that a GI is left without any legal 
representative in the eventuality of a collapse of the producer group. 
3. Simplification and streamlining of procedures 
• Copa and Cogeca do not oppose the merging of the provisions for registration, 
amendments, cancellation, protection, and control of GIs for agricultural products and 
spirit drinks.  
• Regarding wine GIs, Copa and Cogeca believe that registration, amendments, cancellation, 
and protection provisions can be harmonised with those for agricultural products and 
spirit drinks, but the texts cannot be merged. It should remain in the CMO Regulation to 
ensure all provisions related to wine management remain in the same text to ensure 
coherence.  
• Copa and Cogeca agree that wine GIs should keep specific control provisions, as laid down 
in the CMO Regulation. 
4. Sustainability 
• Copa and Cogeca believe that the introduction of specific sustainability criteria for GIs 
should remain strictly voluntary. 
• GIs are a scheme designed to guarantee the authenticity and the quality linked to a 
specific region and its savoir faire, there are other specific schemes for sustainability. 
Moreover, sustainability schemes are also used by producers of the same GI to 
differentiate their products on the market. Consequently, producer groups must have the

possibility to adopt ad-hoc sustainability undertakings not directly included in the GI 
general specifications. 
• It is also essential to underline that sustainability criteria should be based on all three 
pillars of sustainability: environmental, social and economic, and a definition of 
sustainability based on those three pillars should be directly provided in the Regulation. 
• Health and nutrition should not be part of any definition regarding sustainability criteria 
for GIs. The modification the formulation of a GI for nutrition purposes would be a direct 
contradiction with the nature of the GI system, which in part exists to provide consumers 
with a guarantee of a specific and authentic gustative quality. 
• If criteria for the recognition of sustainability standards for GIs are to be established, they 
should be directly included the in the Regulation. The EC should not be granted powers to 
adopt delegated acts based on an ex-post definition of sustainability which would apply 
retroactively to producers. This could create legal uncertainty for producer groups which 
already intend to establish sustainability undertakings. 
• Producer groups should also be provided with means to play a role in disseminating and 
teaching sustainable agricultural practices to producers. 
5. Protection and controls 
• Definition of evocation: 
Copa and Cogeca believe that protection of GIs against evocation is extremely important. 
Nevertheless, we are concerned that by introducing a very restrictive definition of 
evocation, the proposal might weaken this protection, as evocation is an evolving practice 
that cannot be encompassed by a very restrictive definition. We would thus recommend 
deleting the definition and instead relying on the European Court of Justice’s 
jurisprudence.  
• New elements for protection: 
o Copa and Cogeca generally welcome the proposal’s introduction of new elements for 
the protection for GIs such as: extension of the protection to domain names using 
GI names, protection of GIs used as ingredients in processed products, additional 
protection duties for Member States including electronic commerce, and the 
introduction of certificates for GI producers.  
o For the protection of GI rights in domain names, we believe that it should be 
extended to all domain names, not just those of EU Member States. The .eu domain 
should also be included. 
o Copa and Cogeca believe that the revocation or transfer of a domain name misusing 
a GI name should be automatic and not upon request of a natural or legal person 
with a legitimate interest or right. 
o In the article for the general protection of GIs against any misuse (Art.27 §1 of the 
proposal), the extension of this protection to GIs used as ingredients should remain 
in the text, even if the proposal also includes specific articles for this protection. 
o We call to include in the proposal, in the section on controls, specific control 
provisions and sanctions on misuses in the use of a GI name. 
• Controls 
We believe that the revision should amend article 19 of the Commission Implementing 
Regulation (EU) n°2019/34 to offer the possibility for the annual verification to be 
limited to administrative controls when those administrative checks alone allow to 
ensure full compliance with the requirements and conditions laid down in the product 
specifications. 
6. PDO and PGIs 
• Copa and Cogeca believe that Art. 48 (3) should be deleted. It states that products which 
by nature cannot be traded within the internal market (but can only be consumed in or 
near their place of production), as well as products that are contrary to public policy or 
accepted principles of morality, cannot be registered as a GI. 
• The Proposal removes the possibility of replacing trhe term "protected designation of 
origin" with the term "registered designation of origin". The term AOC is widely used by

operators and known by consumers, we thus believe that it is important to maintain this 
possibility. 
7. Support for new comers 
• Copa and Cogeca believe that the Regulation should mention that GIs should be strongly 
supported by EU policy to promote agricultural products.  
• We also believe that the Regulation should contain a provision requiring Member States to 
promote GIs in their public food procurement policies. 
• Copa and Cogeca also believe that the Regulation should establish an obligation for 
Member States and the Commission to provide GI producers with the expertise and 
administrative support necessary for GI registration, especially in regions that are under-
represented in the GI scheme.

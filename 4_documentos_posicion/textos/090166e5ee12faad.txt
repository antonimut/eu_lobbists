  
 
As a technology company committed to using innovation to help decarbonize industry and accelerate 
global climate solutions for ourselves and our customers, 3M would like to stress our support for a 
science-based regulation with robust phasing out deadlines for certain fluorinated gases (“F-gas”) We 
welcome the European Commission’s proposal for a Regulation on Fluorinated gases (“Regulation”). We 
see the Regulation as a small but critical element for the EU to meet its commitments made in the Paris 
Agreement on Climate Change and towards the goals of the Montreal Protocol on Substances that 
Deplete the Ozone Layer.  However, we have concerns that the proposed Regulation is overbroad and 
could unintentionally delay introduction of sulfur hexafluoride-free (SF6-free) solutions in High Voltage 
Switchgear (“HV Switchgear”).   
 
F-gas regulation proposal will delay introduction of SF6-free solutions in High Voltage Switchgear. 
 
We raise our concerns regarding the proposed placing on the market prohibition (POM) for High Voltage 
Electrical Switchgear (HV Switchgear) as stated in Annex IV, Section 23. The initial F-gas regulation sent a 
signal that the market needed to develop alternatives to SF6 for gas-insulated switchgear (GIS) 
applications. Industry made significant investment with over a decade of research to develop an 
alternative, heptafluoroisobutyronitrile (iso-C3F7CN), that achieves as much as 99% reduction in 
greenhouse gas (“GHG”) emissions compared to SF6. Nevertheless, the current proposal places a limit 
on this technology by making an arbitrary global warming potential (GWP) cutoff, despite the fact that 
C4-FN actually exceeds the F-gas objectives.   
 
As worded in Annex IV, Section 23, the POM requires providing evidence that “no suitable alternative is 
available based on technical grounds” with GWP < 10. This requirement will prevent further investments 
into iso-C3F7CN technology due to the uncertain long-term use. It also has the potential to restrict 
competition in Europe since the only technology that could potentially meet the application 
requirements given such a GWP limit is a combination of high-pressure air and vacuum systems, which is 
available from one supplier. The proposed limitations are not appropriate, as they exclude C4-FN 
mixtures which have been demonstrated to be some of the best technologies with respect to reducing 
the CO2 footprint & environmental impact of HV Switchgear. Existing installations of HV GIS, gas-
insulated lines and circuit breakers which utilize gas mixtures containing the iso-C3F7CN have shown 
that this technology can reduce GHG contributions versus SF6 by 99% and have the lowest climate 
impact when performing a full Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) due to reduced size and material utilization. 
 
Overall, 3M has significant concerns that the F-gas regulation proposal as currently defined will delay 
the introduction of SF6-free solutions. Therefore, 3M recommends that evidence of “no suitable 
alternatives” should be required only for gases with GWP > 2000.  
 
General comments and recommendations. 
 
[1,1,1,2,3,4,5,5,5(or1,1,1,3,4,4,5,5,5)-nonafluoro-4(or2)-(trifluoromethyl)pent-2-ene] is inappropriately 
classified in Annex II under “Unsaturated hydro(chloro)fluorocarbons.”  The aforementioned substance 
neither contains hydrogen nor chlorine. As an unsaturated fluorocarbon consisting solely of carbon and 
fluorine, it is more appropriately classified in Annex III, Section II: Other fluorinated compounds.  
 
Further, certain substances with extremely low global warming potential, such as 
[1,1,1,2,3,4,5,5,5(or1,1,1,3,4,4,5,5,5)-nonafluoro-4(or2)-(trifluoromethyl)pent-2-ene], are categorized in

Annex II with a requirement to report leaks and relabeling. 3M recommends that the Commission 
incentivize use of gases with extremely low global warming potential by moving them to Annex III where 
they will be monitored until the Commission adopts delegated acts regarding the global warming 
potential following The Montreal Protocol.  
 
There is a discrepancy throughout the proposal between whether industrial names or chemical names 
are used for various fluorinated gases, which may cause confusion. We suggest using the two categories 
of names consistently. Commercial names such as FC-43, HFE-7000, HFE-7100, HFE-7200, HFE-7300 
should be removed and naming conventions throughout the proposal should remain consistent. We 
recommend updating HFE-7300 commercial name to the industrial name HFE-64-13 and structure to 
(CF3)2CFCF(OCH3)CF2CF3.  
 
In Annex III, 3M suggests removing the following three specific isomers: n-HFE-7100, i-HFE-7100, and i-
HFE-7200.  Both HFE-7100 and HFE-7200 are manufactured and commercialized as isomeric mixtures 
(i.e., commercially available HFE-7100 includes both n-HFE-7100 and i-HFE-7100 isomers and HFE-7200 
includes both n-HFE-7200 and i-HFE-7200 isomers).  None of these specific isomers are available 
individually. As the isomer mixtures are already listed in Annex III, they do not need to be added.    
 
“Fluorinated greenhouse gases” are no longer defined in Article 3 but are referenced many times 
throughout the proposal.  3M recommends defining the term as using the 2015 F-Gas definition: 
 
“‘fluorinated greenhouse gases’ means the hydrofluorocarbons, perfluorocarbons, sulphur 
hexafluoride and other greenhouse gases that contain fluorine, listed in Annex I, or mixtures 
containing any of those substances” 
 
In addition, there is a need for clarification regarding which Annexes are in scope for which Articles as 
“fluorinated greenhouse gases” are referenced either in general or by Annex. For example, Article 4, 
paragraph 5 relays Declaration of Conformity requirements, but does not reference an Annex. 
Additionally, please see proposal text below and help clarify if Annexes I, II and III are included in Article 
2, or just Annexes I and II,.  
 
"This regulation applies to the fluorinated greenhouse gases listed in Annexes I, II, and II, 
whether alone or in a mixture." 
 
Further, in Article 10, paragraph 1, there is reference to, “… other relevant alternatives to fluorinated 
greenhouse gases,” which is ambiguous. We recommend clarifying other relevant alternatives. 
 
3M interprets the use of the term “gases” in Annex IV to apply to the gas which is in actual use within 
the equipment. Therefore, in the case of mixtures, the GWP would apply to the mixture as a whole, as 
described in the beginning of Annex IV, rather than the GWP of any individual component.  Accordingly, 
we recommend clarifying that the GWPs cited in Annex IV apply to the mixture when those are the gas 
in use within the application.  
 
In Annex III, Section 2: Other fluorinated compounds: perfluoro-N-methylmorpholine has a GWP value 
listed in reference 13 which is incorrect. The information is for PFTBA, not PNMM. The Correct GWP is 
8380 (reference: https://echa.europa.eu/registration-dossier/-/registered-dossier/10245/5/1). 
 
  
Two calculation methods for global warming potential are used throughout the Regulation, making the 
calculation of actual greenhouse gas emissions opaque. We suggest keeping to one of the two to ease 
monitoring and reporting.

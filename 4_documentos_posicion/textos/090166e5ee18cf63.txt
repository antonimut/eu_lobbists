EFCEM view  
on the Proposal for Regulation of fluorinated greenhouse gases 
– Document COM(2022)150 - 05/04/2022 
 
EFCEM (European Federation of Catering Equipment Manufacturers) supports the 
alignment of the F-gas Regulation with  the European Green Deal, European 
Climate Law, obligations on HFC s under the  Montreal Protocol, progress and 
lessons learned. 
Professional products represented by EFCEM have a small share on the market, in 
percentage terms, compared to other ma rket sectors covered by the proposed 
regulation. 
We would like to make some specific comments, as detailed in the following chapters, on the 
approach taken for the definitions , placing on the  market prohibitions and possible impacts 
on the professional and commercial refrigeration products. 
1. Definitions 
1.1 Article 3 Definition (9) ‘hermetically sealed equipment’ 
The purpose of the  definition ‘hermetically sealed equipment’  should s pecify 
parameters in order to understand if an equipment is hermetically sealed and it is not 
relevant “where” the manufacturer put in place the process to fulfil the requirements, 
in particular these parts are subject to leakage test (Arti cle 5) and this is a way to 
check the manufacturer’s process result. 
We would highlight the need of a proper definition o f ‘hermetically sealed 
equipment’ (that will not exclude remote installations).  
EFCEM recommends deleting “during its manufacturing process at the 
premises of the manufacturer” in Article 3.9. 
 
1.2  Term “Self-contained equipment” 
The definition of s elf-containment is no t present in the Commissi on's proposal 
(2022/0099 (COD)). 
This definition must be read with ANNEX IV category 11, together with the distinction 
between HFCs and fluorinated greenhouse linked to prohibition date 1st January 2024 
and ANNEX IV category 12. 
EFCEM recommends adding a definition of “self-contained equipment”.

1.3 Term “Producer” 
The wording referred to “producer” is not clear to us. Especially, it is not clear if it is a 
producer of gas or refrigera ted appliances. This notion i s particularly relevant in 
relation to quotas (article 18, 19 etc.). 
 
EFCEM recommends adding a definition of “producer”. 
1.4 Term “Fluorinated greenhouse gases” 
A definition of  fluorinated greenhouse gases is no t present in the Commissi on's 
proposal (2022/0099 (COD)) as well as always precise references, apart from as done 
for example in Annex XI points from (4) to (9) when for fluorinated greenhouse gases 
a clear reference to Annexes where are listed  is done, helping in clarifying which are 
the references. 
 
EFCEM recommends adding a definition of “fluorinated greenhouse gases” 
or add references. 
 
1.5 Article 5.1: Leak checks 
The l imit added for unsaturated hydro (chloro) fluorocarbons  
(ANNEX II, secti on 1),  is 1 kg  and this i s a criterion for  regular leak checks . 
In case of hermetically se aled equipment, this limit can be increased  up to 2kg , 
considering that this is a climate-friendly gas. For these reasons, the limits should be 
increased. 
EFCEM recommends the following amendment:  
“1. Operators of equipment that  contains 5 t onnes of CO2 equivalent or more of 
fluorinated greenhouse gases listed in Annex I or 4 kilogram or more of fluorinated 
greenhouse gases listed in Annex II, Section I,  not contained in foams, shall ensure 
that the equipment is checked for leaks.  
Hermetically sealed equipment that contains less than 10 tonnes of CO2 equivalent of 
fluorinated greenhouse gases listed in Annex I or 6 kilograms of flu orinated 
greenhouse gases listed in Annex II, Section I,  shall not be checked for leaks, 
provided th e equipment i s labelled as hermetical ly sealed and its conne cted parts 
have a tested leakage rate of less than 3 grams per year under a pressure of at least  
a quarter of the maximum allowable pressure.” 
2. Placing on the market prohibitions referred to in Article 11(1) in ANNEX IV 
2.1 Item 11 
The enforcement deadline seems to be retroactive due to the change of the wording. 
of "hermetically sealed" to “self-contained” – without defining the latter. This change 
applied to past prohibition dates can creates a situation of “legal instability”. 
EFCEM recommends another wording and an increased deadline: 
 
 
Refrigerators and freezers for commercial use (self-contained equipment)  
- hermetically sealed equipment that contain HFCs with GWP of 2 500 or  more 
(hermetically sealed equipment). 1 January 2020 
- hermetically sealed equipment that contain HFCs with GWP of 150 or more  
(hermetically sealed equipment). 1 January 2022 
- self-contained equipment that contain other fluorinated greenhouse gases with GWP 
of 150 or more  (‘self-contained equipmen t’). 1 January 2027  
EFCEM recommends three-year delay of 1 January 202 4 after the 
publication of the legislation. 
2.2 Item 12 
Any self -contained refrig eration equipment that contains fluor inated gr eenhouse 
gases w ith GWP of 150 o r m ore: 1 January 2030  
EFCEM recommends five-year delay of 1 January 2025 after the publication 
of the legislation. 
A clear distinction betwe en ‘self-contained equipmen t’ and ‘stationary 
equipment’ is of high importance to avoid overlapping among items 12, 13 
and 1 4: practically, the sam e appliance could fall under both categories 
(self-contained and stationery).  
The redesign of the appliances to fulfil the new target s and the need of 
new components are a high challenge for manufacturers in some product 
categories. From our perspective the proposed timeline is overambitious. 
3. Challenges for manufacturers to meet the market prohibitions 
3.1 Alternative refrigerants below 150 GWP 
Appliances with limited charge of flammable refrigerants according to  
IEC 60335-2-89:2019 (for example R290, R-1234yf and others with lower GWP) shall 
require a redesign and the introduction of new components. Nowadays the 
components market is not yet ready to support these new machines. For example, 
most of the 1400 liters blast chillers (negative temperature versions) use condensing 
units that do not have a suitable alternative for refrigerant currently used able to 
assure the required power for performing freezing cycle. The same situation is for ice 
makers, whose machines that won’t find any suitable alternative are about 20% of 
the whole production and that, due to limited charge of R290, cannot neither use this 
solution with the present machine configuration. A complete redesign is thus 
required, by the implementation of a double refrigerating circuit, which could make 
harder the safety rules application.  
The redesign needs development time, as well the evaluation of the quality and 
reliability of the new technical solutions applied.  
3.2 Timeline, development and additional energy crisis challenges 
The d evelopment of p rototypes is more complicate d due to the difficulties o f the 
supply chain of components. The delivery time is sometimes very high.  
 
 
In the next months, Companies will probably suffer a  heavy energy crisis and  a 
market downturn, due to the uncertainty and the instability as consequence of the 
war between Russia and Ukraine, and require Companies to invest in developing new 
appliances, for allowing them to place the products on th e market, could put these 
Companies in serious difficulties. 
 
EFCEM is concerned from the fact that some impacts could be underestimated by 
policy makers.

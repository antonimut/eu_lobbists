DAIKIN CHEMICAL EUROPE’S FEEDBACK ON THE LEGISLATIV E PROPOSAL (PUBLISHED 
5th  OF APRIL 2022) TO UPDATE REGULATION (EU) N° 517/20 14 (THE ‘F-GAS REGULATION’) 
 
 
 
 
This is a statement by Daikin Chemical Europe GmbH and focuses on the topic of MDIs only.  
 
 
Who is Daikin Chemicals Europe? 
Daikin Chemical Europe, is one of the largest suppliers of high performance fluorochemical products in Europe 
and the only global producer of medical propellant Solkane 227 pharma (HFC-227ea pharma) used in metered 
dose inhalers (MDI) , both in Europe and in the world.  
 
General Comments:  
Daikin Chemicals supports the EU’s aims to make Europe carbon neutral by 2050. However, as already expressed 
by many industry stakeholders during the 2020 public consultation, reaching this carbon neutrality target by lifting 
the exemption for MDIs and imposing a rapid phasedo wn for currently available F-gases must be proporti onate 
and consider the needs of healthcare patients, especially as at present no market-ready alternative exists for HFC-
227ea pharma.  
As stated by submissions to the consultation, it is  also important to keep in mind that the proportion  of 
greenhouse gas emissions from pharmaceutical uses of HFAs is less than 0.1% of the total EU emissions, and pMDI 
propellants constitute <0.05% of anthropogenic carb on emissions. This minimal impact means it would no t be 
inappropriate to allow time for F-gas users to complete the development and regulatory approval for alternatives 
to current gases, whilst preserving the right for doctors to tailor care for their respiratory patients. We therefore 
support, as others have also stated, the maintaining of current medical exemption on the F-gas regulation until at 
least 2030.

Propellant HFC (HFA)-227ea pharma and MDI 
Summary : Propellant HFC-227ea pharma (EC number: 207-079-2 ) is mostly used for drug-combination products 
and moisture sensitive drugs, for instance for the treatment of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) and 
asthma. HFC-227ea pharma is currently the best solution as propellant in MDI drug combination products, while 
research and development for alternatives are underway.  
Daikin’s Product : Solkane TM1 227 pharma (HFC-227ea) is manufactured at the Daik in Chemicals Frankfurt site 
(Germany), the propellant is chemically inert, stable, non-flammable and nontoxic.  
Application : Pressurized Metered Dose Inhaler (pMDI) for treat ment of asthma and COPD - chronic obstructive 
pulmonary disease, in combination drug products 
Functionality : Propellant, to carry the drug in to the lung for respiratory disease treatment, such as asthma and 
COPD 
Why is Solkane 227 pharma preferred : a pMDI is simple to use, children and elderly peo ple are the preferred 
patient group for drug combination products because they have less side effects than other medicines 
Context : Solkane 
TM 227 pharma is the only (HFC-227ea) propellant used in MDIs approved by regulatory bodies 
(US FDA and EU EMA) 
MDI vs DPI (dry powder inhaler) : Drug combination products can be applied in both dry powder inhalers (DPI) 
and in MDIs and the two are not competing delivery systems, but complementary. DPIs are usually used for daily 
medication in the long-term management of asthma. M DIs on the other hand, are used by patients who oft en 
have sudden and acute symptoms.  
 
Alternatives to Solkane 227 ea – What we know today 
At present no market-ready alternative exists for H FC-227ea pharma. However, to the best of our knowle dge, 
there are two alternatives in various stages of development. HFC-152a and HFO-1234ze are still in phase one (out 
of four phases) of MDI product development and their toxicity studies are still ongoing. In addition, flammability 
might be an issue during the development, the approval process and for the final use. 
Pharmaceutical product development takes, on averag e, around 10 to 12 years for an experimental drug t o be 
developed in the laboratory to placing on the marke t. For general pharmaceutical products only five in  5,000 
compounds that enter preclinical testing make it to  the human testing phase.  At completion, only one in five of 
these tested in volunteers is approved [by Dale E. Wierenge, Ph. D. and C. Robert Eaton, Office of Res earch and 
Development Pharmaceutical Manufacturers Association].  
It should also be considered that inhaler devices a re not interchangeable with regard to pharmaceutica l 
performance and each inhaler needs to be developed for a specific drug propellant formulation.  We que stion 
therefore the compatibility of time needed for deve lopment in the pharmaceutical field with the timefr ame of 
removal of MDI exemption envisaged by the Commissio n’s proposal.  According to the Eurostat, diseases of the 
respiratory system are one of the main causes of death in the EU (they accounted for 7.5 % of all deaths in the EU 
in 2016, not including cancers) and include conditi ons such as COPD, pneumonia or asthma.  Security of  
medication and treatment to these patients is an important action to protect vulnerable members of society.

Ambitious phasedown could lead to competition for supply 
Regarding the applicability of the quota-system to F-gases used in MDIs, the exemption for MDIs for 
pharmaceutical ingredients included in the 2014 EU F-Gases Regulation is coherent with the 85% HFC red uction 
target by 2036 (for developed countries) introduced  by the 2016 Kigali Amendment to the Montreal Proto col. 2   
The pathway set out in the new EU proposal reduces F-gas emissions by up to 96% by 2036.   
During the first three-year-period, 2024 - 2026, a F-gas quota allocation for MDI uses of 100% of the annual 
average quantity placed on the EU27 market in the baseline period 2020 - 2022 is planned. However, in the same 
period, 2024 - 26, the RACHP sector will undergo a phase-down that is even stricter than foreseen by the current 
F-gas Regulation. This could create competition of supply between the pharma and technical F-gas secto rs and 
lead to shortages in one or another starting from 2024. At the minimum there is concern that F-gas prices, which 
have seen recent increases, would continue an upward trend as availability of gas is squeezed. In 2027, both the 
MDI and RACHP sectors would be affected by a signif icant phase down step, thus increasing the competition for 
quota even more. 
In order to avoid a competition for quota between t he RACHP and MDI sector, both quotas should be kept  
separate from each other.    
 
Concluding remarks       
At present, commercially available alternatives for  the current MDI propellants (HFC-134a) and (HFC-22 7ea) do 
not exist, but the Commission proposal is to phasedown the sector by approximately 57% in 2027 (and by approx. 
80% in 2030). This has the potential to put at risk supply of critical medicines.  
The proposal to lift the EU exemption for MDIs has created uncertainty within the propellant industry and 
healthcare sector over supply of critical medicine to patients.  In our view, the speed over changes t o treatment 
should also be driven by clinical considerations an d patient preference.  Any changes to treatment sho uld be 
clinically justified, and switching devices without  patient input and clinician support could have a n egative 
environmental impact via worsening symptom control, in addition to the adverse impact on treatment. 
Daikin Chemicals is supportive of the EU’s climate goals. However:  
- At present no market-ready alternative exists for HFC-227ea pharma; 
- We strongly support that no decision on a phasedown  of HFC-227ea pharma should be taken whilst the 
commercial availability of a suitable alternative i s unknown. When an alternative is available, a 
reformulation and clinical trialing of the medicine with the new propellant would need to be undertaken. 
Since this takes time, we support a postponement of the removal of the exemption for MDIs to 2030;  
- Even after this, imposing a rapid phase-down of cur rent propellants in MDIs must be proportionate and 
to the needs of the patient receiving healthcare; 
- The quota allocated for F-gases used in MDIs and th e quota allocated for F-gases used in RACHP 
(refrigeration, air conditioning and heat pumps) should be kept separate from each other in order to avoid 
the competition of the two sectors; 
- Changes to medical treatment should be driven by cl inical considerations and patient preference. It is  
essential that patients with serious and complex re spiratory diseases are able to benefit from the mos t 
effective medicines tailored to their individual need.

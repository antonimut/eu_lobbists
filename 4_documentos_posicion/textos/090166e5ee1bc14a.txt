 
 
June 2022 
BAXTER RESPONSE  to the EC Proposal for a REGULATION OF THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND OF THE 
COUNCIL on fluorinated greenhouse gases, amending Directive (EU) 2019/1937 and repealing 
Regulation (EU) No 517/2014 
 
Sustainability is a priority for Baxter, and we support the overall objectives of the proposed F-gas regulation. 
As a pharmaceutical and medical device manufacturer, we have been working for years with clinicians and 
hospitals across Europe to provide drugs (both inhaled and intravenous anaesthetic agents) that allow 
clinical treatments to be tailored to specific patient needs. We understand not only the clinical benefits 
but also the environmental challenges posed by inhaled anaesthetic gases, and therefore believe that the 
use of gas capture technology  offers a solution moving forward for reducing negative impacts on the 
environment, without compromising patient care.  
 
Baxter is  committed to delivering  life-saving and sustaining  products, while minimizing environmental 
impact through carbon reduction , resource conservation, responsible procurement and logistics, and 
product innovation.  We work in partnership with hospitals and customers to help them meet their 
sustainability ambitions , in addition to advancing our own environmental commitments , including  to 
achieve carbon neutrality for our direct operations by 2040 and  to reduce absolute Scope 1 and 2 
greenhouse gas emissions  25% by 2030 from a 2020 baseline, aligned with a well -below 2 ⁰ Celsius 
science-based target. 
 
As such, Baxter supports the general objectives of the new EU fluorinated gas (F-gas) proposal aiming to 
prevent additional f-gas emissions, thereby contributing to EU climate objectives and ensuring compliance 
with protocols and obligations related to hydrofluorocarbons (HFCs).  
 
However, we have serious concerns about the inclusion of inhaled anaesthesia gases (sevoflurane, 
isoflurane and desflurane under Annex II, Section 2 ) and specifically the proposed prohibition of use of 
desflurane by January 2026 (Art. 13) . We believe the environmental benefits of this proposed ban are 
disproportionate to the negative impact it could have on clinician choice, patient safety, clinical outcomes 
as well as efficiencies versus the overall contribution to limiting F-gas releases. The proposed ban is indeed 
contrary to the stated objective of achieving this regulation ‘without compromising safety, functionality and 
[energy] efficiency’. Gas capture technology of anaesthetic gases provides a more effective solution to 
protect the environment  while maintaining clinical choice and ensuring the availability of 
different anaesthetic agents, which is vital for optimal patient care and clinical outcomes.  
 
In the forthcoming paragraphs we outline environmental and clinical support of our view.  
 
Environmental Impact:  
 
From the estimated 200 million anaesthetic procedures carried out globally every year, inhaled 
anaesthetics sevoflurane, isoflurane and desflurane combined  that are released into the environment 
have a climate impact of about 0.01% of the carbon dioxide released globally just from the burning of fossil 
fuels (Sulbaek Andersen, 2012). Therefore, their elimination will provide negligible greenhouse gas (GHG) 
reductions, but it will negatively affect anesthesiologist choice, patient safety and recovery.    
 
In addition, we have several questions with regard to the European Commission’s impact assessment: 
 
First, it does not quantify the actual saving potential from the ban of desflurane, which is grouped under 
‘other’, showing a relatively limited effect compared to other measures.

Second, while the impact assessment suggests that intravenous anaesthetic agents could replace inhaled 
agents (IA), the complete environmental impact of switching entirely to total intravenous anaesthesia 
(TIVA), and the resulting environmental burden, has not been quantified. In fact, studies demonstrate that 
TIVA with Propofol may  cause pollution of  water courses/oceans (eg. through improper discharge  and 
patient excretion) and contribute to waste accumulation (eg. single-use consumables) (Allen and Baxter 
2021; Mankes, 2012; White, 2020) . Propofol has a Persistence, Bioaccumulation and Toxicity Score of 
6/10 (Mankes, 2012), and its overall environmental impact  needs to be explored further  before its use 
can be proposed over inhaled agents (Kostrubiak, 2020; White, 2022; Hu, 2021).  
 
In addition, nitrous oxide (N2O), the remaining anaesthesia gas, while not an f -gas and hence not in the 
scope of the EC proposal, has the most significant atmospheric lifetime of the main IA gases with 114 
years, and a GWP of 298, and is not generally captured. Therefore, a shift in usage from inhaled F-gas 
agents to N2O could lead to increased use of another harmful/potent gas (Sulbaek Andersen, 2012). 
 
Clinical Impact:  
 
Baxter is a key provider of both inhaled and intravenous anaesthetic agents. It is important to highlight 
that there is no universal anesthetic agent which suits the need s of all patients . Anesthesiologists 
recommend the best anesthesia option for each patient, depending on state of health, other medical 
conditions and the type of surgery – and so protecting availability and choice of anaesthetic agents is 
important to maintain high quality therapeutic outcomes.  
 
There are several clinical and therapeutic benefits for using desflurane and sevoflurane over other agents 
depending on patients’ and procedural needs. For example, desflurane has been shown to confer benefits 
versus other agents in obese patients (Liu, 2015), during neurosurgery, allowing intra-operative and more 
rapid postoperative neurological evaluation (Magni, 2009; Gökçek, 2015), and in the elderly (Chen, 2015). 
Furthermore, surgical theatre productivity is essential as hospitals continue to address and recover from 
the impacts of COVID-19. Faster and more predictable patient recovery provided by desflurane compared 
to other anesthetic agents may allow for improved patient through-put in the operating room and reduction 
of waiting times for patients (Dexter , 2010: Agoliati, 2010: Watchtel, 2011). In addition, desflurane has 
shown the potential to provide my ocardial protection in patients undergoing cardiac surgery (Bonanni, 
2020), reducing the release of postoperative troponin (Tritapepe, 2007 ) and preserving left ventricular 
function (De Hert, 2003). These specific health conditions, combined with the practical experience of those 
administering these medicines, underline that for patients in certain high-risk groups, the clinician should 
be able to choose the best and safest combination of drugs for the patient.  
Moreover, according to some International and European published surveys, the use of TIVA is far from 
being the preferred anesthetic in clinical practice, with a range of preference that goes from 8% to 36% of 
responders (Benhamou, 2015; Goh, 2019; Koch, 2022; McGain, 2019). A lack of real-time monitoring of 
Propofol concentration (Wong, 2018) and a l ow availability of anaesthesia depth monitoring  (Echeverry-
Marin, 2017) were the main concerns related to use of TIVA; faster induction/awakening times is cited as 
the main reason for choosing desflurane (Benhamou, 2015; McGain, 2019). In this scenario , forcing the 
switch from inhaled anesthesia to TIVA could be problematic. 

 
 
 
In addition, recent supply constraints for Propofol (TIVA), one of the main alternatives to inhaled agents, 
due to an unexpected increase in demand (Socal, 2021) clearly demonstrate the need for the availability 
of a variety of clinical options. These supply constraints occurred during COVID -19’s peak despite having 
desflurane on the market. If desflurane were to be excluded from the market, and if only sevoflurane and 
propofol were used instead, supply constraints, especially during public health emergencies, could 
potentially be even more severe. 
 
 
Gas Capture Technology:  
 
Environmental needs, patient needs, and economic factors  need to be balanced in order to create a 
sustainable anaesthesia practice – something that Baxter and many clinicians globally are fully committed 
to. Our focus on sustainability prompted Baxter to pilot gas capture technology in 2020, in partnership with 
third-party ZeoSys Medical  GmbH. The technology is currently being tested in more than 60 hospitals 
across Europe. In these hospitals, gas capture is preventing more than 99% of the anaesthetic gas exhaled 
by the patient in the operating room from escaping to atmosphere (Baxter data on file, 2022). Independent 
Life Cycle Assessment (LCA)  studies of the technology (eg. production,  transport, gas recovery, etc.) 
demonstrate an 89% reduction in greenhouse emissions associated with gas capture (once regulatory 
approval for reuse is granted), compared to the baseline of venting gas to  the atmosphere (Baxter DoF; 
Costelloe, 2022).  
 
 
 
Baxter believes that gas capture technology for the effective capture, destruction or potential reuse  of 
anaesthetic gases (pending regulatory approval) can improve the sustainability of inhaled anesthesia while 
still allowing clinicians to  choose the type of anaesthetic which is best for the patient in each case. The 
use of this technology can play a key role in helping reduce greenhouse emissions at hospital level and 
help achieve the targets set by the European Commission.  
 
We therefore believe that the C ommission proposal should consider the availability and implementation 
of new technologies (the impact assessment only refers marginally to their existence without providing 
further references or data on their use or benefits to contribute to emission reduction ) with IA instead of 
de-facto prohibiting a whole therapeutic option for anaesthesia  that has important medical benefits, or 
mandating a time-consuming pre-authorization, justification & reporting process, which effectively creates

In conclusion, Baxter supports the overarching goal s of the EC F-gas proposal to reduce emissions and 
achieve the EU’s climate ambitions, but we believe that the impact of removing IA gases as a therapeutic 
option has not received the necessary consideration in terms of potentially harmful impact from a patient 
care perspective and the unassessed environmental impact caused by alternatives, and therefore requires 
further consideration and consultation with medical experts/pan -EU societies, patients and industry. 
Sustainable anaesthesia practice  in accordance  with the global consensus statement (Whi te, 2022) , 
supported by clinician education and implementation of gas capture technologies will reduce GHG 
emissions without compromising clinician choice and patient care.

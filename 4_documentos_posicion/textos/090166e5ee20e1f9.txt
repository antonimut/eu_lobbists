 
 
  Brussels, Belgium (27 June 2022)    The European Commission released its amended proposals for the EU Regulation on fluorinated greenhouse gases (f-gases) and ozone depleting substances (ODS) on Tuesday, 5 April 2022.  The Clean Cooling Coalition welcomes the possibility to send feedback following the release of the EU F-Gas proposal. The Coalition believes that the most effective way to achieve the EU's carbon neutrality goals by 2050, and secure high level of environmental protection, lies in the quick reduction of the use of fluorinated substances.  The Coalition members welcome some improvements of the proposal; however, the members believe that there is still room for greater ambition.  The Coalition welcomes the addition of bans targeting the stationary split air conditioning and heat pump sector. These prohibitions will drive the uptake of climate-aligned equipment, such as systems running with natural refrigerants. These natural refrigerants-based systems not only boast high energy performances, and low life cycle climate performances, but also negligible direct emissions. A market investigation into the Europe-based natural refrigerant-based heat pump segment has found that the industry is ready to scale up as policy measures kick in. The technology is ready and already available on the market, across Europe.   The Coalition also welcomes the further tightening of the HFCs phase down schedule, as well as the alignment of the EU HFCs phase down with the provisions of the Kigali Amendment to the Montreal Protocol. As the production and use of these substances will be reduced, the Coalition is pleased to see in the current EU F-Gas proposal an extension to training and certification programmes on alternatives to f-gases, to streamline the safe handling of natural refrigerants.  Moreover, the addition of 20-year Global Warming Potential (GWP) values alongside the 100-year GWP values will better inform users of f-gases of their short-term effect on climate change. This is of topical importance given the climate crisis we are currently facing; when choosing to deploy a fluorinated refrigerant, stakeholders should be aware of not only its long-term effect on in the atmosphere, but also its short one, as this often more strikingly mirror its climate altering potential.  Finally, the Coalition supports further action to regulate trade of fluorinated substances, with the aim to stop their smuggling.  Nevertheless, the members of the Coalition regret the lack of ambition of the EU F-Gas proposal on other aspects. Given the climate emergency we live in, swift and prompt action on global warming agents such as HFCs contributes to keep the temperature increase within bearable levels, and Europe should lead the path towards the global switch to natural alternatives across all sectors.  The Coalition feels that the current proposal is a lost chance to drive innovation and secure EU leadership worldwide. An increasing number of EU-based manufacturers and end users have already successfully adopted RACHP solutions with natural refrigerants, and ask for higher policy ambition in this space. The lack of the current proposal’s ambition, especially concerning commercial and industrial refrigeration is worrisome: further action can ensure these applications can deliver concretely on the EU climate target by phasing out completely potent greenhouse gases such as hydrofluorocarbons. This should be taken into even more consideration as today any size of stationary commercial and industrial application can be deployed with natural refrigerants, as well as mobile systems.

  More bans, and tighter ones, should be designed across the different sub-sectors, to allow for innovation with natural refrigerants.   The lack of bans in the transport refrigeration sector is worrisome, as the sector is experiencing growth and has high leakage rates. In addition, transport refrigeration systems charged with natural refrigerants are a reality today.  The Coalition members also believe that the exemptions allowed at points 14 and 15 of the EU F-Gas proposal Annex IV are not technologically necessary, as products relying on alternatives to fluorinated greenhouse gases are present on the EU market today.  The Coalition also regrets that the current proposal is a missed opportunity to target the carbon intensity of fluorinated refrigerants production, both within and outside the EU. Not only these substances require more energy to be produced than their natural counterparts, but also imported substances should be manufactured in line with the applying EU environmental standards.  Finally, the members are worried by the EU F-Gas proposal’s lack of specific reference to the work undergoing in the context of the poly- and perfluoroalkyl substances (PFASs) Restriction Intention under the EU Regulation on Registration, Evaluation, Authorisation and Restriction of Chemicals (REACH).   The Clean Cooling Coalition looks forward to engaging in the policy making debate in the coming months to shape an ambitious EU F-Gas Regulation.

Aligning the F Gas Regulation with the objectives of 
REPowerEU  
EdEn welcomes the objective set by the Commission to reduce the GHG emissions associated 
with the use of HFCs.  
 
It is however essential that the revision of F Gas Regulation 517/2014 does not hinder the 
deployment of heat pumps, which is a key aspect of the Commission’s strategy to decarbonise 
the EU building sector. 
 
It must be recalled that the carbon footprint of heat pumps over their life cycle is significantly 
lower than that of other heating solutions, even when assuming that the HFC fluids are only 
partially recovered at the end of life, which is less and less the case. 
 
It must also be noted that the HFC phase down set in Regulation 517/2014 is already 
challenging in itself for the industry as the timeline and objectives were set at time when the 
target for the deployment of heat pumps was much less ambitious than the target that has now 
been set in the REPowerEU plan. 
 
In addition, the switch from HFC fluids to other refrigerants will be a challenge for the industry 
as options are limited: 
• The use of HFOs is restricted under REACH, which is why they cannot be considered 
as an appropriate option for building heating; 
• Natural refrigerants such as CO2 or ammonia do not appear as viable options for 
building heating either because of toxicity issues and poor thermodynamic properties. 
 
With the current proposal, it seems that the Commission assumed that Propane heat pumps 
could be put on the market in the short or medium term, which does not seem realistic for two 
key reasons: 
 
Taking into account the necessary timeline for industry adaptation 
The proposal published by the Commission on April 5th, 2022 strongly reinforces the phase 
down of the maximum amount of HFCs allowed to be placed on the Union market. 
The proposed phase down is not realistic for the heat pump industry as it would clearly require 
a widespread use of propane for hydronic heat pumps and air to air heat pumps of rated capacity 
up to 12 kW, from 2024. 
The deployment of this technology is currently very limited and manufacturers expect that the 
switch from the current technology to Propane heat pumps will require at least 15 years, largely 
exceeding the timeline proposed by the Commission.  
 
Taking into account the safety issues associated with the use of Propane 
Propane is a highly flammable gas (classified A3), which is why indoors usage seems unlikely 
and outdoors usage would require increased safety standards. 
In addition, the handling of propane will require training for installers, which cannot be carried 
out within the time limits provided for in the draft regulation. 
These reasons explain why the industry will need at least 15 years to switch to Propane heat 
pumps. The significantly shorter timeline proposed by the Commission does not seem realistic 
and could endanger the safety of consumers and installers.

Conclusion 
Heat pumps are much more climate performant than other heating solutions available on the 
market, emitting on average over their entire lifespan 4 times less CO2 than a gas condensing 
boiler and 6 times less than an oil boiler.  This is precisely why the European Commission aims 
at doubling the rate of their development. 
 
In order for the revised F Gas Regulation to contribute to this objective, EdEn recommends 
exempting heat pumps (hydronic and air to air) from the quota system implemented in the F -
Gas regulation as the phase down of regulation 517/2014 currently in force is not aligned with 
the RePowerEU objectives.

Feedback to Commission adoption: Review of EU rules on 
fluorinated greenhouse gases 
 
29 June 2022 
 
SUMMARY  
• The Commission proposal applies to  chemicals that are used in or with medical devices, such as 
inhalers, anaesthetic gases, blood bank refrigerators, and centrifugation systems. This includes both 
manufacturers of and products that contain anaesthetic gases; therefore, producers of medical 
equipment that are used to deliver, capture, or monitor anaesthetic gases and the services activities to 
ensure the quality of the product including the patient safety , critical equipment calibration , could be 
impacted. These substances are used in relatively small quantities in industry  and these groups of 
chemicals have limited alternatives.  
 
KEY ASKS 
• Certain applications of F -gases, such as when used in the manufacturing of medical devices, the 
healthcare environment or laboratory testing, should be exempted within the regulation, as the use is 
necessary for patient care and diagnosis. If exemptions cannot be granted, there needs to be clearer 
categorisation for medical technology and associated laboratory equipment.  
• The sector requires clarification on the anaesthetic agents used in the production process or for 
preventive and corrective maintenance of medical devices under Article 13.4 and considers that there 
should be an exception when anaesthetic agents are used by the sector to ensure medical device 
performance and quality. 
• The revision should consider applications of F -gases when no suitable or technically feasible 
alternatives are available or where alternatives to F-gases have not had their environmental impact or 
impact upon healthcare delivery fully assessed.  
• The medical technology sector will require extended transition times to implement design changes and 
not disrupt product availability. A realistic timeframe should consider the stringent safety requirements 
of sectoral legislation and the mandatory involvement of a Notified Body in the certification process.  
The timeframes should consider the extended IVDR transition timelines. 
 
MedTech Europe, the European trade association for the medical technology industry including diagnostics, 
medical devices and digital health welcomes the opportunity to provide feedback on the upcoming review of 
EU rules on fluorinated greenhouse gases.   
 
Applications of F-gases in the medical technologies sector  
F-gases are essential for medical technologies. They are used in refrigerants for in-vitro diagnostic medical 
devices (IVDs) and laboratory systems such as centrifuges, chemical analysers, automation platforms, and 
sample storage, for example:

• Chemistry analysers measure analytes in serum, plasma, urine, and other bodily fluids. Chemistry 
analysers enable the early detection and diagnosis of diseases, for example, enzym e levels for liver 
function tests and urinary creatinine to assess kidney filtration capacity.  
• Centrifugation systems are used to separate liquid and solid components, for example, blood and other 
biological fluids for processing, analysis, and in vitro diagnostic testing. Blood banks depend on consistent 
and accurate centrifuge operation to separate serum/plasma from cellular components.   
 
In addition, F -gases have applications in medical devices , there is a wide range of medical technology 
applications that require precise temperature control (to <1°C accuracy) using pumped fluids rather than 
chilling air spaces, su ch as process chillers and water baths. These applications require a high -capacity 
refrigeration system that exceeds the current hydrocarbon (HC) refrigerant charge limits . Applications  in 
medical devices include: 
• Refrigerated sealed systems : Certain medical technologies depend on a refrigerated sealed system 
that uses a variety of refrigerants; these include professional refrigerators, freezers, ultra-low temperature 
freezers, blood storage, and vaccine storage.  
• Medical anaesthetic devices receive medical gases under pressure (used in combination with oxygen, 
nitrous oxide, and air) to  accurately control the flow of each gas individually. These types of medical 
devices are used in operating rooms to provide general anaesthesia to patients undergoing medical 
procedures. According to  Article 4.2 "Operators and manufacturers of equipment and installations", the 
proposal applies to any product containing gases, indicating that the proposal could impact any medical 
technology manufacturers of anaesthetic delivery machines, vaporisers, and gas capture equipment.  
• Respiratory gas modules and calibration gas: Respiratory gas modules are used i n hospitals and 
clinics to monitor patient breathing gases in critical care environments and operating rooms. They are 
always connected to a host medical device such as a patient monitor, anaesthesia machine, or ventilator. 
For the host medical device, the  gas modules can identify and measure CO2, O2, and N2O gases, and 
Anaesthetic Agents (AA) Sevoflurane, Isoflurane, Desflurane, Enflurane, and Halothane .  
- The gas modules are calibrated at the manufacturing site by identifying and measuring all 
gases. The AAs are used as chemicals and not as inhalable AAs. AAs use and emission are 
optimised during the production process to reduce environmental impact while still ensuring 
required gas identification and concentration measurement accuracy for patient safety. 
- Calibration gas is used globally during preventive and corrective maintenance of gas 
modules to calibrate the gas sensors in the gas modules to correlate with the calibration gas 
containing gas  concentrations. It is a gas mixtu re, usually  containing low volumes 
of Desflurane. 
 
The potential impact of the revision  
The revision of the F -gases regulation could have an impact on the medical technology sector in a number 
of ways , including implementing new equipmen t and compliance, and, for the end -user, limiting clinician 
choice, patient safety and outcomes, hospital productivity as well as supply shortages.   
• In certain cases, the two-year period specified in Article 11 for the supply of spares and consumables is 
inadequate for some companies to sustain their current operations.

• For devices connected to a laboratory ecosystem that includes pre-and post-analytical sample processing 
and analytical sample analysis, replacing a chemistry analyser, c an take months or even years to 
complete, depending on the size and complexity of the hospital and reference laboratory.    
• Companies could experience difficulties to implement the new rules  and ensuring compliance for their 
products in a timely manner . For example, manufacturers of refrigerated centrifuges are subject  to 
stringent regulatory requirements (including IVDR (EU)2017/746), which are necessary to protect 
patients, users and third parties. Given the complex development and approval process, a simple and 
short-term conversion of all products to an alternative refrigerant with GWP 150 is incredibly challenging. 
• In the case of anaesthetic gases, a ban on Desflurane and restrictions on other agents can limit 
physicians' access to the full range of available treatments as there is no universal anaesthetic agent that 
can fulfil the requirements of all patients. Anaesthesiologists recommend the best anaesthesia option for 
each patient based on their state of health, other medical conditions, and the type of surgery. 
Therefore, ensuring the availability and choice  of anaesthetic agents and their delivery devices is 
essential to sustaining high-quality therapeutic outcomes. 
- The requirement to provide proof of the medical necessity to authorities could increase 
bureaucracy for hospital staff. Desflurane is already being phased out of anaesthesia in 
certain cases for  more environmentally friendly gases. One approach to minimise  
anaesthetic emissions could be to use minimal-flow anaesthesia, which could be supported 
by data analytics tools and training. 
• In terms of the environment, a ban on Desflurane and a restriction on other agents could lead to the use 
of other agents with potentiall y unknown implications . The use of environmentally beneficial medical 
devices, such as mandatory gas capture, could reduce the environmental impacts, without compromising 
patient outcomes that an outright ban or limitation on use would cause. 
• Certain companies produce Respiratory Gas Modules and Calibration Gas with advanced technology to 
control and minimise GHG emissions - these are also exported to the global market from the EU. The 
potential restriction or control of use and export requirements could ca use EU manufacturers to lose 
market share in other non-EU regions that can still use Desflurane.  
• Refrigeration systems are used at universities, hospitals, clinics, and laboratories to perform routine 
preventive and diagnostic tests to determine the pati ent's diagnosis and provide actionable data. As 
highlighted previously, the use of a refrigerant maintains the optimum temperature required to preserve 
the analytes' integrity, which can be affected by the heat generated during the centrifugation process. 
Other tests provide results that can be used to make healthcare decisions, allowing for more targeted 
therapy at a lower cost: 
- Diabetes, Endocrinology, Cancer  
- Liver disease, Pancreatic disease 
- Heart disease, Drug Monitoring  
- Cardiac: for the diagnosis or risk assessment of acute myocardial infraction  
- Thyroid disease, Reproductive Endocrinology  
- Bone metabolism, Anaemia, Infectious diseases

Alternatives 
i. Difficulty in finding alternatives 
When considering transitioning to viable alternatives, the medical technology sector could experience  
challenges such as:  
• Undergoing design changes, including using different refrigerants, companies must undertake  and 
document design planning, inputs, outputs, review, verification, validation, transfer, and risk management, 
which is a time-consuming process with numerous steps needing review and approval by regulators .  
• Certain applications, such as refrigerated cen trifuges and larger process chillers, may not have viable 
technical alternatives. New standards are being developed to cover the design issues of using higher HC 
charge limits (e.g., IEC 61010 -2-011). However, once the product standards are published, it w ill take 
time for building codes for the facilities where this type of equipment can be installed to become aligned.  
• Some of the alternatives that fulfil the required GWP are propane-based, which would introduce additional 
risks such as flammability and pressure hazards that are not currently  present in existing technologies. 
Additionally, natural refrigerants or other substances with a lower GWP are not necessarily the most 
suitable technological alternative for refrigerants in products containing cooling units.   
 
ii. Using alternatives 
• Finding a suitable alternative is challenging due to the types of gases currently available and their global 
approval status for certain applications.  
- Some of the new gases are either Ethane or Propane based which would not be acceptable 
for use in Centrifuges. Due to the nature of the design and technology, any failure during 
operation will be catastrophic (generally rotor escape causing serious damag e to the 
containment enclosure), and flammable refrigerants could  significantly increase the danger 
posed by these types of events in a laboratory environment. 
- For systems used to analyse human fluids, longer and more complex validation and 
verification protocols are required to ensu re operational temperatures and pressures are 
maintained throughout the sample's process.  
- Any design changes to allow for the use of alte rnative gases in these types of systems will 
require regulatory requirements that could take up to 7 years to complete and relabelling. 
• Total intravenous anaesthesia (TIVA) is the only alternative to the use of inhaled F-gas agents, although 
it is a relatively new technique that anaesthesiologists could be unfamiliar with , raising both medical and 
environmental concerns. 
- Recent guidelines published by the Association of Anaesthetists on the safe practice of TIVA 
recognise that patients treated with TIVA may require additional monitoring using target -
controlled infusi on pumps in surgery by hospitals, which may compromise patient safety, 
and access to healthcare if these monitoring requirements cannot be met1.

Koura’s Position for Public consultation 
Background 
Pressurised Metered Dose Inhalers (pMDIs) are an essential and well-established platform for the 
delivery of inhaled medicines by patients around the world.  They are cost effective and are suitable 
for the largest number of patients, including young children, older people and patients in an 
emergency situation such as an asthma attack, for whom alternative inhalers are often not suitable. 
Patient preference is recognised as an important factor in ensuring compliance with treatment 
regimens and pMDIs are preferred by many patients.  
Koura led global efforts to phase out the use of ozone-depleting chorofluorocarbon (CFCs) 
propellants in a successful response to the Montreal Protocol and the introduction of current 
propellants, principally Zephex® 134a. We now believe that there is scope for another step change 
reduction in the environmental impact of respiratory treatments and have been working for some 
years to develop and bring to market a new propellant with a significantly reduced carbon footprint. 
 
Zephex® 152a – a sustainable pMDI propellant 
Koura’s new medical propellant is called Zephex® 152a. Transition to 152a from the current 
propellants will achieve a greater than 90% reduction in global warming associated with pMDI use. A 
detailed, peer-reviewed cradle-to-grave environmental impact study, carried out by the University of 
Manchester (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0959652619325934?via%3Dihub), 
shows a 152a-based pMDI to have a total carbon footprint (gas included) comparable to equivalent 
multi-dose Dry Powder Inhalers (DPIs). On an environmental basis, 152a makes pMDI effectively 
equivalent to DPI and other propellant-free delivery platforms whilst retaining all of the attractive 
performance and economic characteristics associated with pMDI. 
152a is not considered a PFAS (per and polyfluorinated alkyl substances) under the latest OECD 
definition and is outside of the scope of the current EU REACH restriction process. 
152a is currently used in a variety of other applications and since 2009 Koura have conducted 
intensive research to determine and establish its suitability for medical use and the environmental 
benefits that it offers over current medical propellants and other propellant-free inhaler platforms 
now available. We have also examined the practical implications for manufacturing and device 
filling. An intensive six-year, multimillion-dollar programme of inhalation safety studies is nearly 
complete These studies included a clinical trial in human volunteers to assess the acceptability of the 
new medical propellant to patients. Results to date show no safety or acceptability compromise with 
respect to the current propellants. The final results are being analysed and reviewed and we are 
confident that Zephex® 152a will be acceptable to the regulators as a safe and sustainable medical 
propellant for patient use going forward. 
Having established the environmental benefits of a transition to 152a and safety in use, major 
investment programmes are currently underway in order to bring inhalers using 152a propellant to 
the market. Our new Zephex®152a production facility, opened in April 2022 in Runcorn UK, will 
provide pharmaceutical companies with commercial scale quantities of medical grade propellant to 
allow them to begin to transition their inhaled medicines to low-carbon 152a-based products. We 
will shortly be able to release final data on the inhalation safety program to establish a Drug Master 
File and Technical Dossier with regulators in the UK, EU and US. These resources are essential tools 
to facilitate pharmaceutical companies progressing their required  regulatory approval processes.

The future of F-Gas Regulations 
The European Commission has published its proposal to amend the F-Gas Regulation. With a 
number of aspects that would impact the medical propellants sector if implemented.  
The EU proposals would remove the current exemption for medical propellants from the overall F-
gas quota phasedown and result in a 58% reduction against the 2022 baseline for 2027 and a further 
20% reduction for 2030. Koura understands and broadly supports the reasons for the removal of the 
medical exemption and for the overall phasedown. Although presenting many challenges, including 
the availability of suitable device manufacturing facilities, we believe that the introduction of 152a 
propellant will make a phasedown on this scale possible. However, we are concerned that the rigid 
schedule set out by the EU may not be compatible with the timescale for the introduction and 
approval of new medical products. Attempting to meet such a precipitate timeline independently of 
a corresponding medical regulatory approval framework timetable runs the risk of patients being 
moved to inappropriate alternatives, the costs of essential treatments rising with an unavoidable 
impact on healthcare budgets, and increases in patient exacerbations (with the associated costs, 
carbon impacts and trauma that these inevitably bring), due a shortfall in availability of life-saving 
essential medications globally. 
Koura would encourage the Environmental and Medical regulators in each jurisdiction to recognise 
the goals, ambitions and regulatory requirements applicable in the pMDI sector and to work 
together to ensure that any environmental targets and timelines are achievable within the medical 
regulatory framework and that this framework recognises the environmental imperative driving the 
carbon-reduction goals.   
Koura would encourage the development of clear operable & achievable safety-net options in the F-
gas Regulation for continued supply of these vital medicines in the event of, for example, delays in 
the medical product approval process.

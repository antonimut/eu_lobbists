SEMI Europe comments on  
the proposal for a Regulation on fluorinated greenhouse gases, amending Directive (EU) 
2019/1937 and repealing Regulation (EU) No 517/2014 
June 2022 
 
Semiconductor device (chip) manufacturing in Europe is a key enabling technology that will underpin 
significant parts of the European Green Deal initiative including smart transport and smart energy. The 
EU Chips Act also recognises the strategic importance of the industry to ensure Digital Sovereignty, 
security of supply, and to maintain the EU’s technological leadership. It is therefore important that 
Green Initiatives, such as the proposed update to the F-Gas Regulation, do not affect the ability of the 
European semiconductor industry, and its associated worldwide supply chain, to compete on the world 
market. 
The processes and equipment used in the manufacture of semiconductor devices require precision 
control of the temperature and humidity to ensure the devices (chips) meet the required electrical 
specification, reliability and manufacturing yield. A variation of <10C can result in loss of the product. 
The process chiller systems used to provide temperature and humidity control are therefore a critical 
part of the semiconductor manufacturing equipment. The process chillers used in semiconductor 
manufacturing typically have a charge size of <40 T CO2 equivalent, operate over a range of 
temperature down to < -500C and have an expected service life of 15-20 years. 
Process chillers are not defined in the F-Gas Regulation, but it seems clear that process chillers must be 
a type of refrigeration equipment. 
Process Chillers are defined in ‘implementing Directive 2009/125/EC of the European Parliament and 
of the Council with regard to ecodesign requirements for professional refrigerated storage cabinets, 
blast cabinets, condensing units and process chillers’, Article 2(f) 
‘process chiller’ means a product integrating at least one compressor and one evaporator, capable of cooling down 
and continuously maintaining the temperature of a liquid in order to provide cooling to a refrigerated appliance or 
system; it may or may not integrate the condenser, the coolant circuit hardware and other ancillary equipment;  
It is not clear if process chillers are to be understood as ‘stationary refrigeration equipment’, particularly 
in regard of the restrictions given in Annex IV for self-contained stationary refrigeration equipment 
(points 13 and 14). Process chillers do not cool products per se, but in our industry are used to cool 
various components in semiconductor manufacturing equipment that come into contact with work-
pieces (semiconductor chip ‘wafers’ in the course of their processing). 
SEMI members recognise that the existing F-Gas regulation has been very successful in reducing the 
damaging effect of high GWP gases entering the atmosphere. The regulation strikes a careful balance 
between encouraging industry to switch to lower GWP gases (where technically possible) and the 
potential negative impact to EU industry competitiveness on the world market. SEMI members fully

endorse the EU’s focus on maintaining this progress, as outlined in the proposed update to the F-Gas 
regulation, but we would like to bring to your attention five areas of concern. 
• Process Chillers for industrial process control are not defined in the F-Gas regulation. 
• Possible conflict in prohibitions for Stationary refrigeration equipment and Self-Contained 
refrigeration equipment. 
• Prohibition for the servicing or maintenance of refrigeration equipment with GWP>2500; 
Removal of the 40T CO2 equivalent charge size. 
• Lack of clarity on the application of some GWP prohibitions to F-Gas mixtures. 
• Phase down and challenges with access to quota authorization. 
 
1) Process Chillers for industrial process control are not defined in the F-Gas Regulation 
There is a need to clarify whether a prohibition applies to F-Gas used in Process Chillers for industrial 
process control that are required to control the temperature of a process and not a product. It is not 
possible to change significant aspects of the process chillers used in the semiconductor industry because 
of the obligation to robustly demonstrate that changes will not impact the ability to produce chips. We 
are also uncertain how a change in F-Gas GWP would impact our process chiller efficiency obligations 
defined in implementing Directive 2009/125/EC. 
2) Possible conflict in prohibitions for Stationary refrigeration equipment and Self-Contained 
refrigeration equipment 
The revised F-gas proposal has introduced a new on the market prohibition (Annex IV (12)) for self-
contained refrigeration equipment that contain fluorinated greenhouse gases with GWP of 150 or more. 
There is no definition of ‘self-contained refrigeration equipment’ in article 3 (Definitions).  
Depending on the chiller configuration some Process Chillers (possibly considered to be Stationary 
refrigeration equipment) used for Semiconductor manufacturing have the potential to be defined as self 
–contained refrigeration equipment leading to a conflict with the placing on the market prohibition 
(Annex IV (13 & 14)).  
The focus of prohibition Annex IV(12) should be on commercial and domestic refrigeration equipment 
that typically use very low GWP gases. This is currently covered by prohibitions Annex IV (10 & 11) for 
HFC’s.  
We would therefore recommend that prohibition Annex IV(12) should define the scope as 
commercial/domestic applications or explicitly exclude Stationary refrigeration equipment. 
3) Prohibition for the servicing or maintenance of refrigeration equipment with GWP>2500; 
Removal of the 40T CO2 equivalent charge size. Article 13, 3 ‘Control of Use’ 
 ‘From 1 January 2024, the use of fluorinated greenhouse gases listed in Annex I, with a global warming 
potential of 2 500 or more, for the servicing or maintenance of refrigeration equipment is prohibited’.

• Chillers with GWP>2500 were only prohibited from 01/01/2020 (Annex IV(13)). The servicing 
and maintenance prohibition (Article 13.3) will apply from 01/01/2024 with an exemption to 
2030 for reclaimed/recycled F-Gas. 
 
Process Chillers supplied just prior to the prohibition in 2020 will therefore essentially become obsolete 
in 2030 with less than 10 years of service life. Replacing a process chiller system in semiconductor 
manufacturing is a complex task requiring a full re-qualification of the equipment and processes. Re-
qualification can take 6-12 months to ensure device reliability (a critical element in automotive devices) 
has not been compromised. 
The time period 2024 -2030 may also be challenging if the supply of recycled/reclaimed F-gas is 
restricted by high demand in the industry. 
 
We would like to request the European Commission engage directly with the EU Semiconductor (Chip) 
industry to review the feasibility of the proposed control of use prohibitions and the retrofit 
requirements this may impose on the semiconductor manufacturing equipment in existing facilities.  
 
4) Lack of clarity on the application of some GWP prohibitions to F-Gas mixtures (refer to Annex 
IV) 
Annex IV, (14) Stationary refrigeration equipment, that contains, or whose functioning relies upon, 
fluorinated greenhouse gases with GWP of 2 500 or more except equipment intended for application 
designed to cool products to temperatures below – 500C 
The F-Gas regulation does not explicitly define fluorinated greenhouse gas with regard to mixtures. The 
regulation generally refers to those gases listed in Annex I, II or III. A mixture is defined as: 
(2) ‘mixture’ means a fluid composed of two or more substances, at least one of which is a substance 
listed in Annexes I, II or III; 
Manufactures of chiller/refrigeration systems have introduced a range of F-Gas mixtures whose GWP 
calculated according to annex VI are <2500. However, these mixtures may contain individual F-Gas with 
GWP >2500, e.g R407c has a GWP of 1774. The mixture contains R-32 GWP 677, R-125 GWP 3500 and R-
134 GWP 1430. 
It is generally assumed that a mixture as defined in annex 3(2) with a GWP of <2500 is also regarded as a 
Fluorinated Greenhouse gas (as used in Prohibition annex IV(14) with GWP <2500 regardless of the fact 
it contains R-125 with GWP>2500. 
It would make for greater clarity if a definition of Fluorinated Green House gas was included in Article 3: 
• Fluorinated Green House Gas: Any gas listed in annex I, II or II or any mixture of such gases 
 
5) Phase down and challenges with access to quota authorization. 
The phase down of quota allocation has a significant effect on the willingness of primary quota holders 
to sell quota authorizations to importers of pre charged equipment. Semiconductor chip manufacturers

in the EU are generally the importer of record of Semiconductor Manufacturing Equipment and are 
therefore required to have quota authorization if they import >100 tonnes CO2 equivalent in any 
calendar year. 
The growth in the EU Semiconductor manufacturing is resulting in many manufactures to exceed the 
100 tonnes threshold and consequently requesting semiconductor equipment suppliers to ship chillers 
empty of F-gas.  
This has significant consequences on the cost of the chillers to EU manufacturers. Chillers are critical 
elements in the performance and process stability of Semiconductor Manufacturing equipment and are 
fully tested prior to shipment. Removal of F-gas prior to shipment and subsequent refilling and re-
qualification during install at an EU customer site incurs a significant cost. 
 
We would like to request that the Commission considers raising the quota authorization exemption 
threshold for pre-charged Process Chillers for industrial process control (possibly considered to be 
Stationary Refrigeration Equipment) to 500T CO2 per calendar year for use in Semiconductor 
Manufacturing applications.

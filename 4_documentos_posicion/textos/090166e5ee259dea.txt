Chiesi’s view on the Proposal for a REGULATION OF THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT 
AND OF THE COUNCIL on fluorinated greenhouse gases, amending Directive (EU) 
2019/1937 and repealing Regulation (EU) No 517/2014 
 
As a B Corp, Chiesi welcomes the EC proposal to review the F -gas regulation as we share 
authorities’ concerns related to the environmental impact of hydrofluorocarbon (HFC) 
propellants used in pMDIs (pressurized metered dose inhaler).  
Chronic respiratory diseases have been identified by the European Commission as one priority 
area for action in its “Healthier Together EU: Non-Communicable Diseases Initiative”1. Patient 
health is at the heart of Chiesi’s current and future therapeutic solutions and we are committed 
to maintaining access to the range of treatment options that patients need globally, while 
striving to minimise the impact of those treatments on our environment and continuing to 
invest in all technological platforms (including both pMDI and DPI – dry powder inhalers). 
In 2019 Chiesi committed €350 million to develop the first carbon minimal pMDI by 2025, 
replacing the current HFC propellant (HFA 134a) with a new propellant with low Global 
Warming Potential (GWP), thereby reducing the carbon footprint of our pMDI products by 
nearly 90%. Chiesi aim at becoming net-zero by 2035. 
 
Phasing down F-gases for medical use 
Chiesi supports the removal of the exemption for F -gases used in MDIs . The exemption’s 
removal should be complemented by the inclusion of a review process and by sound and 
effective safeguard measures  to avoid any risk of jeopardizing the availability of c ritical 
medicines for patients. The development and delivery of innovative healthcare solutions is a 
long and strictly regulated process that requires  careful management  and considerable 
resources to ensure a beneficial outcome for patients and the environment. Chiesi supports 
the inclusion of measures to integrate the uncertainties around clinical development and 
EU/global regulatory approvals of pMDIs using innovative lower GWP potential propellants. To 
this end, Chiesi envisages the creation of an “expert group” focusing on the pMDI transition. 
The group should assess the quotas scenario before the phase down starts and it should build 
on yearly inquiries on the status of the transition. The  group should in volve the European 
Commission, the EMA and other regulatory authorities, pharmaceutical companies, pharma 
grade propellant suppliers and other relevant stakeholders ( scientific societies, patient 
advocacy groups, and national EU regulatory agencies). This evaluation must be taken into 
account before formally triggering the proposed phased -down to secure patients’ access to

Chiesi believes the Regulation should incentivise innovation in health and a complete sector 
transition to medical -grade low-GWP propellants by removing them from the scope of the 
Regulation. In addition, the EU should also prioritise resources to accelerate the regulatory 
pathways for low-GWP inhaled medicine. 
Considering the impact of chronic respiratory diseases such as asthma and COPD and the ir 
growing prevalence, the quota for medical F-gas should only be utilised for medical uses  to 
avoid jeopardizing availability of life saving-medicines. 
Labelling 
Chiesi supports the need for meaningful and reliable information for patients. Labels should 
carefully consider patient implications, including the potential impact on adherence to 
treatments. Propellant type is already reported in product leaflet and package. As labelling of 
medicinal products is tightly regulated by EMA and requires time to change, EMA needs to be 
involved to define the exact information to be provided and the precise timeline for updating 
labels on products and leaflets. Changes to labels need to be designed considering the limited 
space available. 
Europe’s global leadership in pMDIs 
Europe has a leadership position in the manufacturing of pMDIs, their export to the rest of 
the world and the development of innovative solutions. The new regulation should foster this 
positive ecosystem in line with the objectives of a more resilient, autonomous and greener 
biopharmaceutical sector as set in the EU Pharmaceutical2 and Industrial 3 Strategies. The 
absence of any limitation to the import of pre-filled pMDIs to the EU  risks creating an incentive 
for manufacturers to produce MDIs outside of the EU, which could have a long-term negative 
impact on the industrial development of the EU and the achievement of its climate objectives.  
Chiesi believes the F -gas regulation should offer sufficient safeguards regarding unfair 
competition coming from outside t he EU while encouraging the uptake of alternative using 
low or ultra-low GWP propellants. 
Chiesi advocates for the ratification of the Kigali amendment in all regions where it operates.

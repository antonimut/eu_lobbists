Schneider Electric's response to the proposal for a 
regulation on F-gases 
 
Today, distribution networks and installations commonly rely on SF6 gas. Sulfur hexafluoride (SF6) is a man-made 
fluorinated gas (F -gas) that has outstanding dielectric properties, which allows for safe and compact equipment. 
This is critical in many buildings and applications where space is scarce. Thus, 85% of the SF6 produced today is 
used in high (HV) and medium voltage (MV) electrical switchgear along with other gas -insulated electrical 
equipment. The downside is that it is a potent greenhouse gas with Global Warming Potential (GWP) 25,200 higher 
than that of CO2 and produces toxic by-products in the process of current breaking. The gas is estimated to remain 
in the atmosphere for 3,200 years.  
The EU has set itself the goal of reducing its F-gas emissions to contribute to reaching the 55% of GHG 
emissions reductions by 2030, compared to 1990, and net carbon neutrality by 2050. A large part of this is to be 
covered by reducing gases described in the Montreal Protocol - including SF6. The reduction measures initiated 
include limiting the amount of hydrofluorocarbons (HFCs) sold in the EU, banning the use of some F-gases in 
many new types of equipment, and preventing SF6 emissions in electrical switchgear through monitoring, 
maintenance and end-of-life (EoL) gas recovery. Schneider Electric supports the European Commission’s plan 
to effectively reduce SF6 gas. This decision will have a major impact on the timeframe in which electricity-related 
climate targets are achievable. 
Alternative solutions to SF6 already exist. They vary by manufacturer, with some focusing on GWP reduction 
through alternative F-gases. Schneider Electric has chosen to avoid the uncertainties associated with new and 
unproven alternative F-gases altogether and instead use pure air for this green transition. This air-based 
approach is not only safe for people and the planet, but also reduces future regulatory risks.  
Please find below our feedback on the recast of the F-gas regulation:  
We understand the prohibition in annex IV annex IV point 23 (a), (b), (c ), (d) as follows:  
For each product area, after the specified target date,  
• it will not be permitted to install or replace equipment with products containing gases with a GWP ≥ 10 
unless there is no offer on the market, 
• in which case the equipment should contain gas with a GWP≤2000 or if still no offer on the market above 
such value (even SF6) is permitted 
 
Date of prohibition 
Date annex IV 23 (a) and (b) The European Commission proposed to phase out SF6 from MV solutions below 
24kV in 2026 and between 24 and 52 kV in 2030. We fully support the European Commission proposal as we see 
the market will be ready to deploy commercial solutions at the given date and even in advance in order to allow 
homologation and field tests by the main country’s users.  
 
GWP level for prohibition 
Schneider Electric's solution is based on the use of air as a dielectric and vacuum for switching, resulting in a 
GWP value of 0. 
In MV, there are mature alternatives to SF6: ambient air-insulated switchgear or alternative solids, fluids or gases 
for insulation (air, mixture of natural origin gases or mixtures using Fluoroketon, a fluorinated alternative listed in 
Annex III with a GWP of 0,29). For switching Vacuum is the state of the art. A fixation for a GWP<10 despite 
existing alternatives with a GWP close to 0 would be a setback. 
To be in line with the objective of F-gas regulation, it must promote the best solutions available on the market 
from the point of view of sustainability, while taking into account the possibility for end users to be supplied by 
different producers. For this reason, the GWP restriction in annex IV 23 (a) and 23(b) should be lowered to a 
GWP<1 instead of 10.

Substances listed in annex III 
Include the substances listed in annex III into clauses 1 of Art. 4 (prevention of emission), Art. 5 (Leak checks), 
Art. 6 (Leakage detection systems), Art. 7 (Record keeping), Art 8. (Recovery and destruction), Art. 9 (Producer 
responsibility schemes), Art. 10 (Certification and training) and Art. 12 (Labelling) to effectively prevent emission 
of these new substances introduced on the market. As a precautionary measure, these fluorinated substances 
(e.g. Fluoroketone or Fluoronitrile) that can be either with high GWP and/or with unknown effect due to their 
decomposition product in the atmosphere shall be monitored on the same level as SF6. 
 
Clarification needed on the term “installation and replacement” (Annex IV, (23))  
Annex IV is about “Placing on the market prohibitions referred to in Article 11 (1)”. There is a confusion possible 
between “placing on the market” and “installation and replacement”. In the electrical switchgear business, 
“Installation” is addressing new installations or the replacement of the equipment of existing installations. 
Replacement is not defined in Article 3 and is used only once in the whole document including annexes. 
Schneider Electric suggests replacing “Installation and replacement” of the following electrical switchgear:” by 
“Electrical switchgear for installation:”. 
 
Clarification needed for the process giving the evidence that no suitable alternative is available 
based on technical grounds (Annex IV, (23), note 2)  
Regarding note 2 of Annex IV “The evidence referred to in point 23, shall include documentation establishing that 
following an “open call for tender” no suitable alternative on technical grounds, given the demonstrated 
specificities of the application, was available that could meet the conditions set out in point 23. The documentation 
shall be kept by the operator for at least five years and shall be made available to the competent authority of the 
Member State and to the Commission, upon request” 
Our understanding of “Open call for tender” does not correspond to the practices in electrical switchgear markets 
(including both Industry and Utility). In order to avoid confusion, Schneider Electric suggest to remove “open call 
for “and leave “tender” in the note 2. 
 
Art 11 , §4 to become a delegated act   
To ensure uniform application of the rules and a level playing field, Member States should not be given to 
derogate from legislation by implementing acts. The exemption to allow the placing on the market of products and 
equipment listed in Annex IV (Art. 11 §4) should instead follow the spirit of law by replacing implementing by 
delegated acts.  
 
Additional points than can be better reflected  
Answering the Public Consultation initiated by EC end of 2020, and based on two empirical studies conducted by 
the Fraunhofer Institute and the Grenoble School of Management, Schneider Electric demonstrated that MV grid 
carbon neutrality can still be achieved by 2050 through the following three policy actions supported by three policy 
enablers : 
1. Install  only SF6 -free switchgear starting in 2025  
2. Begin the replacement of the existing SF6 switchgear installed base in 2025 in order to replace 
all SF6 MV switchgear s by 2050  
3. Strengthen end -of-life regulation of SF6 equipment  
Enabled by:  
✓ Ensuring  that alternative solutions are with GWP <10 or close to 0, up to 52kV at least  
✓ Financial in centives that place a cost on SF6 emissions  
✓ A label that certifies green switchgear use

Conclusion  
As electrifica tion rises across Europe, so too does the need for more switchgear. New renewable 
generation installations also require switchgear to connect to the grid, as well as many commercial and 
industrial electrical installations.  Beyond already existing SF6 alternatives that are market -ready, 
customers appear to be anticipating change and are willing to move to those alternatives . All new 
installations of switchgear for wind, PV, E -vehicle charging stations will be connected to the distribution 
grid. It is therefore critical to ensure that alternative solutions up to 52 kV at least will use a gas or gas 
mixture with GWP < 10 . Now is the time to speed up transition to green power tec hnologies as the window 
on a + 1.5 °C future rapidly closes .

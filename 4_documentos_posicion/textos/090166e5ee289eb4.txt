ABB comments on the draft revision of 
EU Regulation on fluorinated green-
house gases 
 
– ABB welcomes the opportunity to provide its feedback and recommendations for the European Com-
mission's proposal to review the Regulation on fluorinated greenhouse gases (F-Gas regulation). 
 
– We strongly support the effort to substantially reduce potent and harmful greenhouse gas emissions 
by two-thirds by 2030 compared to 2014 levels.  
 
– More specifically, ABB welcomes the proposed framework designed to phase out the use of Sulfur Hex-
afluoride (SF6) in electrical equipment due to its extremely high environmental impact and Global 
Warming Potential (GWP). 
 
– As one of the market leaders in Medium Voltage (MV) switchgears, ABB is fully committed to develop 
and roll out SF6 -free products in the MV electrical switchgears market segment . Therefore, we wel-
come the Commission’s proposal to define the dates of SF6 prohibition in switchgears based on volt-
age levels. The ambitious roadmap will help accelerate the development and rollout of SF6-free switch-
gears while providing a realistic timeline for manufacturers to prepare for the transition.  
 
Before the legislative considerations on the new F-Gas Regulation begin in the EU institutions, ABB would 
like to draw attention to several elements of the legislation that require further clarification, as well as 
propose the following recommendations:  
 
• The definition of “placing on the market” should be clarified to reflect different commercial mar-
ket circumstances, e.g.: 
 
o The definition of “placing on the market” (Article 3, para. 6) refers to the physical handover 
of products. In contrast, Annex IV on placing on the market prohibitions  refers to the in-
stallation and replacement of electrical switchgears. In reality, there could be a significant 
time gap between these two moments, e.g., when a Distribution System Operator (DSO) 
orders a number of switchgears and installs them at a much later date whe n a specific 
building is ready. At the same time, the delivery time agreed in the contract might be im-
pacted by non -foreseeable impacts, e.g., raw material shortage, the extended delivery 
time of electronics or delay in the erection of the substation build ing. This could lead to 
uncertainties in the market for users, especially if the prohibition date falls between the 
handover of the product and the installation date or even after. To provide more certainty 
to market operators, we recommend the definition of “placing on the market” to refer 
to the contractual delivery date. 
 
o Framework contracts are a common commercial practice in the MV switchgear market , 
e.g., when a user orders a certain number of units, which are then delivered in batches 
over a certain period, sometimes exceeding several years. Since placing on the market is 
a physical handover of products, this could create uncertainties for users  when

prohibition dates are within the duration of the framework contract or the validity of ex-
emptions change during that time. 
 
o The relationship between the definitions “export” (Article 3, para. 6) and “placing on the 
market” is uncertain. In the MV switchg ear market, producers often export products 
through system partners within the EU , e.g., switchgears installed in other products or 
equipment, such as windmills. However, under the current definition, the producer would 
not be able to deliver switchgears c ontaining SF6 to its system partners after the prohi-
bition date, even though it is not intended to be placed on the EU market.  
 
o Users of MV switchgears would greatly benefit from clarifying that the current definitions 
and the scope of the prohibition do not cover the installed base and allow for the repairs 
and maintenance of currently installed products. It would assure users that maintenance 
services and spare parts will remain available after the prohibition dates.  
 
• Article 26 para. 1 c urrently reads as <…> hydrofluorocarbons or quantities exceeding one metric 
tonne or 100 tonnes of CO2 equivalent of other fluorinated greenhouse gases <…>. We believe the 
intention was to refer to  <…> hydrofluorocarbons of quantities exceeding <…>. This change 
would be in line with the paragraph 4 of the same article.  
 
• Article 10 para. 6 of the proposal requires Member States to establish certification programmes 
for undertakings carrying out installation, servicing, maint enance, repair or decommissioning of 
the equipment containing fluorinated greenhouse gases and other relevant alternatives  to f -
gases. While the list of f-gases is provided in the annexes of the proposal, the definition of “ rele-
vant alternatives” in the context of mandatory certification is less clear. Since numerous alterna-
tives to f-gases have varying degrees of environmental footprint or health impact, the users of 
such alternatives would greatly benefit from further clarification on which products fall within the 
scope of this requirement.  
 
• ABB believes that the regulation of High Voltage (HV) switchgears should be distinguished from 
Medium Voltage (MV) switchgears due to the former’s unique applications and different inven-
tory. Over the recent years, the producers of HV switchgears achieved significant breakthroughs 
in developing SF6-free insulating gases for their products, falling substantially below the GWP of 
2000. The F-Gas Regulation should further incentivize the rollout of such technologies and accel-
erate the phase-out of SF6 in the HV switchgears market. Therefore, we recommend removing the 
lower level of GWP 10 and only us ing GWP 2000 to replace SF6 in this segment of switchgears . 
Otherwise, the legislation risks reducing the diversity of alternative solutions developed by man-
ufacturers and severely impacting product availability. This could undermine and delay the rollout 
of SF6-free technologies and Europe’s transition to a green electricity-based system.

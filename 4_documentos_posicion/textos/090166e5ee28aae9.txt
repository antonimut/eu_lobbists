Honeywell manufactures and supplies low-global warming molecules for refrigeration/air-conditioning systems and heat 
pumps, blowing agents for building insulation foams, aerosols for personal and household care, and solvents widely used in 
high-tech industries. These molecules enable superior energy efficiency, lower costs of electricity usage, safety, easy 
maintenance, low environmental impact and are a requisite to reach the EU “Fit for 55” decarbonization objectives.  
 
Honeywell products contribute meaningfully to the safe and efficient functioning of many modern applications such as 
chillers, heat pumps and air conditioning systems for residential, industrial and commercial buildings as well as cars and 
trucks. As a result of evolving technology and constant investment, Honeywell’s products are already available to support the 
market in meeting the emission reduction targets of the proposed regulation. 
 
It is essential that the F-Gas Regulation maintains a high level of climate protection while ensuring European citizens continue 
to access the benefits of cooling and heating, insulation, solvents and aerosols.  
 
We are broadly supportive of the Commission’s proposal. The current F-gas Regulation has been a success. It improved 
containment, reduced emissions and resulted in the adoption of lower GWP F-gases. The revised regulation must maintain 
this approach.  
 
• The future revision must be aligned with other key initiatives such as Green Deal and REPowerEU. This is not visible in the 
current proposal. Nor is its linkage with the Energy Efficiency First Principle. 
• Honeywell is strongly supportive of the extension of measures to stop illegals and improve containment including 
reporting, training and certification, as well as end-of-life treatment to all F-gases, including HFCs, HCFOs and HFOs. All 
containment measures should apply to non-fluorinated refrigerants as we believe there should be an equal treatment 
policy. At the moment, only the training provisions were extended in the Commission proposal to these refrigerants. 
• The proposal further appears to put at risk the planned transition, including the continuous supply of product to the 
market. We are hearing from the market that the phase down timelines are very challenging. This is particularly relevant 
given the REPowerEU deployment push on heat pumps. 
• The phase down also assumes that alternatives to F-gases are readily available for heat pumps and air conditioning and 
can completely change over to alternatives by 2027. This is not the case, Honeywell believes the market should continue 
to have access to a full choice of refrigerants 
• It is important to understand that all existing installations such as air conditioners and heat pumps will need to be 
maintained beyond 2027. Therefore the future regulation must not require that any such equipment is prematurely taken 
out of service. The lack of clarity on definitions and certain provisions appears not to guarantee this. 
To conclude, the current F-gas review proposal misses an opportunity to complement the EU’s energy efficiency and Net Zero 
goals and to have more ambitious emission reduction targets by expanding the future Regulation to all refrigerants.  
In places, it goes against the existing Regulation philosophy suggesting an inherent (and possibly unintended) technology bias. 
It also leaves the wider market with little room to maneuver on business decisions and planning. The market needs more 
certainty on outlook in the future regulation. This will ensure continued investment and allow the sector to contribute 
technology and innovation for a sustainable future in the EU.  **ENDS**

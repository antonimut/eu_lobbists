Position Paper on the text Proposal  
for the revision of the EU F-Gas Regulation 
 
 
The revision of the regulation 517/2014 EU called " F -Gas II " must  find a balance 
between the climate impacts related to fluorinated greenhouse gases and the 
achievement of the EU's climate and energy efficiency goals. 
 
Founded in 2013, ADC3R members are key players in the distribution, collecting and treatment of 
refrigerants in Europe. We fully support the F -Gas review as well as other related EU climate change 
and decarbonisation initiatives, such as Green Deal, Fit for 55 and most recently REpowerEU. 
 
Fluorinated greenhouse gases are used in many crucial sectors: food processing (e.g. agriculture, 
convenience stores, supermarkets, storage, deep -freezing, etc.), health (e.g. hospitals, blood banks, 
vaccine storage, emergency rescue tents, MDI s, etc.), renewable energy (e.g.: wind turbines, heat 
pumps, ...), the cold chain (succession of logistical stages that maintain products and foodstuffs at a 
specific temperature to preserve their sanitary, gustatory and nutritional qualities from production to 
consumption), transport, digital technology (e.g. datacenter, ...), high and medium voltage electricity 
... , foaming agent to insulation, spray, extinguishing agents, which are key to achieve Europe’s climate 
and energy goals. 
 
According to the Annu al European Union greenhouse gas inventory and inventory report
1,    
Fluorinated gas emissions account for 2.7 % of total EU GHG emissions as a result of the continuous 
efforts of the entire sector for several years. Refrigeration and air conditioning, the largest key 
category, accounts for 81 % of fluorinated gas emissions in 2020. 
In addition, the sum of fluorinated emissions has decreased significantly since 2016.

The current EU F-Gas Regulation requires the RACHP industry to reduce HFC consumption by 88% in 
2030 compared to 2015 and has become the effective gold standard worldwide for reducing  F-gas 
emissions. The first impact on overall F-gas emissions has already been reported with a reduction of 
13% emission in 2019 since its entry into force 2 and a reduction of HFCs supply by 47% in the same 
time period. 
According to the European Environmental Agency report3, EU consumption of HFCs in 2020 was 52% 
below the maximum imposed by the Montreal Protocol’s Kigali Amendment. 
The EU remains on track under the HFC phase-down phase of the EU F-gas Regulation: EU-wide placing 
on the market of HFCs in 2020 was 4% below the market limit.  
 
The F-Gas review must build on these successes by taking into account the following points: 
- further reduce emissions from F-Gases 
- Technology-neutral solutions 
- Circular economy 
- consider the drive for decarbonising of heating as well as for EU energy independence 
- Energy efficiency 
- foster the EU as a centre of production and export base for sustainable RACHP equipment. 
- Harmonization of the application of the regulation and sanctions in the member states. 
- Safety of people and equipment 
- Quotas & illegal fluids 
 
Against this background, ADC3R welcomes many of the provisions of the European Commission's 
proposal
4, in particular the extension on requirements on leak checks, containment, reporting and 
certification and training to all refrigerants (fluorinated or not), F -Gas end -of-life management by 
promoting recovery, reclamation, strengthening traceability of movements and fighting illegal trade. 
 
Given the central role of F -Gas distributors / reclaimers in achieving the EU's climate and energy 
objectives, we would like to express our strong concerns about certain hypotheses and propositions 
that seem unrealistic, unproductive and antinomic with the European global climate objectives in 
terms of capacity, available technology and deadlines.  
In short, we believe that it does not provide the technical justification to support the policy options 
proposed in the as Green Deal, Fit for 55 and especially the REpowerEU not taken into account because 
published after the text was published. Given that very insufficient information on modelling has been 
made available, the following points summarise our main concerns  the HVACR (Heating, Ventilation, 
Air Conditioning, Refrigeration) market.

Measuring climate impacts of substances: 
The international standards for measuring climate impacts (GWP) of F -Gas are based on the Fourth 
Assessment Report adopted by the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change. 
In order to maintain coherence and the possibility of comparing the efforts of each party, it seems 
necessary to us not to mix (4th or 6th depending on the substance) the types of measurement and to 
remain on the Fourth Assessment Report adopted by the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change. 
 
Definition: 
Some definitions are missing. It is essential to ensure clear definitions for legal c larity and the 
implementation of future rules. The following definitions need to be added: 
- Fluorinated gases - use the definition from the current F-Gas II 
- Heat pump 
- Split air-conditioning and split heat pump 
- Self-contained equipment: The term “self -contained” is not defined. It is basically a catch -all 
term which would include any refrigeration equipment, such as transport refrigeration or even 
chillers used for refrigeration. Such a widespread ban would not be realistic within the 
proposed timeframe. 
- perfluorocarbons’ or ‘PFCs- use the definition from the current F-Gas II 
- Plug-in 
- Technical aerosol - use the definition from the current F-Gas II 
 
Safety Standards 
It is unclear why the safety exemption, which the European Commission proposed in bans on split air-
conditioning and split heat pump equipment, would not also be applicable to all equipment, as they 
may also face safety limits as well. 
 
A proposed HFCs phase down which is more like a HFCs phase out by 2027 that would 
seriously jeopardize an accelerated and comprehensive heat pump roll out in the EU in the 
coming years. 
 
The new baseline of 176 million tons of CO2e (average of 2011-2013), compared to the current baseline 
of 181 million tons of CO 2e (average of 2009-2012), incorporates new needs: Metered Dose Inhalers 
(MDIs), which would account for 10 million tons of CO2e in 2020, and export activities. 
The drastic reduction of these quotas risks inciting European exporting industries to move their means 
of production outside the European zone.

well as for after-sales and maintenance.

The EU needs millions of new heat pumps to be installed quickly to fulfil its 2030 climate targets. This

ADC3R recommends a more measured phase -down approach, taking  into account the realistic 
transition time for the sector, the ambitions of the European Green Deal and the acceleration of 
REPowerEU. 
Keeping the current phase-out in place until at least 2030 would ensure that sufficient refrigerants 
are available to allow for the necessary deployment of heat pumps. 
 
Moreover, the new inclusion of MDIs in the European quotas poses a significant risk to the 
pharmaceutical industry and to the health of European citizens. The average time for testing and 
validation of new propellants is 10 years, with deployment time to be counted in addition.  
As the health authorities have not yet given their approval on the currently tested propellants, there 
is a real risk of shortage of certain inhalers. 
 
As is the case in the current F-Gas II, ADC3R suggests excluding HFCs used in MDIs from the European 
HFCs quota.

The product bans below would have an impact on the development and technology of heat pumps 
which is essential for EU building decarbonisation and REPowerEU ambitions. In the context of building 
renovations, AIR to AIR heat pumps are a key contributor to the decarbonisation of heating systems. 
No heat pump technology (neither air-to-air nor hydronic) should therefore be subject to bans in this 
revision. Such a removal would not be in contradiction with the overall HFC phase down and the EU 
commitment for the Montreal Protocol. It is essential that the necessary HFC quota are made available 
to all heat pump  manufacturers, which need the time for a proper phase down while providing for 
affordable heat pump solutions in all EU member states. 
Specific bans on heat pumps would be detrimental to the objectives of REPowerEU and must be 
removed. 
• Annex IV (17): Plug- in room and other self -contained air -conditioning and heat pump 
equipment that contain fluorinated greenhouse gases with GWP of 150 or more – from 2025 
The term “self -contained” is not defined. It is basically a catch -all term which would include any 
refrigeration equipment, such as transport refrigeration or even chillers used for refrigeration. Such a 
widespread ban would not be realistic within the proposed timeframe 
• Annex IV (18b) Split systems of a rated capacity of up to and including 12 kW containing, or 
whose functioning relies upon, fluorinated greenhouse gases with GWP of 150 or more, except

The ban concerns any split system -  single or multi- split, regardless of its vector (air, water, direct

efficiencies and installation considerations for all these vectors, types (single and multi -split) and 
capacity ranges. Thus, we question how the proposed capacity (cooling and/or heating) was 
determined. 
• Annex IV (18c): Split systems of a rated capacity of more than 12 kW containing or whose

Moreover, the placing -on-the-market-bans of products and equipment now also include “parts 
thereof”, which would impact spare parts and other components and thus make the repair and 
upgrading of existing equipment impossible. This is contradictory to the EU Circular Economy Strategy 
and EU Sustainable Product Initiative (SPI). 
 
ADC3R proposes that, in view of the overall objectives and constraints, to 
- Delete the prohibitions in item 18 (a) and (b) and adapt the prohibition in item 18 (c) by no 
longer making reference to either the capacity or the refrigerant load, i.e: Stationary single 
split system air conditioning and single split system heat pump equipment that contain or 
rely on fluorinated greenhouse gases with a GWP of 750 or more, except where such gases 
are necessary to meet safety standards - 01/01/2027.  
 
- Add a ban related to Air Conditioning Appliances Cooling only, i.e. : Fixed Cooling only single 
split or multi split air- conditioning equipment containing or relying on fluorinated 
greenhouse gases with a GWP equal to or greater than 150, except reclaimed HFC 
refrigerants unless such gases are necessary to meet safety standards by 01/01/2027. 
 
We also propose to amend the bans on refrigeration (Annex IV [13] and Annex IV [14]) by revising 
the GWP limit to 1500 except reclaimed HFC refrigerants. 
 
ADC3R welcomes the removal of the 40 tCO2e limit from 1 January 2024 on the use of fluorinated 
greenhouse gases with a GWP of 2,500 or more for the servicing or maintenance of refrigeration 
equipment; except until 1 January 2030 for reclaimed or recycled fluorinated refrigerants. 
 
The extension of provisions to effectively prevent emissions are welcome and should go 
further 
 
ADC3R welcomes that Member States promote the recovery, recycling, reclamation and destruction 
of fluorinated fluids. 
 
ADC3R also welcomes the proposed extension of the requirements to HFOs but has questions on the 
proposed thresholds and recommends to widen the scope to all types of refrigerants, including non -
fluorinated alternatives. This would have numerous benefits in terms of environmental protection, 
safety, and energy efficiency thus indirect emissions.

Furthermore, ADC3R welcomes the extension of the EU training and certification requirements to 
HFOs. 
However, the use of non -fluorinated refrigerants does not require any certification. This is a missed 
opportunity given the potential for high flammability (hydrocarbons ) or high pressure (CO2) or highly 
toxic (ammonia). From a safety and energy efficiency point of view, certification to handle such 
refrigerants is essential. 
In addition, there are two possibilities for calculating the frequency of leakage checks dependin g on 
whether it is an HFC (in tCO 2e) or an HFO (in Kg). This distinction is confusing for the control 
requirement and could even change the control frequency for existing systems. The majority of low 
GWP refrigerants are HFC/HFO blends. It is therefore necessary to clarify this point.

Reducing F -Gas emissions from life -saving inhaled medicines in a 
responsible way    
AstraZeneca supports the goals of the proposed EU F -Gas Regulation, recognising the significant positive impact 
it could have on climate and health.1 It is critical, however, that the draft regulation evolves to avoid the risk of 
limiting life-saving inhaled medicines used by people living with asthma and chronic obstructive pulmonary 
disease (COPD).  
   
AstraZeneca is committed to accelerating the delivery of net zero carbon healthcare.2 As part of this, the Company 
is investing heavily to complete a transition of its pressurised metered dose inhalers (pMDIs) to  use propellants 
with near-zero global warming potential (GWP).3 This major effort is an essential part of the Company’s flagship 
Ambition Zero Carbon  sustainability commitment.4 Through this programme, AstraZeneca is on track to reduce 
greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions from our global operations by 98% by 2026.4 Our science-based target is to halve 
our entire value chain footprint by 2030, on the way to a 90% reduction by 2045 to become science -based net 
zero.4 For all residual emissions, our aim is to become carbon negative from 2030.3 
 
PMDIs use F -Gases as the propellant 5 to deliver a range of life-saving medicines which are an important 
therapeutic option for many patients with respiratory diseases.6-9 These products answer to specific medical needs 
and include several medicines listed as essential by the World Health Organization.10  
 
Transitioning pMDIs to other gases takes time due to clinical work and safety assessments  
 
Climate-friendly propellants are still in clinical development for medical use.3 AstraZeneca is the first company to 
have announced results from Phase I clinical studies. 3 Because medicines development concerns human health, 
transitioning to new propellants for medical use is complex. This involves process validation and pre-clinical and 
clinical studies to ensure the safety and efficacy of the propellants. The propellants are used to reformulate 
existing approved medicines into low-GWP inhaled medicines. They are subject to regulatory review and approval 
by the European Medical Agency (EMA) and other regulators worldwide. Timelines, historically, vary per medicine 
and regulatory jurisdiction.  
 
The EU pMDI manufacturers estimate that the global transition of inhaled medicines to low-GWP pMDIs will take 
at least until 2030.11 
 
Safeguard respiratory patients’ access to their inhalers 
 
The proposed EU F-Gas regulation does not provide sufficient time to complete this transition. It suggests steep 
reduction of the available quota for medical F -Gas as of 2027 by 58%. 12 This risks a shortfall in availability of 
medical F-Gas for producing life-saving inhaled medicines in the EU and creates significant uncertainty for people 
living with respiratory diseases and healthcare professionals not only in the EU but globally.  
 
The scale of the EU’s policy choice and its potential impact on respiratory care is hard to overstate. Respiratory 
diseases disproportionally affect the young, 13 the old,13 and the economically disadvantaged. 14 To put the draft 
regulation into context, n early 60 million people in Europe 15 and approximately 550 million people globally  live

with chronic respiratory diseases, including asthma and COPD, with increasing prevalence.13 In 2016, 339,00016 
people in the EU died from respiratory diseases. These diseases account for approximately 4.1 million deaths per 
year globally.17 
 
Inhaled medicines are the most used medicine in respiratory care  globally.18 In 2021, 70% of all individual 
inhalations of respiratory medicine s delivered in the EU5  (France, Germany, Italy, Spain and Poland) 19 were via 
pMDIs, and this figure rises to 78% globally.19 
 
There is a significant clinical need for pMDIs which are important therapeutic options. 6-9 Familiarity with device, 
limited lung function, young or advanced age are all important considerations when choosing the type of inhaler 
that best suits the patient.6-9  
 
Loss of access to the inhaler of choice forces switching of patients to alternatives. Switching patients to alternative 
inhalers (for example, dry powder inhalers - DPIs) should not be a solution to the potential short of medicines in 
the draft regulation. Switching without clinical need may be associated with poor health outcomes.20-23 Poor 
disease control is a potentially life-threatening situation.24,25 Further, data show that uncontrolled asthma patients 
have a higher carbon footprint , which includes increased healthcare resource utilisation requiring GP visits and 
hospital care resulting from asthma attacks. Maintaining patient access during the transition to next -generation 
inhalers is, therefore, critical for the health of both patients and the environment. 
 
Even amongst the patients where switch to an alternative may be safely ach ieved, current supply and 
manufacturing capacity of DPIs are unlikely to make up for the pMDI shortfall within the timelines proposed by 
the draft regulation.    
 
Safeguard patient access worldwide and foster strategic EU-based MDI innovation and manufacturing 
 
As a leader in global respiratory medicine manufacturing, the EU has taken the responsibility to supply these life-
saving medicines to patients globally. Currently, 349 million inhaled medicines are manufactured in the EU each 
year and the vast major ity are exported. 26 The regulation is also likely to impact s ome low-, middle- and upper 
middle-income countries where patients rely heavily on pMDIs ,27 as typical practices mean these countries  will 
likely be the last to complete regulatory reviews of low-GWP inhaled medicines.  
 
The EU-based industry holds a key position in the global supply chain for this segment of life-saving pharmaceutical 
products. A 2021 survey of IPAC members showed that 75% of member companies’ volume manufactured within 
the EU was exported in 2020 to the rest of the world.26 The final EU F-Gas regulation should preserve world-class 
EU pMDI innovation and sustainable production, in line with Europe’s Pharmaceutical and Industrial strategies. 
 
AstraZeneca requests that the EU work in partnership with  the healthcare community, patients and industry 
toward solutions that avoid potential unintended consequences of this proposed regulation.  These include, an 
F-Gas phase-down schedule which protects patient safety and support the transition to low-GWP alternatives, 
whilst meeting carbon reduction targets by 2030.28 
 
Specifically, there is need for the EU to:  
3 
 
o maintain the proposed quota reference values for medical F -Gas in 2024-2026 through 2030 to ensure 
continuity of care globally without compromising EU GHG emissions reductions targets 
o support efficient regulatory pathways for low-GWP inhaled medicines towards a successful transition 
o regularly review the state of transition to low-GWP pMDIs and efficiently enact safeguards in case of the 
phase-down schedule causing disruption 
 
Any climate regulation has a social duty to safeguard patient safety and reduce risk of increased GHG emissions 
for the duration of the transition to 2030.  
 
AstraZeneca looks forward to  working collaboratively with the EU, health authorities, and the respiratory 
community to deliver propellants with near -zero GWP efficiently, whilst adhering to the highest standards of 
patient safety, and ensuring medicine availability and patient access are maintained. We stand ready for dialogue 
to ensure this regulation comes w ith achievable implementation timelines for people living with respiratory 
diseases.

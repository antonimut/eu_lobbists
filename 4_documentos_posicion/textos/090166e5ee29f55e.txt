 
Chemours F-Gas Regulation 
Revision Proposal Response 
  
As a leading producer of the next generation of low -climate impact F -Gas Refrigerants 
(HFOs), Chemours welcomes the Commission’s proposal for a revision of the F -Gas 
Regulation that will drive the mark et towards refrigerants with low global warming potenti al 
(GWP).  
However, we believe that the proposed HFC quota phase -down is too steep, and risks 
undermining the goals of both RePowerEU and Fit for 55 and, especially, the switch to energy-
efficient, low-climate impact waste -heat recovery applications includ ing: heat pumps, retail 
store heating, and industrial waste recovery conversion for district heating, all of which support 
the key objectives of RePowerEU by moving heating and cooling away from fossil  fuel use 
and towards energy -efficient solutions. The u ltra-low GWP HFOs are essential to the 
decarbonization of European heating, as a significant part of the HFC emissions reductions 
achieved through the F-Gas Regulation comes from switching to lower GWP F-Gases1.  
Indeed, the Commission’s own Impact Assessment and supporting study for the Evaluation 
recognizes that, under the proportionate action scenario2, alternatives, such as propane, are 
not viable due to lack of qualified, trained installers, avail ability of components and safety 
concerns, which are addressed in regional and national safety regulations and standards3. In 
addition, safety limitations preclude the use of flammable alternatives to F -Gases in several 
applications essential to reaching the Green Deal goals, such as building renovation.   
Chemours strongly supports the Commission’s intention to step up enforcement of measures 
to combat illegal trade in F -Gases. However, given that illegal trade already makes up a 
significant part of the F-Gas market, the proposed HFC quota phase-down schedule will create 
an even greater incentive for the trade in illegally imported F -Gases. While the proposed 
measures on enforcement in the proposal, including integration with the Single Customs 
Window, dedicated customs offices to customs clear F-gases and material containing F -
gases, a ban on import, supply and export of non -refillable containers, as well as stricter 
controls on transits of F -gases through the EU are all welcome, the proposed quota phas e-
down does not allow sufficient time for e ffective and harmonized implementation in Member 
States. Likewise, while Chemours does not oppose quota fees in principle, their effect will be 
to drive more of the market towards illegal trade in the short-term while enforcement measures 
are either lacking or lagging.  
Below follows Chemours main concerns over the proposal as well as suggestions for how the 
F-Gas sector can continue to contribute to increasing EU energy-efficiency.

1. The proposed quota phase -down s chedule undermines the EU’s 
energy-efficiency goals 
• As the leading producer of ultra-low GWP F-Gases, Chemours supports the HFC phase-
down. However, the Commission’s Impact Assessment severely  overestimates the 
speed at which the market can transition to l ow GWP alternatives. For example, it 
assumes that most of the Air Conditioning Systems and Heat Pumps below 12kW will 
move to R-290 (propane) between now and 2024. Given that the Regulation is unlikely 
to be adopted before 2023, the assumptions in the supporting study should be revisited 
by the Commission.  
• The HFC phase down proposed targets 95% & 97.5% reduction by 2030 & 2045 
respectively, whereas the RACHP industry’s (EPEE) HFC modelling tool indicates 
substantially higher quota requirements for this time horizon to meet the demand for, for 
example, heat pumps. The modelling indicates that HFC demand for servicing existing 
RACHP equipment would exceed the available quota from 2027, leaving nothing for new 
equipment in the EU4. 
• All new equipment from 2027 would need to immediately use near zero GWP refrigerant, 
such as hydrocarbons or HFOs, which is an unrealistic timeline both in terms of 
technological development, wide availability of endorsed components and in terms of the 
needed number of installers trained and certified to handle the new refrigerants, many of 
which are flammable. 
• Furthermore, SMEs are expected to be the most impacted, especially in some sectors 
such as commercial refrigeration and maintenance contractors as the technology 
investment required by alternative technologies (li ke CO2 or Hydrocarbons) would de 
facto preclude SMEs from remaining part of key markets like Commercial refrigeration. 
• Finally, the lack of supply to feed market demand will accumulate year -over-year from 
2024 and lead to a direct increase in illegal imports.  
Our recommendations:  
• We suggest following the adjusted baseline for the phase -down until 2030 and the 
Montreal Protocol phase-down schedule afterwards5. 
2. The enforcement measures are welcome but need time and resources 
to ensure harmonized implementation across Member States 
• The illegal trade in F-Gases and imports to the EU is a significant obstacle to effectively 
transition to F -Gases with lower GWP. While the proposal for the revised Regulation 
contains many positive initiatives to step up the fight against illegal imports, they will take 
more time to implement than the proposed HFC phasedown schedule provides for. Illegal 
imports were estimated to be up to one third of the legal market in 2019 – and this is 
expected to increase as the quota size decreases.  
• The call for dedicated customs offices for clearance of F-Gases is welcome but remains 
unclear how it will be implemented while ensuring the right level of know -how level and 
harmonization across the EU.

• The proposed quota fee is welcomed if its revenues collected are ear marked to fund 
enforcement measures in Member States. However, introducing it before the new 
enforcement measures are implemented will only put an additional burden on legally 
operating businesses. 
Our recommendations:  
• Introduce a minimum penalty threshold 
• Clarify the Single -window integration steps to drive real -time functionality for customs, 
which is currently missing.  
• Provide clear instructions to customs officials, including on the frequency & procedure of 
customs checks to ensure harmonized implementation across the EU.  
• Mandatory implementation of the Single Window Environment for Customs on fluorinated 
greenhouse gases as early as possible. 
• The controls on the supply of HFCs into the EU should be strengthened further to either 
ban the sales of F-gases on on-line marketplaces or to introduce mandatory certification 
for undertakings selling bulk F-gases online. 
• The proposed 3-euro quota fee implementation should be delayed, and the funds should 
be earmarked for enforcement measures in Member States. 
• For the proposed measures to be successful in reducing the illegal trade of HFCs, they 
need to be supported by adequate Member State enforcement resources, know-how and 
equipment. 
3. The proposed quota fee should be waived until the necessary 
enforcement measures to combat illegal trade are implemented 
• In the absence of effective enforcement, the quota fee will merely function as a tax on 
legal activities and incentivize further illegal imports.  
4. Extend measures to improve reporting, verificati on and to prevent 
leakage from equipment as well as address end-of-life treatment to all 
F-gases, including HFCs, HCFOs and HFOs  
• We warmly welcome the inclusion of all F-gases in the scope of the Reg ulation as it 
ensures consistency and a level playing field. However, the kilogram-based threshold for 
ultra-low GWP gases is not implementable in practice and would put as well residential 
users in scope.  
• To improve reporting and verification measures of  the Regulation, thresholds and 
exemptions must be removed or changed to avoid misuse by players on the market and 
to ensure that they do not present a risk to the Union’s compliance with their international 
obligation under the Kigali Amendment. 
  
 
Our recommendations:  
• Recovery and subsequent recycling or recl amation should be prioritised where 
technically feasible.  
• Require Member States to encourage the development of producer responsibility 
schemes under Article 9 for the recovery of F -Gases and their s ubsequent recycling, 
reclamation, or destruction.  
• Introducing a requirement for Member States under Article 29 to collect, evaluate and 
monitor mandated logbooks in electronic format would help identify leakage and 
emissions.  
• Article 5 mandates leak chec ks for Annex I gases applications over 5 tonnes of CO2 
equivalents and Annex II, section I gases applications over 1 kilogram. We recommend 
raising the 1-kilogram threshold to 6 kilograms.

H      Siemens AG welcomes the European Commission’s proposal, released April 5th., to revise the Fluorinated Greenhouse Gases (F-gas) regulation to prevent further F-gas emissions and foster the uptake of climate and environmentally friendly solutions.  Sulfur-hexafluoride (SF6) is one F-Gas, that has been used for more than 35 years in electrical Transmission and Distribution (T&D) equipment as a switching and insulating gas in both high voltage (HV >52 kV) and medium voltage (MV < 52 kV) applications. For T&D equipment, a phase-out of this highly potent greenhouse gas is now proposed. This has been expected and prepared by the industry, following the last revision of the F-Gas Regulation in 2014.  F-gas free solutions for T&D equipment are now available  Most of T&D equipment manufacturers have committed to move away from SF6 and have developed their own technological solutions. In a joint statement issued on November 2, 2021 “Toward T&D equipment free of fluorinated gases for sustainable, climate-neutral power grids”, ten leading European and international T&D companies committed to deliver T&D equipment free from SF6 and PFAS to ensure truly sustainable power grids (s. Appendix 1).  Siemens AG has developed F-gas-free alternatives, that use only natural origin gases (‘Clean Air’) for gas-insulated switchgear (GIS).  Siemens’ portfolio already includes F-gas-free solutions for 12 kV and 24 kV. Siemens has GIS solutions in service for primary and secondary distribution level at 12 kV, while the first 24 kV solutions are already deployed and 36 kV versions are to be introduced accordingly. In total, several thousand solutions are in the field.  For MV switchgear up to 52kV it is possible to replace SF6 as an insulating and switching gas completely free of F-gas, avoiding any possible impact on environment and health.   Siemens Clean Air solution has a GWP <1, is harmless, extremely stable, non-toxic, non-flammable, and suitable for all operating temperatures. It is therefore possible to modernize and expand existing power grids at the highest levels of safety while simultaneously reducing the ecological footprint without compromising on performance or economic feasibility.  As average lifetime of electrical products is at least 35 years, direct change to F-gas 
free solutions is the most future-proof way to comply with all possible upcoming 
environmental regulations. In medium voltage the competitive situation is significantly 
more heterogeneous compared to high voltage sector. There is no constricting market 
access limitation by introducing the proposed GWP < 10. 
 Our solutions will be ready by the timelines proposed by the European Commission, 
we support the position and statements made by VDE FNN (German Forum Network 
Technology). 
 SF6 should not be traded against other hazardous substances: We are concerned 
about the possibility to replace one fluorinated substance (SF6) with another one 
(belonging to PFAS - also called “forever chemical”) impacting the environment and 
health of the operator while handling T&D equipment. An evaluation of alternative F-
gases should not only rely on technical specifications and GWP. Their decomposition 
products, acute toxicity and Carcinogenic, Mutagenic or toxic for Reproduction (CMR) 
impacts should also be taken into consideration right from the beginning to avoid 
adopting solutions that might be removed later due to further toxicity assessments (see: 
https://www.cired-repository.org/handle/20.500.12455/66). 
 For SF 6 used in electrical switchgear, the existing F-gas Regulation contains 
requirements on leak checks, record keeping, certification & training and labels (Ref. 
Article 4, 5,6, 7,8,9, 10,12). Should any new F-gas be used in electrical switchgear, the 
same obligations should continue to apply for the safety of workers and the 
environment. These new F-gases should be added together with SF6, under annex I, 
section 3. 
 We support the positions of ZVEI and T&D Europe, esp. with regard to indirect export.

Switchgear is an equipment that isolates, protects, and controls electrical networks or 
installations. The term “switchgear” is a generic one and includes: 
- Disconnectors ensure, in the open position, an isolating distance to provide "dielectric 
independence" between the two circuits. 
- Switches are used to reconfigure the electrical system or in combinations with fuses to 
protect an MV/LV transformer. 
- Fuses are used for protection of MV/LV transformers and MV motors in case of short 
circuit. 
- Circuit breakers are used for fast interruption of short circuit current to minimise 
installation damage. 
Source: https://www.tdeurope.eu/transmission-distribution/the-electricity-grid.html 
 
PFAS - Per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances are a large class of thousands of synthetic 
chemicals that are widely used throughout society and found in the environment.  
They all contain carbon-fluorine bonds, which are amongst the strongest chemical bonds in 
organic chemistry. This means that they resist degradation when used and also in the 
environment. Most PFASs are also easily transported in the environment covering long 
distances away from the source of their release. PFASs have been frequently observed to 
contaminate groundwater, surface water and soil. Cleaning up polluted sites is technically 
difficult and costly. If it continues, it could accumulate in the environment, drinking water and 
food. 
Source: https://echa.europa.eu/hot-topics/perfluoroalkyl-chemicals-pfas

Decisions on REACH restrictions are made in the European Commission by the EU Member

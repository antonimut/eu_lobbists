I. Issue and question
The FEhS - Institut für Baustoff-Forschung e. V. (hereinafter: FEhS Institute) researches, tests, and ad-
vises on ferrous slag, a by-product in the production of iron and steel, and the building materials and 
fertilisers made from them. The EU Commission published the draft of a new Construction Products 
Regulation on 30 March 2022. This is intended to repeal the previous Regulation (EU) No 305/2011  
(EU Construction Products Regulation, EU CPR) and amend Regulat ion (EU) 2019/1020 (Market Sur-
veillance Regulation). Economic operators have to submit an opinion on the initiative via the "Have 
your say" platform until 12 July 2022. The FEhS Institute would like to submit such a statement with 
regard to the non -inclusion of by -products in manufacturer obligations and in the awarding of con-
tracts, among other things. This opinion is to be prepared by this note.

II. Legal assessment/Draft opinion 
Ferrous slag is inevitably generated during the production of iron and steel in the sense of a technical 
unavoidability, so that it is a by -product in the sense of Art. 5 of the Waste Directive. In Europe, the 
production of iron and steel generates around 45 million tonnes of ferrous slag annually (2019). For 
many decades, they have already been used as high-quality secondary raw materials for the produc-
tion of road construction m aterials or cement  thus replacing primary raw materials such as natural 
stone and quartz sand. What is more, the ferrous slag-based building materials have superior techno-
logical characteristics like high strength, high durability, high resistance to chemical stresses, and high 
frost and heat resistance.  
One of the overarching goals of the new draft of the Construction Products Regulation is, among other 
things, the resource-saving manufacture of construction products and the promotion of circular econ-
omy and sustainability in the construction industry . This goal is considered in the new draft of the 
Construction Products Regulation by imposing environmental obligations on the manufacturer in Art. 
22 of the draft of the Construction Products Regulation. The European Commission's proposal also 
includes a new Article 84 on environmentally oriented public procurement. Art. 22 of the draft Con-
struction Products Regulation overlooks the importance of by -products (see below under 1.), while 
Art. 84 of the draft Construction Products Regulation is not very ambitious (see below under 2.).  
1. Extension of manufacturer obligations to the use of by-products 
According to Art. 22 of the draft Construction Products Regu lation, the manufacturer shall give pref-
erence to recyclable materials and materials obtained by recycling (Art. 22 (2 ) (b) of the draft Con-
struction Products Regulation) and comply with minimum requirements for the recycled content. With 
regard to the definition of recycling, the draft Construction Products Regulation refers back to the term 
in Art. 3 No. 17 of Directive 2008/98/EC (Waste Framework Directive, WFD) (Art. 3 No. 33 of the draft 
Construction Products Regulation ). Conceptually, recycling requires that waste materials are repro-
cessed into products, materials or substances. However, since ferrous slags are by -products, partly 
because they can be reused (Art. 5 WFD), they do not become waste at any time in the sense of Art. 3 
No. 1 WFD. The further use of ferrous slag in concrete or cement can therefore not conceptually be 
recycling. Nevertheless, the further use of ferrous slag leads to waste prevention within the framework 
of the waste hierarchy and thus even has priority over recycling (Art. 4 WFD). In particular, the manu-
facturer obligations in Art. 22 of the draft Construction Products Regulation should therefore also be 
extended to by-products insofar as they relate to recyclable materials or raw materials obtained from 
recycling. 
To this end, we propose the following specific amendments and/or additions to the draft Construction 
Products Regulation:

2. Concretisation of the requirements for green public procurement 
Art. 84 of the draft Construction Products Regulat ion does not contain any requirements that would 
apply directly - in advance of delegated acts and independently of their adoption. It follows from this: 
Unless and until the EU Commission adopts delegated acts, there are no binding requirements for 
green public procurement of construction products. The time that elapses until the adoption of dele-
gated acts is thus "lost time" for the construction products concerned in the sense that contracting 
authorities may continue to procure without environmental orientation during this time. 
Furthermore, Art. 84 of the Commission draft does not maintain any obligation for the Commission to 
have to adopt delegated acts. This applies both in principle and in terms of timing. It is therefore not 
certain that the Commission will adopt delegated acts at all, and it is also not certain that the Commis-
sion will adopt delegated acts in the short term or promptly or otherwise within a certain period or by 
a certain date. Thus, the Commission may not adopt delegated acts or may do so only after a very long 
period of time. In this way, contracting authorities may never be obliged to engage in green public 
procurement, or only after a very long period of time.

Therefore, Art. 84 of the draft Construction Products Regulation should be supplemented to the effect 
that at least a basic obligation to environmentally oriented public directly applies to contracting au-
thorities both independently of the adoption of delegated acts and even before the adoption of dele-
gated acts. 
To this end, we propose the following specific amendments and/or additions to the draft Construction 
Products Regulation:  
Art. 84: 
"Green public procurement 
1. Contracting authorities as defined in Article 2(1) of Directive 2014/24/EU and Article 3(1) o f 
Directive 2014/25/EU or contracting entities as defined in Article 4(1) of Directive 2014/25/EU 
shall, when awarding public contracts, take into account sustainability aspects of construction 
products to be supplied in public supply contracts or to be us ed in works or service contracts 
for activities which are the subject of the contract, in accordance with the conditions laid down 
in this Article. 
2. In the description of the contract, contracting authorities shall include and allow construction 
products which fulfil the obligations set out in Article 22(2). Contracting authorities shall not 
exclude such construction products in the description or otherwise in the procurement docu-
ments. Insofar as delegated acts of the Commission pursuant to Article 22(4), first sentence, in 
conjunction with Article 87 or harmonised standards pursuant to Article 22(4), second sen-
tence, are applicable, the contracting authorities shall observe these delegated acts and stand-
ards when including and approving construction products pursuant to sentence 1 and sentence 
2. The obligations under sentences 1 to 3 shall not apply insofar as the construction products 
are not suitable for the intended use or their use is contrary to legal provisions. If a contracting 
authority invokes an exception pursuant to sentence 3, it must document the reasons for the 
exception in the award notice in a textual justification. 
3. Where construction products referred to in paragraph 2 may be offered in the award of a con-
tract, contracting authorities shall determine the most economically advantageous tender on 
the basis of the best price-quality ratio, assessed with due regard to the qualitative criterion of 
the extent to which the construction products offered comply with environmental obligations 
referred to in Article 22(2), without prejudice to the right of contracting authorities to use cost 
or a cost-effectiveness approach such as life -cycle costing or other criteria to assess the best 
price-quality ratio.

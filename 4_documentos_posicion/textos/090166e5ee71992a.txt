DISTANCE MARKETING OF FINANCIAL PRODUCTS 
Since 2002, distribution of financial products, both on the Internet and by telephone, has intensified. 
The Distance Marketing of Financial Products Directive (DMFSD) needs to be revised to ensure that the 
framework for this sales technique guarantees consumers' rights.  
UFC-Que Choisir, which defends the principle of constant improvement of consumer rights, is 
particularly concerned by the Commission's current draft.  
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY OF UFC-QUE CHOISIR'S DEMANDS 
- The association calls for a ban on unsolicited telephone marketing of financial products; 
- Failing this ban, it asks that the Directive should be at least as strict as the framework that 
prevails in France since the Law No. 2021 -402 of 8 April 2021 on the reform of insurance 
brokerage and brokerage  in banking and payment services . Alter natively, the Directive 
should be for minimum harmonisation to allow Member Stat es to adopt or to retain more 
protective provisions; 
- In case of subscription by unsolicited telephone canvassing, the association calls for the 
introduction of a 30-day withdrawal period starting from the date of payment of all or part 
of the first insurance premium; 
- The association supports the introduction of a " withdrawal button". It also calls for the 
introduction of a "cancellation button" available at all times to allow consumers to notify 
their demand to terminate the contract at due date; 
- It calls for a ban on withdrawal fees. 
The association asks the Commission to take these arguments into consideration and present them 
to the co-legislators so that they amend its initial proposal accordingly.

INTRODUCTORY ELEMENTS 
At this stage, the Commission’s current draft is for maximum harmonisation in order to promo te the 
establishment of a cross-border market at EU level. For UFC-Que Choisir, this objective must not be an 
end in itself, especially if it is used as an alibi for a political choice that undermines consumer rights. 
This is why the association is in favour of minimum harmonisation directives that allow Member States 
to maintain or establish stricter consumer protection measures. 
UFC-Que Choisir notes that the draft directive authorises cold calling  for financial products. Our 
association calls for a ban o n this dangerous sales technique. Besides being an infringement into 
people's peace of mind, the resulting commercial relationship is irreparably flawed. Solicited without 
their consent, consumers cannot make an informed choice, and even less  shop around. Moreover, 
when we understand that the information given verbally is often incomplete or even inaccurate, we 
can better imagine the scale of the complaints received by our volunteers. 
Unsurprisingly, sectors that make massive use of this marketing technique are those that concentrate 
the most disputes. This is particularly the case in insurance. While health and provident insurance are 
sold massively by telephone canvassing, and represent less than a third (29%) of contracts, they are 
the source of nearly three-quarters of complaints (72%)1. 
Within our association, the complaints relate to aggressive and/or misleading commercial practices 
aimed at vulnerable groups, particularly isolated elderly people. The seriousness of these practices has 
been documented by the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) in France, which has 
sanctioned professionals on several occasions.

ARTICLE 16 BIS 
INFORMATION REQUIREMENTS FOR DISTANCE CONTRACTS FOR CONSUMER FINANCIAL SERVICES 
 
Summary of the main provisions of the proposed directive according to UFC-Que Choisir 
In case of cold calling, and if the consumer has agreed to continue the call, the professional will have 
to provide limited information by derogation from the general regime.  
Indeed, the professional will only have to specify his identity and main activity (a), a description of the 
main characteristics of the financial service (f ), information on its total price (g) and the right of 
withdrawal (p). 
UFC-Que Choisir's observations 
UFC-Que Choisir considers that the proposed framework constitutes an unacceptable step backwards 
for French consumers' rights. In this respect, the associ ation wishes to draw the attention of the 
European Commission to the regulation of cold calling in France since 2022. This system, which was 
originally introduced by an agreement at the Comité Consultatif du Secteur Financier (CCSF) between 
public authorities, insurers, brokers and consumer associations and has the force of law since this year, 
can be summarised by the following four pillars.  
Pillar 1: Respect for the peace of mind of consumers 
In case of cold calling, the distributor must obtain the consumer's prior agreement to continue the call.  
If the consumer does not explicitly agree, the distributor must end the call and refrain itself from calling 
again. 
After having obtained the consumer's explicit consent, the distributor is still obliged at any time to 
terminate the call without delay, if the consumer shows no interest or does not wish to continue the 
call.  
Pillar 2: Strengthened duty to advise 
If the professional offers an insurance contract for a risk that is already covered, he must ensure that 
the consumer can cancel his current contract at the same time as the proposed contract takes effect. 
Pillar 3: condition for a more informed subscription 
In case of cold calling, the  sale of an insurance contract can only occur after two separate calls. The 
consumer can only sign the contract after the second call. This call has to take place 24 hours after the 
professional has ensured that the consumer has received the pre-contractual information documents. 
Consumer choice is also protected. The consumer can only take out an insurance contract after signing 
it. The signature must be handwritten or electronic. It cannot be done during a telephone call. In any 
case, the professional cannot sign the contract on behalf of the consumer. 
Pillar 4: Possible controls through systematic recording 
To enable the authorities to monitor the respect of this framework, professionals are obliged to record 
and retain all telephone conversations that take place before the conclusion of the insurance contract 
for a period of two years.  
While this framework may be perceived as ambitious, it  should be noted that the overwhelming 
majority of professionals questioned during an assessment conducted by the Financial Sector

this reform.  
UFC-Que Choisir’s demand 
UFC-Que Choisir calls for a ban on cold calling for financial products.  
Failing this, the association is asking that DMFSD to be at least as protective as the French framework 
on insurance cold calling.

ARTICLE 16 TER 
RIGHT OF WITHDRAWAL FROM DISTANCE CONTRACTS FOR FINANCIAL SERVICES 
 
Summary of the main provisions of the proposed directive according to UFC-Que Choisir 
In the case of distance selling, consumers have a withdrawal period of 14 calendar days to withdraw 
from the contract without penalty and without having to give any reason.  
The period starts from the day the contract is concluded (a) or the day the consumer receives the pre-
contractual and contractual information material (b). 
A withdrawal button would be placed visibly and available (permanently) for the duration of the 
withdrawal period on an electronic interface. 
UFC-Que Choisir's observations 
UFC-Que Choisir points out that French consumers benefit from a longer withdrawal period for certain 
insurance products. For example, this period is set at 30 days for life insurance product or “assurances 
affinitaires” (insurance for breakage of mobile phone, tablet, etc.).  
It should also be noted that some professionals use stratagems to make the withdrawal period 
ineffective. In France, for example, some professionals have deliberately arranged for the first 
insurance premium to be debited after the withdrawal period has expired. In this way, the consumer 
who is not aware that he has taken out an insurance product is unable to cancel it before the expiry 
date of the contract. 
This is why, in France, on the basis of an agreement at the Comité Consultatif du Secteur Financier 
(Financial Sector Consultative Committee) between the public authorities, insurers and consum er 
associations, for “assurances affinitaires”, the professionals have undertaken to extend the withdrawal 
period to 30 days and to make it run from the payment of all or part of the first insurance premium.  
UFC-Que Choisir's demand 
UFC-Que Choisir reques ts that in the case of telephone canvassing, the withdrawal period be 
systematically set at 30 days and that this period begin from the date of the payment of all or part 
of the first insurance premium. 
UFC-Que Choisir supports the "withdrawal button" proposal. This interesting proposal would benefit 
from being completed by a "cancellation button" permanently available on an electronic interface 
and allowing the consumer to notify the insurer of his choice to terminate the contract on its due 
date.

ARTICLE 16C 
PAYMENT FOR THE SERVICE PROVIDED BEFORE WITHDRAWAL 
 
Summary of the main provisions of the proposed directive according to UFC-Que Choisir 
When the consumer exercises his right of withdrawal, the draft allow the professional to charge the 
consumer. The amount to be paid may not exceed a proportional fee depending on the service already 
provided (a) and that should not be interpreted as a penalty (b). 
UFC-Que Choisir's observations 
Cold calling is an attack on consumers peace of mind. It is also particularly risky, as shown by the many 
disputes that have arisen from this sales technique. It is therefore totally unacceptable that consumers 
should have to pay a fee to exercise their right of withdrawal.  
At the same time, the framework to set  the amount of the  withdrawal fees proposed by the 
Commission is particularly lax and subject to the arbitrariness of the professionals. Indeed, it is illusory 
to believe that the supervisory authorities will be able to assess if the withdrawal fees are justified or 
not. Furthermore, the reality of the so called “service" provided during cold calling may be particularly 
low. As an illustration, a broker sanctioned by the ACPR distributed its contracts after a selling time of 
4 minutes and 44 seconds2. 
UFC-Que Choisir's demands 
UFC-Que Choisir asks for a ban on withdrawals fees.

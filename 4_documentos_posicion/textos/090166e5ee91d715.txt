Leaseurope Feedback on Commission Proposal for a Distance Marketing of

General Remarks  
 
Leaseurope, th e European Association representing the leasing and automotive rental 
industries, welcomes the opportunity to provide comments on the Commission’s proposal for 
“a Directive amending Directive 2011/83/EU concerning financial services contracts 
concluded at a  distance and repealing Directive 2002/65/EC” , the so -called Distance 
Marketing of Financial Services Directive (DMFSD). 
 
Leaseurope brings together 45 member associations representing the leasing, 
long term and/or short term automotive rental industries in the 32 European countries in which 
they are present. The scope of products covered by Leaseurope members’ ranges from hire 
purchase and finance leases to operating leases of all asset  categories (automotive, 
equipment, machinery, ICT and real estate). It also includes the short term rental of cars, vans 
and trucks. It is estimated that Leaseurope represents ov er 90% of the European leasing 
market.  
  
Asset finance and leasing markets have developed to respond to business investment and 
consumption needs as well as to accompany the development of local industrial production 
and distribution. The types of institu tions represented by the Federation include specialised 
banks, bank -owned subsidiaries, the financing arms of manufacturers as well as 
other, independently-owned institutions, of which a big part are SMEs. 
 
Amongst others, l easing is used by more European SMEs than any individual category of 
traditional bank lending taken altogether (around  47% of all European SMEs make use of 
leasing which is more than any other individual form of lending)  and is also popular amo ng 
larger corporates[2]. 
 
Leaseurope endorses the Commission’s goal to  improve consumer protection online and to 
ensure the free movement of financial services in the single market by harmonising certain 
rules related to contracts concluded at distance. However, we believe that these objectives 
would be more appropriately sought through a targeted revision within the existing 
framework, which fundamentally still remains relevant and functional.  
 
The Unfair Commercial Practices Directive , the Unfair terms in Consumer Contracts Directive  
as well as the upcoming Digital Services Acts and the Digital Markets Act which will soon start 
to apply, are sufficient to ensure a high -level of consumer protection online. We suggest

Leaseurope welcomes the clarification in the Commission’s proposal  according to 
which where there is duplication in disclosure requirements between different legal 
texts, product -specific legislation will take precedence  against the general rules  
provided for under the DMFSD . In particular, regarding the obligation to provide pre -
contractual information and the right of withdrawal,  Leaseurope supports the provision 
whereby “where another Union act governing specific financial services contains rules on pre-
contractual informa tion or on the exercise of the right of withdrawal, only the respective 
provisions of those other Union acts should apply to those specific consumer financial services 
unless provided otherwise in those acts ” (Article 16a(6) and Article 16b(6) of proposal) . 
Likewise, regarding the provision on the obligation to provide “adequate information” (Article 
16d(4) of the proposal). 
 
To achieve the  high-level consumer protection and a real single market, legal certainty is 
key. It is important therefore to avoid any unintended market confusion and to ensure that 
the overlaps on the disclosures obligations put forward in sector-specific legislation for 
particular products and those put forward in the new horizontal rules on distance financial 
services of the new part of Consumer Rights Directive are avoided. 
 
Leaseurope further supports that the definition of “distance contract’ remains unchanged, i.e. 
any contract concluded between the trader and the consumer under an organised distance 
sales or service-provision scheme without the simultaneous physical presence  of the trader 
and the consumer, with the exclusive use of one or more means of distance comm unication 
up to and including the time at which the contract is concluded . This also contributes to 
ensuring legal certainty.  
 
 
In summary, Leaseurope is in favour of the following: 
- Clear adherence to the principle of lex specialis whereby product-specific legislation 
takes priority. 
 
- Flexibility for the service/product providers on how to facilitate access to the right 
to withdrawal of consumers. Adding a “button” is not always the obvious solution.  
 
- Unfair Commercial Practices Directive1, the Unfair terms in Consumer Contracts 
Directive2 as well as the upcoming Digital Services Acts and the Digital Markets 
Act which will soon start to apply, are sufficient to ensure a high-level of consumer 
protection online. The provision of additional protection offers no added-value at 
this stage.

Detailed Comments 
 
➢ Sector specific v. horizontal rules 
The DMFSD dating back to 2002 has been superseded by both horizontal and product-specific 
legislation, e.g. the Consumer Credit Directive (CCD) and Mortgage Credit Directive (MCD) 
as well as  the General Data Protection Regulation  (GDPR). The strict adherence to the 
principle of lex specialis must be set out  to avoid legal unclarity between all relevant 
frameworks and the DMFSD.   
 
Furthermore, the issues addressed in the Commission proposal are incorporated as relevant  
in the current processes aiming to review sector-specific rules. Particularly, the Commission 
proposal for the new CCD review includes adapted provisions to account for the new 
updated digital reality, and to help consumers make an informed decision and to assess 
whether they are adapted to their needs and financial situation, .e.g in relation to the provisions 
of information, the right of withdrawal, and, where applicable, the right to human intervention 
in case of automated processes. 
 
In light of the above, Leaseurope believes careful assessment and caution are required before 
finalising any new horizontal rules on financial services provided at distance, in order to fully 
observe the principles of better regulation and proportionality.  It would be unwise to 
introduce new general rules on distance marketing of financial services prior to having 
completed the legislative and review processes of the CCD and MCD as well as relevant 
regulatory developments in connection to AI and expected open finance initiatives. 
 
Leaseurope welcomes, as  mentioned above, the clarification in the Commission proposal 
(Article 16a, 16b and 16d) that where there is duplication in disclosure requirements between 
different legal texts, product -specific legislation takes precedence. In light of the principle  of 
lex specialis, in relation to the above -mentioned provisions, we believe that this approach 
should also be applied in relation Article 16c on “payment of service provided before 
the exercise of the right of withdrawal”  as the proposed wording would likely provide 
for a misalignment with sector-specific legislation, e.g. the Consumer Credit Directive. 
Such a situation with differing rules between the sector-specific and general framework would 
only risk to lead into legal uncertainty and consumer confusion.   
 
 
 
 
➢ Additional Protection 
Leaseurope believes that the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive 3, the Unfair terms in 
Consumer Contracts Directive4 as well as the upcoming Digital Services Acts and the Digital 
Markets Act which will soon start to apply, are sufficient to ensure a high -level of consumer 
protection online. We suggest examining the outcomes of application of these r ules and at a 
later stage examining whether there is an added-value for additional protection as prescribed 
in the proposed Article 16e.

➢ Pre-contractual information 
Leaseurope fully supports the aim that requirements for pre -contractual information 
disclosures should be adapted to digital environment. This means that information must be 
made accessible on different media and in an appropriate format.  This need has an 
impact on the volume of information  that should be provided to customers. It should be 
kept in mind that consumers may often be overwhelmed by the large amount of information 
provided, which can be viewed as complex and technical, and in any case overwhelming to 
overview on mobile devices.  
 
There is a  general need to better to simplify and streamline the inf ormation relevant to the 
different stages of the process. Consumers must be enabled to make informed comparisons 
and decisions and avoid the issue of information overload.  At the same time, it would help 
meet consumers’ demand for a smooth and seamless experience. Leaseurope supports the 
possibility given to the traders to present the required information in layers and in table 
of contents (Article 16a(4) and recital 22). 
 
The necessity of the information provided to customers should be carefull y considered, as 
should the interaction  and consistency  of different disclosure requirements arising from 
different EU legislation. This assessment should be made with reference to existing and 
upcoming sector-specific rules. 
 
The observations made by the financial sector to the Proposal for a new Consumer Credit 
Directive specifically on pre-contractual information must be taken into account. 
 
Below, there is a list of points arising out of the Commission proposal for a revised DMFSD in 
relation to pre -contractual requirements . However, the required adaptations should be  
aligned with the product-specific rules to ensure legal coherence. 
 
1. Information to be provided before the consumer is bound by the contract  
(Article16a). The exact point of time when the consumer is “ bound by a distance 
contract” may in some cases bring about misunderstandings. Leaseurope believes 
that the consumer should be considered bound by a contract concluded by electronic 
means, either after the signature of contract or after the consumer’s consent has been 
explicitly given. 
2. Means of communication provided by the trader shall enable the consumer to 
contact the trader quickly and communicate with him efficiently  (Article 
16a(1)(b)). The wording “quickly and efficiently” cannot be really defined and they 
should be deleted as they provide no added -value to the obligation. Artice1 6a(1)(b) 
provides for an extensive list of the information to be provided to consumers regarding 
communication with the trader. 
3. The trader shall provide the information at least one day before the consumer is 
bound by any distance contract (Article 16(3)). Consistency should be ensured with 
relevant sector -specific legislation  where such timing requirement s is not at hand . 
Moreover, c onsidering the right of withdrawal, this will not deliver any measurable 
consumer-benefit, and conversely disrupt the seamless customer journey sought and 
expected by consumers . Ultimately, the proposal will only provide for consumer 
detriment. 
4. The information shall be made available to the consumer on a durable medium 
(Article 16a(4)). The definition of “durable manner” should be clarified to include 
examples of recent communication, such as email, phone messages or any other chat 
messages that are currently used  built upon the relevant case law of the Court of 
Justice. This will prevent unnecessary disputes which work to the detriment of 
customers’ experience.

➢ Right of withdrawal 
It is essential to continuously ensure that consumers benefit from having access to 
relevant information at the appropriate time, to allow them to make informed decisions and 
to exercise their various rights, via both on- and offline channels. Leaseurope agrees that 
the right of withdrawal should be at least as easy to exercise as the entry into of 
lending agreement, e.g. if a credit can be agreed online, the right of withdrawal should  
also be allowed to be exercised with the same ease.. 
As mentioned above, t he potential issues to be adapted should  be rather addressed in 
product-specific legislation: 
1. The trader should provide a possibility to use a withdrawal button in order to 
facilitate the consumer’s exercise of the right of withdrawal (Article 16b (5)). 
In our opinion, making available a button through which customers can declare their 
withdrawal would not increase consumer protection compared to the Directive 
currently in force, according to which customers can declare their withdrawal in writing 
or on another durable medium available and accessible to the recipient. On the 
contrary it could entail, for the obligated traders additional costs for the technical 
changes that need to me done  (e.g. IT infr astructure, study of the interface and 
computer developments). What is more, it should not be forgotten that other EU sector-
specific rules do not provide an obligation for a “button” of withdrawal. More flexibility 
should be given to the service/product providers to facilitate access to the right of 
withdrawal in the way that is more appropriate to the nature of their business, products 
at hand and the more precise forms of distribution. 
 
 
 
➢ Adequate explanations 
We reiterate out comments above that there are sector -specific rules that provide for 
relevant and more detailed disclosures obligations and rules on the distribution 
process which should render Article 16d  inapplicable. Leaseurope warns against 
creating a legal framework that adds to the complexity and further risk to overburden 
consumers with excessive and duplicated information. 
 
 
Leaseurope is willing to engage in a constructive dialogue with the Commission to 
further explain its perspective if needed.

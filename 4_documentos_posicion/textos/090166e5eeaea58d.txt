 
 
Feedback to Public Consultation “Construction Products Regulation 
(CPR)” 
Date: 12. July 2022 
 
The European PVC Profiles and related Building Products Association EPPA 
represents the manufacturers of PVC window systems and related building products 
in Europe. About 25,000 employees process about 1,4 million tonnes of PVC 
creating a turnover of €4 billion with profile systems and building products. Based 
in Brussels, EPPA provides a common platform for bundling national activities in the 
fields of PVC window technology, recycling, environment and public affairs.  
We welcome the opportunity to provide feedback in the course of the profound 
revision of the Construction Products Regulation. 
 
 
Article 1 – Subject Matter 
EPPA welcomes the inclusion of environmental and climate related performance in 
the CPR. However, we would like to underline that windows are construction 
products in the sense of Regulation No. 305/2011 but are faced with a considerable 
overlap in scope of the Ecodesign Regulation for Sustainable Products (ESPR) and 
CPR. While the ESPR covers the same environmental indicator horizontally for all 
products, we underline the need for legal certainty. Accordingly, we trust that the 
European Commission will avoid any double regulation by these two legal 
instruments that both apply to windows.  
  
Furthermore, we would like to insist on the need to clearly define the newly 
introduced product requirements. As presented in the proposal the competence for 
the EU Legislator is too broad right now without sufficient involvement of technical 
experts. The definition of technical characteristics should be completed by 
technical experts in CEN as a rule, involvement of the EU legislator should be the 
clearly defined exception.  
 
 
Article 2 – Scope 
With concern, we have noticed that re-use has now come within the scope of the 
CPR and CE-Marking obligations. In particular with a view to the obligations 
outlined in Article 29 we must draw attention to the fact that economic operators 
de-installing windows are usually micro-enterprises, that will not have the capacity 
to establish and implement protocols on further use of de-installed windows. 
Finally, we would like to express serious doubts on the ability to supervice such 
requirements via market surveillance authorities.  
 
 
Article 3 – Definitions 
We understand that “items for assembly” do explicitly not qualify as construction 
products, while “key parts” are specified by harmonized technical specifications. 
We invite the Commission to clarify the relationship between these two definitions 
Ref. Ares(2022)5062059 - 12/07/2022
 
and its consequence for application of the CPR to PVC profiles from which windows 
will be build.  
Furthermore, we understand that delegated acts do now fall into the definition of 
“harmonized technical specifications”. In this regard, we would like to underline 
that delegated acts are legislative tools and should not be put on equal footing 
with harmonised standards (hEN) or EADs. The development of hENs can be 
supported but must not be replaced by delegated acts. Technical experts would be 
excluded from definition of technical characteristics and requirements, which must 
be avoided to ensure the definition of technically sound specifications, assessment 
methods and requirements.  
 
Article 4 – Essential Characteristics of Products 
The fact that the European Commission can take over work of CEN entirely is 
worrying and risks excluding technical experts from the highly technical work 
surrounding the CPR. The use of delegated acts should be narrowed down and be 
more cleary defined. Only if the work in CEN is clearly opposing legislative 
requirements under the CPR, the Commission should have the ability to intervene. 
EPPA welcomes that the CPR is to be used as the core framework to also 
accommodate for additional union or national legislative requirements concerning 
construction products. This will guarantee a level playing field in the EU.  
 
Article 5 – Product Requirements  
The revised Regulation allows for broad and detailed regulation of individual 
product performance requirements. While this is welcomed as such, we notice that 
the exact requirements for product manufacturers are not clear from the 
Regulation but are entirely up to later definition via delegated acts. This leads to a 
lack of legal certainty and leaves the door open for lengthy Commission-internal 
procedures before exact requirements are finally set per product group. Given the 
large discretion that is left to the Commission, it is also not clear whether and how 
industry experts will be involved in the definition of technical requirements. This is 
not supported by EPPA, in particular because we assume that the European 
Commission (and Member States’ representatives) lack the technical expertise on 
PVC profile systems to set sound requirements.   
 
Article 7 – Harmonized Zone & National Measures 
EPPA welcomes a full harmonization of requirements on the EU market, removing 
barriers to the Single European Market (SEM) by establishing a harmonized zone.  
 
Article 13&14 – Declaration of Conformity 
While we welcome an alignment of CE-Marking requirements across product 
legislations, we are concerned about the additional bureaucratic burden on 
manufacturers to produce declarations of conformity on top of the Declaration of 
Performance (dop) and CE-Marking. In fact, consistent feedback of the industry has 
shown that delivering dop and CE-Marking are already considered a bureaucratic 
double effort. It is therefore irritating that the Commission proposes another 
bureaucratic procedure and document, instead of slimming down the existing ones. 
 
 
 
Article 18 – Other Markings 
EPPA welcomes the implementation of a harmonized, level-playing field across 
Europe, moving away from individual, national, private requirements.  
 
Article 22 – Additional Environmental Obligations of Manufacturers 
We welcome the dedicated regulatory context for environmental sustainability for 
construction products which is essential for implementing the Green Deal 
We furthermore welcome that the previously voluntary EN15804 is to become the 
new mandatory basis for the calculation of environmental impacts of construction 
products. We underline that the CPR should be the only legislative framework to 
regulate environmental performance and communication thereon for construction 
products. A double regulation via the ESPR must be avoided. 
 
Article 78 – EU Construction Products Database or System 
We welcome that a link to the ESPR is made, however it is not clear whether a 
separate database is to be build or whether construction products will be 
integrated in the DPP (digital product passport). This is important for windows, 
which may be subject to both legal frameworks and hence subject to information 
requirements in two databases.  
Naturally, we assume that such a database should render SCIP obsolete for 
construction products.

Feedback on the European Commission’s proposal to  
revise the Construction Products Regulation (CPR) 
Environmental Action Germany (DUH - Deutsche Umwelthilfe) – 12.07.2022 
DUH welcomes the European Commission’s proposal to revise the Construction Products Regulation (CPR). Due to the once 
in a decade opportunity to set a clear pathway to effectively decarbonise the construction sector in the CPR, we urge you to 
consider the following adjustments:   
 
 Ensure an effective alignment of the CPR with the Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation (ESPR) 
so that most ambitious minimum environmental requirements for construction products apply.  
Due to the parallel process and linkage between the CPR and the ESPR , mismatches in provisions already exist: An-
nex I part D provides directly applicable product information requirements in CPR. These proposed parameters do 
not fully align with the inherent requirements on performance and are lacking parameters (such as e.g. carbon foot-
print, full bill of materials, chemical substances and composition, or microplastic release ), as it is the case under the 
ESPR. Thus, a built-in mechanism for assessing the alignment between these two legislations needs to be put into 
place. This would mean that the CPR entering into force must be assessed against the text of the ESPR.  If the result 
of such assessment would be, that the ESPR is setting more ambitious environmental requirements, the CPR must 
ensure that the ESPR provisions apply accordingly.  
 
 Effective and defined solutions shall be introduced to overcome shortcomings of the standardisation 
process.  
By defining clear criteria for the fall-back option presented in Art. 4(3)(a-g) the substantial problem of pending 
standardisation requests can be mitigated. Thus, a definition of the term “undue” from Art. 4(3)(a) shall be provided 
and a direct action by the Commission with legal acts should be ensured if standardisation requests are not meeting 
deadlines or are rejected from citation in the OJEU. 
 
 Redefine the scope of the CPR to exclude cement. All intermediate products should be covered by the 
ESPR, to guarantee that minimum environmental requirements apply swiftly to products with high-em-
bodied emissions such as cement. 
The CPR proposal only ensures that minimum environmental requirements for construction products are applicable 
if delegated acts are developed. Due to the prolonged development time for such Delegated Acts, valuable time to 
reduce the high environmental impact of intermediate products such as cement will be lost. Therefore, cement 
should be integrated into the ESPR with other intermediary products and those high-energy intensive intermediary 
materials should be addressed with the highest priority in the ESPR. 
 Comprehensively assess embodied environmental impacts with the PEF methodology for construction 
products, set mandatory thresholds and environmental performance classes supported by a fast devel-
opment of a mandatory labelling system.  
As the ESPR also benefits from advantages of the PEF method as underlying methodology (e.g. better data quality, 
comparability and more comprehensiveness compared to the EPD method) the CPR should also use the PEF 
method. With such reliable data, threshold limits and environmental performance classes for construction products 
in construction works must be set to phase out worst performing products from the European market . To utilize the 
environmental performance classes effectively, a mandatory labelling system should be developed swiftly and intro-
duced through a Delegated Act.  
 Ensure an ambitious timeline, swift development and introduction of inherent product requirements.   
According to the current draft of the CPR, no inherent product requirements are set for construction products be-
fore a Delegated Act is introduced. In order to reduce the environmental impact of construction products in-line 
with the Paris Agreement, an ambitious timeline to develop binding product requirements must be incorporated in 
the CPR. The timeline must also include preparatory studies to identify key environmental impacts per product cate-

 The CPR shall create the basis for a circular and material efficient use of construction products.  
Firstly, a mandatory comprehensive Digital Product Passports (DPP) including all information set out by the CPR 
must be introduced to provide the necessary product information throughout the full product’s lifetime. Further-
more, a clear definition of already existing parameters such as recycled content or resource efficiency is necessary. 
Recycled content should only include post-consumer materials. Resource efficiency requirements should be set 
based on an ambitious baseline within the product category that grows with innovative technologies over time. Ad-
ditionally, EU-wide rules for Extended-Producer-Responsibility schemes for construction products should be intro-
duced to utilize reuse potentials and high quality recycling. 
 
We hope that the EU Commission will act swiftly and decisively on this matter of construction products. Given the urgency 
and environmental importance of the CPR, we would appreciate the opportunity to discuss this with you at the earliest op-
portunity. Please do not hesitate to contact us if you have any questions.

About Environmental Action Germany: 
Environmental Action Germany (Deutsche Umwelthilfe - DUH) is a non-governmental environmental and consumer protection 
organisation in Germany. We are politically independent, recognised as a non -profit organization and entitled to bring legal 
action. DUH supports all sustainable ways of life and economic systems that respect ecological boundaries. In the area of re-
cycling management, DUH advocates waste prevention, responsible consumption and sustainable waste management.

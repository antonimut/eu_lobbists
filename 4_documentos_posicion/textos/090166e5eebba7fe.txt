Re: Construction Products Regulation – revision   
  
Pyrowave is a pioneer in the electrification of chemical processes based on low carbon footprint 
microwaves. Pyrowave is also a Canadian leader in the plastics circular economy and chemical 
recycling to regenerate post-consumer and post-industrial plastics into new materials, reclaiming 
these resources’ full value. Its patented high- powered microwave catalytic depolymerization 
technology platform is the most advanced in the world and is now at the forefront of the next 
generation of raw materials. By restoring plastics to their molecular state (plastic to monomer) 
identical to virgin materials, Pyrowave technology enables infinite recycling of plastics and other 
materials, and provides a circular economy solution to meet the global plastics recycling 
challenge. Our high- value monomer can be used for construction materials  but also synthetic 
rubber and other activity sectors, such as electronics, automotive and packaging.  
As an equipment manufacturer, we have a demonstration plant and an R&D center in Canada 
and we partnered with the Michelin Group in 2020 to deploy our technology in Europe by 2023. 
We are also developing in the Asian market. This goes to show that our technology is 
commercially ready and operational. The accelerated growth Pyrowave has seen i n the last few 
years is partly due to the evolution of a favorable regulatory framework in Europe aiming at 
incentivising the circular economy and innovation to achieve ambitious sustainability goals. We 
believe that the best application of our technology to serve the circular economy is through the 
integration of low-carbon recycled content in high-value sectors, such as construction products . 
This is why we salute the intention of the Construction Products Regulation revision to 
establish harmonized rules for the integration of recycled content and recyclability in the 
construction industry.  
1 Recycled content targets 
Pyrowave supports the CPR including recycled content as an essential characteristic of 
construction products in Article 4 (Annex 1) and the obligation to declare minimum recycled 
content (Article 22). Pyrowave is confident that upcoming delegated acts will set realistic yet 
ambitious targets for recycled content in construction products , as m ore ambitious recycled 
content targets will stimulate innovation and development for recycling technologies. The 
Pyrowave technology is capable of supplying 100% recycled styrene for construction materials , 
using plastic waste as feedstock for plastic circularity. The hurdles for a circular economy are no 
longer technological – the issue remains in the shifting of the value chains into a circular model. 
Ambitious recycled content targets will provide the stimulus necessary for this shift  and allow 
innovative technologies to be commercially deployed. 
PYROWAVE’S RECOMMENDATIONS: To set ambitious recycled content targets in the 
delegated acts for all types of construction materials.  
 
 
2 Performance indicator to monitor the implementation of the regulation 
In addition, to promote the integration of recycled content in construction materials and ensure 
coherence between EU legislation, we propose integrating ambitious sustainability requirements 
from the Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation into the CPR revision. For example, the 
CPR revision should integrate a similar performance indicator to monitor the implementation of

3 Accounting method for calculating recycled content  
Harmonized calculation methods for recycled content are also crucial to ensure transparency and 
equity between actors. There are many ways to calculate recycled content in a Chain of Custody 
model which ISO standards are based upon. As such, not all recycled content is equal, as the 
various ways to calculate recycled content offer a spectrum from a physical traceability 
(segregation) to an accounting rule (book and claim). This diagram from ISO Standard on Chain 
of Custody illustrates the level of physical traceability of each approach (Source : ISO 22095)
1 : 
The mass balance approach includes various degrees of traceability, from batch segregation, 
where a specific batch can be located, to a book and claim approach, which refers to an 
accounting rule where there is no actual recycled content in a specific product or packaging but 
content accounted for and attributed to this specific product or packaging.  
While in some cases, a mass balance approach is required for certain hard- to-recycle plastics, 
we believe the recycled content that is claimed should reflect the physical recycled content of the 
product in priority . This is an important element of trust for consumers. There is a need to 
distinguish physically traceable recycled content in order to instill trust among consumers and 
allow clients to use the resin of their choice based on transparent and complete information. The 
CPR should integrate accounting guidelines in its Annex to guarantee a level playing field that is 
the most ambitious for sustainability targets.

4 Feedstock availability and quality 
Beyond the integration of recycled content in new construction products, it is also crucial that the 
CPR address the end-of-life issue of construction materials. Commitment to “recyclability and the 
capability to be remanufactured” and “capability of different materials or substances to be 
separated and recovered during dismantling or recycling procedures” are addressed in Annex 1 
Part C. A portfolio of solutions is the key to address the end-of-life issue of construction materials. 
However, recycling technologies  require high- quality feedstock to ensure high- quality raw 
materials. Harmonized standards for collection and sorting of construction materials would 
guarantee the availability and quality of feedstock. Investment in sorting and collection 
infrastructure specific to the construction industry is also crucial.  
 
PYROWAVE’S RECOMMENDATIONS: To set standards for the collection and sorting of 
end-of-life construction materials to guarantee availability and quality for feedstock for 
recyclers.  
 
 
In conclusion, Pyrowave supports the intention of the Constructions Product Regulation revision 
to integrate sustainability requirements, such as life cycle assessments, recycled content, and 
recyclability of materials. As noted in the Explanatory Memorandum (Problem 4), Pyrowave 
agrees that there is a need for harmonized assessment methods for performance of construction 
products with regards to the use of natural resources – but these standards must also be 
ambitious to be in line with the EU’s commitment  to the circular economy. The first step to 
achieving sustainability in the construction sector is effective disclosure of information and 
accurate labelling, which will promote the development of low -carbon technologies such as 
Pyrowave’s. If our recommendations are met, we believe that the Construction Products 
Regulation revision can successfully tackle sustainability in the construction industry and 
streamline the transition towards a low-carbon, circular economy.

Pharma Industry Finland’s comment on EHDS public hearing 
The legislative proposal on a European Health Data Space (EHDS), along with the Data Act proposal and the 
Data Governance Act provide for a future health data ecosystem. It forms an essential basis for the Health 
Union and brings competitiveness to the EU. 
Pharma Industry Finland supports the legislative proposal for the European Health Data Space, and the 
effort to increase patient control, as well as access to their own health data. However, to increase 
competitiveness, the legislative base needs to enable research and innovation activities. The legislation 
should not bring hurdles to data access or to technical development.  
As a representative of the research-based industry we would like to emphasize the need to ensure all 
different incentives supporting research, as well as development and innovation activities, especially IP-
rights and trade secrets. Without proper incentives Europe will not remain a world leader in the 
development and manufacture of medicines.  
Pharma Industry Finland strongly encourages taking advantage of the Finnish experience in implementing 
the national Act on the Secondary Use of Health Data. The legislative base should be consistent with other 
data-related initiatives (GDPR, AI Act, Data Governance Act, Data Act etc.). We also think it’s essential that 
the EHDS does not overlap with other, already existing legislation.  
The legislative proposal on the EHDS will bring several new actors, and their roles should be clear and not 
overlapping. Collaboration between these new actors, as well as all relevant stakeholders, is essential.  
The legislative proposal will bring the framework for the free movement of health data. However, since the 
EHDS proposal includes many delegated and implementation acts to be given, they will have a profound 
effect on how effectively the aim to have data available for broader use is achieved.  
General observations to chapters I-III 
Pharma Industry Finland supports the vision of free movement of data. Data is the basis for research and 
for improving health care. The data used in everyday health care needs to be of high quality. In addition to 
electronic health care systems there needs to be incentives for health care professionals to ensure the 
information is available and correct. 
To get the data to flow throughout Europe requires common standards and common rules for the EHRs. 
Not only do the systems differ, but also the question of different languages must be solved in an efficient 
way. The EHDS proposal doesn’t provide any solution to this at the moment. 
Technical development is rapid and legislation should not prevent its evolution. Technical development also 
improves access to data in a secured way. The citizens’ ability to control their own data is critical as it also 
increases trust. However, we think that the personal scope of the EHDS proposal should be clarified in 
relation to whose data it covers. According to Article 1 point 3(b) of the proposed Regulation, it applies to 
“controllers and processors established in the Union processing electronic health data on Union citizens and 
third-country nationals legally residing in the territories of Member States.”. It seems to limit the personal 
scope of whose data can be in question when applying the proposed EHDS Regulation to only EU citizens or 
those third country nationals who are legally residing in the Union. This creates many uncertainties, such as 
who should verify that a third country national is residing legally in the Union and when should a person be

process? This would also diverge from the personal scope of the GDPR, and thus create additional burden. 
Another issue on scope relates to controllers or processors in third countries in e.g. situations such as those

In general, the terminology should be as consistent as possible with existing legislation, especially with 
GDPR. 
Secondary use of data (Chapter IV) 
Data is the basis for research, and R&D brings new innovations for the benefit of patients and the society. 
Bringing data to wider use will improve health care in many different forms, and it enables Europe to be the 
leader in technical development. 
The EHDS proposal is ambitious, and it brings broad categories of health data available. The current text, 
however, does not clarify from which moment onwards the data must be available. For example, if older 
data would be in electronic form but stored in an electronic archive and available only at high expense 
would this need to be available and who would be responsible for the costs? For data holders, and to keep 
the costs on a solid basis, the time limits should be clarified. In addition, we would like to point out that the 
EHDS proposal does not change the existing requirements for storing data and the requirement to share 
data in all these situations.   
Especially with company data, it is essential to provide a grace period during which access requests do not 
need to be complied with for in order to ensure the company rights to IP.  
Based on the Finnish experience with the secondary use of health data, we want to emphasize the 
importance of timelines for data permission processes, as well as for data deliveries. In our experience, the 
collaboration between different stakeholders is essential for meeting the timelines. In addition, the EHDS 
proposal should provide clarity on what is meant by a health system and how to form the standards for free 
movement of data etc.  
Pharma companies conduct different kinds of research, from clinical trials to survey-based studies. In 
addition to research activities, companies provide patient support data. Therefore, we think that the 
proposal should give more clarity to which kind of data is included in the intended regulation. More clarity 
is also needed in the rules on who can request company data and how it can be requested.  
At this point, it’s not very clear what kinds of obligations the proposal would bring to companies or how the 
work would be compensated. For instance, the proposal doesn’t clarify whether companies should take 
care of anonymization or pseudonymization of data. It’s also unclear how the work around refining the data 
during the clinical trials is compensated to companies since they are obligated to provide the data to other 
stakeholders.  
To reach the intended goals, it is evident that the regulation would increase the costs at all levels. However, 
to some extent the costs should be seen as investments in health systems throughout Europe, as well as 
investment in the EU’s competitiveness. Therefore, the investments should not be included in their full 
extent in fees charged from data users. The fees for permissions and data delivery should remain on a level 
that will not end up decreasing the EU’s competitiveness. 
 
The role of pharma companies 
The proposal suggests that IP and trade secrets should not prevent from sharing data, and it includes 
pharma companies as data holders. It also states that measures can be put in place to offer protection that 
will continue to motivate clinical research in the EU. Pharma Industry Finland emphasizes that more 
clarification is required on when and with whom the IP-protected data is expected to be shared and what 
mechanism will be applied to protect the proprietary information.  
Further clarification is required on when and to whom the IP protected data is expected to be shared and 
what mechanism will be applied to protect the proprietary information. It is also important to consider 
Regulatory Data Protection when considering the obligations for pharmaceutical industry to share their 
data. At the moment, however, the EHDS proposal is not very clear on how and when it requires the 
sharing of data with the private sector, including competitors. 
Ensuring the EU’s competitiveness and the incentives for research, as well as respecting IP-rights is 
essential. 
As pharma companies are also data holders and the regulation brings new requirements, the pharma 
industry should be considered for representation within the European Health Data Space Board.

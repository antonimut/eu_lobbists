 
 
 
AIM position on the European Health Data Space 
 
 
AIM’S FEEDBACK TO THE COMMISSION CONSULTATION ON THE  
COMMUNICATION AND REGULATION ON THE EUROPEAN HEALTH DATA SPACE 
 
 
1. Introduction 
The International Association of Mutual Benefit Societies (AIM) is an international umbrella organisation 
of federations of health mutuals and other not -for-profit healthcare payers , which provide 
compulsory/and/or supplementary health coverage to around 2 30 million people around the world, 
including close to 200 million people in Europe, on a not-for-profit basis.  
We have studied the Proposal for a Regulation of the European Parliament and of the Council on a 
European Health Data Space (hereinafter "European Health Data Space")  and would like to t hank the 
European Commission for this opportunity to provide input from the payers’ perspective regarding the 
Commission proposal.  In this note we have set out our conclusion s and recommendation s on the 
European Health Data Space. 
 
2. Conclusions & Recommendations 
 
2.1 Primary use of healthcare data 
• Telemedicine as an important step for cross-border healthcare 
The European Health Data Space aims to ensure that telemedicine services are easy to use, accessible 
to different groups of natural persons and health professional and do not discriminate. Member States 
should offer the possibility of choosing between in person and digital services. Different reimbursement 
policies should not constitute ba rriers to digital health services such as telemedicine . When digital 
services accompany the physical provision of a healthcare service, the digital service should be included 
in the overall care provision.1 
 
AIM welcomes these provisions where the objecti ve is to turn telemedicine into a standard medical 
service, accessible to every European patient and fully covered by the respective social security system. 
Telemedicine plays a major role for the development of medicine and healthcare delivery in Europe. It 
allows different healthcare professionals to collaborate from different locations, including from 
different countries. This can improve the quality of services provided and opens the possibility to expand 
the spectrum of medical services available. However, the European Commission addresses areas in the 
Directive 2011/24/EU on the application of patients' rights in cross -border healthcare that might limit 
the opportunities for telemedicine treatments2: Systems of reimbursement allow that the national state 
may reimburse a part of the treatment to control the national budget (Article 7 (9)). In addition, prior

• Citizens need to understand their right to give and revoke an approval to use their personal 
data 
The Commission proposal gives the right to patients as well as to health professionals to immediate, 
free, and easily readable access to electronic health data for use, and the right to receive a digital copy. 
In addition, it provides for the right to allow healthcare or social security data holders to access  or 
request that their electronic health data be tra nsmitted immediately, free of charge and  without any 
hindrance to a recipient of their choice in the healthcare sector. It also  establishes the right to restrict 
healthcare providers' access to all or part of their electronic  health data. Finally, the righ t for patients 
to obtain information about the healthcare provider who has had access to their electronic health data 
for the provision of care has  been included. Citizens can restrict access to their health data. However, 
in cases where the vital interest of the citizen is at stake (e.g. in acute care situations), the healthcare 
provider can still access the necessary electronic health data. Afterwards, however, the citizen must be 
informed about this. Member States may add additional safeguards in national legislation. 4 
 
AIM welcomes that the proposal gives citizens rights to have more control over their health data. In 
many Member States some of these rights may already exist. However, patients need to understand 
that they have the right to give and revoke an approval to use their data. In addition, they must be given 
the tools to manage their approvals (also for digital -illiterate citizens). Clarification is needed whether 
the regulation of the Commission proposes a system that implicitly assumes the consent of citizens and 
in which they must explicitly object to the making available of their health data or a system in which the 
patient has to explicitly express her/his consent. It is essential that Member States ensure that patients 
are aware of the system and its consequences.

• Digital health authorities need time to be implemented in national systems and legislations 
The Commission proposal foresees that each Member State shall designate a digital health authority to 
ensure that the rights of citizens under the proposed regulation are respected.5 
AIM health insurers are in general positive about this provision. The proposed digital health authority  
entails new executive tasks and responsibilities, which still need to be further translated and 
implemented in the different national systems and legislations. Some Mem ber States are more 
advanced than others. Therefore, the timeline of implementation in 2025 does not seem realistic.  
 
• Cross-border infrastructure for primary use of electronic health data is important 
The proposal of the Commission regulates the cross -border interoperability of primary health data 
through a federated infrastructure MyHealth@EU to which national digital care contact points will be 
connected.6 It focuses on a certain type of data exchang e such as patient summaries, electronic 
prescriptions, electronic dispensations, medical image s and image  reports, laboratory results and 
discharge reports.7 
AIM members welcome that the proposal regulates the cross-border interoperability of primary health 
data through a federated infrastructure. It leaves the data at the source  and doesn’t require central 
storage. AIM is positive that health data are accessible in cross-border situations. Many EU citizens travel 
to other countries for holidays or for wor k and might need to use healthcare services abroad. It is 
essential that citizens’ health data are accessible in another Member State.  
 
2.2 Secondary use of electronic health data 
 
• Genomic and related health data should never be used without informed consent of the citizen 
The Commission proposal provides a legal basis under the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) 
for the secondary use of electronic health data for research, innovation, policy - making, patient safety 
or regulatory activities.8 It defines types of data that can only be used for specific purposes without prior 
consent from the citizens.9 In addition, the proposal sets a number of prohibited purposes of secondary 
use of electronic health data. 10 Governments and EU authorities can make requests for data without 
the need for a permit, but in accordance with the purposes and prohibitions included in the proposal.

AIM welcomes the fact that there is a clear legal basis for the secondary use of health data. It will 
improve the availability of health data in Europe. For AIM, it is important that health data will only be 
made available pseudonymised, and that the citizen is actively and clearly informed  about it. From 
experience, consent for the use of the right data for scientific research, innovation and p olicy is not 
always easy to achieve. AIM is concerned about the number of categories of health data, which have to 
be made available.  Genomic data and similar data should never be made available without informed 
consent of the citizen.  
• Health data access bodies 
The proposed Regulation foresees a system of permits issued by a designated government body in each 
of the Member States, the so-called “Health data access bodies”. This can be an existing body or a newly 
created body. The permit is issued based on an application which needs to provide details on a number 
of items such as a description of the requested data, the reasons for seeking access, the intended uses, 
the safeguards, the duration and whether the data should be made available in an anonymize d or 
aggregated format.11 
AIM is in favour of the designation of one or more public bodies responsible for providing access to 
health data for secondary use. It seems to be a possibility to make health data available in a secure and 
reliable way. However, what has been said for the digital health authorities, counts as well for the health 
data access bodies. Not every Member State has such a public body yet. To set up bodies like this, 
Member States need time to evaluate the consequences of such a body and how it can be implemented 
in the national system and legislation. The deadline of the year 2025 does not seem to be realistic.  
In addition, AIM would like to emphasize that the permit process for health data should be organized 
effectively, to avoid situations like the process related to the Medical Devices. The certification 
procedure of medical devices  currently poses challenge for innovators to enter the market. 
Organisations that had to handle the certifications were overloaded with certification requests, which 
is still the case today. When introducing a certification procedure for the European Health Data Space, 
lessons should be learned to ensure that an efficient and unified certification process is put in place.  
Therefore, AIM members ask to reduce the administrative burden and to look into e.g. audits. However, 
the strict material requirements for the authorisation of medical devices as well as the exchange of data 
should remain in place.  In addition, data access bodies operating differently, leading to different speeds 
of approval or different execution of approval processes, risking diffe rent criteria setting, should be 
avoided. 
AIM is concerned that there is still considerable doubt among many citizens about the exchange of 
health data. More emphasis should be put on where the solidarity community benefits from the 
exchange of data. 
• European Health Data Space Board: Member State participation should be guaranteed 
The proposed Regulation foresees the European Health Data Space Board (EHDS Board), which is 
supposed to promote the consistent application of this Regulation. In addition, the EHDS Board will set 
up subgroups and networks to coordinate the work of the digital health authorities, dealing with primary 
use of health data, and the health data access bodies, dealing with the secondary use of health data.  
AIM members propose to have a stronger participation of Member States in the EHDS Board.

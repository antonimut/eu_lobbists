PGE Polska Grupa Energetyczna S.A. comments on  Proposal for a Regulation of the 
European Parliament and of the Council  amending Regulation (EU)  2021/241 as regards 
REPowerEU chapters in recovery and resilience plans and amending Regulation (EU) 
2021/1060 , Regulation (EU) 2021/2115, Directive 2003/87/EC and Decision (EU) 2015/1814 
 
The REPowerEU package is an important step on the EU way towards net-zero economy. It is 
based on a right diagnosis and it sets the right goal – to wean the EU off the Russian energy 
and fuel supplies. However, the proposed package of measures needs further elaboration. 
PGE Polska Grupa Energetyczna S.A. contributes to this important work with the following 
points: 
 The reforms, and especially investments related to energy security that are to be 
included in the REPowerEU chapters in Recovery and Resilience Plans (RRPs)  will be 
mostly large-scale projects that involve long-term investment process. It is therefore 
necessary to amend the RRF regulation also with regard to the deadline for the 
completion of the reforms and investments. The current deadline of 31 August 2026 
forces Member States and investors to rush in an ext remely unpredictable market 
environment with inflation rates skyrocketing, supply chains disrupted and shortage 
of skilled workforce . In this circumstances  the most ambitious projects, with the 
highest added value from the local and EU perspectives, are in  a competitive 
disadvantage due to the frontloading principle. For this reason we are convinced that 
the deadline, at least for the largest REPowerEU projects, should be postponed until 
the end of 2030. This is because the Russia’s invasion over Ukraine not only prolonged 
negative COVID-19 consequences for the European economy but also caused even 
more severe humanitarian and economic crisis.  
 In order to limit consumption of Russian gas wherever it is possible, and replace it 
with renewables in a short t erm perspective, the EU has to focus on modernisation 
of existing energy infrastructure. Firstly, we need to address bottlenecks that 
constrain deployment of RES installations, therefore the new REPowerEU chapters 
in Recovery and Resilience Plan s should fo cus on electricity distribution grid 
investments. Secondly, electricity storage remains one of the biggest challenges of 
the energy transition, and one of the few feasible and reliable options we have is 
pumped-storage power plants . Additionally electricit y storage used for the local 
balancing of distribution grids will also play a more and more important role.  . 
Therefore, the new REPower EU chapters in Recovery and Resilience Plan s should 
support the plans of modernisation and expansion of the existing pumped-storage 
power plants, new investments in these installations as well as new investments in 
energy storage.

as a whole. The project s that contribute to energy security and  have been rejected 
from the Recovery and Resilience Plans due to inconsistence with the 2021 version of 
Technical guidance should be reassessed by Member States and the Commission, and 
in justified cases, they should be included in the new Recovery and Resilience Plans.  
 The allocation key of the new revenu e to the RRF of EUR 20 billion  should be 
reassessed. The proposed allocation key is based on the RRF Regulation and reflects 
the scope of challenges of the COVID -19 pandemic. The challenges stemming from 
the Russian aggressive actions on the EU energy market have different consequences 
and different costs across the Union. In order to decrease the level of dependence on 
Russian energy supplies, a new allocation key should reflect the share of fossil fuels 
in the electricity and heating fuel mix. The bulk of the new revenue should be directed 
to those Member States which are heavily reliant on fossil fuels and which have to 
move away from them. 
 With the proposed allocation mechanism,  many Member States, especially these 
from the Northern part of the EU are put in a disadvantage compared to these from 
the South of the continent. This is a result of a disproportion between the percentage 
share of the input to the MSR of respective Member States on one side, and the 
percentage share of the output from the RRF on the other side. For example, in case 
of Poland, share from the proposed new source of revenues would need to be raised 
from the proposed  7% (share of the funds as defined in the RRF) to 12%, which is the 
Polish size of Polish s hare in the MSR. This means that under the proposed solution 
Poland would be the net payer and would finance investments in other, mostly richer, 
parts of the EU from what would normally be its auctioning revenues that the country 
could spent on its own investments.  
 Furthermore, the allowances released from Market Stability Reserve should not be 
accounted for the purpose of the calculation of the Total Number of Allowances in 
Circulation as to avoid counting them towards the market surplus . Otherwise this 
will lead to a situation that the 24% intake rate will absorb more allowances into the 
MSR in the near future. 
 We welcome the fact that transfers to the RRF of other European Funds which 
Member States can request are foreseen to be voluntary. In our opinion no change 
should be made with regard to that issue: Member States should be allowed to decide 
if they want to complement their allocation of funds from the RRF with other 
European Funds or if they prefer to invest this money in other relevant projects. The 
current energy crisis shows very clearly, that energy security investments pay -off 
sooner or later, therefore the EU should look for a new source of money, instead of 
redistributing the funds that have been already allocated to Member States for 
different purposes.

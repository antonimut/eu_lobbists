   
 
ACHIEVE Coalition input to the European Health Data Space Proposal  The ACHIEVE Coalition welcomes the European Commission’s proposal for a European Health Data Space (EHDS) to establish clear rules, infrastructures, and a governance framework for the use of electronic health data.  The importance of health data collection for research and better evidence-based policy making, from disease prevention to diagnosis and care, cannot be underestimated. The EHDS proposal specifically mentions the need to improve the fight against cancer, including cancer prevention, across the EU by securing cross-border access and sharing among healthcare providers, as a means to ensure continuity of care. We understand that this will be possible thanks to the collection of personal electronic health data, which includes data on health determinants, such as social factors.    The prevention of hepatitis-related liver cancer is a case in point where data collection is needed, as more than 70% of liver cancer cases are related to a chronic hepatitis B (HBV) and C (HCV) infection. Eliminating viral hepatitis by 2030, in line with the objective of the WHO Global Strategy, will thus be key to preventing cancer and reducing the liver cancer burden. Viral hepatitis is still today highly stigmatised infection, with social factors playing a significant role. Notably, HBV and HCV affect many different risk groups, including but not limited to vulnerable populations.  With vaccination against HBV and effective treatment for HCV, the world is in a rare position to end the viral hepatitis epidemics and significantly reduce morbidity and mortality of related cancer. However, good quality data on viral hepatitis diagnosis and patient pathways is scarce. As the ECDC highlighted in its most recent report, the European Union is still not on track to reach the elimination goals for viral hepatitis by 2030. The lack of good quality data makes it difficult to measure progress in the fight against viral hepatitis and complicates the design of effective measures to drive forward elimination. Furthermore, good quality data is also needed to ensure a timely diagnosis of follow-up on diseases such as liver cancer and cirrhosis.   Hence, we hope that the EHDS will help trigger much-needed momentum for collecting better and comparable data on viral hepatitis and related non-communicable diseases, such as liver cancer and cirrhosis, enabling better disease prevention, increased diagnosis and care, without neglecting data privacy and security.

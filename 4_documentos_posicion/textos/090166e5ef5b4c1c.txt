Input to Consultation on European Health Data Space   HIV Outcomes, a network comprising of over 35 individuals and organisations jointly advocating for improving the well-being of people living with HIV, welcomes the European Health Data Space (EHDS) proposal and its foreseen health-specific data sharing framework. HIV Outcomes is pleased that the proposal embodies a step forward in enabling individuals to access, share, and manage their health data, as well as the use of this data for research, innovation, policy making, patient safety, and regulatory purposes.  Given the successful scale-up of antiretroviral therapy globally, healthcare digitalisation has the potential to transform HIV care and scientific progress beyond viral suppression in terms of comorbidity management, health-related quality of life (HRQoL), and patient-reported outcomes. We must now focus on maximising its implementation for achieving the triple aim of improving the experience of care from an individual perspective, improving the health of the population, and reducing the cost of care per capita1.   In line with these objectives, this proposal should seek to improve integrated care for individuals requiring access to a range of health services, such as people living with HIV. Such multidisciplinary, multispecialty care requires systematic coordination and interoperability to streamline service provision, ensure patient-safety, and to enable effective data sharing between the different care providers involved. Therefore, the adoption of tools for communication and data sharing across disciplines is vital and implementation barriers must also be addressed2.   Data security and privacy are important pillars of the proposal for a European Health Data Space. This is of particular importance for HIV Outcomes as a way avoid stigma and discrimination when accessing individuals’ health data. Nevertheless, data on health-related stigma and discrimination is equally critical for improving health interventions and the HRQoL of people living with HIV3. The collection of data via an established framework is also a potentially valuable tool for accessing and integrate patient-reported outcomes (PROs) in electronic health records as a means to advancing people-centered care4.   As HIV Outcomes goes beyond viral suppression, we would welcome a visionary proposal that goes beyond data collection and use for care provision and establishes ground rules for the provision of quality and value-based health care by extending the scope of the proposal to include QoL. • Ensures patient’s privacy and security and addresses stigma and discrimination in the use and management of personal health data (e.g., towards people living with HIV and other stigmatised conditions) by allowing the reporting by data owners and healthcare professionals. Structural and legislative barriers to the interoperability between data systems, as well as privacy concerns must be addressed. • Does not mandate data sharing at any point as a means to protect individual choice and allows an opt-out at any time. People living with HIV often do not disclosure their HIV status due to fear of privacy breach, stigma and discrimination. The EHDS should ensure that people living with HIV can be confident that all services across the system offer a level of safety and security and feel comfortable sharing their data.  • Promotes and supports the implementation of digital services that can enhance integrated and multi-disciplinary care and improve cross-border access to health services (particularly for people living in remote areas), as well as retention in care. Such integration and multidisciplinary approaches can provide higher quality of care, often needed in the management of comorbidities of people living with HIV. The benefits of such services should be carefully monitored and evaluated in comparison with more traditional in-person services to ensure they do not create new health inequities.  • Considers the ‘digital divide’ and provides guidance on the use of digital tools to improve access to care and quality of care, considering the needs of key populations and minimising health-related and literacy-related disparities. In this regard, the proposal should also explore solutions for people that are reluctant to share their personal health data, leaving no one behind and closing the inequities gap.  • Improves the patient experience and collects patient-reported data (PROs) to monitor the quality of care and health outcomes, as a means to improve the HRQoL of people living with HIV at clinical, policy and institutional levels. The use of this data needs to be ensured at the individual, regional, national, and European levels and should be subjected to scientifical evaluation to support technical implementation.  • Ensures community and patient involvement in the implementation and evaluation of the EHDS framework, allowing it to be tailored to individual needs. This can ensure that all patients in the EU benefit from the EHDS framework, as the new data-sharing possibilities may lead to better (digital) care across countries. Sharing of anonymised data and best practices can contribute to an overall improvement of care in all EU countries. • Harmonises the data-sharing framework with existing health facilities’ systems and procedures to integrate and harmonise data in a single platform, as well as facilitate its use. Overarching goals for the framework's development should be standardised and interoperable communication systems that are properly integrated at the national and European levels.

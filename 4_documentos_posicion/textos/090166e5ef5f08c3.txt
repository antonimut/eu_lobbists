 
 
ESR Statement in response to the European Commission Consultation on the 
European Health Data Space 
July 2022 
 
The European Society of Radiology (ESR) welcomes the European Commission’s proposal 
for the European Health Data Space (EHDS), a crucial way towards ensuring patient-
centered healthcare, by using patient data to continuously improve patient care and health 
outcomes.  
 
The European Health Data Space aims to exploit the wealth of health data in a trustworthy 
European framework by encouraging Member States to align their use of health data, to 
stimulate research and enable data-driven healthcare that benefits citizens, while protecting 
their privacy. 
ESR fully embraces the concept of the EHDS and has worked on various elements to 
analyse existing hurdles and to facilitate sharing of imaging data in the future. ESR is a 
member of the ehealth Stakeholder Group of the European Commission and researchers 
and clinicians from the ESR membership are coordinating major European research projects 
to advance sharing of imaging data within the framework of the AI4Health Cluster of Horizon 
2020. Also, the ESR is contributing strongly to the implementation of the European Cancer 
Imaging Initiative of the Europe’s Beating Cancer Plan and has supported a strong 
consortium to address the recent Digital Europe call on federated European infrastructure for 
cancer images data. Hence, ESR is eager to engage with the EHDS and to support 
implementation.  
However, although digital transformation of healthcare is a major priority in European health 
policy, hurdles remain impeding the adoption of digital health technology in the healthcare 
setting. 
As regards the EHDS, the ESR welcomes that medical images and image reports are a 
priority area for Member States to make available for the primary use of health data.   
In terms of the secondary use of imaging data, while there is the potential for a huge volume 
of data to be made available, hurdles preventing cross-border data sharing are numerous. 
The ESR has experienced complexities in the sharing of data in small research projects, 
where partners often have difficulties in sharing data, despite agreements being in place. If 
data sharing is improved for healthcare, this has the potential to bring about significant real-
world benefits. For example, improving access to high-quality real-world data to train AI 
algorithms and for the clinical validation of medical imaging devices and AI algorithms. The 
ESR therefore welcomes that data quality is specifically addressed in the proposal, as high-
quality data is essential to realise these benefits.  
Clarification is needed where there are potential overlaps between primary and secondary 
use of data, such as in clinical trials. 
In ESR’s view, the following aspects of the proposed EHDS legislation require further 
clarification and explanation: 
• Clear governance and a secure environment are needed to ensure trust.   
• Clarification of the interrelation between EHDS and GDPR regarding processing of 
personal health data is needed. GDPR presents hurdles with data sharing and it has 
not been implemented in a harmonious way across Europe. For example, there is a 
need to look at how GDPR has been implemented in the scientific research field in

each Member State; uncertainty and ambiguity in interpreting GDPR provisions and 
obligations seems to still exist (as outlined by FEAM in its April 2021 paper) 
• Potential overlaps with the forthcoming Data Act and Artificial Intelligence Act need to 
be assessed, in addition to existing legislation, such as on medical devices. It is 
important to outline what legislation takes precedence where there are overlaps. 
Legal clarity is needed and unnecessary burden must be avoided.   
• Issues of interoperability need to be further explored. The proposal for a European 
health data exchange format will be very challenging to implement. However, 
standards already exist for the exchange of image data.  
• It is important to continue to invest in upscaling digitalisation in member states and 
related infrastructure across the EU. This should include the upskilling of workforce 
and education of patients and citizens to improve trust in e-solutions. Digitalisation is 
currently varied across member states and support payments should be considered 
for countries or regions lagging behind.  
• Clear guidance regarding secondary use of health data is needed to facilitate cross-
border exchange of data.    
• Commercialisation of health data and the ability of data holders to sell health data 
needs further discussion and clarification. The possibility of private companies being 
involved has the potential to advance the EHDS but this would need to be well 
thought out and controlled. In addition, contradictions need to be considered between 
commercialisation of data and European Commission research projects that 
encourage open data science, where free open access has to be provided via a cloud 
for all research data.  
ESR looks forward to working with the EU institutions during the ongoing legislative process 
and offer to share our experience in the imaging field, with regards to interoperability, data 
exchange, and image repositories.

Object: European Health Data Space 
 
COMMENTS OF FRANCE ASSUREURS ON THE EUROPEAN HEALTH DATA 
SPACE PROPOSAL (EHDS) 
 
France Assureurs supports the objective of improving healthcare delivery by ensuring that EU citizens have 
greater control over their electronic health data, but also to promote better exchange and access to different 
types of electronic health data in a private and secure environment for the common good.  
 
Therefore, we would like to highlight the following points: 
 
The definition of key concepts and data categories is of crucial importance. France Assureurs calls for 
a better definition of the notions of primary use electronic health data as well as a precision of the 
secondary purposes allowed. 
 
Primary Use  
 
Under the proposal regulation, individuals will have a right of access to their electronic health data and the right 
to give access securely to third parties of their choice from the health or social security sector, immediately and 
free of charge via a digital interface.  
 
In insurance, certain health data are required by insurers to assess certain risks or to implement and execute 
insurance contract (e.g., for reimbursement of health costs in the context of supplementary health insurance, 
for the management of bodily inju ry claims, for the underwriting of provident insurance contracts, for the 
underwriting of loan insurance contracts). With easier access to electronic health data such, individuals will be 
able to share their data with their own insurer, making subscription or compensation easier and faster.  
 
The proposal regulation is not clear on the possibility of access to health data by insurers. 
 
The current definition of primary use electronic health data refers to "the provision of health services to assess, maintain 
or restore the health status of an individual " and " administrative or reimbursement services ". Administrative and 
reimbursement services could for example be understood to include any type of insurance service that involves 
the processing of electronic health data (private health insurance, accident insurance).

Secondary use of anonymised or pseudonymised electronic health  
 
France Assureurs welcomes the EC's initiative to promote health research and innovation through the 
establishment of mechanisms for secondary use of anonymised or pseudonymised health data.  Among 
unpermitted3 purposes for which electronic health data cannot be processed for secondary use, Art. 35 (b) lays 
a specific prohibition in relation to premium setting in insurance and prohibits secondary use to refuse a cover. 
 
Insurers have a social and economic role in society. The insurance sector has already demonstrated its role as 
an economic and social stabiliser, to ensure that everyone has an adequate level of protection, at the right price. 
 
The Solvency II Directive req uires insurance companies to guarantee the appropriateness, 
completeness and accuracy of all the data used in the calculation of their technical provisions . The 
regulation4 establishes detailed data accuracy/quality requirements. Insurers must demonstrate in particular that 
they use sufficient historical information to assess the characteristics of the underlying risks and to identify 
trends in those risks. 
 
For insurers, greater availability of data lead to better monitoring and assessment of risks. It allows insurers to 
offer more affordable rates or to offer insurance for risks that were previously uninsurable, due to information 
gaps that can be filled by the increased av ailability of data. Insurers' access to types of health data therefore 
facilitate access to care, improve health outcomes and reduce overall health care costs in society in application 
of the principle of mutualisation which is the founding principle of the insurance mechanism.

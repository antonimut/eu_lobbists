Our views on 
The European Health Data Space (EHDS) 
 
Executive Summary 
 Bayer strongly supports the European Union’s (EU) efforts to increase patient and citizen access to 
and control of their health data through the proposal for a Regulation on a European Health Data 
Space (EHDS). In addition, the removal of barriers to healt h data for scientific research will mean 
that patients will benefit from the discovery of innovative treatments, medical devices, and 
diagnostics.  
 
Data Sources and Interoperability 
 A federated data network that ensures re -usability of health data via common standards is highly 
welcomed. To this end, existing frameworks and internationally agreed common data models that 
address different data domains, and ensure interoperability and data re-usability, should be applied. 
 Fragmentation and varying interpretation or application of the Regulation across EU Member States 
must be avoided to ensure that the vision of the EHDS can be achieved. This can best be 
accomplished by ensuring standardization, harmonization, and interoperability within and beyond 
the EU.  
 The provisions relating to datasets do not specify any time limits for when electronic health data is 
no longer relevant for an access request or grace periods during which access requests do not need 
to be complied with. Bayer would request that the EHDS Regulation set out such limits.  
 
Data Access and Flows 
 Bayer welcomes the fact that Industry is recognised as a key stakeholder in healthcare research and 
delivery and that the draft Regulation enables access to data for research purposes. 
 Bayer stresses the importance of maintaining international data flows for research purposes, via 
international data transfers and access to health data. This is critical, as research divisions are 
located all over the world and the ability to exchange data can l ead to unprecedented discoveries 
and improvements in the safety and efficacy of medicine. 
 
Data Privacy and Security 
 Bayer welcomes the fact that the EHDS Regulation provides a legal basis for secondary use of health-
related data because this provides additional legal certainty. 
 A raft of other Regulations affecting clinical data apply in parallel to the proposed EHDS Regulation 
(for example GDPR1 and CTR2) and Bayer urges for consistency in application of this Regulation with 
other existing legislation at both EU and national level.   
 Similarly, a unified interpretation of terms such as sufficient ‘anonymisation’, ‘pseudonymisation’, 
‘scientific research’ and ‘secondary use of data’ is essential to drive harmonisation and resolve the 
current fragmented impl ementation and different approaches when it comes to data access for 
research.                                                                                                                                                          Cont.

 Bayer welcomes the fact that the draft legislation states that all measures necessary to protect IP 
rights and preserve the confidentiality of trade secrets must be taken. However, more clarity on the 
mechanisms by which how trade secrets will be protected is essential. This includes clinical trial 
data, which if shared too early, could potentially unblind trials as well as give away IP.   
 
Governance 
 Bayer welcomes the mandatory creation of Health Data Access Bodies and Digital Health Authorities 
in Member States. However, coordination, harmonization, and a consistent implementation 
approach across access bodies at EU and Member State level will be critical to enable the success of 
the EHDS.  
 All stakeholders, including Industry, should be involved in Data Access Bodies to ensure that the 
challenges all stakeholders face are considered. 
 Given the proposed Regulation covers all electronic health data and  given existing European 
requirements around sharing of clinical trials data, clarity is needed on the expected additional 
contribution for sharing clinical trial and genomics data, and the timing of doing so. Additionally, 
clear thresholds and stakeholder  roles and responsibilities within the EHDS are needed to create 
certainty. 
General Comments  
Bayer strongly supports the EU’s efforts to increase citizen and patient access to and control of 
their health data, while at the same time giving researchers and innovators the opportunity to 
realize the potential of health data in a trusted and secure way . Removing access barriers to 
health data for scientific research will propel innovation activities and means that patients can 
and might benefit in the future from the discovery of innovative treatments, medicines, medical 
devices and diagnostics.  
The European Health Data Space is an unprecedented opportunity to create a favorable 
environment which ensures that the European Health industry remains an innovator and world 
leader in the development and manufacture of medicines, supporting faster access to care for 
patients. Tomorrow’s breakthroughs in medicine and broader healthcare solutions rely on an 
appropriate framework that supports research tod ay. Establishing an interoperable framework 
and a trustworthy setting will also support the development of data-driven approaches at scale. 
These will optimize the planification and use of healthcare resources, enabling the potential of 
the EHDS to improve healthcare delivery and ultimately the efficiency of healthcare systems. 
Data Sources 
Bayer strongly supports the ambition to ensure data in the EHDS are of high quality and 
harmonized, with clarity regarding data ownership, and flexibility of data categories for use 
and reuse. Existing frameworks that ensure data re -usability should be applied to ensure the

quality of the data and harmonization of different data sources. Data must be of high quality to 
enable secondary use.  
Bayer welcomes the categories of health data falling within the remit for use and re -use being 
as broad as possible . This will allow flexibility and enable future use of sources of data not 
currently being routinely collected. However, given the proposed Regulation seems to cover  all 
electronic health data, clear thresholds and stakeholder roles and responsibilities within the 
EHDS are needed to create certainty. Additionally, given existing European requirements around 
the sharing of clinical trials data, clarity is needed on the expected contribution for sharing clinical 
trial and genomics data.  
The electronic health data from clinical trials could contain non -patient related personal 
information together with proprietary information that is classified as commercially confidential 
and therefore protected from publication under the GDPRi and CTRii. The safeguards of the EHDS 
need to ensure that the principle of open access to any “legal person” enshrined in this 
Regulation does not inadvertently breach those rights and protections conferred under other 
existing legislation. Clinical trial data should only be shared with qualified researchers seeking to 
answer a sound scientific question. Clinical trial data should only be published in accordance with 
the checks and balances already in place within the EU in relation to the Public Access to 
Documents Regulationiii, GDPRi, CTRii and European Medicines Agency (EMA) Policy 0070iv which 
should be mirrored in the EHDS Regulation.  
The provisions relating to datasets do not specify any time limits for when electronic health data 
first becomes available or is  no longer relevant for an access request, nor does it specify grace 
periods during which access requests do not need to be complied with. We would request that 
the EHDS Regulation sets out such limits, specifically that: 
 no retrospective data or data from ongoing clinical trials can be requested; 
 different limits are applied depending on the category of electronic health data; and 
 specific periods and situations are identified in the Regulation during which no data is shared 
(e.g., until after a decision has been reached on the application for a marketing authorization).  
With respect to transparency of data quality and availability of datasets, Bayer strongly supports 
the establishment of an EU Datasets Catalogue. This would connect the national catalogues  of 
datasets of each Member State to each other and enable accurate implementation of the data 
quality and utility label of data sources. Bayer would welcome the opportunity for data holders 
to be consulted on defining the metadata for the Catalogue. Bayer would also welcome clarity as 
to the content and structure of these catalogues and the role they may play in defining the scope 
of data access requests.

The concept of supporting the improvement of the original database and  further use of 
enriched datasets will enhance the value of the EHDS. However, this should only be applied to 
the clinical setting where great value to patient care can be added rather than to research 
settings in which there will be numerous challenges regarding maintaining the scientific integrity 
of datasets that would make the process unfeasible. Additionally , it would add clarity and value 
to ensure that criteria are established for what constitutes an improvement or enrichment in the 
data that must be returned to the data holder, when this must be returned, and any obligations 
of the data holder related to the enriched data.  
Interoperability 
Bayer strongly supports enabling access to data between different providers at the sub-national, 
national, and pan -European level to sup port research, development, and clinical care. A 
federated data network  that ensures re -usability of health data via common standards would 
be highly welcomed. 
Bayer agrees that the relevance of different categories of electronic health data for different 
healthcare scenarios varies, as do levels of maturity in standardization, resulting in the 
implementation of mechanisms for their exchange being more or less complex depending on the 
category. Therefore, Bayer is supportive of improvement of interoperabili ty and data sharing 
being a gradual process and that there should be prioritization of categories of electronic health 
data that will be exchanged. Bayer would urge the Commission to ensure that a roadmap is put 
in place which clarifies current and future priority categories to allow stakeholders to prepare 
adequately. 
In relation to standardization, common quality standards, formats, content, and requirements 
will be essential for interoperability  and linking of electronic health record systems and other 
sources of health data. Bayer would strongly urge that technical and semantic specifications are 
commonly agreed and there is alignment with other activities for furthering the maturity of 
global standardization and harmonization as well as existing standar ds that address different 
data domains. To that end, Bayer would propose the inclusion of a specific Annex to the EHDS 
Regulation which lists the required standards for specific domains: “Essential requirements on 
interoperability" that need to be defined and based on international standards already mandated 
at EU level. This includes the ISO Identification of Medicinal Products (IDMP) as mandated in the 
Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) No 520/2012, Chapter IV, Article 25, which mandates 
“use of inte rnationally agreed terminologies" and Article 26, which mandates the “use of 
internationally agreed formats and standards”.

Interoperability between data sources with Common Data Models that address different data 
domains, should be implemented to respect  and ensure health data principles are effectively 
applied, such as privacy by design 1. This would also allow for large scale data analysis and 
research covering large populations in different healthcare systems and geographic areas.  
Data access and flow 
Bayer supports the harmonization of access to data for secondary use across EU Member States 
with common principles for sharing aggregated data and consistency in the way data access is 
requested. This should include a coordinated approach to sharing data outside the EU for 
research purposes, since research organizations are global operatio ns and need to share data 
across jurisdictions to conduct research efficiently. The EHDS Regulation should ensure such data 
sharing is properly protected but not hindered.   
The provision for mutual recognition of data permits across Members States should be 
strengthened to create efficiency by enabling one access point and permit. 
The timeline of 2 months for the data holder to make the requested data available to the data 
access body should be considered and evaluated by the EHDS Board to ensure appropriateness. 
If defined as appropriate, the ‘exceptional cases’ under which this may be extended for an 
additional 2 months should be further clarified to facilitate timelines being consistently met. 
The Regulation specifies that the maximum duration of a data permit will be 10 years (5 years 
plus a potential single renewal of 5 years). The wording of the Regulation in this regard should 
be modified to align with the legal requirements imposed on data holders by other legislation 
(e.g., CTRii and Pharmacovigilance Legislationv) and to ensure that source data used for scientific 
research can be retained in order to provide proof of scientific integrity.  
With regard to fees to be paid for data access for secondary use  of health data, Bayer would 
encourage adoption of a fee structure that is proportionate to the data type, quality, size of 
dataset and intended use. 
Data Privacy and Security 
To achieve the key objectives of the proposed Regulation, it will be crucial to reach consistency 
in interpretation and application of this Regulation throughout EU Member States and to reach 
a common understanding among EU Data Protection Supervisors and EHDS governance bodies 
of key GDPR i and EHDS-relevant terms. Conflicting interpretation of terms in the GDPR such as

“scientific research”, “secondary use of data”, “anonymization” and “pseudonymization” are 
proving to be a major obstacle in enabling access to data and conducting research i n Europe. 
Efforts fostering uniformed interpretation of legal terms would streamline research processes, 
make Europe more attractive for R&D investments and boost its competitiveness at the global 
level.  To this end, the European Commission should issue g uidance on the standard of 
anonymization required, or at a minimum the EHDS Board should issue guidance on the principles 
of anonymization and pseudonymization, when pseudonymized data may be requested and how 
the decision is made on whether it is provided . Such guidance needs to carefully balance the 
need to protect personal data with the need to utilize such data. Thus, Bayer would encourage 
the Board to ensure that the guidance when developed establishes a process that is pragmatic 
and works in practice.  
Bayer welcomes the fact that the EHDS Regulation provides a legal basis for secondary use of 
health-related data because this can provide additional legal certainty. However, national data 
privacy legislation exists in parallel to the GDPRi in some Member States resulting in the need for 
consent of the natural person for processing of their personal data including health-related data 
for primary and/or secondary purposes. To avoid an ambiguous situation developing regarding 
the legal bases and consent req uirements for using health-related data for secondary purposes 
once the EHDS is implemented, Bayer urges the Commission to make clear in the Regulation text 
which legislation takes precedence . Additionally, it will be critical to work towards common 
definitions and interpretations of respective terms (e.g., secondary use of data), across different 
legislations. 
The EHDS Regulation would also benefit from clarity regarding who will anonymize data sets , 
according to what anonymization methods,  and whether the cost of anonymization is to be 
borne by the data holder. 
To that end, the Regulation would also benefit from clarity regarding the  interplay between 
GDPRi, CTRii and the EHDS Regulation particularly regarding the role of consent-based collection 
of data and whether data collected on the basis of consent can be used for scientific purposes 
independently of what use has been agreed to in the original consent. Moreover, there needs to 
be regulatory and legal certainty over the rules for Industry to process and utilize data. Clear 
definition of datasets that can be re-used for defined purposes, clarity on fees for re-use of data, 
together with clarity on who can analyze what data and over what timelines are needed.  
The Regulation specifies that when  the results of the research  could assist in the treatment of 
the natural person concerned, the data user should inform the health data access body, which 
may inform the natural person or their healthcare professional. Bayer would urge that it is 
clarified in the Regulation that this applies only to the clinical setting and does not apply to clinical

trials or secondary use of data by Industry. In these settings, unblinding of results could damage 
clinical research, or because no identifying information on respective individuals are processed 
and therefore providing information to such individuals might result in “disproportionate efforts” 
or “could be impossible” as described in Article 14 (5)(b) of the GDPRi. 
In addition, clarity is needed regarding the conditions under which data holders are not obliged 
to provide data for secondary use . For example, when the secondary use has generated data 
that is commercially confidential and should not be disclosed or when the data has been obtained 
under a consent that did not include permission for such a disclosure of the data. There should 
be provision within Article 46 for protection of trade secrets and commercially confidential 
information. The EHDS Regulation requires data users to make public the results or output of the 
secondary use of electronic health data, including information relevant for the provision of 
healthcare, no later than 18 months after the completion of the electronic health data processing 
or after having received the answer to the data request referred to in Article 47.  This timeline is 
not in line with provisions in other legislation  where disclosure is linked to the marketing 
authorization application or where redaction of such information is permitted and should be 
amended.  
The Regulation requires public authorities and Regulators to have access to data benefiting from 
specific legal protection such as intellectual property from medical device companies or 
pharmaceutical companies. This is for the purpose of public and regulatory activities for instance, 
in the event of pandemics, to verify defective devices and protect human health, and to carry out 
their legal mandate, while complying with, where relevant and possible, the protection enjoyed 
by commercial data. We urge that  circumstances under which commercially confidential 
information must be provided to public and regulatory authorities should be clearly defined 
having due regard to the specific legal protection that exists in other Union legislation.   
Similarly, in relation to making electronic health data available for secondary use, details of the 
methods that may be used to preserve commercially confidential information and trade secrets 
should be specified (e.g., redaction of such data by all stakeholders). Bayer believes that IP rights 
such as patents already provide a balanced system where the applicant of a patent needs to 
publish its invention and only then is entitled to get a patent granted if all patentability criteria 
are fulfilled. This syst em should remain untouched as it provides for transparency, and in turn 
fosters innovation. However , the point in time at which commercially confidential data should 
be provided needs to be specified so that trade secrets can continue to be protected (e.g. , 
disclosure should be limited to the post-marketing authorization phase for medicines).

Governance 
Bayer supports the proposed participatory governance model with the right to request access 
to data for secondary use (with application of strong safeguar ds for security and privacy). This 
right should not be limited by the type of user, provided the request to access data is in line with 
the purposes set out in the Regulation which  includes scientific  research, development, and 
innovation activities. 
Bayer emphasizes that a harmonized approach to interpretation  and implementation of the 
Regulation is required so that scientific research is not hindered. This includes harmonized 
principles on data governance, quality, and interoperability. 
Bayer welcomes the  mandatory creation of Health Data Access Bodies and Digital Health 
Authorities in Member States. They are key enablers of the vision set out in the draft Regulation; 
promoting and facilitating the reuse of health data whilst ensuring the strong safeguards  for 
security and privacy outlined in the Regulation.  However, with the potential creation of multiple 
access bodies within each Member State, in addition to existing Member State Digital Health 
Authorities, there is a clear risk that there may be differe nces in implementation and 
enforcement of the provisions in the EHDS Regulation. A fragmented approach to interpretation 
and implementation of the Regulation could damage future scientific research and hinder the 
goals of the EHDS being met, reducing its e ffectiveness and utility. Bayer believes that 
coordination, harmonization, and a consistent implementation approach across bodies at EU 
and Member State level will be critical to enable the success of the EHDS.  
Bayer therefore welcomes the proposal to create the EHDS Board that will facilitate the 
cooperation between Digital Health Authorities and Health Data Access Bodies and contribute to 
the consistent application of the EHDS regulation within Member States and throughout the EU. 
Involvement in the Health Data Access Bodies for all stakeholders, including Industry, should be 
ensured and should be consistent across all Member States. This will leverage expertise and 
lessons learnt by all stakeholders that are wo rking to establish an optimal data and digital 
ecosystem. In addition, Bayer would urge that Health Data Access Bodies also cooperate with 
data holders in addition to stakeholders, including patient organizations, representatives from 
natural persons, healthcare professionals, researchers, and ethical committees in the exercise of 
their tasks to ensure equal and equitable representation by all stakeholders.  
Overall, consistency with other data related initiatives  (GDPRi, AI Actvi, Data Governance Actvii, 
Data Actviii, Trade Secrets Regulationix etc.) and other sectoral legislation (MDRx, IVDRxi, CTRii, and 
Pharma Acquis xii) is imperative to ensure consistent application of the terms, definitions, and

About Bayer

major challenges presented by a growing and aging global population. Bayer is committed to driving 
sustainable development and generating a positive impact with its businesses. At the same time, the Group 
aims to increase its earning power and create value through innovation and growth. The Bayer brand stands 
for trust, reliability and quality throughout the world. In fiscal 2021, the Group employed around 100,000 
people and had sales of 44.1 billion euros. R&D expenses before special items a mounted to 5.3 billion 
euros.  
Our Pharmaceuticals division concentrates on prescription products, especially for cardiology and women’s 
healthcare, and on specialty therapeutics focused on the areas of oncology, hematology, ophthalmology

range of key products that are among the world’s leading pharmaceuticals for their indications. The

pharmacies and hospitals.

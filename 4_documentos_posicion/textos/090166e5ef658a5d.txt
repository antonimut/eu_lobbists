European Health Data Space - proposal for a regulation 
European Cancer Organisation’s vision contributing to the European Commission’s 
feedback consultation 
July 2022 
 
The European Health Data Space  (EHDS) could be a game changer for the healthcare and research 
sectors. If properly implemented, EHDS represents the opportunity to bring much-needed efficiencies 
to healthcare, by facilitating health data sharing , use for primary and secondary purposes, improve 
efficiency in delivery of (cancer) care. 
 
The ambition of the EHDS should be to contribute to faster patient access to new treatments :  the 
EHDS has to unleash the power of data for the benefit of patients , by enabling an environment that 
can provide rich insights to inform medical research and find new cures or define new standards of 
care.  
 
While t he European Cancer Organisation  (ECO) welcomes the legislative proposal and a common 
European commitment on matters like the quality of health data, data governance and 
interoperability, we believe that the following aspects should be addressed to ensure a successful 
Digital Health transition: 
 
 
• Stakeholder engagement & patient centricity. 
The starting point of any initiative pertaining to health data should be what matters most to 
the improvement of patient care. In this respect, the main objectives of the EHDS cannot be 
achieved without the necessary stakeholder involvement and promoting citizen ’s trust: 
patients, clinicians and researchers’ engagement should be better defined within the EHDS by 
developing relevant roles and mechanisms for cooperation. Stakeholder engagement should 
be fundamentally integrated into the governance of the EHDS with specific provisions, 
ensuring that health data exchange in Europe is based on the core principles of openness and 
transparency.  
Particularly, the legislation sets out the creation of a European Health Data Space Board. ECO 
makes the suggestion that patient, healthcare professionals and all other relevant 
stakeholders groups representation on the Board should be secured, alongside further 
guarantees on the transparent functioning of that Board.  
 
• Regulatory harmonisation & clarification

associated to the GDPR. Reading the EHDS legal proposal, it remains a risk that

• Interoperability of health data  
To fully exploit the potential of exchanging health data, it is essential to ensure that the various 
sources of health data are able to “talk” to each other: health data should be findable, 
accessible, interoperable and reusable  (FAIR). Although significant progress has been made, 
full interoperability remains to be achieved.  
ECO believes that the EHDS, in its current format, could be more fully framed  to address the 
fragmentation of the European health data landscape.  
o In order to further boost interoperability of health data exchanges, ECO recommends 
that EU targets/indicators for interoperability of health data exchanges be committed 
to in the EHDS legislation, mainly in the form of guidance on standards, leveraging the 
learnings from the Electronic Health Records format.  
o In addition, ECO highlight s that traditional centralized architectures for health data 
sharing has shown its pitfalls and building the EHDS on a federated structure might be 
a solution to the most common health data sharing challenges. 
 
ECO highlights that, in discussing the EHDS implementation, the European Commission should 
consider the different levels of readiness to implement the provisions of the EHDS and the necessary 
support for some Member States. Targeted forms of funding support, such as is being made available 
to achieve an EU Network of Comprehensive Cancer Care Centres, is suggested to help bridge these 
gaps.

Johnson & Johnson’s Contribution to the  
Public Consultation on the European Health Data Space 
28 July 2022 
 
 Johnson & Johnson welcomes the EU Commission Regulation on a European Health Data 
Space (EHDS) intended as a patient- and consumer-centric data ecosystem unlocking the 
power of data based on the full respect of ethics, privacy and security principles.  Its 
objectives should be to empower citizens & patients and to foster healthcare innovation, 
including the development of innovative health solutions, medicines and medical 
technologies, and help improve healthcare delivery, disease prevention, diagnosis, health 
policymaking. 
 
If the EHDS maintains the right level of ambition, it has the potential to provide a step 
change across healthcare. By tearing down silos and connecting previously segregated 
information, it could help deliver better medicines, diagnostics, medical technologies, and 
consumer health products. Key upsides from accessing and re-using high quality, 
interoperable data range from facilitating the discovery of new treatments and vaccines, 
digitalisation of surgery, delivery of personalized care and improving patient and 
healthcare professional experience, tracking of disease transmission in real-time, 
epidemiological research and its application in clinical practice, from developing digital 
twins and external control arms for clinical trials to utilising RWD/RWE for research and 
regulatory decision-making as well as HTA/payer purposes. These will all significantly help 
decision-makers and industry to target investment more effectively. Access to health data 
will also be critical for the development of robust Artificial Intelligence / Machine Learning 
(AI/ML) solutions, for instance for use in clinical research, clinical decision support 
systems, remote patient monitoring, public health management or patient support 
solutions.  For the integration of AI/ML algorithms into the clinical IT ecosystems of 
European healthcare providers, AI/ML developers from science and industry need access 
to  data from clinical care e.g. from EHR systems.  
 
 Private and public stakeholders should work hand in hand to build a trustworthy EHDS, 
therefore we welcome the participatory governance model envisaged in the Regulation 
and we urge for the inclusion of the healthcare industry on the EHDS Board, alongside 
other stakeholders. The composition should be varied to benefit from multistakeholders’ 
experience and knowledge and understand their challenges. 
 
 Johnson & Johnson would like to reinforce that the EHDS should build on federated data 
networks, which can remove the barriers to accessing health data while ensuring the 
highest level of protection of personal data and commercial IP.  
The pharmaceutical and medical technologies industries can offer lessons learned with the 
development of federated data networks most notably via the European Health Data and

Evidence Network (EHDEN) public private partnership. EHDEN mission is to develop a 
federated network of Data Partners harmonized to a common data model (OMOP OHDSI) 
to enable execution of research across disparate database types and locations. J&J has 
also set up the Haematology Outcomes Network in Europe (HONEUR) to partner with

Drawing on this experiences, J&J advocates for the adoption and development of

quality standards. 
 
 We think that the success of EHDS will depend a lot on the ability to strike the right 
balance between facilitating data access (to data users) and responsibilities and 
obligations to share data (as data holders) while protecting trade secrets and IP. 
The EHDS must give clearer assurances around how IP and trade secrets will be protected 
when data is requested from a data holder including in times of public emergency (which 
is also a provision included in the draft Data Act). 
Trust is essential for data sharing and unlock the potential of health data. It can advance 
medicine knowledge and ultimately improve public health. A relevant example is the Yale 
Open Data Access (YODA) Project, founded to promote data sharing among the scientific 
community and advance responsible data sharing. YODA provides increased access to 
anonymised pharmaceutical and medical device clinical trial data and clinical study reports 
provided by businesses supporting the initiative. A panel in the project independently 
reviews and makes final decisions on all requests from qualified researchers, physicians 
and investigators looking to access such data for the benefit of healthcare innovation. We 
support this type of initiatives, which enhance transparency while respecting businesses’ 
rights to data confidentiality. 
 
 Healthcare stakeholders, including industry will need more legal clarity on some aspects 
of the proposal to better understand its impact on industry and broader society. There is 
a mismatch between some definitions in the EHDS Regulation and GDPR as well as some 
unclarity regarding the interplay between EHDS Regulation with the Data Governance Act 
and the proposed Data Act and AI Act, and sectoral legislation such as the Medical Device 
Regulation and the Clinical Trials Regulation.  
There is for example some uncertainty related to the definition of EHR-systems and 
whether their scope include medical devices and “high-risk AI”. This is paramount to help 
companies understand when they need to go through the self-certification scheme for 
EHR-Systems. Duplication and confusion will result in a poorer development, selection and 
quality of these products and services in Europe. 
Ambiguity can lead to fragmentation. Instead, an alignment between GDPR and EHDS 
Regulation will be crucial to support the goals of the EHDS and foster scientific research. A 
harmonized interpretation and implementation of the EHDS Regulation for secondary use 
of data needs a harmonised interpretation of GDPR regarding responsible re-use of health 
data and Health Data Access Bodies to promote the adoption of multi-stakeholder 
Compacts / Codes of Conduct. These efforts should accelerate access and processing of

such data in a harmonized manner within each, and across all, Member States in 
cooperation with all public and private stakeholders. The Compacts should look into 
procedures, types of data, purposed for data re-use, fee structures, IP and trade secret

 A core component of this legislation will be the empowerment of patients to control and 
access their own electronic health data. An easily understandable framework for patients 
to access and share their data would unlock a wealth of data for the delivery of care and 
the development of crucial health innovations. 
 
 Innovation in healthcare and pharmaceutical R&D are global activities. Europe needs a 
clear framework for data transfers to third countries in order to remain competitive on 
the global scene. In the proposed Regulation, it is unclear which rules will apply for the 
transfer of personal and non-personal data outside the EU. Fostering international 
networks will help the EU to develop a robust R&D and increase their competitiveness in 
the data economy.  
 
 Finally, we think that to make the EHDS a reality in the EU we will need to work on more 
than just the legal text by building the necessary capabilities, making sure to commit 
sufficient funding to establish the necessary infrastructures in the countries, and boosting 
skills and raising awareness amongst civil society. Private and public stakeholders should 
join forces in raising awareness about the value of sharing health data, for example via 
ongoing initiatives such as the TEHDAS Joint Action.

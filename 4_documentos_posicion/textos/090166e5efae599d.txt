 
EDF welcomes the Commission’s ambition in its proposal for regulation on nature protection. EDF 
shares the view that action is imperative and supports binding targets to ensure the rest oration of 
ecosystems at European level. We also acknowledge the fact that each habitat has its own 
particularities and welcome the differentia ted approach  within the criteria d efined for each 
ecosystem. However, we would like to stress the need for clarity and coherence between European 
and national legislation. The production of decarbonised energy goes hand in hand with  the 
enhancement of biodiversity. It is essential t o reconcile them in the best possible way and exploit 
potential synergies. Finally, we underline the need for EU -based low-carbon and renewable energy 
sources to achieve EU climate targets and the EU’s energy sovereignty. 
Firstly, we welcome the identification and definition of restoration criteria  for all ecosystems . 
However, some uncertainties remain, especially on the evaluation of the ecosystems’ condition and 
on the implementation of national plans. For example: 
- Gathering historical data going back 70 years will be a real challenge in France. How do we 
address situations where data is unavailable or too costly to collect? 
- Should restoration of lost ecosystems be required only for those which would have a chance 
to develop again in the context of climate change?   
- Will the status of restoration be measured at national level or for each individual ecosystem? 
And if so, on what conditions would the fundings  be based on and how would they be 
distributed?  
These uncertainties make it difficult to assess the impacts of the Regulation o n the territory and risk 
hindering the effective implementation of restoration measures. The guidelines planned for 2024 will 
be most appreciated and needed as soon as possible. 
We also welcome the comprehensive approach advocated by the Commission concerning Member 
States’ (MS) rest oration plans.  Indeed, we support the proposed assessment of the ecosystems’ 
favourable reference area which takes into account the projected changes due to climate change (art. 
11). This would allow prioritizing the most efficient measures to implement as well as having a 
reference baseline scenario for comparison once the measures are in place. In addition, we support 
the global evaluation of the co -benefits (social, economic and environmental) associated w ith 
restoration measures (art. 12-j). 
Concerning the restoration of free-flowing rivers, we ask for strong coherence with national legislation. 
In this sense, the reference made to the Water Framework Directive is positive and necessary since it 
has alread y been integrated in MS’ energy plans. However, a clear definition of what constitutes 
“25 000 km of free-flowing rivers” should be made available and unified at European level.  
Finally, we support the reference to the notion of “overriding public interes t” but adjustments are 
needed for greater clarity . To ensure coherence across legislation , we recommend using the same 
wording as mentioned in the Habitats Directive, i.e. “imperative reason of overriding public interest”. 
In addition, in the current context of energy crisis and to reach the EU climate ambition, we underline 
the need for all decarbonised sources of energy: wind, solar, hydro, nuclear.  All projects of 
decarbonised energy sources  contributing to the EU energy sovereignty should be considered 
“imperative reason of overriding public interest” .  The independent production of clean energy will 
contribute to the fight against climate change and can, either directly or by a knock-on-effect, enhance 
biodiversity protection.  
EDF has built a pro-active biodiversity programme and is committed to seek "win-win" solutions that 
ensure a clean energy transition, while providing greater biodiversity protection and societal benefits.

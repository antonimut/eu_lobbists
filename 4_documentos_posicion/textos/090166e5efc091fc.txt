 
 
 
 
 Brussels, 5th August 2022 
 
 
Copa and Cogeca’s Feedback on the European 
Commission’s Proposal for a Revision of the Part on 
Food Waste Reduction Targets of Directive 
2008/98/EC on Waste 
 
Copa and Cogeca appreciate the opportunity to comment on the European Commission’s 
Roadmap Inception Impact Assessment (IIA) on the Proposal for a revision of the part on the 
food waste reduction targets of Directive 2008/98/EC on Waste. 
 
Copa and Cogeca is fully committed to reducing food waste. Over the past few years, the 
farmers and agri-cooperatives we represent have actively engaged in various projects and 
programmes aiming at reducing food waste in primary production and the processing stages of 
the food chain. They have developed many programmes focusing on finding new outlets for co- 
products that were previously overlooked. They have launched actions focusing on shortening 
the food chain to limit waste by developing direct sales. They have collaborated with academia 
and national authorities to develop methods for food waste diagnosis which allow them to 
implement optimization programmes for food waste reduction in food processing. They have 
also engaged in awareness raising campaigns to improve consumers’ understanding of labelling 
and promote reducing food waste at home. Finally, they have also actively participated in food 
donation activities, and they have worked on re-purposing food that would have been wasted 
due to unavoidable food surplus when it could not be donated (e.g. production of hand 
sanitizer with surplus wine or fertiliser with surplus fruits and vegetables). Farmers and agri- 
cooperatives have now fully assimilated that food waste reduction is not only a social and 
environmental responsibility, it is also an economic opportunity. 
 
We thus welcome the Commission proposal to fix reduction targets for Member States, 
however, this process needs to follow a carefully designed and incremental plan as set in 
Directive 851/2018/EC amending Directive 2008/99/EC on waste. Furthermore, it is crucial 
that the path to the reduction of food waste carefully take into account the imbalances of power 
in the food value chain so as to ensure a fair distribution of responsibility between all actors. In 
this sense, we would like to present in the comments below our position regarding the different 
options proposed as well as some aspects that we believed should be improved or further 
explored: 
 
1. Policy options for the scope, the expression and the way the targets 
should be set for Member States

In the context of this initiative, and as expressed in Article 9(6) of Directive 2008/99/EC, in 
2023, the Commission will consider the feasibility of establishing food waste reduction targets 
for 2030. However, as established in Article 8(g) of this same directive, the objective is to 
follow the objective of the Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) 12.3, which is to halve per 
capita food waste at the retail and consumer levels and reduce it at the production and 
processing stages. We thus would like to underline that when it comes to the primary 
production and processing stages, the target set by both the directive and SGD 12.3 is to reduce 
food waste, but not by 50%, as is the case for the retail and consumers stages. These different 
levels reflect the fact that most of the food waste takes place at the consumer stage (more than 
50%) and that the small part occurring in primary production (around 11%) is closely linked to 
policies set at the retail stage. It is consequently very complicated for the primary stage to 
reduce its food waste compared to other stages. We thus strongly argue that food waste targets 
for the primary production and processing stages should not be set at the same levels as the 
consumer and retail stages, and we favour option S2: setting targets covering only the retail 
and consumer stages. 
 
It is also important to underline that food losses fall out of the scope of Directive 2008/99/EC 
and that food waste does not include plants prior to harvesting as expressed in Article 3(4A) of 
the Directive referring to Article 2 of Regulation (EC) No 178/2002. In addition, agricultural 
material used in farming or for the production of energy are not considered as food waste as 
established by Article 2(f) of Directive 2008/99/EC. 
 
Expression: 
We believe that the targets should be expressed in % of food waste reduction of the amount of 
food waste in the baseline year, because figures for absolute amounts are complicated to 
establish and we would have to fix different amounts per type of food waste to ensure their 
representativeness (e.g. the absolute amounts of waste for sugar beets in comparison to 
truffles). 
 
We cannot support the baseline of 2020 proposed by the Commission. Over the past few years 
a lot of efforts have been made to reduce food waste, and setting targets with 2020 as a basis 
would not take into account all the work already achieved. Using 2020 as a basis could result in 
setting impossible targets, because some actors have already reduced food waste to such an 
extent in the past 6 years that it would be impossible to reduce it much more. Consequently, we 
believe that, as set out in Directive 2008/99/EC and in SDG 12.3, the baseline year for targets 
should be 2014 or 2015. Furthermore, 2020 will not be a representative year for food waste 
because of all the disturbances that occurred in food supply chains due to the COVID-19 crisis. 
 
The way the targets are set for member states: 
We believe that the target level should be the same for all Member States as long as the 
baseline year is 2014 or 2015. This baseline is important considering that some Member States 
have heavily invested in food waste reduction over the past few years and it would thus be 
unfair not to take this into account by setting 2020 as baseline. 
 
2. Comparison of the expected environmental, economic and social 
costs and benefits for different levels of targets 
 
We welcome the comparison of the expected environmental, economic and social costs and 
benefits for different levels of targets as this would allow to choose levels taking into account

possible trade-offs. Nevertheless, it essential for this analysis to take into account that the 
environmental, economic and social costs and benefits need to be evaluated on an equal basis. 
 
3. Additional element to consider in the setting of targets 
 
Food waste occurring at the primary production stage is highly impacted by elements beyond 
the control of primary producers, such as unfair trading practices like last-minute cancellations 
of orders. Therefore, it is crucial that reduction targets, and the distribution of the sub-objectives 
and actions to achieve them, carefully take into account the imbalances of power in the food 
value chain so as to ensure a fair distribution of responsibility between all actors.

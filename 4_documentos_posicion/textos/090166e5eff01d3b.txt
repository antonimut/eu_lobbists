ACE’S CONTRIBUTION TO THE PUBLIC CONSULTATION ON THE 
REVISION OF THE WASTE FRAMEWORK DIRECTIVE 
 
 
 
ACE represents the beverage carton industry in Europe.  ACE’s members are the main beverage carton 
converters Elopak, S IG and Tetra Pak and their liquid packaging board suppliers, BillerudKorsnäs and 
Stora Enso.  Our members are committed to provide a low carbon sustainable and circular packaging 
solution to support resilient food systems. 
 
ACE welcomes the Commission’s public consultation on the revision of the Waste Framework Directive. 
The Waste Framework directive is the backbone to waste management and an essential tool to optimise 
waste management systems in support of higher recycling rates. 
 
The ACE members are committed to support increased recycling of beverage cartons increased collection 
and recycling of beverage cartons. Investments to recycle the fibre and the non -fibre components of 
beverage cartons will ensure an increased recycling of beverage cartons over the coming years. 
However, the predictability of the volumes collected is critical to sustain the investments and innovation 
in recycling. Separate collection is critical in this respect. 
 
ACE would like to share its views on some key principles, including waste hierarchy, essentiality of 
packaging, food waste prevention and separate collection of waste. 
 
 
Waste hierarchy 
 
We strongly support the Commission’s intention to safeguard  the waste hierarchy  according 
to which  measures that deliver the best overall environmental outcome shall be taken 1. This “may 
require specific waste stream departing from the waste hierarchy, where this is justified by life cycle 
thinking”2. It states that “… technical feasibility and economic viability, protection of resources, as well 
as human health, economic and social impacts should also been taken into account” 3. 
 
Turning waste into resources is key in waste prevention and can be achieved through  recycling or re-
use which are complementary approaches.  
 
Setting reuse targets for packaging is not necessarily the most environmental  sound option, as 
demonstrated through different studies4

Separate collection is the pre-condition to recycling 
 
An effective collection system is required for a well-functioning recycling system. Without collection 
and sorting packaging designed for recycling will not have access to the appropriate 
recycling process and hence cannot be recycled.  
 
Member States must effectively comply with their mandatory separate collection obligations under the 
WFD. The exemptions for mandatory separate collection of waste granted under Article 10(3) of the 
WFD should be minimised.  
 
Today, beverage cartons are recyclable and are recycled at scale. Recycling of beverage cartons in 
Europe (EU-28) has steadily increased over recent decades, with around 450,000 tonnes recycled in 
20195 (51%), with some countries, like Belgium or Germany, officially recording rates ove r 70%. But 
some Member States lag behind mainly due to poorly functioning collection schemes. 
 
ACE calls for a 90% collection target for fiber-based packaging  currently not reaching a 
70% collection rate. The increased volume of materials available for recycling, the predictability and 
the high-quality waste stream provide a strong incentive for investment and innovation in sorting and 
recycling technologies.  
 
One such example is beverage cartons. A study by Roland Berger 5 for the beverage carton 
industry shows that such a collection target would present many benefits including:6: 
o Significant savings in GHG emissions contributing to the EU climate ambitions (ca 190 k tons 
to 340 k tons reduction per year). 
o Contribution to the paper recycling rate and to Member States targets contributing to the EU 
circularity objectives and goals.  
o Level playing field for all packaging (beverage cartons are discriminated against their main 
competitor (PET) that have a collection target set up in the SUPD).  
o Increased traceability of recycling of beverage cartons. Member States report recycling per 
material and hence beverage cartons contribute to the paper and plastic recycling rates.  
o Harmonised approach across the EU – some Member States have a collection target for 
beverage cartons, while others support a collection target for used beverage cartons ste at EU 
level. 
o Incentive to increase investments in sorting and recycling –predictability of volumes collected 
would be beneficial and complementary to our industry’s continuous investments on recycling.  
o Administrative costs for Member States and the Commission would be negligible as the 
responsibility for reporting and aggregating data is with PROs (Producers Responsibility 
Organisations).  
 
 
EPR Fees should be based on the net cost principle 
 
EPR fees should focus on the main objective which is to ensure that the costs of collection, 
sorting and treatment are covered in case they are not covered by the value of the 
secondary raw material obtained (net-cost principle). The existing Design for Recycling 
guidelines should also be taken into consideration for EPR fees. Design for Recycling Guidelines should 
be based on industry’s expertise. ACE will soon issue its Guideline which will be reviewed on a regular 
basis.

Food waste and essentiality of packaging 
 
ACE welcomes the Commission’s objective to reduce food waste. Beverage cartons are 
instrumental in avoiding food waste as they allow to safely transport, use and store 
sensitive products such as milk and juice.  The essential role of packaging should be recognised 
while ensuring that packaging is fit for purpose and recyclable. All packaging need to be designed to 
optimally fit the product and deliver the needed functionality. 7 
 
Beverage cartons protect food and beverages products against degradation (spoiling) caused by light, 
air, and contaminants prolonging their shelf-life 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
ABOUT ACE 
ACE provides a European platform for beverage carton manufacturers and their paperboard suppliers to  
benchmark and profile cartons as renewable, recyclable and low carbon packaging solutions. Engaging  
with stakeholders and partners seeking high environmental stewardship, it contributes expertise to EU  
policy, legislation and standard-setting. 
ACE members include beverage carton producers Tetra Pak, SIG Combibloc and Elopak, who develop,  
manufacture and market systems for the packaging and distribution of food and produce packaging  
material at 20 plants in Europe. Most of the paperboard used by ACE members in beverage cartons in  
Europe is produced by Stora Enso in Skoghall (Sweden) and Imatra (Finland) and BillerudKorsnäs in Gävle 
and Frövi (Sweden), who are also members of ACE.

   
 
NIA feedback – Call for evidence on the initiative Chemical safety – better access 
to chemicals data for safety assessments (August 2022) 
 
The Nanotechnology Industries Association (NIA) welcomes the opportunity to provide initial 
feedback on the Commission initiative to present a proposal for a Regulation to streamline 
and improve access to chemicals data for the purpose of safety assessments.  
We would like to offer the following comments: 
• We reiterate our support for this initiative and its stated goals of improving efficiency 
and transparency in data sharing. This will be essential in avoiding duplication of efforts, 
which can impose an additional burden on industry; 
• We also welcome the stated goal of building on existing databases and improving 
awareness of their availability;   
• In reference to point 3, Section A of the background document: enabling the EU and 
national authorities to “commission the testing and monitoring of chemical substances 
[…] where further information is considered necessary” will need to be done in a 
coordinated manner, to avoid any duplication of efforts which might run contrary to the 
stated goals of this initiative; 
• The actions identified in the background document are grouped under the umbrella of 
the one-substance-one-assessment (OSOA) approach. We note that this approach might 
not be immediately applicable to chemicals in the nano dimension, since different 
nanoforms of the same substance can present different properties – an aspect which is 
reflected in the recent amendments to the REACH Annexes, and which we hope will be 
kept into consideration in the implementation stage; 
• Although the background document outlines why an impact assessment is not deemed 
necessary for this initiative, we would like to note that, depending on the details of its 
implementation and in particular the uptake of any novel IT-based tools and strategies, 
an additional burden might be imposed on industry. This could represent a 
competitiveness threat at least at the beginning of the implementation phase, which 
should be kept into account when considering the entry into force of the final Regulation 
and the phase-in of new obligations; 
• The further uptake of the FAIR (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, and Reusable) data 
principles, currently under development in several EU-backed initiatives, would 
contribute to the general objectives of this initiative. However, the principles are not yet 
fully implementable, with interoperability in particular being difficult to achieve at the 
moment. We would recommend that further research and adaptations of the principles 
are performed in order to incentivize their uptake in a consistent way for all economic 
operators in the chemical space, to ensure a level-playing field; 
• It should be noted that transparency in data reporting may only be applicable to 
validated methods at this stage, to avoid affecting research on new alternative 
methodologies (NAMs). Furthermore, intellectual property rights and confidential data 
should continue to be protected at all times;

• Finally, the NIA is active in several initiatives concerning databases development, the 
FAIR principles, as well as NAMs; we would therefore like to express our interest to be 
included in the upcoming targeted stakeholder consultation. 
We thank you for your attention, and remain available for any questions you may have.

Confederation of Danish Industry (DI) on the revision of the Waste framework 
Directive 
DI welcomes the ambition of the Commission to transform the EU’s linear economy to a circular economy. 
A well-functioning circular economy depends on EU harmonized measures and a well-functioning secondary 
raw material market. DI therefor welcomes the planned revision of the Waste Framework Directive (WFD) 
and hopes that the review of the WFD will be carried out in line with the policy objectives of the European 
Green Deal and the new Circular Economy Action Plan with the purpose of ensuring an innovation-driven 
policy agenda. DI supports the creation of a framework that encourage sustainable growth and both 
ambitious and economically viable solutions to scale-up circularity and make visible the role of circular 
economy measures in contributing to climate neutrality.  
 
Initially, DI wants to emphasize a general but crucial element in the revision of the WFD – the need for 
harmonization of policy measures. Among our members there is a growing concern that measures to 
increase recycling and waste reduction are implemented differently in the Member States – and especially 
that national initiatives are implemented in parallel with EU's initiatives. National initiatives on e.g. product 
requirements or criteria for modulated fees, standards and methods often constitute an obstacle to the 
internal market and for the upscaling of circular economy. 
  
In the following DI’s comments on the upcoming revision of the EU WFD especially focus on ways to ensure 
more and higher quality recycling, increase of waste reduction, harmonization of Extended Producer 
Responsibility schemes and reporting.  
 
An efficient market for secondary raw material 
Large separately collected and uniform waste streams throughout Europe are prerequisite for increased 
recycling rates in the EU and a crucial basis for delivering higher quality of recycled material.  
 
Harmonizing the waste collection system through the implementation of separate collection obligations 
and introducing an EU harmonized labelling system for collection material as well as for packaging will help 
consumers to correctly sort their recyclables and ensure more uniform waste volumes in the EU. With 
knowledge of the quantities and composition of the waste streams, the waste sector can to a greater extend 
target their development and investments within sorting and recycling technologies and facilities.  
 
An effective collection system and labeling, together with sorting and recycling infrastructure are essential 
to ensure recycling in a higher quality and to boost recycling rates. The link between packaging and Member 
States’ collection obligations must be adequately reflected in both the revised PPWD and WFD. 
 
We already have good experience to draw on. In 2017, a national pictogram system was launched in 
Denmark and in 2020 the system was launched in Norway, Sweden and Iceland. The plan is to launch it in 
Finland in 2022. Using the same waste symbols, citizens can receive the same communication on waste 
sorting, making it easier for all citizens to sort their waste correctly and thereby contributing to an increase 
in material recycling.

In addition to an efficient collection system a continuous demand for products with recycled content is 
important to create the right incentives. Green public procurement together with ecodesign criteria set in 
the upcoming ESPR could help stimulate the market. Also, the development of standards that determine 
the quality of the recycled materials would add to the efficiency of the market for secondary raw materials. 
Standards will create increased trust and transparency in the market for recycled materials. 
Finally, DI suggests that the EU Commission harmonize and ensure consistency in the rules for the 
classification of waste across EU Member States both when it comes to end-of-waste criteria and the by-
product regulation. It is crucial that authorities in different countries implement the rules uniformly. This will 
support companies’ continued efforts towards replacing virgin materials with recycled. 
 
Waste prevention 
Until now waste preventions measures have not been sufficiently prioritized either in the EU or in the 
individual member states. DI welcomes the Commission's focus on waste prevention and increase of re-use 
in the upcoming revision of the WFD and ask the Commission to set ambitious target for reduction of waste. 
 
Prevention should always be the first step. Waste prevention targets can be a driver for new circular business 
models. Companies are already in the process of developing new business models, where they create 
sharing schemes, re-use products or sell services instead of products. There needs to be further focus on 
incentivizing both consumers and companies to change old habits from the Linear Economy that create far 
too much waste to new ways of ensuring that raw materials are used optimally in companies and that 
products have longer lives. 
 
Important focus areas:   
- Scaling of re-use solutions 
- Giving products longer lives 
- Promoting circular business models 
- Green Public Procurement 
 
The development of supportive standards is necessary. It could be for the design of reusable packaging, for 
washing facilities and return systems. Such standards should further strengthen the re-use business as it 
would lead to a more harmonized and accepted infrastructure for the end-consumer. 
 
Supporting digitization as a driver for the circular economy and waste prevention is also an important factor. 
 
DI is concerned about the approach in the current WFD, which heavily relies on national measures to prevent 
waste. DI prefers harmonized legislation in the EU. As a minimum, it should be ensured that wherever there 
is harmonized legislation, e.g. in the ecodesign directive and the upcoming ESPR, it should be prevented 
that national legislation establishes barriers to the internal market. 
 
Furthermore, while DI wholeheartedly supports the focus on waste prevention in the WFD, we would also 
like to point out the risk of double regulation as other legislation, for example the ESPR and the PPWD, will 
also contribute towards reducing waste through specific measures.

Many companies have already begun a journey on preventing food waste, and food loss. Some have been

Extended Producer Responsibility Schemes (EPR) 
DI welcomes the extension of EPR to other product categories, such as textiles and oils. EPR is an effective 
tool to incentivize design of more circular products that can easily be recycled and re-used – and it has the 
potential to create efficient waste handling systems throughout Europe. Though if implemented differently 
in the member states, large parts of the potential will be lost.  
 
Fee modulation criteria in the different EPR Schemes (e.g. for EEE and packaging) must be harmonized 
across Member States. This, to send a clear and effective signal to producers on circular design criteria.  
 
The Eunomia report  to support the preparation of the Commission’s Guidance for Extended Producer 
Responsibility Schemes rightly highlighted that: ”fee modulation criteria across Member States should be 
harmonised. This is important not only to ensure the smooth functioning of the internal market, but also to 
maximise the potential for positive environmental change. All else being equal, the magnitude of the shift 
achieved by a certain level of fee modulation will be greater if it is replicated consistently across many - or better 
still all - Member States. The application of a consistent signal using harmonised criteria will give a much 
stronger, and indeed clearer, incentive for producers to, for example, change their packaging design, than if 
different criteria were applied across Member States”.  
 
Another step towards harmonizing and simplifying the Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) process is 
to create an EPR “one-stop-shop”, a centralized information portal providing the latest up to date 
information of different EPR requirements across all the European Member States, both at a national and 
product-specific level. Companies working across boarders use a lot of time familiarizing themselves with 
the EPR regulations in different countries. 
 
Harmonization of waste reporting  
Across the EU, waste reporting is not harmonized. In fact, each Member State has their own method to 
report waste-related information and to collect data required from producers. Companies that have facilities 
in different EU Member States are exposed to different regional and country-level methodologies when it 
comes to reporting and collecting waste data. It is critical that the revision of the WFD provides guidelines 
on the information that must be gathered in a standard manner to obtain high-quality meaningful data that 
can be compared across countries. This would furthermore support the initiatives on sustainable reporting 
and the EU taxonomy for sustainable activities.

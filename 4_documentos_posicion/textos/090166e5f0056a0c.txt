 
EDANA’S INPUT TO THE PUBLIC CONSULTATION ON THE  
THE WASTE FRAMEWORK DIRECTIVE 
August 2022 
 
EDANA, the association representing the nonwovens industry, appreciates the Commission’s Public 
Consultation on the revision of the EU Waste Framework Directive.  
 
Nonwovens are innovative, high-tech, engineered fabrics made from fibres. They are used in a wide 
range of consumer and industrial products either in combination with other materials or alone. 
Many nonwoven-based products are essential in nature and serve a health, environmental, social 
and hygienic purpose, such as medical nonwovens, geotextiles, absorbent hygiene products and 
protective clothing. 
 
EDANA would like to focus its contribution on Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) and is sharing 
its views to help regulators make informed decision with regards to future EPRs.  
 
Well-functioning EPRs are consistent with the polluter-pays principle and essential to achieving the 
ambitions of the EU’s Circular Economy Action Plan. All actors alongside the entire value chain share 
efforts to establish a better and more efficient collection, sorting and recycling infrastructure for 
post-consumer waste. At the same time, it is also essential to engage customers and consumers to 
achieve this goal. 
 
EDANA’s principles for new EPRs  
 
• New EPRs applying to new product categories should be thoroughly assessed and not conflict 
with already existing provisions set out in the WFD.  
• EPR should only be considered if there is a functioning, scalable and economic viable recycling 
technology available, that also makes sense from an overall environmental point of view and 
that allows to practically achieve an effective recyclability towards full circularity of 80% or 
more.  
• New EPRs should consider the availability of technology and processes to significantly increase 
the circularity and recovery of secondary raw materials to divert separate collected waste 
from landfilling or incineration. Full circularity is the norm, downcycling not an option. 
• In addition, we believe that EPR programs should be consistent with general Organisation for 
Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) guidance and have clearly defined objectives 
and desired results, be built upon realistic timelines, have a clearly defined scope and 
definition of “producer,” and require the participation of all eligible companies in a market. 
• New EPRs applying to new product categories should be evaluated against potential 
disproportionate costs. It should be noted that additional EPRs are cumulative to existing EPRs 
on packaging waste.  
• EPRs should only fund economically viable circular solutions and consider life cycle assessments 
to ensure sustainable products are not put at a disadvantage or that unsustainable products are 
wrongfully incentivised. 
• EPR systems must fund waste management infrastructure, not other government initiatives; 
be applied across a large enough region to support efficiency; harness the power of innovation 
to find new solutions based on science; be material-neutral and applied even-handedly and 
equally to all products in a category; and be managed by the industry in the form of producer 
responsibility organizations (PROs).

• New EPRs should not compromise the affordability of essential products. EPR should not nullify 
incentives for essential product access, for instance VAT reductions.  
• New EPRs should not negatively affect the performance of products.  
• Industry representatives should be consulted in the form of an expert or advisory group when 
EPRs are developed at EU level. 
• EPRs should be harmonised as much as possible between Member States to ensure a smooth 
operation of the Single Market. 
• The EPRs should operate with the lowest possible organisational complexity. The system should 
ideally build, where possible, on existing local systems and consider best practices.  
• The system should have the highest possible degree of transparency thereby ensuring 
accountability for the public, local authorities as well as producers.   
 
 
About EDANA

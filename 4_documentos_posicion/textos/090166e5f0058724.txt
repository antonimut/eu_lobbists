VANTAAN ENERGIA POSITION PAPER ON THE REVISION OF THE WASTE FRAMEWORK DIRECTIVE (WFD) Vantaan Energia welcomes the efforts of the European Commission under the upcoming reform of the Waste Framework Directive (WFD) to improve waste management and promote circular economy.  For Vantaan Energia, the following summarised policies are essential in this revision: § Giving waste management companies equal access to all waste streams is a more powerful tool than tax-based measures for ensuring the waste hierarchy is accurately followed § The revision should allow energy recovery only from non-recyclable waste and promote the usage of CCS and CCU in waste incineration § The WFD should remain technology-neutral since the best measures to achieve higher recycling rates may vary across Europe and over time      Key points for Vantaan Energia in the revision of WFD: 1.  Establish correct market incentives for waste management  With central importance, the revision should provide equal access to waste streams for all waste management companies. Vantaan Energia considers this is a substantially more powerful tool than tax-based measures for correctly implementing waste hierarchy in practice. To name an example, when household waste is exclusively collected by municipal waste management, the waste is regularly neither recycled most efficiently nor exposed to the best recycling technology. Therefore, the position of the public sector, including publicly owned or managed companies often possessing monopoly markets and large waste infrastructure, should be examined, including their exclusive rights to collect waste under the WFD revision.  Correspondingly, a tax on waste incineration is not an efficient way to promote recycling solutions. As supported by the study of Finland’s Prime Minister’s Office1, a tax on waste incineration is an ineffective mechanism since it results most probably in higher costs for households. Instead, Vantaan Energia supports the existing rules of the Waste Framework Directive, which encourage Member States to adopt economic incentives to implement the waste hierarchy on a voluntary basis. For this reason, Vantaan Energia encourages the European Commission to conduct an assessment report on the accessibility of waste streams to economic actors and of the impact on recycling rates across the Member States. This necessary comparison and sharing the best practices across EU Member States on waste management would consequently avoid unproductive additional costs in the value chain.   1 Prime Minister's Office, Finland. 2. Promote sustainable processes in waste-to-energy plants Within the revision of the WFD, Vantaan Energia wishes to highlight that waste-to-energy plants are an essential part of the circular economy chain and should be utilised as sustainably as possible. In pursuance of this objective, it is important that the revision incentivizes waste-to-energy plants to only utilize waste that cannot be recycled. It is clearly preferable to incinerate the inevitable waste rather than place it in landfills. Therefore, the objective of Vantaan Energia is to increase the recycling rates so that only unavoidable rejects end up in incineration.  Secondly, energy recovery from unsorted municipal waste or plastics utilizing solid recovered fuel (SRF) should be prohibited due to the harmful impact on the environment. Additionally, the definition of unrecyclable waste should be restricted as technology develops. Lastly, the revision should encourage solutions for carbon capture, use and storage technology (CCS and CCU) in waste incineration plants. Such technology is necessary to compensate for the unavoidable emissions produced by the waste treatment plants.2 Although the EU’s Innovation Fund has recently financed CCU and CCS projects in waste-to-energy plants, these supportive mechanisms need to be accelerated.   3. Support a technology-neutral approach and the relevance of data  In order to avoid unproductive consequences in the waste management sector, the WFD revision should abstain from favoring specific procedures or technologies. For instance, the legislation should not prioritize separate collection or centralized recycling facilities, as every operator in the circular economy chain has a different yet important role in recycling. Vantaan Energia sees that wide policy frameworks promoting various methods and focusing on the achievement of objectives provide the most effective incentives for sustainable practices. It is also necessary that the revision takes into account the growing importance of data in measuring recycling rates and in waste treatment at recovery facilities. Crucially, the required complementary role of recovery facilities to source separation plays an essential role in boosting recycling rates in a cost-effective manner. Therefore, the WFD revision should stimulate the use of data in recovery facilities and not only rely on source separation of waste.  Furthermore, the ownership of the data of waste composition should remain within the company collecting it. Sharing transparent information about the origin of waste and documentation of waste streams is also essential to ensure the quality of the waste received for further use.

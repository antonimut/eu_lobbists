EC public consultation/ Call for evidence: 
 
Chemical safety – better access to chemicals data for safety assessments (europa.eu) 
 
FEFANA position 
FEFANA1 is the united voice of the specialty feed ingredients 2 (SFIs) industry in the European Union. Specialty feed 
ingredients are key elements for optimized feed formulations. They enable more efficient and sustainable livestock 
production through reduced use of natural resources, less waste and livestock emissions, less reliance on antibiotics and 
high-quality animal products. 
FEFANA members welcome the EU initiative towards a better access to chemicals data as it is expected to have positive 
impacts on safety assessment through simplification and reduction of administrative burden,  while helping to minimize 
duplication of effort in data generation.  Although the assessment of the safety of chemicals is initiated under various 
pieces of legislation, we would like to emphasize the  horizontal value of certain elements which may significantly harm 
European businesses. Those include: 
- Confidential business information (CBI): certain data such as about a production process (in cluding detailed 
information of chemical composition, concentrations and microorganisms used in fermentation) must remain 
confidential as publishing such data will harm the business. 
- Compensation for data owners: broader access to data is good if the data owners are compensated by third 
parties who wish to use their data. Under REACH, this is adequately regulated, but not under other regimes. 
- Transparency-related provisions should not translate in avoidable administrative burdens, confidentiality 
management and reporting obligations should be proportionate and fit for purpose. 
FEFANA members regrets the EC reasoning that no impact assessment is needed  before introducing Community 
transparency requirements on chemical data involved in regulatory assessment, especially considering the experience 
recently acquired via the update of the General Food Law. Already after one year of enforcement of the transparency 
regulation, a significant impact on the lifecycle of applications for authorisation is noticeable. The procedures put in place 
to comply with the transparency rules, in particular those related to the confidentiality management, have been shown 
to be significantly m ore time /energy demanding than what originally estimated. Th e resulting increasing delay is

FEFANA members consequently call for: 
• Observance of the EU better regulation guidelines to prevent possible negative impacts  deriving from the 
proposed new regulatory approach  
• Caution in the proposed extension of relevant transparency measures from the EU’s General F ood Law 
framework to avoid increasing administrative burdens which may contribute to further delays for processing of 
EU businesses’ applications 
We therefore call on the Commission to take more time to evaluate the consequences of this significant horizontal 
legislative initiative on data together with stakeholders that are involved in the production of data, and in placing on the 
market and using of regulated products and substances. FEFANA is convinced that a thorough impact assessment (on top 
of targeted consultations with selected stakeholders)  coupled with close participatory processes with food-feed area 
stakeholders will be the key to making the better access to chemicals data approach a success which can effectively reduce 
the duplication of work.

FEEDBACK ON PUBLIC CONSULTATION ON THE REVISION OF 
THE WASTE FRAMEWORK DIRECTIVE 
 
 
 
FIR represents the recycling industry of inert Construction & Demolition Waste (C&DW) and 
of Incinerator Bottom Ash (IBA). Those member states where FIR has representation cover 
60% of the EU population. Besides that we have members in the UK, Switzerland and in 
Mexico. It is the aim of FIR to achieve high quality recycling in the EU. FIR provides a 
platform for those interested in recycling and we actively share our common knowledge. 
 
 
Introduction 
Whereas the European Commission is elaborating a revision of the Waste Framework 
Directive, FIR wishes to inform about actions needed to improve the management of inert 
C&DW which represents about 1/3 of all waste in the EU. Instead of responding to specific 
questions related to the public consultation, FIR chooses to provide more elaborate 
clarification of the state of play and to identify what is needed to improve the current 
situation.  
 
Whereas energy-from-waste is still developing in Europe, a new issue is created: Incinerated 
Bottom Ash (IBA). It is wrong in our view that IBA is not at all identified in European waste 
legislation or in existing programs and roadmaps as a major waste stream to be well 
managed. Besides C&DW, the issue of IBA is highlighted in this paper. 
 
The status of C&DW management in Europe 
The actual situation of C&DW management is not well known. Existing data such as 
available from Eurostat proof not to be reliable. A major issue is that many member states 
rely on backfilling to achieve the EU 70% target.  
 
Within the member states where FIR has representation, the overall recycling rate is 
approximately 55%. This includes high performing member states such as Germany, The 
Netherlands and Belgium (Flanders). In two member states where FIR has representation 
C&DW is hardly recycled at all. This leaves us to conclude that proper management of 
C&DW, and therefore also of inert C&DW, only happens in a limited number of member 
states. In a few other member states recycling is happening and initiatives aim at further 
improvement. 
 
High recycling rates are well achievable 
Inert C&DW (which excludes excavated soils) may be the most easy waste to recycle. 
Technology is fully matured and there is 40 years of experience in Europe. There is no secret 
to it and it only takes commitment and determination to succeed. The member states where 
recycling is happening show the way. There is a common approach in these member states 
which has been summarised by FIR in a 10-step approach [http://www.fir-
recycling.com/factsheets/factsheets].

FIR beliefs that almost full recycling of inert C&DW is possible throughout Europe. It may be 
that (initially) such recycling is not of the highest quality everywhere. In a development stage 
use of inert C&DW for backfilling operations may be an adequate interim solution, but under 
very strict conditions: 
- There must be a genuine need: inert C&DW must substitute non-waste material which 
would otherwise be applied. 
- Waste used for backfilling must comply with technical requirements for such use. 
- The environmental safety of inert C&DW must be assured. 
- Receivers of C&DW for backfilling must be authorised to do so. They must apply 
waste management procedures and registrate and notify all waste received. 
 
Full focus should however be on high quality recycling of inert C&DW. The embodied CO2 is 
too high to just do away with it and the circular economy is not served with low key solutions. 
Quality recycling is: production of recycled aggregates for use in new concrete and in brick 
production. This is not yet feasible in most member states, therefore use of recycled 
aggregates in road construction is currently the best circular option in most cases. This can 
be achieved both by mobile crushing or crushing of inert C&DW in fixed plants. Importantly, 
in all cases environmental requirements and requirements related to the regulation of 
construction products must be met. 
 
The examples of some Member States show that almost full and high quality recycling of 
inert C&DW is possible. It can be achieved everywhere if only the full implications of a 
circular economy are acknowledged and lived up to. It is highly recommended that Member 
States start drafting a plan to achieve full recycling of inert C&DW. Experience has shown 
that if such a plan exists, high quality recycling can be reached. We belief that in 2030 each 
Member State should be able to recycle 80% of inert C&DW and by 2050 more than 90%.  
 
Urgency to recycle inert C&DW 
Proper recycling of inert C&DW requires that hazardous waste and non-inert materials are 
separated from it. It therefore facilitates better hazardous waste management and recycling 
of other materials. When inert C&DW is not recycled, it is a carrier of all other materials which 
are not well visible in it and are part of the big volume of mainly inert materials. All hazardous 
wastes and non-inert materials go along with the big volume of inert waste to some final 
destination – not seldomly open nature in parts of the EU. In those Member States where 
C&DW recycling has matured, recycling of inert C&DW has always been the first step. 
 
It is needless to say that inert C&DW recycling contributes to all policies and targets which 
the EU has set related to sustainable growth, climate neutrality and saving of resources. In 
addition, inert C&DW recycling creates new jobs. Whereas inert C&DW is almost 1/3 of all 
waste in the EU, the contribution of recycling it are significant. 
 
Requirements for recycling 
The main requirement for better management of C&DW is that it is brought to recycling 
facilities. This requires: 
- Discouragement of landfilling, the most effective way being the banning of landfilling 
or else imposing high landfill taxes. 
- Proper tracking and tracing of C&DW. 
 
It is essential that at national level (electronic) systems are put in place that enable the 
tracking and tracing of all C&DW. There are good examples of tracking and tracing of 
C&DW, for instance: 
- In The Netherlands full registration of C&DW is carried out. Each batch of C&DW is 
sent to a recycling or sorting facility as landfilling is banned. Receiving companies of 
C&DW keep record of all waste coming in and all recycled materials going out. Per 
month all data are uploaded to a national registration system.

- In Flanders for each demolition work > 1,000 m3 a plan for tracking and tracing is 
required. The plan is part of the application for a demolition permit and must describe 
the collection and further destination of waste. A common system called Tracimat was 
developed by industry and government enabling the electronic tracking and tracing of 
C&DW. 
 
It is essential that demolition is performed selectively. This means that inert material must be 
kept fully apart at all stages from hazardous materials and non-inert materials. There are two 
important issues: 
- Whereas it will be extremely difficult to enforce selective demolition at sites, it is 
important that the environmental quality of inert C&DW and products derived of it is 
controlled in subsequent steps in the chain. This means that recycling facilities or 
other receivers of this material should have waste acceptance protocols and refuse 
materials that do not comply. Furthermore for each application of materials derived 
from inert C&DW there should be a framework ensuring the environmental sound 
use. 
- Selective demolition and further recycling steps will not be undertaken when there is 
no market for recycled materials. Use of recycled aggregates should be promoted by 
all authorities, for instance (and in the first place) by prescribing their use in road 
construction. 
 
Planning and reporting 
The situation in many member states could improve if there were a proper follow up on 
progress being made. This would enable assessment of the actual situation and precise 
expectations. At current any information available on C&DW relates to (nationally generated) 
data on waste volumes. There are however many other indicators that provide insight into 
what really happens, such as: 
- The number of permitted C&DW recycling facilities 
- The amount of recycled aggregates produced 
- The number of other licensed facilities that receive C&DW for recovery operations 
- The rules set out to ensure that in the case of recovery environmental protection is 
safeguarded and that non-waste materials have been substituted 
- The measures taken to ensure the establishment of sorting systems for C&DW 
according to art. 11.1 of the Waste Framework Directive 
- The existence of a framework to assess the environmental characteristics of recycled 
aggregates 
- Public procurement benefiting the use of recycled aggregates 
 
Setting recycling targets 
Currently the Commission is studying the setting of new targets for C&DW and its material 
specific fractions. FIR beliefs that in the first place the setting of a target for the inert fraction 
is of highest importance: 
- This fraction represents some 80% of all C&DW 
- Recycling of inert waste is the most feasible of all materials in C&DW. The technology 
is very mature and existing practices and all tools related to it can easily be 
implemented throughout the EU 
- Not setting targets for inert waste would facilitate the current practices met in member 
states where C&DW is ill managed. When inert waste would be taken out of the 
massive volumes of C&DW, only a small fraction would remain, the ill disposal of 
which would be made much more difficult. 
 
We understand that the setting of new targets will not be dealt with in the current revision. 
We like to highlight the issue though and flag it for future improvements.

Using the Waste Framework Directive to improve C&DW management 
FIR beliefs that the overall situation of C&DW management needs to improve and that the 
Waste Framework Directive is a key piece of legislation to do so. In order for that, we think 
that the following issues need to be considered when revising the Waste Framework 
Directive: 
- Banning of the landfilling of C&DW or high taxation for landfilling should be promoted 
or made compulsory. 
- Member States should be required to implement and enforce specific legislation 
enabling the recycling of C&DW. 
- Appropriate tracking and tracing of C&DW should be made mandatory, for instance 
by required electronic registry. 
- The WFD should require the establishment of a separate waste management plan for 
C&DW, which makes clear how a situation will be achieved whereby per 2030 at least 
80% w/w and by 2050 more than 90% w/w of inert C&DW will be recycled in a high 
quality manner and which addresses at least the following: 
o Measures taken to divert waste to recycling facilities 
o Measures taken to ensure the uptake of recycled materials from C&DW 
o Measures taken to manage asbestos 
o Measures taken to track and trace C&DW 
o Measures taken to ensure the sorting of C&DW in accordance to art. 11.1 of 
the WFD 
o The existing and planned infrastructure for C&DW recycling 
o Measures taken so that only suitable inert C&DW is used for backfilling 
o Measures taken to develop management of inert C&DW to higher quality 
applications 
- The WFD should enable following up of the progress of (inert) C&DW management in 
Member States, addressing issues as mentioned above. 
 
Incinerator Bottom Ash 
As energy from waste develops in the EU, the amounts of residues from incineration 
increase. Currently some 19 million tonnes of IBA are generated annually. IBA may therefore 
turn out to be a next waste problem, but on the other hand there is potential to benefit from 
this material: 
- IBA contains ferrous and non-ferrous metals which are mostly recovered. There is 
however potential to improve recovery rates substantially. 
- Non-inert material can be processed into aggregates which can replace gravel and 
sand.  
 
There is no driver yet for member states to develop a circular approach for IBA and thus 
improve energy from waste as a whole. FIR beliefs that such a driver is needed as otherwise 
IBA may turn out to become a problem waste. We belief that the WFD could be the starting 
point for better management of IBA.

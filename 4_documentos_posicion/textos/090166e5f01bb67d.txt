“Better access to chemicals data for safety assessments”Eurometaux response to the EU Commission call for evidence
Eurometauxwelcomestheopportunitytocontributetothecall forevidenceinrelationtothenewEuropeanCommissioninitiative“Chemical safety–better accesstochemicalsdataforsafety assessments”.
We support suchaninitiativewhichisinlinewithour industryeffort toestablishacommonand robust dataset to be used for riskassessment, promotingtheconsistent reuseof dataacross various regulatory frameworks in line with the “One Substances One Assessment(OSOA) principle.
Having read the call for evidence document, we have identified several points that mightrequireparticular attentiontoensurethesuccessof suchaninitiative. Pleasefindhereafterour comments:
- Data-generationmechanismfor EUandnational authorities- Itwill beimportantto define proper frameworks under whichsuchmechanismswill beestablishedandtoensuretransparency. Thisisprobablysomethingthatcould beconsideredaspartof the REACH revision.
- Obligations for industries and (external) testing laboratories to notifyauthorities of any studies they commission - We believe that such notificationmechanisms could help enable transparency and avoid duplication of efforts.Notification mechanisms should be “simple” and not lead to unnecessarydelaysintesting activities. It will be also important to define scope, rationale andresponsibilities of such obligations.
- A proper balance between notification obligations and aimof the test workshould be required from the start. The notification requirement should not hinderscience development to reduce uncertainties or novel issues. A distinction maytherefore be made between testing for pure science development and those forregulatory purposes.
- Data owners’ rights and fair and transparent cost sharing mechanisms (asapplied in the different regulatory frameworks) need to be considered whenimplementing a fully open data policy, also to ensure protection of confidentialbusiness information.
- Standards, formatsandcontrolled“vocabularies/dictionaries”- Amoreuniformapproach in data formatting may facilitate data processing andexchange, howeverthe differences in data requirements to support sector-specific regulatory reviews

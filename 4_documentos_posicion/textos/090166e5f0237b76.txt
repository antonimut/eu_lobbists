Remeo is a leading Finnish environmental management company, that is promoting a society that produces no waste. By developing cutting-edge technology at eight waste recovery and reuse facilities across Finland, Remeo is ambitiously at the forefront to raise recycling rates as well as reduce the amount of waste to end up in incineration.  
Remeo position paper on the revision of the Waste Framework Directive (WFD) With the upcoming reform of the Waste Framework Directive (WFD), Remeo warmly welcomes the plans of the European Commission to improve waste management and accelerate the transition to the circular economy.  Remeo considers it imperative that the revision focus on the following issues: § Ensuring equal access to waste streams to all economic actors allowing optimal technology and increased recycling  § Increasing economic incentives directed at waste management companies to follow the waste hierarchy, thus raising the recycling rates and reuse materials, rather than setting tax-based measures targeting incineration of waste  § While source separation of waste should be utilized to the greatest extent, waste management at recycling and recovery facilities is also required and should therefore be encouraged        Key concerns for Remeo in the WFD revision: 1. Reinforce access to waste streams and develop compensation mechanisms Within the waste management sector, equal and open access to waste streams is imperative in enabling efficient recycling processes.  For instance, household waste that is collected by municipal waste management is often neither recycled most efficiently nor exposed to the optimal recycling technology. This lack of incentives to recycle waste in publicly managed areas is fundamentally in contradiction with the ambitious targets of the European Union. To combat the problem, Remeo advocates for more analysis on the issue and the sharing of best practices across the Member States. To further incentivize recycling, Remeo proposes that the Commission consider a compensation mechanism analogous to ETS allowances. The mechanism should allow companies with a positive carbon handprint (i.e. more emissions avoided through recycling and recovery than used for the process) to sell “allowances” to companies with a negative carbon footprint. This would create incentives for more ambitious recycling and recovery while lowering the cost for households. This possible mechanism should be reviewed by the European Commission since larger incentives for recycling practices are required in the near future.

2. Raise recycling rates by promoting new technologies and waste treatment  Remeo, in pursuance of ambitious recycling rates, encourages the WFD revision to promote the new recycling and recovery technologies. For instance, mechanical recycling techniques that crush waste do not allow the efficient reuse of materials. New, innovative recycling and recovery technologies already available on the market can play an essential role in improving the recovery of recycled raw materials, and increased EU funding for innovation and deployment of novel technologies are recommended. Between mechanical and chemical recycling, the WFD revision should also note the recent technological developments that have demonstrated mechanical recycling to be both more efficient and environmentally safe. To raise recycling rates, the WFD revision should not only rely on source separation of waste. While source separation remains the preferred option, waste treatment at recycling and recovery facilities is a necessary counterpart to the separate collection. Particularly, the role of data is essential in the field. These decisions are vital for improving recycling rates since a system consisting of solely source separation is neither cost-effective nor sufficient for increasing recycling rates.  3. Encourage sustainable waste incineration and clarify EPR To save emissions and establish the waste hierarchy in practice, only waste that cannot be recycled should be incinerated. Most notably, the incineration of untreated waste should be avoided for environmental reasons.  Moreover, regarding extended producer responsibility (EPR), the WFD revision needs to clarify the ownership of waste. For instance, the ownership of clean secondary raw materials that are recovered as materials remains ambiguous, and since the market exists for such products, a more precise definition can increase recycling rates. Remeo finds it essential that the waste, as well as the data acquired from it, are owned by the company that processes it. In pursuance of coherent calculations, the WFD revision should focus on recycling rates rather than the sorting rate, since sorting waste does not provide any guarantee of recycling of the material. Additionally, the rates should be calculated as a facility-level average over a certain period of time instead of on a per-batch basis. Moreover, to promote circular economy, Remeo welcomes the Commission’s consideration of mandatory requirements for recycled content, especially in the field of plastics.

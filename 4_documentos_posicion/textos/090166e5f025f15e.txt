 MPE contribution to the public consultation on the revision of the EU waste framework   Metal Packaging Europe (MPE), the association bringing together European producers of rigid metal packaging, welcomes the opportunity to contribute to the ongoing work of the Commission in preparation of the targeted revision of the Waste Framework Directive (WFD) scheduled for 2023.  MPE members manufacture cans made both of aluminium and steel, packaging materials which are top performers when it comes to recycling, with rates of 76%, and 85,5%1 respectively. Over the years, the metal packaging industry has worked closely with European, national and local authorities, Extended Producer Responsibility Schemes, waste management operators and, last but not least, customers, brands and civil society at large, to invest in collection and sorting systems that underpin the recycling infrastructure.  In addition to a number of noteworthy points to increase waste prevention outlined in the public consultation, and with a view to reducing pressure on our environment and our planet, MPE would like to raise the following points:    • The need for a definition of high-quality recycling.  • The role of permanent materials in helping to reduce the need for primary raw materials. • The importance of effective waste management systems, including separate waste collection and clear rules in order to achieve the ambitions of the CEAP and increase resource efficiency. • The importance of analysing, holistically, if and under what conditions, reusable packaging is most likely to incur the least environmental impact and/or ensure resource efficiency/waste prevention in a true circular economy    1 Recycling rate respective aluminium beverage cans (2019) and all steel for packaging (2020) segments.

1. The need for a definition of high-quality recycling in the WFD   As the need to reduce the pressure on natural resources increases, so does the need for high-quality recycling. High-quality recycling ensures that secondary raw materials which come out of the recycling process are suitable for use in product applications manufactured using the same virgin raw material, thus enabling a more circular economy.   Both the Waste Framework Directive and the Packaging & Packaging Waste Directive refer to “high-quality recycling” but do not, however, define this concept.  Metal Packaging Europe (MPE) believes that the definition of high-quality recycling must be based on the following aspects:  • The material to be recycled must be able to withstand multiple recycling loops without any change to its inherent properties and with minimum material losses during the recycling process, thereby guaranteeing high yields.  • The existence of an efficient, well-designed infrastructure enabling proper separate collection, sorting and recycling to collect a significant share of the tonnage put on the market and to deliver recyclates meeting the quality equal to the original virgin material. This will ensure that packaging is not only recyclable but also effectively recycled at scale and can find sufficient end markets to substitute the use of primary raw material. • MPE also considers that the future definition of “high-quality recycling” should refer to the secondary material’s ability to replace the primary material in products of similar value.   2. The role of permanent materials in helping to reduce the need for primary raw materials  As permanent materials, aluminium and steel can be recycled over and over again without losing their key intrinsic properties, thus maintaining circular material loops. A ‘permanent material’ is a material whose inherent properties do not change, regardless of the number of times it goes through a recycling process. It means that once it is produced for the first time and properly collected, sorted and processed at the end of its life, it becomes the raw material for new and endless production loops. The importance of effective waste management systems, including separate waste collection and clear rules in order to achieve the ambitions of the CEAP and increase resource efficiency.  Optimized separate collection and sorting of waste are a prerequisite to guaranteeing high-quality input into the recycling operations. Well-sorted aluminium and steel scrap already constitutes a major resource in the production of new products. Key factors in the drive to increase the total amount of packaging being collected, sorted and recycled includes incentivising the use of modern and efficient sorting equipment and separation technologies, implementing well-managed, effective and cost-efficient Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) schemes and well-functioning Deposit Return Systems (DRS) for beverage packaging, and ensuring better eco-design of packaging. MPE also believes that door-to-door collection schemes guarantee high erformance of separate waste collection especially in terms of quantity of the collected waste. However, collection schemes should aim to achieve the best results not just in terms of quantity but also in the quality of separately collected materials without having unintended environmental and economic consequences. Harmonised separate collection should not impede high-quality recycling or other recovery of waste, in line with the waste hierarchy. Therefore, some degree of flexibility needs to be retained for scenarios where adherence to the harmonised schemes is not delivering the desired outcomes. This could be done via derogations based on relevant evidence of underperformance. MPE considers that some parameters should be harmonised across the Member States. For example, harmonised colour of the collection bags and/or waste bins for recyclables would help with avoiding consumer confusion. It is particularly important in view of the continuously increasing movement of persons across EU borders and the multiplicity of existing waste collection systems in the EU. If the colour of the bags and their contents are harmonised across the EU it would lead to lower misthrow rates as well as increasing the overall collection rate and quality

The importance of analysing, holistically, if and under what conditions, reusable packaging is most likely to incur the least environmental impact and/or ensure resource efficiency in a true circular economy  Based on existing literature, there is insufficient data to allow us to conclude that the environmental benefit of reusable packaging is greater than that of highly recyclable one-way packaging across all product categories and all markets in the EU. In order to make sure that reuse is put in place where environmental benefits can realistically be achieved, holistic and dynamic impact assessments at both product category and market levels are necessary.  For packaging, keeping in mind the financial and administrative burden associated with such Impact Assessments, policymakers may wish to consider to achieve the Commission’s objective of having all packaging fully recyclable or reusable by 2030 through the use of targeted policy tools and initiatives such as eco-modulated fees in Extended Producer Responsibility Schemes, consumer awareness-raising campaigns, harmonized separate waste collection, expansion and modernisation of sorting and recycling capacity and technologies, Deposit Refund Schemes and separate beverage packaging collection targets, etc.

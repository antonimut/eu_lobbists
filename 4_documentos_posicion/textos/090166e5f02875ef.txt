 
 
The European Paper Packaging Alliance (EPPA) is a not -for-profit association representing food and 
food service packaging companies from across Europe. EPPA’s priority is to provide paper-based low 
carbon and health-safe products with a low environmental impact and improved recycling solutions to 
the European population. 
 
EPPA supports science -based EU policies that foster s ustainable and efficient use of renewable 
resources and recogni ses the major role that packaging can play in that common objective :  it is 
therefore pleased to contribute to the public consultation on the Food Waste Reduction Targets as a 
key contributor to food waste reduction by:  
 
− Providing outstanding performances on hygiene and food safety (point 1 below)  
− Allowing food-portion control  through fit -for-portion packaging and expanding food  lifespan 
(point 2)  
  
EPPA would therefore like to see the positive contribution that paper-based food packaging brings to 
food waste reduction included in the road map document. 
 
We are indeed disappointed that the only reference to packaging in the roadmap document is to 
identify the increased use of packaging to reduce food waste as a potential negative impact of a food 
waste reduction strategy, ignoring all the fundamental benefits provided by the paper -based 
packaging industry for European citizens.  
 
In this respect, it should be noted that: 
 
1. Paper-based food packaging protects the consumer by providing the best hygiene and food 
safety performance and preventing cross-contamination, compared to reuse  
  
− Fiber-based single-use food packaging was invented with the aim of achieving the best possible 
hygiene and food safety standards: it remains as of now the necessary and undisputed 
convenient material for hospitals, social and frail care systems and prisons.  
 
− According to official EU documents 6 The central goal of EU food safety policy is to protect 
both human and animal health .  We cannot compromise on these standards  as foodborne 
diseases impede socioeconomic development by straining health care systems and harming 
national economies, tourism and trade.  
 
− The report 7: Food hygiene challenges in replacing single -use food service packaging with 
reusable food service items - Professor McDowell 2020 report (Emeritus Professor of Food 
Studies at the Ulster University) found that the transfer of foodborne disease remains a cl ear 
and present hazard to consumers and that " there are greater risks of cross contamination 
within reuse systems, due to multi -location cleaning, sanitation, storage and transport ". 
Professor McDowell notes that banning or reducing the use of food service disposables in the 
absence of significant radical changes in good hygiene practice will lead to greater persistence 
and circulation of foodborne pathogens within the human food chain, and increased risks of 
human foodborne illness.  
 
− Paper-based packaging also provides the necessary communication to inform consumers of 
possible ingredients which may cause serious health impact:

of ingredients, for example by using a different font, letter size or background colour. In the

2. Paper-based food packaging is a major contributor to food waste prevention through portion 
control and extending shelf life  
  
Food packaging is essential and universal: essential because without packaging the safety and 
quality of food would be compromised, and universal because almost all food is packaged in some 
way. 
 
EPPA's members’ paper-based products provide fit-for-portion packaging to the HORECA sector, 
allowing to minimize food waste through portion-control by: 
 
− Limiting oversized servings that generate food waste. 
− Offering different sized packaging, allowing choice to consumers according to their specific 
needs. 
− Providing through information:  
o a measurable amount of food and drink,  
o together with a qualitative input of food constituents,  
o that both help the consumer to better manage their personal nutrition performance. 
− Providing food operators with a tool that fits to their type of serving and nutritional approach, 
limiting food waste along the full value-chain. 
 
Without EPPA’s members’ paper -based packaging solutions, none of the following would be 
feasible:  
 
− Food quantity would remain random, leading to food wastage.  
− Food nutritional impact would be difficult to assess by the consumer.  
− Food operators would not be able to operate efficiently and unable to limit food wastage.  
  
Compared to reusable packaging, paper-based food packaging provides flexibility to adapt quickly 
to any changes in seasons, regulation, nutritionist advice, customer needs and industry innovation 
related to food waste reduction.  This flexibility is key to implement changes to packaging in a swift 
and efficient manner, while limiting the environmental impact.  
  
Packaging also plays a decisive role in extending food lifespan and reducing food waste:  
  
− Simply put, shelf life is the time during which all the primary characteristics of food remain 
acceptable for consumption. Thus, shelf life refers to the time span for which a food can remain 
on the retailer's and then the consumer's shelf before it becomes unacceptablei.  
− Commission Regulation (EC) no. 2073/2005 states: " shelf life means either the period 
corresponding to the period preceding the 'use by' or th e minimum durability date, as defined 
respectively in Articles 9 and 10 of Directive 2000/13/EC."  
− Packaging's Role in Minimizing Food Loss and Waste Across the Supply Chain is well 
documented as in the often -cited publication ii in the International Journal Packaging 
Technology and Science by Verghese, Lewis, Lockrey and Williams.  
  
 
 
In the case of EPPA’s paper-based products, many applications contribute to expanding 
packaged food shelf life such as:   
− Pre-prepared sandwiches wraps  
− Pre-prepared soup containers  
− Pre-prepared cereal containers  
− Food platters for office service  
− Any type of food held in a hot or cold holding cabinet ready for consumer selection:  
o Cooked chicken from retailers  
o Meat or fish portions   
o Snacks...  
  
EPPA’s members’ packaging is critical to maintain food quality and safety to extend the shelf-life 
period of products often from hours to many days.  
  
More broadly, it should be reminded that:  
 
− The lack of packaging or the use inadequate packaging in the distribution sector of developing 
countries causes 30% to 50% of all food to decay before it reaches the consumer.  
− In complete contrast, in "packaging-oriented" countries the amount of food that deteriorates 
is only 3%.  
− Packaging generally represents only 3 –3.5% of the carbon footprint of a food or beverage 
product, while 80% is caused by growing and preparing the food and 7.5% by transpo rt – 
therefore the environmental benefit of reduced food waste is significantly higher than the 
environmental burden of the packaging.  
− Regarding the impact on environment for EPPA member’s products, please note that: 
o Paper-based food packaging is renewable, recyclable and effectively recycled, as explained 
in the recent Waste Management Call for evidence. 
o The environmental burden is significantly less than for reusable as established through 
life cycle thinking, because of the energy, water and detergent needed in the use phase. 
o Paper-based food packaging contributes to decoupling economic growth from resource 
use by reducing waste generation through multiple recycling. 
 
Recommendations 
 
EPPA would request that:  
 
 
• Single use paper-based packaging is considered as a  necessary tool to prevent food waste 
through effective fit-for-portion food packaging and a means for extending shelf life of food. 
 
• EU policymakers fully recognize that renewable and recyclable paper-based packaging is an 
asset to minimizing food waste. 
 
• EU policymakers facilitate dialogue across the entire value chain to ensure that food waste is 
minimised.

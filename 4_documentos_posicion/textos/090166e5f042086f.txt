 
Additional Comments by IK Industrievereinigung 
Kunststoffverpackungen 
Public Consultation on the revision of the Waste Framework Directive 
(WFD) 
 
Bad Homburg, 16.08.2022 
We thank you for the opportunity to participate in the public consultation on the revision of 
the Waste Framework Directive (WFD). We would like to complement our responses by the 
following suggestions that might help to promote the targets of the Green Deal and the 
Circular Economy Action Plan. 
Our key suggestions are the following: 
1. Waste hierarchy: Put climate protection before waste prevention 
2. Make separate waste collection simple and accessible everywhere  
3. Implement state-of-the art waste management practice across Europe 
4. EU wide harmonization, implementation and enforcement are top priority 
 
1. Waste hierarchy: Put climate protection before waste prevention 
The waste hierarchy provides a reasonable heuristic for measures that lead to less waste, 
better waste management and thus more sustainable production and consumption 
patterns.  
However, it is important to notice that waste prevention is not an environmental goal in 
itself but serves to protect resources, the environment and, above all, the climate. 
Although waste prevention and preparing for reuse are on the top of the waste hierarchy, 
recycling of waste contributes to the same environmental goals and can potentially even 
outperform them. This can e.g. be the case if extra transportation is needed for repair or 
preparation for reuse. 
Although we welcome certain waste prevention targets, e.g. on food waste, the 
shortcoming of waste prevention and reuse/repair targets in general is that they focus only 
on one single indicator of a product’s environmental performance without taking into 
account the environmental burden of other life cycle stages like the manufacturing 
processes or transportation. In the case of packaging, too strong focus on waste reduction 
may even conflict with the goal of preventing food and other product waste. Only a

comprehensive life cycle assessment can evaluate a product’s carbon footprint and other 
environmental impacts and serve as a fair basis for an ecological comparison of alternative 
options that serve the same customer benefit. 
Waste prevention and reuse/repair targets also make no difference whether the waste is a 
waste that can be recycled into a secondary raw material that are of economic value in a 
Circular Economy or if it is merely a waste to be disposed of with no further value. 
Differentiation between types/value of waste is necessary. 
Therefore, the waste hierarchy should be used as a guidance but not applied in a strict 
manner, especially in order to prevent conflicts with climate change mitigation targets. 
Political measures that target the reduction of greenhouse gases (such as CO2 pricing) will 
ultimately foster waste prevention, reuse and repair where this contributes to emission 
savings but will equally also foster recycling of waste where this is more efficient.  
If waste prevention targets are to be set, they should focus on waste for disposal only. 
Other waste prevention and reuse/repair targets should not be set unless clear evidence is 
provided that they do not conflict with overarching environmental goals. 
2. Make separate waste collection simple and accessible everywhere  
Separate waste collection for recycling remains a bottleneck that needs to be addressed by 
the revision. “Pay as you throw” taxes are meant to financially incentivize consumers to 
better separate their waste by imposing costs related to the amount of residual waste that 
they produce. However, practice in Germany shows that such measures can lead to higher 
contamination of recycling bins with residual waste, thus increasing the costs and lowering 
the quality in recycling.  
Separate collection therefore must be increased by different measures. First and foremost, 
waste separation must be simple for consumers and possible everywhere – at home, at 
school, at the workplace, in public buildings as well as on the road, in railway stations and in 
public spaces. A uniform marking of bins and products might also help. 
3. Implement state-of-the art waste management practice across Europe 
Waste management infrastructure must be kept up-to-date throughout Europe. We 
suggest a process similar to the EU’s “Sevilla process” to evaluate and monitor the best 
available techniques in waste collection management and recycling infrastructure that 
should become mandatory and enforced in all MS after a certain time period. The Sevilla 
process is already applied for the implementation of the Industrial Emissions Directive 
(IED). Such a process would foster high environmental standards and the transition to a 
Circular Economy in Europe.  
4. EU wide harmonization, implementation and enforcement are top priority 
The review must address barriers and bottlenecks resulting from insufficient harmonisation 
and weak enforcement of existing EU provisions at national level. Key provisions must be 
clearly defined in the legislative text for maximum harmonisation across the Member States 
and enforcement mechanisms must be strengthened to oversee their effective 
implementation. 
 
About IK - IK Industrievereinigung Kunststoffverpackungen e. V. is the trade association 
representing the interests of manufacturers of plastic packaging and films in Germany. The 
industry, which is characterised by medium-sized companies, has more than 90,000 
employees and generates an annual turnover of 15 billion euros. With an annual 
production of 4.5 million tons, Germany accounts for approximately 22% of the European 
production of plastics packaging. In support of the EU Plastics Strategy (2018) and Circular 
Economy Action Plan (2020), our association has become member of the Circular Plastics 
Alliance (CPA) and has set measurable branch targets to increase recyclability and reuse of 
packaging as well as the use of recycled content in Germany.

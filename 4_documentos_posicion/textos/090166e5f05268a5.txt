IMA-Europe position on the Nature Restoration Law (NRL) Revision 
                                                FINAL (22 August 2022)  
 
Industrial Minerals Association Europe (IMA -Europe) representing the interest of Industrial minerals 
producers in Europe, welcomes, the proposal on Nature Restoration initiative presented by the 
Commission (22 June 2022). While we understand the rationale to move forward with this initiative, as 
minerals sector we have some reservation on its legal shape and the content proposed. Please find below 
some of these concerns to be considered in the final shape of the proposal:  
 
Balanced approach in the way forward:  
1. The surrounding environment provides both the natural habitat and it is a source of goods , as 
provides for basic human needs: food, energy, infrastruc ture, housing, and other manufactured 
goods. While we are all aware of the high pressure on the environment, the ultimate goal would be 
to achieve balanced decisions for sustainable development of ecosystems that allow the human 
development and a good quality of life, while minimising the negative impact on various ecosystems. 
 
2. As an example, the extractive activities are a temporary use of land. The minerals sector supports 
Biodiversity restoration efforts for a long time:  
➢ As sector, we have experience and developed practices that demonstrate this across EU.  
➢ The evidence shows that thanks to mining activities, the biodiversity is improved/boosted 
during and at the end of extractive operations.  
➢ The concept of ‘temporary nature’ has been mapped in various EU projects. The extensive 
evidence has demonstrated that nature is dynamic and not static. 
 
Legal form of the proposal: 
3. A regulation is a legally b inding instrument, and it is implemented as such without any flexibility 
possible. Proposal, explains that undefined legal terms, will be clarified later in delegated acts.   
➢ This lack of precision will create a lot of difficulty for operator’s and authorities that will need 
to comply and enforce its provisions. As an example, we would like to list the large number of 
court cases under the Habitat Directive (article 6 et al).  
o Rulings of the European Court of Justice: Article 6 of Habitat Directive  
o Rulings: Nature & Biodiversity Cases rulings by the European Court of Justice. 
➢ The proposal in addition, fails to address the impact of it will have on the exiting restoration 
permits by extraction companies.  
 
4. Experience has shown that Biodiversity is best managed locally, and flexibility is crucial for real and 
effective biodiversity restoration practices.  
➢ Biodiversity restoration goals need the flexibility to adapt to local situations, that is possible in 
the frame of a Directive and not in a Regulation. 
➢ A directive will give flexibility to Member States to make the right choices applied to the local 
conditions.  
➢ Recognize the role and responsibility of local stakeholders is crucial for success stories to thrive.

Setting realistic goals for wider stakeholder engagement: 
5. Set ambitious but realistic goals and move in a stepwise approach 
➢ The proposal  describes a goal of >20%  for EU ecosystems , whi le the Biodiversity Strategy 
defines a target of >30% EU ecosystems 
➢ In article 4, there are legally binding targets proposed for all Member States independently how 
heavily populated they are, or what habitats are possible to be developed at national/regional 
level 
➢ Setting unrealistic and unreachable policy goals creates frustration (and results in setting even 
more stringent goals in the revision stage)  
➢ This results in a demotivation to involve and stimulate the concerned stakeholders 
 
6. Recognizing the dynamic nature of environment, the Nature Restoration Directive should ensure the 
protection and enhancement of biodiversity not against humans but thanks to human intervention 
and good management practices. 
➢ Local stakeholders from different concerned sectors should be involved and not excluded.  
 
Avoid policy making in silo, but assess the big picture for EU needs: 
7. Avoid the conflict of EU objectives between EU goals.  
➢ Not possible to have autonomous and industrialized EU without access locally sourced 
minerals.  
➢ Conflicts between (protected) nature restoration areas and mineral extraction shall be avoided, 
to allow for EU self-sufficiency in sustainably sourced mineral resources.  
➢ Renewable energy generation from EU will also create less dependency from non-EU energy 
sourcing. A constant river water flow is needed for constant generation of hydro power and as 
condition for reliable water abstraction for many manufacturing sectors. Therefore, before any 
decision-making, proper hydrological assessment, and modelling on (sub)catchment scale is 
needed to predict effects on existing industrial activities. 
 
8. Before the initiative moves forward, the Commission should carry out an economic impact assessment 
of:  
➢ Cumulative impact of EU legislations on EU business  and assess EU dependencies (Renewable 
energy, other economic activities). 
➢ Economic impact of businesses not operating in EU, particularly with respect to supply of 
multiple industries with minerals (see 7. above).

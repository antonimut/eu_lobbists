Cepi feedback on the proposal for a Nature Restoration law 
Cepi supports the overall ambition to enhance the health and resilience of ecosystems. The European 
pulp and paper industries source 86% of the wood they use in the EU and share the strategic objective to 
keep healthy and growing forests. This includes maintaining and enhancing biodiversity, which is a key 
component of ecosystem stability and productivity. At the same time, we have some remarks on specific 
provisions for forest ecosystems. As a general principle, we believe that sustainable forest management 
remains the solution to enhance environmental functions of forests, including biodiversity, alongside social 
and economic ones. This includes balancing biodiversity protection and restoration with other essential 
functions of the forests, such as provision of sustainable and renewable raw materials and energy.  
Specific observations: 
1) We regret that the impact assessm ent, although very thorough, does not take into account the full 
forest-based industrial ecosystem. The impact assessment accompanying the Regulation devotes a 
chapter to forest ecosystems. The cost of restoration is calculated as capital cost per hectare for the 
measures plus annual cost for maintenance (including cost of avoiding or maintaining timber 
harvesting). It is expected that such cost will be incurred by private landowners and managers, who 
should receive compensation. It is recognised that “ opportunity costs could stem from decreased 
biomass harvests. These would involve economic costs for forest owners and the forest-based sector, 
in terms of market value and employment.”  However, no estimation on future raw materials availability, 
nor the impact on employment in the forest sector, is provided. It is stated that “ foresters will be able 
to gain in the medium to longer term, since restored forests can provide new sources of income such 
as eco-tourism, or based on public and private payment schemes for ecosystem services”. This implies 
a fundamental shift in the business model concerning forest management that would de facto reduce 
the potential development of the forest-based bioeconomy, which would deserve more consideration 
and assessment. Pr oper impact assessment is also key to evaluate trade -offs between different 
management options, for example considering that very stringent policies may have the unintended 
effect of shifting part of the production of forest products outside of the EU, with consequent impact on 
European jobs1. 
 
2) Par. 6 of Art. 4 states that “ Member States shall ensure that the areas that are subject to restoration 
measures show a continuous improvement until good condition is reached” and that areas where good 
condition is reached do not deteriorate. Art. 11 further states that National Restoration Plans should 
contain detailed indication of the measures that will ensure the non -deterioration of the areas. Cepi 
wonders how to prove such trend considering that forest ecosyste ms, and in particular boreal 
ecosystems, have very long cycles and that restoration measures can bring result only after decades. 
In case this would practically mean protection of the areas as the easiest way to prove compliance, we 
would stress again the need to have an impact assessment on the effects of such policy on raw material 
provision.

equal to “bad condition”. We believe that this could lead in an “ overshoot” and raise the question

Cepi is the European association representing the paper industry.  We offer a wid e range of renewable and recyclable wood -based fibre 
solutions to EU citizens: from packaging to textile, hygiene and tissue products, printing and graphic papers as well as speciality papers, but also 
bio-chemicals for food and pharmaceuticals, bio-composites and bioenergy. We are a responsible industry: 85% of our raw materials are sourced 
from within the European Union and 78% of the wood comes from certified forests, 92% of the water we use is returned in good condition to the 
environment. We are the world champion in recycling at the rate of 71.4%. At the forefront of the decarbonisation and industrial transformation of 
our economy, we embrace digitalisation and bring 21 billion value addition to the European economy and €4.5 billion investments annua lly. 
Through its 18 national associations, Cepi gathers 495 companies operating 895 mills across Europe and directly employing more than 179,000 
people.

Svemin (the Swedish Association of Mines, Mineral and Metal Producers) represents around 60 companies 
with more than 13 000 employees in mining, exploring and technology. 
1. BIODIVERSITY AND MINING 
The objective of the proposed Regulation of the European Parl iament and of the Council on nature 
restoration is to put in place restoration of ecosystems to good condition and enhancing biodiversity. 
Svemin has been given the opportunity to give input on the proposal. 
Svemin recognizes the importance of restoration of important habitats and measures to enhance 
biodiversity. It is positive that restoration measures are coordinated and assessed in a comprehensive 
process to identify areas and measures. The regulation also contains opportunities to weigh dif ferent 
interest against each other. 
The mining industry is essential in the green transition, as there is need for an increased domestic supply 
of critical raw materials and other metals and minerals within the European Union. New mines will always 
have competing interests, and is always affect the environment. The initial phase of mineral exploration 
is to search for deposits . As good mineral deposits are rare and hard to find it is important that it is 
possible to perform exploration also in protected areas or areas under restoration. In contrast to almost 
any other operations, the location of a good mineral deposit also dictates the location of the operation ; 
the mining itself and supporting activities. If th e deposit is within a protected area or a restoration area 
without any exemption or compensation possibility, the minerals a strict protection will make it impossible 
to extract the materials. It is therefore of great importance that impact from human activity, that promotes 
sustainable development, is accepted within restoration areas and that restoration of  one area can be 
exchanged for restoration of another area. 
The Swedish mining operations already have extensive programs for restoration of nature. Svemin 
launched a roadmap as a first step towards net gain of biodiversity from 2030.

2. AMENDMENTS TO THE PROPOSAL 
2.1 A Directive is more suitable than a Regulation 
2.2 Article 1  
According to Article 1, paragraph 2, the objective of the regulation is to establish a framework 
within which Member States shall put in place effective and area-based restoration measures 
which together shall cover, by 2030, at least 20 percent of the land and sea areas in the EU, and, 
by 2050, all ecosystems in need of restoration. However, in article 4 and 5 such measures should 
be in place on at least 30 percent of the area of each group of habitat types listed in Annex I and II 
that is not in good condition. By 2040 the number shall be 60 percent and by 2050 90 percent for 
Annex I and 100 percent for Annex II. The connection between Article 1 and Articles 4 and 5 needs 
to be clarified. As Svemin understands, 20 percent of all land and sea areas in the E U is a bigger 
area than 30 percent of habitats in Annex I which are not in good condition.  
Furthermore, Svemin finds that the scope of all ecosystems in need of restoration is indistinct. It is 
unclear how large the total area affected by the regulation will be and what effect the regulation will 
have on sustainable development and operation necessary for climate change mitigation.  
 
2.3 Definition of good condition 
Svemin finds it positive that the definition of good condition is an assessment of the conditions and 
characteristics of the specific areas to ensure that measures and protection is custo m made for 
each area and are based on the best available knowledge and the latest scientific evidence. It is 
important that the assessment of the condition and needed measures is based on the specific 
areas and not on general perceptions and comparisons with a large number of theoretically 
decided indicators. Any such theoretic indicators should be non-binding and serve only as a 
guidance in the assessment of good condition. A practical approach is required based on resilience 
and functional ecosystems. 
It should also be clarified that good condition is not the same as unaffected and does not exclude 
impacts from human activity. The objective is to implement measures that ensures viable and 
resilient habitats which is not the same as unaffected habitats. Ecosystems with good condition can 
co-exist with human activity, especially if this activity is essential for a sustainable development and 
the green transition. It is also important that neither the Union nor Member States develop binding 
indicators and assessment criteria that equals good condition with an unaffected habitat.  
Article 19 should be amended to clarify that any indicators that are developed under the Regulation 
are non-binding for the assessment of good condition. 
 
2.4 Article 4 
Svemin finds it positive that the Regulation includes possibilities to make exemptions from the 
restoration objective due to other sustainable projects. However, Svemin believes that there is a 
need to make amendments and clarifications on the application of the Regulation.  
2.4.1 Non-fulfilment 
The reason for non-fulfilment of restoration obligations under the proposed Regulation should have 
a different threshold than authorization for a plan or project in accordance with Article 6(4) of the 
Directive 92/43/EEC. New activities within Natura 2000 sites with a negative impact will have to

meet the criteria in Article 6(4) of the Directive 92/43/EEC to be authorised regardless of the 
proposed Regulation.  
Natura 2000 sites have a strict threshold for when an operation can have a nega tive impact on 
natural values within the site. It is disproportionate to have the same strict threshold for other areas 
or habitats that are not designated as a Natura 2000 site. Such regulation would have a negative 
impact on sustainable projects that contribute to climate change mitigation and other societal goals. 
Restoration areas will cover a substantial part of the Member States’ territory. The principles of 
subsidiarity and proportionality mean that Member States must be able to adapt land use over t ime 
to changing needs in society and to change and replace the designated areas and the restoration 
plans. 
Member States should have at least the same room for derogations as under the Water Framework 
Directive Article 4 paragraph 7. However, derogations under the Regulation should generally be 
combined with compensation measures. 
Svemin therefore suggests an amendment to Article 4, paragraphs 8 and 9, and Article 5, 
paragraphs 8 and 9, as follows. 
 
The non-fulfilment of the obligations set out in paragraphs 6 and 7 is justified if it is caused by 
new sustainable human development activities and the following conditions are met:  
(a) all practicable steps are taken to mitigate the adverse impact the condition of the 
ecosystem, 
(b) the reasons for the sustainable human development activities are specifically set out 
and explained in an amended national restoration plan, 
(c) the reasons for those activities are of overriding public interest and/or the benefits to 
the environment and to society of achieving the objectives set out in paragraph 1  and 2 
are outweighed by the benefits of the activities to human health, to the maintenance of 
human safety or to sustainable development, 
(d) the beneficial objectives served by those activities cannot for reasons of technical 
feasibility or disproportionate cost be achieved by other means, which are a significantly 
better environmental option and 
(e) the habitat type affected by the activities shall, where possible and appropriate, be 
compensated through restoration of the same habitat type in the same biogeographical 
region.  
 
Such regulation will enable Member States to prioritized use of land and water in the best way  to 
ensure a sustainable development and necessary climate mitigation measures. The possibility for 
Member States to weigh different sustainable interest against each other on areas that can exceed 
one third of the Member States land and sea areas must be in line with the requirement for the 
principle of subsidiarity, especially when it is not a designated Natura 2000 site.  
2.4.2 Flexibility and compensatory measures 
For the same reason, the regulation must ensure such flexibility that enables initiated restorat ion 
measures to be amended or in some cases discontinued when it is discovered that the measures 
will not have the foreseen impact on natural values in the area or cannot be implemented for other 
reasons. There should also be a possibility to compensate for negative impact in an area 
designated for restoration due to sustainable development and climate change mitigation projects

2.5 Climate change mitigation 
According to Article 11, paragraph 6, the development of national restoration plans should be 
coordinated with the designation of the renewables go-to areas which is positive. However, the 
development of restoration plans should not only consider the production of renewable energy, but 
all operations that have a positive effect on climate change mitigation. i.e.  the need and benefit of 
restoration measures should be weighed against all climate change mitigation operations wh en 
selecting areas where measures are most effective and beneficiary. This needs to be clarified in 
the text of the Regulation. 
 
2.6 Preparation of the national restoration plan 
To assess the impact, effect and benefit of restoration measures in a certain area, input from 
operations in the area can be essential to choose the right measures and area for implementation. 
The preparation of the national restoration plans shall according to Article 11, paragraph 11, be 
open, inclusive and effective and give the public early and effective opportunities to participate in its 
elaboration. With experiences from the consultation process in the Water Framework Directive it is 
not enough to ensure that input is considered by the responsible authorities. The paragraph should  
therefore be amended to ensure the inclusion of these operations in the process. Svemin therefore 
proposes a new paragraph 12. 
 
(new) Article 11, paragraph 12 
Member States shall ensure that activities in proposed restoration areas or activities affected 
by proposed restoration measures are part of the preparation of the restoration plan to 
contribute with knowledge with regards to the area, its natural values and impact on the 
operation due to proposed measures. 
 
2.7 Impact on exemption according to the Water Framework Directive 
It is crucial that the application of heavily modified waterbodies (HWB) according to Article 4.3 and 
the exemptions in Article 4.5 and 4.7 in the Water Framework Directive are not affected by the 
regulation and that no other measures than those set in the application of HWB and the 
exemptions are demanded for these areas as stated in Article 7, paragraph 1. The future 
implementation of these articles should not be restricted by the regulation on nature restoration. 
The restoration objective should not override the exemptions or HWB according to the Water 
Framework Directive if the purpose of the designation has a positive effect on climate change 
mitigation. 
There is also a need to clarify that the objective of 25 000 km of free-flowing rivers is fulfilled if the 
main furrow in the river is free-flowing, even if there are obstacles in streams in the catchment area 
running to the main furrow. With regards to Sweden, it should be noticed and stated that there are 
526 000 km of rivers and that in February 2022 a systematic review of all hydro powerplants was 
initiated that will ensure that all conditions for hydro power operations are in line with EU legislation, 
e.g. the Water Framework Directive and the Habitats Directive.

 
 
The Polish Union of the Cosmetics Industry comments on the Waste Management 
Directive revision public consultations 
24 August 2022 
 
The Polish Union of the Cosmetics Industry welcomes the Commission initiative of the Waste Framework 
Directive revision  and the public consultations on that area . The WFD is a vital p iece of environmental 
legislation, serving as base for financing and functioning of the waste management system. The 
effectiveness of this system depends on the details of the WFD provisions and its current revision.  
In general it should be stated that the survey does not allow for participation of most of the stakeholder, 
such as recyclers, sorters  and the business organi sations representing the companies placing the pre-
packed products on the market , such as the Polish Union of the Cosmetics Industry . The vast majority of 
the questions is worded as addressed to the citizens/consumers. Therefore, there is not legitimate to answer 
the questionnaire on behalf of Just a few questions in the questionnaire were possible to be answered on 
behalf of the Union.  To enable t he answers, t he questionnaire should differentiate the questions to be 
addressed separately to the various stakeholders, participants of the waste and packaging waste 
management value chain. The Commission is stating in the survey, that this consultation of stakeholders is 
conducted to collect additional evidence but this is not the case that all stakeholders are represented in the 
survey. Therefore, answer to most of the questions on behalf of the Union was not possible. 
The Polish Union is of the opinion that the public consultations of WFD should include all stakeholders being 
addressees of its provisions, namely recyclers, converters, sorters, producers of the packaging and of the 
pre-packed products. Therefore, the questionnaire should  be accordingly prepared and contain the 
questions addressed to those stakeholders engaged, including experts in waste management but not only 
citizens/laypeople. But unfortunately, according to available information, the EC is not planning to conduct 
additional consultations at this stage, which would be  dedicated to other stakeholders, participants of the 
waste and/or packaging waste value chain. 
It is hardly to expect the EC would be able to achieve exhaustive and reliable conclusions based on the 
internal services and external consultants with the perspective of the citizen s as only stakeholder  in the 
consultations. The Chemical Strategy on Sustainability could be serve as an example that this is not th e 
best way of preparing the EU law, according to the Better Regulation Principles. In the case of the Chemical 
Strategy, the public concern towards chemicals (but not the science, facts and current state of chemical 
safety) was taken as main source of the EC decisions and proposals of the subsequent regulations. This 
should not be the case nor in chemical nor aby other areas, such waste management.  
The Union is of the opinion that to build effective system of the waste management in the EU, as many as 
possible areas of  waste management - namely labelling of the products, sorting, recycling technologies 
should be harmonized as far as possible. We do not have "national" products or national packaging today 
on the EU market and this of one of the greatest achievements of the EU. One of the greatest responsibilities 
of the producers is to optimized the products towards minimizing the waste generation. To be able to 
ecodesign products and packagings, the rules on waste sorting and recycling should be harmonized in the 
EU. The system would not work  effectively if the packaging ecodesigned in one EU Member State, would 
be non-sortable or non-recyclable in other Member State. 
Moreover, to build the effective EU waste management system, the European law should be timely 
implemented in all the Members States. This  is not the case in certain countries, including Poland. The 
previous revision of the WFD (EPR directive 2018/851/EC) was still not implemented in Poland. Therefore, 
this implementation is already delayed for more, than 2 years. This situation pose a challeng e to the all 
stakeholders, as from one hand producers pay low fees to place the products in the packaging on the 
market, but on the other hand - there is no financial resources to build the effective system of the waste

management. Such delay does not allow for improvement in the infrastructure, technologies  and 
organisation of the packaging waste management system  in Poland . There ar e certain costs including 
environmental, social and economic of such delayed implementation.  There is no ecomodulation system in 
place in PL and therefore, there are no financial incentives  for stakeholders to ecodesign and minimise 
waste generation.  
There are plenty of individual, voluntary initiatives undertaken by the companies towards packaging waste 
management according to the waste management hierarchy in place. However, harmonized regulatory 
framework in place in all EU Members States is necessary element of the effective waste management 
system, ensuring the equal obligations, rights and chances for the industry. 
Therefore, revision of the WFD should take into consideration the overview of the local, national situation in 
terms of the directives implementation, available technologies, infrastructure, financing and other resources. 
Moreover, the WFD revision should also envisage harmonization (as far as possible) of the rues for waste 
management system and the  related products requirements (as labelling, ecodesign or ecomodulation) at 
EU level. 
The Polish Union of the Cosmetics Industry, organisation representing the producers of the pre-packed 
products and the producers of packaging is interested in participation in further steps of regulatory process 
for WFD revision, including consultations, workshops, surveys etc.

The Polish Union of the Cosmetics Industry, as the only organisation in Poland, represents and supports the 
strategic goals of entrepreneurs exclusively in the cosmetics industry. For over 18 years it has been an active voice 
of the sector in the process of law making. It effectively cooperates with institutions of Polish and European 
administration on a daily basis, and together with member companies it develops and implements solutions that 
lead to the development of the Polish cosmetics market, which is now fifth in the European Union. 
The Union is a member of the Cosmetics Europe - the European trade organisation and Confederation Lewiatan - 
the most influential organisation of employers in Poland. It is also involved in activities building the position of the 
sector abroad and helps promote the export of Polish cosmetics, builds a network of partnerships and, together 
with partners, removes barriers in international trade. The organisation also acts as a platform for the exchange 
of experiences - educates and trains companies, improving the quality of the entire sector. For 13 years, it has also 
been conducting the social project Beautiful Life Foundation. 
The Union brings together  over 200 companies , including cosmetics producers and distributors, labor atories, 
consulting companies and teaching centres, as well as their branch partners - suppliers of packaging and raw 
materials. It unites and works for startups, small family businesses as well as large international corporations and 
the largest Polish cosmetics companies, which work together effectively in an atmosphere of mutual respect and 
trust, while maintaining all competition rules.

Amendment concerning the proposal for a REGULATION OF THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT 
AND OF THE COUNCIL on standards of quality and safety of substances of human origin for 
human use (SoHo) and repealing Directives 2002/98/EC and 2004/23/EC. 
 
The reasons and objectives of the proposal refer (source page 1 point 1) to Directives 
2002/98/EC and 2004/23/EC. These have contributed to the "safety of millions of patients 
treated with blood transfusions, transplants, or medically assisted reproduction." The new 
safety standards are intended to update the blood, tissue and cell regulations, which are 
outdated after more than 20 years, to achieve a high level of health protection for EU 
citizens and to ensure that they have access to safe and effective blood, tissue and cell 
products. This request is absolutely to be welcomed! 
 
However, the formulations in the current proposal would lead to an overall increase in the 
personnel and organizational effort required in the manufacture of medicinal products. Due 
to the increased effort, there is a risk that pharmaceutical companies will incur excessive 
costs. As a result, the stock of some naturopathic preparations, e.g. microbiological 
medicines, would be endangered. For patients, this would mean that they would no longer 
be available. Furthermore, the increased expense for patients could lead to a reduction in 
the therapeutic range and restriction of the freedom of choice of treatment methods. 
 
This would also be an additional burden for health professionals and professional users in 
Europe, as the practice could be declared a SoHo facility. Common natural therapies, such as 
all forms of autologous blood treatment, ozone therapy, symbiosis control of the gut, auto-
urine application, etc., would be complicated or made impossible. Microbiota of human 
origin currently represent an indispensable tool in the treatment of many immune disorders.  
 
Autologous blood treatments have been performed in Germany, but also for example in 
France, Italy, Spain and other EU countries for decades in many thousands of practices by 
professional practitioners. These therapeutic procedures have a high health benefit for 
patients and have been proven to be extremely low-risk. It is therefore imperative that the 
new guideline be formulated in such a way that such proven therapeutic procedures as 
autologous blood treatment are excluded from the new guideline so that they continue to 
be available to patients.  
 
This should therefore be ensured by making minor changes to the wording of the definitions 
and the definition of the scope of application.

Justification 
 
The scope of the proposed EU Directive aims to make blood transfusions and transplants 
from one patient to another patient safer. These proposals will be introduced into national 
legislation by amending the Transfusion Act and the Transplantation Act. 
In transfusions, transplantations or donations of SoHO in general, one person (donor) 
donates, and after processing a SoHO, another person (recipient) usually receives this SoHO 
preparation. The processing operations that take place at different locations, e.g., for 
collection (SoHO facility), storage (SoHO facility), and administration (another or the same 
SoHO facility), create a process that is accompanied by many risks. These are minimized by 
the aforementioned regulatory framework.  
 
1. Example of autologous blood treatment:  
 
Here, it is essential to ensure that this does not inadvertently define, and thereby include, a 
treatment procedure as an "SoHO procedure" in which a patient receives his or her own 
blood back immediately, without delay and without leaving the treatment situation or room. 
In principle, patients are not donors in all autologous blood treatments - but they would be 
defined as donors by an unfavorable wording in the current proposal!  
In all variants of autologous blood treatment, the professional user draws blood from a 
specific patient and procedurally always administers it back to the same patient. Therefore, 
there is no storage and the blood never leaves the immediate, direct control of the user. 
There is therefore no labeling and there is explicitly no risk of transmissible infection, as the 
procedure is regulated by medical standards (Krinko recommendations). There is no risk of 
infection for the patient from pathogens contained in the blood, as the patient receives only 
his or her own blood for therapy. 
 
The following methods of autologous blood therapy are used: 
- Usually, the blood is mixed with medicines approved under pharmaceutical law, usually 
homeopathic medicines, or with aqua ad injectabilia (for hemolysis) and immediately 
injected back to the same patient.  
- In other variants of autologous blood treatment, the blood is taken aseptically through a 
collection system and, without being separated from the patient, is immediately shaken with 
an oxygen-ozone gas mixture of a certain quantity and infused back.  
- In another variant, the blood is treated with a CE-certified irradiation device (UVB) before 
being returned (= Hematogenous Oxidation Therapy = HOT). Here, too, the blood is returned 
to the patient without any delay or spatial change. 
All autologous blood treatment variants thus differ demonstrably clearly from autologous 
blood donation (in the guideline default "autologous use of SoHO").  
 
Unfortunately, the definitions and scope of the current proposal are such that those health 
care professionals who perform autologous blood treatments would automatically become 
"SoHO facilities." Practice owners would have to apply for "authorization of SoHO 
preparations" for each treatment procedure, and therefore for each patient, under Article 
40.  
 
Article 51 would make only those health professionals "SoHO facilities" if they have the 
"formal qualification as a physician" and the "at least two years of practical experience in the 
relevant fields." For all other professional users, this would be tantamount to a ban on

Proposed Changes - We urge that the text be changed in two places and suggest the 
following wording: 
 
Original proposed text: 
"Article 2, Scope Paragraph (2). 
(2) In cases of autologous use of SoHO in which.  
(b) the SoHO are processed and not stored prior to retransfer, only the provisions on 
vigilance under Article 35, on SoHO rapid alerts under Article 36, on registration of SoHO 
establishments under Article 37, on authorization of SoHO preparations under Article 40, 
and on collection and reporting of activity data under Article 44 of this Regulation shall be 
applicable,  
 
(c) the SoHOs are not processed or stored prior to the retransfer, this Regulation does not 
apply." 
 
Amendment A 
 
(c) the SoHO are not processed and stored prior to retransmission, this Regulation does not 
apply.  
Mixing blood with a drug or enriching it with an oxygen-ozone mixture or irradiating it as 
part of an autologous blood treatment is not processing for purposes of this regulation. 
Original proposed text 
"Article 3 Definitions. 
(23) 'autologous use' means the retransfer of SoHO to the same person from whom it was 
obtained, with or without other SoHO activities between obtaining and retransferring;" 
 
Amendment B 
 
(23) "autologous use" means the retransfer of SoHO to the same person from whom they 
were obtained, with or without further SoHO activities between collection and retransfer; 
not "autologous use" but individual therapy is the mixing of blood with a drug or enrichment 
with an oxygen-ozone mixture or irradiation as part of autologous blood treatment. 
 
2. Example of auto-urine treatment:  
 
In the application of auto-urine, urine from the patient is reinjected into the patient after 
sterile filtration (all bacteria and all pathogens greater than bacteria). It is an application to 
the same patient, so there is no risk of infection. The only exception is HIV viruses, but these 
come from the same patient and do not pose an additional risk due to the low numbers 
detectable in the urine. 
The urine is not processed and stored.

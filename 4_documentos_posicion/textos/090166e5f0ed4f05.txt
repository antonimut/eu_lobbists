⇒ Confusion about the limit between “SoHO” and “SoHO preparation” for microbiomes

Importance of definitions 
• The PRI appreciates the EC willingness to take into account the feedback from various stakeholders in the new definition of SoHOwhich 
provides regulatory clarity for currently unregulated SoHOsuch as human microbiomes.
• This new broad definition is flexible enough to allow for current as well as unforeseen future innovation (as to avoid difficulties encountered for 
human microbiomes up to now). 
• However, some definitions such as “SoHO preparation” and “SoHO establishment” merit further clarification. 
• All microbiomes require a “preservation step” at collection => are they considered as “SoHO preparations” at the time of collection?
• In the particular case of microorganisms isolated from microbiomes preserved at the time of collection (SoHOs? SoHO prep?) and 
stored in culture collections, it is not clear whether they are still under the definition of SoHO preparation. 
• “SoHO preparation” means a particular type of SoHO that: 
a) has been subjected to one or more SoHO activities, including processing, in accordance with defined quality and safety parameters;
b) meets a pre-defined specification; and
c) is intended for application to a recipient for a specific clinical indication or is intended for distribution for manufacture of a product
regulated by other Union legislation, or as the starting and raw material thereof;
 “And” versus “Or” between point A and points B&C remains to be specified.
• “SoHO establishment” means a SoHO entity that carries out both processing and storage of SoHOs
 Examples: 1) A SoHO entity performing only processing or storage of SoHOs is not considered an establishment. 2) A SoHO entity 
performing only quality control testing of SoHO is not considered an establishment even if the quality control testing is key to ensure the 
quality and safety of the SoHO. 3) what about an establishemnt which would already be a pharma facility and does processing and storage of 
SoHOs raw material. Would this entity need to be both a SoHO establishement and a Pharma facility ? 
• The term ‘intestinal microbiota’ is used. “Microbiota” refers to the bacterial population only, excluding non-bacterial matter. This would 
therefore exclude any non-bacterial matter from the definition. Reference to “intestinal microbiome” and “microbiomes” along thedocument 
would be more comprehensive.

• Isolation processes and strain characterization is a research activity and associated with major risk mitigation: Therefore, the status of the cell collections and research 
cell banks is not as sensitive as long as traceability between donor and recipients of future SoHO derived products is maintained and appropriate standards based on 
intended use are applied to the future Master and Working cell Banks created and kept under appropriate pharma, food or other frameworks and standards. 
 “SoHO preparation” definition should be clarified as to exclude strain isolation, research cell banks and culture collection from the definition (research activities).
 Requiring SEC of the SoHO preparation from which the strain is originating at deposit in culture collection or introduction within the research, master and working cell 
banks would ensure traceability to the donor and donation characteristics 
Importance of the risk analysis
• Risk analysis will also drive collections conditions and standards based on the nature and intended use of the SoHO.
⇒ Need for a work on the definition of the types of risks associated with the various SoHOs (for example various human microbiome sources) 
and/or SoHO preparation (based on clarification of the definition) in the context of the variety of their potential intended use.
• For human microbiomes, the risk may vary based on the donor as well as the time of donation for a single donor => to be integrated in the risk 
analysis.
o Repetition of administration from the same donor to the same recipient still carries a significant risk as composition of the
microbiome varies along time1,2 and environmental factors have a strong influence on microbiome composition3.
o Thorough characterization of the microbiome for each donation is necessary for SoHO preparations for which the risk has not been 
mitigated by manipulation. In some indications, composition of the microbiome may impact efficacy4,5,6 and compositional and 
functional characterization may be needed for proper risk/benefit assessment for the intended recipients.
• As microbiome alterations participate in the development or aggravation of non communicable diseases, non communicable disease transmission 
through microbiome interventions cannot be excluded on a long-term basis. Current state of the art does not allow direct imputation to specific 
components or activities of the microbiome; therefore, this will require:
 Long-term follow up of the recipients and donors based on types of applications and intended use (chronic diseases, age, etc)
 extensive prospective cohorts (which could be financed through R&I framework programs) in order to better address long-
term risks in the field of microbiome.
Importance of transparency, procedure duration and fees
• As the proposal for a regulation on SoHO covers a large spectrum of substances but also a large spectrum of products for which SoHOare 
starting/raw material
 A large variety of industries will be impacted as they use human microbiomes as starting material to manufacture products ranging from 
food/food supplement/FSMP to medicinal products, medical device and cosmetics=> all stakeholders within the different fields concerned 
should be involved in defining standards and guidelines for their field as not to hinder future innovation.
o EDQM current activity in the CD-P-TO committee does not allow for public inquiry before publication  public consultation should be part of any 
standards and guidelines drafting process as to ensure all potential risks and the variety of potential applications are taken into account.
o The PRI wishes that the regulation and/or delegated acts ensure that procedures duration is defined so as not to hinder innovation due to lengthy 
procedures or unequal treatment among national competent authorities. 
o Also, the PRI feels that the proposal has omitted whether there will be fees for the various procedures and if harmonization is anticipated at the EU 
level or will be managed at the member state level. In this regards discounted fees for research organizations and SMEs couldbe envisaged to 
support innovation and early interaction with competent authorities.

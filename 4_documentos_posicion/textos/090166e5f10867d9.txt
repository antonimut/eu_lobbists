European Society of Human Reproduction and Embryology (ESHRE)  welcomes the  Commission’s 
publication of the Proposal for a Regulation on standards of quality and safety for substances of 
human origin (SoHOs) intended for human application . While we have already made a number of  
recommendations for the field of medically assisted reproduction (MAR)  during its drafting phase, 
with this statement, we would like to express our position on the published proposal and present 
additional recommendations and remarks to be considered in the upcoming negotiations.  
1 HARMONIZATION OF PRACTICES  
First and foremost, ESHRE is appreciative of the Commission’s efforts to allow for and ensure higher 
harmonisation of practices in the field of MAR  across European Member States by proposing a joint 
regulation to replace the current Directives 2002/98/EC and 2004/23/EC on blood, tissues and cells. 
As we advocated for, it indeed remains of utmost importance within our field that the authorisation 
process of new MAR technology/methodology  and of SoHO establishments, together with the data 
collection, and inspections’ quality, content and evaluation methods are harmonised at EU level.   
2 DEFINITIONS  
ESHRE further welcomes the revised and newly proposed definitions of ‘within couple use’ and ‘third 
party donation’ to replace the  current terminology referring to ‘partner  donation’ and non -partner 
‘donation’ which is in use within the scope of Directive 2004/23/EC and its implementing acts.  
Nonetheless, ESHRE would like to appeal that this distinction need to be clarified for the MAR 
field, together with the distinction between the t erms ‘recipients’ and ‘donors’, throughout the 
whole legislative text, and mainly its definitions provided in Article 3 of the proposed Regulation.  
In our professional opinion, in the context of ‘within couple use’ (Article 3(63)):  
→ the person supplying their own oocytes for a MAR treatment should be considered and 
defined as a ‘patient’ at the time of stimulation and procurement, and as a ‘recipient’ at 
the time of the embryo transfer or intrauterine sperm insemination, given the revised 
definition of this term; while 
→ the person supplying their own sperm should be defined as a ‘partner’, not as a ‘SoHO 
donor’, with such clarification added to the definition of a ‘SoHO donor’ in Article 3(8), 
stating the following: ‘SoHO donor’ means any person who has p resented themselves to 
a SoHO entity with a view to makin g a donation of SoHOs, whether the donation is 
successful or not; the person supplying sperm for ‘within couple use’ is not considered a 
‘SoHO donor’, but a ‘partner’ 
Consequently, the definition of ‘within couple use ’ in Article 3( 63) shall be adapted : ’within 
couple use ’ means use of reproductive cells  for medically assisted repr oduction from two 
persons with an intimate physical relationship, where one person supplies their own oocytes 
(‘patient’) and the other person supplies their own sperm (‘partner’) 
In the context of a ‘third-party donation’ (Article 3(62)) in MAR, it needs to be clarified that: 
→ the person is  defined as  a ‘recipient’ when they receive donated o ocytes or embryos 
(from their own oocytes), which were inseminated with third-party donated sperm; and 
→ the person is a defined as a ‘donor’ when they presented themselves to a SoHO entity 
with a view to making a donation of SoHOs , whether that donation is succ essful or not  
(as provided in Article 3(8)).

In this regard, we also find it important to emphasize that partners in within couple use should 
not be mistaken for nor referred to as ‘allogeneic donors ’, and w hile we see the intention of 
legislators in Article 53(1) on Standards for donor protection to exclude within  couple partners 
from its scope, it needs to be clarified in the text to avoid ambiguity. Therefore, we would like to 
propose that Article 53(1) explicitly refers to both ‘allogeneic donors and third-party donors’. 
With reference to Article 3(18) , providing the definition of ‘release’, we would like to highlight that, 
in the field of MAR, the ‘defined quality criteria’ for release of  SoHOs or SoHO preparations can be 
applied to the processes, but not to the biological material itself, as the experience in our field shows 
that inseminating with suboptimal sperm samples or transferri ng morphologically suboptima l 
embryos may both lead to the birth of healthy children, with no increased risk of adverse outcomes 
for the mother and child.  
3 DONOR, OFFSPRING AND RECIPIENT PROTECTION 
Furthermore, ESHRE welcomes the strengthened scope of the proposed Regulation regardin g the 
protection of third-party donors and offspring born from MAR. ESHRE recognizes that the following 
developments in this regard will be in a positive direction and highly relevant to our field: 
→ mandatory third-party donor health evaluations;  
→ mandatory registration and follow-up of third-party donors subjected to a surgical procedure 
in order to donate, donors who are treated with hormones to facilitate  donation, and those 
who donate on a frequent and repeated basis; and 
→ mandatory reporting concerning b oth third-party donors and offspring from MAR, including 
serious adverse occurrences (SAOs) reporting, which is now proposed to additionally entail 
genetic conditions transmitted to offspring from MAR as SAOs.  
 
ESHRE also appreciates the efforts to regulate the conditions for compensation and reimbursement 
allowances from SoHo entities to donors for losses related to their participation in donations  at the 
national level; including the obligation for national authorities to  set and/or approve an upper limit 
of these allowances to ensure their financial neutrality and compliance with standards that prevent 
the provision of financial incentives or inducements to promote third-party donations.  
With regards to the protection of reci pients, ESHRE we lcomes that Article 58 (10) shall constrai n 
SoHO entities from applying SoHO preparations to recipients without  proven benefit, unnecessarily, 
and from advertising or promoting particular SoHO preparations to potential  recipients or health  
care professional s using  information that is misleading  (in particular, as to the potential use and 
benefits to recipients of the SoHO concerned ). ESHRE acknowledges this as a positive development 
that shall hopefully address and mitigate the use of unvalidated add-on procedures for MAR.   
4 EXPERT AND PATIENT INVOLVEMENT  
ESHRE is also appreciative of the extended role that experts may /will play within the scope of th is 
Regulation, including the assessments of SoHO preparations, inspections of SoHO establishm ents 
and SoHO entities, and their participation at the meetings of the SoHO Coordination Board.  
On the other hand, we would  still like to see more explicit provisions on the involvement of 
experts from scientific societies (e.g., ESHRE) in the development and updating of ECDC and 
EDQM guidelines. At the moment, there is no formal requirement for these expert bodies to 
consult and cooperate with experts from scientific societies in relation to their guidelines on

SoHOs, and our involvement as a society for the field of MAR , although having worked very 
well, is rather ad hoc. Consequently, we would like for such a requirement to be defined, for 
instance, in  a cooperation agreement between the Commission and respective expert 
bodies; so that at least two experts from each SoH O field (i.e., representatives of scientific 
societies) are present and involved in the development and updating of these technical 
guidelines.  
ESHRE additionally advocates for increased possibilities for active patient participation  in the SoHO 
Coordination Board, for instance, by inviting patient organisations and representative groups to join 
its meetings as observers. Since the provisions of Recital 38 and Article 67 on the SoHO Coordination 
Board are both quite open in this regard, we would like to see that the involvement and role of 
patients in the  SoHO Coordination Board is more clearly described in the future  rules of procedure 
or implementing act of this Regulation.  
5 QUALIFICATIONS AND SPECIALIZED TRAINING OF INSPECTORS 
ESHRE further welcomes that , in li ne wi th o ur recommendations, inspectors of SoHO entities and 
establishments are now proposed to not merely possess evidence of their formal qualifications in a 
relevant field, but they shall also undergo a specific induction training, complemented by specialized 
training for inspection of specific types of establishments  (e.g., MAR clinics) and continuous training 
throughout their careers; as provided in Article 32 on Specific obligations concerning inspectors in 
the proposed Regulation.  
6 DATA COLLECTION  
ESHRE also supports and welcom es the extended requirements for data collection, as provided in 
the proposed Regulation, for instance to collect donor follow -up data and activity data of SoHO 
entities. Moreover, we are appreciative of the role that th e international registries may play within 
the scope of this Regulation, such as taking over certain tasks related to collection and submission of 
data to the EU SoHO Platform.  
ABOUT ESHRE 
ESHRE is a European non -profit organisation with international membership, whose main mission is 
to promote the study and research of reproductive science and medicine as well as the treatment of 
infertility. Established in 1984, the Society now comprises more than 8.000 members and has 
become the leading  Society in repr oductive sci ence and medicine world wide. Our members are 
medical professionals, scientists and researchers working in reproductive science, reproductive 
medicine and embryology.

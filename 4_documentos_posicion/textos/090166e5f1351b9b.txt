ASSUC is the voice of European sugar traders, representing over 60 sugar trading companies across Europe 
through its membership. 
 
ASSUC welcomes the opportunity to provide feedback on the revision of the Union Customs Code and looks 
forward to the publication of the UCC reform package by the European Commission. ASSUC recognises the 
importance of having a modern, straightforward, and harmonised customs system which addresses the 
needs of business, governments, and consumers.  
 
Whilst the EU’s Customs Union has brought tremendous benefits since its inception in 1968, there remain a 
number of critical drawbacks to the current system which hamper its full potential, burdening businesses 
and undermining the greater goals of the single market. 
 
In the case of sugar, the European Union depends on imports to meet the demands of industries, 
manufacturers, and consumers. As such, customs operations play an integral role in the sugar business, 
ensuring smooth imports and exports of sugar to and from the European Union.  
 
Sugar traders are particularly concerned with special customs procedures such as inward and outward 
processing, using the INF and related procedures. Whereas the digitisation of the INF procedures is 
welcome, for some time the process has resulted in numerous challenges that are burdensome, costly, and 
discouraging both for operators and customs officials alike. 
 
Improving customs operations for sugar trade is a priority of the European Association of Sugar Traders. It is 
our fervent hope that the European Commission will make a careful consideration of the recommendations 
of trade and industry in the reform of the Union Customs Code. 
 
Recommendation 1: improve interoperability between the EU trader portal and Member States national 
customs systems 
 
Today both data from export and import are manually entered from the national customs system into the 
INF portal. This places an extra burden on customs officers, which creates backlogs and delays in processing 
the data through the INF portal.  
 
As the information is entered manually into the INF portal, a risk for the IP holder is created as mistakes can 
easily be made. In case these mistakes are not identified / corrected in time, this could lead to incorrect IP 
transactions, which are then the responsibility of the IP holder, despite the fact the error was created 
beyond the control of the IP holder.

in the portal needs to be available in proper fully exportable data sets. Having enhanced data 
interoperability between the EU trader portal and the MS national customs systems could reduce the

Recommendation 2: more regular guidance by DG TAXUD to Member States customs authorities on the 
application of the UCC provisions on IP and to improve the INF procedures 
 
In some Member States, the customs authorities demand that the IP holder issues an INF for every single 
export declaration. This results in an unworkable situation and places an unreasonable burden on the IP 
holder. 
Additionally, the UCC provides the possibility for an extension of period of discharge, as per article 257. 
ASSUC is of the view that the UCC permits this extension to be request without providing any justification or 
reasoning for the request, but some MS customs authorities request a justification. This places the decision 
on the extension at their discretion. Some MS customs authorities request a lot of information / 
documentation to justify the request for an extension. 
 
Finally, although there is a 6 month period of discharge under IP, the reality is that this period is limited by 
the practical steps needed to process the INF. To begin with, it takes time to get the exports from the 
national system into the INF portal. After the “Confirmation of exit” is in the national system, it also needs to 
be manually processed in the INF portal. This whole process takes in total between 4 to 8 weeks after the 
export date, which means that there is only 4 months left to import the goods. 
 
All these issues could be addressed by a more active approach by DG TAXUD in setting out guidelines, 
recommendations, and guidance for Member States in order to promote the harmonised and consistent 
implementation of the UCC, and to simplify and reduce the administrative burden for operators, customs 
authorities, and DG TAXUD itself. 
 
 
ASSUC would encourage the European Commission to focus on a reform package for the UCC which truly 
promotes a resilient, sustainable, and dynamic single market, and ensures that the UCC is a reliable and 
consistent instrument to enable its users to meet the demands of trade and customs in the 21st century. 
 
In this regard, the establishment of a European Customs Agency, if done well, could provide support to the 
Commission and the Member States to carry out their mandates, though careful consideration should be 
taken to avoid the duplication of tasks, or an increase in bureaucracy and in administrative costs for all 
concerned. 
 
ASSUC wishes to be included in future discussions on this topic, and remains available for any question or 
more information that the Commission may require.

Concrete proposals to improve the inward processing regime 
The purpose of the present document is to provide concrete proposals to improve 
transparency in the current inward processing regime.  
We emphasize that these proposals would not require a revision of the existing Union 
Customs Code legal package1 but would offer mechanisms and procedures to improve 
transparency in the EU and guarantee the rights of defense of each interested party 
during the inward processing procedure. This would ultimately improve the analysis to 
be made by the Commission and Member States of the “economic conditions” when an 
inward processing application is filed, as required by the Union Customs Code.  
1. ISSUES AT STAKE 
Article 211(4)(b) of the Union Customs Code provides that inward processing 
authorisations “ shall be granted only where […] the essential interests of Union 
producers would not be adversely a ffected by an authorization for a processing 
procedure (economic conditions)”2.  
The way IP requests are currently examined undermines the practical effect of this 
provision.  
• Member States are in charge of examining IP requests. In case a Member State has 
evidence that the essential interests of Union producers are likely to be adversely 
affected as a result of an IP request, it is required to submit it to the EU Customs 
Expert Group to have an examination of the economic conditions at EU -level. 
However, a Member State will only examine an IP request and its potential impact 
in the light of its national industry, thus avoiding an examination of a potential 
impact on Union producers located outside its territory. In case this Member State 
has no local industry for that product, it would not be able to perform an 
appropriate examination of the IP request in accordance with Article 211(4)(b) of

2. PROPOSALS TO IMPROVE TRANSPARENCY 
To guarantee full practical effect of Article 211(4)(b) of the Union Customs Code, CIRFS 
submits the following proposals: 
1) The creation of a database at the EU level for interest ed parties to consult the 
non-confidential versions of inward processing applications and authorizations  
a. Access to the non -confidential versions of inward processing applications 
filed in the 27 Member States 
Since there is currently no public information  allowing Union producers to become 
aware of the inward processing applications submitted in the 27 Member States, they 
are thus not able to contribute to the assessment made by the Member State where

While the cooperation between Member States at an earlier stage should also be

The access to the non-confidential versions of inward processing authorizations in the 
27 Member States is critical for the Union producers to assess the cumulative impact 
of any new inward processing applications filed before the national authorities of a 
Member State or to determine if a precedent already exists.  
c. Proposed model for a centralized EU-wide database 
The database could be designed on the same model as the existing European Binding 
Tariff Information (EBTI) database which appears to be accessible to the public online4 
and which was set up for transparency reasons 5, as should be the case for IP 
applications and authorisations. 
An online platform at the EU level - the Customs Decisions System (CDS) – was already 
set up to enable economic operators to introduce their applications for customs 
decisions or authorisations bef ore the national authorities of the Member States. 
Documents regarding inward processing applications and decisions are thus already 
exchanged online via the centralized EU-wide CDS. However, Union producers or their

2) Creation of a mechanism to ensure that the cumulative impact of inward 
processing authorization is factored in when examining the economic conditions 
When examining the economic conditions, the EU Customs Expert Group examines the 
inward processing application individually and fails to examine the economic 
conditions in the light of the global volume of non-EU product already imported under 
the IP regime (‘cumulative impact’).   
In certain sectors, due to the already existing inward processing authorisations 
granted, the combined volume processed under the inward processing regime can 
become so high that, per se,  it severely affects essential interests of the Union 
producers. This is all the more true as the EU conclusions taken on the basis of the EU 
Customs Expert Group’s advice serve as a precedent for other applications or 
authorisations6 that would no longer be subject to an examination of the economic 
conditions. 
To ensure that the cumulative impact of inward processing authorisation is factored in 
when the economic conditions are examined, it might be relevant to establish 
economic indicators at sectoral level  which would trigger an automatic examination 
of the economic conditions at EU level. For instance, a maximum threshold of imports 
under the inward processing regime could be set, beyond which it would be prima 
facie assumed that any additional inward processing authorisation will affect the 
essential interests of Union producers.

3) Revisions of the EU Customs Expert Group’s procedure 
According to the Terms of Reference of the EU Customs Expert Group, the CEG adopts 
rules of procedures by a simple majority of its members on a proposal by and in 
agreement with DG TAXUD8.  
As with the Member States in charge of assessing whether an inward processing 
application could affect the essential interests of the Union producers, the 
contribution of the Union producers is equally essential  in the context of the 
discussions that will take place within the EU Customs Group. Indeed, the Union 
producers are the best placed to explain whether an inward processing application in 
a given context is likely to adversely affect their essential interests9 and their views are 
thus essential to make a proper examination of the economic conditions as required 
by Article 211(4)(b) of the Union Customs Code. 
To ensure that the Union producers can duly exercise their rights effectively during the 
discussions of EU Customs Expert Group, CIRFS calls therefore for a revision of the EU 
Customs Expert Group’s procedure by adding the following rules which do not require 
any revision of the UCC legal package but only a majority vote within the Customs 
Expert Group.

a. Automatic consultation of the Union producers when the EU Customs 
Expert Group (CEG) is required to examine the economic conditions  
While the Union producers are generally consulted by the Commission when the 
economic conditions are examined by the EU Customs Expert Group, this is only a good 
practice which largely depends on the Commission’s own willingness. 
CIRFS calls therefore Article 8 of the CEG rules to be amended with a view to require 
the Commission to consult the relevant EU associations  or companies concerned – 
either directly or through a publication on the CEG website – when it examines the 
economic conditions of an inward processing application.  
b. Right to take part of the discussions if there is an appeal by the inward 
processing applicant following a negative indicative vote   
Currently, Union producers are not entitled to provide their views in case the inward 
processing applicant appeals a first indicative vote proposing to reject the inward 
processing application. This means that o nly the arguments of the inward processing 
applicant will be put forward during the second round of discussions which will lead to 
the final vote. This difference of treatment can have significant impact on the decision 
of Member States and thus significantly disadvantage the Union industry10. 
Article 8 of the Rules of Procedure of the Customs Expert Group– which already reflects 
the right to be heard for the inward processing applicant – should be amended to allow 
for Union producers to be consulted during  the second round of discussions in case 
the inward processing applicant makes use of its right to be heard.  
In the meantime, we also note from the Rules of Procedure of the Customs Expert 
Group that the Chair of the EU Customs Expert Group may decide to invite 
representatives of other third parties or experts with specific competence to 
participate in the work of the group or sections, on his/her own initiative or at the 
request of a member of the group 11. CIRFS calls for the Commission to regularl y use 
this possibility to ensure an effective representation of the Union producers concerned 
during the appeal stage.

d. Immediate publication to interested parties of the outcome of the vote in 
the EU Customs Expert Group on IP requests  
As explained above, when an inward processing authorisation is granted, the Union 
producers have no way to know it. While it is true that the Commission publishes 
minutes of the EU Customs Group – which allows the Union producers to have an 
indication of t he EU Customs Expert Group’s opinion on the examination of the 
economic conditions – those minutes are most of the time published on the 
Commission’s website several weeks/months after the vote takes place.    
However, the standard rules of procedure for e xpert groups make it clear that DG 
TAXUD shall publish the agenda and other relevant background documents in due time 
ahead of the meeting, followed by timely publication of minutes12.   
While the drafting and finalization of the minutes of the EU Customs Group may take 
some time due to the number of topics addressed during the meeting, CIRFS calls for 
the Commission to immediately release to interested parties the outcome of the vote 
regarding IP requests to ensure transparency.

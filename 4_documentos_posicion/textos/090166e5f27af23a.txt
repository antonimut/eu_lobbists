“CountEmissions EU” - Initiative 
IATA Response to Consultation 
With reference to the EU framework for harmonized measurement of transport and logistics 
emissions - “CountEmissions EU” initiative and related consultation, IATA has prepared a 
response to reflect the opinion of airline members and other industry stakeholders. 
  
Current State and Need for 
Harmonization of CO2 Calculations. 
There has been a noticeable increase in interest by 
passengers to understand the environmental 
impact of air travel. Corporates have set internal 
emissions reduction targets and demand CO 2 
information likewise. Shippers and Freight 
Forwarders also make similar demands for CO 2 
transparency on a cargo shipment level.  
Following this surge of demand for CO 2 
transparency, the aviation and travel sector has 
recently seen  a proliferation of emissions 
calculators and methodologies. This has resulted i n 
confusion among passengers and, as regards 
accuracy, even distrust.  
IATA therefore welcomes the initiative to harmonize 
CO2 emissions calcula tions and the intent of the  
European Commission to develop a common 
framework for calculating GHG emissions. To make 
this an effective exercise, and to avoid a duplication 
of efforts in view of existing  standards and 
methodologies, IATA would like to provide input to 
the “CountEmissions EU” consultation process. 
Methodological Framework: Scope, 
Consistency and Acceptability 
In 2021 , IATA received a mandate from  its airline 
members to develop a meth odology to accurately 
calculate per -passenger CO2 emissions for the 
purpose of displaying results within third party 
Online Travel Agent (OTA) booking portals, Travel 
Management Companies (TMCs) , flight search 
engines, aggregators, or airline programs. It was 
recognized that the application of such a sta ndard 
must be global and should aim for highest adoption 
in the sector to make CO2 results truly comparable.  
Correspondingly, IATA worked with a group of  
industry experts, 20 airlines and manufacturers , to 
develop an industry  Recommended Practice  (RP). 
The group developed a set of guiding principles that 
were expressed as follows: 
• Accurate: provides credible information 
• Precise: provides detailed information that 
vary based on inputs 
• Simple: easy to generate and interpret data 
• Transparent: customer can understand 
how results are generated 
• Informative: customer can make well -
informed decisions on flights/airlines 
• Resilient: adaptable and flexible to meet 
future requirements 
• Aligned: aligns with widely used standards 
(e.g., CORSIA) 
 
The work comprised of  an in-depth review of 
commonly used  methodologies such as  ICAO, 
DEFRA, EN16258, ICAO CERT, EcoTransit, ADEME, 
Atmosfair, and others.  Recognizing existing best 
practices, preference was given to incorporate 
those into the RP, as opposed to developing a 
methodology entirely from scratch.  
The expert group also shared their  findings with 
various stakeholders across the industry, including 
international standards-setting bodies such as ISO, 
SFC, DIN, and major freight forwarders and shippers.

Following its development , the methodology - 
RP1726 was approved by IATA Passenger

RP1726 was officially released in March 2022 and 
provides the most accurate CO2 calculation for 
organizations and individuals to make informed 
choices about flying sustainably . The RP is publicly 
available on the IATA website and can be used by 
anyone. Since its release, the standard has gained 
wide acceptance in the airline and travel industry 
and we are seeing broad adoption by key  
stakeholders such as airlines, OTAs , TMCs. , and 
web search engines. 
IATA has also developed a methodology for the 
calculation of cargo shipments (RP1678), currently 
under revision to reflect  changes and to align wi th 
the passenger RP.  The updated version of RP1678 
is expected to be approved by the Cargo Standards 
Conference (CSC) at the end of 2022.

Implementation Specifications 
An industry harmonized approach requires a high 
degree of very specific guidance to calculate CO 2 
emissions on a per -passenger or cargo shipment 
basis. It starts with the area of applicability (e.g., 
airlines, OTAs, TMCs) , the emissions scope (e.g., 
revenue flights vs. non-revenue flights), followed by 
detailed guidance on the fuel , and respective CO2 
allocation to calculate precisely and accurately the 
carbon footprint of a passenger for a given flight. To 
that effect, the IATA RP1726 provides very clear and 
detailed guidance with some of the key areas 
highlighted here:  
• Methodology based on aircraft-specific fuel 
consumption 
• Recommends globally agreed ICAO CORSIA 
fuel measurement methods 
• Clearly defined CO 2 scope, including 
guidance regarding non-CO2 emissions and 
RFI 
• CO2 allocation between pax and cargo 
based on proportional weight 
• Guidance on passenger weight, and 
standard weight 
• Reflecting cabin class configurations and 
use of robust cabin class weightings based 
on industry data 
• Guidance on non -revenue pax/cargo and 
no-show pax 
• Guidance on SAF and carbon offsetting 
• Recommends the use of independently 
audited data

The ICAO CORSIA Standards and Recommended 
Practices (SARPs) ensur e a global application of 
established processes for the monitoring, reporting, 
and verification of CO 2 emissions. The universal 
application of these standards is key to the 
successful implementation of CORSIA, providing a 
level playing field and treating all airlines equally. The 
CORSIA SARPs contain clear requirements 
concerning the verification of data, which must be 
performed by state -accredited independent audit 
organizations.  
To avoid unnecessary administrative burden and to 
reduce costs to airlines, the established processes 
under CORSIA must be leveraged in the context of 
CO2 per-passenger calculati ons. Duplication of 
efforts must be averted and the global application of 
the SARPs, and their stringent data validation 
mechanism to ensure completeness and accuracy 
of data, should provide for robust source to 
calculate CO2 emissions.

Technical Support Measures 
The aviation sector can leverage a high level of 
connectivity and interoperability, and by applying 
existing standards such as the RP1726 a future 
requirement for per -passenger or cargo CO 2 data 
(e.g., at point of sale), can be implemented in a swift 
and most efficient way.  
Only by reliance on existing back -end processes 
and support mechanism specifically developed for 
the aviation sector, can airlines of different size or 
level of sophistication be reached.   
Fuel consumption and airline load factor data used 
for the calculation of CO2 data is commercially highly 
sensitive information, and existing processes can 
eliminate any of the risks linked to infringement of 
data confidentiality or data ownership of airlines.

✓ Data ownership must reside with airlines

Policy Instrument 
IATA would like to offer the support of the industry 
in finding the appropriate type of policy instrument 
for an effective implementation of the initiative.  
The instrument must be fair in view of the respective 
business and operating models of airlines, offering a 
holistic approach when it comes to comparing 
different transport operators/modes of transport 
and associated lifecycle emissions.  
Nearly 50 IATA members offer  CO2 calculation 
results at point of ticket sales and as pa rt of 
voluntary offset programs. Similarly, cargo 
operators are starting to develop cargo booking 
solutions that will provide emissions transparency at 
point of booking of a shipment. Following the 
release of the IATA standard RP1726 (and revision 
of RP167 8 for cargo), we are already seeing many 
airline operators adopting the methodology f or 
future use. Government support can help accelerate 
the adoption rate and ensure consistent application 
and use of the standard methodology by most 
airlines.  
IATA Response to Policy Option Dimension – 
Policy Option: 
✓ Work in collaboration with the aviation 
sector, reaching the highest level of 
adoption of a single standard methodology 
✓ Avoid unnecessary administrative burden 
and additional costs 
✓ Apply industry best pr actices and rely on 
existing methodologies adopted by a large 
majority of the sector 
✓ Achieve emissions transparency and 
highest level of accuracy through the use of 
aviation-specific and real airline data

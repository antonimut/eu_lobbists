Deutschen Bahn AG: Comments on the CountEmissions EU initiative 
 
1. Introduction and background 
Mobility is vital for our society and economy. Our quality of life is determined in no small part by 
our ability to get around. Yet the transport sector is responsible for a high percentage of harmful 
greenhouse gas emissions (GHG emissions), and absolute GHG emissions have been stagnating 
for many years compared with other sectors. One of the aims of the EU Green Deal is to reduce 
transport-related GHG emissions by 90% by 2050.1  
Rail is already the most climate-friendly mode of transport. Of the roughly 25% of Europe's CO2 
emissions caused by the transport sector, rail is responsible for only about 0.5%. While the entire 
transport sector in Germany still emits exactly th e same amount of CO2 today as it did in 1990, 
the base year, Deutsche Bahn (DB) has been able to reduce CO2 emissions in its rail business 
by around 70%. This difference is due to the high degree of electrification in the rail system – 
electric traction is used for around 90% of the volume sold in passenger and freight transport – 
and to our strategy of increasing the percentage of renewable energy in DB's traction current 
significantly faster than the percentage is increasing in the public grid. Rail's future contribution to 
climate protection will primarily be to enable further growth and the modal shift to rail. The goal is 
for rail to become the backbone of sustainable mobility in the future: more robust, more powerful, 
more pioneering, and of course as part of an intelligent network with other modes of transport. 
EN 16258 is currently the only standard that establishes a methodology for calculating GHG in 
the transport sector, but it is merely a guideline and not binding for companies. The standard is 
therefore being revised and will become an international standard, ISO 14083. Germany's car 
labelling regulation (Pkw-Energieverbrauchskennzeichnungsverordnung, "Pkw-EnVKV") and the 
specifications of Directive 1994/94/EC apply to the disclosure of the CO2 emissions of cars. The 
results are not comparable with the results obtained when emissions are calculated based on EN 
16258.  
This means that emissions information differs de pending on which standard is used to calculate 
it. The different methodologies mean that GHG emissions cannot be compared across modes of 
transport. This makes it more difficult for consumers to make sustainable decisions relating to 
transport and mobility. Yet making it easier for stakeholders to choose sustainable transport 
options in an efficient multimodal transport system is crucial if we are to achieve the modal shift 
to rail.2  
By launching the CountEmissions EU initiative, the European Commission intends to create a 
harmonised European framework for calculating and reporting transport-related GHG emissions. 
The framework will apply to both passenger and freight transport and will provide transparent 
information that will enable service providers to reduce their emissions and improve the efficiency 
of their transport services. It will also help EU citizens choose the most sustainable means of 
transport.

2. DB's position: methodological and organisational requirements  
Our Strong Rail corporate strategy is our response today to the question of how we are accepting 
responsibility for our planet and society: We are approaching the DB Group's Green 
Transformation from all angles, meaning we are not just making our products and services 
greener; we are also profoundly transforming the way we work. The Green Transformation 
encompasses the four environment-related areas for action: climate protection, resource 
protection, nature conservation and noise reduction. Meeting our responsibility to society is also 
part of this. To achieve our goals, we are systematically advancing the DB Group's Green 
Transformation. 
 
We are relying on three strong tools to achieve climate neutrality by 2040: making the power we 
use greener; using alternative, climate-friendly fuels; and supplying our depots and buildings with 
climate-friendly heat. We have already made great progress in making the electricity we use 
greener. Renewable energy sources already make up over 62% of DB's traction current mix in 
Germany. We will increase this share to 80% by 2030, and we will convert DB's traction current 
to 100% green electricity by 2038 at the latest. In addition, all of our depots, office buildings and 
railway stations in Germany will be powered by 100% green electricity as soon as 2025. 
 
DB welcomes and supports the European Commission's initiative to introduce a harmonised EU-
wide framework for calculating and reporting GHG emissions. Consistent methodology and 
reporting for different modes of transport will improve the comparability of environmental 
performance and prevent greenwashing. This will make it easier for consumers to choose the 
most sustainable mode of transport, which will reduce GHG emissions in the transport sector. 
These are high-priority goals at DB, which is why DB backs this initiative. However, before the 
Commission presents a legislative proposal, it is important to consider a few methodological and 
organisational aspects in the development of calculating and reporting rules. We discuss these in 
more detail below.  
 
2.1. Methodological requirements 
 
The transport sector has long-established tools that are compatible with the EN162583 standard: 
EcoPassenger (EP) and EcoTransIT World (ETW), which calculate well-to-wheel GHG 
emissions.4 These tools will also be compatible with ISO 14083. 
EP is an online calculator for comparing the energy consumption and CO2 and exhaust emissions 
of planes, cars and trains in passenger transport. ETW is a software that calculates the energy 
consumption, GHG emissions, air pollutants and external costs incurred in freight transport. It can 
calculate full transport chains with different modes of transport (truck, rail, ocean-going vessel, 
inland waterway vessel and aircraft), including transhipment and storage, worldwide. DB 
Schenker (provider of all transport services) and DB Cargo (for rail freight service ordered to and 
from China) already heavily rely on this tool to calculate GHG emissions for freight transport. DB's

The international ISO 14083 standard, which is currently in development, should be used as the 
binding standard for the development of a new GHG accounting methodology in the context of 
the CountEmissions initiative. This would facilitate and accelerate the introduction and 
implementation of a uniform methodology for companies, as it is valid and used around the world. 
Creating an EU standard that would not be compatible with the international ISO 14083 standard 
would work against the objective of improving the comparability of the environmental performance 
of transport modes. 
 
2.2. Organisational requirements  
 
In addition to the methodological aspects, organisational requirements must also be taken into 
account. The most important of these requirements is to ensure a balanced cost-benefit ratio, 
manageability on the company side and an appropriate level of bureaucratic effort.  
In order to ensure comparability across the EU, the European Commission should issue the new 
arrangements for calculating and reporting GHG emissions as a regulation so that they can be 
implemented in national law by the member states.  
 
 
3. Note on the basis for introducing an ecolabel 
The harmonised GHG accounting basis should also form the basis for ecolabelling and contribute 
to implementing the goals of the EU Green Deal. DB is in favour of the plan to introduce an 
ecolabel in passenger and freight transport based on GHG accounting. An ecolabel would be a 
relatively low-threshold policy instrument, but it would be highly effective. It would increase 
consumers' environmental awareness and encourage them to choose the most sustainable mode 
of transport, which would contribute to reducing GHG emissions in the transport sector. 
 
 
Summary: methodological and organisational requirements  
 
1. Uniform accounting rules must be set in order to ensure that the environmental 
performance of different modes of transport can be compared.  
2. In specifying a new GHG accounting methodology under the CountEmissions initiative, 
the EU should be guided by ISO 14083, which is in development. This would also facilitate 
and accelerate the introduction and implementation of a uniform methodology for 
companies.  
3. The new arrangements for GHG accounting and reporting should be adopted as a 
regulation to ensure comparability across the EU. 
4. An ecolabel should be introduced together with the arrangements on accounting and 
reporting.

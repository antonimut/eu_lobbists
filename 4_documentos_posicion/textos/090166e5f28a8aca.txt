Contribution to the Count Emissions EU public consultation
The Zürich 5 Coalition (hereinafter ‘the Coalition’) supports the EU’s ambition to become the first climate 
neutral continent by 2050 through the Fit for 55 Package. The transport sector, particularly road transport, 
has a particularly key role to play as transport accounts for a fifth of today’s greenhouse gas (GHG) 
emissions in the EU. The Coalition supports the Commission’s initiative to set a common methodology to 
calculate and monitor transport GHG emissions. Transparent and harmonized information will enable users 
to choose the most sustainable option. The Coalition believes that the methodology should be developed 
according to technology-neutral criteria. All solutions are needed across different transportation modes.
The existing emissions standards legislation takes a “tank-to-wheel” (TtW) approach which only counts 
transport fuels’ emissions from the moment of refueling. By excluding the emissions generated by the 
production and distribution phases of the energy carriers, this methodology does not correctly account 
for the environmental impact of the used energy. In contrast, a technically sound “well-to-wheel” (WtW) 
calculation, incorporating a “life-cycle approach” (LCA) of vehicles and fuels emissions - which includes all 
phases of its life cycle from the extraction of raw materials to their use should be implemented. 
Based on the above, the Coalition encourages the development of a common methodology to calculate and 
monitor transport GHG emissions that foresees a role for renewable fuels such as biomethane based on the 
whole lifecycle approach. 
In taking a WtW approach, counting the transport fuels’ emissions from the moment of producing the fuel/
energy carrier, biomethane represents one of the lowest GHG intensive possibilities. This is concluded in a 
recently published Science for Policy report by the Joint Research Centre (JRC)1, the European Commission’s 
science and knowledge service. Biofuels can be at least as good as battery- electric or fuel cell drivetrains 
in terms of WtW climate performance.2 With 100% biomethane, the WtW climate change impact is reduced 
by around 65% compared to fossil CNG or 70–75% compared to diesel, or 95% compared to either of them 
with system expansion.3 On maritime decarbonization, a recent study by SEA-LNG shows that bio-LNG can 
reduce GHG emissions by up to 80% compared to diesel.4
Moreover, there are international standards for lifecycle assessments such as ISO 14044.5 Harmonization with 
the international standards ISO 14044 6, which provide the comprehensive methodology to calculate life-
cycle emissions from cradle to grave, is needed. Moreover, the LCA emissions quantification methodology is 
already implemented by RED II and proposed FuelEU Maritime and should be pursued by other EU policies 
(e.g. the whole Fit for 55 package). 
It is in the EU’s best interest to provide a common methodology framework for the uptake of low-carbon 
alternative fuels such as biomethane to reach 55% GHG reduction by 2030. This is essential to provide 
for transparency and to allow informed decisions on the best ways to achieve the emissions reductions 
target and climate-neutrality by 2050. The WtW approach would help emission reductions in the whole EU

The Coalition therefore asks for a correction in the methodology used to calculate and monitor emissions 
from road transport. The WtW approach, which provides transparent information on the extraction of the 
energy source to their use, would confirm that biomethane is an affordable, and available fuel, with positive 
GHG results. This would open many opportunities for additional GHG savings, new jobs, more investments, 
and for consumers to use different (cheaper) renewable fuels. Moreover, Biomethane production is linked 
to waste management and nutrient recycling, which also plays a crucial role in the circular economy.
The Coalition is eager to clarify its position and further engage with policymakers on this subject.

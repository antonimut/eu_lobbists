WSC response to question 28 on CountEmissions EU  CountEmissions EU represents an important effort within the EU framework to harmonise measurement of transport and logistics GHG emissions.  The purpose is to provide shippers and others with a tool to help compare GHG emissions across different modes of transport serving EU supply chains.   The World Shipping Council is the united voice of liner shipping, working with policymakers and industry groups to shape the future growth of a socially responsible, environmentally sustainable, safe, and secure shipping industry. The World Shipping Council is committed to working with the EU Institutions to achieve the Green Deal’s goals through good policy that will support industry greenhouse gas (GHG) reduction targets and move as fast as possible to zero GHG emissions.   WSC welcomes the CountEmissions EU effort to help improve transparency leading to collaborative efforts among all transport modes and terminals to reduce GHGs across the transportation supply chain. CountEmissions EU can build upon the existing common and verified GHG accounting framework for maritime shipping, extending the existing reporting tools to non-maritime modes.  Three examples illustrate how shipping helps inform logistics GHG accounting:    1. Twenty years ago, liner shipping joined the Clean Cargo Working Group (CCWG) that forged methodologies, input data development and validation for common reporting. In fact, the Smart Freight Center reports that ocean container companies participating in CCWG account for over 85 percent of the global container cargo carried.  2. The maritime sector participates fully in European and international policy efforts to report fuel consumption and related emissions of CO2 and other GHGs. EU MRV Regulation 2015/757 currently requires that shipping reports all data that appears necessary for input into CountEmissions EU common metrics.  3. Current ship reporting (i.e., EU MRV) provides a model to ensure consistent requirements in ISO/DIS 14083 under the International Organisation for Standardisation (ISO).   While maritime shipping currently reports necessary data to evaluate GHGs for waterborne voyage segments of the logistics supply chain, we recognize two important elements that need to be considered in developing common metrics and reporting of end-to-end logistics.  • Expanded reporting for multi-modal logistics under a future CountEmissions EU needs to fully align with the EU reporting burden(s) under MRV.  Under EU ETS, MRV will be updated to cover well-mixed greenhouse gases, including carbon dioxide (CO2), methane (CH4) and nitrous oxide (N2O). Therefore, duplication of MRV reporting requirements can be avoided. • Reporting details for environmental transparency purposes must focus on GHG emissions and prevent the sharing competitive and sensitive information related to cargoes.  This includes protecting business-sensitive data of shippers who contract maritime transport services.  Annually aggregated reporting as currently required under MRV with similar cargo details (e.g., TEUs for container shipping, mass for vehicle carriers, etc.) achieves this goal, providing sufficient detail for any requirements under CountEmissions EU.   WSC offers some specific discussion and detail related to these primary considerations in the following paragraphs.

Liner shipping helped develop methodologies, validation, and common reporting  For nearly two decades, liner shipping companies have participated in and co-led collaborative efforts to develop the methodologies, data inputs, and verification needed to report GHGs emitted from maritime and multi-modal transport.  WSC member companies, in particular, were among the leaders who worked with policymakers, academic researchers, and non-governmental groups like the Smart Freight Centre and the Clean Cargo Working Group to develop common metrics that could benefit cargo shippers and logistics providers.   Shipping reports greenhouse gas emissions accounting for IMO and for European Union  The maritime sector participates fully in European and international efforts to report fuel consumption and related emissions of CO2 and other GHGs. These include the European Union (EU) Monitoring, Reporting and Verification (MRV) Regulation, and the International Maritime Organisation (IMO) Data Collection System (DCS).   In the EU context, the MRV reporting system includes all necessary input information for the maritime inputs to any multi-modal and supply chain GHG accounting that the EU may develop or endorse under this initiative. Shipping already reports the necessary inputs to estimate cargo-based GHG emissions through the EU Monitoring, Reporting and Verification (MRV) Regulation [Regulation (EU) 2015/757].  MRV requires annual reporting that summarizes for each voyage the following: a) ports of departure and arrival; b) amount and emission factor for each type of fuel consumed; c) CO2 (expanding to be GHGs measured in CO2e) emitted; d) distance travelled; e) time spent at sea; f) cargo carried*1; and g) transport work.    The International Maritime Organisation (IMO) has, since 2018, required ships of 5,000 tonnes and greater “to collect consumption data for each type of fuel oil they use, as well as other, additional, specified data including proxies for transport work.  Annually aggregated data is reported to the flag State, which verifies the reporting and issues each ship a Statement of Compliance.  Flag States transfer this data to an IMO Ship Fuel Oil Consumption Database.  IMO summarizes the data collected and produces an annual report to MEPC.”2   Current MRV reporting by container ships conforms with ISO/DIS 14083 Methodologies, inputs, and reporting output of these collaborations with liner shipping are well incorporated into current and emerging industry standards including ISO/DIS 14083. CountEmissions EU input data can be drawn from current reporting by container ships and vehicle carriers under MRV Regulation (EU) 2015/757, Annex II, A.1(f) and A.1(g).  For container ships, this will be fully aligned with ISO/DIS 14083, which states “For containerized shipping … TEU is the unit established within the industry for assignment and is recommended as the assignment unit to be combined with assignment distance when calculating transport activity in this transport specific subsector.”3    1 MRV Regulation (EU) 2015/757, Annex II, A.1(f) specifies monitoring on a per-voyage basis that “for container ships, cargo carried shall be defined as the total weight in metric tonnes of the cargo or, failing that, the amount of 20-foot equivalent units (TEU) multiplied by default values for their weight.” For vehicle carriers, “cargo carried … shall enable the taking into account, where applicable, of the weight … of cargo carried.”  2 https://www.imo.org/en/OurWork/Environment/Pages/Data-Collection-System.aspx  3 ISO/DIS 14083 Note: “if a conversion back to emission per tonne is required the net weight of the freight, if known, should be used; otherwise, a standard conversion factor of 10 tonnes per TEU may be used.”

Reporting by vehicle carriers (not addressed in ISO/DIS 14083) can follow EU MRV For ships handling vehicles and other rolling cargoes such as heavy machinery (mining, construction, farming equipment), the most consistent practice would be to follow EU MRV reporting requirements in MRV Regulation (EU) 2015/757, Annex II, A.1(g), i.e., reporting for vehicle carriers per “cargo carried … shall enable the taking into account, where applicable, of the weight … of cargo carried.”

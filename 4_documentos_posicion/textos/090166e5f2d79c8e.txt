OCI’s Position Paper 
EU Soil Health Law  
 
Introduction 
The European Green Deal and the EU Biodiversity Strategy for 2030 set the objective of 
addressing soil and land degradation to reach the EU’s commitments on land degradation 
neutrality. In this regard, in the new EU Soil Strategy for 2030 the EU sets the vision to achieve 
healthy soils by 2050 and make the protection, sustainable use and restoration of soils the 
norm. At the international level, the EU is committed to achieving land degradation 
neutrality by 2030 and restoring degraded land and soil, as underlined in the United Nations 
Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) 151.  
OCI welcomes the Commission’s ambitions to ensure soil protection and reduce soil 
degradation, while guaranteeing food security in Europe and globally . In this regard, 
promoting efficient use of mineral fertili zers via the application of sustainable 
intensification practices represents an effective and valid solution to achieve these 
objectives, ensuring higher crop yields from the same land area, with a reduced impact 
on the climate and environment.  
General remarks 
In the context of the ongoing public consultation on the EU Soil Health Law proposal, OCI 
would like to draw attention to the need for:  
• A harmonised definition of “Soil health” . A common definition of “soil health” is 
needed to facilitate the development and implementation of measures stemming 
from the EU Soil Health Law, and should be shared across the agricultural value chain. 
In this regard, the definition provided by the EU Soil Strategy (“soils are healthy when 
they are in good chemical, biological and physical condition ”) is unclear and needs 
further explanation. OCI endorses the definition proposed by Fertilizers Europe, based 
on studies conducted at international level by the International Fertilizers Association 
(IFA)2.  
• A harmonised, science-based approach tailored to regional contexts . Healthy 
soils play a crucial role in living ecosystems and sustainable food production. 
However, natural and human -induced processes impact the quality of agricultural 
soils, that can result in  agricultural productivity losses. In order to address soil 
degradation more efficiently, it is crucial to develop a harmonised scientific

• Soil fertility protection measures that ensure a sustainable production of crops to

prevent the expansion of  agricultural production on to uncultivated land, and thus 
avoid further loss of biodiversity4 and the release of sequestered carbon5, it is crucial 
to supply existing agricultural land with a sufficient amount of nutrients. The 
importance of healthy soils  for agricultural production  is particularly crucial in the 
current geopolitical situation , where we are confronted with a  serious food security 
threat, also resulting from an abrupt reduction in fertilizer  production, with 
consequences across the whole agri -food supply chain. By applying  sustainable

existing farmland while minimising the environmental  impact on the same land

approach to contribute to the objectives of  the EU Soil Health Law, including

o The use of harmoni sed standards and indicators such as the Nitrogen Use 
Efficiency Indicator, developed by the EU Nitrogen Expert Panel 6, which 
provides information about resource use efficiency. 
o Increasing replacement of conventional mineral fertilizers with Enhanced 
Efficiency Fertilizers (EEF’s) such as those containing urease inhibitors which 
improve fertilizer use efficiency, mitigate climate change, and significantly 
reduce nitrogen losses to the environment 7.

Specific remarks 
Alongside the general remarks presented above, OCI would like to elaborate further on the 
answer to question 17.  
OCI welcomes the target to reduce nutrient losses to the environment by 50%. Achieving this 
goal while maintaining crop yields will require highly effective fertilizers which can deliver 
the largest possible proportion of plant nutrients to agricultural cr ops. The properties of 
modern mineral nitrogen fertilizers mean that farmers can apply precision doses and 
ensure accurate distribution of highly available nutrients to supply specific crop demand. 
Such precision application not only facilitates the supply of the correct balance of essential 
plant nutrients but also takes account of the particular pH and organic matter content of 
the soil. Crucially and in line with the 50% nutrient loss reduction target, avoidable losses to 
the environment can be effective ly reduced through the use of stabilised fertilizers, which 
control ammonia emissions, nitrate leaching and GHG emissions 8. With this combination of 
precision dosage, accurate distribution and the use of stabili sed fertilizers, the result is a 
large incre ase in fertili zer use efficacy, meaning that application rates can be reduced 
without a negative impact on yield, or can provide an increased yield from the same input9,10. 
In conclusion, modern and innovative stabilised fertilizers  are a technology that  enables 
targeted application, support s high sustainable food production and reduce s nutrient 
losses, thus playing a valuable role in achieving the goals set within the Farm to Fork 
strategy. 
Conclusion 
OCI recognises the crucial role of soil in the nutrient, carbon and water cycles and the 
importance of maintaining a high level of soil fertility and health, in order to achieve the EU 
sustainability objectives while guaranteeing food security . In this regard, sustainable 
intensification practices allow  farmers to maintain – or even increase – the agricultural 
output on their  land while reducing nutrient loss es and avoiding the  need to expand 
farmland. Within its work on the EU Health Soil Law, it is therefore crucial that the European 
Commission promotes and supports the uptake of agricultural practices that maintain soil 
health and fertility via efficient use of fertiliz ers, maximising yields while minimising the 
environmental impact on soil, biodiversity and climate change.

Soil health – protecting, sustainably managing and restoring EU soils - Public consultation 
 
ACEA SpA (IT) - Contribute to this consultation 
 
Soil health is strictly dependent on the organic carbon and nutrients content. The latter can be 
increased by good agronomic practices and decreased by wrong ones. The use of organic fertilizer 
is a way to bring carbon and nutrients to the soil in a manner that reduce leaching and run off effects.  
 
According to the definition of the european fertilizer regulation a soil improver  is an EU fertilising 
product the function of which is to maintain, improve or protect the physical or chemical properties, 
the structure or the biological activity of the soil to which it is added.  
 
The positive effects on soil health of soil improvers such as compost and other organic materials are 
widely recognised in literature. 
 
As regards the advantages offered by the use of compost (whether it is carried out in centralized 
plants or in decentralised manner), benefits to be mentioned are:   
 the decrease of GHG emissions;  
 the supply of nutrients to soil;   
 the reduction in use of other fertilizers (minerals, peat, ...); 
 the sequestration of carbon in the soil; 
 the suppression effect of weeds; 
 the reduction in the use of herbicides; 
 the increase in crop yields;  
 the reduction of soil erosion; 
 the increase of the soil moisture content; 
 the improvement of the workability of the soil; 
 the improvement of the biological properties of the soil  
 the improvement of soil biodiversity; 
 the increase of the nutritional quality of crops. 
 
Furthermore, the benefit offered by an organic fertilization become even more important in 
mediterranean regions which are more likely to be affected by desertification. 
 
The strategic role of the use in agriculture of organic fertilizers and other organic materials 
recovered from domestic waste  has become more evident not only as a GHG emission reduction 
measure but also as a response to the fertilizer prices increase due to the Russia and Ukraine conflict 
and to the nutrient and fertilizer supply instability.

For this reason Acea believes that great importance should be done in the soil health directive to all 
circular economy solutions that allow to bring back nutrients and organic carbon to the soil: compost 
and digestate but also other organic matrix derived by sludges from urban wastewater treatment. 
 
Acea deems that the soil health directive could play an important role in encouraging the use of 
those sustainable materials and incentives could be provided to farmers that use compost and other 
organic fertilizing materials for exemple in the form of economic subsidies, white certificates 
emission possibility, tax deductions and others. Economic incentives are needed to boost the 
sustainable use of organic materials in agriculture because still a lot can be done to reach full 
circularity.

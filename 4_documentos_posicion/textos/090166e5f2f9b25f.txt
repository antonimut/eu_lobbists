– Soil Health – 
Protection, sustainable management  and restoration  of  EU 
soils 
 
ICL Group welcomes the opportunity to contribute to the preparation of the Soil Health Law by the 
European Commission .  Soil is the primary medium for agriculture, from planting crops to food 
production, therefore ensuring healthy soils across the European Union  is of paramount importance 
for Europe’s strategic autonomy of food systems.  
 
ICL is closely engaged in the policy debate regarding the issue, we a lso contributed to the call for 
evidence during the first consultation on soil health this year1,  
As a manufacturer of fertilizing products, ICL provides the key nutrients needed for soil and plants to 
grow healthy crops  and ensure soil fertility.  ICL also believes that investing in soil health can be 
profitable if done within the right framework and with appropriate guidance.  
To provide an effective framework for soil health, we call the Commission to consider the following:  
• Differentiate soils uses and focus on agricultural soils 
 
• Provide a positive framework for a sustainable soil management, to maintain and restore soil 
fertility of agricultural soil 
 
• The importance to obtain a harmonized legislation without overlap 
 
1. Differentiate soils uses and focus on agricultural soils 
The initiative aims to protect, maintain and restore soil health and in order to ensure an enabling 
policy framework, we suggest that only agricultural, forest and natural lands are considered within 
the scope. The artificial and built areas, industrial areas and other infrastructures are part of a 
complete different land use discussion, which is also a very important one. But each one deserves 
its own specific rules and this discussion should happen in another arena.  
 
In addition to the scope of the initi ative, ICL does not support the inclusion of the land take 
obligation in the soil h ealth law init iative. ICL acknowledges the importance of addressing the 
challenge of urban and other artificial land development however we believe that this discussion 
should be part of another initiative.  
 
The EU soil health law should focus on building-up the relevant definition of healthy soil linked 
to its  use and the set of indicators adapted to each soil use , and in the case of soils under 
agricultural use, determine how soil fertility can be ensured.  Developing a harmonized 
framework across the EU for agricultural soils , if it is done in a n appropriate way, will  help 
maintaining or restoring soil health and soil fertility in EU soils, supporting the EU food security 
and quality objectives.

2. Provide a positive framework for a sustainable soil management, to maintain and restore soil 
fertility of agricultural soil 
Today, there is no harmonized soil health analysis method in the EU and therefore there are 
significant differences in soil indicators across the Member States. We support the development 
of a set of indicators for soil health for the Agricultural use, flexible enough to be meaningful for 
each local condition . This will provide the necessary toolbox to assess the soil health status of 
agricultural soils . But, having the results of different soil chemical, physical and biological 
characteristics does not mean having a proper evaluation of the soil health status and even less 
an understanding of the objectives. This requires the definition of ideal targets for each indicator. 
For the Agricultural use, the upcoming Soil Health Law should propose different set of indicators 
having the capacity to take into account the specific pedo-climatic conditions, composition, and 
land-use history  of each EU soil . An expert group representing all EU conditions and 
stakeholders could define the indicators for soil health and include soil assessment methods. 
Once the indicators are in plac e, their use sh ould serve as a basis for the developmeent o f 
sustainable soil management strategies  and provide guidance to farmers to adopt measures 
including soil am endment and fe rtilization. In term of measure s, we support the use of 
scientifically based recommendations, like a decision support system allowing a tailored 
approach of the fertilization for each field  (or even more detailed when precision farming tools 
are used) of the farm, while reducing nutrient losses , maintaining or restoring soil fertility and 
improving yields. This approach could be coupled with a mid-term plan to improve soil health and 
be rewarded through payment for ecosystem services.   
In the EU, one of the reasons of the limited investment in soil health is the short-term land leasing. 
Farmers have no incentive to invest in the health of their soils as they don’t see the benefits of 
the inv estment. Encouraging longer term land leases is one way to support their investment. 
Another option is to introduce a standardized assessment that gives value to the investment made 
by farmers. Therefore, we support the development of a soil health certifi cate which could be 
used for land transactions, and which could support payments to farmers renting lands for 
ecosystem services.  
3. A harmonized legislation without overlap 
Considering the complexity of the subject  and the other existing and upcoming initiatives  (Integrated 
Nutrient Management Action Plan, Farm to Fork Strategy, Common Agricultural Policy, etc.) , it is key 
that the future EU Soil Health Law proposal ensures policy coherence and avoids double legislation and 
additional administrative burden.  
In the agricultural domain, the Soil Health Law needs to avoid redundancy, and should prioritize setting 
up the appropriate framework, mechanisms and resources to ensure that soil fertility is maintained or 
restored, while ensuring food security for European Union.

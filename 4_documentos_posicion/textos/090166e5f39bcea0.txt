Position Paper 
On the Commission’s Proposal for the Media Freedom Act 
 
 
ZVEI is strongly aligned with the objectives of the proposed Media Freedom Act (MFA). Protecting media 
freedom and strengthening the EU internal market are crucial cornerstones of a strong and resilient Europe.  
 
The MFA has the potential to strengthen the internal market through a reliable and harmonised legal framework 
across the EU. Fragmentation of the internal market and an inconsistent law enforcement stand against this 
goal and are rightfully addressed by the proposal. However, the measures proposed in the draft need further 
adjustment to tackle the complexity that has arisen from 27 national media laws in the EU and the legal 
uncertainty and additional costs that go along with it.  
 
For creating a stronger internal market and more reliability and predictability in this sector the following points 
are most relevant: 
 
• Ensure harmonisation: European harmonisation of the legal framework is most important. The 
proposed non-binding guidelines on prominence by the Commission can only mitigate the problem but 
will not grant sufficient legal certainty. ZVEI calls for a decent and more reliable legal framework. 
 
• Reduce complexity: Complexity of regulations must be reduced and overlapping regulations must be 
avoided. It must be ensured that the scope of the MFA is consistent with existing EU legislation such as 
the Audiovisual Media Services Directive (AVMSD), the Digital Markets Act, or the Digital Services Act. 
 
• Keep proportionality: The proposed regulations must stay proportionate to their pursued goal. The 
individual measures proposed have to be justified on the basis of a specific regulatory objective, 
otherwise their proportionality cannot be assessed.  
 
• Apply coherent enforcement: A uniform application must be ensured, and enforcement needs to be 
strengthened at the European level. Regardless of how the cooperation between national authorities is 
organised, additional burden by creating overlapping structures must be avoided.

Further Harmonisation 
Guidelines on prominence (Art. 15 para. 2 lit. a) 
 
It is the right approach to aim for more harmonisation of the national regulations on prominence of content of 
general interest. The AVMSD allows Member States to create rules for prominence regulation (Art 7a AVMSD), 
without providing any guardrails. This leaves too much room for interpretation and divergent national solutions, 
which are increasingly fragmenting the internal market. This already led to additional operational burden and 
requires immense efforts to ensure compliance of media services and products with the divergent national rules. 
As Recital 28 of the MFA rightfully points out, it is thus necessary to ensure a consistent regulatory practice 
across Member States. The fact that the MFA explicitly mentions Commission guidelines on prominence (Art. 15 
para. 2 lit. a MFA) is an important step towards a more harmonised legal framework on prominence regulation. 
 
It is necessary that those guidelines also reflect the technical feasibility and effectiveness of EU-wide 
requirements on prominence. They should set boundaries on how prominence regulation should be 
implemented by Member States, ensuring that manufacturers of devices and operators of user interfaces are in 
a position to develop coherent user experiences for the entire EU market, while at the same time respecting that 
the specific content in scope of prominence rules will remain different across Member States. To come to fair 
and appropriate guidelines, it is necessary to set up a transparent stakeholder dialogue with the Commission 
and the new European Board for Media Services. ZVEI will be happy to contribute technical expertise and 
advice to support such a process. 
 
However, these guidelines will always remain non-binding for Member States. They will thus only have limited 
effect on a convergent implementation of the AVMSD. A comprehensive adoption of these guidelines by the 
Member States would be necessary to reduce the market fragmentation caused by the diverging national 
requirements on prominence. It is questionable whether relevant national legislation will be adapted in Member 
States which have already introduced rules on prominence, such as Germany and France. Nonetheless, legal 
certainty is a basic requirement for manufacturers of consumer devices who sell TVs, set-top-boxes, and TV 
sticks on the entire internal market. It is therefore necessary to further strengthen the legal framework to 
safeguard the competitiveness of the internal market. ZVEI calls for a decent and more reliable legal framework 
beyond non-binding guidelines which creates legal certainty on the entire internal market. Specifically, we would 
welcome a binding provision in the MFA that prevents Member States from adopting diverging prominence rules 
that would ultimately force manufacturers to develop different product designs or user interfaces for different 
parts of the internal market. 
 
 
Opening clause for the Member States (Art. 1 para. 3) 
 
Art. 1 para. 3 MFA introduces an opening clause for Member States to adopt more detailed rules in certain of 
the areas addressed by the MFA. To not call into question the intended harmonisation of rules this opening 
clause must be interpreted narrowly and remain limited to the referred sections as well as to the adoption of 
more detailed rules. It must not lead to rules that are different in their level of regulation. Conversely, it is 
important that Art. 1 para. 3 MFA clarifies that the provisions in the sections not referred therein are conclusively 
regulated by MFA and that any deviating regulation by the Member States is prevented. 
 
 
Reduced Complexity  
Cooperation in ‘technical standards’ (Art. 15 para 4) 
 
While it is important to avoid diverging technical standards, a solution for harmonisation should always be 
sought within the existing European and international standardisation bodies. Thus, it remains unclear why 
Art. 15 para. 4 MFA calls for fostering the cooperation between audiovisual media service providers,  regulatory 
authorities, manufactures, and developers on ‘technical standards’. Any new mechanism would only be justified 
in the event of an unlevel “playing field” in the existing European and international harmonised standardisation. 
ZVEI does not observe such shortcomings in European or international standardisation that would require the 
creation of an alternative working group. 
 
Furthermore, the term ‘technical standards’ is misleading, since the usual terms used in technological regulation 
are either ‘harmonised standards’ or ‘technical specification’. In contrast to ‘harmonised standards’, which refers

to agreed standards in European or international standardisation bodies, ‘technical specification’ describes a 
regulation that is issued by the Commission based on an explicit authorisation in the law. The MFA does not 
authorise the Commission to issue technical specifications for digital signals or for the design of devices or of 
user interfaces as mentioned in Art. 15 para. 4 MFA. By using the term ‘technical standards’ it is somehow 
implied that a mixture of both is aimed at, without defining the implications in the proposal.  
 
Quite apart from the misleading wording, it is unclear which regulatory gap this proposal addresses. On the one 
hand, digital signals for the reception of audiovisual media services are already standardised worldwide (DVB 
project). On the other hand, the design of devices or of its user interfaces should not be subject to any kind of 
standardisation. For manufactures, the individual design of devices and user interfaces are key competitive 
factors. Regulatory intervention in the design of devices represents a disproportionate infringement of 
entrepreneurial freedom and restricts innovations. Without being able to demonstrate a regulatory necessity for 
such a serious intervention, this measure cannot be justified and is disproportionate.  
 
 
Proportionality and effective regulation 
Customization of user interfaces (Art. 19) 
 
It is the goal of manufacturers of devices and providers of user interfaces to empower the user to find the media 
offers they want. Users should have the right to choose their media offers based on their personal interest and 
they should have full control over their media consumption. On that basis, personalisation and customization 
options are a key differentiator in the diverse landscape of devices and user interfaces that are available on the 
EU market. We therefore do not see a need to codify user convenience in connection with the purposes of the 
MFA. 
 
In case the provision was to be maintained, the wording of Art. 19 MFA needs clarification. The term “default 
settings controlling or managing access to and use of the audiovisual media services offered” must be defined 
more precisely. It should be limited to settings concerning the sorting and arrangement of audiovisual media 
offers on user interfaces. By contrast, user interface elements primarily serving the operation of the dev ices as 
such (e.g., menu guides) or other elements of the user interface, that are unrelated to the use of the audiovisual 
media services should be clearly excluded from the scope of Art. 19 MFA to avoid divergent interpretations. 
 
It is further questionable how the reference to Art. 7a AVMSD in the last sentence of Art. 19 para. 1 MFA must 
be understood. In any event, it must be clarified that this reference does not indicate that any content that is to 
be made prominent under national prominence rules would have to be excluded from the customization options. 
Otherwise, this provision would even represent a step backwards compared to the status quo. It would then 
restrict the customization options that are available to users. Users should not be limited in their autonomy over 
their media consumption. Media offers which have to be made prominent according to national regulation on the 
basis of Art. 7a AVMSD should not be fixed on user interfaces with no possibility to change. The y should not be 
excluded from the right of the user to configurate its user interface, as arguably proposed in Art. 19 MFA.  
 
Finally, the proposal for compulsory customization of any device and user interface according to Art. 19 lacks 
proper justification. The scope of the Regulation must remain proportionate and its implementation technically 
feasible. On that basis, any customization that would require changes to the hardware is disproportionate and 
must be clearly out of the scope to avoid divergent interpretations. A right to rearrange or sort the media offer on 
user interfaces should only apply insofar as its implementation would be technically feasible and could be 
realised with proportionate effort. 
 
In the context of Art. 19 MFA, it will further be important to properly align the transitional periods in Art.  28 MFA. 
ZVEI welcomes that Art. 28 para. 2 MFA suggests that manufacturers of devices and providers of user 
interfaces will only be obliged to amend their offerings in line with Art. 19 para. 2 MFA by 48 months after the 
MFA enters into force. Any shorter deadline would be disproportionate considering relevant design and 
production cycles. However, this specific transitional period currently only applies to the obligation to fa cilitate 
customization in Art. 19 para. 2 MFA. It does not yet apply to the users’ right to customization in Art. 19 para. 1 
MFA, which would thus apply from 6 months after the MFA’s entry into force. We assume that this was merely 
an editorial oversight. Nonetheless, this should be aligned in the further legislative process to avoid 
contradictory implementation deadlines.

Coherent and uniform enforcement 
More coordination through the European Board for Media 
Services 
 
ZVEI advocates for a coordinated enforcement on European level that leaves no room for divergent and 
excessive implementation. To avoid compliance burden for cross-border services and providers of goods, it is 
necessary to ensure a coherent and uniform application of EU legislation. To achieve this goal, more 
coordination at the EU level is needed.  
 
The European Board for Media Services could play an important role, as proposed in the MFA. Regardless of 
how the cooperation between national media authorities is organised, additional burden by creating overlapping 
structures must be avoided. The competences of the European Board for Media Services must remain clearly 
distinct from the competences of the national authorities.

ZVEI: Electro and Digital Industry Association 
 
The ZVEI represents the common interests of the electrical and digital industry and the associated service 
companies in Germany and at international level. The association has more than 1,100 member companies, 
and 170 employees work in the ZVEI Group. 
 
The industry employs around 882,000 workers in Germany (as of June 2022). In 2021, its turnover was around 
200 billion euros. 
 
The electrical and digital industry is one of the most innovative economic sectors in Germany. Almost a quarter 
of the industry's turnover is accounted for by product innovations. Every third innovation in the manufacturing 
industry as a whole gets its original impetus here. Almost a quarter of all R&D expenditure in the manufacturing 
sector in Germany comes from the electrical and digital industry. Every year, the sector spends around 20 billion 
euros on R&D and more than seven billion euros on investments.

Eramet contribution to the future European Critical Raw Materials Act 
November 2022   
Eramet, a European mining and metallurgical group, is a key player in the extraction and refining of base 
metals necessary for the overall growth of our economy (manganese, mineral sands) and those critical for 
the transition to a climate neutral economy (nickel, lithium, cobalt). 
As a European company and currently chairman of the European Raw Materials Alliance (ERMA) Battery task 
force, we are working towards becoming a major contributor to fulfil the European demand in base metals 
by 2030.  We are developing activities with high growth potential, from our own mines of Lithium in 
Argentina and Nickel/Cobalt in Indonesia and New Caledonia to metallurgical transformation activities in 
Europe, combined with batteries recycling development projects, wit h the aim to become a one-stop 
shopping place for raw materials supplying the European electrical vehicles (EV) value chain.  
▪ The Group intends to become a partner of choice for the European battery industry, through 
responsible primary metals extraction and batteries recycling activities. 
▪ The Group supports the transition to a carbon neutral economy  by developing activities 
strategic for Europe to sustain its leadership and autonomy in this transition.  
 
Eramet welcomes the European Commission (EC) initiative to propose a Critical Raw Materials Act. Covid 
and Ukrainian crisis have highlighted the necessity for the European Union (EU) to secure its supply of CRM, 
particularly nickel, cobalt, and lithium because they are necessary to achieve the goal to be climate neutral 
by 2050. 
 
Meanwhile, the US Inflation Reduction Act is a game changer in industrial policy. It has unleashed a wave 
of domestic investment announcements in just a few months. The risk for Europe is that companies will now 
prioritise the US market over Europe’s : the Critical Raw Materials Act should support resilient and 
sustainable metals supply for the European Green Deal. Europe should also consider its own version of the 
“US IRA”, i.e. add local content requirements to the millions of euros of electric vehicle subsidies that EU 
governments provide each year. 
 
As the demand for those base metals in the coming years is going to explode and the risk of EU dependencies 
on external resources is real, this new regulation must support the urgent need to secure and develop our 
supply in critical raw materials.  
Eramet will take actively part in the upcoming discussions, as they will contribute to assure a green and clean 
future for the EU.  
 
 
 
1. To endorse a responsible mining standard to favor European mining production  
 
The more efficient way to support the  level playing  field required for being competitive against non -
European mining players is the development and the implantation within the EU of a set of responsible 
mining standards. Only a compelling regulation applied to any domestic production but also to any import 
of raw materials on our single market can create the adequate level playing field , to ensure that our 
competitiveness is not jeopardized by our best-in class CSR standards.  
 
But aligning with strong CSR standards, that European companies  are self-adopting1, is currently not 
recognized by the European regulator as a prerequisite to access to the EU market and we deeply regret it.

This lack of consensus about an appropriate ESG framework  puts European offers in a situation of unfair 
competition with other players who do not respect our rules, to the detriment of environmental protection 
and human rights. 
 
> Eramet recalls that adopting a responsible mining certification at a European level is a 
recommendation that was made in Philippe Varin's January 2022 report , on securing the supply of 
mineral raw materials. 
 
> Eramet ask EC to endorse a n European responsible mining standard, based on highest standards 
already available in the world, such as  the Initiative for Responsible Mining Assurance  (IRMA) 
and/or a methodology to certify alternative but equivalent standards  (with an  European 
certification body by instance, to recognize the conformity of CSR standards and/or due diligence 
mechanisms applied by suppliers of minerals raw materials). 
 
> We urge to benchmark existing ESG performance standards and not to create a new one.  
 
Only a duly recognised and harmonised European standard can ensure sustainable extraction, use, 
transformation and disposal of resources. European regulations and standa rds should prove to the end -
users and citizens that doing mining & metals sustainable is possible, as proven by European companies 
in their daily operations.  
 
2. To develop European dedicated financial tools to support upstream industries  
The development of mines and first transformation metallurgical activities is highly capital intensive. For 
some critical metals such as nickel, a single project can account for more than two billion euros. And on 
markets which are highly volatile, as it is the case for  nickel, only China is currently investing massively in 
new greenfield projects : in many countries, and one can refer to the recent US policy updates, there is now 
a true understanding that the most strategic value chains such as the e-mobility will be developed in a timely 
manner only with a State financial support.  
European mining projects are suffering from a lack of debt financing,  but more importantly are weakened 
by the struggle to attract equity investors at the appropriate level. Many financing tools do exist in Europe 
but only a strong political incentive will route this financial liquidity to the mining projects already 
identified by European operators.  
> State-owned national and European financial institutions must be in capacity to grant  debt 
financing and take equity participation in the mining projects outside Europe. The credit policy of 
these institutions (the first one being EIB) must be adapted to the specificities of the sector, 
especially to undertake the country risk that is inherent to most of these projects.  
 
> A dedicated Raw Materials public/private investment fund should be created  to fill-in the gap of 
existing financial tools and ensure that all tranches of risks are undertaken by appropriate 
lenders/risk-takers.  
 
> Such a financial support shall also be provided to the scaling-up of industrial projects that have been 
supported by Europe through initial stages of development of R&D and innovation.  
 
 
3. To secure international strategic partnerships with regions best equipped to deliver short -term 
supply of strategic minerals  
 
Europe will remain highly dependent over the short term on import of critical raw materials. Pressure on 
resources will increase and Europe is in danger in losing access to the  resources it needs  for supporting 
industries related to our transition to a low carbon economy. We need to secure mining deposits overseas,

because many countries in the world are working to take a global leadership by capturing natural resources 
used by these industries , but also  because with growing resource production, it becomes increasingly 
important to address issues related to sustainability of mining operations and achieving that goal 
undoubtedly requires controlling and operating these upstream businesses. 
 
> Europe shall include strong raw materials chapter in all EU Free Trade Agreements and develop 
strategic partnerships with countries that have the capacity to deliver important volumes of critical 
raw materials over the coming 3 to 5 years. Strategic partnerships shall be developed firstly with 
Latin American countries for Lithium and Indonesia for Nickel;  African countries shall also be 
prioritized but only when a pipeline of projects has already been identified by European private 
companies. 
 
> Eramet calls for having  chapters on environmental and social guarantees included in these 
partnerships, to ensure that any European investment realized in third countries, or any supply 
agreement developed un der the European Commission umbrella , is realized with in the right ESG 
framework. It is the only way to differentiate European offer from those of competitors. 
 
4. To make mining and metallurgical activities eligible and aligned to taxonomy  
 
Eramet estimates that the Critical Raw Materials Act shall be articulated in a way that gives consistency 
to all upcoming regulations, including the Battery regulation,  the revision of the waste legislation,  the 
CSRD and CSDDD proposals and taxonomy.  
 
Activities of extraction and transformation of raw materials which are necessary for our energy transition 
should be eligible and aligned to taxonomy, as they should be considered as enabling activities in the sense 
of art16 of Regulation 2020/852 due to the downstream outlets.   
 
These are sectors in which practices are very diverse globally and where European players are positioned as 
best-in-class. These differentiating factors must be objectively assessed and recognized to establish the 
adequate level playing field.  Taxonomy should favor the most environmentally responsible extractive 
operations, and encourage other sites and projects, both European and non -European, to improve their 
performance through a continuous improvement approach.  We call for the inclusion of non -EU activities 
performed by EU companies. 
 
> Eramet calls the European Commission to confirm the alignment of the extractive industries (NACE 
codes 07, 08, 09) and primary processing stages activities (NACE code 24.4) with the Taxonomy 
objectives. It is essential to avoid an extremely negative signal sent to financial institutions that will 
jeopardize our developments.  
> As a technical work within the Sustainable platform is ongoing, Eramet asks for a political support 
for our activities to be included in the next taxonomy Delegated Acts expected by 2023.

EU Critical Raw Materials Act 
Response to Call for Evidence – November 2022  
Executive Summary 
Raw materials are a necessity for achieving a climate neutral future and reaching the EU’s 
climate targets. Northvolt therefore welcomes the European Commission’s initiative on a 
Critical Raw Materials Act. Overall, Northvolt wants to highlight three core objectives that 
should be included in the act to address constraints most efficiently in the supply of critical raw 
materials for the battery industry:  
1) De-risking and de-stigmatizing responsible investment in high-quality, strategically, and 
economically viable projects that otherwise suffer from a lack of access to viable financing 
opportunities.  
2) Developing and expanding the coverage of Free Trade Agreements and trade/investment 
protocols with resource-rich countries in which extant high-quality reserves, potential high-
quality reserves, and efficient refining capacity of critical raw materials exist. Particular 
focus on third countries including (but not limited to) the following : Australia, Chile, DRC, 
Madagascar and Bolivia. 
3) Strengthening recycling capacity by ensuring that existing and future EU regulatory 
mechanisms directly benefit, or do not negatively impact supplies of critical raw materials. 
Especially the export of black mass, end-of-life batteries and manufacturing scrap should 
be limited to keep valuable recycling resources available to the European market.  
Strengthening the EU’s critical raw materials value chain 
Identifying strategic CRMs  
Northvolt supports the Commission’s approach of identifying critical raw materials via its list of 
Critical Raw Materials (CRM). To ensure Europe’s strategic independence and the market 
competitiveness of the European battery industry, nickel and manganese are essential 
additions to the existing list as those metals are core elements of a lithium -ion-battery. In 
addition, to steer future policy making for the European battery ecosystem, a similar list on 
intermediate battery material products including black mass should be developed. 
Strengthening waste and circularity framework  
To reduce the reliance of virgin raw materials, we need to promote the circularity framework 
through material-efficient recycling of CRMs. Circularity implies that valuable recycling 
resources, such as end-of-life batteries, battery waste, black mass and manufacturing scrap 
must remain available for the European recycling and battery materials industry. This is of 
particular importance for black mass, which is the intermediate product of recycled batteries, 
containing nickel, manganese, cobalt and lithium, and can be re-fed into the production process

Securing diversified access to CRMs  
In the more immediate timeframe, as recyclable battery volumes are not sufficient yet to fully 
replace the use of virgin raw materials, primary raw materials extraction and refining into 
battery-grade products will be necessary for battery production.  
Here, we need to see Europe’s geology for what it is. There is potential for the development of 
new commercial operations based around known resources, and exploration of new reserves. 
However, to meet European long-term environmental, economic, and strategic objectives, 
Europe’s supply of known high-quality resources is inadequate.  
The levels of environmental impact in raw materials projects depend on the grade and quality 
of the ore body, the flow sheet, the jurisdiction, energy mix, and availability of chemicals, among 
other factors. European projects are generally of lower grade, contain higher impurities, and are 
subject to considerably higher energy and capital intensity than resources abroad ultimately 
resulting in suboptimal CO2 profiles and economic impracticality. 
Therefore, any expansion of refining capacity must necessarily depend on the import of 
intermediate products from resource-rich third countries. For any further refining and ultimately 
production capacity in Europe to be realized – and for this capacity to be fed by materials of 
the highest quality, environmental profile, and ethical standard – supplies from these third 
countries must be secured, and incentives for development of additional high -quality third 
country capacity must be provided.  
Ensuring better access to funding  
In many cases, projects with high quality potential are developed by actors with the backing of 
major strategic regions, operated at a substantially lower environmental and social standards, 
and ultimately still feed value chains that provide intermediate or finished products at attractive 
prices to European producers. This harms European strategic autonomy  and inhibits the 
application of European standards along the value chain. 
As a result, the critical raw material supply landscape in Europe could substantially benefit from 
policies to de -risk direct European investment in strategically viable, competitive, and 
responsibly developed projects that otherwise suffer from a lack of access to viable financing 
opportunities.

This might be done via tools such as grants, equity and project finance for high -quality mining 
and refining projects both in the EU and in resource-rich third countries and for recycling projects 
in Europe.  
De-stigmatize mining industry  
Additionally, initiatives should be funded to raise public awareness of the necessity of critical raw 
materials extraction for achievement of European environ mental, economic, and strategic aims, 
de-stigmatize the industry, and broaden public support for critical raw materials policy. This 
could incorporate a forward -looking concept for “Mining 2.0”, or responsible mining, to contain 
other aspects of sustainabi lity than just carbon footprint, and eventually create 
mechanisms/schemes for affected communities (e.g. education programs, nature preservation, 
etc.). Mining is an essential component of a net zero future, and this message must be 
communicated effectively.  
Ensuring a sustainable level playing field across the Single 
Market 
Northvolt is a firm believer in critical raw material supplies needing to improve their ESG profile, 
and therefore supports safeguarding minimum ESG standards for the import of critica l raw 
materials into the EU. Imported raw materials need to adhere to the same ESG standards that 
are imposed in the EU.  
Strengthening the EU external actions 
Negotiation and implementation of Free Trade Agreements or other potential trade protocols 
(such as strategic partnerships) with resource -rich third countries will be an essential part of 
solving critical raw material supply constraints, and ensuring Europe meets its environmental, 
economic, and strategic objectives.  For the battery industry, pr imary focus countries and the 
critical resources on which such an agreement would have an impact are listed below:  
Tier 1  
- Australia (Nickel, Lithium) 
- Chile (Lithium) 
- Madagascar (Cobalt) 
- DRC (Cobalt) 
- Bolivia (Lithium) 
Tier 2  
- Gabon (Manganese) 
- Philippines (Nickel) 
- Argentina (Lithium) 
- South Africa (Manganese) 
Tariff suspensions should be granted in full and for the normal period of time for battery product 
imports when there is no EU supply, currently these are granted at 50% for one year at a time. 
Additionally, availability and comprehensiveness of trade statistics / data is inconsistent among 
states. Development of a forum or mechanism for sharing of this data would yield a variety of 
benefits, for example in facilitating the bankability of potential projects.

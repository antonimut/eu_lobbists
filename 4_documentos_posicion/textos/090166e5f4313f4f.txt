Novelis: Critical Raw Materials Act 
 
Novelis welcomes the European Commission’s announcement to propose a European Critical Raw 
Materials Act and the intention to include policy measures specifically focused on boosting the EU’s 
circularity of critical and strategic materials alike.  
In building EU strategic autonomy, w e call on the European Commission to reco gnise the strategic 
value of aluminium and the untapped potential of circularity to boost its supply. As a permanent 
material, aluminium is  infinitely recyclable and reusable. Aluminium is also strategic for the dual 
green and digital transition.    
For exampl e, the recent  Metals for Clean Energy  Report (attached) sets out that to reach the EU’s 
Green Deal goals in 2050, Europe needs 30% more aluminium to manufacture clean technologies such 
as electric vehicles, batteries and wind turbines; at the same time the most recent  Circularity Gap 
Report (attached) highlighted that global circularity has actually decreased in recent years (from 9,1% 
in 2018 to 8,6% in 2020), as did the EU’s self-sufficiency for aluminium.  
The European Critical Raw Materials Act is therefore an opportunity to close this circularity gap and 
to develop the secondary raw materials market. This in turn will  boost EU open strategic autonomy 
and resilience  as well as reduce energy intensity of the sector (recycled alumin ium uses 95% less 
energy than virgin). 
Novelis welcomes the principle of establishing objective p re-set cri teria for determining strategic 
materials. These criteria should as a priority promote secondary raw material s. Recycled aluminium 
should be increasingly promoted as a main source for new aluminium products  given the climate, 
energy, and biodiversity benefits.  
First, the general classification of raw materials should be improved to better capture strategic goals, 
recognizing materials as strategic by taking into consideration at least the following criteria: 
1. Identify materials that are critical to the twin transition 
2. Identify materials that are necessary for societally critical sectors (energy, mobility, food) 
3. The materials’ circularity and permanency; including the ability to recycle a material 
domestically and transform it into pre -material for further high-quality value-added products 
reduces import-dependency 
The above criteria and those being considered by the European Commission clearly bring forward 
aluminium as a strategic (secondary) raw material:  
1. The EU energy transition will lead to a 30% increase in aluminium demand by 20401 
2. Aluminium is important for the food, transport, pharmaceuticals, and mobility sector 
3. European recycling facilities for aluminium are critical assets that support a circular and closed 
loop aluminium sector, reducing net carbon emissions and mitigating against supply shortages 
and lowering import dependency

4. Secondary aluminium offers a signif icant (95%) reduction in the level of energy needed for 
primary production of aluminium ( where electricity costs account for approx. 40% of entire 
production costs ) and importantly reduce the level of dependency upon constant baseload 
energy supplies.  
Finally, the material-efficient recycling of CRM, whether in the form of secondary raw materials, or 
strategic raw materials , should be transformed and improved to create an ecosystem that allows 
proper market conditions for developing a closed-loop circular economy. This ecosystem should allow 
valuable materials to remain in circulation within the European economy, while supporting the full 
recycling value chain. 
This should as a priority  include deployment incentives to significantly scal e up infrastructure to 
strengthen the collection, sorting, and pre-treatment of raw materials, as well as ensuring a growth of 
reverse-logistics. These measures cou ld include incentive schemes to stimulate investments in 
recovery- and separation technologies for CRMs. 
Further e nhancing the circularity potential of materials such as aluminium will boost the circular 
economy, while reducing carbon emissions, energy intensity and dependencies on third nations.

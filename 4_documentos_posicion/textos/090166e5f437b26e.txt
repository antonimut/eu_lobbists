Background and the importance of protecting drinking water sources
The European Commission (EC) has published legislative proposals to revise the lists of pollutants for 
groundwater and surface water in the Groundwater Directive (GWR) Environmental Quality Standards 
Directive (EQSD), subsidiary directives of the Water Framework Directive (WFD). The EC proposes to 
include new medicine residues (endocrine disruptors, analgesics and antibiotics), pesticides and a 
selection of PFAS substances in these priority substances lists. 
Medicine residues, pesticides and substances released from industrial discharges (including PFAS) are a 
major risk to drinking water supply sources. Drinking water companies monitor hundreds of these 
substances at their drinking water sources and incur high monitoring and treatment costs. Inclusion of 
the mentioned substance groups in the priority substances lists, including the corresponding standards, 
is therefore of utter importance. The lists provide tools to reduce emissions of these substances to the 
environment and drinking water sources . Member states are obliged to take action to curb emissions if 
priority substance standards are exceeded. This is in line with the European precautionary principle and 
the source-control approach. 
Support the proposal to include medicine residues, pesticides and PFAS substances in the 
revision of the lists of pollutants for ground and surface water in the Groundwater Directive 
(GWR) and the WFD Priority Substances Directive;

PFAS
For PFAS, the WFD, EQSD and GWD are valuable tools to reduce emissions to the environment, but from 
the European precautionary principle, it would have been better to assess and completely ban this 
substance group when it was introduced on the European market (REACH). PFAS are  now found in food, 
air, dust and (drinking) water. Vewin and the water boards therefore advocate a total European ban.
Seek an European total ban on the production, use and emission of PFAS as soon as possible;

Additional (European) commitment to WFD targets is necessary to protect drinking water 
sources 
In its Environmental Implementation Review (EIR) in September 2022, the European Commission 
assessed the implementation of environmental legislation by member states. The report on the 
Netherlands concluded that the WFD water quality targets are still far out of sight.  Additional efforts to 
reduce emissions are therefore required. The current proposal to focus extra on medicine residues, 
pesticides and PFAS, and the proposal to attach strict standards to them, will bring the European WFD 
targets closer in sight. Because of the integrated river basin approach and the cross-border nature of 
both the Rhine and the Meuse (both sources of drinking water), Vewin supports the proposal to set 
standards at European level (Annex 1) .
Support the proposal to include standards at European level (Annex 1); 
Implementation and reflection in EU legislation is essential for the quality of water
To have a real impact on water quality, the priority substances lists must be implemented in EU and 
national legislation on industrial emissions, domestic wastewater, plant protection products (both 
admission and use) and chemicals (REACH). To track whether these substances are adequate 
implemented in the various regulations and contribute to the overall goal of less pollution and cleaner 
water, , Vewin advocates for 3-yearly progress reporting and monitoring on the implementation of the 
priority substances lists in connection to other EU and National legislation. 
Ensure that these substance lists feed into the Industrial Emissions Directive, the Urban Waste 
Water Directive, the authorisation of substances on the European market (REACH) and the 
authorisation and application of pesticides; 
Monitor and report 3 yearly on the implementation progress and results of priority substances 
lists.

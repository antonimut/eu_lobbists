REC Solar Norway feedback Open Public Consultation on   
European Critical Raw Materials Act  
  
  
To The European Commission  
  
REC Solar Norway appreciates the opportunity to provide feedback on the European Commission’s public 
consultation on the European Raw Materials Act. We strongly support policies focused on promoting 
European autonomy and sustainable raw materials. Yet, we also would like to take the opportunity to 
explain the importance of covering solar grade silicon in the forthcoming work.  
  
In the transition from fossil to renewable energy, solar energy will play a major role. Installation of solar 
panels is one of the faster ways of converting to renewable energy. Due to the PV Solar Strategy 1 and the 
ambitious installation plans for Europe, the demand for raw materials for solar panels is growing fast 
from an already high level. At the same time, there are ongoing discussions on how to bring the PV value 
chain back to Europe.  
  
At the start of the 21st century, China made a strategic decision to take the role as a major player in the 
PV industry. To achieve this, China subsidizes its industry by e.g., cheap/free land, subsidized loan rates, 
utility subsidies on electricity and water and relaxed labor laws. China has invested over USD 50 billion in 
new PV supply capacity – ten times more than Europe − and created more than 300 000 manufacturing 
jobs across the solar PV value chain since 20112 . The resulting fast growth in China caused the previously 
thriving European PV industry to wind down, and by now China completely dominates global PV 
manufacturing.   
  
More than 90 percent of solar panels are made from silicon, and silicon is defined by the EU as a 
strategic raw material 3. There are only a few manufacturers of solar grade silicon left in Europe, and they 
are relatively small.  Around 80 percent of global solar grade silicon production is Chinese2, with Xinjiang 
province accounting for close to half of global polysilicon production. Xinjiang solar grade silicon is mainly 
made using coal-based electricity, and there is evidence that many producers use forced labour4.    
  
With more than 100 years of metallurgical industry history, Norway has a significant share of global 
metallurgical silicon production. Elkem, the largest Norwegian silicon producer, invested over twenty 
years and hundreds of man-years in the development of a metallurgical purification process for solar 
grade silicon, leading to the industrialization of the new process by Elkem Solar (now REC Solar Norway) 
in 2009. Originally, the starting point for this process was an adapted electric arc reduction process of 
quartz with carbon, followed by proprietary purification steps.   
  
Currently, the major waste issue from production of solar panels is silicon waste (fines) from wafer 
production. As much as 30% of the refined silicon is cut away in this process, and around 250 000 tons 
are generated every year (growing with increased production). In 2016, Elkem Solar made a 
breakthrough in using the silicon waste from wafer slicing as a raw material and developed and 
industrialized this circular process over the following years. In mid-2022, REC Solar Norway had 
converted its production to using recycled fines as 100% of the raw material. Recycling wafer slicing fines 
back to solar grade purity has been a goal of much academic and industrial research for a long time, but 
REC Solar remains the only company that has succeeded in industrializing such a process.

 In addition to providing a technical solution to a large and growing industrial waste problem, the new 
process further reduces the already industry leading low energy consumption of the Elkem Solar process 
(now around 85% lower than typical industry values) and due to the low carbon energy mix of Norway, 
the embedded climate gas emissions are more than 90% lower than major Chinese competitors.  
  
There is a target to reach 100 GW of PV production capacity in Europe by 20305. In order to support this 
with domestically produced solar grade silicon, an annual capacity of about 200,000 metric tons is 
needed. If all wafering takes place in Europe, this will generate roughly 60,000 metric tons of fines that 
can potentially be upgraded by the REC Solar process, with significantly lower total climate gas emissions 
and no silicon fines going to waste as a result. 
  
The number of solar panels reaching the end of their service life will increase in the years to come, and in 
10-15 years from now, waste from these panels will potentially be a large headache to the industry. 
Together with other European players we have started to look at the possibilities of recycling End-of-life 
panels back to high value raw materials.  
  
The conversion to circular production within the field of renewable energy is demanding and has required 
continuous investments. In 2022 the new production line of REC Solar Norway was finally ramped up, but 
operation cost has been high due to extreme power prices. For the last few months, there has been a 
temporary production stop. Sustainable and predictable electricity cost is a main factor for the factory to 
restart production.  
 
Recommendations from REC Solar Norway on needed actions to expand production of solar grade silicon 
and other critical raw materials in Europe: 
 Predictable electricity cost at an affordable level 
 The state aid regulations have to be adjusted, so that policies supporting local production can be 
put in place 
 Regulations regarding social standards and environmental impact of the manufacturing steps  
 Green Public Procurement regimes and offtake agreements  
 Border adjustment tax

• Objectives of the CRM List 2.0: Focus on substitution only when most rational, for example in 
case of mined natural graphite vs. synthetic graphite. As currently being on the CRM List is linked 
to increased focus on substitution, some producers of materials are reluctant to support their material 
being included. Thus, the List does not create enough buy-in from producers. We believe that 
substitution should be an objective only in specific, limited cases, for example in case of synthetic 
graphite as a replacement for natural, mined graphite. In such cases, substitution materials should be 
able to benefit from the same support schemes.  
• Principles of designing the CRM Act. Hydro proposes that the CRM Act be drafted with the following 
principles in mind: 
1. Focus on both new and existing operations. In the discussion currently taking place, we are 
observing bias towards new investments. For an effective functioning of raw materials 
producers in Europe, public policy needs to also address framework conditions for existing 
production facilities, otherwise no new investment will thrive in Europe.  Take aluminium as an 
example. Bauxite, the ore of aluminium, is on the 2020 CRM List. Policy intervention could 
theoretically focus on new investments in bauxite mining but when we see 50% of EU 
aluminium smelting capacity curtailed because of the energy crisis, such intervention would 
have no strategic nor economic rationale.  
2. Focus on the whole value chain, not only mining. Similar to point 1 above, focus of the CRM 
Act cannot be only on mining. We have seen before that the metals/raw materials ecosystem 
functions in an optimal way when all segments of the value chain are present in Europe. When 
one element falls, it causes a domino effect. We therefore encourage the European Commission 
to design the Act also with the needs of refining, smelting, manufacturing and recycling in 
mind. Please note that, for example, it is expected that in 2050 more than 50% of the demand 
for aluminium will be satisfied by recycling.  
3. Offensive and defensive interests. Again, similar to the above, in the public consultation 
questionnaire, we have noticed bias towards offensive commercial interests of the EU. Some 
options proposed are to further develop raw materials chapters in free trade agreements and 
establishing partnerships with third countries. Hydro supports these proposals. At the same 
time, we would like to underline that in parallel to advancing the offensive interests of the EU, 
Hence, we 
would like to encourage the European Commission to include wording in the Communication 
on the use of trade defense instruments when justified. 
4. Holistic policy approach: Energy, sustainability, international trade, innovation. An 
industry can only thrive in Europe if framework conditions in overall are not less favorable in 
comparison to the framework conditions outside Europe. These conditions cover a whole 
plethora of topics, and there is a need for the European Commission to propose measures/ 
actions/ new approaches to the European energy, sustainability, trade and innovation policies 
needed for a level playing field for European industry, which should be all aimed at 
strengthening raw materials production in Europe. 
5. Have Norway in mind as an EEA member and an important supplier of raw materials to 
Europe. Norway is a member of the European Economic Area, an important supplier of raw 
materials to the EU and a like-minded country. It is also currently a testing ground for many 
policies foreseen in Europe, for example the incentivized uptake of electric vehic les and early 
prohibition of sales of internal combustion engine cars. Consequently, it is economically 
rational to keep Norway in mind when designing the CRM Act.

o More clarity on public funding opportunities needed. At the moment when a company is 
considering a new investment, it is very difficult to obtain a full picture of available public 
funding support at both national and EU level. There is often no clarity on available funding per 
project, eligibility, state aid intensity, or the information is dispersed among various so urces. 
While this challenge is relevant also to other sectors, it would be of help to European raw 
materials producers if the EU established an easy-to-navigate database on public funding.  
o Funding mechanisms: Be inspired by the U.S. Inflation Reduction Act. Hydro believes that 
state aid mechanisms in the EU could benefit from being inspired by the IRA mechanisms. IRA 
support distribution is much more mechanical and incentive-based than EU state aid, which is 
very uncertain till the moment of obtaining final approval.  
• Symbolic stretch targets: 25% mining, 50% refining/smelting, 75% recycling. To strengthen the 
weight of the CRM Act, Hydro believes that the European Commission should propose an ambitious 
target for raw materials supply in Europe. While exact targets would need to be fixed at individual 
material or even application level, for communication purposes, we would propose a headline target of 
25% mining, 50% refining/smelting, 75% recycling

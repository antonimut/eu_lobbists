EU Critical Raw Materials Act 
 
Jastrzębska Spółka Węglowa (JSW) would like to express its support for the EU CRM initiative. JSW is 
a major producer of coking coal - a key steel-making ingredient and one of the critical raw materials 
(CRMs). As one of the leaders of European extractive industry we believe that without a strong 
domestic raw materials industry the transition of European economy will result in significant increase 
of dependence on third country suppliers. Given that investments in extractive sector are long -term 
and highly capital-intensive, coherent and predictable regulatory framework are crucial for investors.  
Coking coal is currently critical for steel production. However, its by products are strategic for battery 
production – needle and metallurgical coke a s well as  High Temperature Coal Tar Pitch  are used in 
synthetic graphite production (a substitute of natur al graphite and one of materials used for Li -ion 
battery production). Besides being used for batteries synthetic graphite is used in electrodes for 
electric arc furnace (EAF). The example of coking coal and its derivates  shows how complex the issue 
of crit ical raw materials is and that each critical raw material has a wide range of applications in 
strategic sectors. Further on, the intensification of research on mining waste generated in the process 
of extracting coking coal currently results in promising data on the possibility of recovering such raw 
materials as: lithium, REE from this waste (a number of data in this regard were presented during the 
RM Week 2022 conference in Brussels).  
Therefore, JSW welcomes the Commission’s initiative to create a comprehensive framework dedicated 
to critical raw materials and will gladly contribute to the public consultation. Without further action on 
the EU -level to address the challenges of extractive industry , the  EU will continue to grow it’s 
dependency on third country suppliers while phasing out the domestic production.  
 
Key issues: 
1. Defining priorities and objectives for EU actions: CRMs and strategic critical raw materials 
The aim of creating the CRM list was to identify raw materials economically and strategically 
important for the European based on the criticality assessment methodology.  The existing list is 
subject to revision every three years and, as it’s based on a known and prov en methodology, it 
provides clarity for industry and investors. Therefore, we don’t see any obvious reasons or need to 
create alternative list , unless transparent procedures apply . Such decision might also cause 
additional uncertainty and confusion for investors.  
More details regarding this initiative would be necessary, including: 
➢ Who and how will set the criteria and the overall methodology to determine the list of 
strategic CRMs? 
➢ Will the EC ensure that both lists are of equally important? 
➢ How often will the strategic list be revised/updated? 
Above all we believe that all CRMs are considered strategic for the EU and every project aimed at 
CRM production should be a priority. Moreover, we expect the regulation to provide equal funding 
opportunities and incentives for all CRM related projects.

2. Strengthening the EU’s critical raw materials value chain (mining, refining, processing, 
recycling): strategic projects & permitting 
Investments in mining need a stable and predictable regulatory framework. CRM projects should 
be assessed based on transparent procedures to ensure predictability for both industry and the 
financial institutions, as all are of critical importance for the industry. Especially taking into account 
current growing dependence on raw materials imports, each new domestic CRM project should be 
recognized as strategic. Moreover, as the extractive industry is a highly capital-intensive sector with 
a long -term invest ments duration, adequate funding opportunities to attract investors and 
accelerate new projects shall be ensured. Another important issue is the lack of public acceptance 
for mining projects in Europe, which translates into lack of skilled workforce. Educational campaigns 
shall present mining as a sustainable, innovative , digital and highly automated industry. Another 
important issue are the existing permitting procedures which differs between Member States, are 
lengthy, complex and unpredictable and this should be addressed by the EU CRM Act . Projects 
aimed at increasing the production of CRMs should have prioritized access to funding and be subject 
to common fast-track permitting procedure.  
3. Ensuring a sustainable level playing field across the Single Market 
To ensure competitive position for EU-based CRMs production European Commission shall consider 
introducing a n Important Project of Common European Interest for CRMs . To identify ex isting 
barriers within the EU regulatory framework, the Commission should perform a thorough test 
involving stakeholders from mining industry.  
Conclusions: 
JSW welcomes the CRM Act and believes that in order to r eestablish mining, EU needs to  focus on 
develop smart mining  (using digital and autonomous solutions), anticipate risks, strengthen value 
chains and change the overall perception of the industry. According to estimates the biggest challenge 
for the  development of zero-emission steel production will be significant demand for e nergy from 
renewable sources, both for the production of hydrogen (to ultimately replace metallurgical coke) and 
for the production of steel in the EAF technology. In this context, the role of the BF technology and 
coking coal is and will still be significant at least until the clean steel production will be able to meet 
the expected demand for steel. Thus, coking coal will play an important role in transformation in the 
upcoming years.  
The new initiative should provide predictable framework necessary to accelerate investments, as well 
as propose measures to partly take risk of large -scale investment projects. Main objective of this 
proposal should be  to support domestic production and sourcing of CRMs , which will be impossible 
without a level playing field for European industry, which unlike producers from third-countries, must 
meet strict requirements set by European regulations including ESG standards. Considering the above 
JSW reiterates the importance of taking urgent action to ensure stable and reliable supply of CRMs for 
the European economy and twin transition in the years to come, and urges for a comprehensive and 
coherent proposal for all the Critical Raw Materials.

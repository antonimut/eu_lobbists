Feedback to the call for evidence: the European Critical Raw 
Materials Act  
 
November 2022 
 
 
 
Introduction  
 
Rare Earths Norway strongly support s the European Commission’s announcement to propose a 
European Critical Raw Materials Act  (CRM Act). Today, approximately 98% of Europe’s demand for 
Rare Earth Elements ( REE) is supplied by non -EU/EEA countries, and we would like to highlight that 
there is significant potential for REE mining in Europe today. Norway may have continental Europe’s 
largest REE deposit in the Fen Complex, a mineral deposit situated 110 kilometres southwest of Oslo 
in Nome Municipality in Telemark County.1 Early estimates suggest that the Fen Complex could supply 
up to 30% of Europ e’s LREE demand in 2030. 2 The deposit contains substantial amounts of 
neodymium and praseodymium, which are rare earth elements used in permanent magnets. The Fen 
Complex is well situated with access to existing infrastructure, renewable electricity supply, industrial 
processing facilities on Herøya, and sea -based transport to Europe. The development of the Fen 
Complex could form the basis for a compact and complete European value chain for mine -to-
magnets.  
 
In this paper, we lay out our recommendations to EU policy makers for addressing what we perceive as 
the main challenges to advance raw materials projects in Europe today, namely:  
 
1) Exploration and feasibility studies are  expensive and time -consuming, with no real available  
access to de-risk financing; 
2) Permitting processes are slow and unpredictable, hence adding additional risk to the projects; 
3) There is a skills gap in Europe today when it comes to REE mining and refining; 
4) Unfair competition could jeopardize the long-term financial viability of European projects.  
 
Access to finance in early-stage 
 
Existing EU funding mechanisms (e.g., Horizon Europe, the European Innovation Fund) fail to address 
the need for de-risk finance in the early stage of strategic mining projects. De-risk finance should 
cover the mapping and exploration phase s, but also the stage of feasibility studies, as this often 
represents a costly bottleneck for project promoters. The European Investment Bank (EIB) could play a 
more active role in de -risking priority projects.  De-risk finance in the form of loans, grants or public 
investments could also be realized through the creation of a new Exploration Fund, or in the context 
of the proposed European Sovereignty Fund.  
 
The recognition of Strategic European Projects  would send an important investment signal to 
financial institutions, which is of crucial importance to realise competitive and financially sustainable 
mining projects in Europe. Inclusion of the mining industry within the EU Taxonomy would further attract 
both public and private investments.

Enhanced mapping & faster permitting  
 
Regulatory uncertainty represents significant risks and costs for the mining industry. Today, the 
average permitting process takes 10-15 years. The CRM Act should encourage all national authorities 
to increase efforts to map  and prioriti se critical  mineral projects, and to ensure more predictable, 
transparent, and efficient permitting processes. A method for defining Strategic European Projects and 
project prioritisation could be built on the  existing Projects of Common Interest (PCI) model 
under the Trans-European Energy Infrastructure (TEN-E) Regulation. This would ensure legal certainty 
through a common European framework for facilitating permitting procedures , ensuring that critical 
mineral projects receive adequate funding , and that one -stop-shops are set up to ease cross -border 
coordination. New forms for accelerated permitting procedures could be tested in selected prioritised 
critical raw materials sourcing projects, before being considered for wider application. 
 
Targeted REE R&D  
 
The CRM Act should stimulate increased and more targeted efforts when it comes to  research 
and development (R&D) for REE mining and refining processes , an area in which no or minimal 
expertise currently exists in Europe.  Significant R&D is also  needed to develop efficient recycling 
processes, ensuring a viable future for a market for secondary materials. Further, Rare Earths Norway 
sees great potential in developing mining waste into new products, and we are already working with 
several European research in stitutions on developing our tailings into new by-products for i.e., the 
construction sector. Existing EU funding mechanisms must continue to support such projects.  
 
A competitive European mining industry  
 
The CRM Act should include instruments that level the playing field for European companies in 
the global market. Measures must be put in place to safeguard the European mining industry against 
dumping and unfair subsidisation. Certain state aid provisions  will be necessary for prima ry raw 
materials extraction to be competitive in global markets. In this context, Rare Earths Norway fully 
supports and encourages the rapid establishment of an Important Project of Common European 
Interest (IPCEI) for the raw materials industry in Europe. Member States should have the possibility to 
provide de-risk finance and targeted support to prioritized strategic projects. 
 
European mining projects promote the highest environmental and social standards availab le, 
including best practices with regards to community engagement. Environmental sustainability and 
decent work conditions should be actively promoted for any project to receive strategic status and 
preferential treatment , including in external trade agree ments. The application of Rules of Origin, 
traceability requirements, and carbon footprint declarations are measures that will support the 
competitiveness of European raw materials.  
 
Summary of recommendations 
 
The Fen REE complex, primarily developed by Rare Earths Norway, is a great example of a Norwegian 
mining project which can help the EU meet its climate targets. In the context of the EU -Norway Green 
Alliance and the strategic partnership on value chains for raw materials and batteries, as well as  the 
upcoming Norwegian mineral strategy, we recommend that the EU and the Norwegian government 
work closely together in defining priorities and strategic projects.

Further, in addressing the main challenges for the European mining industry , we encourage the 
European Commission to take on board the recommendations outlined in this paper:  
 
• Access to finance is of crucial importance to realising mining projects in Europe, especially 
in the early stage. De-risk financing must cover the entirety of a project, including the mapping 
and exploration of deposits, and the feasibility study. A dedicated Exploration Fund could 
be put in place to support critical raw materials exploration in Europe. In addition, mi ning 
should be included in the EU Taxonomy to attract additional investment and funding.  
 
• Regulatory certainty and accelerated permitting processes could be achieved through the 
establishment of a list of Strategic European Projects . This list could follow the same 
model of project prioritisation as for the PCI-list under the TEN-E Regulation.  
 
• The CRM Act should stimulate increased and more targeted R&D efforts when it comes to 
REE mining and refining processes, an area in which no or minimal expertise currently exists 
in Europe. EU funding should be specifically dedicated to this under Horizon Europe.  
 
• The EU must be effective in addressing unfair competition in order to render European raw 
materials projects economically viable  in the long term . State aid provisions should be 
considered, including the rapid launch of an Important Project of Common European 
Interest (IPCEI) for raw materials. Further, high environmental and social standards should 
underpin any preferential treatment.  
 
 
About Rare Earths Norway  
 
Rare Earths Norway (REN) was established in 2016 with the purpose of developing the Fen deposit, 
and currently holds exploration permits for the majority of the REE-bearing iron-dolomite carbonatite 
(FDC) at Fen . The company has two owners: Hustadlitt AS and Brennebu AS. Hustadlitt AS is a 
holding company with majority ownership in REN and in Norsk Mineral AS, a company with diversified 
operations and over 70 years of experience in mineral extraction and processing. Brennebu AS is a 
holding company located in Ulefoss, close to the Fen deposit. The history of Brennebu’s owners and 
their involvement in local iron mining and smelting industry dates back to 1657. 
 
REN’s vision is to become an important European supplier of mixed rare earths concentrate, enabling 
the green industrial transition, climate change mitigation, and fuelling the digital transition.

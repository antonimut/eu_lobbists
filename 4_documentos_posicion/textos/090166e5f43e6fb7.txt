 
 
Orano’s contribution to the public consultation on the European Critical Raw 
Materials Act 
Orano thanks the European Commission  (EC) for giving the opportunity to contribute to the 
elaboration of the European Critical Raw Materials (CRM) Act.   
Securing the supply of critical and strategic raw materials is crucial given the current geopolitical 
context and considering that the demand for those CRM will significantly increase to cover the needs 
of the green and digital transitions. To speed up the implementation of the actions identified in the 
CRM Action Plan in 2020, an ambitious legislative act is strongly needed.  
Orano, as a European company, boasts unique, internationally recognized expertise in the nuclear fuel 
cycle. The group is committed to providing a secure access to nuclear materials and proven industrial 
solutions in the recycling of used fuel, radioactive waste management a nd non-power applications. 
Through its business, Orano intends to contribute to the fight against global warming by improving 
access to competitive low-carbon electricity for all, to the preservation of resources through recycling 
and to health through nuc lear medicine . In particular, thanks to its know -how in recycling and 
recovering nuclear materials, Orano is exploring new fields of activities for the benefit of society. 
Indeed, our group has more than 40 years’ experience spanning the entire fuel cycle,  including in 
chemistry, hydrometallurgy and process industrialization. Orano also holds a know -how in powder 
metallurgy and sintering and effective hazardous materials risk management and controlled 
atmosphere. These competences can be applied to the recycling of critical raw materials, for instance 
those present in end-of-life batteries or scarp manufacturing of Gigafactories. Together with strategic 
partners, Orano has developed an innovative low -carbon process that allows materials of interest 
(cobalt, manganese, nickel, lithium, graphite) found in battery modules to be recovered and purified 
for the reuse in new battery components.  
Moreover, Orano group is being involved in research and industrialization projects for a short loop 
magnet recycling and r emanufacturing to re -establish an industrial base for the production of high -
performance sintered magnets for the European market (see the letter below). Since they are used in 
electric vehicles and wind turbines, two markets undergoing strong growth, high -performance 
permanent magnets are strategic for the decarbonization of the economy. These permanent magnets 
systematically comprise rare earths, 80% of which are currently produced in China  and for which 
Europe is dependent at 100% . According to the EC ac tion plan on critical raw materials, China is the 
biggest supplier of heavy rare earths to the EU.  This dependency on these value chains constitutes for 
Europe a strategic risk for its economy and twin transition. 
In addition to this, Orano is also committed to recycle Platinum Group Metals (PGM) from used nuclear 
fuel. Today, the supply of Platinum Group Metals (PGM) is concentrated in  Russia (Palladium) and 
South Africa (Iridium, Platinum, Rhodium and  Ruthenium). PGMs are high value metals used in a  
variety of industrial applications such as catalysts in the automobile and  chemical industry. In 
particular, PGMs are increasingly used in hydrogenation reaction in chemical industry and should take

a central stage in the hydrogen economy. Several strategic PGMs, including palladium, ruthenium and 
rhodium, can be  found in used nuclear fuel as fission products. Orano estimates that important  
quantities of PGM could be recovered at  Orano’s La Hague facility, hereby significantly reducing the 
security of supply risk for EU supply chains. As of today, the process of treatment and recycling of used 
nuclear fuel does not allow recovering those platinoids and would therefore require targeted R&D. 
Orano’s activities in the nuclear back-end could also support the recovery of Xenon (it is estimated that

That is why Orano is a member of the European Raw M aterials Alliance, of the European Battery 
Alliance and the Renewable and Low-Carbon Fuels Value Chain Industrial Alliance , and a core partner 
of the EIT Raw Materials. 
Based on our experience, and in order to support the EU’s effort to increase its resilience and strategic 
autonomy, particularly in the industrial sector, Orano proposes to include the following elements in 
the legislative proposal. 
Defining priorities and objectives for EU actions 
First, circularity should be at the core of the upcoming proposal. It is important to incentivize recycling 
(including from production scrap) and refining to limit EU’s dependency towards third countries, 
especially for CRMs which are neither mined nor processed in the EU. Circular processes should be 
supported in the EU: recycling is a permanent supply source to EU’s economy. Thus, the regulatory 
framework should introduce the collection, recycling, and reincorporation of secondary raw materials 
into new products as well as refining of primary raw mater ial. These considerations must go hand in 
hand with a legislative framework to avoid the leakage of strategic materials outside the EU: as 
mentioned in the EC consultation, “ export of waste containing critical raw materials increases the 
shortage of critical raw materials in the EU and limits potential for circularity”. 
Second, the CRM Act should focus on the critical raw materials which are considered as the most 
strategic to achieve the twin transition. While the new CRM list is expected by 2023, this prioritization 
will offer the opportunity to put efforts where actually needed.  
Strengthening the EU’s critical raw materials value chain (mining, refining, processing, recycling) 
In order to guarantee the development of the CRM value chain in Europe, projects identified as 
strategic all along the value chain must be supported at all maturity levels based on best available 
technology not entailing excessive costs: additional money should be available for the actions related 
to CRM access in Horizon Europe (a new partnership should be settled), to facilitate access and 
communication around the funding opportunities developed by the EIT Raw Materials as well as by 
the ERMA, update the EIB policy to encourage more investment in strategic projects related to raw 
materials and facilitate the access of such projects under the Innovation Fund. Moreover, the 
opportunity for developing a new IPCEI or extending the existing ones should be explo red. Lastly, the 
                                                                                   
 
upcoming sovereignty fund, recently announced by the EC, should include among its priorities the 
access to strategic raw materials.   
In order to speed up investments, the taxonomy should be considered as an important tool. For that 
reason, the inclusion in the taxonomy of all useful mining activities for the transition is crucial.  
The European value chain should start with mines respecting corporate social responsibility (CSR) 
practices. As a responsible miner, Orano’s objective is, in a truly pro-active manner, to manage the 
social, environmental, societal and techno-economic issues arising from its activities at each stage of 
the mining cycle, in a sustainable, coordinated and balanced manner. In Niger, for instance, Orano 
helped stepping up a healthcare system, facilitating access to water and strengthening food security. 
The Group is a member of the International Council on Mining and Metals (ICMM) and is committed 
to adopting the principles established on the basis of the sector's best  CSR practices and to working 
with other mining players to further advance them.  This international standard should be promoted 
concerning non-European sourcing. Compelling a company to move from one standard to another is 
burdensome and won’t change much for the consumers. However, allowing only companies officially 
respecting one of the compliant standards is a way to ensure all the actors are committed to respect 
CSR practices. 
Ensuring a sustainable level playing field across the Single Market 
Guaranteeing the emergence and the competitiveness of the EU industry should be one of the major 
pillars for the upcoming legislative proposal.  
Orano is fully aligned with the EC statement in the public consultation stating that “a level playing field 
for critical raw materials-based products and components that are crucial to the green transition such 
as rare earth permanent magnets, for instance by setting recycling obligations or an information 
requirement on the carbon footprint of their production process, inside and outside of the EU”. Indeed, 
as recycling is an important tool to improve autonomy, collection and reincorporation rates should be 
established for the CRMs which are the most crucial for the twin transition and starting with the HREE. 
The proposals introduced in the battery regulation should serve as a model for the CRM Act.  
Finally, a sustainable level playing field implies that all the EU circular economy has the same access to 
wastes. Thus, facilitating the exchange of waste with a recyclable potential between EU member states 
will be crucial.

Subject: Actions to secure European strategic autonomy on rare earths and permanent magnets 
 
Summary: 
Faced with the energy challenge arising from the objective of climate neutrality by 2050, the 
European Union must secure the value chain of rare earths and permanent magnets. 
The implementation of a strategic autonomy requires first of all to create the regulatory conditions 
which will allow the development of such Rare Earths magnet industry.  To do so, the French 
permanent magnet sector suggests the following: 
- to impose on all European and international players compliance with UE’s high 
environmental and human standards through the principle of best available technology 
not entailing excessive costs (action 1); 
- to avoid  exporting waste containing used magnets from European territory and  
alternatively to further develop the “urban mine” (action 2); 
- to actively support the implementation of an ambitious Critical Raw Materials Act clearly 
encompassing rare earth s, in particular via quantified targets for collection and 
reincorporation (actions 3, 4 and 5); 
- to mobilize all the available tools of the European Union to allow the emergence of an 
industrial sector (actions 6 and 7). 
 
 
Dear Vice-President Šefčovič, 
Dear Commissioner Breton, 
 
The objective of climate neutrality by 2050 triggers an unprecedented transition challenge in energy 
production and e -mobility. Achieving this objective depends in particular on the major/outstanding 
role of certain mineral raw materials, vis -à-vis which the limited number of producing countries can 
lead to structuring impacts in situations of tension. The scale and rate of growth of raw material needs 
makes their supply a key strategic point, as the Member States underlined in the conclusions of the 
Versailles European Council of March 2022 on the security of supply of raw materials. 
These developments have led to the adaptation of industrial value chains, in particular that of rare 
earths and permanent magnets. Indeed, rare earths are essential components to manufacture the 

 
 
permanent magnets present in the electrical machines (electric generators of wind turbines or electric 
motors of vehicles). Without the energetic implementation of actions at European level, the current 
vulnerabilities and dependencies on this value chain are bound to further deteriorate. The President 
of the European Commission rightly recalled in her State of the Union 2022 address that "our demand 
for rare earths alone will increase fivefold by 2030", underlining the need to define strategic projects. 
 
Strengthening Europe's strategic autonomy in rare earths and permanent magnets will involve both 
securing the supply of rar e earths and developing new production capacities in Europe based on 
technologies and processes compatible with the best standards. 
This strengthening of European strategic autonomy will also materialize through the implementation 
of the recycling of used permanent magnets. Indeed, used/end of life permanent magnets remain “by-
products” a non-tapped metal secondary source as they are today either buried or incinerated, despite 
their remarkable recycling potential. Today, less than 1% of permanent magnets ar e recycled, and 
when they are, it is – more often than not - by leaving European territory to be reprocessed in Vietnam 
or China, the later having already a strong monopoly (98%) on the production of these magnets. This 
leak of strategic raw materials constitutes a real challenge for the autonomy of the European Union in 
that it prevents the establishment of a European industrial value chain. 
 
Faced with these challenges, the French permanent magnet sector is now being structured. It supports 
the recent initiatives launched by the European Union to guarantee its strategic autonomy and 
sovereignty for critical raw materials, in particular the ann ouncement of a European legislative 
proposal on critical raw materials (the "CRM Act"). 
To restore strategic independence to Europe and all its Member States  along the value chain of rare 
earths and permanent magnets , it nevertheless seems urgent to share with you some proposals for 
targeted actions: 
1. Incorporate into all projects a requirement to respect  the UE’s high  environmental and human 
standards through the principle of best available technology not entailing excessive costs, in order 
to guarantee a l evel playing field between existing or future European players and their 
international competitors. This action would be applicable both for supplies from partner countries 
and their manufacturers, and for the development on European territory of new indus trial units 
and/or the development of technologies; 
2. Reduce, as much as possible, exports of used magnets from European territory so as not to 
compromise the emergence of an industrial value chain that takes benefit from the “urban mine”; 
3. Clearly include rare earths within the scope of  the projected CRM Act, taking into consideration 
their indisputable relevance for the strategic objectives of the European Union’s twin transition; 
4. Provide within this legislative act an impact analysis that remains compatible with the end of the 
European legislature in 2024; 
5. Set ambitious targets within this CRM Act on (i) the collection rate which could be set at [30%] in 
2025, with an increase of [5%] every 5 years and (ii) the reincorporation rate which could be set at 
[4%] in 2030 and then at [10%] in 2035; 
6. Enlarge and uncap European, national and regional funding tools to critical raw materials and on 
all process steps of the value chain (mine, refining, recycling, etc.) in order to (i) support research 
and innovation for more sustainable and circular  production processes, and (ii) promote the 
development of industrial units requiring heavy start -up investments. The establishment of a 
dedicated IPCEI, or the amendment of the IPCEI Batteries to extend it to critical raw materials, 
must be studied; 
7. Accelerate the establishment of the new European Sovereignty Fund and open it to European 
projects in the value chain of rare earths and magnets; 
 
 
 
These action proposals are also in line with the recommendations made in the report “Rare Earth 
Magnets and Motors: a European call for action” written by the ERMA (European Raw Material 
Alliance) in September 2021. 
 
Please accept, Sir, the assurance of our highest consideration.

Appendix: Composition of the French permanent magnet sector 
The French permanent magnet sector was set up in the spirit of collective and concerted action 
between economic players and public authorities. 
It follows on from the work of Mr. Philippe VARIN and the Direction Générale des Entreprises (DGE) 
within the framework of the intergovernmental mission on critical metals. 
It brings together manufacturers whose activity and projects are part of the entire value chain, from 
upstream to downstream: 
- Permareco via REEfine, Poral, Arelec, ECM; 
- Caremag through Carester; 
- MagREEsource; 
- Magnolia via Orano, the CEA, Paprec, Valeo and Daimantel France; 
- “Collecting Permanent Magnets” via Daimantel France. 
It benefits from the active support of the competent authorities (DGE), a professional association 
(A3M) and a consultant (VIAL-Consulting).

EUROPEN response to the call for evidence and 
public consultation on a European Critical Raw 
Materials Act 
EUROPEN, the European Organization for Packaging and the Environment , is supportive of the European 
Commission’s initiative to develop a European Critical Raw Materials (CRM s) Act in order to secure a sustainable 
and diversified supply of critical raw materials  in Europe , while addressing existing bottlenecks and boosting 
circularity.  
Recent and ongoing crises have exposed the need to make the supply of raw materials more resilient and fit for the 
needs of the EU in terms of decarbonisation, digitalisation and strategic autonomy.  
EUROPEN is therefore keen on working with the Commission to identify policy options that can address key targets 
and vulnerabilities, optimise the use of critical raw materials and diversify the sources of supply.  
This paper complements EUROPEN’s reply to the public consultation’s questionnaire. 
 
1. Impact of Critical Raw Materials disrupti ons on the 
packaging value chain 
Price fluctuations, inflation, unilateral actions by Member States and overall lack of cooperation and information -
sharing can lead to unavailability of certain raw materials for packaging and disrupt the functioning of the packaging 
value chain in the EU single market. This can impact the supply and distribution of essential goods in the agri-food, 
health, hygiene and pharmaceutical sectors, which are highly dependent on a well-functioning packaging supply 
chain and have proved to be of utmost importance in recent crises.  
As an example, t he ongoing conflict in Ukraine is reminding us of how essential packaging is for critical product 
groups, including perishable agri -food goo ds, pharmaceutical and hygiene -related products . For instance, 
packaging is vital to ensure the supply of field rations for active -duty servicemen, or for civilians depending on 
emergency aid and field kitchens. Packaging also plays a key role in ensuring the uninterrupted delivery of pre -
packed goods from farms to factories, and final consumers. Similarly, during the Covid-19 pandemic, packaging 
had a key role in the safe delivery and preservation of medicine, including COVID -19 vaccines, medical devices 
and self-care products that protect and save lives. 
Overall, recent years have highlighted how, despite its resilience, the packaging value chain can be vulnerable to 
external shocks and that overdependence on few suppliers for critical goods and materials is a risk not just for the 
Single Market, but more directly to consumers and livelihoods.  
 
2. Ensuring a reliable supply of Critical Raw Materials  
Common challenges require common action. EUROPEN is supportive of coordinated measures such as 
contingency planning, monitoring and early warning mechanisms to anticipate supply chain disruptions, as well as 
stockpiling at the EU level. More specifically, EUROPEN encourages the European Commission to consider the 
following aspects during the elaboration of a CRMs Act: 
▪ Recognise that packaging and its raw materials are an essential value chain . The unavailability of 
critical raw materials, such as m agnesium which is used for lightweight alloys for packaging  or raw

materials used as catalysts in chemical processes, can disrupt the packaging value chain and negatively 
impact the availability of sufficient and appropriate packaging used in essential industries, such as the food 
and pharmaceutical sectors.  
▪ Ensure that measures to anticipate, better communicate on, coordinate and manage the supply of 
critical raw materials are foreseen as part of the CRMs Act. These measures should be adopted at 
European level to ensure a harmonised approach and high degree of coordination among Member States.  
▪ Establish an extraordinary EU/Member States crisis fund to accompany this initiative.  Strategic 
storage and stockpiling of Critical Raw Materials come at a significant cost for the industry and authorities, 
as such acti ons could distort market prices and syphon off resources from other jurisdictions and 
industries. An extraordinary crisis fund would therefore be helpful to mitigate the effects of stockpiling.  
Increased funding for research projects on resource efficiency , recycling and substitution of Critical Raw 
Materials through Horizon Europe would also be welcome. 
▪ Ensure constant engagement with industry players , based on the example of the European Raw 
Materials Alliance, for the identification of key raw materials and projects, their targets and stockpiling.  As 
members of the Expert Group on European food security crisis preparedness and response mechanism 
(EFSCM), we support the establishment of a similar model for involving  industry stakeholders under this 
initiative.  
▪ Evaluate the role of packaging on the critical raw materials market.  Packaging is not only directly 
dependant on the supply of certain critical raw materials, but it can also be a source of secondary raw 
materials via recycling. However, the potential to recycle Critical Raw Materials from waste is not yet fully 
exploited in Europe and there is room to further improve their circularity. As an example, the deployment 
of adequate waste collection and sorting infrastructures in every Member State is essential to prevent the 
loss of valuable resources due to landfilling or incineration . Similarly, a  stronger policy framework is 
necessary to facilitate and strengthen the transboundary movements of waste across the EU  to support 
the creation of a Single Market for Secondary Raw Materials.  
▪ Ensure consistency across the policy spectrum . EUROPEN encourages the European Commission 
to take a holistic approach and coordinate this policy framework with different ongoing initiatives, such as 
the REPowerEU strategy, the Single Market Emergency Instrument, and the EU Food Contingency Plan 
to ensure food security in Europe at times of crises.  
▪ Prevent regulatory inconsistency and double regulation. Measures related to the circularity of critical 
raw materials from packaging waste, should be consistent with existing EU legislation and legislation 
currently being developed, such as the ongoing revision of the Packaging and Packaging Waste Directive.

Executive Summary  
SEMI Europe, the global industry association representing more than 2,500 companies and 1,3 million 
professionals from across the global electronics manufacturing and design supply chain welcomes the 
opportunity to comment on the European Commission’s upcoming initiative on critical raw materials. 
With the European Chips Act and recent upsurge of investments into the expansion of  Europe’s 
semiconductor manufacturing and R&D ecosystem, the supply and access to raw materials has become 
ever so essential to further development of Europe’s industrial resilience. To ensure unimpeded growth of 
Europe’s technological capabilities in an efficient and effective manner, the European Raw Materials Act 
needs to strengthen Europe’s position as global player in the raw materials ecosystem, ensuring that in 
Europe’s semiconductor industry players are not left behind the global competition. 
As the world’s largest semiconductor industry association and voice of the semiconductor supply chain, 
SEMI Europe sets out 6 priorities for the European Critical  Raw Materials Act to address.                                                
With over 300 European members collectively working on securing Europe’s digital future, SEMI Europe 
seeks to actively pa rticipate in key discussions around the supply and access to critical raw materials.                           
We believe our approach can provide guidance to the European Commission and drive a common position 
on pressing issues within the microelectronics industry.    
SEMI Europe  recommendations for the European Critical  Raw Materials Act  
▪ Recognize and position the semiconductor industry and supply of raw materials critical to 
production of advanced electronic components and systems as key priority of the European 
Raw Materials Act.  
 
▪ Introduce additional IPCEI workstreams for establishment semiconductor materials pre-
competitive research and commercialization facilities of new and innovative materials, 
gasses, and chemicals for Europe’s semiconductor ecosystem. 
 
▪ Establish close cooperation between industry and the envisioned European network of raw 
materials agencies to stress tests and identify the effects of likely and impactful raw 
materials supply disruptions and unilateral export measures on Europe’s microelectronic 
ecosystem. 
 
▪ Support exporters of raw materials by implem enting assistance programs aimed to 
introduce new technologies for enhanced extraction, processing, and supply of raw 
materials. By linking development policy objectives with raw materials partnerships, Europe 
can foster social and economic progress in dev eloping countries, while accelerating the 
supply and production of critical electronic components at home.  
 
▪ SEMI recommends a multi-stakeholder approach to develop and harmonize environmental 
and social performance standards used globally to achieve “one standard, one certification, 
accepted everywhere”; and utilize the broad free trade agreement (FTA) networks of Europe 
with its likeminded partners to promote the uptake and application of such standard.   
      
▪ The development of certification schemes for global raw materials community should match 
specific industry technical and quality requirements set by existing industrial community 
standards and best practices. Here, a joint global industry -governments overs ight 
committee can help coordinate future certification activities and facilitate a closer 
collaboration between the Europe and its partners.

Position raw materials central to semiconductor industry as 
strategic priority in the European  Critical  Raw Materials Act  
Today, Europe’s semiconductor industry is highly depended on access to precious minerals, industrial 
chemicals, and gasses - present across every step of industrial production. While the quantities used 
vary across different segments of the semiconductor value chain, their criticality for manufacturing 
and functioning of electronic components cannot be underestimated.  
The number of elements used across the semiconductor industry has quadrupled since 1980’s, making 
use of almost every element present in the nature 1. Critical materials, gases, and chemicals such as 
silicon, germanium, palladium, cobalt, gallium, rare earth elements, fluorspar, argon, sulfuric acid, 
high purity solvents (IPA/PGMEA), neon or xenon to name a few, represent the foundation upon 
which many European players in the semiconductor value chain rely on. Critically,  minerals such as 
tantalum, tungsten, tin and gold  (also known as 3TG minerals ) are commonly used in the 
manufacturing and functionality of advanced lithography systems, in which Europe holds a strategic 
global market position.   
According to SEMI market s tatistics, the global demand for raw materials will continue its growth.                         
For example, in 2021 on the strength of robust secular demand for chips and the industry’s expansion 
of production capacity, wafer fabrication materials and packaging materials revenues totaled $40.4 
billion and $23.9 billion, respectively, for year-over-year increases of 15.5% and 16.5% . The silicon, 
wet chemicals, chemical -mechanical planarization (CMP), and photomask segments showed the 
strongest growth in the wafer fabrication materials market, while packaging materials market growth 
was largely driven by the organic substrates, lead frames, and bonding wire segments 2. With such 
growing global demand, the need for raw materials critical to the semiconductor  industry to be 
positioned high on the Europe’s agenda of strategic priorities has grown significantly. 
Considering Europe’s ambition to become one of the leading powers in the global semiconductor 
ecosystem, it is necessary to recognize the urgency to sec ure and sustain access to raw materials 
critical to the growth and resilience of our domestic semiconductor industry.  
 
SEMI Europe Recommendations  
▪ Recognize and position the semiconductor industry and supply of raw materials critical to 
production of advanced electronic components and systems as key priority of the 
European Raw Materials Act.

Introduce a strategy on sustainable access and supply of critical raw 
materials with particular focus on the needs of Europe’s 
semiconductor industry ecosystem   
With technological advances depending less and less on geometrical shrink and more on the design 
and manufacture of new, advanced transistor and interconnect structures using new and complex 
material stacks, secure global raw material supply chains are ever more critical. 
From a semiconductor industry perspective, critical raw materials are sourced, refined, processed, and 
exported globally only from a handful of locations. For example, rare earth elements  reserves are 
limited and found only in a handful of countries such a s China (57.5%), United States (15.6%), 
Myanmar (12.3%) or Australia (7.0%)3, while large quantities of materials such as silicon, germanium 
or gallium can be largely found in Russia and China, creating significant dependencies on a handful of 
countries. 
Considering global sourcing limitations, the setup of mining operations, refining, and processing 
facilities, all liked with the environmental risks of opening new mining locations, establishment of 
additional raw materials winning, and processing industry  capacities becomes challenging.                                    
To secure a sustainable supply of raw materials for Europe’s semiconductor industry, we need to seek 
alternative approaches to the winning and re-use of materials critical to our economy.  
 
SEMI Europe Recommendations  
▪ Introduce additional IPCEI workstreams for establishment semiconductor materials pre-
competitive research and commercialization facilities of new and innovative materials, 
gasses and chemicals for Europe’s semiconductor ecosystem. 
 
▪ Establish close cooperation between industry and the envisioned European network of raw 
materials agencies to stress tests and identify the effects of likely and impactful raw 
materials supply disruptions and unilateral export measures on Europe’s microelectronic 
ecosystem. 
 
▪ Support exporters of raw materials by implem enting assistance programs aimed to 
introduce new technologies for enhanced extraction, processing, and supply of raw 
materials. By linking development policy objectives with raw materials partnerships, Europe 
can foster social and economic progress in dev eloping countries, while accelerating the 
supply and production of critical electronic components at home.

Advance a common, industry -backed international approach and 
strategies on standardization and certification schemes for raw 
materials sourcing, refining, and processing  
For the semiconductor ecosystem, transparency and trustworthiness of supply is of cr itical 
importance. Any deviations from quality standards and best -practices in mining, refining, and 
processing of raw materials are critically monitored, inspected and addressed by the industry. 
However, many issues surrounding responsible sourcing, refini ng, and processing of raw materials 
continue to prevail mostly due global asymmetries in regulation of transparency and responsible 
conduct. 
 
Harmonization of environmental and social performance standards and certification schemes is a 
challenge for many countries and industries, as the markets grow more global, and a clear view of 
requirements for safety and interoperability can become a challenge. In this regard, any European 
effort to introduce unilateral environmental and social performance obligations  on activities related 
to raw materials sourcing, refining and production would not reach the necessary critical mass unless 
other global players agree to introduce comparable measures as well. 
 
It is the view of SEMI Europe that the European Commission sh ould engage proactively with key 
players in the global raw materials market in order to increase awareness on the complexities of raw 
materials extraction and supply and advance the discussions about the opportunities joint 
cooperation can deliver. Responsible raw materials sourcing should be recognized by the Critical 
Raw Materials Act as a global issue that requires international solutions where industry realities, 
humanitarian impact and environmental security are jointly taken into account. This would require 
a joint call for action for industry, governments and international organizations to work closely 
together in order to reach the necessary critical mass and deliver a multi -stakeholder approach on a 
variety of pressing issues. 
 
SEMI Europe Recommendations  
▪ SEMI recommends a multi-stakeholder approach to develop and harmonize environmental 
and social performance standards used globally to achieve “one standard, one certification, 
accepted everywhere”; and utilize the broad free trade agreement (FTA) networks of Europe 
with its likeminded partners to promote the uptake and application of such standard.   
             
▪ The development of certification schemes for global raw materials community should match 
specific industry technical and quality requirements set by existing industrial community 
standards and best practices. Here, a joint global industry -governments overs ight 
committee can help coordinate future certification activities and facilitate a closer 
collaboration between the Europe and its partners.

Conclusion  
SEMI Europe would welcome the opportunity to discuss the recommendations with the European 
Commission. The microelectronics sector is a key enabler and remains ready to work together with 
all interested stakeholders to develop new avenues of growth to maintain and sustain Europe’s 
leadership in critical, enabling technologies.

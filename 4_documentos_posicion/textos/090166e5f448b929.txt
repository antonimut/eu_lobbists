Input RAG Austria AG on Call for Evidence regarding Critical Raw Materials Act  
 
 
As RAG Austria AG, we welcome the opportunity to comment on the Critical Raw Materials 
Act’s call for evidence. 
 
RAG Austria AG as the fourth largest gas storage company in Europe is a pioneer in the 
development of hydrogen (H2) technologies, large-scale H2 storage and the integration of H2 
into the existing gas infrastructure. 
In addition to methanation projects (e.g. Underground Sun Conversion 
http://www.underground-sun-conversion.at), RAG Austria is advancing projects in water and 
methane electrolysis /pyrolysis and long-term underground storage (e.g. Underground Sun 
Storage) and is currently developing the world's first 100% H2 storage facility in an 
underground pore storage site (www.uss-2030.at). Furthermore, RAG Austria is also active in 
the field of research and implementation of carbon cycles (e.g. C-CED project https://www.rag-
austria.at/forschung-innovation/carbon-cycle-economy-demonstration.html) and is also driving 
the development of an international H2 value chain within the H2EU+Store consorti um 
(https://www.h2euplusstore.com). 
 
The current energy crisis very well shows that methane and hydrogen are not only important 
energy carriers for Europe’s households but are also urgently needed as raw materials in 
various sectors, now and in the future. Hence, it needs to be ensured that a sustainable 
production of these critical resources (raw materials + energy carriers) is carried out long-term 
and that steady streams of these resources are available  for Europe’s businesses and 
customers at any time.  
 
Based on our extensive experience in different decarbonization projects, we would like to 
recommend in the CRM Act not only to consider rare earths and other metals, but also to 
define early enough other liquid and gaseous resources (in particular methane [CH4], 
hydrogen [H2], carbon dioxide [CO2] and solid carbon [C]) as critical raw materials and 
thus to derive and implement measures for their future supply.  
 
Methane plays an important role as a carbon-containing energy carrier and raw material 
in many industrial/chemical processes.  
For the future, however, it must be ensured that methane can be used in a climate -neutral 
manner in any (industrial/chemical) use. In addition to the use of regionally produced natural 
gas or imported natural gas with low value chain GHG emissions in combination with the 
material or gaseous separation of C or CO2 by means of CC US technologies , the 
exploitation of green gas potentials plays an important role here. In this context, besides the 
production of synthetic CH4, the existing European biogas and biomethane potentials 
and their utilization are particularly relevant.  Clear regulatory framework cond itions and 
funding/financing incentives are crucial for this. 
 
Also, hydrogen is not only an energy carrier or fuel for sectors that are difficult to 
decarbonize (e.g., industry/chemistry and transport), but is already an important raw 
material for chemical compounds as well as metallurgy and is increasingly used for the 
production of renewable fuels and high -value chemical products.  To keep these

manufacturing processes in Europe, the long -term and cost -effective supply of H2 as a 
feedstock must be  ensured. Therefore, in addition to utilizing limited sources of naturally 
occurring white H2 (regionally or from strategic partnerships), a technology-open approach to 
generation should also be taken as regards the choice of H2 production technology. Therefore, 
we call for climate-neutral H2 (e.g., from methane electrolysis/pyrolysis or steam reforming + 
CCUS (carbon capture, utilization and [intermediate] storage) to be considered as a critical 
feedstock in addition to renewable H2. This allows a higher degree of diversification and 
security of supply, which is particularly important since Europe will remain a net importer of H2 
in the future.  
 
As regards CO2, it is also crucial for Europe to provide for and quickly develop technical 
solutions for industrial CO2 capture and direct air capture.  
Besides a use of CO2 in various industrial and energetic processes, C O2 is also 
considered an important resource  in the food industry, e.g., in meat production, for 
greenhouses for vegetable growing . A closed cycle use of CO2 can be successfully 
achieved by climate-neutral capture in industry in combination with interim storage in depleted 
gas and oil reservoirs and subsequent reuse in the food ( e.g., proteins as meat substitute) or 
chemical industries. 
 
Solid carbon is an increasingly important and promising raw material.  
Produced in a climate-neutral way with the methane electrolysis/pyrolysis technology or even 
with negative emissions using biomethane , this raw material can be used  and will be 
needed in large volumes in agriculture as a soil improver.  Also used in construction, 
textiles, batteries, and various other applications, this feedstock can quickly and sustainably 
improve the carbon footprint in a lot of sectors. Moreover, the methane electrolysis process 
consumes only a quarter of the renewable electricity required compared to water electrolysis, 
but at the same time produces two valuable output materials, hydrogen and carbon.  
 
These feedstocks all urgently need a clear and effective regulatory environment as well as 
opportunities for technology -open generation evaluated solely on achieved CO2 
reductions, which together stimulate appropriate customer demand, and financing and 
funding for market ramp-up and development of storage and transport infrastructure in 
Europe.  
 
We kindly ask you to consider our arguments in the legislative proposals and are available for 
discussion at any time.

A response from EIT RawMaterials and the European Raw 
Materials Alliance (ERMA) to the European Critical Raw Materials 
Act proposal 
 
Europe faces an unprecedented low resilience in its global energy and raw materials supply chains, which has 
the potential to jeopardise major strategic industries and the 2050 zero -emission targets. Most prominently, 
the substitution of 155 billion cubic meters of Russian gas by rene wable energy sources calls for immediate 
action and investments in the raw materials sector. Materials like lithium, copper, nickel, rare earths, silicon, 
and platinum are key and indispensable enablers to carbon -free energy generation and the making of 
renewables; thus, the ambitious energy transition targets can only be achieved through a significant increase 
of raw materials capacities.   
 
The European raw materials sector  views the European Critical Raw Materials Act  as the single, most vital 
action needed to reform  the industry, strengthen the technological advantage, and allow Europe to compete 
globally on a level playing field.  
 
With the concurrent rise in geopolitical fractures  and instabilities, Europe must grow its domestic capacities 
and develop raw materials into a major strength for Europe. In accordance with the REPowerEU Plan, a series 
of measures need to be implemented to speed up the green transition and spur massive investment in 
renewable energy. Europe must position a sustainable and stable raw materials sector at its core  in order to 
maintain jobs, strengthen industrial competitiveness and achieve the EU Green Deal. This is Europe’s single 
biggest opportunity to differentiate itself and to build on its  technological advantage that takes industrial 
modernisation and compliance with ESG standards  to the next level. Europe has no alternative but to act 
swiftly, boldly, and pragmatically, to enable a highly resilient continent to emerge and to build the foundation 
for strategic autonomy for raw materials using diversified value chains. 
 
The response provided in this document is based on the recommendations made by EIT RawMaterials  
stakeholders and the European Raw Materials Alliance (ERMA)  task forces.  
 
1. Defining priorities and objectives for EU actions: Governance and 
Permitting 
Recommendation 1:  Strengthen Governance and Stakeholder Involvement   
Define EU strategic autonomy and diversification targets along the full value chain s of raw materials: Set u p 
specific quantitative EU objectives and a monitoring scheme to track priority investments for securing 
industrial capacities along all critical and strategic raw materials’ value chains.  
• Define an EU Raw Materials expert group under the leadership of the European Commission and EIT 
RawMaterials [timing: Q2 2023]. 
• Run a dedicated  stakeholder engagement process involving industry from across the value chain, 
universities, research organi sations, and wider society [ timing: Q2 2023; quarterly meetings in the 
initiation phase in 2023, followed by annual meetings].

Recommendation 2: Accelerate and facilitate the permitting process  
Simplify and streamline the permitting process with clear milestones to de-risk mining and processing projects 
involving both primary and secondary resources. Mineral deposits are rare, hard to find, and canno t be 
relocated. When a new mine is proposed, there will always be competing interests  with other activities and 
land uses. These competing interests must be resolved in an objective manner that considers local, regional, 
state and EU-wide implications. 
• To build consumer understanding of the need for domestic mining, initiate a long term educational 
and awareness strategy of the fundamental need for raw materials , to best prepare for the Social 
License to Operate [timing: Q2 2023]. 
• Establish a process and criteria throughout the European Commission services whereby exploration 
and mining projects can be fully and objectively assessed, so that Imperative Reasons for Overriding 
Public Interest (IROPI) can be considered and executed [timing: Q3 2023]. 
• Fast-track the permitting process for projects identified as being of EU strategic importance (list to be 
provided by ERMA) [timing: Q2 2023]. Ensure a permitting time frame consistent with the operational 
implementation plan of the project. 
• Incorporate responsible mining, which is  in conformity with the 
highest ESG standards  possible (e.g., IRMA certified), as an 
economic and environmentally sustainable activity within the EU 
Taxonomy regulation [timing: Q3 2023]. 
• Under the guidance of ERMA, certification scheme s should be 
defined and implemented  for refining activities of critical and 
strategic raw materials , to guarantee the automatic alignment to 
the Taxonomy [timing: Q4 2023]. 
• Create an easy access platform summ arising EU Member States’ 
raw materials strategies , projects  and practices, including 
permitting procedures, to increase transparency, enhance learning 
from best practices, and promote fair competition.  
 
2. Improving the EU’s monitoring, risk management, and governance in 
the field of CRM 
Recommendation 3: Review and monitor criticality to predict value chain disruptions  
Extend the scope of the Act to ‘critical and strategic raw materials’ as the definition of criticality may fall short 
in highlighting e conomic risks linked to high volume raw materials of high economic importance. Create a 
quantitative mapping and a respective ‘early warning system’ to predict future shortages and disruptions that 
guides actions and policies accordingly, i.e., on an annual basis (timing: Starting 2023). 
• Review the process of criticality assessment. 
• Establish a Task Force to predict disruptions in supply chains and to recommend appropriate actions.

3. Strengthening the EU’s critical raw materials value chain (exploration, 
mining, refining, processing, recycling) in a global context  
Recommendation 4: Launch regulatory measures to incentivise exploration, mining, and a circular economy 
of critical raw materials 
Promote exploration and mining using best practice strategies, methods and technologies for securing the 
lowest environmental impacts. Set the legislative framework for closing materials loops and develop a market 
for end-of-life (EOL) products to generate a contracted flow of materials in a EU Circular Economy.  
• Reassess existing waste directives , set recycling target rates, and related legislation to develop 
measures that enforces the establishment of systems for the collection, extraction, and processing of 
critical raw materials.  
• Enforce the retention and tracking of EOL products containing critical raw materials in Europe, ensuring 
the necessary legal framework to close material loops.  
• Determine the maturity level of Member States’ raw materials policies , community consultation, 
mining codes and incentives for exploration to address the strategic needs of critical raw materials. 
• Promote exploration of brownfield projects and secondary deposits (tailings and mining waste) as they 
are particularly important for fast-tracking production within a short time frame of less than five years. 
 
Recommendation 5: Create raw materials investment facilities 
Prices of minerals and metals can be very volatile and are often controlled by factors that are not in line with 
competitive market conditions. Joint public -private investments and strategic stockpil ing of critical raw 
materials increase supply chain resilience. 
• The Sovereignty Fund should consider, with high priority, grants and loans for critical and strategic raw 
materials projects. The Fund should secure financing of projects of up to 100 million EUR. 
• Create and support a European Raw Materials Fund (ERMF) as a v ehicle to fast-track and mobili se 
investments into the sector. 
• Establish a European Raw Materials Agency (EMAG), a state-supported investment and procurement 
organisation acting as an entity leveraging and ensuring supply of raw materials demand across 
European countries. The mandate of the  Agency would be to act as a clearing house  to match 
downstream demand with upstream supplies and investment needs.    
The European Raw Materials Agency (EMAG) would bundle European supply needs fully adhering to 
EU competition law, foster long -term supply agreements with strategic partnership countries of the 
EU, manage raw materials stockpiles in conjunction with private companies and  proactively involve 
trading companies. It would be backed by the EU , national structural banking and financing 
instruments, and would issue equity and debt guarantees. It is recommended that t he EIB secures 
engagement with the Agency to connect finance and raw materials needs.

• Allocate 3 billion EUR (over 2023-2027) in competitive grants to finance projects to decarbonise and 
reduce the environmental and social impact of mining, helping to gain the social license to operate at

Recommendation 6: Continue to promote supply and trade agreements with like-minded countries  
• Foster and further develop existing partnerships and trade agreements (e.g., Canada, Ukraine, Japan) 
and advance new agreements with resource-rich countries and regions (e.g., Norway, Australia, Africa, 
Greenland, Kazakhstan, and Latin America).  
• Involve industry and research delegations to progress concrete projects and collaborations. 
 
Recommendation 7: Increase investment in the primary and secondary raw materials sector and ensure a 
robust R&D and innovation ecosystem 
Strengthen Europe’s global leadership in science and research to accelerate innovation output, i.e., to 
encourage the deployment and commercialisation of technology to the market, particularly via public-private 
open innovation ecosystems.  
• Allocate an additional 2 b illion EUR in competitive grants (e.g., Horizon  Europe grants, Innovation 
Fund, Life) to boost EU R&D and innovation capacity. 
• Establish IPCEIs for critical and strategic raw materials value chains  constrained by market failure s, 
enabling the EU Industrial Ecosystems and the twin transition.

• Incentivise investments in the EU CRM sector by providing subsidies, grants, credits and equity

4. Ensuring a sustainable level playing field across the Single Market 
Recommendation 8: Secure reciprocity regarding state subsidies and the externalisation of social and 
environmental costs  
The European raw materials sector operates under non -competitive market conditions for a range of critical 
raw materials, most notably (but not exclusively), for rare earth elements.  
• Establish a n advanced EU sustainability standard and certification scheme including an analysis of 
existing schemes by a dedicated and independent EU-financed taskforce. 
• Create a level  playing field using fiscal measures backed by regulatory mechanisms  and designed to 
properly price sustainability along the entire value chain (environmental, social, and governance cost). 
• Allocation of 3 billion EUR (over 2023-2027) in competitive grants to provide CAPEX and OPEX support 
for EU projects with strategic importance - see also Recommendation 5. 
• Consider the introduction of a raw materials ‘passport’ based on the EU’s sustainability standards and 
respective regulations (including procurement).  
• Align import duties and tariff exemption regimes with the overarching policy objective of promoting 
EU strategic autonomy according to the imperative of a fair free trade mechanism.  
 
5. Develop a European raw materials skills and capacity building initiative  
Recommendation 9: Extend skills building and retention activities  
Professionally educated and  specialised human resources are indispensable to explore, extract, process, 
refine, design, and recycle raw materials and use them in applying advanced technologies.  
• Allocate funding (10 million EUR annual budget) to scale up the European Raw Materials Academy and 
to develop and offer the modules necessary to attract human capital for industry [Timing: Q1 2023]. 
• Launch a European concerted action to save jobs by promoting people at all ages to transit from brown 
to green jobs, ensuring inclusiveness and diversity.  
• Promote capacity building in public project permitting offices, i.e., at local, regional, national, and 
European level (training of personnel, and increase number of personnel) [Timing: Q1 2025].

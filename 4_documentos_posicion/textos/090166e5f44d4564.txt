BDE Feedback: Announcement of a Proposal for a CRMA  The BDE welcomes the Commission's initiative for a Critical Raw Materials Act as ground-breaking. The problem regarding Europe's increased dependence on raw material exports from third countries with autocratic and undemocratic structures was already expounded about 20 years ago on German national level. With the outbreak of the Russian war of aggression on Ukraine, it is, more than ever, urgently necessary for the European Union to take concrete measures, better late than never.  The decisive issue here, both the reason for the European Commission's taking action in the first place, and for its goal, is to safeguard the European Union as an industrial location. Under no circumstances must we fall into a similar dependency as has happened with the supply of oil and gas from Russia. The supply of critical raw materials to the European Union must be reliably secured, in the short, as well as in the long term. To achieve this goal, two fundamental prerequisites must be established. First, a binding and directly applicable legal framework, which requires a Regulation. Since this topic is of crucial importance for the whole Union, and there is the strong necessity to take action rapidly, we need harmonised rules that apply automatically and uniformly to all Member States, without any transposition into national law, and without any transposition deadlines that slow down the whole process. Second, all processes along the entire value chain, from the extraction to the recycling of critical raw materials, must be freed of bureaucracy. Procedures must be harmonised and simplified, most notably by means of more digitalisation.  As the Commission has already rightly emphasised, a diversification of strategic partnerships is urgently needed. This will lead both to raw material security, as well as to a harmonisation of the legal framework outside the EU. The European Union must guarantee investment security for all companies involved, to enable them to invest in new technologies, for example for the recycling of lithium. The core problem in that respect is, that the recycling rates for the increasingly important rare earth elements are less than one percent. raw materials and to strengthen circular economy, there is an urgent need to create a level playing field between primary and secondary raw materials.  The uncertainty of the geopolitical situation due to the Russian war of aggression on Ukraine has a very strong impact not least on the supply of the critical raw material phosphorus. Among other things, this raw material is urgently needed as fertiliser in agriculture, and for food security. The high import dependency on China and Morocco requires a more strategic approach to this finite resource. Therefore, the recycling of organic waste, which is used both energetically and materially through cascade use, must be strengthened in particular. This will provide environmentally friendly and high-quality raw material alternatives that contribute to tackle several problems, such as raw material dependency, droughts, and soil degradation.  Finally, there is the need to closely link a European Critical Raw Materials Act with the European Green Deal. If one of the explicit goals of the Green Deal is to decouple economic growth within the Union from resource use, then the management of critical raw materials in a closed loop is inherent in this. We can only achieve this goal, which runs parallel to the above-mentioned goal of safeguarding Europe as an industrial location, the European Union needs to provide assistance, for example by means of structural funds or subsidies. It is only in this way, that we can maintain the competitiveness of the European Union at international scale in the long term.

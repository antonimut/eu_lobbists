 
 
 
 
 
 
POSITION – November 2022 
European Commission’s Impact Assessment – Legislative initiative for a 
Critical Raw Material Act  
 
AFEP CONTRIBUTION TO THE PUBLIC CONSULTATION 
 
 
AFEP would like first to thank the European Commission for giving the opportunity to submit comments 
at the occasion of the public consultation for the impact assessment of a legislative proposal regarding the 
adoption of a Critical Raw Material Act.  
Large French companies clearly welcome the preparation of such initiative, announced in May 2022 in the 
initial presentation of Repower EU and confirmed by the president of the European Commission in her 
speech on the State of the European Union on 14 September.  
In complement to its individual replies to the public consultation’s online set of questions, AFEP shares the 
following comments regarding different aspects of the intended legislative proposal and accompanying 
measures:   
 Internal market and international dimensions of Critical Raw Material supply chains  
 
 Large French companies fully support the two-pillar approach outlined in the call for 
evidence: simultaneously developing a sound regulatory framework for new extractive and 
processing/recycling capacities in the EU and strengthening the EU international strategy for 
securing the access to critical raw materials in third countries, in line with the previous 
building blocks of the EU Critical Raw Material Strategy since 2008. 
 
 Given the geographical allocation of raw material deposits, it is indeed necessary to continue 
to source a significant part of them outside the EU territory in more secured and sustainable 
terms while strongly building up EU extractive and processing capacities, with the same 
objective to reduce dependencies toward a too limited number of foreign suppliers and 
resulting supply chain disruptions.  
 
 Since supply chain dependencies are as severe for manufacturing processes as for the 
extractive stage, an EU comprehensive strategy aimed to secure critical raw material supply 
chain should therefore include the different manufacturing steps in its scope. 
 
 Regarding the international dimension of the initiative, Afep also welcomes the suggested – 
and already enforced to some extent- combination of strategical partnerships on raw material 
with resource-rich third countries and trade and investment-related commitments 
undertaken under the Energy and Raw Material chapters of EU bilateral FTAs concluded with 
selected partners. Past developments have largely showed that political, industrial, technical, 
or capacity-building agreements do not necessarily prevent from the introduction of trade 
and investment restrictive measures, hence the need for legally binding commitments under 
EU FTAs, and, conversely, strategical partnerships are an opportunity to improve the 
concrete and sustainable access to raw material resources through jointly invested 
extractive/processive projects or infrastructure building/financing.  
 
 Energy and Raw Material chapters in EU FTAs should include both goods and investment 
market access commitments to secure both the import of CRM goods in the EU as well as EU 
investment in extracting and processing countries. Ideally, the EU should complement its FTA 
approach by the conclusion of bilateral investment treaties including investment protection 
clauses for the EU-invested projects.

 The list of priority countries to the conclusion of strategical partnerships should be aligned 
with EU industrial priorities in terms of critical raw material supply chains. For instance, EU 
strategical interest for metals and minerals required for the energy transition require 
concluding strategical partnerships with lithium and nickel-rich countries.  
 
 Scope and purpose of a priority list of critical raw material  
 
 Large French companies could agree with the very notion of a priority list of critical raw 
materials that would be given a legally binding effect and would therefore commend several 
important aspects of the EU internal and external policy strengthening the critical raw 
material supply for the EU industries: allocation of financial support, guidance for investment 
decisions for companies or financial institutions, setting of production quantitative targets, 
identification and facilitation of strategic projects within or outside the EU etc. 
 
 However, given the heavy policy consequences of such prioritisation, and notably in terms of 
funding allocation, they insist that the future CRMA list does not depart excessively from the 
existing indicative list of critical raw materials updated in 2020 and, in particular, is not shrunk 
to a top priority list of 6 to 9 materials. Critical raw material supply chains serve a large 
number of EU strategic industries, going beyond the need of the green transition and, 
therefore, the CRMA list should then have a broad scope to match this diversity. 
 
 Monitor critical raw material value chains and stocks 
 
 Afep agrees that the monitoring of critical raw material value chains should be 
strengthened at EU and Member States level, especially to make up for uneven technical 
or administrative capacities across the EU: hence, enhanced cooperation between 
Member States authorities and improved supervision at the EU level would be an asset. 
Meanwhile it is also important that data resulting from the monitoring by public 
authorities are also made available to EU companies being part of these raw material 
value chains so that they develop further awareness of vulnerabilities and likely 
shortages.  
 
 The scope covered by monitoring activities should be extended to an assessment of 
market future trends (i.e., the evolution of supply and demand of critical raw material in a 
near future) with a view to providing EU manufacturing companies with data necessary 
to better calibrate their strategic planning and improving EU supply chain management. 
 
 Notions of early warnings and stress tests are interesting but should be subject to more 
elaboration and precautionary measures.  In this respect, early warnings should be 
handled with much care since such alerts, if too widely spread, can have an adverse effect 
on commodities and stock markets, triggering a further increase of inflationary 
anticipations.  
 
 Strategic stocks are a key component in the list of preventive measures aimed at reducing 
supply disruption risks. Large French companies do not favour the option of mandatory 
storage and prefer the issuance of non-binding EU or Member States guidelines on the 
storage at companies ‘level.   Should a mandatory approach be nonetheless opted for, 
Afep insists that it is kept at companies ‘level as well. The same way, large French 
companies are not in favour of developing EU or national rules on rationing or 
redistributing stocks. In any event, an in-depth assessment of critical raw materials likely 
to be subject to such strategic storage is essential, including the material scope, stage of 
processing involved and the impact on pricing.

 Increase and facilitate the extraction and processing of critical raw material within and outside 
the EU 
 
 As said above, Afep supports policy developments aiming at increasing the production of 
critical raw material within the EU, from a regulatory as well as a financial perspective. In 
addition to a stronger effort to identify valuable deposits across the EU, hence further 
research and cooperation on geological data, the future CRMA should entail a vast array of 
measures on the reduction of administrative constraints for the obtention of extraction or 
procession permits (especially in member states that have not yet simplified their domestic 
legislation) and on a better funding of such industrial projects by public and private finances.  
 
 A cornerstone for developing extractive and processing activities is also the consolidation of 
common standards for responsible mining (from environmental, labour and human right point 
of view) in the EU but also in third countries, aiming at a maximum level of standard 
harmonisation. Implementing such standards to extractive and processive activities is 
essential to guarantee the sustainability and acceptability of such projects by Member States 
civil society.  Nonetheless, if the EU would only one of the few jurisdictions enforcing these 
standards, this discrepancy would result in a unlevel playing field and undermine the EU 
mining industry competitiveness. Therefore, responsible mining should also be at the core of 
the EU international strategy on critical raw material, through binding commitments in 
bilateral FTAs or strategic partnerships.   
 
 Substitutability and circularity 
 
 Large French companies agree that substitutability and circularity should play a very 
significant part in reducing EU dependencies on foreign markets and should therefore be 
promoted again through regulatory and financial measures. 
 When it comes to the circularity agenda under the future CRMA, the impact assessment 
should clarify two important matters before a choice is made in terms of concrete measures 
proposed in the legislative initiative to come:  
 
o First of all, the European Commission should assess carefully the impact and benefits of a 
large array of options : in addition to the options outlined in the public consultation on-
line set of questions (recycling targets for waste operators, mandatory waste 
management scheme by Member States, design requirements, minimum content 
requirements, support to research and innovation), large French companies suggest also 
measuring the potential impact of time-limited export bans on waste and recycled 
material, at least for specific materials, in order to set up a robust domestic recycling 
industry and the expansion of important project of general economic interests (IPCEI). 
The Commission should further monitor flows of CRM generated during the industrial 
production process (“scrap”) or end-of-life products, in relation with the future digital 
passport provided for by the Eco-design Directive. In more details, an export ban should 
be explored when it cannot be evidenced that CRAM from scraps and end-of-life 
products will be re-used as secondary raw materials in the country of destination.  
 
o Secondly, as the European Commission has already launched the revision of the 
circularity package, the impact assessment should also verify whether circularity 
measures related to critical raw materials should be proposed under the future CRMA or 
under this upgraded package.  
 
 In any event, large French companies favour a gradual and proportionate approach in the roll-
out of these different supporting measures for circularity: design and minimum requirements

About AFEP  
 
Since 1982, AFEP brings together large companies operating in France. The Association, based in Paris and 
Brussels, aims to foster a business-friendly environment and to present the company members’ vision to French 
public authorities, European institutions and international organisations. Restoring business competitiveness to 
achieve growth and sustainable employment in Europe and tackle the challenges of globalisation is AFEP’s core 
priority. AFEP has around 111 members. More than 8 million people are employed by Afep companies and their 
annual combined turnover amounts to €2,600 billion.  
 
AFEP is involved in drafting cross-sectoral legislation, at French and European level, in the following areas: 
economy, taxation, company law and corporate governance, corporate finance and financial markets, 
competition, intellectual property and consumer affairs, labour law and social protection, environment and 
energy, corporate social responsibility and trade.

Summary 
Form Energy welcomes the European Commission’s Critical Raw Materials (CRM) initiative, which 
comes at a key interval  as the EU takes action  to advance the energy transition, strengthen  its 
strategic autonomy and ensure reliable, affordable energy supply. To reach t he “Fit for 55” goals 
with only existing technologies available at large commercial scale, EU demand for critical raw 
materials will i ncrease fivefold by 2030 1. We need to ensure a secure and sustainable supply of 
(critical) raw materials without increasing the E U's reliance on only a few third countries.  
 
Form Energy is commercializing and manufacturing a pioneering iron -air battery fo r the electric 
grid, capable of continuously discharging electricity for 100 hrs at system costs competitive with 
legacy power plants. This new class of multi -day energy storage enables a reliable and fully 
renewable electric grid , year-round, by relying on super-abundant, low -cost materials not listed in 
the EU’s 2020 Critical Raw Materials list2: iron, water  and air.  
 
Form’s iron-air battery technology is scalable, relying on diverse existing steel supply chains to 
serve gigawatts (GW) of electricity dema nd by 2030, while other novel energy storage 
technologies face uncertain supply chain constraints. Our iro n-air battery technology can help the 
EU achieve its Fit for 55 and REPowerEU goals while strengthening existing EU supply chains, 
supporting green st eel, and building new EU battery manufacturing capacity.  
 
Form Energy submits the following actionable recommendations for the CRM initiative: 
 
1. Prioritize materials, technologies and projects that reduce needs for CRMs, such as iron -
air batteries in electr ic grid applications 
2. Classify iron and iron ores as strategic materials, given their importance for long-duration 
energy storage and the production of green steel , and introduce mechanisms to support 
the expansion of an EU green steel supply chain

Priorities 
The EU should  prioritize technologies that reduce or avoid the need  for CRMs  under an 
“efficiency first” principle. Form Energy’s iron-air battery technology does this in two ways:  
 
1) lowers the overall amounts of new renewable energy and storage capacity needed to a chieve 
a reliable, renewable energy electric grid ( see below); 
2) supplies energy storage capacity from  low-cost, globally  abundant iron, which can be 
domestically sourced  in the EU. 
 
We therefore recommend the EU to pursue the development of a suite of po licies to support the 
growth of EU demand and supply of technologies such as iron -air batteries, as these reduce the 
need for CRMs while supporting existing EU industries that can be rapidly scaled to support new 
applications. 
 
To this end, we suggest the CRM Regulation classifies iron and iron ores as strategic material s, 
given their importance for the manufacturing of low -cost, long-duration energy storage solutions  
and the co-benefits the y provide in terms of establishing an EU green steel supply chain. 
Funding 
Support for expanded EU green steel supply chain 
To support the priorities set out abo ve, the CRM Regulation should introduce mechanisms to 
support the expansion of an EU green steel supply chain . This can include: 
 
1. Expanded iron mining in the EU  
2. Expanded iron processing in the EU, including via accelerated permitting for facilities that 
produce green DRI or supply the iron-air batteries for the electric grid  
3. Expanded green steel production 
4. Green hydrogen production for zero -carbon DRI production

Impact Assessment 
In parallel to the ongoing work on the critical raw materials initiative, we  recommend that the 
European Commission pursue a high -priority series of assessments to better understand how 
emerging forms of long -duration energy storage can support its Fit for 55, REPowerEU and 
strategic autonomy goals  and estimate the resources needed to achieve these goals . 
 
Iron-air multi-day energy storage systems fill a critical technology gap that is not addressed by 
other commercially available energy storage technologies for the electric grid. The benefits of 
iron-air batteries and other emerging forms of long and multi -day energy storage have yet to be 
modeled comprehensively by policymakers in the EU.  
 
We advise the Commission to analyse  how diverse types of emerging long -duration energy 
storage technologies, including those with durations of 100 hours or more, can support EU goals 
to decarbonize the electric sector.  
 
Scope 
 
This assessment should aim to identify optimal future resource needs and how they vary with and 
without emerging forms of long -duration energy storage. This analysis will help the Commission 
evaluate the extent to which emerging long -duration storage technologies can reduce needs for 
both generation and energy storage  technologies that rely significantly on CRMs.  
 
This assessment should consider optimal resource needs in climate -neutral scenarios under a 
range of weather conditions, including periodic heat waves, cold spells, renewable energy lulls, 
and weather-induced fossil fuel shortages. Importantly, the study should aim to determine how 
many GW of emerging non-lithium-ion energy storage technologies are part of optimal near -, mid- 
and long-term resource portfolios, and how long-duration storage can avoid dependence on fossil-
fueled power plants by delivering firm, zero -carbon energy supply.

Anticipated benefits 
Based on modeling of the California electric grid  that Form Energy has performed in partnership 
with the consulting firm E3 and with funding from the Califor nia Energy Commission, the benefits 
of diverse forms of long-duration energy storage can be significant. 3  
 
In California, for example, when modeling a zero -carbon electric grid, we found that the inclusion 
of emerging long -duration storage and multi-day storage technologies in zero carbon portfolios 
resulted in: 
● 13% lower overall resource costs  
● Avoidance of 92 GW of lithium -ion batteries and 17 GW of solar generation  
● 80% reduced renewable energy curtailment  
● 140,000 acres (approx. 567km 2) of avoided land-use impacts 
● A true zero-carbon grid at cost parity with current policy  
 
These results have borne out in numerous other studies we have performed bilaterally with 
utilities: the inclusion of low -cost multi -day storage lowers overall needs for renew able energy 
capacity, transmission capacity, and lithium -ion storage capacity, and helps achieve 
decarbonization goals at lower cost.  
 
We encourage the European Commission to support EU -wide, national, and utility -specific 
studies to understand the benefi ts that emerging long -duration storage technologies can 
provide.

Why we need low-cost, multi-day energy storage 
The electric grid now faces a challenge: how to manage the multi -day variability of renewable 
energy without sacrificing reliability or cost. To run the grid reliably and affordably, we need new 
cost-effective technologies capable of safely storing ele ctricity for multiple days. Iron -air 
batteries are the best solution to balance the multi-day variability of renewable energy due to their 
extremely low cost, safety, durability, and global scalability.

The basic principle of operation is 
reversible rusting: while discharging, 
the battery “breathes in” oxygen from 
the air and coverts iron metal to rust. 
While charging, the app lication of an 
electrical current converts the rust 
back to iron, and the battery “breathes 
out” oxygen.  
Advantages of Form’s iron-air 
battery for electric grid applications 
Form Energy’s modular battery design allows the system to be sited anywhere on the electric grid, 
without geological requirements. This flexibility means o ur siting decisions can balance 
environmental justice considerations and electric system benefits, including air emissions 
reductions and resiliency.  
 
● Low cost: The active components of Form’s iron -air battery are among the  world’s 
cheapest and most abundant materials : iron, water, and air. At global scale, iron -air 
batteries have the potential to deliver electricity at rated capacity for multiple days without 
recharging, with all-in system installed costs one -tenth that of lithium-ion (<US$20/kWh). 
● Scalability: Iron is one of the most globally abundant materials and is mined on every 
continent, enabling Form Energy to scale iron -air battery production to address global 
energy system decarbonization needs while also supporting energy and supply chain 
security. 
● Modularity & siting flexibility: Iron-air batteries are modular and can be sited anywhere on 
the grid and at the highest -value interconnection locations. In addition, t he geographic 
flexibility of iron-air batteries allows the technology to contribute to environmental justice 
and Just Transition goals through carefully selected siting to provide the greatest benefits 
to disadvantaged communities. Form’s system design applies the same principl es of low -
cost, modular architecture used in solar and lithium -ion projects today.

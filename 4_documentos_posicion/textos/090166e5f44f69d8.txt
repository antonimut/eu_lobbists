Public consultation on the European Critical 
Raw Materials Act  
- Boliden Response - 
 
 
Boliden support the European Commission´s increased efforts to handle climate 
change and reduce green house gas emissions through the new initiative the Critical 
Raw Materials Act. We welcome this opportunity to respond to the public consultation.  
The European Commission´s goal to transform Europe into the world´s first fossil-free 
continent relies highly on the supply of sustainable raw materials (critical & strategic). 
It is of great importance that the supply reflects the demand in order to develop the 
renewable energy technologies and low carbon industrial processes needed. Boliden´s 
view is that the transformation of Europe towards a fossil-free future, needs to be based 
on the development of better techniques and the incentive of profit.  
Boliden represents the non -ferrous mining and metals industry of Europe. We are a 
leading producer of sustainable non -ferrous metals (base metals, precious metals, 
critical raw materials) and our products enable the fossil-free transformation of society. 
Boliden´s value chain starts with exploration for new mineral deposits and then extends 
through our company all the way to finished products of metal and recycling  of 
electronic waste (e-scrap). As an owner of one of the world´s biggest recycling facilities 
of metal from electronic materials (copper smelter Rönnskär in Sweden) we also know 
that it´s not possible to relie on recycl ed material alone to fill Europe´s demand for 
metals. Alongside recycling, primary sustainable production from mines in Europe will 
be needed for many more years to come in order to battle climate change.  
We take long-term responsibility in order to limit our impact on nature and the climate 
while producing metals with the best av ailable technique with a much lower carbon 
footprint than the global average. Despite this we face many challenges on a local and 
global scale.

To meet the increased supply demand of critical (& strategic) raw materials in Europe 
and produce the metals requi red for the green transition , the European mining and 
metals industry need: 
• A European raw materials legislation with a focus on strategic raw materials 
rather than critical to decrease our dependency on imports 
 
• Predictable and efficient permitting procedures on a national level  
 
• Strengthen support for and investments in exploration and responsible mining 
activities 
 
• Access to fossil-free energy at competitive prices  
 
• Increased awarenes of and approval for our industry  
 
• An EU with higher collection rates of electronic waste (e-scrap)  
 
• Measures to stop illegal exports of electronic waste outside Europe   
• Time and regulatory stability  to implement the necessary low -carbon 
technologies.  
 
• Access to skilled work force 
 
 
A focus on strategic raw materials is needed to decrease 
Europe´s dependency on imports 
 
In the new European raw materials legislation, Boliden would like to encourage the 
European Commission to focus on strategic raw materials instead of critical raw 
materials as a complement to the Critical Raw Materials List.  
 
Raw materials that today are deemed critical on the European Commission ´s fourth 
list of Critical Raw Materials for the EU ( COM (2020) 474 final)  often occur in small 
quantities in the bedrock. They are usually  discovered and mined as by -products to 
minerals that are more common  in th e earth ´s crust. As an example, Boliden´s 
Kankberg mine in the Skellefte  district in northern  Sweden is worth mentioning. 
Kankberg is a gold mine that was discovered through exploration activities for gold. It 
was only after the discovery was made that geochemical analyses showed economic 
grades of tellurium in the ore body . Therefore, Boliden made the necessary 
investments for a new leaching plant so  also Tellurium could be mined and sold as a 
product. Tellurium should have been on the critical raw materi als list without this 
discovery. Tellurium from Kankberg is used to create solar panels. Since many of the 
minerals needed for new technology are only requested in small quantities, one new 
discovery of a mineral deposit  can make all the difference for Europe’s domestic 
supply.

On the other hand, minerals that are more common in the earth´s crust and are mined 
in larger quantities like copper, could also be essential for the development of the green 
technologies. Therefore, a mineral like copper could also be considered critical in the 
near future simply due to the huge demand. 
 
To conclude, the request of raw materials changes all the time as a direct consequence 
of the development of the renewable energy technologies. Europe should not focus on 
raw materials deemed critical today, instead we need to focus on strategic minerals for 
the future. 
 
Europe needs increased  mineral exploration in order to 
accelerate domestic metals production 
 
Mineral deposits of economic importance are hard to find and they take a long time to 
develop (on average ~10-20 years f rom a new discovery a new mining operation ). 
When a deposit is discovered and a mine is developed, there will always be competing 
interests. Many of these competing interests are protected under EU law, especially 
conservation interests. Environmental legislation often allows for some derogations 
and a balancing of different public interests against each other, but in some cases the 
protection is absolute, and it is not possible to make derogations. Such absolute 
environmental protections can be particularly problematic for mining, since mineral 
deposits, unlike most other industries, cannot be relocated. If a mineral deposit is 
located right below a body of water or a protected habitat, the mining company cannot 
choose an alternative place for their industrial establishment. 
Boliden´s experience s from Sweden and Finland is that permitting procedures are 
clear but to many authorities are involved in the process. Local authorities has to much 
responsibilities and interpret the law in different ways. Therefore companies are often 
requested to provide additional information during the permitting process, which leads 
to delays.  Today´s focus in society to protect and preserv e acts hindering for 
exploration activites an d sustainable mining projects  important for Eu rope. A reason 
for this is that  the mining industry seldom is prioritized by authorities/government if 
there are competing interests. This means that  projects are bounced around in the 
legal system for several years.  
In order to increase the  domestic supply of raw materials for industries in Europe, 
Boliden belives that certain parts of the EU legislation that regulate the use of land and 
water need to be improved. Member States need to make general adjustments in the 
national permitting processes to make them more predictable, streamlined and 
efficient.

Access to fossil-free energy at competitive prices  
 
During the past decennia, the mining and metals industry in Europe has made a big 
switch of its energy usage from fossil fuel to electricity. Therefore, it has become a very 
electricity intensive industry, and electricity usage is expected to continue to increase 
significantly during the next few years. Consequently, access to fossil -free energy at 
competitive prices is a key for the industry to be competitive in a global market.  
Given the energy intensity of the mining processes, the industry has a natural interest 
to become as energy efficient as possible. In this context, we believe  that past efforts 
should be considered when assessing compliance with new conditionality 
requirements, to make sure that conditionality measures do not penalize companies 
that have already invested in the best available technologies. Boliden’s operations run 
on a high degree of fossil-free electricity. Due to the marginal cost pricing of electricity, 
European industry still pays the same price as electricity generated from coal, natural 
gas and oil, even if the electricity is fossil free. Our industry is unable to pass on these 
extra costs to our customers, which creates a competitive disadvantage for European 
industry compared to producers outside Europe. We need regulatory changes in 
electricity market design to ensure investments int o sustainability and expansion of 
production. Also to ensure long -term investment for raw materials in Europe and to 
keep supply chains in the Single Market, certain state aid provisions will be necessary 
to be able to model OPEX within a reasonable margin  of fluctuation to be competitive 
on and in global markets. This pertains in particular indirect CO2 cost compensation.  
 
Increased awarnes of and approval for our industry  
 
All industry has some impact on the surrounding environment, but modern industry has 
much less impact than previously . At Boliden we know that it is possible to produce 
sustainable metals at the same time as we do our very best to protect the ones effected 
by our business. 
The knowledge of the mining and metals industry is often low among the different 
stakeholders that are affected by our operations. More information on all levels in the 
EU is nessecary in order to inform people of the role of metals and minerals in the 
green transition. This is something we work with continuously , but Boliden would like 
to encourage the European Commission to join us and do much more.

Recycling & Stockpiling 
The new legislation will improve recycling in general and gain better access to strategic 
metals and materials for a sustainable future. Europe should take care of its own 
generated waste and recover valuable metals. Measures to stop illegal exports of 
waste like electronics, battery, end of life vehicles outside the EU are also needed.  
Transport of waste within the EU, rather than outside the EU, for recycling should be 
encouraged and its administration should be supported digitally rather than the current 
physical documents that create multiple regulatory problems. Producers of Electric and 
Electronic Equipment should publish the chemical content of their products and design 
for recycling. This should lead to higher recycling rates of WEEE. (i.e. t oday circuit 
boards contain flame retardents that reduce the recovery of base and precious metals 
at copper smelters).   
Stockpiling on national and/or EU level is a strategic decision depending on the liquidity 
in the market. As an example, Russian Norilsk Nickel has 40% of the palladium market 
hence there is obviously a risk for market disruption. Stockpiling could serve to dampen 
the impact on the market in the short term.  
Boliden, as many European companies, is a small player globally and the amo unt of 
capital needed for stockpiling is considerable if we are to stock relevant quantities. It 
would not be an viable business option, even with financial incentives. Therefore 
Boliden belives that the responsibility for stockpiling needs to be at national and/or EU 
level. It is important that the obligation does not land at the company level.

New climate targets 
Seen from a global climate perspective, Boliden has a very competitive position. As 
a result, two special products, Low-Carbon Copper and Low-Carbon Zinc, have 
been successfully launched. Boliden has now decided to raise the bar further with 
sharpened carbon dioxide emissions goals to be achieved by 2030. The new target 
is for actual CO2 emissions to be 40 percent lower by 2030, compared to 2021 
(Scope 1 and 2). 
 
“In many respects, we are driving developments toward fossil-free base metal 
production. The new target will create further momentum for positive changes in 
both Boliden and the industry as a whole. It will provide a long-term boost to 
competitiveness," says Mikael Staffas,” President and CEO of Boliden. 
  
In parallel with its own climate efforts, Boliden has also decided on climate 
performance goals at the supplier level. Emissions must be 30 percent lower at the 
supplier level by 2030 compared to 2021 (Scope 3). New product intensity targets 
for copper and zinc for the total production are set at the limit values for Low-
Carbon Copper, 1.5 metric tonnes CO2e/t Cu, and 1.0 metric tonnes CO2e/t Zn for 
Low-Carbon Zinc. Boliden has applied to join the Science Based Target initiative in 
conjunction with its decision on new climate targets.

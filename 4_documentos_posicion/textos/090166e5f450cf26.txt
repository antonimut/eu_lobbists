Offshore Norway's input to the European Commission's public 
consultation on the forthcoming European Critical Raw Materials Act.  
 
Offshore Norway welcomes the EU Commission's proposal to establish a Critical Raw Materials Act 
and its aim to increase and diversify the EU's supply of critical raw materials. In this regard, when 
identifying strategic projects in the EU that have the potential to help secure the EUs diversified access 
to raw materials and have strong environmental and social performance, Offshore Norway would like 
to draw the Commission's attention to the potential of deep-sea mineral extraction on the Norwegian 
continental shelf (NCS).  
Having identified mineral deposits consisting of metals such as led, zinc, copper, gold, silver, 
manganese, iron, titanium, cobalt, nickel, cerium, zirconium and certain REEs, the resources on the 
NCS represent a large opportunity to the EU's overarching goal of increased raw material security. 
Furthermore, deep sea mineral deposits often have a higher metal concentration compared to land-
based deposits. This paves the way for a less area intensive operations with the potential for lower 
overall environmental impacts. Both in terms of ecosystem disturbance and CO2 intensity.  
There is an ongoing opening process for areas with regards to the exploration and extraction of deep 
sea minerals on the NCS. Norway's Deep Sea Minerals Act entered into force in 2019 and stipulates 
the requirements for such an opening process, whereby an environmental impact assessment is one 
such condition. The aim of the assessment is to shed light on the potential impacts an opening of areas 
may have on the environment, and presumed commercial, economic, and social factors. The 
Norwegian government has recently published this impact assessment as a step towards future 
activity on the NCS. Overall conclusions include:  
 The impact assessment sheds light on and identifies impacts associated with exploration, 
extraction, and cessation of the activities. 
 Activities associated with exploration and cessation has generally been found to result in 
minor environmental impacts. 
 The potential impacts from extraction activity will depend on the technical solution, including 
relevant mitigation measures. 
 Norwegian technology communities in both the petroleum, maritime, processing and mining 
sectors have opportunities to provide the required competence and expertise. 
 Seabed mineral activities have the potential to become an entirely new industry.  
A final decision regarding the opening of areas on the NCS for mineral activities will be made by 
parliament (Storting) in spring 2023. Offshore Norway and industry actors acknowledge the need for 
a stepwise approach to mineral activities on the NCS. We also recognise the knowledge gaps that exist 
with regards to, among other things, the full environmental impacts of deep-sea mineral extraction.

The key next step for further knowledge gathering in this regard would be the opening of areas 
offshore for exploration under the existing legal framework. If a commercially attractive mineral-
deposit is discovered, a new area-specific impact assessment must be established and approved by 
the government before extraction can take place.  Norwegian industry actors have long traditions and 
a proven track record with regards to operating in challenging conditions with a strong focus on 
personnel safety and high environmental and technical standards. This will undoubtedly also apply to 
deep sea mineral activities as governance in this area has been developed over decades of offshore 
operations.  
With this in mind, Offshore Norway therefore encourages the commission to state the role that deep 
sea mineral exploration and extraction on the NCS can have in securing critical raw materials to the 
EU market, enabling the digital and green shift. 
 
About Offshore Norway 
Offshore Norway in an employer and industry organization for companies with activities related to the 
Norwegian continental shelf. Member companies' activities range from oil and gas to offshore wind, 
carbon capture and storage and the future exploration and extraction of deep-sea minerals.

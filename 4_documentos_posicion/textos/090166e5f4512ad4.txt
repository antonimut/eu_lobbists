2) Challenges for securing sustainable supply of critical raw materials 
In the current process of ecological transition and change in the global economic paradigm, an in -
depth review of all economic activit ies, particularly those related to the extraction of the planet's 
mineral resources, is required to ensure their social, environmental, and economic sustainability, 
while promoting a sufficient degree of strategic autonomy that does not make European indus trial 
production dependent on third parties. 
This transition is taking place in a complex international context, with challenges that were 
unthinkable just a few years ago. The COVID -19 pandemic crisis has revealed vulnerabilities that 
threaten national security and citizens' well-being, such as reliance on foreign supplies and the fragility 
of international trade flows. Furthermore, the current geopolitical context, triggered by Russia's 
aggression, is marked by significant instability and increased strategic competition.  
It should be noted in this context that the mineral deposits of strategic raw materials for a 
decarbonized and electrified economy are distributed globally. It is therefore critical to ensure their 
production in a sustainable and efficien t manner, while recognizing the benefits and employment 
potential of the green economy for both developed and developing countries, where raw material 
extraction and processing could become key activities to support human rights, communities of 
interest, and improve governance. 
The current situation is critical for the EU and its member countries. As the EU strives to achieve 
climate neutrality by 2050, the current situation necessitates a thorough rethinking of how we ensure 
the security of our energy supply, as well as the development of a stronger economic foundation. This 
entails reducing our strategic reliance in the most vulnerable areas, such as key raw materials, through 
strategic partnerships, exploring the building up of strategic reserves , and pro moting the circular 
economy and resource efficiency. 
The convergence of supply and demand factors, the early development of circular economy 
technologies, and limited reuse projections all come together in an economically recovering from 
COVID and geopolitically unstable environment. Key raw materials will continue to play an important 
role in the economy and industry under these conditions.

The key now is to capitalise this context to boost industrial sectors that ensure essential resources, 
promote technological sovereignty, increase the resilience of the EU economy, and strengthen the 
EU's position as a global strategic player. 
For all these reasons, we at Cosentino consider that the drafting of this Regulation is a crucial step  to 
strengthen European sov ereignty, reduce our reliance, and to design a new growth and investment 
model in terms of critical raw materials. 
However, we believe that its approach should address the main issues listed below: 
The necessity of broadening the dimension from critical to strategic Mineral Raw Materials and to 
identify Strategic Mineral Raw Materials as complement to the Critical Raw Materials List. 
There is no doubt about the relevance of Critical Mineral Raw Materials, which are required for specific 
aspects of new technological developments. However, huge quantities of most Mineral Raw Materials, 
not just the Critical Materials, will be required to meet the energy and digital transitions on schedule  
as well as to satisfy the necessities of current economic key industrial sectors in Europe. 
For example, t he mineral raw materials that ensure the supply chain in the manufacture of high -
performance/high-quality products for construction are essential for the maintenance of a truly 
competitive industrial capacity in Spain and in Europe.  
These raw materials are of a priority nature because of: 
• The high procurement consumption by the manufacturing industries of high value -added 
industrial minerals for the construction industry. 
• The economic and social development in t he areas of research, processing and treatment of 
minerals (in terms of jobs, repopulation, rural development, etc.) 
• The risk of supply in high quantities and high technical performances due to the extra -EU 
country risk. 
These mineral raw materials are als o necessary to be able to carry out the extensive renovation and 
improvement of energy efficiency in buildings that the EU is seeking (EU renovation wave), without 
sacrificing something that has proven to be crucial for citizens, such as the provision of a modern and 
adequate number of hospitals, health centres, and other care facilities. 
In this regard, the EU methodology for defining Critical Raw Materials  may overlook materials of 
strategic importance and necessity for EU industry, as well as materials t hat may become critical in 
the future as a result of the sustainable and digital transition, as well as from a security policy 
standpoint. As a result, a broader definition of strategic materials defined at an EU or Member State 
level should supplement the  current definition to avoid shifting bottlenecks from one material to 
another. Special regulations and support programmes should also be established for Strategic Raw 
Materials.  
Include public support and funding for R&D in all phases of the mining project. 
Innovation plays an essential role in the sustainable management of mineral raw materials, security 
of supply and competitiveness of the sector. New technological solutions in the field of substitution 
and recycling allow, for example, to diversify sources of supply, while the development of innovative 
 
 
 
   
 
solutions and techniques in areas such as prospecting, exploration, extraction and processing allow 
access to resources in a more sustainable way.  
It is therefore essential to support research and development in all processes that make up the value 
chain of the mineral raw materials industry, with a particular focus on technological improvements in 
prospecting, exploration and evaluation of mining projects, which in many cases are excluded from 
public R&D funding and subsidies or suffer from many administrative hurdles in their implementation 
that make them unfeasible but remain crucial processes to ensure the viability and the environmental 
and social sustainability of the mining project. 
3) Possible measures to ensure a secure and sustainable supply of critical raw materials on 
which we agree 
• Favour local supply. 
• Build up a long-term Eu Pipeline to future primary production. 
• Fostering the license to operate for mining & quarrying (legal and political). 
• Ensure an agile, rational, clear, coordinated, digitalised, integrated administrative procedure, 
time-limited, in which the legislation has a clear and adequate structure, the competent 
authority is determined and application of simplification mechanisms or tools such as positive 
administrative silence, responsible declarations or prior communications is favoured. 
• Identified strategic projects should benefit from better access to investment and improved 
permitting procedures. 
• Absolute need to ex pand the scope from critical to strategic Mineral Raw Materials and to 
identify Strategic Mineral Raw Materials as complement to the Critical Raw Materials List. 
• Reasonable balance between the protection of the environment and biodiversity and 
extractive activity, clearly establishing and ensuring compatibility between the industry and 
Natura 2000 Network other protected areas. 
• Provisions related to Circular Economy and industrial symbiosis in the mining & quarrying field 
and regulation, mainly in the area of waste utilisation. 
• There is also a general need to progressively fill gaps in the geological knowledge of Mineral 
Raw Materials deposits in the Member States. 
• The EU’s Raw Materials Act should be integrated into future reviews of EU environmental 
legislation. 
• Provisions stipulating that extractive activity cannot be excluded a priori or in a generic 
manner from municipal, regional and supra-regional urban plans must be assessed. 
• For better governance, a Network of Raw Mineral Materials Agencies in all the EU 27 can be 
implemented.

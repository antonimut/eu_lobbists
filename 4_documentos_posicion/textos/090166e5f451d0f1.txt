Introduction  Savannah Resources Plc (‘Savannah’) is the owner of the Barroso Lithium Project in Northern Portugal which contains the most significant spodumene lithium resource in Western Europe. Following a positive Scoping Study which outlined a conventional operation producing 175,000t of spodumene concentrate per annum, Savannah is progressing the development and environmental licencing of the Barroso Lithium Project.   The Company is listed and regulated on AIM and the Company’s ordinary shares are also available on the Quotation Board of the Frankfurt Stock Exchange (FWB) under the symbol FWB: SAV, and the Börse Stuttgart (SWB) under the ticker ‘SAV’.  As a member of ERMA, EIT Raw Materials, EIT Innoenergy and Euromines, we are an important participant in the development of the European battery value chain.  As the owner of Europe’s largest known spodumene lithium resource, the Barroso Lithium Project is a key European critical raw material development and of clear strategic importance to the EU.  It is vital that the EU recognises the enormous raw material opportunities that exist within Europe that can and should contribute to, and underwrite, the EU’s objectives of the Green Deal, RePower, and its strategic autonomy.     Within the worldwide push for energy transition and decarbonisation is unprecedent global competition for the Critical Raw Materials (“CRMs”) which are the fundamental enablers of this change.  Other nations have been quick to recognise this risk and opportunity and invested heavily in state of the art mineral developments and processing facilities. Europe also has a rich and diverse mineral endowment including CRMs, however, it has to date failed to support this historically important sector that is essential for our future.  Decades of underinvestment has seen Europe share of global mining fall to just 5% in 20201.  This has exacerbated the critical raw material crisis we are now facing.  By not supporting the raw material pipeline of exploration, only 9% of the EU’s present raw material demand can presently be met from known domestic sources.  In September 2020, the European Commission added lithium to its Critical Raw Materials List and estimated the bloc’s demand for lithium would grow 18 times by 2030 and 60 times by 2050. Despite a path to creating a circular economy with expanded recycling capacities, and European production as well as refinement capacities, these will not be sufficient to guarantee a stable supply of CRMs for EU industries.  It is therefore crucial that Europe recognise the need for a significant and fundamental shift in its stance towards domestic CRM supply.  This will require not only acknowledging which parts of the value chain it has failed to invest in and foster, but also to recognise that these measures must at least be commensurate in scale and velocity with those in competing regions in order to secure the stability of the European economy.  As a company that has worked steadily for a number of years on developing an asset in Europe, we believe our experience and insight is particularly relevant in highlighting opportunities for improvement and areas were support should be targeted to ensure the competitiveness of Europe’s own CRM sector.   1 https://energypost.eu/critical-raw-materials-for-the-energy-transition-europe-must-start-mining-again/

Recommendations  To date, EU initiatives and investment support have disproportionately focussed on downstream segments of the supply chain with shorter development timeframes.  While we expect the CRM Act will address the full value chain, we wish to emphasise the need for support of primary raw material production, and with special consideration for domestic projects.  If the CRM Act is to be competitive with government support in other countries and ensure a level playing field, this requires a suite of policies and programs across the development cycle to ensure a steady and secure stream of raw materials.    Our recommendations may be summarised as follows, with benchmark examples of similar initiatives elsewhere:  Administrative processes and governance  • Creation of a Raw Materials Agency / Office.  Operating as a coordination point and interface between Member States, Sponsors and the relevant organs of the EU, an EU Raw Materials Agency should be tasked with facilitating and promoting development of raw material and mining projects based on deposits within the EU.  It should serve as a centre of expertise and information provider to the Commission and its member states about prerequisites for developing and fostering resource projects, handling potentially conflicting policy objectives, and applying safeguarding measures to take into account when designing legislation and policies on issues that could jeopardise the ability to secure a sustainable and responsible supply of raw materials from EU sources.  Further, the Agency should be mandated to promote and inform the public about the EU mining industry, and to ensure that Member State permitting processes adhere to current best practice and are efficient and operate effectively. Examples of dedicated national agencies focussed on some of these themes are: o Australia’s Critical Materials Office o The German Mineral Resources Agency (DERA)  • Within the Raw Materials Agency, the creation of an EU Strategic Raw Materials Project list.  Similar to the EU’s Projects of Common Interest (PCIs) list for interconnecting energy system infrastructure, qualifying projects would be reviewed and updated at regular intervals and include sustainability criteria in the assessment process.  EU Strategic Raw Materials Project designation would allow for: o Accelerated permitting processes  o Priority eligibility for specific CRM funding facilities o Dedicated project manager support as a single point of contact o Dedicated compensation, retraining, and other support schemes for stakeholders negatively impacted by Strategic Raw Material project developments  Early stage development:  Exploration, scoping studies, pre-feasibilty study phases  • Public funding for the support and ongoing development of a pan-European database of geoscience data. In June 2022, the US Department of State established the Minerals Security Partnership (MSP) with a group of international partners including Finland, France, Germany and Sweden. The MSP is intended to ‘help catalyse investment from governments and the private sector for strategic opportunities – across the full value chain – that adhere to the highest environmental, social, and governance standards.’  The European Commission could consider match funding any governmental investment secured under this programme from a EU member state Government, or for a project or business based in an EU member state country.

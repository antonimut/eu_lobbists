1 In General 
The Association of the Automotive Industry (VDA) represents more than 600 companies in 
the automotive industry such as manufacturers of motor vehicles and their engines, set-ups, 
installations, containers and vehicle parts and equipment with production in Germany. In 
2021, the automotive industry has a share in overall turnover of manufacturing industry in 
Germany of about 22 percent and generated a turnover of more than 410 billion euros. Ap-
proximately 786,000 employees manufactured about 3.1 million cars in Germany. This does 
not include commercial vehicles (trucks and buses) produced by our member companies. To-
gether the industry researches and produces for a clean, safe, and sustainable mobility of the 
future. So far, 20 percent of German passenger car production are already electric vehicles. 
2 The transition to e-mobility needs primary supply 
of critical Raw Materials 
Under the Paris Agreement, many nations have committed to the goal of limiting global warm-
ing to well below 2 degrees Celsius above pre-industrial levels. If possible, global warming 
should even be limited to 1.5 degrees Celsius. The German automotive industry is clearly 
committed to this goal.  
 
In this context, e-mobility is the cornerstone of the automotive industry's decarbonization 
strategy. To achieve the ambitious goals of ramping up e-mobility, a critical amount of battery 
raw materials, such as lithium, nickel sulphate, graphite, manganese and cobalt, but also rare 
earths, such as neodymium, praseodymium, dysprosium and terbium for the permanent mag-
nets in the traction motors are needed. In this context, the automotive industry is heavily de-
pendent on imports from non-EU countries. To achieve better diversification and long-term 
stable supply of CRMs needed for the decarbonization of the transport sector, a reorientation 
of the European raw materials strategy is indispensable. Other major world regions are ac-
tively onshoring respective value chains to support their industries. Europe is at risk of being 
left behind. Therefore, the German Automotive Industry fully supports the goals of the Euro-
pean Critical Raw Material Act.

3 Identified challenges and proposed fields of ac-
tion of the European Critical Raw Material Act 
need to be adapted 
High energy costs cause problems for refining industry  
The challenges identified in the european dependence on imported raw materials and unused 
potential in the European Union (EU) miss mention of the immensely high by international 
standards energy prices in Europe. Even before the outbreak of the Russian war in Ukraine, 
smelting in the European Union was declining due to high operating costs. To improve the 
competitiveness of the raw material processing industry in Europe, this challenge must be 
clearly addressed. 
 
The list of Critical Raw Materials should be shortened to strategic critical raw materials 
The list of critical raw materials (CRM) should help stimulate the production of CRMs by en-
hancing new mining, refining and recycling activities in the EU. However, some CRMs are 
needed more than others. As of today, the list includes 30 critical raw materials and does not 
guide investments strategically.  
 
For this reason, the list should be limited to strategic critical raw materials necessary to fulfill 
the goals of the European Green Deal, which are: 
 battery raw materials 
o lithium 
o nickelsulphate 
o manganese 
o cobalt  
o graphite 
 rare earths for permanent magnets  
o neodymium 
o dysprosium 
o terbium  
 raw materials for the hydrogen production value chain  
o iridium  
o scandium.  
 
The list should be flexible and reviewed at least every five years.  
 
Raw material procurement should also include raw material refining and recycling 
The automotive industry needs highly pure materials for its production (e. g. battery-grade for 
battery production). Europe is highly dependent in the procurement of refined materials as it 
is in the procurement of raw materials. Therefore, every political measure to support the raw 
materials supply chain should also address raw materials refining and recycling to reduce de-
pendencies and building up a closed European battery production chain. 
 
Centralized storage of strategic raw materials is the wrong strategy 
The VDA rejects state stockpiling of strategic raw materials. Central stockpiling only puts ad-
ditional pressure on the supply, which is already scarce. Moreover, the storage of critical raw 
materials and their refinements is expensive. Instead of investing in storage, the investment 
should be provided to expanding the supply of critical raw materials.

Local content requirements lead to a further narrowing of the market 
Setting EU targets for capacity expansion at different stages of the value chain can only work 
if the potentials in the European value chain have been identified beforehand. Those targets 
must not be implemented as local content requirements for batteries, as this leads to a nar-
rowing of the market and higher cost for customers.  
 
Changing the global commodity market requires large-scale stimulus from political in-
stitutions 
The proposed field of action to strengthen the EU's critical raw material value chain does not 
go far enough. In addition to identifying projects, facilitating permitting procedures, and simpli-
fying financing, the economic environment in the EU must be improved, e. g. by reducing en-
ergy taxes. It needs large investments and subsidies to compete globally, a strategy which 
we also see in other regions of the world.  
 
Raw material procurement is not only promoted by an ECRM Act 
The international exchange in the envisaged network for the rapid exchange of information is 
welcomed by the German automotive industry. However, priority of the ECRM Act should be 
to ensure sustainable sourcing, refining, and recycling of raw materials. Other policies, such 
as REACH, IED and battery regulation, also have a major impact on the procurement of raw 
materials and its intermediates. These regulations already intervene restrictively in the mar-
ket.     
 
In guaranteeing a level playing field, it is very important not to create immense amounts of 
bureaucracy. Especially for SMEs, the creation of more transparency and the proposed prod-
uct passports must not result in an additional burden. The announced product passports must 
not lead to uncertainties in the industry until they are introduced. 
 
Closing material loops is not a lifeline 
Recycled materials will not be able to cover the demand for raw materials during the market 
ramp-up of e-mobility. At the beginning of the next decade, recycling will only be able to meet 
a single-digit percentage of demand. For this reason, the automotive industry continues to 
rely on primary raw materials from Europe and abroad. 
 
Promoting existing ESG-standards for mining and refining 
The automotive industry is leading by example when it comes to making supply chains more 
sustainable, robust and transparent. In various initiatives and projects, the automotive indus-
try are committed to greater transparency, respect for human rights and the sustainable ex-
traction of raw materials. Therefore, the ECRMA should support: 
 the Initiative for Responsible Mining Assurance (IRMA) as the recommended industry 
standard for mining, 
 the Responsible Mineral Initiative (RMI) as the recommended industry standard for 
processing companies and 
 the Responsible Supply Chain Initiative (RSCI) as industry standard to monitor the 
supply chain on social compliance and sustainability parameters.

4 Strengthening the three pillars of sustainable raw 
material  
It is the automotive industry's claim to operate sustainably and freely, to fulfil its due diligence, 
to transform its production and to contribute to maintaining prosperity in Germany and Eu-
rope. In order not to slow down the ramp-up of e-mobility and thus jeopardize the achieve-
ment of climate protection goals, a coordinated and updated raw materials strategy at Euro-
pean level and a more active external raw materials policy are urgently needed. 
 
The European strategy for raw materials should be based on the three pillars of sustainable 
raw materials procurement: procure raw materials from abroad, promoting domestic raw ma-
terials extraction and develop the circular economy.  
 
The European Raw Materials Act can be strengthened by the following instruments: 
 
Raw materials from abroad 1. Expand existing raw material partnerships with raw material-rich third 
countries 
In order to support the automotive industry in securing raw materials abroad, 
energy and raw material partnerships and local government support are 
needed now more urgently than ever. This includes the expansion of the raw 
materials competence centres (e. g. German Mining Network) to support the 
establishment of local networks, the initiation of business relationships and the 
risk hedging of projects through a federal agency to be created for strategic 
mining projects. 
 
2. Accelerate negotiation and ratification of free trade agreements  
Securing free trade in raw materials and energy should be an integral part of 
the EU's free trade agreements. Path-breaking agreements already being ne-
gotiated are with Canada, Chile, Mexico and Indonesia. Other important agree-
ments are on the EU's agenda, such as with India, Australia and other ASEAN 
states as well as Mercosur.  
 
The VDA reiterates its call for swift ratification of the CETA agreement with our 
partner Canada; Germany and the EU must send clear signals that trade 
agreements with the EU remain possible and are supported by political will. 
Cooperation with USA on the development of a sustainable raw materials 
value chain must also be intensified, for example within the framework of the 
Minerals Security Partnership. Furthermore, relations with China will continue 
to play an important role.. 
 
3. Establish a European agency for strategic mining projects and a Euro-
pean raw material fund 
Mining projects are highly capital intensive. Before information on the concen-
tration of ores from new projects is known, enormous amounts of invest are 
needed for feasibility studies. To promote and support projects at an early 
stage, the automotive industry recommends the establishment of a European 
agency for strategic mining projects. This agency is to discover and develop 
mining projects for strategic raw materials. It should also invest directly in the 
projects in an early-stage with venture capital out of the proposed European 
raw material fund. The EU should be a shareholder in this agency. JOGMEC in 
Japan or the Federal Agency for Leap Innovations (Sprin-D) in Germany can 
serve as a model. Especially in difficult economic times, a monetary

raw materials extractions 4. Improve the location conditions for the refining industry in Europe 
Europe is the only region in the world where mining has declined over the last 
20 years. This is partly due to high by international standards energy prices 
and the social acceptance of mining. For mining, but also smelting and refining 
of raw materials to expand in Europe, the conditions in the EU must be im-
proved. Permitting needs to be unbureaucratic and swift for companies to pro-
duce and remain competitive. Therefore, a modern permitting process is key to 
achieve the European climate targets.  
 
Circular Economy 5. Intensify the support for the circular economy in the transformation 
towards recycling of electric vehicles 
The automotive industry and the circular economy are two strong sectors that 
are working together in the sense of shared responsibility on the market-based 
development of recycling systems for current and future generations of vehi-
cles.  
The fundamental transformation and innovation currently taking place in the 
automotive industry must also be continued by the players in the circular econ-
omy. This includes, for example, the trained handling of high-voltage batteries, 
but also additional investments in dismantling and recycling capacities for bat-
tery raw materials. 
To increase the amount of high-quality secondary raw materials available in the 
market, ERMA may provide incentives for projects or entities capable of recy-
cling key materials to encourage recyclers to build up more capacity. 
 
6. Continue and expand the support for research and development 
To increase the proportion of high-quality secondary raw materials, the availa-
ble recycling technologies are crucial. These technologies generally follow the 
product specifications. Recycling processes for lithium-ion-based traction bat-
teries are becoming increasingly established but are not yet technologically so-
phisticated. Technological development can therefore continue to be supported 
by specific research and development programs of the European Union. The 
same also applies to the development of high-quality, resource-saving and re-
cyclable materials and material designs, the closing of material cycles in pro-
duction, and the digitalization and automation of end-of-life vehicle dismantling 
and recycling processes. 
 
7. Counter illegal disposal of end-of-life vehicles and strengthen certified 
recycling companies 
In order to prevent illegal practices in the handling of end-of-life vehicles, the 
final deregistration of a vehicle for the purpose of recovery may only take place 
through the mandatory presentation of a certificate of destruction. Only dis-
mantling companies that are licensed by the authorities and approved under 
waste management law are authorized to issue this certificate of destruction. It 
should be possible to transmit this digitally to the vehicle registration system. In 
support of this, a system should be set up that obliges the owner of a vehicle to 
continue paying an insurance fee and / or tax as long as no clear proof of the 
sale, export, theft of the vehicle or a certificate of destruction is presented. In 
addition, the competent authorities should cooperate European wide and be 
equipped with the appropriate powers to identify, sanction and, if necessary, 
close down the businesses that are operating illegally.

8. Ensure environmentally compatible export of vehicles 
Regarding trade, the European customs authorities should be able to stop the 
export of potentially environmentally hazardous used cars. To this end, the au-
thorities should have a catalog of criteria to hand to aid in decision-making, 
which takes account of the interests of the exporting and the importing coun-
tries, too. The Swiss system could serve as a model. 
 
9. Collect raw CO2 data on secondary raw materials and materials 
Life cycle analyses are used in the automotive industry as a central managing 
tool for reducing the CO2 footprint. The use of secondary raw materials and 
materials is automatically promoted if this leads to an improvement in the over-
all balance of a vehicle.  
To ensure that numbers on the CO2-footprint are globally used and comparable 
the continuous development of the generally accepted methodology for calcu-
lating the CO2 footprint (ISO 14040) is as important for high-quality life cycle 
assessments as the collection of raw data rather than the use of generic data . 
Only in this way CO2 reduction measures in the supply chain can be reflected 
in the end product. This also applies to the use of secondary raw materials and 
materials. 
Not worth mentioning, that there is a need to balance between transparency 
and confidentially. We should be careful when deciding the level as to what ex-
tent the manufacturers are required to report their supply chain information. 
Sharing of such information should only on a need-to-know basis.

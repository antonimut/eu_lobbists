CECIMO’s Contribution: Public Consultation on Critical Raw Materials Act 
 
November 2022 
 
CECIMO welcomes the announced new legislative proposal, the Critical Raw Material Act, to build 
competitive and resilient EU industries and thus shape the digital future and green transition. The 
COVID-19 pandemic and Russia -Ukraine war caused severe suppl y chain disruptions to global 
markets caused and exposed vulnerabilities to the security of the supply of raw materials critical for 
industrial production Across all industrial sectors, there is a significant shortage of different 
materials/raw materials and components, which disrupts production growth. In order to contribute 
to strengthening the EU supply chains, CECIMO would like to highlight the current concerns regarding 
our two sectors: Machine Tools and Additive Manufacturing, based on the survey and analysis we 
conducted.  
 
Key Takeaways for Policymakers: 
• Considering future reports and analyses of critical (raw) materials, we propose a more 
sector-specific approach that would include more details about main economic sectors 
such as machinery and equipment. 
• We want to highlight the processed materials used largely in different sectors such as 
Machine Tools, which are considered imperative for their production line. For instance, 
certain metals (steel, iron, aluminium and copper) and plastics should also be regarded as 
critical materials due to their necessity for manufacturing. Therefore, the  Critical Raw 
Materials Act should cover a broader approach and include the significance of processed 
materials across sectors and not only “raw materials”.  
• Critical Raw Materials Act should put more focus on  the materials used for electronic 
components manufacturing to better address the vulnerabilities along the European 
supply chain. 
 
In line with the CECIMO’s survey results (see Annex below), the most pressing needs for the Machine 
Tool sector in critical materials are steel, iron, aluminium, copper, brass, plastics and polyethylene 
and, on that note, businesses mostly fall short in steel, iron, aluminium, copper and plastics. 
Concerning the demand and the shortcomings in electrical/electronic components such as 
semiconductors, connectors, cabling, automation components etc., they are twofold times higher 
than in technical/mechanical components such as bearings, gears, shafts and belt drives. Henceforth, 
we would like to stress the importance of these high -tech components for manufacturing, not only 
machinery but also other technology-based products. 
 
Although most materials come from inside Europe, benefitting the autonomy of the European 
market, the high-tech components still come from Japan and China. That implies that machine tool 
industries remain dependent on imports of various processed materials as well as 
electrical/electronic components, thus remaining exposed to vulnerabilities along the supply chain.

In addition to imposed sanctions and export restrictions, the availability of essential materials and 
components is affected by significant disruptions in transportation and late delivery. Consequently, 
to estimate future demand and competition, (r aw) materials for technologies and manufacturing 
sectors have to be considered carefully, as different sectors require different materials, not to 
mention that even different machinery requires different materials.   
 
Last but not least, Additive Manufacturing rapidly disrupts traditional supply chains and conventional 
manufacturing technologies. As reported in the Commission’s Foresight Side for Critical Raw 
Materials, the most relevant materials for Additive Manufacturing technology are aluminium, 
magnesium, nickel, titanium, iron, chromium, cobalt, copper, manganese, molybdenum, niobium, 
silicon, zirconium, cobalt, hafnium, niobium, scandium, silicon metal, tungsten and vanadium. 
Therefore, we strongly support the Commission’s efforts to diversify the sup ply chain of the 
aforementioned critical raw materials to recycle components for Additive Manufacturing and 
strengthen Europe’s industrial competitiveness.

SURVEY RESULTS: CRITICAL MATERIALS/COMPONENTS FOR MACHINE TOOL SECTOR  
 
November 2022 
 
The COVID-19 pandemic and Russia-Ukraine war caused severe supply chain disruptions  to global markets caused and 
exposed vulnerabilities to the security of the supply of raw materials critical for industrial production. Across all industrial 
sectors, there is a significant shortage of different materials/raw materials and components, which disrupts production 
growth. 
 
Following the European Commission’s call for evidence and p ublic consultation in preparation for the European C ritical 
Raw Materials Act, CECIMO has conducted a survey to identify materials and components that are indispensable for the 
Machine Tool (MT) industries in terms of quantity.  The survey encompassed 32 Machine Tool (MT) companies from 
Europe (EU + UK + EFTA + Türkiye), identifying shortages in critical materials and components and their main supply 
markets. 
 
To extract better results for the Machine Tool industries we divided critical materials into the following five categories: 
ferrous metals; non -ferrous metals, oxides (ceramics), polymers; composites  as well as components into the following 
categories: technical/mechanical and electrical . Machine tool companies answered 14 closed questions and 2 open 
questions on a scale of 1 (no importance/no shortage) to 5 (significant importance/signifi cant shortage), presenting the 
aggregated results hereafter . Hence, the next pages outline the survey results a long with CECIMO’s recommendations 
regarding the upcoming European Raw Materials Act.  
 
Note: as most of the questions were answered on the Likert scale, the responses will be shared on the percentage level 
in charts and a corresponding mean score 1. 
 
 
Importance of Ferrous Metals 
 
 
 
The results show that machine tool manufacturers have indicated steel, cast iron and stainless steel as the most important 
ferrous metals in their production processes. Around 93% of companies agree that Steel constitutes a critically necessary 
metal for the manufacturing process of machine tools . More than 61% of companies also consider Cast Iron a crucial 
material for their production lines and around 36% of companies regard Stainless Steel as a necessary material.

Shortage of Technical/Mechanical Components vs Electrical/Electronic Components  
 
 
 
The chart shows explicitly the difference between shortages in technical/mechanical components and 
electrical/electronic components. Machine Tools industries encounter shortages in electrical/electronic components 
twofold higher than in technical/mechanical components.   
 
Regarding the supplying markets, the majority of our companies source their critical materials from Europe, including 
Türkiye and the UK. According to the survey results, such European supplying markets are mainly Italy, Germany and 
France. However, a few enterprises indicated their imports of metals from outside European borders, such as China, 
Ukraine, and Russia. Henceforth, the supply chain of critical materials for machine tools manufacturing comes mainly 
from inside Europe, signalling a certain degree of European strategic autonomy in the market of critical materials.

Conversely, machine tools companies sour ce components from both inside Europe and outside Europe. To exemplify, 
our businesses import overall technical/mechanical components from Germany, Italy, Japan and China. However, the 
majority of electrical/electronic components are imported from Asia, such as Japan, China,  Taiwan, South Korea , and 
Australia. Only a few companies purchase them from European countries such as Germany and Italy. Moreover, regarding 
the impact of  ussia’s war against U raine and CO I -19 lockdowns in China in critical materials supply, around 5 0% of 
machine tool enterprises feel affected. The survey results showed a positive turning point in raw materials supply market 
since multiple companies source their commodities from Europe. Although, this does not imply that they are independent 
of Asian or other supplying markets.   
 
To sum up, we recognise that critical materials are essential for building competitive and resilient EU industries, thus 
shaping the digital future and green transition. For the Machine Tool sector, the most pressing needs in critical materials 
are steel, iron, aluminium, copper, brass, plastics and polyethylene. Whereas businesses mostly fall short in steel, iron, 
aluminium, copper and plastics. Nevertheless, companies fall short in electrical/electronic components such as 
semiconductors, connectors, cabli ng, automation components , etc., and twofold times less in technical/mechanical 
components such as bearings, gears, shafts and belt drives. Most of the materials come from inside Europe, benefitting 
the autonomy of the European market, although a vast numb er of components remain dependent on Japan and China. 
Therefore, machine tool industries rely predominantly on imports for many critical materials and remain highly exposed 
to vulnerabilities along the supply chain, notably electrical components.

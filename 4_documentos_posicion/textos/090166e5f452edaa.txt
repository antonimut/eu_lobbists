European Critical Raw Materials Act 
 
Feedback from Finnish Mining Association 
Finnish Mining Association welcomes the European Commission’s planned 
adoption of a European Critical Raw Materials Act (henceforth ECRMA). 
 
We underline the importance of the Commission’s clear commitment to 
ensuring that high environmental and social performance standards are 
met.  
 
We would emphasize the following aspects: 
 
Exploration 
 
It’s of great importance to understand the role and the process of 
prospecting and more detailed exploration activities. Finland is one of the 
places in Europe where exploration takes place. 
 
It is most important to develop the EU regulation so that it also includes 
exploration. The EU environmental regulat ion today does not recognize 
this. 
 
ESG 
 
We believe that high Economic, Social and corporate Governance (ESG) 
standards into any critical minerals policy is fundamental to the success 
of mining activities. Finnish Mining Association has developed a TSM 
based standard not only for mining but also for exploration. Together 
with our CSR reporting (reporting in Finnish language and open for public  
in web ) the FinnMin  ESG framework is a unique one. We strongly 
recommend using voluntary based systems. 
 
Primary raw materials 
 
A large share of the European Union’s primary raw materials needs is 
covered by imports from outside the EU. However, recent geopolitical 
developments such as the Russian invasion of Ukraine has underlined the 
EU’s dependencies when it comes to raw materials supply.  While 
satisfying supply needs in the short -term, such imbalances expose the 
Union to the risks of sudden disruptions and may limit its scope for

In the spirit of the “open strategic autonomy”, we therefore call on the 
EU institutions to minimize the risk of such dependencies by promoti ng 
the domestic sourcing of primary raw materials. For example, Finland 
disposes of notable reserves of many primary critical raw materials such 
as cobalt and lithium and has the only operating phosphate mine in 
Western Europe. 
 
Subsidiary principle 
 
The C ritical Raw Materials Act offers a unique opportunity to focus 
specifically on ensuring raw materials supply security through EU mining 
and production. 
The EU must ensure that the body of EU legislation that affects the 
sourcing of critical raw materials is aligned with the aims of the ECRMA. 
 
Mining projects should be considered projects of “overriding public 
interest’’ akin to the amendment to the Renewable Energy Directive 
proposed by the European Commission in the context of the REPowerEU 
plan. 
 
The subsidiarity principle for permitting remains intact and the

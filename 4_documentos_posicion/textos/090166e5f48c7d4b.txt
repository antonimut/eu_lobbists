ESF contribution to consultation on proposal 
for revision of Directive 2009/148/EC 
(asbestos) 
Date : 01/12/2022 
 
 
 
 
We are glad to see the European Commission’s commitments to promote health and 
safety at work by revising Directive 2009/148/EC, however we feel that the Directive's 
revision might be limited, only looking at the OEL without considering additional 
measures that can help protecting workers and meet the revised OEL. We wish 
the EU legislators to consider the following policy actions: 
  
• Promote mandatory respiratory face fit testing to help protect respirator 
wearers. Mandatory respiratory face fit testing for those workers required to 
wear a respiratory protective equipment (RPE) is already in place in a few 
European countries (e.g. Sweden, France and the Netherlands for asbestos 
risks, while Italy and UK even for all risks). Face fit testing helps to ensure 
workers are correctly using the protective equipment in order to help reduce 
exposure to airborne particles, including asbestos fibers. Personal protective 
equipment (PPE) need to correctly fit the workers to protect them. Obviously, 
face fit testers need to prove the necessary competence to perform this 
important work. 
• A new OEL implies new PPE selection, workers and employers and site 
managers should be trained on their use. We recommend introducing 
mandatory training for those working with asbestos and develop European 
common requirements and certification schemes.  
• Provide time and incentives for industry to make this happen: PPE 
manufacturers may need to develop additional technical capabilities to meet or 
exceed OELs, adding considerable issues for delivering even more protective 
occupational health and safety solutions. Companies working with asbestos 
will have to increase the investments in PPE (and other equipment) and also 
in the training of the personnel. This will require time and resources. 
Surveillance by authorities on products and training will be necessary to obtain 
the required quality level. 
• While considering lowering the OEL, EU and national legislators should take 
into account the impact on the PPE assigned protection factors, varying at 
national level, as well as reflect the legislative changes in the related 
European standards (e.g. EN 137). 
• Workers should be appropriately protected from any potential risk of 
exposure to asbestos, even very low levels, until full risk assessment is 
carried out. Screening in buildings widely varies across member states,

• Looking at building screenings, public authorities should consider the 
applications in energy sectors and specifically the implications of 
decommissioning nuclear plants, happening or being planned in several 
member states, in order to make sure that workers are not exposed to any 
risks. Asbestos is commonly used as insulation material; reactor pressure 
vessels and reactor containment buildings were commonly insulated with 
material containing asbestos. 
• Increasing costs for asbestos removal might have the effect that (private) 
building owners avoid working with recognized asbestos removal companies 
to save costs. Member states need to set up systems to avoid the risk of 
asbestos removal other than by recognized specialists.  
• Include also provision for the waste treatment of asbestos as well as of the 
PPE (and other equipment used during asbestos removal and treatment), as 
this is also a step in the overall process where the risk of exposure of workers 
is to be controlled.

About ESF: 
 
The European Safety Federation (ESF) was founded in 1991.  National organisations each 
representing the manufacturers and suppliers of PPE (Personal Protective Equipment) in their country 
decided to group and unite the European manufacturers, importers, distributors and service provide rs 
of PPE and to represent them at government level of the European Union and other European 
institutions and instances.  
 
Today, ESF represents over 600 companies, of which at least 70% are SMEs.  The members of ESF 
and the enterprises that affiliate to the national federations are dedicated and committed to provide 
compliant CE certified and high quality PPE.  Moreover, they link quality and service by giving expert 
advice and assistance in the process of risk assessment and analysis as well as training and advice in 
all aspects related to PPE.  Supporting a safety conscious way of life is a common interest to all of us.

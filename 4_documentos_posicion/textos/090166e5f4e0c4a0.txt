On the European Disability Card 
The European disability card should be recognised, and their holders should have equal access 
to discounts and preferential charges in all EU countries without any predjudice. The equality 
in access entails that the country of origin of a person and/or a period of stay (short or long 
term) should not be considered. 
With focus on people with registered impaired disability , holders of the EU Disability Card 
should have the following rights in the following cases: 
1. Possiblity of booking and using specialised transport, including door -to-door type of 
taxis, for people with registered significant or visible impairment disabilities 
(wheelchair users, people with crutches)  coming for exchange/tourism or 
medical/cultural purposes. Such purposes usually imply a short stay of up to 90 days; 
thus, the EU Disability Card should be sufficient for identification and utilisation of the 
abovementioned services. At present, this type of services is not available for them or 
available at comercial rates; 
2. Possiblity of obtaining similar discounts or free of charge tickets for all means of public 
transport around the city in all EU countries for people with (visible) physical 
impairments of the first and second degree . For example, in Capri (Italy) people are 
entitled to a free ride on funicular. However, such practice in not uniform across the 
EU Member States; 
3. Exemption from City tax fees. Similar to example in paragraph 2, the practice across 
the EU Member States is not consistent (e.g., some countries, such as the Czech 
Republic, already provide for City tax exemption while other countries do not);

4. Harmonisation of rules related to  discounts on visiting  tourist attractions  (theatres, 
museums, national parks, concert halls, cinemas , etc.). Currently people with the EU 
Disability Card are entitled to discounts in some forms in various EU Member States: 
a) Only the carer of the person with proven significant disabilities has a free entrance 
to the mentioned institutions; 
b) The disabled person and the carer have free entrance or a discount on entry; 
c) The disabled person without a carer cannot get any discount and is obliged to pay a 
full rate fee; 
5. Posibility of using local assistants (while going to the theatre; during sightseeing; etc.) 
on the same bases as local  residents. Currently, only residents  of each Member State 
are entitled to the mentioned benefits. Non-residents have to pay commercial rates, 
which are excessively high, preventing persons with disabilities from engaging in cross-
border tourist activities;  
6. Discounts and special rates for the holders of the EU Disability Card should be clearly 
visible, and a person with disabilities should be informed about the applicable discounts 
and rates while making a payment; 
7. The tour operators organising tours/services for people with disabilities holding the EU 
Disability Card  should have the possiblity of making  group reservations at special 
preferential rates. Due to the small number of people in the group (usually below 15 
people), special rates for hotels or ferries are generally not applicable.

European Media Freedom Act (EMFA)Position Paper
1. Key messages
● As a leading publisher in Scandinavia, Schibsted welcomes the European Commission'srecognition of the social role of the media and the importance of a diverse, strong mediaecosysteminEuropethat providescitizenswithrelevant, critical andindependent informationand contributes to the protection and enhancement of democratic values across the Union.
● Therefore, Schibsted recognises the need for a European MediaFreedomAct onanoverallandprincipal level. However, theRegulationmust takeintoaccount largenational differencesinmediapolicyinEurope, and, therefore, ensurethatwell functioningnational mediasystemsare not weakened.
● We support the important principles in Articles 3-6 on rights and duties of media serviceproviders and recipients. We do, however, want to emphasize that these Articles shouldremain as principles, and there should benoenforcement or sanctioningof theserightsandduties on EU level.
● Article 6, which sets out the relationship between the owner/publisher and the responsibleeditors, should be clarified. The Article should clearlyrecognisethepublishers’ right toset apublication’seditorial line. Thespecificdutiesofpublishersandeditorsshouldalsobeclarifiedin the Article.
● In Article 17, we support the provision on the relationship between media service providersand very large online platforms (VLOPs). The statement of reasonsrequiredunder Art. 17.2should applytoall typesof interferencefromplatformsoneditorial content. Therefore, Article17.2must beamendedtoalsoincludesituationswhereVLOPsintendto“restrict”thecontentof a media service provider, as is specified in Article 17.4.
● The new European Board for Media Services (EBMS) should solely focus on cross-bordersituations. It should not have any power over national medialegislation. Wealsostressthatany decisions made by the Board must be non-binding.
● There is merit in additional transparency around media ownership. However, we do notsupport any requirements that introduce new national legislation on media concentration.
● The current system of assessing media concentration by national competition authoritiesworks well in the Nordic market. Any additional assessment bynational mediaauthoritiesortheEBMSmust belimitedtoverysignificantcross-bordercasesandbedoneinparallel tothecompetition assessment to avoid delaying the acquisition process.
● Assessments of media pluralismshould take into account all players onthemarket suchaspublic service media and very large online platforms, asbothhaveaninstrumental effect onthe financial viability of the media sector.

2. Provisionsin theEMFA thatareimportantforfreedomofexpressionandeditorial independence in Europe
2.1. Overall justification
Theconditionsfor freedomof thepressandfreedomofexpressioninEuropehavedeterioratedinrecent years. More and more countries are showing authoritarian traits where the free andindependent media are threatened by restrictions and sanctions. Considering the decline in thenumber of democracies, polarization, intensifiedspreadof conspiraciesaswell asfalsificationsinsocial networks, freeandindependent mediaismoreimportantthanever.Inthisnewenvironmentwe acknowledge the need for a regulatoryframeworkthat establishessomebasicprinciplesandconditions for free media, as these  are unfortunately not adequately ensured at national level.
2.2. The right to a plurality of news and current affairs (Article 3)
According to Article 3, EUcitizens shall havetheright to"receiveapluralityof newsandcurrentaffairscontent". Thisisapowerful andambitiousright. It must, however, beunderstoodasarighttoreceive“content fromthecitizens' owngeography,intheirownlanguage”.Citizensmustbeableto relate to news they have access to and feel that it is relevant to themin their own society.Furthermore, this general provision raises a number of questions about definitions andinterpretation, such as what is meant by "..right to receive.." andhowtodefine"..plurality..". TheEU's ability to implement such an ambition in the short term is also questionable. This isnevertheless an important provision that should be kept as a justification for this Regulation.
2.3. Protection of editorial integrity and protection of sources (Articles 4-6)
Articles 4, 5 and 6 are particularly important asfreedomandindependenceof themediaarethefoundation of a functioning media landscape and a prerequisite for citizens' trust in the media.
More specifically, Article 4 provides a clear prohibition against public interference in editorialpolicies. Article 4 (2,b) protects sources and prohibits the authorities from demanding thesesources to be revealed. This would ensure that people feel safe and can rely on confidentialitywhen reporting and giving evidence about objectionable conditions.
Article 5 includes an important provision that ensures that public service mediaisgovernedandoperated according to the same basic principles as private media. This is important becausehistoryshowsthat illiberal statesusuallytakecontrol of thestate-fundedpublicservicemediaanduse it as an instrument for state propaganda.
Article6rightlyaimsat defendingeditors’ freedomtomakeeditorial decisionswithoutinterferencefromowners.Wewould,however,clarifytherelationshipbetweentheownerandtheeditortoavoidconfusion (see amendment proposal below).

2.5. Allocation of state advertising (Article 24)
Article 24 introduces key provisions of considerable financial importance to the media sector inmany countries, namely that public entities shall grant public funds to media service providersthrough transparent, objective, proportionate and non-discriminatory criteria and procedures.
Moreover, public entities must disclose their advertising expenditure allocated to media serviceproviders. Thisisimportant becauseof thecleartendencyshownbyilliberal authorities,aswell aspublicly owned companies in some countries, to prioritize media companiesthat are“friendly” tothe government when placing public advertisements. In the Nordic countries, where publicadvertising is not misusedto“punish” critical media, suchincreasedreportingrequirementsareasmall price to pay compared to the advantages this provision may bring to more vulnerablecountries.
3. Schibsted´s proposals to strengthen editorial independence in Europe
While we applaud the inclusion of important principlespertainingtomediafreedomintheEMFA,we urge caution in the approach and careful consideration of possible unintended negativeconsequencesresultingfromtheintroductionofnewregulationapplyingtothemediasectoracrosstheEU. Therefore,itisveryimportantthat,initsquesttoroll backanti-democraticdevelopmentsinotherpartsofEurope,theEMFAdoesnotweakenwell-functioningsystemsintheNordiccountries.For the Regulation to be acceptable, this balance must be upheld. Any attempt to regulate themediasector shouldalsotakeintoaccount thediversestructural, cultural, political, andeconomiccontexts that exist for media across the EU.
3.1. Duties of media service providers providing news and current affairs content (Article 6)
Article6(2) isakeyprovisionof thisregulation.Weagreewiththeproblemanalysisinrecital 15.Itis, however, important tostatethat ownersmust havethepossibilitytoset theeditorial lineof thepublication. Thisisakeyrightoftheownerthatensurespredictabilityandfinancial control overthepublication.
Therefore, forthesakeofcompletenessandbalance,andtoavoidambiguity,weproposetoadd language from recital 20 to the text of Article 6(2):Media service providers should adopt proportionate measures to guarantee, once the overalleditorial linehasbeenagreedbetweentheir ownersandeditors,thefreedomoftheeditorstotakeindividual decisions in the course of their professional activity.
AccordingtoArticle6(2) “mediaserviceprovidersprovidingnewsandcurrent affairscontentshalltake measures that they deem appropriate with a view to guaranteeing the independence ofindividual editorial decisions”. This gives owners the right to establish measures for ensuringeditorial independence. 3.2 European Board for Media Services (Articles 8-12)
The Commission proposes to establish a newEuropean Board for Media Services (EBMS) thatwouldreplacetheEuropeanRegulatorsGroupforAudiovisual MediaServices(ERGA).TheEBMSwould, according to the proposal, have far-reaching powers, especially over audiovisual mediaservices, but wouldalsobecomeanimportant“gatekeeper”betweentheCommissionandnationalauthorities for enforcing this Regulation.
Our main concern with the EBMS is that it may be granted evenmorepowersastheEuropeanCommission’sproposal makesitswaythroughtheOrdinaryLegislativeProcedure,becoming,overtime, apolitical power inthefieldofmediaregulation.ThedirectimpactoftheEBMSonEuropeanpublishers is that it would have a say in national cases of mediamergersandacquisitions. Thiscould delay and add red tape to the process, even in countrieswherethecurrent systemworkswell. As such the EBMS could have important business implications on media companies inEurope.
We support strengthening cooperation of national media authoritiesbut areof thestrongopinionthat theEBMS’ roleshouldbelimitedtocross-border issues. TheEBMSshouldnothaveanyrolein the enforcement of articles 4-6, as these are principles that are established in nationalconstitutions and self-regulatory systems. We also caution against the EBMS having a say inmedia mergers and acquisitions that only have an impact on the media sector in one MemberState.
Therefore, we proposethat Article12(g) bedeletedandthat Article12(f-ii) bereformulatedand limited to questions of a cross-border nature.

3.4Assessment of media market concentrations (Articles21-22)
Articles 21-22 constitute the main concern for Schibsted in the proposed EMFA. As a leadingpublisher in the Nordic market andasamediaowner that iscommittedtoensuringavital mediasector, we need legal certainty and predictability in possible mergersor acquisitions. Webelievethat mediaownershipisalreadywell servedbytheexistingcompetitionframework. Thiswasalsoconfirmed when Norway decided to repeal its media ownership law in 2016. Therefore, mediamergers and acquisitions in our markets shouldcontinuetobeassessedbynational competitionauthorities. Combined with other regulations related to transparency and various reportingrequirements, this provides a solid basis for overseeing media ownership in the EU.
Wearethereforeof thestrongopinionthat theEMFAshouldnotrequiretheintroductionofany new national legislation on media ownership.
However, we acknowledge that the criteria used for assessing media concentration could beupdated and adapted to the new realities of the media sector. In their assessments, nationalauthorities should take into account the sector’s financial realities. Importantly, theyshouldalsofocus on theroleof verylargeonlineplatformsinonlineadvertising, amarket that theydominatedespite not producing their own editorial content. This is crucial when assessing mergers andacquisitions in the media market according to competition legislation.
Wethereforeproposethat anyfutureCommissionguidelinesbasedonArticle21(3)shouldincludebroadcriteriafor assessingmediapluralism,includingtheroleplayedbyverylargeonlineplatforms, but alsobynationalpublicservicemediathatisindirectcompetitionwithpublishers for media consumers’ time and attention.

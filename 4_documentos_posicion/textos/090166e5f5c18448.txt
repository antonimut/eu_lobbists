1. Introduction and general remarks  
Intesa Sanpaolo , one of the top banking groups in the European Union, welcomes the 
opportunity to provide preliminary feed -back to the co -legislators about  the European 
Commission’s proposal for an Instant Payment Regulation. 
As highlighted in other European Commission’s public consultations (the most recent one 
available here), Intesa Sanpaolo fully supports the development of Instant Payments (IPs) across 
EU countries.  
We shared the European Commission’s concerns on the still low level of adherence to the SCT 
Inst scheme by EU PSPs and urged the Commission to act and take most effective measures to 
make IP  available to every European citizen  across the EU  by establishing a reachability 
obligation for EU PSPs under the SCT Inst. Scheme.  
IPs are, for Intesa Sanpaolo, a key driver for innovation in digital payments. This is the reason why 
the Group was one of the very first banks to adhere to the voluntary SCT Inst scheme back in 
November 2017, thus allowing its customers to be among the first in the EU being able to make 
and receive payments in real-time.  
While praising the Commission for having acted on this matter, we would also like to express our 
concerns about some measures of the current proposal  as they are, in our opinion, 
disproportioned compared to the goals the proposal itself aims to achieve. 
We understand that the Commission’ intention is to develop a pan-European payment solution 
based on IPs, as it is a high-priority objective in the EU policy agenda.  
However, even though we agree that IPs could be a valuable tool in reaching this goal, it is 
equally true that IPs alone cannot be the silver bullet. IPs are just a way to transfer funds account 
to account to be used for specific use cases when such a service level is required and are not 
a payment network . We see a risk of a very low uptake of IPs by EU consumers, if the same 
experience offered by cards and other alternative payments methods wouldn’t be m et (e.g. 
customer protection, unique experience for payment initiation, adequate branding awareness, 
merchant services).  In other words, we consider that the current proposal is disproportioned in 
terms of efforts required to PSPs and, even if implemented as it is, it will not be able to fully 
achieve the objectives it intends to reach. 
Please, find below our key considerations and suggested amendments to the proposal.

2. Intesa Sanpaolo key concerns 
Mandatory provision of instant credit transfers in euro (Article 5 a) 
As said above, Intesa Sanpaolo is very much supportive of the uptake of IPs in the EU. However, 
the proposed Art. 5a goes far beyond of what it is necessary to the development of IPs in the 
EU.  
The current article would oblige PSPs to offer IPs to all channels where a regular transfer is already 
offered, to all customers and for bulk payments . This massive implementation of IPs will entail 
high costs for PSPs, in front of very limited benefits.  
 
1. Requirement to extend IPs to all channels:  
• First of all, this provision does not consider the different peculiarities of channels that 
can be equipped with a user interface (for example, web site or app), distinguished 
by connection server to server (for example, the connection host to host, as used by 
corporates) or using an interface intermediated by an agent (for example, physical 
or virtual branches). The extension to all channels would be critical, as such a massive 
adoption would mean huge investments and efforts, e.g. in terms of human resources 
to manage IPs 24/7/365 and in less than 10 seconds. Introducing such a requirement 
for channels with low uptake and not suitable for offering IPs, would be excessive. This 
is the case e.g. of credit transfers executed by our cl ients at ATMs, since our data 
shows that they currently only amount to 1.3% during the period January – September 
2022. In addition, please consider that IPs are used by only 17% of our customers for 
specific use cases , which means that their use is limited only to customers really 
interested in relying on this service. 
• Risk of frauds : While in most cases SEPA credit transfers (not in stant) are refunded 
thanks to the recall of funds and interoperability agreements among PSPs 1, IPs are 
very attractive to fraudsters because of their immediacy and irrevocability . In our 
view, the draft regulation could lead to the risk of exposing millions of customers to 
an increase of frauds and to new ones, i.e. at ATMs where new security scenarios 
could emerge . For example, in case of card’s theft and access to PIN through 
phishing, fraudsters could immediately and irrevocably transfer the funds on their 
accounts. Or consumers could be obliged by criminals to transfer money at the ATM. 
Let us be clear: IPs are not unsafe “per se” and we are able to manage the related 
risks, thanks to the protection systems that we have put in place , but the costs as 
explained above (see a) and risks connected to new channels , would be much 
higher than the benefits.  
2. Obligation to offer multiple IPs : in our view , it is not coherent with the use cases we 
observe in practice. Indeed, multiple credit transfers are usually performed by corporates 
who do not practically have needs in terms of IPs, as they usually concentrate their 
payments during working days and hours, with no needs of 24/7. Their transactions can 
be largely antici pated in advance (such as, for example, salaries and invoices). 
Therefore, the need to dispose of IPs is limited and, in any case, it can be autonomously 
satisfied by the market.  
3. Offering IPs to all customers: the requirement does not consider the fact that customers 
should be free to choose whether to rely or not on this service, and that PSPs should be 
able to autonomously assess the cybersecurity their risk’s profile, before extending IPs to

Key messages:  
✓ The obligation to offer IPs on all channels should be deleted. Instead , a mandatory 
obligation for reachability should be maintained in the text. Otherwise, costs borne by 
PSPs would be disproportionate compared to the benefits  for customers. PSPs should 
autonomously choose, according to their marketing and security policy strategies, 
whether to offer the possibility to send IPs, for which channels and customers.  
o IPs service should be activated upon customer’s request, and, in any case, 
subordinated to the PSP’s cyber risk assessment, also considering the PSD2 
liabilities in case of fraud. PSPs should also be able to disable or temporarily 
suspend the IP service, selectively or totally, because of well -grounded security 
reasons and in the  interests of their customers. We remind that IPs are typically 
appealing for fraudsters, as they are immediate and irrevocable. As a second -
best solution, we ask that customers can disable this service, also considering their 
real needs. 
✓ PSPs should be allowed to voluntary offer IPs through multiple channels, considering that 
making all channels mandatory will require huge investments not only from an IT, but also 
from an anti -fraud perspective. As highlighted above, bulk payment offering is usually 
provided to corp orates which operate during working days and hours. Therefore, this 
obligation is excessive, and the final decision to provide or not this service should be left 
to the PSP. Also, the customer should be made aware, before authorizing the payment,  
of the information required by Art. 5.1, letter a) of PSD2’s RTS 2 (the payment amount and 
the payee) for each single transfer. This information is even more crucial in case of an IP, 
which is both immediately executed and irrevocable. Therefore, the draft IP regulation 
should provide the modalities about how to provide the above-mentioned information to 
the payer to allow him/her to detect any potential fraud.  
Finally, the timeline to comply with this measure is too stringent. 
Charges for IPs Article 5(b) 
According to this provision, charges applied by a PSP to IPs should not be higher than those 
applied to a credit transfer.  
This obligation does not consider that these two services are different, with different features and 
that their use is conceived for specific use cases. The offering of an instant credit transfer requires 
huge technological, infrastructural and security investments.  
Key message: 
We believe that their difference in costs is objectively justified (as for the most premium 
services in any market) , and prices should be left to be determined by the market , in 
accordance with the free market principle.

We consider that the efforts required to develop this kind of service, which is not further detailed 
in terms of how it should be offered, is disproportionate wh en compared with its efficacy and 
usefulness for the purpose of  contrasting frauds ( see the document PSFPWG "CoP formal

limited effects to certain types of frauds such as  Scams and Whaling (Business email

verification should be performed according to specific functional requirements to clearly define 
the PSP liability, especially when informing the PSU about the verifications’ results and the 
following incorrect and fraudulent execution of the IP. Finally, it would be unworkable and  
excessive for PSPs  to be obliged to offer this service for all PSU interfaces, including ATM s, 
branches, and batch payments.   
In terms of liabilities, several aspects need to be addressed. First, the management by PSPs of 
the verification’s results towards their customers. No harmonized standards are proposed by the 
regulation to manage these results, and, in case of uncertainty, customers could feel confused 
and would not know how to act.  
Second, a clear sharing of liabilities, as provided by the PSD2 amon g the payer’s PSP, payee’s 
PSP, and the customer in case of denied payments, should be established, also considering the 
need for aligning IPs provisions with those of the PSD2, in case their revision will be considered as 
needed. The same goes for the liability between the PSP of the payer and the PSP of the payee 
about the accuracy of the data used for the consistency verification. 
Key messages:  
➢ First, a third entity (such as the EPC, for example) should be mandated to draft 
guidelines and technical standards to be used to offer the Confirmation of Payee 
(CoP) service. Only when these implementing measures will be made available  
to the market, then the timeline for the implementation of the CoP should start to 
take effect. 
➢ A fifth pillar in the proposal about how to holistically address frauds is missing, as 
the CoP cannot be considered the silver bullet for all kinds of (also emerging) 
frauds. We would like to reiterate also in this occasion, other structural measures 
that should be adopted with a wider aim of fostering digital operational resilience 
at European Union level. We believe it is fundamental to strengthen the protection 
of citizens in an increasingly digital economy with a more effective operational 
response at EU level to the phenomenon of online frauds. An example of area to 
be tackled, is the clarification of the powers of the Bank/PSP when it identifies a 
fraudster among its custome rs, to speed up processes related to block and the 
retrieve of money fraudulently transferred. This topic has a greater importance in 
relation to IPs, considering the increased difficulties in recalling fraudulent 
transactions. Furthermore, another area to be considered, refers to the substantial 
obstacles when sharing cyber threat information, especially when these involve 
personally identifiable information (PII), due to the different interpretations of EU 
and national requirements. Therefore, it is important to define cases of exception 
when information sharing can lead to high benefits, for example in anti -fraud 
management, in compliance with competition rules, where difficulties related to 
the presence of data associated to natural persons may been coun tered. A first 
step in this direction, is the inclusion of a specific provision for information sharing

➢ We propose to delete the obligation to provide the degree of discrepancy (recital 
12), as it could entail useless liabilities on the PSPS. The PSP should only be required 
to check that the IBAN and the beneficiary’s name correspond or not. The PSP’ 
payer should not take on the burden to indicate to the payer the percentage of 
discrepancy between data (IBAN and name). As a second -best solution, 
standardized criteria to identify such percentage should be introduced. Anyway, 
a clear liability framework for PSP s involved in the CoP service should be 
introduced. Finally, in case a PSU authorises a payment transaction despite a 
discrepancy is detected, we welcome recital 13 and we ask it is introduced in Art. 
5c4, to clarify that in such case, the PSP shall not be liable for the execution of the 
payment transaction to an unintended payee, in accordance with Article 88 of 
the PSD2.  
Screening IPs for EU sanctions (Article 5d) 
We welcome this provision that introduces harmonized measures at EU level on sanction 
screening. However, more clarifications on certain provisions are needed. 
 
Key messages:  
Considering that the proposal does not foresee the screening of the transaction, Art. 5d 
(1) should specify that the obligation to verify whether any of its customers are designated 
persons or entities subject to EU sanctions lies with the payer's PSP, being the one that 
executes the instant credit transfer , and not with the payee’s PSP, as this latter will be 
subject to the existing EU sanction regulations. Consequently, should the payer's PSP fail 
to carry out such verifications, it shall be held liable under Art. 5d (3) vis-à-vis the payee’s 
PSP. 
Timeline to comply with this rule (6 months) is too short, as it requires the revision of internal 
processes for the control execution on financial sanctions for harmonization purposes. 
We ask for at least 12 -month grace period before the proposal enters into force, 
considering the relevant number of millions of personal data records involved in the daily 
sanctions screening. 
 
Overall, the timeline to comply with all the above-mentioned measures, also considering the 
fact the legislative process timeline is uncertain, is too short. We therefore ask for an extension 
of 6 months for all the obligations.

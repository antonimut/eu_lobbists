ETPPA position on theEuropean Commission’s proposed Instant Payments Regulation
ETPPAwelcomes theEuropeanCommission’slegislativeproposalforaregulationoninstantpayments.Aregulationtosecurethewide-spreadavailabilityofinstantpaymentshaslongbeenamissingbrickinthePSD2 ecosystem. Webelievetherehas beenaclear needfor swift legislationinorder tohelppaymentinitiation service providers (PISPs) and other payment service providers (PSPs) ingeneral leveragethehuge investments already made into PSD2 infrastructure on open banking.
Theavailabilityof instant payments for all consumers inmakingoutgoingSEPAInstantCreditTransfer(SCTInst) payments is anunderlyingnecessityfor achievinghome-grownandpan-Europeanpaymentsolutions. ETPPA believes that all account servicing payment service providers (ASPSPs) offering non-instant SCT today should be mandated to offer SCT Inst in an equal form and without any premium fees.
Knowing that SCT Inst has not found widespreadadoptionacross EUMember States despitetheSEPAschemes’ self-regulatory stipulation, this must nowbe enforced by legislation so that its benefits aremade available to EU consumers without any further delay.
ETPPA calls on the co-legislators to support this mandate and to ensure the certainty of instantpayments, thereby future proofing the EU’s retail payments market so that it can compete globally.
That said, there are six important areas where improvements to thelegal text areneededinorder tocreate an optimal outcome for all EU consumers and businesses.

Experience fromother markets (e.g. UK where CoP only corrects misdirected payments and Swedenwhere an attempted CoP rollout was aborted) demonstrates that CoP is incrediblydifficult to implement and does not tackle APP fraud. Instead, we find that CoP:○ adds friction to the payment experience;○ conditions payers to click away information boxes (like cookie requests);

● Apparently, some ASPSPs are already considering increasing/introducing charges fornon-instant SCT to this effect. Consumers will not use instant payments if there aredirect charges per transaction. In their Q&A, the Commission has stated that it is“expected that competitive pressure, scrutiny by consumer organisations and publicpressure would reduce the likelihood of suchprice increases by banks toanticipate theeffect of newEUrules proposed”. Wewouldsuggest not just torelyonsuchpressures,but add a stipulation in the instant payments regulation to this extent.● For instant payments tobecomethenewnormal,therecannotbeany“transactionfee”for theconsumer, andanyother formofapotentialfeemustbenon-discriminatoryandin line with regional or domestic non-instant credit transfer alternatives.● Therefore, ETPPA calls for stronger assurances that ASPSPs would not be allowed toarbitrarily increase standard credit transfer fees in response to the instant paymentsproposal.
4. Settlement finality - theproposedInstant Payments Regulationshouldincludeaprovisiontoamend the Settlement Finality Directive (SFD)● The current proposal rightfully exempts non-bank PSPs from the requirement to offerinstant payments due to the existing legal barriers within the SFD preventing them frombecoming direct participants of designated payment systems.● Until access to settlement accounts is extended, non-bank PSPs are only able to accessthe payment infrastructure indirectly through credit institutions, leading to an unlevelplaying field and competition concerns, as well as preventing the full potential of instantpayments in the EU.● The current discussions on the Instant Payments Regulation are the right time to discussthe extension of direct access of non-banks PSPs to settlement accounts of designatedpayment systems and this would help ensure that instant payments succeed.● Ensuring the necessary legislative changes now is important to give non-bank players thecertainty and legal clarity on their ability to make the most out of instant payments.● It would also be an opportunity to already feed into the discussions of the upcomingPSD2 review, which will cover a much wider scope of issues, and avoid policy-makersbeing under time pressure due to the busy legislative calendar and the upcoming end ofthe current mandates of the institutions.● Such discussions should accordingly lead to amendments in Article 2 of the SFD so thatall types of PSPs could choose to directly access designated payment systems forsettlement purposes.
5. Reimbursements - ensuring the possibility for refunding an SCT Inst without the need for SCA● Theproposedinstant payments regulation, shouldensurethepossibilityforamerchanttorefundits client without performinganSCA, as is currentlypossiblefor paymentsbycard.

● When a card refund/reimbursement is initiated by the merchant, the merchant cancredit the card on the basis of the initial operation, without the need for performingSCA. If the merchant realises the same operation (initiatingareimbursement) withaninstant payment, an SCA is needed.● To create a level playing field and similar payer andpayeeconvenience, thesameSCArules should apply to reimbursements, regardless of the means of payment (card or IP).
6. Timing - ensure swift adoptionof Instant Payments Regulationanddonot repeat thetimingmistakes from PSD2 and SEPA(Article 5a)● Whilst ASPSPs mayclaimtheyneedmoretimeforimplementation,ETPPAwouldcallonthe co-legislators to ensure the swiftest possible adoption of this regulation.● PSD2 demonstrated how excessive implementation periods and extensions of theseperiods lead to serious detriment to TPPs, fintechs more broadly and the end user.● PSD2 has also shown that allegedly “too short” implementation periods are not theproblem, but rather the willingness for proper implementation. Today wearestill inasituation where many ASPSPs across the EU have still not implemented PSD2 adequately.● The initial introduction of a SEPA (before instant) also suffered fromseveral delays inadoption before a timeline was mandated. These delays all seriously held the marketand European developments back.● Themosturgentandimportantfunctionality,outgoinginstantpayments,isalreadygivenan extended time.● ASPSPs should already be preparing and budgeting for instant payments intheir 2023financial year. There should be no extension of the timeframes proposed.● SCT Inst has been available for over 5 years. Laggards should not be rewarded bygranting them further delays.
ETPPA calls on the co-legislators to support these essential points ensuring the competitiveness ofinstant payments, and thereby enabling home-grown, pan-European payment solutions, which willfuture proof the EU’s retail payments market and can compete globally on a level playing field.

Bizum would like to thank the European Commission for the opportunity to provide 
feedback on this proposal for a Regulation. Please find below these lines our detailed 
comments on the proposal. 
 
1. About Bizum. 
Bizum, S.L. (hereinafter "Bizum"), is a Spanish company that provides information 
society services2. It was set up in 2016 as an innovative and collaborative project of the 
Spanish banking industry. Its aim is to offer a payment solution based on the instant 
transfer of funds between users' accounts in an efficient, fast, and secure manner. 
Bizum currently has a network that brings together more than 22.5 million users, 33 
Spanish and Andorran credit institutions, 41,000 businesses and more than 7,300 
NGOs. It offers a value-added service  for its members ( payment service providers , 
hereinafter "PSPs") and their customers, covering around 99% of the Spanish market 
share. Bizum's functionalities allow linking in the same register -a database or directory- 
the telephone3 number and the IBAN code of a user's bank account, together with other 
relevant data about the user  (ID number, First and Last Name, Alias 4, and optionally 
email or postal address). The information in the directory enables immediate transfers 
to be made using the users' phones as proxies. 
It is easier for the user to enter a 9 -digit phone number or select it from the address 
book than to enter a 24-character IBAN code. This increases convenience for the user, 
as it reduces the time required to make a payment while minimising the risk of errors. 
Bizum is, therefore, a payment solution that complements the basic functionalities of 
its member institutions, making them more attractive and allowing them to offer 
services that improve and enrich the user experience offered to end-users.

3. Control obligations: Identification of the payment account. 
 
3.1 Scope of application. 
Art. 5c  of the Commission's proposal for Regulation (EU) 260/2012 addresses 
discrepancies between the name and identifier of a beneficiary's payment account 
in the case of immediate credit transfers. As provided for in Art. 5c (5), this obligation 
applies regardl ess of the payment service user interface used by the payer . 
According to Recital 8, the different interfaces can be online banking, a mobile 
application, an ATM, or a branch or a telephone. In other words, the article is not 
restricted to immediate transfers issued through the ordinary PSP interface. 
 
Bizum therefore considers that its value service is also bound by these control 
obligations. However, it would be advisable to further clarify the scope of application 
of the article, as Art. 5(5) is excessively vague and may lead to misunderstandings. In 
fact, as already introduced and further developed below, Bizum regrets the absence 
of a tailor-made regulation for this type of value service, which leads to unavoidable 
uncertainties concerning the scope of application.

3.2 Information and analysis of the proposed obligations with the greatest impact on 
Bizum. 
Article 5c (1) establishes that the payer must provide two pieces of information to 
make an immediate transfer: the identifier of the beneficiary's payment account, for 
the definition of which reference is made to the concept of unique identifier in Article 
4 (33) of the PSD2 Directive; and the name of the beneficiary.  
According to the concept included in the  PSD2 Directive, the identifier is the 
combination of letters, numbers or signs specified by the PSP to uniquely identify 
the beneficiary (e.g., IBAN). 
 
3.3 Modalities under which Bizum services are provided, fit with the proposed 
obligations and comments.  
 
3.3.1. Transfers between individuals (P2P). 
P2P transfers are currently the main modality in which Bizum services are provided, 
with more than 22.5 million active users. 
 
To send funds (push mode) to other users, their phone number is entered, either 
manually or by selecting a contact from the address book. The mobile number acts 
as a proxy for the unique identifier . The payer does not enter the name of the 
beneficiary, but once the mob ile number has been entered, the beneficiary's Alias 
(which incorporates both the beneficiary’s first name and the initials of his/her last 
name) is displayed on the screen  before the user can check this information and 
confirm the transfer. 
 
There is als o a way to request funds  (pull mode) from another user, selected by 
entering his or her mobile number. The recipient of a request will receive a 
notification containing the amount requested, the Alias of the requestor, as well as 
his or her mobile number, and can accept or reject the request.

Regarding the unique identifier, it could be understood that the text is not limiting 
the use of the IBAN as the unique identifier of a payment account (by admitting the 
definition of the PSD2 "A combination of letters, numbers or signs"). Therefore, the use 
of the users' telephone number, which in combination with the country code results

Regarding name entry, it is important to re-emphasize on the relevance of allowing 
different mechanisms of validation of the beneficiary . This can be either by manual 
entry or through value added services. 
 
The ease of use is one of the main values that Bizum aims to offer to end-users, and, 
for this, it is essential to be able to operate simply by entering a phone number . 
Otherwise, the service would be distorted if the requirement to enter a name we re 
added (as it would add significant friction to its user experience). 
 
Restricting the beneficiary validation mechanisms to manual name entry would 
hinder the use of immediate transfers . Examples of these difficulties are the usual 
minor differences between the name entered and the name registered as holder or 
authorized on the payment account, particularly in the case of Spain due to the way 
people are named (e.g., hyphen between the first and second surname; or entering 
only one surname when there are two).

4. Control obligations: EU sanctions for use of immediate transfers. 
Art. 5 d of the Proposal of Regulation obliges to verify that persons and entities 
sanctioned by the EU are not using instant transfer services. As has been the case with 
these types of obligations to date, it is understood that the responsibility for performing 
these checks lies with the financial institutions/PSPs and not directly on Bizum. 
For other matters, Bizum welcomes the proposed wording of Art. 5 d. It considers it is 
positive to introduce ex ante verification obligations on the payment service instead of 
additional checks during the execution of the transactions, as this ensures the normal, 
proper functioning of the payment system. 
Bizum already has a service that allows the blocking of a user at the request of its 
banking entity. Therefore, Bizum is prepared to facilitate the execution of these controls 
 
10 
 
by its member entities when it comes to allowing the use of this capability for blocking 
sanctioned persons to its services. 
Bizum considers that this type of control obligation could help explore the possibility of 
playing a central role in the use of its blocking services for users of accounts previously 
identified as fraudulent (although these are different economic crimes compared to the 
ones covered in this proposal) and that the member entities have detected as potential 
scammers leveraging on immediate transfers to perpetrate these crimes (as occurs in 
other means of payment). 
 
5. Setting limits on immediate payments. 
The Proposal of  Regulation does not provide a setting of the limits on immediate 
payments. Bizum considers that, at the level of the payment standard or infrastructure, 
these limits should not exist or be very high ( to allow them to be adapted to the 
particularities of each solution). 
However, Bizum stresses the importance of payment solutions being able to de fine 
limits suited to their use cases  (e.g., in P2P transfers, it would not be reasonable for 
there to be no limits) and to each customer segment  (e.g., when offering payment 
services for minors, legal guardians may legitimately require stricter limits  for their 
wards). The security component that this ensures should be noted, since it can prevent 
the use of all the funds of each user in the event of theft. On the other hand, the setting 
of limits would be con sistent with those existing in other means o f payment, such as 
credit and debit cards.

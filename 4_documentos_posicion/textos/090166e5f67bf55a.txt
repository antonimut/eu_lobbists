Enabling timely patient access to breakthrough medical 
device innovation in Europe 
Context  
 
With the proposal for a Regulation (2023/0005) submitted on January 6 2023 extending the transition 
period of the EU regulations on medical devices, the European Commission is working to ensure 
smooth implementation of its regulatory framework for medical devices, ensuring their availability, 
safety and effectiveness. While we acknowledge the Commission’s proposal’s primary purpose is the 
deadline extension, the setting of a dedicated process to ensure that breakthrough innovations 
which address an unmet need reach patients in a timely manner is still missing . 
 
Breakthrough innovation  in this field , as defined in the EU Regulation on Health Technology 
Assessment (Regulation (EU) 2021/2282, Article 7, point 4), 1 encompasses medical devices and in 
vitro diagnostic medical devices which either meet unmet medical needs, are first in class, have a 
potential impact on patients, public health or healthcare systems, incorporate software using artificial 
intelligence, machine learning technologies or algorithms, have a significant cross -border dimension, 
or bring major EU-wide added value. 
 
1. The EU Medical Device Regulation – placing patient safety at the centre 
Europe has one of the world’s strongest regulatory frameworks for medical devices : the EU Medical 
Device Regulation (MDR), which came into force in 2017 and is in application since 2021, was an 
important milestone for patients in Europe, placing their safety at the centre.  
This is an exciting time for the medical devices sector in Europe, with cooperation ongoing to ensure 
that the transition under the new legal framework is as smooth as possible. But the work is far from 
over, and some key challenges remain to be overcome, beyond the certification and recertification of 
all existing devices in compliance with the MDR by the May 2024 deadline. What is currently 
missing in the discussion is the need to ensure that breakthrough innovations which address 
an unmet need reach patients fast enough.   
 
2. The need to accelerate patient access to breakthrough innovation 
The EU has clear ambition to become a global leader in scientific and technological innovation, 2 yet 
patients in Europe may still miss access to life -saving breakthrough technologies. This is because 
innovators’ journeys to bring such highly innovative health technologies to patients with unmet needs 
in a timely and harmonised manner is long and complex , and becoming more so,  from a regulatory 
point of view,3 which risks delaying patient access to life-saving medical devices.  
The development of innovative or breakthrough health technologies takes a long time. For instance, a 
Class III medical device can take more than 10 years from the first prototype to its ultimate market 
introduction.4 Throughout the development phases, innovative technologies have to successfully go 
through a series of testing and evidence generation phases, as well as post -market monitoring and 
evaluation.

Before being placed on the European market, all medical devices must be certified by a designated 
Notified Body. Currently, time- to-certification for a new certificate takes between 6- 18 months in the 
majority of cases and can be as long as 24 months5– a situation exacerbated by certification bottlenecks 
ahead of the 2024 deadline.  
Ensuring the safety of new devices before they reach patients is crucial. However, for breakthrough 
technologies that address unmet needs, patients can be harmed just as much by delays to approval 
for these lifesaving or life-changing medical devices. 
The EU MDR provides a robust , high -quality regulatory framework for medical devices in Europe. 
However, there is no dedicated process to facilitate the development and expedite the review of 
innovative devices which treat serious  and life-threatening conditions and address an unmet medical 
need.  
 
3. A possible way forward: A priority review process for breakthrough medical devices  
Adopting a specific process that allows to prioritize the review of breakthrough technologies would help 
support the patients that are most in need of these innovations. Such a process would get these devices 
to the patient earlier, while securing the highest level of safety and best clinical evidence at different 
stages of the clinical development and utilization.  
Today, international standards and MEDDEV guidance already recommend - in exceptional cases 
when major benefits justify relatively high levels of uncertainty - granting technologies that address 
unmet needs
6 access to the market based on limited clinical evidence and stringent post market clinical 
follow-up plans.  
A priority review process already exists or is planned in other areas  and could be looked at as a 
reference.  
 The European Medicines Agency (EMA) may grant a conditional marketing authorization for 
medicines that address unmet needs. This means that for such medicines less comprehensive 
clinical data is allowed than normally required, where the benefit of immediate availability of the 
medicine outweighs the risk inherent in the fact that additional data is still required.7 
 In the US, the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has introduced a voluntary Fast-Track 
Breakthrough Program for certain medical devices and device-led combination products 
that provide for more effective treatment or diagnosis of life-threatening or irreversibly debilitating 
diseases or conditions.8 
 In the UK, new plans to strengthen the regulation of medical devices were published in June 
2022. The updates intend to support the availability of medical devices across the  UK, whilst 
ensuring the highest quality and safety standards. This includes introducing alternative routes 
to market to support increased availability of innovative devices and streamlining access to 
the UK market.9 
An EU priority review process for breakthrough medical devices addressing unmet needs would 
make sure that the EU does not lag behind in access to breakthrough innovations.  
Ultimately, it would support the EU to improve patient access to lifesaving and life- changing 
breakthrough innovation, facilitate and optimize collaboration between Notified Bodies, authorities, 
manufacturers, and stakeholders, and contribute to improving clinical and innovation excellence in the 
EU.

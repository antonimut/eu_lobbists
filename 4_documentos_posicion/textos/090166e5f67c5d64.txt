AGU FEEDBACK TO EUROPEAN VEHICLE EMISSIONS 
STANDARDS – EURO 7 FOR CARS , VANS, LORR IES AND 
BUSES 
AGU, a CEFIC Sector Group, welcomes the opportunity to share its 
feedback on the European Commission’s legislative proposal for Euro 7 
emissions standards for cars and heavy-duty vehicles. 
About AGU.  
European urea producers are represented by the Automotive Grade Urea Sector Group  (AGU), a 
sector group of Cefic, European Chemical Industry Council. AGU is based in Brussels and groups 
six major European urea producers dedicated to the responsible usage of NOx r eduction agent 
AUS 32, also known as AdBlue®. 
  
Timely adoption of new standards needed.  
AGU welcomes the Commission’s legislative proposal for the Euro 7/VII emission standards for 
cars, vans, lorries and buses. We encourage the co-legislators to swiftly adopt the proposal, 
preferably before the next EU elections in Q2 2024, in order for all actors across the value chain 
to be ready to comply with the new standards at the time of application of the new standards. 
  
How high-quality reagents help OEMs meet NOx standards.  
AGU stands ready to provide the Commission and co -legislators with evidence as to how OEMs 
can meet the to -be-agreed NOx emissions standards. By far the best way to decrease NOx 
emissions without affec ting overall engine performance is through the after -treatment system, 
such as selective catalytic reduction (SCR), which is now recognised as the best available 
technology to reduce NOx. By properly designing the after -treatment system, the engine -out 
emissions can be reduced by more than 98 %. Essential for this purpose is the reagent, which is 
basically a solution of urea in water. In Europe, this reagent is known under its trade name 
AdBlue®. AdBlue® is a pure colourless liquid injected into the SCR sys tem’s catalyst, where it 
triggers a chemical reaction with ammonia. This chemical reaction converts the toxic NOx into 
nitrogen (N2) and water vapour (H2O). Water vapour and nitrogen are naturally occurring gasses 
that are harmless to the environment. The quality of the urea solution is essential to ensure 
reliable and stable operation of the SCR system and is defined in the ISO 22241.

High-quality reagents ensure continued compliance with durability requirements.  
The Euro 7/VII legislative proposal strengthens the durability requirements compared to Euro 6/VI, 
for vehicles to comply with the standards as much as, depending on the vehicle category, up to 
875,000 kilometres and 15 years of age. In order for the future emissions standards to be met over 
such a period, all components and systems within the vehicle need to function properly, including 
the aftertreatment system. Adequate and high-quality reagent use is essential to ensure durability 
of such systems over the longevity that will be required under Euro 7/VII. AGU members believe 
that the use of reagents of low quality and non -compliant with ISO 22241 may lead to the 
aftertreatment system’s malfunctioning and subsequently to non -compliance of OEMs with the 
emissions standards. In fact, high-quality reagents are readily available on the market. Both their 
use and quality should be upheld and promoted through adequate regulation to ensure that OEMs 
will be able to meet the emissions standards under Euro 7/VII.  
  
AGU is ready to cooperate to facilitate Euro 7/VII implementation.  
AGU members therefore stand ready to not only inform the Commission and co-legislators about 
the importance of high-quality reagent use, but also to actively participate in the upcoming AGVES 
expert group meetings and support the  Commission in developing an enabling regulatory 
framework to allow for the actual enforcement of high-quality reagent use for both LDV and HDV 
purposes.

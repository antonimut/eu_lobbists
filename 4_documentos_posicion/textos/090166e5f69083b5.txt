Act. However, Schneider Electric wishes to suggest modifications 
allowing more alignment with the specificities of the industry.

Our overall feedback 
 
Schneider Electric supports the European Commission’s objective to create a 
uniform legal framework of essential cybersecurity requirements for products 
with digital elements. This initiative will help reduce regulation fragmentation, 
increase product security and trust for the European market.  
 
Schneider Electric has been deploying secure development processes and 
implementing cyber security in its products and systems, based on EN IEC 
62443 over the years. Schneider Electric is confident that the Cyber Resilience 
Act high level objectives are aligned with its corporate cyber security policy . 
 
However, Schneider Electric highlights that the industrial sector has 
particularities that must be observed:  
• its legacy base where replacement products and spare parts are 
needed for industry maintenance.  
• industrial setup needs to evolve and follow market needs : industrial 
products must therefore ensure long-term interoperability to secure 
industry investments.  
 
To take those specificities into account in the context of the Cyber Resilience 
Act, Schneider Electric wish to highlight the following issues and solutions.

Conformity assessment (article 24)  
 
The need to build trust of users, as well as to establish legal certainty in the 
market, are objectives which have been identified by the industry a few 
decades ago. Schneider Electric therefore wishes to emphasize the long-term 
continuous investment done by the whole industry on deploying secure 
development processes and implementing security in the existing portfolio of 
products based on European -recognized standards such as the EN IEC 
62443 (recognized by CEN-CENELEC).  
 
Under the current wording of the Cyber Resilience Act, the long-term industry 
investment is penalized by the creation of a new referential and the doubling 
up of third-party certification efforts.  
 
Our recommendation: 
 
Schneider Electric would therefore encourage the European institutions to 
take the long-term industry investment into account and to value it by creating 
a compatibility mechanism.  
 
This compatibility mechanism  could take several shapes such as the 
referencing by harmonized standards or CSA certification scheme of  already 
recognized industrial security standards frameworks (such as EN IEC 62443) 
including associated available IACS certification schemes operated by 
accredited European Conformity Assessment Bodies (CAB) actors . T he 
referred cybersecurity ecosystem also allows European industry to have an 
international reach and market recognition inside and outside Europe for their 
products with digital elements.  
Implementation p eriod (article 57).  
 
The proposed timeline of 24 months would not allow the industry nor the 
conformity and certification assessment ecosystem to transition in a reasoned 
and efficient manner. Reflecting on the specificities of the industry referred to 
above, customers running legacy offers will need to ensure,  in cooperation with 
manufacturers, that their products can be replaced, updated and assured for 
interoperability in time. To do so, more time is required. 
 
Our recommendation: 
 
We believe that an overall implementation period of 48 months, or four years, 
would be more realistic for the industry.

Reporting obligation (article 11)  
 
While reporting of incidents and vulnerabilities has becom e an integral part of 
the cybersecurity collaborative effort to reduce threat, this should not hinder the 
market by imposing burdensome as well as differing requirements depending on 
which legislation one entity would have to follow or not.  
 
Our recommendations: 
 
Schneider Electric encourages a revision of notification obligations by: 
• Notifying only significant incidents. 
• Implementing a v oluntary notification of vulnerability following ENISA 
vulnerability disclosure process. 
• Revising the timeframe to be consistent with the NIS2 framework (24h 
early warning followed by a 72h notification and a one-month final report). 
Known exploitable vulnerabilities (Annexe I (1.2))  
 
The definition of “known exploitable vulnerabilities”  is not precise enough to 
provide the legal certainty needed  to the market , risking a supply chain 
shortage.  
 
Our recommendation: 
 
There is a need to have a clear reference to a European or worldwide public 
vulnerability list  or database, re cognized by the Cyber Resilience Act . The 
ENISA vulnerability database mandated under NIS 2 directive 1 could be a 
good reference to this essential cybersecurity requirement. 
Free patching (Annexe I (8))  
In the industrial sector, while many patching are ma de available free of 
charge, the criticality and complexity of industrial systems and installations 
have led industrial manufacturers  to provide personalized services to 
clients to push , or install,  the patching  on the product or system. While the 
patching itself is free of charge, the personalized service to push and 
install the patch is commercialized under contractual agreement.  
Our recommendation : 
We encourage the co -legislators to clarify the text on the definition of 
‘disseminated’ stating its limit to making available of the patch and not its 
installation on the product, that is subject to industry process related 
specifics ( automation, control and safety).  
 
 
 
1 As requested by NIS2, ENISA is responsible to deliver and maintain an European database which has to be consistent to worldwide vulnerability databases.

Clarification of the methodologies used  
Overall, the industry would benefit from the clarification of the 
methodologies used in the Cyber Resilience Act for:  
• Product classification methodology: The industry needs to have a 
guide and criteria for product classification selection (including 
highly critical product), considering the product intended use and 
associated risks.  
• Risk assessment methodology: The indus try needs to have risk 
assessment methodology selection and guidance to manage in a 
consistent way the risk assessment requested by the Cyber 
Resilience Act . It will also allow to streamline the process with the 
different actors including 3rd party notified bo dy.  
• Conformity assessment methodologies: The conformity 
assessment methodologies used in the different NLF modules 
requested by the Cyber Resilience Act  must be standardized and 
public. 
Red Delegated Act: interplay with the CRA  
The parallel existence of the RED Delegated Act and the Cyber Resilience 
Act creates legal uncertainty in the market. We encourage European 
institutions to clarify the interplay between those two texts in terms . 
Our recommendation s: 
Schneider Electric encourages the institut ions to clarify the situation by 
repealing the RED Delegated Act and keeping the Cyber Resilience Act 
as the legislation on the cybersecurity of digital products.  
However, we strongly encourage to leverage the standardization and 
certification process work done by CEN -CENELEC up until now , under the 
mandate of the RED Delegated Act , in the upcoming standardization and 
certification work for the Cyber Resilience Act.

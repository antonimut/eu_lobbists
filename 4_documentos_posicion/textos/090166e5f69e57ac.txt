I/ Introduction  
 
Alcatel-Lucent Enterprise’s  (“ALE”) vision is to deliver the customized technology 
experiences our customers need. Our mission is to make everything connect by delivering 
digital age networking, communications and cloud solutions with services tailored for your 
business success. In the Cloud, On Premises, Hybrid. 
 
ALE is committed to developing technology solutions that work for our customers, 
connecting people, machine, things and processes, while creating a more sustainable future 
for all. Alcatel -Lucent Enterprise takes pride in its commitment to compliance and

Therefore, we support the legitimate goal of strengthening the cybersecurity of digital

Nevertheless, the provisions detailed below remain unclear and we do call for  some

II/ Question n°1: 24-hour clock of Article 11   
 
As a reminder, according to Article 11 “Reporting obligations of manufacturers":  
 
“The manufacturer shall, without undue delay and in any event within 24 hours of 
becoming aware of it, notify to ENISA any actively exploited vulnerability contained in the 
product with digital elements. The notification shall include details concerning that 
vulnerability and, where applicable, any corrective or mitigating measures taken. 
ENISA shall, without undue delay, unless for justified cybersecurity risk-related grounds, 
forward the notification to the CSIRT designated for the purposes of coordinated 
vulnerability disclosure in accordance with Article [Article X] of Directive [Directive 
XXX/XXXX (NIS2)] of Member States concerned upon receipt and info rm the market 
surveillance authority about the notified vulnerability.” 
 
Comments:  
 
The start of the 24-hour clock remains uncertain for us.  
 
When does the 24-hour clock start? 
• When the manufacturer receives a Common Vulnerability Exposure notification? 
• Once the manufacturer  has analysed the Common Vulnerability Exposure  and 
concluded that the product is potentially vulnerable? or  
• When there is an active exploit reported for the vulnerability?   
 
The delay o f 24 hours may induce more risk exposure of having more exploits without a 
remediation has been actively put in place.

The term ‘actively exploited vulnerability’ should be clarified, we propose that the 72-hour

CRA must also specify the list of trustable databases related to vulnerabilities and exploits.  
 
III/ Question n°2: Article 55 and former products  
 
As a reminder, according to Article 55 “Transitional provisions”:  
 
“1. EU type-examination certificates and approval decisions issued regarding cybersecurity 
requirements for products with digital elements that are subje ct to other Union 
harmonisation legislation shall remain valid until [42 months after the date of entry into 
force of this Regulation], unless they expire before that date, or unless otherwise specified 
in other Union legislation, in which case they shall remain valid as referred to in that Union 
legislation. 
2. Products with digital elements that have been placed on the market before [date of 
application of this Regulation referred to in Article 57], shall be subject to requirements 
of this Regulation only if, from that date, those products are subject to substantial 
modifications in their design or intended purpose. 
3. By way of derogation from paragraph 2, the obligations laid down in Article 11 shall 
apply to all products with digital elements within the scope of this Regulation that 
have been placed on the market before  [date of application of this Regulation refer red 
to in Article 57].”  
 
Comments:  
 
This section 3 of Article 55 does not contain any limitation backward in time. This is a main 
concern that for older and end-of-life products, research and development or intellectual 
properties might have been removed from our resources. In this respect, w e consider that 
this should not apply to products considered as end of life but only for the product for which 
lifetime period has not expired.  
 
Is it possible that the word “not” was mistakenly dropped from this text and that it should 
instead read “shall not apply”?

IV/ Question n°3: patches provided by manufacturers.  
 
As a reminder, according to Article 10 “Obligations of manufacturer”: 
 
“6. When placing a product with digital elements on the market, and for the expected 
product lifetime or for a period of five years from the placing of the product on the market, 
whichever is shorter, manufacturers shall ensure that vulnerabilities of that product 
are handled effectively and in accordance with the essential requirements set out in 
Section 2 of Annex I.” 
 
Annex 1, Section 2, provides that: 
 
“8. ensure that, where security patches or updates are available to address identified 
security issues, they are disseminated without delay and free of charge, accompanied by 
advisory messages providing users with the relevant information, including on potential 
action to be taken.” 
 
(1) Security patches  
 
In a situation where the product expected lifetime is 2 years but for some customer, a 
support contract is signed with extended support beyond (i) 2 years but less than 5 years or 
(ii) 5 years or more, we assume that , as support contracts beyond the product end of life 
date are beyond the product expected lifetime, we are no longer obliged to issue security 
patches free of charge and that we could charge such patches.  
 
We consider that major releases are another product, and we commercialize license 
extension (increment number of users)  on end-of-life product. In this context, do we 
consider as per section 10. 6 and annex 1.8 that manufacturer is obliged to provide security 
patch? ALE currently does not provide security patches on end-of-life product even though 
licenses extension (increment number of users) is authorized.  
 
Recommendation:  
 
We propose to consider that incrementing number of users  (or devices ) for the same 
purposes shall not be treated as new product. That means that it doesn’t change the 
previous security patches duration.   
 
In other words, capacity extension for the same set of purposes is not triggering a new 
lifetime extension.  
 
(2) Product lifetime definition

“Critical products with digital elements shall be subject to the conformity assessment

in Annex VI.”

“Where the product is a critical product with digital elements of class II as set out in Annex

VII/ Question n°6: “substantial modification” definition   
 
As a reminder, Recital 22 provides that:  
 
“(22) In order to ensure that products with digital elements, when placed on the market, 
do not pose cybersecurity risks to persons and organisations, essential requirements should 
be set out for such products. When the products are subsequently modified, by physical 
or digital means, in a way that is not foreseen by the manufacturer and that may imply 
that they no longer meet the relevant essential requirements, the modification should 
be considered as substantial . For example, software updates or repairs could be 
assimilated to maintenance operations provided that they do not modify a product already 
placed on the market in such a way that compliance with the applicable requirements may 
be affected, or that the intended use for which the product has been assessed may be 
changed. As is the case for physical repairs or modifications, a product with digital 
elements should be considered as substantially modified by a software change where the 
software update modifie s the original intended functions, type or performance of the 
product and these changes were not foreseen in the initial risk assessment, or the nature 
of the hazard has changed or the level of risk has increased because of the software 
update.” 
 
Comments:  
 
As defined above, “substantial modification” must be modified and restricted to the major 
release delivered by the manufacturer.

VIII/ Question n°7: Risk assessment  
 
As a reminder, according to Annex I:  
 
“Products with digital elements shall be delivered without any known exploitable 
vulnerabilities;” 
 
Comments:  
 
Providing a product “without any known exploitable vulnerabilities” seems difficult, even 
impossible.  
Product security may be impacted by many factors, such as the changing nature of 
cyberattacks. Such a requirement could be counterproductive, as it would  discourage 
manufacturers from conducting certain security tests, which may potentially  increase 
number of "unknown" vulnerabilities.  
Moreover, the fact that a vulnerability is actively exploited cannot be formally established.  
 
Recommendation:  
 
We suggest changing the wording for:  
 
“Products with digital elements shall be delivered without any known actively exploited 
vulnerabilities to the knowledge of the manufacturer  that would come from a  
recommended trustable source e.g., CERT.”

X/ Question n°9: Conformity assessment procedures for products with digital elements 
 
Considering Article 6.4 which refers to article 24.2 and 24.3  regarding Conformity

For composite products that embed third party complex elements that needs to be

Recommendation:  
 
To avoid a cascade of non -conformities, we suggest introducing a grace period of e.g., 1

XII/ Question n°11:  
 
According to Recital 9:  
 
“This Regulation ensures a high level of cybersecurity of products with digital elements. It 
does not regulate services, such as Software-as-a-Service (SaaS), except for remote data 
processing solutions relating to a product with digital elements understood as any data 
processing at a distance for which the software is designed and de veloped by the 
manufacturer of the product concerned or under the responsibility of that 
manufacturer, and the absence of which would prevent such a product with digital 
elements from performing one of its functions.” 
 
Comments:  
 
If a product uses SaaS services, that product necessarily uses API libraries to use that service. 
If SaaS API libraries were to be excluded from CRA as per Recital 9 confusing definition, then 
products will embed non-compliant components.  
 
Recommendation:  
 
Commission’s choice must be defined and fair regarding compliance rules for every product 
delivery model (SaaS or purchase).

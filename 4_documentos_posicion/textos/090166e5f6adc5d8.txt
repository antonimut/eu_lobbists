DI POSITION ON THE 
CYBER RECILIENCE ACT
Ref. Ares(2023)490497 - 23/01/2023
Regulation: 
Aspect
Horizontal approach based on 
NLF-principles
Positive, will ease compliance. 
Challenges when it comes to software. 
Relationship to other 
regulations
Good the regulation aims at cleaning up the patchwork of legislations that is 
underway on cybersecurity. Would like to have included in the legal text that the 
RED DA will be repealed or amended when the CRA comes into application and how 
the transition is foreseen. 
Need for alignment and no overlapping requirements with NIS 2  and clarifications 
when it comes to the GPSR and the AIR when it comes to conformity for non-high 
risk AI systems.
Minimum requirements for all 
products
Yes, in combination with lex.specialis where needed for instance medical devices. 
In the future lex.specialis should always built on same principles as the CRA.
Risk based approach to 
conformity assessment
Agree. Need to ensure the right criteria for criticality 1 and 2 so the assurance level 
is proportionate to the risk. At the same time, we reserve 3rd party capacity for those 
who don’t have the needed skills for conformity assessment themselves. 
Insufficient impact 
assessment
The business case for the proposal is weak. Thus, the need to ensure proportionality 
in the requirements and to secure sufficient time before application.
The Scope of Digital Products
Aspect
Tangible digital products with 
or without embedded 
software
OK, however, not all direct or indirect logical or physical data connection to a device or 
network need protection and are susceptible to cyber attack. Only those that are 
susceptible to attacks need to be protected – Implications for definition of scope.
It also needs to be clarified, the definition includes the remote data processing(if any). 
See also below regarding overlap with NIS2.
Stand alone software
i.e. Software as a Product and 
Software as a Service
OK, but difficult as a completely new domain. Need guidance from the Commission.
Software as a service – included when needed for the functionality and provided by the 
manufacturer. Need for further clarification for instance the software that forms the cloud 
or the software running on the cloud – or both. NIS2 takes care of most critical software 
as a service including cloud.
Components Should be taken out. When placed on the market they are a finished product. The 
definition of digital products should be revised accordingly. The concept of party 
completed products with digital elements could be introduced, an NLF concept known 
from the Machinery Regúlation (cannot function in itself - intended for incorporation)
Spare parts Should be exempt. Otherwise, a lot of machinery will be impossible to maintain.
Scope, concluding statement Important with clarification, simplification and to limit overlap with NIS2 and product 
regulations. Alignment of definitions with other legislation is needed
Software specific issues
• Substantial modification of software – continuous updating of DOC
• Recovery of DOC’s for10 years vs. 5 year or life cycle 
• Placing on the market
• To be further explored
• Challenge
• Software developers have no knowledge of NLF
Characterisation of Criticality of Products
Aspect
Criteria to determine if 
conformity assessment 
involving 3rd. party is needed.
Agree on risk-based principle. But criteria are too wage also when it comes to what is 
required for critical 1 and critical 2, respectively.
Differentiation of requirements are needed for critical 1 and 2
Basis for inclusion Evidence based evaluation is needed to determine if criteria apply. Only if they apply, the 
product category should be included.
Should be based on manufacturers intended use. Therefore, the lists most likely would 
have to be shortened. General purpose microprocessors should me moved to critical 1.
Industrial application It is assumed industrial application is always sensitive.
That is not the case. Issue that robots always have to undergo 3rd. party. Change to “Safety 
related parts of control systems for industrial machinery”. Robots as such are not critical 2.
Definition of product 
categories
The Commission allow itself one year to finalize the definitions of the products included as 
critical 1 and 2. In that case the manufacturers will need more time to become compliant 
(See also transition period)
Highly critical products The Commission allow itself to define categories of highly critical products which needs to 
undergo mandatory certification. We question the need which is already possible under 
NIS 2. According to the CRA only Member States can opt for mandatory certification.
Criticality of products 
conclusion
Risk based approach is good, but a lot of work is needed to substantiate the categorization 
of products and their definition
Product Requirements
Aspect
Link to RED DA It is positive the requirements are built on the standardisation request for RED DA.
However, two mayor differences between RED DA and CRA exit.
a) Scope: internet connected products vs products with digital elements
b) Time of placing on the market vs whole life cycle
Experiences from the standardisation work needs to be taken into account when finalizing the wording 
of the requirements. Furthermore, the requirements should take into account current applied state-of-
the-art in the form of international standards. Implications for application date (see later). To the 
widest possible extent existing international standards needs to be taken on board to built on already 
implemented work to make products cybersecure, ease compliance and ease global trade.
The requirement only to 
process data of relevance 
to intended use
“minimization of data” limits the development of new business models (and work against the purpose 
of the data act). Relevance of this requirement is questioned.
Requirement only to 
deliver  products without 
any known exploitable 
vulnerabilities
Requirement is not possible to fulfill, as it will disrupt  continuous market access of a launched product. 
Challenges also as to what grant “known” vulnerabilities. ENISA could made responsible for updating 
a registry.
Special requirements for 
SMVs
DI does not believe the product requirements for SMVs  should differentiate from larger companies. 
Burden reduction could be possible at the level of reporting obligations, if any.
Applicability of 
requirements
Positive with NLF alignment ie. only requirements that the manufacturer believes are relevant based 
on the risk assessment applies. Flexibility needed given the very broad scope of the CRA.
Vulnerability Handling Requirements
Aspect
Process requirements to 
handle vulnerabilities
DI supports the use of process requirements to address vulnerabilities through out 
the life cycle
SBOM – software build of 
materials
SBOM should be part of the technical file and only be required to be provided at the 
request of the market surveillance authority. 
It contains confidential information that needs protection.
To make it public available (together with vulnerabilities) is also counterproductive, 
as it will make it easier for hackers to find and exploit the vulnerabilities.
The concept of SBOM is still very new. Guidance rather than a binding implementing 
act is needed regarding the design,
Information without delay Vulnerability management increases the competitive advantage of companies.
In due time/ without unnecessary delay might seem more reasonable.
Necessary to stress confidentiality when it comes to reporting to ENISA
Information Obligations
Aspect
Both e-mail and postal 
address
DI believes the need for a postal address is redundant. An e-mail address should be 
sufficient.
SBOM availability DI believes manufacturers should only be obliged to share the SBOM at the request 
of the market surveillance authorities (see vulnerability handling requirements)
Freedom to choose format of 
information obligations
DI appreciates the possibility of manufacturers to choose to provide the information 
required in either electronic or physical form (Article 10.1)
Mandatory delivery of DOC DI question the need as the DOC is only a tool for market surveillance authorities
Information obligations Looks OK for most parts.
T echnical specifications
Aspect
Use of harmonized standards
hEN
DI appreciates the application of hEN as a means to obtain presumption of 
conformity (in line with other NLF legislation). The hEN should build on the ones 
developed under RED DA with a view to the standards that are already applied 
internationally to ease compliance (international state-of-the-art)
Common specifications DI believes common specifications should only be used as the last resort and when 
certain requirements are fulfilled(see position papers from BE and Orgalim).
Certification schemes DI is concerned about the process in which certification schemes under the CSA is 
developed (not open, non-transparent, non-inclusive processes). At the same time, 
we do believe it should be possible for companies that have be certified to a scheme 
that fulfills the requirements of the CRA to use this as a mean to obtain presumption 
of conformity  to ensure they don’t have to engage with yet another 3rd. party ie
notified body under the CRA to comply (critical 2 products).
Technical specifications in 
general
hEN should be the preferred option when it comes to granting presumption of 
conformity 
Notification/Reporting Obligations
Aspect
Alignment with NIS 2 
(and GDPR)
The same manufacturers/entities have reporting obligations under both the GDPR, NIS and CRA for 
the same event. Attempts were made to align obligations under the former two, but not successful. 
It is of outmost importance efforts are made to lessen unnecessary burden under CRA.
Timeframe and target for 
notifications
Obligations were heavily debated under NIS 2. Advisable to use the outcome of the negotiations to 
align. In the CRA manufacturers report to ENISA within 24 hours when actively exploitable 
vulnerabilities or incidents have taken place. In NIS 2 reporting is done to national CSIRTS for any 
incident that has a significant impact on the provision of their services. An early warning within 24 
hours and more information within 72 hours.  DI believes  CRA should apply 72 hours and only 
require notification of incidents which have significant impact (are severe). If needed reporting on 
actively exploitable vulnerabilities could be added after an evaluation for instance 5 years after the 
legislation is coming into application. Double notification should be avoided. 
ENISA’s role DI questions ENISAs ability to take on board the task of coordinating vulnerability reporting for 
millions of products across the EU. There is a need  to evaluate thoroughly the link between ENISA, 
CSIRTS, MSA etc. and the value added by the information exchange. ENISA could be charged with 
maintaining a database of actively exploitable vulnerabilities.
Proportionality for SMV’s Reporting within 24 hours require 24/7 surveillance. That may create major challenges especially to 
smaller companies. Furthermore, the lifecycle perspective makes it very burdensome.
Application Date
Aspect
hEN to provide for presumption of 
conformity 
The transition period for the regulation is 24 month. Even though the hENs are to be built 
on the hENs under the RED DA that is very short time, since
-standardisation under CRA cannot start before the regulation is published
-the RED DA standards are likely to be delayed
-it takes 2 year to develop the needed standards
-it takes  1- 2 years for companies to implement the standards
48 months transition would be more realistic.
Reporting obligation applies 12 
month before the regulation
The reporting obligations apply one year after the regulation comes into force. 
DI questions the ability to provide the framework for such reporting both when it comes to 
ENISA, then national competent authorities and the companies.
Furthermore it is unclear which products need reporting about. All digital products 
regardless when they were placed on the market ?
Definition of critical 1 and critical 
2
The Commission has one year after the regulation comes into force to develop definitions 
for critical 1 and critical 2 products. This allow only one year to engage with notified 
bodies, if needed. That is too short. Also time to build capacity is needed.
Role of notified body and capacity 
of notified bodies
If the hENs are not available, then 3rd party need to be involved for critical 1 and critical 2 
products. This require even more capacity building of notified bodies under the CRA. At 
present only 72 notified bodies exist under RED, few of which has competences within 
cybersecurity. This also calls for longer transition time.  
Market Surveiliance, sanctions etc.
Aspect
(EU)1020/2019 Positive the CRA falls under the market surveillance regulation
Sanctions Compared to non-compliance with other product regulation the fines are extremely high. 
Even for formal non-compliance they are disproportionate and not aligned with the 
(EU)1020/2019. The implication is that cybersecurity is more important than safety.
It needs to be ensured fines can only be issued under one legislation for the same offence
Competence and 
capacity of market 
surveillance 
authorities
As the cost of being compliant is significant it is of outmost importance to ensure sufficient 
capacity to perform market surveillance. IT cybersecurity is a know domain, CRA requires 
knowhow in product security which is different. On the other hand the role of processes is 
new in product regulation and goes beyond the knowledge of current MSA.  
Furthermore, it is needed to stress guidance before sanctioning.
It may be an idea to suggest article 9 activities from (EU)1020/2019 in the recitals ie
collaboration with businesses and other stakeholders on market surveillance. 
Sandboxing In order to ensure innovation and development of secure solutions a sandboxing concept 
similar to that of the AI regulation could be developed..

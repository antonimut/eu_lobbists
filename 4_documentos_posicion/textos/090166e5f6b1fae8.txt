AMI COMMENTS ON THE PROPOSED MEDIA FREEDOM ACT (MFA) 
 
Asociación de Medios de Información (AMI) is the Spanish association created in 197 7, short 
before the Spanish democratic Constitution was passed. Our association  represents the 
interests of the news media industry in Spain, and holds the largest representation of news 
media in the Spanish market. We enclose as Annex 1 the list of our associates. 
The core of our organization is the defense of press freedom and we take our role very seriously. 
We believe that media play a crucial role in the existence and safeguard of democratic societies 
and also in the education and promotion of fundamental  rights, digitalization  and green 
transition.  
Asociación de Medios de Información appreciates the recognition of the role that media play in 
society by the proposed Media Freedom Act and  also the sensitivity that the European 
Commission is showing in different initiatives to support the media that are under challenge by 
virtue of the digital ecosystem where new major players control the advertisement market and 
also content distribution. 
Despite that, and with all due respect, Asociación de Medios de Información would like the 
Commission to reconsider the opportunity to launch the  Media Freedom Act, as by virtue of 
Article 167 TFEU media regulation is a matter of national competence. The media represented 
by Asociación de Medios de Información are committed to deliver good journalism to  our 
readers, and we hold accountable for the work we do both in the trust of our readers and also 
in our financial responsibility in  Courts in case of any wrongdoing.  The Spanish Constitution 
protects our work and we are responsible for it in the framework of the Spanish legal system. 
Creating a Media Freedom Act will impose additional obligations on the media, and this industry 
has been always self regulated and only limited by the Constitution. Also, the Spanish media 
market shows that international investors are welcomed in this market and s ome of major 
media companies are controlled by other European non Spanish investors with no burdens or 
limits.  
We hope that the European Commission reconsiders its competence to pass  the Media 
Freedom Act and therefore abandons this project.  
Considering the possibility that the European Commission may maintain the MFA, here are some 
ideas to contribute to improve the proposed MFA:  
- We highly encourage the Commission to withdraw from the proposed MDA all the wording 
that questions the quality and independence of European media (p.e page 2, paragrah 2).

The current proposed wording is aligned with those that perman ently work in the erosion 
of the credibility of the media, and as a result of it, citizens r isk to look at the media with 
skepticism. Media are definitely not always right in their proceedings but all media are hold 
responsible in Courts and also in front of citizens. The Spanish media believe that this very 
important fact needs to be respected and given value. The current wording of the MFA  
raises a question of trust in the media that will cause the opposite effect of the goal that 
the Commission is seeking. We therefore encourage the Commission to review the wording 
of the MFA to address its concerns without causing further damage to the media.  
 
- With regard to article 6, we understand that paragraph 6.2.a, should be fully reviewed as 
its content does not respect ownership of the media. Media owners invest their resources 
in the running of the business and are also liable in Court for the content that is published 
in their news titles  -according to the Spanis h legislation , the news company  is fully 
responsible for any damage that arises from the content published by any news title -. In 
Law, the existence of any responsibili ty requires control to avoid the potential damage. 
The current wording that the Commission is including in the MFA deprives control from 
the owner when the responsibility of the news title in terms of financing  and Court 
remains fully in the hands of the owner.   
 
- With regard to article 6.2. b) we suggest the wording to be reviewed: we understand that  
conflicts of interest should be disclosed if they affect the editorial line of the news -not the 
provision of news itself-.  
 
- We understand that  article 6.3 should be fully removed from the MFA. The transparency 
obligation that is settle d under article 6 should be applied to all media companies, 
regardless what their size is. We see no reason to create an exemption on ownership and 
conflict of interest transparency for micro enterprises. These companies are media 
companies, companies that have as core business providing information about other people 
and interests, and surely are able to provide information about themselves to the market. 
We see many times that extreme political parties are creating reasonably small media 
services that prod uce viral content to distribute on line , with high reach and polarization. 
The Commission should not grant a shield to these media companies that should also be 
accountable and transparent.  
 
- While we applaud the creation of a European Board of Media Servi ces that will promote 
exchange of information among member states and coordinates measures related to 
dissemination of content from media service providers established outside the European

Union, we suggest that the European Board of Media Services needs to give an opinion 
on media concentrations. Our existing legal system, both at national and European level, 
already has antitrust institutions that review and pass resolutions on concentrations, and 
they already review the impact that any transaction may have on the media market and 
therefore on media pluralism.  These antitrust institutions already clear or deny 
concentrations, and may well also impose conditions for a market transaction. It is 
important to understand that media concentrations are needed in order to keep on having 
sustainable media, even if this may have an impact on media pluralism. The fact that a new 
regulator intervenes in media concentrations poses a very high risk to either impede  
transactions tha t are needed in the market or to delay the speed that is needed to 
accomplish these transactions.  
 
- As for article 17, we suggest that the Commission goes much further in its attempt to 
protect the media from Very Large Online Platforms. Very Large Online Platforms certainly 
have a crucial role In the distribution of news content and their interests is very often not 
aligned with the interest of media companies. Very Large Online Platforms have regularly 
conflict with media companies. Thes e companies also often impose news companies a 
royalty free license to distribute news contents on their platforms, forcing media companies 
to withdraw the value of the publisher neighboring right. Also, in this context, Very Large 
Platforms may decide to withdraw news content from their platforms – the Media Freedom 
Act uses the word “suspend” but what Very Large Platforms do with news content is not a 
suspension, but to  retire and demonetize news content from their platforms.  Language 
should be more specific: when Very Large Platforms are “suspending” news content, what 
they are really doing is “ removing” news content, censoring news content, because news 
content value is extremely fragile and deeply linked to the time in which it is distributed. 
Once a certain piece of news is “suspended”, it is almost of no future value because by the 
time it might be up again, that news content will be of no interest to the audience at it will 
be “old and faded”.- If the Commission wants to strength the news media industry as a key 
player for citizens information, article 17 should go much further and not entitle Very 
Large Platforms to retire or demonetize news content from their platforms.  Considering 
that the audience is largely on social media, we must ensure that news distribution will be 
granted in these platforms and not rejected by Very Large Platforms as a result of copyright 
issues or other possible disputes with publishers . Otherwise, we risk  a large part of our 
population not having any exposure to news.   
 
- As for article 21, we understand that no more regulation is needed in media 
concentrations. Media services are already subject to regular control of concentrations. The 
most dangerous issue for media independence is either having financially struggling media

incredibly difficult to define the market: we are seeing that many journalists that previously 
worked for legacy media have started their own media projects, acting as small 
entrepreneurs -and sometimes not even that small -. Regulation should not be set up to 
seek control of traditional media organizations. The news media market has grown a lot in 
terms of number of outlets providing news information , as there are multiple means of 
delivering information thorough professional journalism in the digital market . T his 
fragmentation has not helped media business to be stro nger. Media markets need more 
concentration in order to gain scale and be competitive to innovate and be resilient . 
Legislation should not restraint this from happening. 
 
- As for article 23, audience measurement, here are some thoughts:  
 
i) Local media face difficulties when speaking  about audience measurements. 
The smaller a territory is where a media operates, the harder it gets to have a 
good position in audience measurement systems that have been designed 
mostly thinking about national media. This is not a trivial issue, because building 
audience systems designed for local media are extr emely expensive and 
therefore impossible to afford. We recommend the Commission to have a closer 
view on this issue as allocation of state advertising -including regional and local 
advertising- depending on rankings could have a devastating effect on local 
media that serve well the territories in which they are located but may not rank 
well in general news rankings.    
 
ii) Very Large Platforms should be obliged to facilitate audience measurement of 
news consumption on their walled gardens. This is not always happening today 
and it is having a huge impact on the market . In Spain, the new recommended 
measure in the market -GFK- had difficulties in measuring Facebook Instant 
Articles, Google Accele rated Mobile Pages, YouTube videos from the news 
industry. This situation is having a  huge impact in media audiences with its 
corresponding negative economic consequences for media. 
 
- With regard to article 24, on fair and transparent allocation of state advertising, rules on 
transparency and fairness should also be applied to platforms, as public institutions should 
also make publicly available how they spend public funding in platforms, that face challenges 
in different social and legal aspects.

- As for all the recommendations that come along with the proposed MFA,  we just think  
that the Commission should refrain from going this far.  Every company has the right to 
establish the procedures that will guide its business. Media companies also face important 
difficulties in dealing with their relations with their employees, as the difficulties of the P&L 
accounts have led to a number of costs cutting . The recommendations set up by the 
Commission may well lead to conflict with the newsrooms. We should focus our efforts in 
creating value for our readers, not in dealing with internal conflicts. 
 
We believe that the European Commission has the best inten tion in support ing media 
freedom. While we believe this to be true, the proposed MFA -in our opinion and with all due 
respect- lacks competence and will create further challenges to the media. If we want strong 
European media, we need:  
 
i) Advertising coming back to the media , with public in stitutions and private 
companies finding in our sites good audiences in safe ecosystems.  
 
ii) Further support for media subscriptions.  
 
iii) Strong rules that do not allow platforms neither to withdraw not to  
demonetize news content from their social networks  as these actions either  
lead to real censorship from the platforms or to self censorship from the media 
in order to avoid censorship from the platforms and therefore preventing their 
content from being monetized.  
 
iv) Strong rules that prevent SLACS from threatening the media  and rising costs 
that are difficult for the media to afford.    
 
v) Incentives for private companies to advertise their products in media, making 
it clear that when advertising in media companies  private companies are 
supporting democracy and this should be rewarded.  A very helpful initiative 
would be to categorize investment in media -both in terms of subscriptions and 
advertisement- as acti vities that comply with ESG principles  and therefore 
creating an incentive for companies to support the media.   
 
vi) A strong neighboring right that allows media companies to get a fair 
compensation for the use of their content in tech platforms  and economic 
support to build the studies and procedures that may be needed to achieve it. 
 
vii) Support for local journalism , taking into account the specific proble ms of the 
territories, where audience is many times difficult to measure and print

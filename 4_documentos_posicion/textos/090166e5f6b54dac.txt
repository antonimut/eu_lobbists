 i2Coalition Response to CRA consultation 
 Founded in 2012 by a diverse group of Internet infrastructure companies, the Internet 
 Infrastructure Coalition (i2Coalition) is a global organization that supports and represents the 
 companies that build and maintain the Internet’s infrastructure. Our members include cloud 
 providers, data centers, web hosting companies, domain registries and registrars, IXPs, content 
 delivery networks, network protection services, and other foundational Internet enterprises. 
 The i2Coalition appreciates this opportunity to provide public comments on the European 
 Commission’s Cyber Resilience Act (CRA). Our industry is committed to providing secure 
 products and to common, streamlined cybersecurity rules on the European market and globally. 
 The scope of the CRA is very wide, which means that the finer aspects of the legislation should 
 be discussed and thoroughly addressed. Specifically, the i2Coalition would like to note three 
 important issues, the vulnerability reporting requirements, non-alignment with the incident 
 reporting of NIS2, and a general comment on standards. 
 The text of the CRA would mandate the reporting of actively exploited vulnerabilities and 
 centralization within the European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA).  We believe that in 
 order to properly balance safety and transparency, the text should note the necessity of 
 disclosing actively exploited vulnerabilities after they are patched rather than when they 
 are discovered. 
 Secondly, we believe that it is imperative for the CRA to be harmonized with existing 
 legislation, particularly the NIS2 directive.  The  NIS2 directive, which has recently been 
 adopted, mandates specific reporting requirements and focuses on significant incidents. The 
 CRA, on the other hand, widens those requirements for the products that are in scope to any 
 and all incidents. The i2Coalition suggests that the CRA be brought into alignment with the NIS2 
 directive and only mandate reporting to specific incidents. 
 Lastly, the CRA will not operate in a vacuum, therefore it is essential for the EU legislators to 
 take also into account the international context. In order to avoid inconsistencies and legal 
 uncertainty for companies  , the CRA should refer as  much as possible to existing 
 international and voluntary standards.

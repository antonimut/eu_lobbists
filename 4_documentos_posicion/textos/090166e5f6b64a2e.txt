Sky welcomes the opportunity to share our initial feedback on the Media Freedom Act . The Media 
Freedom Act is a timely proposal and it can provide an opportunity to tackle clear and identified 
problems, in particular related to instances of interferences in the independence and editorial 
decisions of media and news providers. 
Sky answers as Europe’s leading media and entertainment company , connecting people to 
moments that matter. Across six European countries, Sky connects 23 million customers to the best 
entertainment, sports, news, arts, and to our own award -winning original content for which we are 
doubling our investment by 2024 . Sky is also launching a new streaming service through a joint 
venture in over 24 European markets and with Sky Studios is already one of the largest investors in 
European content.

As Sky we support the objective s of the proposal , namely, not to unduly incentivise viewers into 
specific AV offers. We also agree with the principle that standalone interfaces and devices should 
not aggressively be commercialised to the detriment of the customer. 
The scope of the new provision , however, also captures devices that are part of  an aggregated TV 
offering by regulated media service providers and this affects editorial polices and decisions, which 
is in direct juxtaposition to Article 4.1. Sky provides its linear TV subscription through a set-top-box 
that displays the exclusive content customers subscribes to, and this includes personalisation 
features similar to that of video-on-demand services. In this case editorial control on the interface 
and the content are intrinsically linked.  
Sky orders and presents content and services based on trends or topics of current relevance, like 
sports during the World Cup, public services, and news channels in light of major events , and 
entertainment during new original releases. Customers specifically subscribe to Sky for our curated 
content so there should be a presumption of customers trusting curation and personalisation. This 
new proposal unduly affects th e right to provide curated m edia offerings  and infringes on the 
freedom to exercise a commercial activity. 
Sky also stresses that this new obligation does not take into account already established industry 
practices and technical feasibility. Not all AV displaying user interfaces are designed in the same way 
as your average mobile operating systems, rather it often is purely an embedment of the available

And lastly, the proposal currently enshrines works categorised as public value as it is , without 
prejudice to AVMSD Article 7a which could lead to the opposite of guaranteeing media plurality and 
freedom of choice, by strictly enforcing the so -called “services of general interests” on user 
interfaces in each country rather than just granting them prominence. For instance, an Italian citizen 
living in Belgium (and not speaking French or Dutch) would have no choice but to be forced to see 
national public Belgium services on the home screen all the time. This goes against the principle of 
pluralism and is a large risk especially in cases where a government has a significant influence on the 
content of public broadcast services. 
The Media Freedom Act sets out to protect and safeguard the independe nce of Media companies 
in Europe through high-level principles. I t is with this intended goal that the regulatory form of a 
Regulation is appropriate and should not be watered down into a Directive. 
Internal market directives in the media landscape have historically created a fragmented patchwork 
of rules that deviate attention away from the possibility to build strong and trusted brands like Sky 
News or TG24 .  Examples of fragmentation introduced from the AVMS directive are  investment 
obligations, distribution quotas, or news reporting obligations. This fragmentation has damaged the 
growth of the sector and continues to undermine the competitiveness of the sector.  
With this mindset, we note that Article 1 (3) provides for the possibility for Member States to adopt 
more detailed rules. Such an opening clause should stay restricted to the adoption of detailed rules 
on the referred sections of the proposal. All other provisions not mentioned by Artic le 1(3) should 
be limited to the scope of the Proposal and Member States shall not be encouraged to deviate from 
them to ensure a degree of harmonisation that provides regulatory clarity. 
Especially for pan European broadcasters  or companies looking to expand,  it is necessary to 
consolidate to build scale and partner to ensure longevity and to be able to keep investing in 
channels with low commercial interest but have a very positive impact on media pluralism like news, 
arts, and kids channels. For Sky, branching out into ancillary fields is key to this. 
We wish to stress that media service providers do not compete in silos where consolidation 
becomes a zero-sum game. Rather if the impact of media companies on media pluralism is based on 
the quality content provided to European audiences, then our biggest rivals really are the different 
platforms that exist, especially so for younger audiences. 
To reflect these two points better in the MFA concretely first Sky proposes to take a wider scope 
when assessing market concentrations beyond companies with full editorial control, and secondly 
that the message to Member States should be to ensure a coherent approach across the EU is 
maintained.

Sky values the new principles put forward for audience measurement systems. We also welcome the 
enhanced transparency of methodologies used by companies in the online environment. The 
recitals justly point out that audience measurement systems  should follow industry benchmarks 
agreed within self-regulatory bodies.   
In each of Sky’s markets we are part of and contribute financially to the joint industry committees  
(JIC). Each JIC member shares and works on the audience measurement systems to ensure these 
are of high standards as it is a large benefit to the sector to be able to proof to advertiser the value 
they pay for. 
However, for Sky there will be cases where we are developing and testing new methodologies and 
live pilots outside of the industry framework , while  still bas ing our methods  on the same 
benchmarks. This is especially the case in non-linear environments. Any fully fledged methodology 
improvements will later be brought to the JICs, but the language in the proposal did not foresee 
these cases and this would effectively fall under the new obligations. And while trusted advertisers 
are often part of this process already, our main concern is that online platforms, who themselves 
have considerable advertising budgets , will be able to get insights into our lates t methodologies, 
which we see as one of the only strategic assets we have to be able to compete with them. Therefor 
new obligations cannot be appropriated by any platform players for the benefit of their own ad 
services. We caution against this new provision giving access to any data. 
Any disruptions in the advertising ecosystem can have serious repercussions on media pluralism, as  
the cornerstone of the broadcasting sector. It is for these reasons we suggest further clarifying the 
proposal to define ‘proprietary measurement systems’ to target the new players in the market as  
so named in the recital (45) and to further protect the work of self-regulatory bodies.  
Article 15.4 currently p roposes an already common practice of standardisation cooperation at 
national level, however, it would be a missed opportunity to not let the Board promote the 
harmonisation of standardisation at a European level. Pan -European operators have to adjust for 
country-specific television standards, providing a significant burden on resources better spend at 
improving the standards. This is why we generally support approaches to develop EU -wide 
standards such as in the upcoming High-level Forum on standardisation created by Commissioner 
Breton. 
Lastly, the term ‘technical standards’ is a juxtaposition. ‘Technical specification’ describes a 
regulation that is issued by the Commission on the basis of an explicit authorisation in the law, which 
is not the case for digital signals or design of devices. Contrarily, ‘Harmonised standards’ refer to 
agreed standards in European or international standardisation bodies of which the Board could 
promote the development of.

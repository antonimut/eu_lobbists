      Brussels, 23 January 2022  European Media Freedom Act (EMFA)    Lie Detectors, an award-winning journalist-led media literacy campaign in Europe, and the Global Media Registry, a social enterprise that promotes transparency and accountability in the information space, welcome the ability to respond to the Commission consultation on European Media Freedom Act (EMFA) proposal for regulation and feed the legislative debate on this topic.   The European Commission is sending the right signal by proposing an Act that promotes and safeguards a healthy European market and fills a gap in European legislation. While media regulation needs to be carried out with extreme caution in order to safeguard freedom of expression, the trends of politicisation of media and economic pressures create extremely bad conditions for the media to exercise its essential function for democracy and as a key source of credible, balanced information.   For all of these reasons, Lie Detectors and the Global Media Registry are pleased to see the Commission turn its attention to instruments and organisms aiming at increasing the transparency, independence and accountability of actions affecting media markets, freedom and pluralism within the EU.   We would like to draw the attention of the Commission to four points:   a. The purposeful consistency of the EMFA proposal with DMA, DSA, AVMS, E-Commerce Directive and competition rules is a positive development.  b. For the EMFA to be effective in securing media independence, its requirements must cover all aspects of capture, including state and corporate, and address the threat that large online platforms pose to media pluralism. Strong standards of funding transparency and ownership transparency must apply. c. Because the trends of media concentration and capture stretch across borders, the EMFA must include strong requirements and incentives a) to develop and implement meaningful indicators beyond the scope of national markets, and b) to support the interoperability of respectively suitable databases, for example through the harmonization of identifiers. d. We share ERGA’s concerns regarding this proposal for regulation on protection from state interference in editorial decisions (Article 4). As ERGA mentioned in their own

These remarks would not be complete without reiterating our belief that only a holistic approach will create the most important and systemic safeguards for consumers, citizens and for democracy itself.   -0-   Lie Detectors is an award-winning journalist-led media-literacy campaign in Europe. The non-profit organisation works with more than 250 professional journalists to secure democracy by empowering tens of thousands of young people and their teachers to tackle online disinformation and by fostering understanding of quality journalism. Lie Detectors works to improve news literacy, increase awareness of misinformation and further the general public’s understanding of the mainstream media industry. It works with academia to evaluate insights from its work and contributes its findings to ongoing policy debates in the fields of education and digital rights. Its long-term position is that news literacy should become an integral part of teacher-training colleges across Europe, so that news literacy can become one of the basic literacies of citizens in Europe.  The Global Media Registry (GMR) is a social enterprise that promotes transparency and accountability in the information space. Registered as a non-profit, charitable LLC under German Law, it owns and operates the Media Ownership Monitor as a public utility and implements it in partnership within a network of like-minded partner organisations globally, enabling local ownership of each country edition. The GMR is a member of the Global Forum for Media Development (GFMD), a consortium member of the Global Media & Internet Concentration Project (GMICP) and a member of the Media Freedom Cohort of the Summit for Democracy.

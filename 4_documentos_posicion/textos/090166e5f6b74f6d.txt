GFMD Feedback on the EuropeanCommission’s Proposal for aEuropean Media Freedom Act
The Global Forumfor Media Development (GFMD) is the largest global communityfor mediadevelopment, mediafreedom, andjournalismsupport. Throughcollaboration,coordination,andcollectiveactionour networkof 147member organisations-- aswell asour dozensofpartners-- creates, promotes, and delivers policies and programmes to sustain journalismas a publicgood. In line with GFMD’ strategy, our organisation currently facilitates a working group withorganizations within the journalismsupport/media development, digital rights, media freedom,andwider democracysupport communities, toexchangeviewsandresourcesinrelationtotheEuropean Media Freedom Act (EMFA).
GFMDis aware that independent media, whichprovidesanessential publicservice, hasbeendeclining for nearly a decade. The collapse of the traditional journalistic business model,growing political polarisation, democraticbacksliding, andtheaddedburdensof theCOVID-19pandemic have all contributed to a potential "media extinction event," as the UNSecretary-General has warned. 4. Transparency of ownership requirements for well-functioning media market(Section 5:Article 21)
A healthy, well-functioning, and pluralistic media sector requires complete transparencyregarding who owns and wields power over media. In line with our recommendations above,meeting certain standards regarding the transparency of media ownership should be aprerequisite toanyprocedural rightsgrantedtomediaorganisationsinthedigital environment.Such transparency requirements should provide information on the entire chain of ownershipand beneficial ownership, as well as all financial relationships with entities such as states,governments, or theEuropeanUnion. Nonetheless, theEMFAcurrentlysupportstransparencyin theory but fails to deliver in practice for several reasons:
1.1. Ownership transparency provisions must be comprehensive, enforceable, andverifiable and the data should be fully and freely accessible to the public on aEurope-wide database.
1.2. Expanding on Article 18’s “structured dialogue” media stakeholders, academiaand civil society should actively participate in establishing, improving andimplementing a potential common methodology for consistent monitoring andreporting on media ownership across the EU. The data gathered through thismethodologyshouldbemadepubliclyavailablethroughaEurope-widedatabase,allowing the analysis of cross-media ownership, cross-border mediaownership,audience measurements, and media concentration levels.

Final remarks
TheEuropeanMediaFreedomAct (EMFA) providestheEuropeanUnionwithauniquechanceto strengthen media freedomand pluralismthroughout Europe. While the condition of mediafreedom in the EU continues to deteriorate, the EMFA should become a top priority forlegislators beforetheendof thisterm. Asatool todefendandpromotedemocracyandmediafreedom, the EMFA should be strengthened in order to effectively serve the interests ofjournalists, media freedom, media plurality, and democracy in the EU.
The struggle for the survival of professional journalism, particularly public interestjournalism, remains one of the most significant hindrances to ensuring a healthy anddiverse information ecosystem. While the EMFA does not addresstheeconomicpressuresthat threaten the sector's survival, other options must be explored, such as the EuropeanParliament's proposal to establish a permanent News Media Fund to protect and empower

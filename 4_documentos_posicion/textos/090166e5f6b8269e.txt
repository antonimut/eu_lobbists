European Media Freedom Act (EMFA) 
Position Paper from Agora Media Group 
 
 
I. General position  
 
As one of the largest media groups with a presence in the Polish market, Agora welcomes the 
European Commission's efforts to protect media freedom, which is the basis for the functioning 
of democratic governments in the European Union. Taking measures to ensure journalistic 
independence and the public's right to obtain relevant, critical and independent information is 
particularly important for those European countries where media free dom has been 
threatened in recent years by intense and often unlawful actions of state institutions.  
 
Agora is a leading media company in Central Europe with activities spanning from publishing 
to radio broadcasting and the operation of cinemas. Agora is the publisher of the largest daily 
newspaper in Poland i.e., Gazeta Wyborcza. As an independent publisher operating in Poland, 
Agora is one of the organizations that was hit the hardest by the decline of state standards in 
protecting independent media and the numerous interferences in the media ecosystem 
motivated solely by political interests.  
 
The European Media Freedom Act (EMFA) is an ambitious proposal that calls for the adoption 
of specific solutions to respond to the crisis in the media ecosystem that is present in some 
European countries. Agora confirms that the creation of a similar act in the form of a Regulation 
is necessary in the current state of affairs. We are concerned that the adoption of new 
proposals in another form (e.g. a Directive) may significantly delay the positive effects of the 
regulations, or even lead to the impossibility of their effec tive implementation. In particular, 
those provisions that impose obligations on Member States to ensure media freedom can 
flourish without fear of interference from or surveillance and intimidation by the State, should 
be welcomed. At the same time, we not e that some of the proposed regulations are far from 
ideal, and it will be necessary to work together on the most appropriate wording of individual 
provisions. Where a proposal concerns, in its assumptions, interference in the operation of the 
press market (which has not been so widely affected by European regulations to date), it is 
likely to cause controversy, and for this reason all proposed regulations should be appropriately 
tailored to the character of individual national markets, so that regul ation does not have the 
opposite effect than intended . We provide additional comments on this topic below and 
indicate our readiness to continue active participation in the legislative process in the future.

II. Key challenges of the new proposal 
i. Rights of media service providers and it’s recipients (Article 3 – 5 of the Draft) 
Clearly articulating guarantees for conducting business as a media service provider 
independent of state authorities and regulators is a necessary and crucial component of the 
proposed regulation. We welcome the proposed regulations on repressive measures and 
protection of journalistic sources on the one hand and transparent rules on the use of spyware 
on the other. However, we call for an analysis of whether the catalog of situations in which it is 
possible to introduce spyware into the devices of media service providers is not defined too 
broadly. In particular, the lack of precise indications in Article 4(2)(c) of the Draft may suggest 
that the introduction of software is possible with respect to a media service provider when the 
suspicion of a specific crime concerns only a family member of an employee of the provider, 
which is certainly not in line with the intention of the introduced regulations. In addition , the 
new regulations should make it clear that the understanding of the term "employee of a media 
service provider" should be as broad as possible, so that the protection applies to all persons 
associated with the provider regardless of the basis of their  employment or the scope of 
services provided. Only in such a situation will the proposed protection system be as airtight as 
possible and immune to attempts by security services to unlawfully bypass it.  
 
In addition, the mechanism for the expression of a n opinion by an independent authority or 
body described in Article 4(3) of the Draft, in order to contribute to an impartial assessment of 
state actions and regulations, should impose an obligation on state authorities to adjust their 
regulations or practi ces when the opinion given is negative. Given the nature of possible 
violations of journalistic independence, the indicated deadline for issuing an opinion should be 
much shorter (e.g., 14 days after a complaint is filed by a media service provider). Such a long 
deadline as 3 months may result in the fact that the actions or practices to which the prepared 
opinion will relate have long since been completed by the authorities, and the damage that will 
be done to journalistic independence will already be i rreversible. In addition, in a situation 
where the opinion prepared by the relevant national authority would be unfavorable to the 
media service provider, he should be able to file an analogous complaint or appeal to the 
European Board for Media Services , whose duty should be to respond to the opinion of the 
national authority in the context of European pluralism and media protection under European 
principles.

ii. National measures affecting the operation of media service providers (Article 20) 
Another provision that is one of the most important elements of the proposal presented are 
the necessary circumstances for the introduction of national measures affecting the operation 
of media service providers. Most important from our perspective is the right to appeal any such 
measure to an appellate body which shall be independent of the parties involved and of any 
external intervention or political pressure liable to jeopardize its independent assessment of 
matters coming before it.  
 
In the case of th e Polish media market, we have unfortunately had to deal repeatedly with 
legislative measures that clearly contradict the principles expressed in the Draft. Polish media 
service providers constantly clash with harmful legislative proposals that affect both  specific 
branches of the media market (e.g., television and radio - attempt to introduce a possibility to 
grant a broadcasting license for period shorter than 10 years) and its entirety (attempt to 
introduce the so-called Advertising Revenue Tax). Some of the new legislative proposals were 
not introduced solely and exclusively because of the massive offensive carried out jointly by 
the entire Polish media market and the public opinion pressure that was associated with it.  
 
At the same time, also  many of the actions taken by Polish regulators in the form of 
administrative acts can hardly be considered adequately justified or non-discriminatory. In this 
context, one can mention the actions of the Polish President of the Office of Competition and 
Consumer Protection (OCCP). The President of the OCCP refused to approve the transaction of 
assuming control over the Eurozet Group (Polish radio broadcaster) by Agora S.A.. The 
proceeding had been pending for an exceptionally long period (almost 15 months) - it seems 
that it was artificially extended multiple times  - and at the end OCCP President's issued 
negative decision without proof of the existence of a significant negative impact of the 
concentration. The decision have already been challenged in Polish court and ruling of the first 
instance completely refuted all the OCCP's claims and approved the transaction. The verdict is 
not final and Agora is awaiting a second instance ruling.  
 
At the same time OCCP's President, allowed for the transaction between  the state -owned 
company PKN Orlen and Polska Press Group after about a month of the transaction review. The 
decision was made very quickly, without conducting detailed market analyses or market test 
despite negative opinions of press distributors about the concentration. OCCP's decision made 
it possible for the state to become the most powerful player on the local press market.

unlawful regulatory actions is necessary t o preserve the proper functioning of the internal 
market. In view of this, there should also be appropriate modifications to Article 20(4) of the 
Draft, which would allow the European Board for Media Services to issue opinions on its own 
initiative, and no t only at the request of the Commission. Similarly, as pointed out earlier - a

In the context of the proposed mandatory alignment of Member States with the opinion of the

iii. Allocation of state advertising (Article 24) 
 
Clarifying the rules for state advertising at the level of European regulation is a key issue for 
EMFA to meet its stated goals. Private media can only be truly free if their financial situation 
allows them to operate independently in the common market. The lack of common rules on 
advertising financing is distorting competition in the media market in many EU countries. When 
it comes to Poland, government support (in its advertising spending) of sympathetic outlets 
and punishment of outlets like Gazeta Wyborcza is clear. An August 2019 report by Kantar 
Media revealed that ad -spending from state ads in Gazeta Wyborcza fell 97% from 2015 to 
2018, while spending during the period rose dramatically in friendly conservative outlets like 
Gazeta Polska (200x) and wSieci (30x). This despite a fall in readership for m ost conservative 
outlets. 
 
In such market environment, the system of controlling the basis for spending public funds on 
advertising must have elements of not only reporting, but also actual accountability of state 
institutions. The obligation to publish information referred to in Article 24(2) of the Draft should 
be modified to set specific dates for publication of information and its location (e.g. websites 
of each obliged entity). The reporting period itself should be shorter than annual - the 
information should be published, for example, on a quarterly basis, so that improper trends in 
distribution of public funds are easy to catch. In addition, the information prepared in this way 
should also be compiled in a single report for all state institutions with simultaneous access to 
aggregate data for all stakeholders in a format that allows for machine analysis. Only by leaving 
such information in the public domain will it be possible for it to be analyzed by independent 
centers and for all information on governmental spending to be communicated to the public.  
 
The powers for national authorities or regulators to obtain additional information set out in 
Article 24(3) of the Draft should also be granted to the proposed European Board of Media 
Services. Otherwise, national authorities will in practice be controllin g themselves and it will 
not be in their interest to inform the public about any irregularities. In addition, the European 
Board should be able to formally comment on aggregate data published by a state institution, 
e.g. at the request of individual media service providers. When irregularities are detected, the

iv. Assessment of media market concentrations (Article 21 and 22)  
 
Concentration in the media market, as pointed out earlier, may be subject to unlawful actions 
by state authorities that significantly affect the distortion of competition in the market. 
However, in our view, such actions have not been possible due to the lack of appropriate 
regulations, but more due to inappropriate and selective application  of legislation  already 
present. In particular, the new regulatory proposals should not be the basis for the introduction 
of new merger approval regimes in individual Member States, which would be specific only to 
the media market. We are concerned that separate mode ls of proceedings may only prolong 
the length of such approvals and be another basis for state regulators to unduly interfere with 
the freedom to  conduct business. In this context, only concentrations involving the State 
Treasury or other state -owned companies  should be subject to special assessment. It is 
precisely in these types of transactions that there is the greatest risk of a breach of pluralism in 
the market and the easy transformation of previously independent editorial offices into 
propaganda centers for government action.  
 
In this context, the proposal to introduce a basis for the European Board as well as for the 
Commission to be able t o issue opinions on concentrations in the media market should be 
viewed positively. Opinions issued under this procedure could be a first step towards ensuring 
that concentrations that disrupt media pluralism are noticed not only locally, but also 
throughout the European Union.  
 
In addition, if new media concentration rules were to be introduced, they should also directly 
apply to transactions taking place in the digital market . The current business environment 
forces media operations to be conducted mainly in the digital environment and any changes in 
ownership there, may cause irreversible consequences for media pluralism. One example of 
this would be the transformations in th e online AdTech ecosystem. Despite this  proposed 
regulation doesn’t take into account the dominance of the digital advertising market by the 
platforms, or that platforms themselves also exercise editorial control by moderating, ranking 
and even removing media content. 
 
We are concerned that proposed model merger regulation could be outdated and ignoring the 
impact of internet platforms on the broader media market which is not fit for purpose and 
which has strong potential to be detrimental to publishers which must compete head on with 
both platforms and publicly funded media.

III. Additional propositions for changes  
 
i. Content of media service providers on very large online platforms (Article 17) 
 
We welcome the attempt to secure the publication of media service providers in an online 
platform market, but at the same time we point out that the solutions adopted in the proposal 
do not go far enough to ensure that the media can function properly in th e new business 
environment. In order to ensure that citizens have universal access to independent and verified 
sources of information, they should be able to consume them undisturbed where they spend 
most of their time in the electronic environment. 
 
In th is context, in order to be effective Article 17 of the Draft  should be strengthened with 
transparent, standard procedures in place for all platforms which distribute media content to 
ensure that  platforms shall  be clearly obliged  to take appropriate measures to protect the 
freedom of expression and the freedom and pluralism of the media. 
Where a provider of an online platform that allows the dissemination of press publications 
within the meaning of Article 2(4) of Directive (EU) 2019/790, audiovisual media services within 
the meaning of Article 1(1) point (a) of Directive 2010/13/EU including licensed radio services, 
decides, on the basis of an alleged incompatibility with their terms and conditions, to remove, 
disable access to or ot herwise interfere with a service (for example limit its reach through 
algorithms) or content made available by one of these editorial content providers executing 
editorial responsibility in accordance with its national rules and regulations, the online platform 
provider shall as a matter of priority: 
a) inform the editorial content provider of its intention using a standard notification 
procedure, 
b) provide a clear and specific statement of reasons and  
c) seek agreement with the editorial content provider before implementing the decision.   
If the editorial content provider and the online platform provider cannot agree, a court or an 
equally competent national, independent authority shall decide on the compatibility of the 
online platform’s intended action with the freedom of expression and freedom of the media. 
Until such a decision is taken, the online platform shall not implement its decision. Platforms 
should be obliged to disclose [on an annual basis] according to a standard set of criteria the 
number of instances of restriction or suspension, as well as the grounds for those decisions

ii. Duties of media service providers providing news and current affairs content (Article 
6) 
 
In strengthening the independence of the media against interference from state institutions or 
online platforms, it is important to ensure that the new regulatory proposals do not also restrict 
the freedom of media service providers to conduct their business. As a publisher of  press 
publications, Agora has a long-standing tradition of independence of editorial decisions from 
the powers of ownership. We believe that this is the most appropriate model, which is the only 
one that guarantees the creation of press content that has t he public good in mind and is 
completely independent of vested interests.  
 
At the same time, we point out that these principles should be introduced into the new 
regulation in an appropriate manner to avoid any ambiguity and differences of interpretation 
as to the scope of the guarantees introduced. Article 6(2)  of the Draft is a key provision and it 
should clearly indicate (not only in Recitals) that owners must have the possibility to set the 
editorial line of the press title. This is a key right of the owner that ensures predictability and 
financial control over the publication. 
 
iii. European Board for Media Services (Articles 8-12) 
 
Notwithstanding all the comments and proposals indicated above on the competences of the 
European Board for Media Services, we point out that a key aspect is to ensure its complete 
independence. The Board's independence should be expressed both from the me dia market 
regulators in the individual Member States  and from the Commission itself. This concerns in 
particular the financial independence and the functioning of its own Secretariat. The 
Commission should only be able to act in situations where the Board  identifies problems in 
ensuring pluralism or inadequate functioning of individual national regulators. 
 
IV. Conclusions 
 
EMFA is a step in the right direction, but in order to restore balance in the European media 
market, the provisions of the new regulation must be properly modified. The regulation should 
emphasize guarantees of the media's complete independence from the state and the possibility 
of their undisturbed operation on the digital market. The introduction of additional obligations 
for media service providers themselves may contribute to further distortion of the principles of 
competition and lead to restrictions on citizens' access to independent sources of information. 
Protecting free media is a key and at the same time the only appropriate response to the crisis 
of democratic power present in some Member States.

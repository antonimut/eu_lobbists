Bundesverband fuer Tiergesundheit comments to the 
Proposal for a REGULATION amending Regulation (EU) 
2017/745 and repealing Council Regulation (EC) No 297/95 
and Regulation (EU) 658/2014 on fees and charges payable 
to the EMA and Annexes 
 
The increase in fees 
The EC proposal represents a  significant increase in fees in all regulatory areas (new submissions, 
post-approval applications, annual fees) for veterinary medicinal products (VMPs) and new fees are 
included for some items, such as referrals not initiated by the applicant.  
 
The proposed cost -based system of fees would be too expen sive for the VMP sector, which has 
traditionally benefited from reduced fees due to the small market size. As well as a negative impact on 
innovation, there will also be a negative impact on medicines availability. 
 
We believe a cost-recovery approach is simply unrealistic for the VMP sector, which will find it difficult 
to support such significant fee increases  due to the small market size.  The Animal Health sector is 
only 3 to 5% of the size of the human medicines sector.  The fees charged need to reflect this economic 
reality in order to avoid damaging an Innovating sector that contributes to Public Health by ensuring 
food security and reducing risks of zoonoses. The availability and affordability of VMPs is also relevant 
to ensure animal welfare. 
 
The proposed fees would generally result in major regulatory fee increase across all other areas 
(innovation and maintenance). The estimated increase is very significant (47-57% increase versus 2022 
costs, based on actual regulatory activity in 2022). 
 
We recognize the benefits that Variations Not Requiring Assessment ( VNRAs) are free of charge, and 
that some (very few) proposals would result in lower fees than currently (limited market requests, for 
example). 
 
Impact on innovation 
This is clearly not an i ncentive for innovation, and it is in total contradiction with the objective of the 
new regulation 2019/6 to stimulate innovation. 
 
Veterinary health is a public good; pharmaceutical innovation has been already slowing down in the 
veterinary domain, especially for farm animals. There is risk that the EU will lose its status as the main 
innovation leader in veterinary medicine. 
 
Impact on availability of VMPs 
There will be a negative impact on the availability of VMPs, especially on the lower-revenue products, 
such as many of the veterinary  vaccines.  The big impact on vaccines, and in particular the limited 
market vaccines, the fish vaccines, the vaccines against emerging diseases and the Vaccine Antigen 
Master File (VAMF)-related submissions, will be a serious blow to the EU strategy to encourage the use 
of alternatives to antimicrobials.  These alternatives should be incentivised, including greater incentives 
for vaccines. Despite the welcome 50% reduction on fees applying to immunologicals, overall fees will 
increase significantly for this class of products as well. 
 
Since some of the costs are proportionate to the number of presentations, there will be a strong 
incentive to rationalize the product portfolio, reducing the number of Stock Keeping Units and thus 
decreasing access to medicines in smaller countries or when simply when the volume of sales is low.

The increase in fees inevitably will lead to price increases  to the end user . Unlike human medicines, 
there are no national health schemes to cover t he cost of veterinary medicines: all costs are born by 
the animal owners. For food-producing animals, this might have a knock-on effect on food prices and 
ultimately contribute to inflation. Talking about veterinary medicines for pets, some pets might no 
longer be treated, which would be contrary to animal welfare. 
 
An increase in fees must be tied to, and conditional upon, good service and performance standards at 
the agency.   
 
Impact on cost of maintenance 
The cost of product maintenance in the marketplace (variations and pharmacovigilance) will increase 
tremendously. The large fee increases for variations are a big concern; vaccines are the most impacted.  
Although vaccines benefit from a 50% reduction in many cases, it is still a very large increase compared 
to the current fees .  This may lead companies to decisions to rationalise their product ranges, with a 
negative impact on the availability of VMPs. 
 
VRA-R (Variation Requiring Assessment with a reduced timetable ) result in reduced assessm ent 
compared to other Variation Requiring Assessment ( VRAs).  Therefore, i f the fees are to be 
proportionate to the work, then VRA-R fees should be cheaper (or attract no fee), than other VRAs as. 
This is currently unclear.  
 
This is particularly important for the maintenance of veterinary vaccines, which frequently involve many 
quality-related variations to the manufacturing process. 
 
Impact on cost of inspections 
Currently, company experience with GMP inspections is that Authorities charge inspection fees p er 
product inspected.  This is not fair as the great majority of the manufacturing systems are the same for 
each product manufactured on that site. If the same site is inspected for different products at the same 
time, then the GMP inspections fees should be reduced to reflect the reduced work for authorities (and 
to reflect a cost-based approach). In other words, there should be a single GMP Inspection fee per site, 
related to the number of products manufactured at that site.  This should be clarified in the proposed 
regulation. 
 
Transparency and budgeting 
A sufficient transition period will be necessary before  revised fees apply  to allow for the annual 
budgeting cycle, due to the very high increases proposed .  The proposed increases are significantly 
above any current inflation rate and could not be absorbed without major budget increases. 
 
Specific comments  
• The fee applying to informed consent applications seems unrealistically high (136 .800 € for an 
administrative review?) and is potentially a mistake. 
• An identical fee (136 .800 EUR) is proposed for generic/hybrid applications (containing technical 
data, e.g. pre-clinical efficacy/safety and/or literature) and applications based on informed consent 
(containing administrative documents only).  This is not logical in a cost-based system.  
• One objective of the initiative is for fees to be attractive for vet erinary vaccines. While there are 
already incentives foreseen in this regard, estimations indicate a significant impact (based on the 
proposed fees for new Marketing Authorisations(MAs)/VRAs/GMP inspections,…). The high impact 
on variation fees is alr eady mentioned above.  The high impact on vaccines, in particular on fish 
vaccines, is described below. 
 
Vaccines 
• The proposed fee system and the new mandatory scope of centralized procedures (for new active 
substances) may be especially impactful for limited market vaccines, such as fish vaccines. Fish

vaccines are sold in very few EU/EEA Member States. If a fish vaccine contains a n ew active 
substance, it would need to be authorized centrally, even if it is sold in only one EU Member State. 
This, together with the proposed fee system and the consequential fee increases, would be 
especially impactful. Consequently w e ask  for more  special considerations for limited market 
vaccines, with further incentives to those currently proposed.  
• We appreciate that there are incentives (100% reduction of annual fees) for veterinary vaccines 
against certain major epizootic diseases. However, this only applies to vaccines against bluetongue, 
pandemic avian influenza, foot and mouth disease and classical swine fever, where the vaccine is 
authorised under normal circumstances and the product has not been marketed within the Union 
at any time during the totality of the period covered by the fee. We ask that other vaccines against 
major epizootic diseases ( e.g. African Swine Fever, Lumpy Skin Disease, Schmallenberg and 
others) are also covered by a 100% fee exemption in the absence of sales.  
• In addition, all vaccines authorized under both exceptional circumstances and limited market 
conditions should benefit from the exemption. It is a significant undertaking to invest (at high risk) 
in developing vaccines against emerging diseases. Providing additional incentives for those would 
be helpful.  
• The fees for VAMF application and VAMF maintenance (recognizing that any post-approval change 
to an existing VAMF may require multiple sequential variations, one to deal with the impact on the 
antigen, and another to deal with the impact on the finished product) are not attractive and will not 
be an incentive for Industry to implement this (otherwise good) master file system.

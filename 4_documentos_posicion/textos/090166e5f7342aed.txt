Position Paper on the review of the priority substances 
list [WFD Subsidiary Directive 2000/60/EC: EQSD] 
The European Commission presented a revision proposal of the Environmental Quality Standards  
Directive (EQSD) on 26 October 2022  (Subsidiary directive of the Water Framework Directive (WFD) . 
According to this proposal, n ew priority substances will be added and standards for existing priority 
substances in surface waters will be tightened. The WFD chemical water quality status will be based on 
monitoring and reporting of these priority substances.  
The new Directive is closely linked to  the Water Framework Directive (WFD) and should contribute to 
the ambitions of the European Green Deal: 
➢ The link with the Water Framework Directive (WFD) is essential. The WFD should remain 
the leading instrument in which water quality targets are set.   
 
1. EQS contributes to improving chemical status of water quality. 
Based on th is proposed  new priority substances list, each Member State will  have to assess what 
measures are needed to achieve the environmental quality standards for the listed substances.  The 
next question is then which instruments can be used  to reduce these substances’ emissions . It is 
important to properly map the presence of pollutants in surface water by means of monitoring surface 
water and at sewage treatment plants. This will help to take more targeted source control measures to 
reduce emissions of these substances to the e nvironment. In this way, additional treatment efforts  at 
‘the end of the pipe’  can be avoided and the  proposed European 'polluter pays' principle will be taken 
into account.   
➢ Water authorities express support for tightening up existing standards and addin g new 
substances to the priority substances list in order to better monitor the chemical status 
of water quality; 
➢ The insight gained from monitoring priority substances should contribute to the 'polluter 
pays’ principle and the source approach to hazardous substances. Bearing in mind that 
the WFD objectives are further out of sight due to this tightening. The feasibility of the 
objectives must be realistic and guaranteed in the proposed legislation.  
 
2. PFAS is an example of why the EQS is a valuable tool. 
For PFAS, we call for a complete European ban on their production and use. It is much better to prevent 
these substances to enter the water cycle in the first place than to remove them afterward s. Persistent 
substances like PFAS that still end up in sewage treatment plants can often only be removed with great 
difficulty and at very high costs. Moreover, the presence of persistent chemicals hampers opportunities 
to recover raw materials from sewage: think of phosphate and bioplastics. The best approach is to tackle 
these substances at their source.  
➢ Seek a total European ban on the production, use, and emission of PFAS as soon as 
possible; 
➢ Priority Substances List is a valuable tool to promote circular economy. 
 
3. Medicine residues as a priority substance strengthens the demand for control 
at source-based approach, Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) & far-
reaching treatment efforts. 
For the group of  medicine residues proposed in the directive's new substance list, a combination of 
source treatment, EPR, and further disposal at the sewage treatment plant is possible. The revision of 
the Urban Wastewater Directive, published on 26 October 2022, will a s well  include rules and 
subsequent targets. Moreover, in line with the ‘polluter pays’ principle, that directive proposes that the 
costs of further treatment by the pharmaceutical industry should be reimbursed via EPR. Particularly, 
the new standards  for medicine r esidues will lead to investments in more advanced treatment. The

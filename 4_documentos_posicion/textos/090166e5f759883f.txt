Attachment to EFPIA’s response to the EU Commission’s Proposal for a 
REGULATION OF THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND OF THE COUNCIL on fees 
and charges payable to the European Medicines Agency 
 
Author: EFPIA  Date: 06 February 2023    
 
The European Federation of Pharmaceutical Industries and Associations (EFPIA) represents 39 
research-intensive pharmaceutical companies as well as membership of 36 national associations.  
 
In this document, EFPIA offers our detailed views on the EU Commission’s proposal for a revised EMA 
Fees regulation.  
 
Sustainability and viability of EU regulatory system 
Heightened by the COVID crisis and the impact of Brexit, the EU regulatory system, including EMA and 
National Competent Authorities (NCAs), is experiencing significant resourcing challenges that hinder 
the ability to achieve optimal efficiency and sustainability. Delays in approval decisions and scientific 
advice as well variation in the quality of medicines assessments are current symptoms. EFPIA 
considers it essential to the viability of the EU regulatory system that these resourcing constraints – 
funding, capacities, and capabilities – be promptly and comprehensively addressed. Funding and 
financials are one part of the challenge along with many other contributors: recruiting technical 
expertise in cutting edge fields, retaining experienced talent (particularly at NCAs), complex activities 
and process redundancies. These resourcing issues limit the EU regulatory system’s global 
competitiveness and hinder the expertise-based decision-making for timely and efficient delivery of 
new medicines. 
 
Sound financial footing for EU regulatory system 
The revision of the EMA Fees Regulation offers an opportunity to improve the EU regulatory system’s 
global competitiveness by becoming faster, more efficient, and flexible. On 25th November 2020, the 
European Commission released its Pharmaceutical Strategy for Europe (EC Pharma Strategy). The EC 
Pharma Strategy notes that the “EMA fees system is key in funding regulatory activities at EU level 
and ensuring coverage of the relevant costs” and that the EC “will consider this in the upcoming 
revision of the EMA fee legislation”. The EC explains that the “broad purpose of this initiative is to 
provide the EMA with a sound financial basis for the future” (Staff Working Document). Improving the 
EMA fees system coupled with the ongoing review of the general pharmaceutical policy framework 
offer a once in a generation opportunity. This is a chance to, not only catch-up with scientific and 
policy advances, but to create an attractive innovation ecosystem that is purpose-built for the future, 
to discover, develop and deliver the next generation of treatments, diagnostics and vaccines for EU 
patients. The EU needs a system that can adapt rapidly to emerging technology and the emerging 
needs of our healthcare systems. The fees system should be adaptable to support this evolution. 
 
EFPIA’s principles on EMA fees 
In its proposals, the EC seeks to streamline the fees system and to simplify its structure, to contribute 
to providing a sound financial basis to the EMA’s operations, and to make the system future-proof. 
These objectives are well aligned with EFPIA’s positions (https://www.efpia.eu/media/676843/efpia-

position-paper-on-ema-fees.pdf). EFPIA recognises significant advances in the EC’s proposals following 
the 2019 Inception Impact Assessment (IIA) and 2021 Impact Assessment (IA).  
 
EFPIA’s members support a revision of the current fee system that is consistent with its fundamental 
principles of transparency, fairness & proportionality, sustainability, simplicity, and 
flexibility. Many aspects of the EC proposal are well aligned with these principles. EFPIA notes that 
the fees system as implemented via this proposal would introduce cost-based, justified, transparent, 
and validated calculation of fees and reduce the number of procedural fees by broadening the annual 
fee for centrally authorised products (CAP annual fee). EFPIA considers that some of these 
proportionate and streamlined measures will assist in resource savings, for regulators and companies, 
over the longer term. EFPIA is providing additional commentary in the sections below, based around 
its principles. 
 
Transparency: 
EFPIA principle: Changes to the fee structure should be based on a comprehensive, transparent  
and independent evaluation of the underlying costs of the services provided, projections of  
future developments and strengths and weaknesses of the current system. 
 
EFPIA comments: EFPIA appreciates the thorough data gathering, stakeholder interviews and 
consultations, and cost-based modelling that contributed to the proposals presented here. EFPIA 
supports the proposed requirement for the EMA to provide ongoing monitoring of costs, including 
substantiated information on costs covered by fees such as performance information and a cost 
breakdown. However, EFPIA believes that further transparency with stakeholders, including the 
pharmaceutical industry, would be required for the information generated from this monitoring.  
 
EFPIA considers that acknowledgement of transparency with stakeholders should be incorporated into 
the new proposal. 
 
The EMA special reports provisioned for in Article 10(6) and the annual activity report delivered to the 
European Parliament, the Council, the Commission and the Court of Auditors should be shared with 
stakeholders. They should also contain information on fees distribution and resourcing issues within 
the NCAs.  
 
The Staff Working Document (SWD) references the extensive cost and revenue evaluation and 
assumptions that were built into the EC’s modeling which formed the basis of its proposals; however, 
this level of detail is not available for stakeholders to review and comment. As the new EMA fees 
system is implemented, EFPIA considers it essential for the cost-based evidence to be shared with 
involved stakeholders, including the pharmaceutical industry, in an ongoing manner. There should 
likewise be ample opportunities for consultation and input from these stakeholders (e.g., through a 
recurring dialogue platform) on the cost-revenue models, key performance indicators (KPIs), and 
change triggers.  Important to the full picture of cost vs. revenue are the NCA budgetary elements 
that contribute to the centralised EU regulatory actions – these should also be shared with the 
involved stakeholders.  
 
EFPIA suggests the addition of the involvement of independent auditors to support the evaluation of 
generated, decision-informing data (e.g., special reports, KPIs, change triggers), appreciating that this 
audit could complement the Article 10(4) reference to the oversight provided by the Court of Auditors.

Fairness and Proportionality: 
EFPIA principle: Fees must correspond to the service provided (i.e., “fee-for-service” principle) and 
should be fair and proportionate for all actors involved. While it is accepted that the majority of the 
EMA budget will continue to be derived from fees payable by Industry, some activities conducted by 
the Agency are part of its general mission to ensure public health and should therefore remain at least 
partly covered by the Union contribution. 
 
EFPIA comments: EFPIA welcomes the EC’s intent to precisely associate the fees to the regulatory 
workload. EFPIA also notes that the EC’s proposed fees system will change based on several factors 
such as inflation. It is clear from the proposals that there are circumstances that will result in 
increases in fees; however, the provisions do not explicitly acknowledge circumstances that could 
result in reduction of fees. For example, there may be streamlining of processes, reduction of 
workflow redundancies, economies of scale, and IT system cost savings for administrative activities. 
Under the cost-based model and consistent with the EC’s proportionality principle, future cost savings 
should result in a reduction of fees that should be foreseen and established into the system. 
 
While the proposal mentions several KPIs (e.g., Annex VI and Table 11, SWD), EFPIA considers that 
there are additional performance measures that should be considered. Such KPIs are important to 
ensure that the EU regulatory system is predictable, which is key for its competitiveness and for 
delivering new medicines for patients. While the proposal includes measurements of costs and other 
workload data, there do not appear to be indicators for actual “performance”. For instance, KPIs could 
be included for the statutory targets relating to regulated procedures (e.g., timelines) for identifying 
any early signals of unanticipated performance delivery challenges. Having well-correlated and 
transparently shared KPIs will be essential for future proofing the funding system, and thus require 
ongoing engagement with stakeholders including industry to maintain relevancy.  
 
We continue to advocate for the innovative use of data sources such as envisaged in the progression 
of DARWIN EU. In fact, in our Regulatory Road to Innovation, EFPIA recognises the value of using 
good quality RWD/RWE as additional sources of evidence for regulatory decision-making. Therefore, 
EFPIA considers it essential for industry to have the opportunity to continue to engage with regulators 
as methodologies and practices are established for DARWIN EU. Specifically, a principle should be 
recognised that companies are consulted on protocols for complex studies that may impact their 
products. In terms of funding, we wish to reiterate our previous comments that important elements 
for the maintenance costs associated with DARWIN EU (e.g., long-term costs and timelines, costs to 
evolve security features, risks of potential cost overruns, strategic development plans) should be 
made transparent, including to fee payers.   
 
More generally, we acknowledge that telematics is a significant area of importance for the EU 
regulatory system and its stakeholders. An appropriately resourced EMA IT infrastructure is an enabler 
to an effective and efficient system. Resourcing should encompass continued development of 
capabilities beyond the financial aspects building on close collaboration with relevant stakeholders, 
such as industry, and clear visibility on roadmaps. The focus should be to ensure robust and 
sustainable funding to maintain EMA IT infrastructure for monitoring, support to regulatory business 
processes and improving efficiencies. The EMA IT infrastructure projects should be linked to KPIs. For 
the monitoring activities, many potential finance models, such as the Union’s contribution and public-
Page 4 of 10 
 
private collaboration initiatives including already developed software and systems, need special 
consideration. To provide more clarity, EFPIA considers that systems such as PLM, IDMP/SPOR, CTIS 
and related interfaces with NCAs could be referenced as part of the essential IT infrastructure 
supporting efficiencies in the regulatory network. Clarification of the term ‘databases’ and how 
essential infrastructure is funded is needed in the fee regulation. The Network and its stakeholders 
should develop a coherent digital strategy based on agreed end goals, including interoperability, and 
data architecture. IT network implementation should be driven by a realistic roadmap to which the 
entire Network should commit.  
 
Finally, considering fairness and proportionality, EFPIA believes that a tapered system for the CAP 
annual fees should be considered, since a decrease in fees may represent a cost-based approach due 
to decreasing workload after a product is on the market for 10 years or more. If implemented, 
supportive data could be captured to demonstrate that the reduced fees are proportionate to the 
regulator workload after a product has been on the market for a number of years.  
 
Sustainability: 
EFPIA principle: To support public health and pharmaceutical innovation, the fee  
structure should ensure adequate availability of resources to support high quality scientific  
assessment by highly qualified experts within competitive timeframes. 
  
EFPIA comments:  
Adequate and appropriate funding of the EMA and NCAs is essential to support the effective and 
efficient operation of the European Medicines Regulatory Network (EMRN) and to ensure public health. 
The European regulatory system, and specifically all its centralised activities, relies on the EMRN’s 
workload capacity. EFPIA welcomes that the EC’s proposals are built upon a cost-based approach 
across the EMRN, including for EMA and NCAs.  
 
The funding of NCAs across the EU is critical to the efficient functioning of the system: the scientific 
expertise provided by the NCAs is the cornerstone of this system. The Fees regulation needs to ensure 
that the funding of activities is adequate to support NCAs and ensure that NCAs that are funded by 
fees can continue to contribute to those centralised initiatives beyond MAA assessment activities. In 
reading the documents released by the EU Commission in the context of this consultation, it is not 
clear what costs actually went into building the model so, due to this limited line of sight, EFPIA is not 
able to weigh in holistically. EFPIA would appreciate confirmation from EU regulators that the new 
proposal would sustainably support future needs, to ensure adequate availability of resources to 
support high quality scientific assessment by highly qualified experts within competitive timeframes. 
The fees should be used at national level to help support continuous development of assessment 
expertise.  NCAs’ workload and costs should also be further taken into consideration in the ongoing 
monitoring. For instance, EFPIA positively notes in article 5(2) that, for fees reductions, the NCA will 
still receive the full amount. However, EFPIA notes that the Performance information (6) listed in 
Annex VI is limited to the number of hours spent by the rapporteurs and co-rapporteurs. It should be 
clarified that these hours account for the experts and colleagues employed by their NCAs to support 
the assessment, which is pertinent for Multi-National Assessment Teams (MNATs). 
 
While not offering input on distributions between EMA and NCAs, EFPIA believes the system should be 
fair and proportionate to the service and resources provided. While appreciating that the Commission’s 
proposals have been built upon a data-driven assessment of revenues versus costs to deliver specific 
services, EFPIA notices that the remuneration for some activities will decrease in comparison to the 
Page 5 of 10 
 
amount in the current system. As an example, the scientific advice (SA) procedure can be mentioned. 
EFPIA members are concerned that NCA remuneration for the SA procedure will significantly decrease 
in real terms. This decrease is being implemented at a time when the resources and capacity of the EU 
Network are under significant strain, and at a time when companies are experiencing delays and other 
challenges (such as obtaining in a timely manner further or additional clarification of scientific advice, 
especially when it is received in writing only) in SA procedures. The proposed reduction is significant, 
with fees for a single SA procedure reduced by 20 to 40%. The distribution to an NCA for its 
participation in an SA procedure will also decrease proportionally compared to some other NCA 
supported activities (e.g., serving as MAA Rapporteur). This could function as an unintentional 
deterrent for NCAs’ contributions to SA, and thus could exacerbate an already significant challenge, 
lessen opportunities for continued development of cutting-edge scientific expertise within NCAs, and 
ultimately have a chilling effect on the support for R&D in Europe. As such, we strongly urge the EC to 
confirm that the fee and remuneration genuinely reflect the actual costs for NCA participation. The 
cost-based approach for SA should include indicators of timelines achieved and should be closely 
monitored and transparently shared. The fees should be drafted in a way that allows EMA to charge 
for the full range of scientific advice (e.g., follow-up scientific advice, SA on drug-device/drug-
companion diagnostic combination, parallel joint scientific advice consultations with HTA bodies). 
 
The resource constraints are shared among the EU Network, but their scale and impact vary among 
NCAs. Some NCAs have little capacity and/or lack the capabilities to participate as much as others in 
EMA-coordinated initiatives. To overcome some of these constraints, the EMA and HMA implemented a 
multi-national assessment team model to complement the co-rapporteur model that has been in place 
since the EMEA was founded in 1995. Therefore, with the addition in Article 1(b), industry wishes to 
ensure sufficient flexibility from the revised fees to progress the use of and appropriate remuneration 
to all contributors to MNATs. 
 
Given its essential role in patient health, EFPIA considers that public monies should contribute a 
significant portion to achieving a well-resourced, robust EU regulatory system, and many non-fee-
generating activities and infrastructure investments should be supported by public budgets. EFPIA 
believes the investment in resources and expertise in these areas is insufficient to guarantee 
functioning of the regulatory system in the future. For example, many technological advancements 
(e.g., new IT systems, databases) necessitate increased public monies to ensure their future viability.  
 
EFPIA strongly supports the built-in flexibility to revise the fees based on the ongoing monitoring of 
the associated costs for regulatory activities. However, any increase in fee-for-service charges should 
only be used to directly support these regulatory actions and not be used to offset current or future 
reductions in public monies.  
 
Appropriate implementation of the EMA’s regulatory science improvement agenda is essential for 
future-proofing the EU regulatory system. Such implementation needs to be built upon ongoing 
stakeholder engagement and appropriate resourcing. As such, EFPIA welcomes the introduction of a 
new fee for the assessment of data packages on an on-going basis and believes that this fee can 
support advances in regulatory and data sciences, and more timely new medicines for EU patients. 
 
To accommodate future changes, EFPIA considers it effective and efficient to establish an ongoing 
consultation platform with involved stakeholders, including the pharmaceutical industry, to exchange 
experiential information and to capture all inputs necessary when the structure and level of fees are 
being reviewed or revised to support the agility objective. EFPIA would welcome discussion about 
Page 6 of 10 
 
other changes to the Fees system that meet the noted principles and that could provide EMA with 
additional financial resources to enact some of the medicine development recommendations from its 
Regulatory Science Strategy. Specifically, potential funding models in the regulatory science areas of 
PRIME, iterative R&D dialogue, and dynamic regulatory assessment should be fully explored. In the 
EC’s proposals, it is unclear if such funding model flexibilities to support PRIME and iterative R&D 
dialogue can be accommodated. The ability for the EC to levy fees for additional/new services by 
means of delegated acts (beyond the so-called “scientific services” for which the EMA may levy fees 
under Article 4) should be clarified. See our proposed legislative revision in the “Summary table of 
EFPIA’s views on specific EMA fees topics” at the end of the document. 
 
Simplicity: 
EFPIA principle: The fee system should be clear and simple to avoid unnecessary  
administrative burden for payers of the fees and for the EMA. 
 
EFPIA comments: EFPIA appreciates that simplification is an underlying objective in the EC’s current 
proposal and that the option 3 ‘light’ was selected, as it would offer the best balance of simplification 
and proportionality. EFPIA welcomes the EC’s proposal to expand the scope of the CAP annual 
maintenance fee to cover the work associated with processing minor variations (i.e., Type 1A/1B 
variations). As proposed, a predictable and equitable fee structure will significantly reduce the 
administrative burden associated with the financial processing of individual Type I variations by 
companies and EMA, while supporting the appropriate level of regulatory oversight. Furthermore, this 
proposal could help to facilitate future developments towards a more streamlined, efficient oversight 
of variations that have no impact on quality, safety or efficacy.  
 
EFPIA appreciates the EC’s acknowledgement that future impactful changes – potentially brought 
about by the pending revisions of the EU’s General Pharmaceutical Legislation (GPL) – may result in 
further simplifications, potential efficiencies arising from increased digitisation or new provisions with 
Fee Regulation implications. The EC states that “any new fee system will need to be agile and 
adaptable in order to respond to the rapidly changing landscape in the pharmaceutical sector to cater 
for any new provisions that may emerge from this revision process” (reference: SWD). EFPIA supports 
proposals which reinforce expertise-driven assessment and make the centralised activities more agile 
by reconsidering EMA’s committee structure and embracing new working philosophies to enhance 
Member State’s ability to bring forward their best expertise in the areas they can best contribute. The 
quality and extent of assessment expertise provided by the NCA should be directly enabled by the fee 
it receives. Similarly, EFPIA supports the removal of marketing authorisation renewals through the 
revision of the GPL, which should lead to a decrease of the annual fees.  
 
The benefits of the simplification currently proposed by the EC should, however, not be lost by 
increasing the complexity of the fees in other areas. For instance, we note that the multiplication of 
fees for inspections could lead to errors and ultimately increase the invoicing and payment burden. 
 
Flexibility: 
EFPIA principle: Reductions and waivers should be allowed for some procedures for certain justified 
categories of medicines and actors (e.g., orphan drugs and SMEs) or in exceptional  
circumstances (e.g., imperative reasons of public health). 
 
EFPIA comments: EFPIA appreciates the acknowledgement in the EC’s proposals that paediatric and 
orphan regulatory activity fees will be waived for applicants. As previously commented, EFPIA 
Page 7 of 10 
 
considers that any new fees in these important research areas could inadvertently serve as a 
disincentive in contradiction with the original intent to foster the development of medicinal products 
for paediatric and orphan diseases. In waiving these fees, EFPIA considers that the new fees will not 
function as a research hinderance supporting sustainable medicine progress; however, this positive 
development and encouragement of new research will need to be carried forward during any potential 
future revision of the paediatric and orphan legislations such that these fee waivers are protected. For 
the same reason, the current fee reductions put in place by the EMA Executive Director for these 
products should also be carried forward. 
 
EFPIA welcomes the flexibility envisaged in the EC’s proposals to implement a future-proofing 
approach to the EMA fees system. In conjunction with appropriate safeguards such as KPIs / change 
triggers, transparency, independent audits, and stakeholder consultation, it seems reasonable for the 
EC to have the flexibility to amend the Annex by means of delegated acts. EFPIA believes that the 
provisions should explicitly note that the pharmaceutical industry will be consulted in the context of 
the delegated act process (see also comments above, under Transparency, and see our proposed 
legislative revision in the “Summary table of EFPIA’s views on specific EMA fees topics” at the end of 
the document). 
 
EFPIA believes that clarification is needed as to whether modification of the annexes includes the 
possibility for the EC to levy (new) fees, where required and whether this can only be done on 
proposal of the EMA in its report or also independently (see also comments above, under 
“Sustainability”). The EC proposal set a minimum of 3-year cycle for the EMA to provide its special 
report and recommendation to the EC. Based on these recommendations, the EC can amend the 
annexes via a delegated act. EFPIA considers that a clear timeline, with a deadline for proposing such 
delegated act as well as its implementation, should be built into the legislation. It should also be 
confirmed that the EC may be able to adopt delegated acts at more regular intervals than the 
standard 3-year EMA special report/review cycle (where warranted by the relevant factors listed in the 
legislation). 
 
As it is unclear from the proposals if it is possible to ‘ring-fence’ or ‘earmark’ (i.e., designate funds or 
resources for a particular purpose) any funding for specific regulatory activities (e.g., regulatory 
science initiatives), EFPIA considers that this opportunity could be inserted into the GPL. 
 
 
Concluding remarks: 
As described in our summary commentary (free text submission), detailed input (attachment to 
submission), and itemised synopsis table (attachment insert below), EFPIA’s members support a 
revision of the EMA fee system and underlying Regulation that is consistent with its fundamental 
principles of transparency, fairness & proportionality, sustainability, simplicity, and flexibility. Many 
aspects presented in the current EC proposal are well aligned with those principles. EFPIA notes that 
the fees system as implemented via this proposal would introduce cost-based, justified, transparent, 
and validated calculation of fees and reduce the number of procedural fees by broadening the CAP 
annual fee. EFPIA further considers that some streamlining measures will assist in resource 
preservation, for regulators, Marketing Authorisation Holders and applicants, over the longer term. 
Incorporating additional transparency and stakeholder engagement opportunities will further 
strengthen the fee system and support future-proofing. Through improvements to the EMA fees 
system, the regulatory system will not only be viable for today, but vibrant for tomorrow.

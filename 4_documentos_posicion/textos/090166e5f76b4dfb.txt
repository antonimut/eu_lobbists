Position on the EC Proposal for Regulation of EMA Fees   
January 2023 
 
1 
 
Executive Summary:  
 
Medicines for Europe supports a cost-based EMA fee system with a simpler fee structure. For generic and 
biosimilar medicines, it is important to keep regulatory fees at a reasonable level to avoid the withdrawal of 
older, but essential medicines from the market. Regrettably, the new fee proposal will lead to an overall 
significant and disproportionate increase in fees for generic medicines producers, mainly caused by too high 
(2,5-fold) an increase in annual Pharmacovigilance fees, although there has not been such a notable increase 
in pharmacovigilance activities in recent times. The reason for such a substantial increase is not clear or 
transparent, although it is a legal obligation to justify any increase in PhV fees.  As a new fee system is 
supposed to be cost-based, a very clear breakdown of these costs must be provided, with  the details of what 
exactly is being/ is going to be funded by these annual PhV fees being clearly justified.  
If this massive increase covers an investment in IT infrastructure and/ or investment in the analysis of health 
data/ real word data to enable a better decision-making process and to support innovation, it should be a 
separate category of fees, funded proportionally by the future beneficiaries and   main users, and not mainly 
by generic medicine companies.   
 
While the cost of regulatory processes may not be as important for high-profit/high-margin medicines, for 
generic medicines with very low margins, regulatory costs can be critical in maintaining older products or 
supplying products to small markets or markets with lower or even zero profitability. Therefore, from a 
generic medicine perspective, it is particularly important to keep fees reasonable and affordable.  
The fees for the initial generic medicine application of 141 200 € appear to adequately reflect the actual 
workload involved in assessing generics compared to NCEs/full dossiers. According to the EC Pharma review 
proposal, the assessment of generic medicines is to be simplified and shortened, which will entail less 
involvement on the part of the assessors. The   costs should be maintained at the level proposed as they 
reflect the cost-based principles well. 
 
We have noted a significant increase in fees for biosimilar medicines for the Initial biosimilar medicines 
application (the fees will change from 200k€ to 575k€ - i.e: a nearly 3-fold increase. As the current trend is 
moving towards abandoning the requirement for comparative clinical trials/ extensive clinical data package, 
the time needed to assess a biosimilar application may now be shorter compared to the assessment 
performed in the past and such an   increase in fees may no longer be justified. We therefore suggest an 
adjustment of fees for the biosimilar medicine application to the level of 450k€ (if clinical comparability 
studies are provided- closer to fixed dose combination rather than to New Entity) and be reduced to around 
400k€ where the data package no longer includes any extensive comparative study. 
 
The reduction of fees for scientific advice is welcomed by companies (making scientific advice more 
affordable), but at the same time we would like to stress that the reduction of fees should not have a negative 
impact on the quality/accessibility of scientific advice and the motivation for experts from national agencies 
to engage in advising. It is therefore particularly important to establish the right proportions in the 
distribution of fees between the EMA and NCAs.   
 
From an industry perspective, it is difficult to assess the fairness of a fee sharing proposal between the EMA 
and national agencies. In general, we support a fair distribution of funds, taking into account the time and 
expertise that experts from national agencies bring to EMA activities. Furthermore, the EMA's fee system 
should not negatively affect the allocation of national agency resources to national regulatory processes 
(including DCP/MRP authorisation).

Key requests:  
• to further reduce Pharmacovigilance annual fees for generic medicines from the current 20% to a 
minimum of 50%  
- If this massive increase of PhV annual fees covers an investment in IT infrastructure and/ or 
investment in the analysis of health data/ real word data to enable a better decision-making process 
and to support innovation, there should be a separate category of fees, funded proportionally by the 
future beneficiaries and main users, not mainly by generic medicine companies.   
• to adjust fees for the biosimilar medicine application to the level of 450 k€ (if clinical comparability 
studies are provided- closer to fixed dose combination rather than to New Entity), to be reduced to 
around 400k€ where the data package no longer includes any extensive comparative study.  
• to maintain initial fees for generic applications at the proposed level as they reflect well the cost-based 
principles and simplification/ shortening of assessment foreseen by the revision of the Pharma Legislation  
• to establish the right proportions in the distribution of fees between the EMA and NCAs.   
• to clarify how all activities leading to regulatory optimisation will be financed (I.e. investments into ISO 
IDMP/ SPOR and in digitalisation of the network) 
 
We understand that the EMA fee regulation is not the right legal instrument to propose systemic changes. 
However, we would like to stress that the reform of the system should be towards an overall optimisation 
of the regulatory processes, where they are   streamlined, time-efficient and less resource consuming (both 
EMA and national procedures). The cost-based approach does not mean that companies should always 
cover costs without any effort on the part of authorities to streamline processes. We trust that the upcoming 
revision of the Pharma Legislation will address this appropriately.  
It is also unclear how all those activities leading to regulatory optimisation will be financed (i.e. IT solutions 
leading to cost savings for administrative processes and necessary infrastructure such as IDMP/SPOR and 
building interoperable system with the NCAs).  
 
Whilst we recognise that the updating of medicine prices for inflation is not dependent on the EMA Fee 
Regulation, we would like to stress that the expectation of the EMA and national authorities that a 
mechanism will be in place to take account of inflation should go hand in hand with the possibility of 
indexing medicine prices at Member State level. Support from the national authorities and the EMA in 
discussions with payers would be welcome. 
 
While we recognise that the majority of the EMA budget comes from fees paid by the industry, it shall be 
also an increased contribution from the European Union as a part of EU contribution to public health. It 
could cover particularly those expenses of the EMA/ EU Network functioning which are not product/ 
procedure related.

Detailed Position Paper on the EC Proposal for Regulation of 
EMA Fees   
 
Background 
 
The European Commission has proposed to update and simplify the legislation on fees charged by the European 
Medicines Agency (EMA). This fee also includes the remuneration paid by EMA to national authorities involved 
in the assessment. The intention of the proposal is to simplify the entire EMA fee structure and to make it more 
cost based.  
 
Assessment of the new proposal  
 
General comments:  
In general, Medicines for Europe supports a cost-based EMA fee system with a simpler fee structure.  
 
For generic and biosimilar medicine manufacturers, regulatory fees affect patient access to medicines. While 
the cost of regulatory processes may not be as important for high-profit/high-margin medicines, for generic 
medicines with very low margins, regulatory costs can be critical in maintaining older products or supplying 
products to small markets or markets with the lowest, or even zero profitability. Therefore, from a generic 
perspective, it is particularly important to keep fees reasonable.  
 
We fully support a cost-based system and the principle that fees must be proportionate to the time spent on 
their assessment. There needs to be a very clear breakdown of the costs of what exactly is funded by the 
fees, especially some more general flat fees such as annual fees (administrative and pharmacovigilance). A 
mechanism of controlling those costs and the allocation of fees shall also be put in place in order to regularly 
assess the fairness of the system.  
 
The proposal also covers fee-sharing between the EMA and the national agencies. Although it is difficult to 
assess the fairness of the proposal to share fees between the EMA and national agencies, we would like to 
stress that the proposed sharing system should not negatively impact the functioning of national agencies 
and their readiness and capacities to provide services, both at EMA level and in national procedures.  
Generally, we support an equitable distribution of funds, taking into account the time and expertise that 
experts from national agencies bring to EMA activities. Furthermore, the EMA's fee system should not 
negatively affect the allocation of national agency resources to national regulatory processes (including 
DCP/MRP authorisation) and should not transfer the cost of missing income onto fees at the national level. 
 
We understand that the EMA fee regulation is not the right legal instrument to propose systemic changes. 
However, we would like to stress that the reform of the system should be towards an overall optimisation of 
the regulatory processes, where they are streamlined, time-efficient and less resource consuming (both EMA 
and national procedures). The cost-based approach does not mean that companies should always cover costs 
without any effort on the part of authorities to streamline processes. We trust that the upcoming revision of 
the Pharma Legislation will address this extensively.  
 
Whilst we recognise that the updating of medicine prices for inflation is not dependent on the EMA Fee 
Regulation, we would like to stress that the expectation of the EMA and national authorities that a 
mechanism will be in place to take account of inflation should go hand in hand with the possibility of 
indexing medicine prices at Member State level. Support from the national authorities and the EMA in 
discussions with payers would be welcome.

Justification:  
The reason for such a considerable increase is not clear or transparent. There has not been such a significant 
increase in pharmacovigilance activities in recent times. In addition, the proposed annual pharmacovigilance 
fee (€ 190 per chargeable unit) has greatly increased in comparison with the amount proposed in the public 
consultation conducted in 2021. (€ 107 per chargeable unit- increased from €75).  
- The cost alignment objective stems from the main Fee Regulation which stipulates that any review of 
the fees shall be based on an evaluation of the Agency’s costs and   the related costs of the services 
provided for by the Member States. The pharmacovigilance fee regulation specifically states that “Any 
future revisions of the pharmacovigilance fees or other fees levied by the Agency should be based on a 
transparent and independent evaluation of the costs of the Agency and the costs of the tasks carried out 
by the national competent authorities.” Thus, a very clear breakdown of the costs must be provided, 
with   what exactly is being/ is going to be funded by these annual fees being very clearly justified. As 
the vast majority of annual PhV fees going to the EMA budget are paid by generic medicine companies 
(due to the calculation of payments based on the so-called 'chargeable unit' related to the number of 
marketing authorisations), this 2.5-fold increase will disproportionately increase our industry's 
contribution to the EMA for activities that are less directly related to   industry activities/ services 
provided by the EMA to the generic medicines industry. If this massive increase of PhV annual fees 
covers an investment in IT infrastructure and/ or investment in analysis of health data/ real word data 
to enable a better decision-making process and to support innovation, it should be a separate category 
of fees and funded proportionally by the future beneficiaries and future main users, not mainly by 
generic medicine companies.

Justification: 
It is positive that Type IA and IB variation fees have been included in the annual fees. It simplifies payments and

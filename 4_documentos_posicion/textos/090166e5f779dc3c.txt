Position Paper 
Euro 7 proposal   
 
Liquid Gas Europe is a European association composed of national LPG associations, main LPG suppliers, 
distributors and equipment manufacturers of LPG and renewable LPG including bioLPG, renewable DME and 
other drop -in and complementary gases. We ad vocate for a safe, supportive legislative and regulatory 
environment for the liquid gas industry to thrive and enable its transition to being 100% renewable by 2050. A 
thriving and innovative European liquid gas industry will ensure a just energy transitio n for our industrial and 
domestic customers, both on the road and off-the-grid. 
On a well-to-wheel basis, LPG’s carbon footprint is up to 23% lower compared to petrol and has been observed 
to emits significantly less s carbon dioxide (CO2) and particulates  compared to gasoline and lower oxides of 
nitrogen (NOx) and particulates compared to diesel. 1The liquid gas industry is fully committed to the objectives 
outlined in the ‘Fit For 55’ package. Liquid Gas Europe believes by 2050 the liquid gas sector can be fully 
renewable. Renewable liquid gases and its blends, such as bio-propane, bio-butane, and renewable and recycled 
carbon DME, are drop-in fuels already available on the market. 
BioLPG and Renewable & Recycled Carbon DME offer a great opportunity to defossilise the set of available fuels 
for the present and future vehicle fleet in the EU. BioLPG can lower the carbon footprint of LPG by around 80% 
while maintaining the same particulate emissions and chemical properties. It can be used in any ratio with 
conventional LPG and can be injected into  existing infrastructure without the need for modificat ion or 
replacement of equipment. Similarly, renewable and recycled carbon DME, which is produced from renewable 
sources such as waste streams , can be combined with LPG up to 20% and can be used in existing vehicles 2for 
additional carbon emission savings.  
Euro 7  presents a unique opportunity for a new generation of fuels: CO2 -neutral fuels. With Regulation EU 
2019/631 setting the stage for their introduction outside the scope of the fleet standards. We believe that clear 
guidelines for the inclusion of CO2-neutral fuels from the onset will pave the way for future-proofing the internal 
combustion engine after 2035, while respecting the principles of technological neutrality.  
 
 
Liquid Gas Europe calls upon EU policymakers to take the following legislative recommendations into account:  
1. Consider LPG as a clean and affordable fuel for vehicles in the Euro 7 emissions regime. 
2. Future-proof the internal combustion engine  by laying the ground stones for CO2 -Neutral fuels to 
become the norm before and after 2035. 
3. Provide manufacturers with appropriate leeway  between the entry into force of the Regulation and 
the applicability of the rules.

1. Consider LPG as a clean and affordable fuel for vehicles in the Euro 7 emissions regime.  
Reducing emissions in transport has different challenges among the 27 Member States. Many Central, Eastern, 
and Southern EU countries rely on older, inexpensive and second -hand vehicles. Reducing pollutant emissions 
while also enabling access to affordable  mobility are the key advantages of LPG. This is evident by the fact that 
over 8.5 million registered vehicles in the EU run on LPG, either through new car purchases or retrofits of existing 
cars3. LPG can also be replaced with its renewable variant bioLP G and blended with renewable and recycled 
carbon DME without any alterations to the refuelling infrastructure or engines.  
LPG presents consumers with a possibility of reducing pollutant levels compared to gasoline and diesel, which 
is why Liquid Gas Europe welcomes the emissions limits set out in Euro 7.  
When assessing real world performance of Autogas cars under Euro 6 rules, LPG cars outperform gasoline -
powered cars. CO2 emissions from LPG powered cars are 11% - 19% lower than gasoline cars4. LPG significantly 
outperforms gasoline vehicles in carbon monoxide (CO) emissions and Particulate Number (PN) emissions, at 
97% and 96% respectively. It’s NOx and HC emissions are similar to gasoline at 54% and 99% respectively.  
Characterized by low energy-specific carbon content and high anti-knock properties, LPG offers engine efficiency 
benefits, which contributes to additional CO2 reductions.  
As mentioned in the explanatory memorandum of the proposal, Euro 7 also sets out to improve the real -world 
emissions throughout the life -time of a vehicle for the second -hand market. As demonstrated, LPG therefore 
fulfils this role.  
 
2. Future-proof the internal combustion engine by laying the ground stones for CO2-Neutral fuels to 
become the norm before and after 2035. 
The developments in energy from renewable sources are so rapid that they need to be monitored constantly . 
The proposal takes a right step in providing the Commission with the flexibility to adopt delegated acts to reflect 
technological progress  in Article 15. Being able to do so ensures that all cost -effective and efficient fuels are 
adopted promptly to garner appropriate recognition. 
This is made even more important with the inclusion of Recital 9a in revision of Regulation EU 2019/631 . It is 
therefore crucial that the Regulation recognizes the  importance of CO2 -neutral fuels in vehicles after 2035 . 
Liquid Gas Europe welcomes Recital 18 of the proposal, anticipating vehicles running exclusively on CO2 neutral 
fuels. However, this is not reflected in Article 15 . We therefore  believe the inclusion of clear wording on 
Delegated Acts on CO2-neutral fuels is essential to ensure affordable mobility.   
Proposed inclusion for Article 15:  
“amend Article 5 to introduce new options for manufacturers, such as vehicles fuelled exclusively by CO2 -
netral fuel, if such vehicles become available.” 
CO2 neutral fuels could be those defined by the official and consolidated data for GHG emissions and GHG 
emission savings for biofuels, bioliquids, and biomass fuels, as found in Annex V and Annex VI of Directive 
2018/2001/EU (RED). According to Annex V, Part C, Point 13, and Annex VI, Part B, Point 13 of RED the emissions 
of these fuels are considered to be zero. Therefore, biofuels, bioliquids, biomass fuels, and RFNBOs should be 
considered "CO2 neutral" when the emissions for the use of the fuel are considered to be zero (REDII. 
Manufacturers also require certainty on the options available for the construction and definition of vehicles, as 
found in Article 5 of the proposal. In order to ensure that manufacturers are aware of the option of CO2-neutral

BioLPG is produced from renewable and organic feedstocks and can reduce LPG’s carbon footprint by up to 80%, 
depending on the feedstock used. Chemically identical to conventional LPG,  it carries the same emissions 
properties, significantly contributing to better air quality. BioLPG can be blended at any rate with LPG and still 
be used in existing infrastructure. It means that distributors and consumers do not need to change or upgrade 
their equipment to switch to a renewable alternative energy solution. Similarly, renewable and recycled carbon 
DME is a gaseous fuel produced from a wide range of renewable feedstock, including waste streams and 
residues. Chemically similar to LPG, it can be blended with LPG up to 20% and used in existing vehicles. 
Liquid Gas Europe believes that the agreement to phase out the internal combustion engine (ICE) by 2035 in the 
revision of Regulation EU 2019/631 is a missed opportunity to  recognise the role of low carbon and renewable 
fuels in decarbonising the transport sector.  By focusing solely on tailpipe emissions (Tank -To-Wheel), the CO2 
standards misleadingly label battery electric and fuel cell vehicles as ‘zero emission’, distorts competition and 
contradicts the principle of technological neutrality. More importantly, it completely ignores the life cycle carbon 
impacts of electric vehicles and the life cycle carbon benefits of renewable fuelled vehicles . Liquid Gas Europe 
believes that the Euro 7 proposal can begin to amend this by focussing on a Life -cycle Approach (LCA) to 
measuring emissions.  
 
3. Provide manufacturers with appropriate leeway between the entry into force of the Regulation and 
the applicability of the rules.  
Finally, the Proposal is set to enter into force 20 days after publication in the Official Journal of the European 
Union. Since the proposal has been delayed and  will have to go through both the Council and the European 
Parliament, it is unlikely that the Regulation will be approved by the Institutions before the end of 2023. Before 
then, no certainty will be given to manufacturers on the final rules. Therefore, the application of Euro 7 as of 
July 2025 for M1 and N1 vehicles is a very tight timeframe. It will also be important for the AGVES working group 
to finalize the discussions on the Implementing Acts that are still outstanding.

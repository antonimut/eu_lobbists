Raising standards for consumers 
ANEC Response to public consultation: European vehicle 
emissions standards – Euro 7 for cars, vans, lorries and 
buses 
 
Overview: 
This initiative, which is part of the European Green Deal, will develop stricter  
emissions standards (Euro 7) for all petrol and diesel cars, vans, lorries and buses.  
To ensure vehicles on EU roads are clean over their lifetime, the proposed rules will 
consider new vehicle technologies and ensure emissions are measured in real-time. 
This initiative is part of the EU’s commitment to accelerate the shift to sustainable  
and smart mobility. 
______________________ 
In general, ANEC welcomes the Commission’s intention to modernise and simplify 
the technical requirements applying to type -approved motor vehicles regarding 
emissions. Air pollution is a very serious environmental and human health risk in 
European cities and the urban population is still exposed to pollutant levels above 
the limit va lues, a large part of which is due to road vehicles. The thresholds 
established by the EURO 6 regulation were outdated and it was therefore crucial to 
review the rules , so that emission limits would reflect the technological progress 
achieved since 2007. 
We acknowledge that this draft regulation proposal contains several positive 
elements that will contribute to reduce emissions of road vehicles: 
• The test conditions proposed in Euro 7 reflect real -life driving conditions 
better than the Euro 6 conditions did, with more realistic ambient 
temperatures and more accurate extended driving conditions.  
• The control of the real -life emissions is also improved, with  emissions 
measurements throughout the longer part of a vehicle’s useful lifetime. This 
is crucial considering the second-hand vehicle export market to EU and non-
EU countries. 
• The introduction of fuel and technology neutral emission limits for NOx and 
CO, as well as for NH3 and HCHO are also a big step forward to clean exhaust

• The introduction of minimum performance requirements for durability of EV

Next to these general improvements, however, we remain critical about some 
other elements of the proposed regulation:  
• The proposed durability of 200.000km or 10 years for passenger cars and 
vans is too lenient. Some manufacturers have already reached this durability 
threshold and provide a 10-year or 200.000 km warranty for their vehicles’ 
main system units. The Euro 7 regulation should incentivise them to innovate 
and do better. Even more importantly, a 10-year durability objective does not 
match the average life expectancy of a car in the EU, which is 11,8 years on 
average, and closer to 15 years in some Eastern European countries.   
• While the introduction  of a brake and tire wear particle emission  control is 
good news, we urge the Commission to develop and implement an efficient 
measurement method as fast as possible . Non-exhaust emissions are very 
harmful to human health and to the environment , so any delay in the  
development of measurement methods must be avoided. 
• The system of vehicle designation, which introduces 8 sub-categories of Euro 
7 vehicles (Euro 7+; Euro 7A; Euro 7G; Euro 7+A; Euro 7+G; Euro 7+AG; 
Euro 7AG; Euro 7ext), is overly complicated and will only prove confusing to 
the consumer. It goes d irectly against the intention to reduce complexity  
announced in the preamble of the regulation.  
• Although we appreciate the concept of an environmental vehicle passport, as 
it would inform consumers and enable them to make educated choices when 
purchasing a vehicle, we see several potential issues related to privacy that 
should be clarified and avoided.  
 
All in all, ANEC expresses a relatively positive opinion about this draft Euro 7 
regulation. T he new whole -vehicle testing procedure , which measures emission 
performance on typical use with larger boundaries and enhanced durability, should 
lead to emission savings.  
We however draw the Commission’s attention to the above -mentioned limitations 
regarding durability thresholds, non -exhaust emission control and vehicle 
designation, which will undermine the emission-saving potential of the regulation , 
and its societal acceptance.

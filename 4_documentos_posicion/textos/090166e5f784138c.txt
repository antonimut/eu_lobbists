The weaknesses of the present electricity market as ruled out by the Commission. 
The present rules make a clear cut between electricity producers, transport and distribution 
with the aim to promote competition for the advantage of the customers. 
Whether the intend is fair, a basic mistake has been made. In an open market, competition is 
only achievable if the product is easily available and freely accessible to any consumer. 
To provide a network with electricity in a smooth way, the power produced must be exactly 
the power called by the network. Adjustments must occur within seconds to avoid blackouts. 
With exception of water dams and peculiar geographical conditions, nowadays, electricity 
cannot be stored at the scale of a region and moreover not at the scale of a country. 
Electricity is not easily available and freely accessible to any consumer. Electricity is not 
suitable for a market structure as presently imposed by the EU commission. 
The market of electricity cannot physically be « liquid » and the cuts between production, 
transport and distribution just increase the problems already known long time ago. They 
became more critical with the preference given to the “green” generators depending on how 
much there is wind or sun.

When there is wind and sun, the traditional power generators must reduce their production to 
give a free way to the “green” power on the network. 
When there is no wind or sun, the traditional generators have to supply the network at fixed 
price to the network.  
When there is no wind or sun, the “liquidity” of the market is provided by flexible generators, 
being mainly gas power plants. 
Production capacities of these plants have either been sold on PPA’s agreement with 
distributors or just kept in stand-by to deliver the expected service to the network.  
The last months of 2022 have shown that when gas power plants are called for delivery the 
power is sold against the gas spot price at that moment. Efficiency on gas being 35-50% at 
the best, the electricity power is loaded with a cost two to three times the gas spot price.  
In the meantime, distributors will enjoy the fixed price of long terms purchase agreements 
with stable generators. They will resale the power at the highest electricity spot price.  
Huge profits are made by the ones taking not any industrial risks. 
 
OEEMO STRATEGY 
100TWh scheme for an Open European Electricity Market Organisation 
As a prerequisite, Europe must have the humility to admit that leading the climate battle 
cannot be done at the expense of the European citizens. Should we remind that in 2018, the 
CO2 “savings” achieved by the European countries did not represent 10% of the “increases” 
of the rest of the world? 
EU’s mission 
EU’s commission must stick on its mission define objectives and market’s rules but must not 
interfere with the players on the market. Its mission is at it follows. 
1. Define the carbon targets calendar for electricity production in EU 
2. Define a carbon tax to cope with the targets. 
3. All electricity generation sources must be allowed. Market will adjust in function of the 
calendar and the tax. 
4. Subsidies may be devoted to only to the developments upstream of the electricity 
generation or downstream for its consumption. 
 R&D subsidies for development deemed to be new and in favour of European 
developments. 
 Give incentive for a better use of electricity. 
5. Protect and develop the competitiveness of the European industry during the transition 
calendar. 
 Organise the transition calendar by allowing new shale oil/gas production and 
exploitation of existing natural gas deposits no longer permitted to day (e.g., Adriatic 
Sea) 
 Adapt the strategy and the targets as it might be required by the international 
competition. 
 Direct the production ways and means towards a maximum economical 
independency of Europe. 
 Favour the competition by giving free access in any country in Europe to all 
European energy generators. 
 
The basic rules of an open market. 
To promote an open market for a non-storable product as electricity the following principle 
must be imposed. 
1. Any generation source must be economically self-sustainable.  
2. Subsidies must not be allowed to already proven industrial processes. 
3. Power generators must be responsible for the delivery of the power from the power plant 
to the consumer’s plugs at home. They are generators and distributors. 
4. Power generators must deliver 80-90% of the production at a maximum fixed price giving 
a fair profit for the industrial risks taken. Price formulas will be defined to average costs 
and profit over given periods of time. e.g., 10% change in the cost of raw of month 
materiel will generate change for the price two month later. 
5. The sale’s price must encompass reasonable profit on assets, the carbon tax, the 
production costs, the delivery of the power on the network, the dismantling of the plants, 
the management of the wastes. 
6. Distributors must be sale agent of the power generators paid against sale’s fees. 
7. Long term price agreements must be limited between power generators to cover 
production shut down incidents. Long term agreement with customers will also allow 
flexible prices adjustment based on clear rules. 
8. Power transport will be under the responsibility of the states to warrantee the expected 
delivery of power to the consumers through either state’s owned company or privates 
ones working under state’s supervision. 
9. Power generators are also distributors, they will have free access to the entire EU 
territory.  
 
Management of the rules. 
Energy, electricity, is a common resource. It must be globally managed.  
Being given the structural weakness of Europe for any energy resource, only the mass of the 
European economy is (still?) a negotiation leverage tool at the scale of the world. 
A supra-national organisation would be in charge for the follow-up of the rules, the 
coordination between the EU’s states (networks interconnexion, equilibrium of the resources, 
mutual support…). 
The EU’s states will set-up local management organisation mirroring the  supra-national 
organ. 
 
Guidelines of the rules. 
Promoting multiple generations sources kept within climate objectives by appropriated taxes 
and subsidies will foster competition for the most performant solution in a market maintained 
within managed price limits and will enhance the negotiation power of Europe on international 
market.

Bosch supports Euro 7/VII as the last regulatory step for exhaust emissions. Euro 
7/VII standards can further reduce the impact of new vehicles (and beyond 2035 for 
vehicles in use) on air quality. Given the rapidly growing share of electric vehicles, it is 
reasonable to also set limit values for particle emissions from brakes and tyres. 
Nevertheless, a monitoring period is advisable to test new measurement methods.       
 
It is consistent that the EU-Commission proposes more moderate exhaust emission 
limits for passenger cars (compared to other segments) because of the ICE phase-out 
by 2035. The stringency of exhaust pollutant limits nevertheless increases for all vehicle 
segments.  
Compliance with proposed pollutant limits will already be more challenging for light 
commercial vehicles (LCV), as the limit values are the same as for PC while higher 
vehicle performance is required. The threshold for underpowered vehicles - which 
entitles them to slightly higher limit values – should be raised to 40 kW/t to more 
adequately reflect the LCV in the market.  
The proposed limit values are particularly demanding for heavy duty vehicles and 
are associated with considerably higher costs than estimated by the EU-Commission.   
 
To keep limit levels feasible at all, RDE test conditions must be well defined.  
RDE test conditions require further clarification, e.g. on the criteria for extended driving 
(= challenging driving situations, for which tests are still considered valid but higher 
pollutant limits apply). Criteria should allow a clear distinction between extended driving 
and test situations no longer representative for real-life on-road driving in Europe.  
The latter shall be classified as non-valid tests. Biased driving must be reliably 
excluded, otherwise compliance with Euro7/VII can only be ensured at very high 
development costs, if at all. Clear rules are necessary for manufacturers, suppliers and 
homologation authorities and inspection/testing services alike.

The introduction of Euro 7 standards to all new PC and LCV in July 2025 is neither 
manageable for OEMs nor for suppliers, technical service companies or homologation 
authorities. All the more so, as essential technical details will presumably not be decided 
definitely before the end of 2024.  
To avoid too much tension in the market and a significant vehicle supply shortage in 
2025, new pollutant limits should apply to all PC types only from (earliest) mid-2026. 
This is still very early and requires that 
1. the essential technical details of Euro 7/VII are at least clarified in the 1st half of 2023 
(for OBM already at the very beginning of the year),  
2. the EU Commission aims for pragmatic/ best feasible solutions in the Delegated 
Regulations (e.g. for OBM; RDE test conditions).  
For LCV, new pollutant standards should apply one year later (mid-2027) to give 
manufacturers with both segments the necessary flexibility. 
Any delay in the process of defining technical parameters or increase in ambition (Euro7 
or Delegated Acts) must result in an extension of the implementation time.   
 
An OBM system is only feasible for pollutants that can be measured with 
available automotive sensors. This is the case for NOx and partly PM-Diesel. For 
NH3, these sensors only exist for Diesel vehicles and their durability is limited. NH3 
emissions can however be determined from sensors-based NOx measurements, both 
for Gasoline and Diesel vehicles.

About Robert Bosch GmbH  
 
The Bosch Group is a leading global supplier of technology and services. It employs 
roughly 402,600 associates worldwide (as of December 31, 2021). The company 
generated sales of 78.7 billion euros in 2021. Its operations are divided into four 
business sectors: Mobility Solutions, Industrial Technology, Consumer Goods, and 
Energy and Building Technology. As a leading IoT provider, Bosch offers innovative 
solutions for smart homes, Industry 4.0, and connected mobility. 
 
Bosch is pursuing a vision of mobility that is sustainable, safe, and exciting. It uses its 
expertise in sensor technology, software, and services, as well as its own IoT cloud, to 
offer its customers connected, cross-domain solutions from a single source. The Bosch 
Group’s strategic objective is to facilitate connected living with products and solutions 
that either contain artificial intelligence (AI) or have been developed or manufactured 
with its help. Bosch improves quality of life worldwide with products and services that 
are innovative and spark enthusiasm. In short, Bosch creates technology that is 
“Invented for life.” 
 
The Bosch Group comprises Robert Bosch GmbH and its roughly 440 subsidiary and 
regional companies in some 60 countries. Including sales and service partners, Bosch’s 
global manufacturing, engineering, and sales network covers nearly every country in the 
world. 
 
With its more than 400 locations worldwide, the Bosch Group has been carbon neutral 
since the first quarter of 2020. The basis for the company’s future growth is its 
innovative strength. At 128 locations across the globe, Bosch employs some 76,100 
associates in research and development, of which nearly 38,000 are software 
engineers. 
 
The company was set up in Stuttgart in 1886 by Robert Bosch (1861–1942) as 
“Workshop for Precision Mechanics and Electrical Engineering.” The special ownership 
structure of Robert Bosch GmbH guarantees the entrepreneurial freedom of the Bosch 
Group, making it possible for the company to plan over the long term and to undertake 
significant upfront investments in the safeguarding of its future. Ninety-four percent of 
the share capital of Robert Bosch GmbH is held by Robert Bosch Stiftung GmbH, a 
charitable foundation. The remaining shares are held by Robert Bosch GmbH and by a 
corporation owned by the Bosch family. The majority of voting rights are held by Robert 
Bosch Industrietreuhand KG, an industrial trust. The entrepreneurial ownership 
functions are carried out by the trust.

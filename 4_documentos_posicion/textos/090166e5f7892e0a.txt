1 Scope of Work In Europe all registered vehicles must be periodically tested for safety on the road and adherence to traffic safety and emission regulations.  When checking the exhaust gases, the change for diesel vehicles in the particle meas-urement from turbidity to number measurement is already announced at the beginning of the year 2021. Nitrogen oxide measurement is additionally illuminated as an im-portant additional parameter. A representative NOx measurement is only possible with the appropriate measurement technology. In addition, the preconditioning of the NOx-reducing exhaust aftertreatment has a major influence on the measurement results. The main goal of the project was to analyze test procedures of NOx emission test in a future Periodic Technical Inspection (PTI) and to develop a new concept for future AU-NEW.  With the sensors available in modern cars it is possible to determine end of tailpipe NOx emissions. CO2 emissions can be calculated out of the fuel consumption.  If the efficiency of after treatment systems of conventionally driven cars are insufficient, the NOx-emissions increase and the ratio between CO2 and NOx emissions (𝑄!"#=$	&'($	!'# ) decreases. This ratio can be measured during real world operation and stored on the vehicle in a suitable way. If a defined QNOx-limit is often fallen below, a manda-tory entry in the error memory can be required and the Malfunction Indicator Light (MIL) has to be activated. 2 Method As a first step threshold values for the ratio gram CO2 to gram NOx-emission had to be found. Therefore already existing data from five EURO 6d TEMP diesel, four EURO 6d TEMP petrol, one EURO 6d petrol, five EURO 5 diesel, three EURO 5 petrol and two EURO 4 petrol cars were evaluated. It can be assumed that a EURO 6d vehicle with an insufficient after treatment system, i.e. almost zero NOx conversion, has roughly the same emission behaviour as a EURO 4/5 standard vehicle.  For the verification of the results gained from the existing data on-road and lab tests were performed with four EURO 6d TEMP diesel cars. The measurements were done with SCR & EGR (Selective Catalytic Reduction & Exhaust Gas Recirculation) off, SCR off, EGR off and standard settings. The results consequently represent emission levels with these different malfunctions and the emissions with proper exhaust control. 2.1 Chassis Dyno Measurements Testcar 01 with an EURO 6d TEMP diesel engine with SCR and EGR was measured with a PEMS on the street and in the laboratory on the chassis dynamometer. In addition, the method for defining the threshold values should be further developed. 4 Conclusion  OBM and OBD represent useful instruments to monitor the compliance of tailpipe emis-sions over the lifetime of a vehicle. Additionally, these instruments could be used for PTI. This can be done by simple linking of existing information within the vehicles (by a simple software adaption). For this task a simple indicator for possible malfunctions in the NOx emission control system was elaborated which does not need any new sensors.  A not properly working exhaust gas control system (especially SCR and EGR for con-ventionally driven diesel cars) leads to high measured NOx-emissions without signifi-cant increase in CO2 emissions. Thus the ratio between g CO2 and g NOx de-creases. To get a representative threshold value of this ratio (QNOx = g CO2 / g NOx) a dataset of measured EURO 4,5, 6d Temp & 6d (diesel & petrol) was used with the assumption that a not working aftertreatment system of EURO 6d Temp & 6d cars leads to a similar emission behaviour like EURO 4,5 standard vehicles.  To verify the method, measurements with four EURO 6d Temp diesel cars were done on the street with an installed PEMS. Vehicle 01 was also tested on the chassis dy-namotor in the laboratory. The WLTC- and the ERMES-driving-cycle with different settings for the after treatment system were measured on the test bench. The tests showed, that preconditioning has a big impact for measured emissions.  

 
18  
The measured WLTC with a not working emission control system (AdBlue dosing & EGR off) showed, as expected, the highest NOx-emissions. Two ERMES cycles with a deactivated AdBlue dosing to the SCR- but a working EGR-system of the car were measured after a WLTC with standard settings (AdBlue dosing & EGR on). The measured NOx-emissions for the first following up ERMES were quiet low, although the AdBlue dosing didn’t work during this measurement, the NH3 stored in the catalyst still leads to a conversion of NOx. The second ERMES cy-cle with the same settings for SCR- and EGR-system was measured afterwards and the NOx-emissions were about 7 times higher compared to the first ERMES cycle while the measured CO2 for both cycles was nearly the same.  Thus, a defect of the AdBlue dosing system can be detected by exhaust gas tests only after several kilometres of driving. However, with continuous on board monitor-ing of the the QNOx ratio such a delay is tolerable.   During the RDE test-runs, the vehicles with the deactivated SCR system could be clearly identified by the QNOx method. These QNOx factors were below the QNOx threshold value only for the vehicle settings with simulated defects in the exhaust gas control.  To define the time window for the QNOx determination at real driving further develop-ment would be necessary, also to define the threshold values.  One more open issue is the definition of “undershot thresholds”. That means the number of QNOx factors per trip or per week etc. below the limit until the MIL is acti-vated.  Advantages of implementation of QNOx measurement: § NOx emissions can be normalized to the vehicle’s CO2 emissions  § QNOx monitoring can guarantee compliance of NOx tailpipe emissions over the lifetime of a vehicle § NOx emissions can be monitored at different load profiles during real-world op-eration of vehicles  § The results of the QNOx calculation could be used for detection of relevant de-fects / to activate the malfunction indicator.  To avoid uncertainties if this system will work reliable over the whole lifetime of the vehicle, regularly checks have to be applied which can be included into the periodical technical inspection: § To ensure the QNOx functionality, the vehicle internal NOx sensor has to be checked regularly within PTI  § In such a check, the vehicle internal NOx sensor must deliver the same level via OBD / OBM as the NOx tailpipe exhaust value, measured with an external de-vice at idle speed

5  Annex  Measurement Technology Used Chassis dynamometer  For the measurements in the laboratory a chassis dynamometer of the FVT at TU Graz was used. This test bench is designed as a single axis driven test bench. The driving resistances are dependent on the speed applied by an asynchronous machine. The mass inertia is also controlled by the asynchronous machine. Maintenance of the test bench is done annually or every half year for certain components. Technical data for the test bench: - Brake:    200 kW asynchronous motor - Max. Speed:   200 km/h - Temperature:  -30°C up to +40°C - Vehicle mass:  up to 3500 kg - CVS flow rate:  6, 10 or 20m³/min - Speed controlled airflow -  The test bench can be run in steady-state and transient operation for braking and tow-ing mode.  Stationary operation mode: In this operating mode a control at constant braking force or at constant driving speed is done.  Transient operation mode: In this operating mode the roller braking force is controlled by using the current speed and acceleration of the vehicle according to the specified vehicle data (rolling re-sistance factors, mass, drag coefficient). In this operation mode certification measure-ments and real world cycle measurements are done. Individual driving cycles with var-iable gradient-profiles can be measured.  Emission measurement on the test bench: Emissions are measured using a CVS (Constant Volume Sampling) system and ex-haust gas analysers from AVL (AMA I60). The emissions are recorded in both way, per bag and over time (max. 3 Hz). The particle measurement system complies with PMP-specifications (dilution system with heating and secondary dilution with CPC 3010 or CPC 3040 and if required also DMA 3081 to detect PN size distribution. Additional non-limited exhaust gas components are measured with a so called FTIR (Fourier Transform Infrared Spectrometer). MAHA PEMS  The AIP PEMS (Portable Emission Measurement System), is used for the determina-tion of the gaseous components CO, CO2, NOx (NO + NO2) and the particle number concentration PN of vehicle exhaust in real driving operations. The PEMS meets the requirements of Regulation (EU) 2017/1151 with amendments 2017/1154, 2017/1347 and Regulation (EU) 2018/1832. It is possible to calibrate the AIP PEMS PN according to ISO 17025.

 The analyzers are integrated in a housing with passive cooling and is self-sufficient supplied with Li-ion batteries.  The carrier is conveniently attached to the trailer hitch with a tension lever. All other systems and sensors (GPS sensor, weather station, OBD, sampling, PC connection) are connected via connectors.   
  Figure 10: AIP PEMS  The gaseous pollutants are detected by a non-dispersive infrared sensor (CO and CO2), chemiluminescence detector (NO) and photoacoustic sensor (NO2).  In particle number determination, particles with an electrical mobility diameter of 23 nm to about       2.5 μm are detected. The measuring system was developed on the basis of the PMP protocol, which is the basis of the current legislation for the determination of the particle number concentration at the exhaust gas roller test bench. The system consists of a first dilution stage (PND1), an evaporation tube (ET), a second dilution stage (PND2) and a condensation particle counter (CPC). The 50 % counting efficiency of the mobile CPC is 23 nm. Sample is taken through heated hoses directly from the exhaust pipe or via sampling probes from the Exhaust Flow Meter (EFM). When taken for gas analysis, the gas is cleaned with a heated filter and fed via a heated sample gas line to the gas cooler.

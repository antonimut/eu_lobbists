The Need for Flexibility: Our current electricity system is not 
able to properly utilize future renewable production 
• Increasing renewable integration with a focus on 
solar will result in the near-term in temporary 
mismatch between renewable generation 
and system load
• As shown in the graphs on the right, Germany 
will - based on its renewable targets - reach a 
68% renewable share by 2027. In the current 
system major shares of renewables could not be 
consumed at the time of generation
• Strengthening of European interconnections is 
only part of the solution, as European 
neighboring countries will see similar solar 
generation patterns.  
-> Integrating flexible technologies quickly that can 
shift renewable energy from time of production 
to time of usage will prevent large-scale curtailment 
of renewable power, reduce price volatility and 
strengthen security of supply

Solution: Utilizing flexible assets like energy storage to         
time-shift electricity creating wider socio-economic benefits 
• Periods with high renewable generation 
typically coincide with low prices, triggering 
energy storage systems to buy electricity from 
the market 
• Periods with low renewable generation 
typically coincide with high prices, triggering 
energy storage systems to sell electricity to 
the market  
• Buying/Selling in wholesale markets moves 
clearing prices for all energy transacted 
during the specific time period
• As demand curves are steeper during high 
price periods than during low price periods, 
energy shifting results in overall price-
reducing socio-economic benefits

The rational for improving the existing market design  
Why does the market not provide a solution? 
Risk of Political Intervention 
• 2022 has seen exceptional intervention in spot markets 
to limit volatility and scarcity pricing. As flexibility 
technologies require volatility in wholesale markets, the 
observed market intervention is contrary to required 
investment signals for flexibility technologies 
Lack of Technology Intimacy
• While proven in the field, many flexibility technologies 
are still novel to financial investors. They are therefore 
hesitant to underwrite a technology that is unknown to 
them but stay with technologies known to them already
© Fluence Energy LLC. All rights reserved. | 6
Lack of Long-term Contracts in current Market
• The value of energy-time shifting is today realized on 
Spot-Markets (DA/ID), without long-term market 
products for arbitrage  
• In OTC trade, traders are today not committing to 
forward hedges on volatility for time-periods required 
for project financing (around 10 years)  
Required Speed to Market 
• Energy markets are likely to provide volatility hedging 
products in the future, once there is a strong business 
case indicated by scarcity pricing and price volatility. 
This may take years to fully develop at scale blocking 
near-term investments in needed flex assets 
➢ Europe can not afford to wait for market mechanism to drive investments based on 
scarcity pricing, but needs to actively trigger investment into flexibility technologies 
as no-regret option (as done with grid or hydrogen)
➢ To incentivize such investments, we need to strengthen investment security via long-
term contracts (by adopting the traditional CfD concept and transferring it to flexibility assets)

• The double-sided Volatility CfD is a financial contract (swap) 
that provides a long-term (10-year) revenue guarantee/ 
hedge against price volatility on Day-Ahead markets  
• Establishing this long-term revenue floor will foster investment 
into flexibility assets by increasing project bankability
• The parties to the Volatility CfD are flexibility asset owners or 
operators and a central buyer (government entity) 
• Volatility CfDs are awarded in annual auctions, based on a 
CfD strike price*, ensuring the lowest cost assets are awarded
• The Volatility CfD is settled on a monthly basis: In exchange for 
the guaranteed strike price, flexibility assets return a benchmark 
revenue based on actual wholesale market volatility. Payments 
between the CfD parties can go either way
• Volatility CfDs are providing a firm revenue floor against DA 
volatility; projects still need to optimize against all available 
markets (energy, ancillary, capacity) to reach project profitability 
• Flex assets have no obligation to operate in DA markets. The 
CfD incentivizes them to operate in those market segments with 
highest potential revenue, as indication of a highest overall 
system benefit

CfD Auction mechanism 
• Volatility CfD are awarded via a voluntary auction scheme 
(comparable to today’s renewable or capacity auctions); 
flexibility assets can also operate outside of the Volatility CfD
• Annual Auction mechanism with t-2 delivery for new-build 
(10-year contracts) and t-1 delivery for existing flexibility 
assets (1-year contract) 
• Auction volume to be defined via political target (short term); 
in medium-term the National Regulatory Entities should 
define the auction volume based on calculation of socio-
economic benefit of additional flexibility assets  
• Auction mechanism as reverse auction with central clearing 
for CfD strike price (see next slide)
• Prior the auction a procurement volume and a maximum 
CFD strike price will be defined 
• Requirements for participating flexibility assets (energy 
storage and DR)
• Ability to charge energy during, or shift consumption to, 
time of high renewable production and release energy, or 
reduce residual load, during periods of high prices  
• Clear technical rules for energy storage, demand response 
and other participating technologies should be defined

CfD strike price 
• CfD auctions are cleared based on the CfD strike price, 
which defines the baseline volatility level against 
which payments are made under the CfD mechanism 
• The CfD strike price describes the spread on the Day-
Ahead spot market between the lowest and highest 
cleared hour for each day 
• The strike price is defined in €/MWh 
• The strike price is a virtual index that does not reflect 
the real technical capability or cost of participating 
assets, such as round-trip efficiency/energy losses or 
marginal cost of energy shifting (e.g. between different 
storage technologies or assets with different durations), 
perfect market foresight, etc. 
• Such technology specific restriction must be 
internalized into participants bids; this allows a 
technology agnostic CfD clearing approach

CfD settlement
• CfD settlement should be made on a monthly basis
• Settlement is a netting of the CfD strike price and the 
theoretically realized wholesale spread:
CfD payment = sum ((day 1 volatility spread (highest – lowest DA 
hourly clearing price) – CfD strike price) + (day 2 volatility 
spread – CfD strike price) + day 3 to day 28/30/31)    
• Payments may be made in either direction, 
depending on the observed volatility; hence the asset 
operator may receive payments or may have to make 
payments to the government
• Payments are paid on a netted basis following the 
monthly settlement. No payments are made in either 
direction during the respective month
• Payments are only made for days in which the asset 
declared availability for DA trading to system operators, 
irrespectively from its participation in DA trading or 
deployment in other markets (ID, AS, CM)

CfD operational requirements/penalties 
OPERATIONAL REQUIREMENTS 
• The CfD is a financial instrument and therefore asset 
owner are under no obligation to provide a specific 
service, such as trading on spot markets  
• As the flexibility asset can be operated across different 
markets, asset owners are free to optimize the asset 
to archive the highest possible revenues
• An optimization of flexibility assets based on market 
prices, which reflect scarcity and therefore system 
needs, does ensure the most efficient deployment of 
the asset in the energy system
• It further prevents lock-in of an asset to operate in a 
specific market segment (e.g. Day-Ahead market), but 
allows the asset to optimize against all current and 
future market products
• The only operational requirement is that flexibility 
assets must be available, for time periods in which 
they receive CfD payments 
PENALTY REGIMES 
• Penalty regimes are often used in energy market 
services to ensure assets are fulfilling the service as 
contracted
• As flexibility assets under the CfD are under no 
obligation to provide a specific service, there is not 
penalty for non provision of such service
• As payments under the Volatility CfD are only a part of a 
flexibility asset’s revenue stack and are not sufficient to 
reach project hurdle rates, asset owners have an 
economical incentive to operate and optimize their 
assets 
• Assets will have to declare their daily availability for 
dispatch in Day-Ahead markets to System Operators, 
irrespectively of their actual operation on DA markets.  
• During periods, for which assets have not declared 
availability for dispatch no CfD payments will be 
made.

Summary of General principles I
14© Fluence Energy LLC. All rights reserved. | 
Target of mechanism Provide long-term revenue guarantee for project finance (same principle as for 
CfDs or feed-in tariffs for renewable assets); purpose of mechanism to 
incentivize low-emission flexibility assets
Definition of market need Procurement volume to be defined via political target; can be delegated to 
National Regulatory Entity which defines volume based on calculation of socio-
economic benefit of additional flexibility assets  
Auction format Procurement via reverse auction with central clearing price (e.g. UK CM 
auction) 
Contract length 10-year term (new-build); 1-year contracts for existing assets  
(Note: does not cover project life-time but will provide sufficient guarantee to 
enable investment; focus on incentivizing investments into new assets)
Contract counterparts 1) Asset Owners – may be transferred to Aggregators, RtM-Providers
2) Central buyer entity – Can be same entity as for CfDs, CMs, feed-in tariffs 
Financing of Mechanism Mechanism expected to be revenue neutral; alternatively cost or profit of 
mechanism to be paid from/contributing to grid fees. 
(Note: even if mechanism produces cost, these are out weight by socio-economic 
benefits of lowering peak prices in market)
Summary of General principles II
Definition of Mechanism Financial derivate, accessible for parties with physical collateral (flexibility 
assets); measured in €/MWh
Underlying revenues Indirect: No revenues are being targeted directly; CfD focuses on Day-
Ahead spot-market volatility, which is the baseline for revenues of 
flexibility assets
CfD contribution to asset 
investment case 
Volatility CfD does not cover project hurdle rate; provides bankable long-
term revenue guarantee, while project profitability depends on asset 
performance in energy markets 
How to prevent gaming of 
system/ 
Penalty scheme 
No penalties; as CfD payments do not cover project hurdle rates, 
optimization of assets are in interest of project owners; 
No Payments under CfD are made if asset is not available for dispatch 
Technologies covered Focus on energy storage and demand response. Requirements include:
• Ability to charge energy during, or shift consumption to, time of high 
renewable production and release energy, or reduce residual load, during 
periods of high prices  
• Clear technical rules for energy storage, demand response and other 
participating technologies should be defined

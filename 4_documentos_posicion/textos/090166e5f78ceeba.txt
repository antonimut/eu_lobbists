JAMA's feedback response to the European Commission on the Euro 7 proposal 
The Japan Automobile Manufacturers Association, Inc. (JAMA) is a nonprofit industry 
association comprising Japan's fourteen manufacturers of passenger cars, trucks, buses, and 
motorcycles. I take this opportunity to express our profound gratitude to the European Union and 
its Member States for the support they consistently extend to our member companies' business 
activities in Europe, including the production, sales, and export of automobiles 
With climate change and other environmental issues being matters of global urgency and with 
Japan having declared in 2020 its intention to achieve climate neutrality by 2050, JAMA and its 
members are committed to addressing the challenges involved in order to help make that 
objective a reality 
Released by the European Commission on 10 November 2022, the Euro 7 proposal contains 
provisions and requirements which, if adopted, would contribute in only a limited way to improved 
air quality in Europe in the mid term (and even less so in the longer term with respect to WHO 
guideline values) and compliance with new air quality standards proposed on 26 October 2022 
We appreciate being given the opportunity to submit to the Commission our comments on the 
proposal's contents, which we present below in summary form 
■ Provision of lead time 
JAMA requests 1) that the timeline leading up to the implementation (ie, adoption into law) of 
Euro 7 be extended, taking into account that test protocols must first be established; and 2) that 
a development lead time of more than three years be granted by the Commission from the date 
of Euro 7's implementation to its enforcement to enable vehicle manufacturers to ensure 
compliance with type-approval certification requirements 
■Emission limits & testing requirements 
JAMA cannot formulate a comment at this time, as test methods and test condition requirements 
are not clear or are non-existent (ie, without boundaries). We request that measures be taken to 
ensure that all necessary technical discussions are conducted on test methods and test 
conditions. 
■Management of test method discussions in AGVES 
JAMA requests that discussion topics be determined and announced in advance and that the 
discussions take into account in a comprehensively inclusive way the views and opinions of all 
the stakeholders concerned.

For climate change countermeasures, achieving carbon neutrality by 2050 is a critical goal and 
we recognise that further technological development and a range of approaches tailored to 
regional characteristics are necessary to achieve this goal. In the road transport sector 
specifically, achieving carbon neutrality will necessitate the implementation of policies to 
maintain technology neutrality; the provision of various types of support (eg, the introduction of 
incentives and general infrastructure development) to ensure the availability of economically and 
environmentally sustainable mobility for all citizens; and the establishment of a 
recharging/refuelling infrastructure throughout the EU, together with the assurance of a stable 
supply of affordable carbon-neutral electricity. Furthermore, measures will also need to be taken 
to protect employment, enable workers' re-education and the upgrading of skills, facilitate the 
transition to carbon neutrality, and secure a virtuous cycle of environmental and economic 
responsibility 
On the other hand, in view of its very broad scope the Euro 7 proposal on controlling air pollutants 
emitted by automobiles would require very significant monetary investment by manufacturers in 
internal combustion engine improvements, which could adversely impact progress towards the 
goal of achieving carbon neutrality by 2050. JAMA experts have closely examined the Euro 7 
proposal and respectfully submit their detailed comments to the Commission in the documents 
attached herewith. Optimising the feasibility of the proposal is a shared concern, and we hope 
that the breadth and depth of our comments will be taken into consideration 
Allow me to convey our thanks to the Commission in anticipation thereof

2. Heavy-Duty Vehicles 
 
2-1 General comments 
 
2-1-1 Entry into force and application 
The application date for HDVs is July 1, 2027. However, the Euro 7 proposal is introducing major 
changes, and a development lead time of more than 3 years is required to implement such 
changes. To ensure the feasibility of this lead time, a detailed test method for Euro 7 must be 
finalized by the beginning of 2024. In addition, since LDVs and HDVs have been subsumed into 
a single regulation in Euro 7, the detailed test method should be applicable at one and the same 
time for LDVs and HDVs. 
 
2-1-2 Limit values 
Detailed test methods (including test conditions) for E uro 7 have not been determined and the 
details of the revision of HDV-CO2 regulatory targets are not yet clear either, as the Commission 
has yet to adopt a legislative proposal. If the HDV-CO2 regulatory targets are too stringent, it will 
be very difficult for HDV manufacturers to comply with the Euro 7 regulation. 
 
2-1-3 Lead time 
Euro 7 requires the implementation of both vehicle -based and bench -based test methods, as 
well as the determination of emissions measurement accuracy for the newly added exhaust 
pollutants. Lifetime requirements have also been extended. Therefore, a minimum development 
lead time of more than 3 years is required. 
 
2-1-4 Alignment with other legislation 
A review of the HDV-CO2 regulatory targets is scheduled to be published on February 15 and 
the results of this review could adversely impact the achievability of compliance with the Euro 7 
regulation. It is therefore necessary that these two legislative proposals be consistent with one 
another and that a balance be struck between the Euro 7 regulatory values and the HDV-CO2 
regulatory target values. 
 
2-1-5 Management of test method discussions in AGVES 
The AGVES meetings are critically important for deci sions going forward on the details of test 
methods. JAMA would therefore like to lend its support to the AGVES meetings and provide data 
as needed. For  this purpose, we would like to be apprised of the agenda discussion items in 
advance and be granted sufficient time and opportunity to contribute to the technical discussions.

6. Tyre Abrasion 
 
6-1 Application date 
Because appropriate test procedures and specific regulatory requirements are still not decided, 
the European Commission’s proposal (LDVs from July 2025) is unrealistic. A lead time of more 
than 2 years is required after technical prospecting for tyres based on final decisions on test 
procedures and regulatory requirements (calibration for vehicle application, securing a specific 
period for tyre certification). 
 
JAMA Timeline No. 3 
 
 
6-2 Technical issues 
 
◼ Test procedures 
The inclusion of bench test s as a measurement procedure is requested , in view of the 
uncertainty of real -world testing and its impact on tyre development schedules owing  to 
lengthy test periods. 
◼ Tyre abrasion limits 
There are conflicts with other regulatory limits, e.g. on tire noise emission and on wet grip 
performance; a balance among all tyre regulatory requirements should be achieved. 
◼ Bench test facilities 
Large investments in test facilities are required of tyre manufacturers, especially for C3 tyres.

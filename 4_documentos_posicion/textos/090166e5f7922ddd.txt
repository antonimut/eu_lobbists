Revision of Urban Wastewater Treatment Directive  (UWWTD) – 
wastewater-based epidemiology to ensure systematic surveillance of 
pathogens 
Bio-Rad position on the UWWTD proposal 
Bio-Rad welcomes the Commission  proposal to review the Urban Wastewater Treatment 
Directive (UWWTD. It presents an opportunity to ensure EU -wide systematic surveillance of 
emerging pathogens in wastewater  and create a foundation for  more rapid public health 
responses.  
Bio-Rad is a heal thcare technology company, a market leader in PCR testing systems, 
committed to developing high-quality products and services that improve biomedical research 
and healthcare in the EU. We are a US company established in 1952 with 15 offices in Europe 
and serving customers in all EU Member States. 
The Covid-19 pandemic has shown that pandemic preparedness is key for the EU to better 
tackle any future health emergency.  It has underlined the importance of using all available 
means to detect variants (including wastewater surveillance) as soon as possible to provide 
appropriate and timely public health responses.1  
EU countries need a sensitive and reliable method for surveying the spread of pathogens in 
their communities to inform public health deci sions. Testing wastewater is a proven and 
effective method to test hundreds to thousands of individuals in one test as opposed to mass 
population screening of individuals.  
By testing wastewater with droplet digital PCR (ddPCR) technology, one can detect Covid-19 
as early as 6 days prior to clinical test confirmation and authorities can act accordingly to 
prevent further community spread. Furthermore, ddPCR technology can test for existing  
pathogens (e.g. Covid-19, mpox, Polio), be quickly adapted to detect emerging pathogens, and 
provide for highly accurate, precise, and consistent results across multiple EU laboratories .2 
This makes ddPCR data easily comparable across EU countries, and it can provide an early 
warning detection system against other potentially serious pathogens and future pandemics.   
This is why Bio-Rad supports the following policy measures: 
1. Ensure a solid EU framework for wastewater surveillance making use of best practices 
and innovation, including droplet digital PCR solutions  
 
2. Consistent and systematic monitoring of pathogens in wastewater to trigger early 
public health response 
 
3. Ensure harmonised testing methodologies across EU Member States for comparable 
data resulting in better coordination in future pandemics

While the Commission UWWTD proposal addresses several of our concerns, Bio-Rad believes 
that EU’s pandemic preparedness can be further improved through the Wastewater Treatment 
Directive by:  
 
1. Including a deadline for Member States implementation of national systems for 
wastewater surveillance by 2025  
 
2. Reporting wastewater monitoring results to national health authorities without delay 
 
3. Ensuring weekly surveillance of pathogens which constitutes a communicable disease 
threat according to the ECDC or a public health emergency or international concern by 
the WHO  
 
4. Establishing harmonised EU approaches to testing of wastewater through guidelines 
 
Bio-Rad is looking forward to cooperating  with EU and national stakeholders responsible for 
wastewater surveillance to ensure that the UWWTD improves EU’s pandemic preparedness.

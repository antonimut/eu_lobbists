EC Consultation on  the Electricity Market Design - Enel’s 
Response  
The sudden increase of day- ahead electricity prices due to the war in Ukraine and its 
effect on the gas market has led policymakers and stakeholders to start debating on 
the need to reform the electricity market design.  
This is an opportunity for introducing changes to the electricity market design that will 
accelerate the decarbonization, while delivering direct benefits to European 
consumers and the decoupling of the electricity market from fossil fuels.. 
The c market design has delivered increased integration and security of supply , but 
we now need to move towards a market design based on customers’ needs. 
While spot price formation in short -term markets based on merit order and marginal 
pricing works well in ensuring  efficient dispatch of generation and flexibility and 
should not be modified, several improvements can be put in place to the current 
market design, with the aim to provide necessary long -term investment signals that 
will protect consumers against price volatility.  
The following ones are the most relevant improvements. 
Long-term Renewable Target on Suppliers 
The introduction at European level of a long -term renewable target on all suppliers. 
Suppliers will reach the target with an array of long -term market instruments such as: 
PPAs, forward markets, Contracts for Differe nce, self and own generation. The 
introduction of a target stimulate liquidity in long-term markets, hedge final customers 
(especially residential and SMEs, which do not have direct access to long -term 
products) and accelerate the decarbonization of the European economy. 
Power Purchase Agreements  
The PPA market could be further developed, in particular for contracts lasting more 
than 3 years. Recommended measures that could promote the expansion of PPAs 
include:  
• Standardization and reduction of guarantee’s costs 
• Simplification and acceleration of RES permitting procedures  
• A harmonized EU -wide GO system and carbon accounting standards  for 
scope 2 emissions  
• State Aid  provisions for support schemes that combine PPAs with tax 
incentives or investment support  
• Carve out of PPAs from the classification of financial instruments and allow 
under EMIR PPAs to be part of the commercial activity or the industrial entity  
Contracts for Difference  
Two-way CfDs represent an additional long-term instrument for new investments 
that can be deployed by Member States alongside PPAs and forward markets . The 
participation should be voluntary and the economic conditions shall be defined b y  
competitive mechanisms i.e. Member States shall avoid any form of administrative / 
regulated strike prices . Their design shall not distort energy and ancillary service

Lastly, Member States shall not be able to impose two-way CfDs by regulatory 
means on existing generation capacity . Existing capacity should be left free to 
enter PPAs with traders, suppliers, and final customers. Two -way CfDs must be 
considered an instrument to provide a long -term investment framework, not a 
mechanism to temporarily stabilize prices. 
PPAs and two -way CfDs are instruments that must work together and be at the 
center of the future market design. In our view they are perfectly suitable – to 
different extents and with different declinations - to provide hedging instruments to 
generators and final customer s and deliver the decoupling of the electricity sectors 
from fossil fuels.  Therefore, we firmly believe that distortive emergency measures 
like the revenue limitation of inframarginal technologies should not be a 
permanent future of the market design.

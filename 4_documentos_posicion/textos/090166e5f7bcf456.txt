Comments from the EPS industry on the proposal of the Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation  
 
EUMEPS, the association of European  Manufacturers of Expanded Polystyrene fully supports the 
objectives of the new proposal Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation to prevent and reduce the 
adverse impacts of packaging on the environment and health. Ensuring the continued sustainable and 
responsible use of Expanded Polystyrene (EPS) is part of our mission. The environmental advantages 
of EPS over potential alternatives have been demonstrated in multiple comparative  studies, such as 
life-cycle analyses (LCAs). Over the years, the EPS industry has been actively engag ed in sharing best 
practices and offering environmentally and economically successful solutions. EPS contributes to 
many economic, social, and environmental benefits and enables a more sustainable, circular economy 
in Europe, as it is a lightweight, highly versatile material widely used for the protection of people and 
sensitive goods. EPS is composed of 98% air, which automatically reduces the amount of resources 
needed to produce the material , and helps to reduce the carbon footprint of EPS products. In most 
Member States, recycling rates of EPS packaging already exceed 50%  and the EPS value chain has 
voluntarily committed to further improve the recycling rates and recyclability.  EPS post -consumer 
packaging is in fact recycled in practice and at scale in Europe in accordance with the criteria defined 
by the Ellen MacArthur Foundation and has been so since before 2017. 
 
In order to further increase EPS recycling rates, EUMEPS has collaborated with RecyClass, and we have 
developed Design for Recycling guidelines  for EPS packaging applications. Most EPS and fish box 
packaging currently in use already comply with class A (full compatibility). 
 
While welcoming the overarching goal o f the Regulation, we are concerned that some parts of the 
draft Regulation might lead to undesired adverse effects, ultimately undermining the environmental 
objectives of the Regulation. Therefore, please see below our general comments, as well as specific  
remarks, which will hopefully be considered in further refinement of the proposed regulation. 
 
General remarks 
EUMEPS is welcoming the principles of the waste hierarchy. However, our view is that the hierarchy 
is being interpreted in a too strict manner, which leads to environmentally unsound solutions and as 
well as to increased CO2 emissions or other environmenta lly problematic issues. EUMEPS therefore, 
recommends that the waste hierarchy can be redeveloped to better allow for a waste matrix, making 
informed environmental decisions easier based on several criteria incl : transport distance, 
recyclability, climate i mpact, etc. rather than only making decisions based on reuse vs. recycling. 
EUMEPS fully recognises that the intention of the waste hierarchy does not have to be strictly 
interpreted, however we see on the market that it is, which may lead to sub-environmental decisions.  
 
• Recycled content requirements - the proposal should define targets in a more general manner 
and establish an appropriate mechanism to achieve them, such as through fee setting 
mechanisms or EPRs, rather than defining hard requirements for individual products. 
• Increased requirements for the reuse should be achievable and avoid the risk of encouraging 
the complex and long-distance return of empty packaging, which would be inconsistent with 
the overall goal of reducing CO2 emissions (Article 26). 
• A holistic approach is needed when assessing and comparing the environmental impacts and 
health-related effects of single use and reusable packaging alternatives . In particular, the 
related CO2 emissions (for collection, cleaning, transport, etc.) should be evaluated on a case-
by-case basis, because general preference might lead to more harmful alternatives.

• The Regulation should hono ur the principle of “material neutrality” , allowing the choice of 
the appropriate packaging materials based on their technical effectiveness and  suitability 
(e.g., to protect the goods) while respecting their environmental profile and ensuring a fit with 
a circular economy. 
• The Regulation should hono ur the principle of "technology neutrality,"  also allowing 
innovative and advanced recycling techno logies, including chemical recycling as a necessary 
complement to mechanical recycling, if it is to meet ambitious goals for a circular economy. 
The diversity of advanced recycling solutions is required to handle the complexity of plastic 
waste, comprise d of  different types of plastics with diverse polymer chemistr y. The Mass 
Balance Methodology will play a key role here, in the context of chemical recycling, which is 
complementary to mechanical recycling , and is a prerequisite for achieving high recycling 
rates of plastic packaging generally. 
 
Specific remarks 
 
Re-use and refill targets  - Article 26 : the economic operator making large household appliances 
(WEEE Directive) available on the market for the first time within the territory of a Member State shall 
ensure that 90% of these goods are made available in reusable transport packaging w ithin a system 
for re-use is considered by us as a very ambitious target which is almost certainly unrealistic. 
 
The requirements for reuse set out in Article 26 of protective packaging for products with a longer 
lifespan (e.g. 5+ years) will lead to increased material use, increasing the climate impact of production 
and transport. The proposal risks harming packaging producers, their suppliers, as well as OEMs and 
other end consumers in industry, logistics, and retail. Furthermore, EPS packaging is used fo r several 
different products. Many electronic products have the packaging moulded to reduce the packaging 
needs;- designing reuse packaging would require more material usage as packaging can no longer be 
“tailored” to the specific application. This will in crease resource use and require heavier packaging, 
leading to a negative climate impact. The current proposal  would not be sustainable for many 
companies, whether packaging manufacturers or users, especially SMEs, a nd would likely result in 
bankruptcies, leading to increased unemployment within member countries. These challenges are not 
specific to EPS packaging but more general, which is why EUMEPS recommends the redevelopment of 
the waste hierarchy in to for instance a waste matrix. 
 
Additionally, for food packaging, the requirements within Article 26 could cause supply and safety 
risks (hygiene concerns), reduced storage life of the food materials, as well as increased food waste. 
If any, only aspirational targets should be set, and it would be very welcome if the Commission would 
define requirements at a more granular level through the delegated acts. 
 
EPS packaging is used in applications where there is constant contact with food and sensitive items  
(fish boxes, vaccines boxes, human organ boxes), because EPS packaging is guaranteed to provide the 
necessary hygiene requirement and the best insulating conditions. EPS packaging truly is reusable but 
applying such a high percentage as given in the propo sal will surely result in highly unhygienic and 
unsafe food contact materials for the end consumers. We need to avoid such situations.  
 
Reuse targets need to be realistic and specific to the packed goods that they are being applied to. In 
the case of EPS this is highly complicated considering the sensitiv ity of contact with food and health 
materials. COVID 19 has shown us how important human hygiene and food safety is and how critical 
it is to maintain certain standards in order to prevent the spread of diseases , thereby maintaining a 
healthy environment for end consumers. Refill and reuse should aim to deliver a better environmental 
outcome than single use alternatives, but they need to be more easily adaptable in real life conditions. 
In addition, refill and reuse should be evaluated according to specific criteria that will enable current 
   
 
   
 
safety, hygiene, and food health requirements to be met. Environmental protection is a common goal 
that we are following together, therefore it is important to make it workable in practice.  
 
Article 7 Minimum recycled content in plastic packaging the mandatory recycled content required in 
2030 and 2040 seem,  (especially when referring to health and safety concerns in contact sensitive 
applications), reachable only with the use of chemical recycling technologies as an enabling condition. 
These should be recognized in the regulation as effective in achieving circularity. 
 
The EPS industry is committed to contributing to the development of a circular economy . We are 
investing in various projects in order to accelerate further increase of recycling rates. Consisting of 
one single polymer only, EPS is fully recyclable , reusable and already recycled at scale in many EU 
Member States. It is a unique product with unrivalled properties for many applications.  
 
We remain at your disposal to discuss further. 
 
 
 
 
 
About us: 
 
EUMEPS is the association and voice of European Manufacturers of Expanded Polystyrene. Our 
members cover the entire EPS value chain from raw material suppliers to EPS converters and recyclers 
as well as supporting industries in cluding machinery and additive suppliers. Members include 
individual companies as well as 23 European national EPS associations. This unique representation of 
the entire value chain ensures that EUMEPS represents both large companies and small- and medium-
sized converters and recyclers. Altogether our membership represents more than 1,000 companies, 
most of them small - and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs), and employs more than 80,000 people.

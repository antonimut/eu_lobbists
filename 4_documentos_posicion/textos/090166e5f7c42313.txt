About CIP 
Founded in 2012, Copenhagen Infrastructure Partners P/S (CIP) today is the world’s largest dedicated 
fund manager within greenfield renewable energy investments and a global leader in offshore wind. The 
funds managed by CIP focus on investments in offshore and onshore wind, solar PV, biomass and 
energy-from-waste, transmission and distribution, reserve capacity, storage, advanced bioenergy, and 
Power-to-X. 
 
CIP manages ten funds and has to date raised approximately EUR 20 billion for investments in energy 
and associated infrastructure from more than 140 international institutional investors. CIP has approxi-
mately 400 employees and 11 offices around the world. 
 
General remarks 
CIP takes note of the European Commission’s consultation as per January 23, 2023, on reforming Eu-
ropean Electricity Market Design  (EMD) to support a clean and affordable energy transition.  CIP is 
grateful to the European Commission for a further opportunity to comment on its evolving thinking re-
garding EMD Reforms. 
 
CIP agrees that the current system has delivered an efficient, well -integrated market for many years, 
allowing the EU to reap the economic benefits of a single energy market, ensuring security of supply 
and spurring the decarbonisation process. We acknowledge the intent of the proposed legislation aimed 
at preserving and enhancing incentives for investments and provide investors with certainty and 
predictability, while addressing the economic and social concerns related to high energy prices. There 
is an urgent need to both focus on direct & indirect electrification of the energy system and not pe-
nalizing any energy vector necessary to advance the energy transition. 
 
In general, we do not agree that the current  regulatory framework on long -term instruments has 
proven insufficient to protect consumers from volatility and higher energy bills. The recent energy 
challenges in Europe stems from high demand amidst insufficient supply due to dependence on Russian 
gas and lack of cheap renewable energy sources (RES). The energy supply and price crises does not 
stem from structural failures in the market design as also concluded in ACER’s “Final Assessment of 
the EU Wholesale Electricity Market Design” from April 29, 2022. 
 
Thus, CIP warns against an overly hurried reform of the European EMD not based on thorough 
analyses and impact assessment, and which alter the fundamentals of the market design. Prolonging 
the emergency inframarginal revenue cap would heavily disincentivize build-out of much needed 
RES to stand against the revolving energy crisis, climate crisis, economic crisis at not least balance out 
any supply chocks resulting in volatile prices for consumers, not to mention geopolitical circumstances 
and favorable investment incentives in other parts of the World, including the US.

Power Purchasing Agreements  
CIP supports strengthening the market for Power Purchasing Agreements (PPAs). The accessibil-
ity of PPAs for smaller consumers could be increased through a market conform manner rather than 
considering mandatory mechanisms (e.g., exploring ways to pool demand - pooling smaller consumers 
into one contract would mean PPA contracts could be offered to SMEs). 
 
Currently, developers are incentivized to offer PPAs as they provide a stable stream of revenue. 
Accordingly, there is no need for public obligations requiring specific amounts of produced electricity 
being traded though PPAs. Such public requirements could be distorting to the market and result in 
developers requiring higher payments for their offered PPAs, ultimately having adverse effects on soar-
ing energy prices for consumers and businesses. 
 
CIP agrees that there is a need to investigate how transparency in the market for PPAs could be 
heightened. Accordingly, we support efforts to establish and strengthen virtual trading hubs for forward 
contracts such as PPAs.  
 
Contracts-for-difference  
We acknowledge that Contracts-for-difference (CfDs) can be useful when the necessary investments 
are not made on a market basis . CfDs can provide certainty for investors as they guarantee a fixed 
price independent of the development on spot markets. Further, two-way CfDs guarantee investors a 
fixed price while any revenue above a set strike price is returned to the state . However, two-way 
CfDs are associated with several pitfalls which should be considered before being applied: 
 
• Two-way CfDs decrease the incentive to invest in new RES if the strike price is set low, lead-
ing to higher electricity prices in the medium to long term. 
• Two-way CfDs mute price signal’s influence on investors’ incentive to build capacity that maxim-
ize production when the value of electricity is highest, as producers do not receive higher reve-
nue when the market price goes up, decreasing efficiency for short-term dispatch. 
 
Thus, CIP strongly warns against mandatory CfD s where producers are forced to enter contracts 
that oblige them to receive a fixed strike price for their electricity. Such mandatory schemes will de facto 
remove the aligning force of the price signal and be a huge setback for the liberalized European elec-
tricity market. 
 
Further, we firmly warn against any considerations to force already existing inframarginal gener-
ators on to mandatory CfDs (option 3 & 4 on page 4 of the consultation document). This would have 
an expropriative character and would violate basic property rights , fundamental to a free-market 
economy and the internal market an jeopardize profitability of already committed investments.

CIP believes that any use of CfDs should always be based on voluntarily basis (support for option

Finally, the strike prices set in CfDs should aim to mimic competitive market prices and be set in 
competitive auctions (or based on real spot prices, not historical prices) – not through ex-post price 
setting to avoid introducing new risks for producers. 
 
Power-to-X 
When renewable electricity generation and electrolysers are co-located before the grid, the electro-
lyser can act as a buffer between the generation and the grid and limit the needed grid connec-
tion capacity and hence also provide the TSO security for a limited need for grid capacity build-out.   
 
Further, Power-to-X (PtX) and electrolysers will provide demand when prices are very low, lifting 
the lower part of the price duration curve and enabling new investments in RES by making such 
investments more attractive. Power procured for PtX can be made available to the market when prices 
are high and hence that demand from PtX reduces volatility in power markets (flattens the price dura-
tion curve). 
 
CIP believes that PtX coupled with a supportive regime for renewables (permitting) will achieve 
price stability much more effectively than any form of intervention and we urge that the EU Commis-
sion considers a “price sensitive load” with ample opportunity to construct new RES.

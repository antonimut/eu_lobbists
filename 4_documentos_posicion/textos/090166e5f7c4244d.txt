 
 
Public Consultation: Revision of the EU’s electricity market design 
 
DigitalES, the Spanish association for digitalization, welcome the opportunity to provide feedback on the 
policy objectives to be pursued by the reform proposal and possible concrete legislative and non -legislative 
measures resulting from them. 
 
Therefore, we sum up hereby some considerations that we would like to outline, taking into consideration 
the importance of the foreseen reform and the direct effects of the possible measures to the digital sector 
within Europe. 
 
Preferred outcomes 
 
1. Acknowledgement of the relevance of energy costs in the service sector  
 
• The service sector is not explicitly considered within the consultation document . While 
industry and domestic consumers have ad -hoc measures to reduce energy prices, the 
service companies do not, although the impact of electricity costs  are also impacting our 
margins and our investment capacity.  
 
• Therefore, the EC and MSs should consider the telco/service sector when applying 
measures to mitigate the energy crisis, in a similar way to the industry sector. 
 
2. Price affordability & lower market price volatility 
 
• Mostly driven by high gas prices, wholesale and retail prices for electricity have increased. 
This has had a significant impact on our ability to sustain infrastructure investment.  
Companies as the ones we represent, need predictability to build strategies and business 
models. 
 
• Our members strongly favour an  increased reliance on long term PPAs that reduce price 
volatility and ensure that we can comply with our commitments regarding renewable 
energy sourcing. 
 
 
3. Regulatory stability.  
 
• DigitalES welcomes measures to increase the share of PPAs and the liquidity of the forward 
markets. But any reform should not negatively affect the existing contracts between end 
users and electricity suppliers which would create additional uncertainty.  
 
• During these last two years, many different ad hoc interventions across multiple countries 
reduced predictability and certainty. Harmonisation is important for stability. 
 
• Finally, the EC should consider flexibility regarding the long -term contracts as nec essary, 
avoiding compulsory measures and any retroactive implementation.

Quick wins: 
In terms of our tangible policy asks, the below should be considered feasible and  
appropriate for this reform package: 
• Taxes and costs 
o As emergency measures for all consumers, DigitalES supports taxes and regulatory cost reductions , at 
least for the duration of the energy price crisis for all consumers. Additionally, welcomes specific 
measures, such as cuts to levies or energy-related taxes, for the service sector. 
 
• Decouple gas and electricity prices:  
 
• Our members consider positively ‘the Iberian Mechanism’, through which Spain it 
has mitigated around 4,500 M€ since its implementation. Despite the softening of 
the impact of the crisis on retail energy prices, the country reduced its natural gas 
demand by 23% since last August, with the key advantage  that there are no extra 
systems to be in place for any redistribution, only a compensation scheme to 
complement the short-term market.  
 
• This allowed our members to reduce their own energy costs, to the extent that 
they are linked to the wholesale energy market. In terms of the possible negative 
impact of the price intervention on the incentives to speed up the introduction of 
renewables, we believe these temporary measures should not affect the trend. 
Other measures foreseen in the reform, such as the promotion of PPAs, are in our 
view much more relevant for the financial sustainability of renewable energy 
projects than temporary interventions in the wholesale spot markets. 
 
• Along the same lines, the EC proposed a specific cap on revenues for inframarginal 
technologies. We believe setting this cap should be assessed at the national level 
and could be more ambitious, not only as a collection tool but also as an efficient 
price signal. This solution could however require more governmental interventions 
and a permanent system for tax collections and redistribution, negatively 
impacting the efficiency of the measure.  
 
Promotion of PPA  
 
• The highly tailored nature of PPAs is one obstacle to their rapid expansion, 
particularly for smaller businesses. A more standard form of contract by industry 
could provide the flexibility required as well as accelerate the approval process.   
 
• The proposals suggested in Q3 of the first section of the questionnaire are the 
ones that would have the most relevant direct long-term impact on our members 
and we strongly encourage the EC and Member States to pursue them

Who we are: 
Gas Networks Ireland is the Transmission System Operator and Distribution System Operator in 
Ireland. We operate and maintain Ireland’s €2.7 billion, 14,664km national gas network, supplying 
over 720,000 Irish homes and businesses. Gas Networks Ireland has developed a gas network that 
plays a vital role in Ireland’s energy system; supporting a diversified and  growing economy and 
providing vital back-up to renewable electricity.  
 
Electricity Market Reform: 
We welcome this important consultation to address short term challenges facing the electricity sector 
across the EU and fully support the ambition to make the electricity sector more resilient, reduce costs 
to the consumer and support the energy transition.  
 
In summary, w e believe that any reforms of the EU electricity market must take account of the 
following: 
• The role gas networks play in providing flexibility and stability (both in the short, medium and 
long-term) to today’s electricity system; 
• The similar role renewable gases and a decarbonised gas network can play in the future clean 
energy system; 
• The need for sector coupling  and energy system integration  to deliver an  optimal 
decarbonised, secure and resilient, least cost future energy system; 
• Existing and new gas -fired generation must be incentivised to provide vital back -up 
generation in a decarbonising electricity system which is highly dependent on intermittent 
renewable electricity generation. 
 
The partnership between flexible gas-fired power generation and intermittent renewable generation 
will be key to optimising the EU and Ireland’s renewable energy production. The gas network’s role in 
providing flexibility and stability to the electricity grid is likely to increase in importance, as the grid 
adapts to meet increasing electricity demand , with flexible gas -fired generation stepping in when 
supplies from renewable electricity fall due to lower wind/solar conditions. The main difference being 
that in the future , the gas network will transport renewable gases , such as green hydrogen and 
biomethane. 
 
Gas networks are central to Ireland’s and the EU’s energy mix and are key enablers to achieving energy 
security and climate action targets. Across the EU, gas-fired generation accounts for around 20% of all 
electricity generation, however in Ireland the figure is closer to 50%. Decarbonising and repurposing 
gas networks will complement the development of renewable electricity, reduce emissions across all 
sectors, and further enhance the security and resilience of Ireland’s and the EU’s electricity and energy 
supplies. 
 
In addition to the short term challenges t his reform is seeking to address, we agree with the 
Commission’s view that the European electricity sector is facing several more long-term challenges. 
These have been triggered by the rising shares of variable renewable energy and the progressive drive 
towards full decarbonisation by 2050 . These challenges will require ongoing policy reflection going 
beyond the scope of the current reform. We also support the Commission’s standpoint that future 
investments into weather independent low -carbon technologies will be required until large scale 
storage and other flexibility tools become available.

Gas Networks Ireland fully agrees with the Commission that a longer-term strategy is required to 
achieve the EU’s ultimate decarbonisation and energy security goals. We consider energy system 
integration to be crucial to delivering on these ambitions and that a holistic approach to energy policy 
development is required.  
 
Along with a decarbonised electricity system, the decarbonised gas network will continue to play a 
central role in Ireland’s and Europe’s future clean and secure energy system, by transporting 
renewable gases. The gas network can and will continue to provide the flexibility and backup to the 
electricity sector, as the production of renewable gases is scaled-up. Gas storage, including hydrogen 
storage, will be central to achieving this.  
 
To enable the deployment of increased shares of renewable electricity, it is critical that investment in 
both existing and new gas infrastructure, including gas storage (for future use as hydrogen storage), 
is maintained, as we transition to a net -zero energy system. Such investments must be compatible 
with the EU’s climate ambitions and obligations, in addition to supporting the transport and storage 
of renewable gases in the future, when they are available at scale. These investments must provide a 
pathway towards meeting the EU’s 2030 carbon -reduction commitments and, ultimately, carbon 
neutrality by 2050.  
 
Given the critical role that gas-fired electricity generation plays within the EU and national energy 
sectors, it is critical from both climate and energy security perspectives that any electricity market 
reforms introduced do not jeopardise the flexibility and availability of existing or future gas -fired 
generation, as the sector transitions away from natural gas to green hydrogen and biomethane.  
 
Moreover, it is important to consider both the operational and financial implications of incentivising 
demand reduction and energy shifting at peak times in the short -term. Gas-fired power generation 
currently provides robust and reliable balancing actions . In introducing a peak shaving product or 
similar ancillary service , regard needs to be had to the long -term gas and electricity m arket 
implications, such as ensuring new generation capacity connects to the electricity network when 
needed. 
 
Capacity remuneration mechanisms, demand response measures and ancillary services supports are 
key drivers of new entrants entering the electricity market, thereby promoting the development of 
(electricity and gas) sector coupling. With the increased penetration of intermittent, non-dispatchable 
renewable technologies, it is important that electrolysers and flexible electricity generation, in the 
form of gas-fired power generation, are appropriately incentivised to make the investment decision 
to enter the electricity market, particularly considering recent security of supply concerns. Without 
appropriate market signals for potential new entrants, there is the risk of undermining the efficient 
operation of the electricity network in the future. 
 
Gas Networks Ireland fully support the EU’s ambitions to decarbonise the electricity sector and to 
enhance the security of electricity and energy supply more broadly. This is now an unprecedented 
priority at both EU and national level, given the scale of the energy and climate crisis. We hope that 
the points raised in this response are considered in the context of any such reforms introduced.

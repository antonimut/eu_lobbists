Revision of the EU Electricity Market Design 
SolarPower Europe Position Paper  
 
Introduction  
The current energy crisis is an energy supply crisis , caused by Russia’s weaponisation of gas supply. The 
European Commission has endorsed gas as a "transitional fuel" for the EU until 2030.  
 
We must respond directly to this cris is by reducing  our dependency on fossil  fuel imports, investing 
heavily into alternative electricity resources , and electrifying end-use consumption through energy 
from renewable sources. It should be accompanied with targeted interventions at the most vulnerable 
consumers, as well as immediate measures tackling the root cause of the crisis: the high prices of gas . 
The solar sector acknowledges that EU M ember States may  have to finance such measures through 
sector-specific and temporary measures. This could include a well -designed, EU-wide cap on market 
revenues and the extraordinary contribution of oil and gas companies, or a broader economic response 
to inflation using the EU’s available financial and monetary tools. 
 
The review of the electricity market design must incorporate fundamental principles of wholesale 
markets, such as marginal pricing . Marginal pricing has been successful at providing the right signals to 
invest into technologies. Marginal pricing is also not an arbitrary choice among alternative market designs; 
it is the common way that prices emerge in free markets. Oil, gas, copper, milk, solar panels - they are all 
subject to marginal pricing. Questioning wholesale market fundamentals would create instability. 
 
Similarly, carbon pricing in the electricity system, through the EU-ETS, should remain intact. The EU-ETS 
system is an essential component of a price signal resulting in  increased investments into clean 
technologies. 
 
Market signals must be preserved. They have provided clear signals to investors and consumers to adapt, 
and innovate. We must avoid implementing a centrally managed solution, or  preventing the proper 
functioning of the market. Otherwise, incumbents might have an unfair advantage, innovation might be 
impacted and the overall energy transition may slow down. 
 
The EU electricity market must remain unified. The EU should refrain from adopting models seeking to 
split the market between types of technologies (e.g., dispatchable vs non -dispatchable).  A smaller, less 
liquid, and less technolog ical-diverse market will not  improve pricing. We need to  adopt a harmonised 
approach; splitting markets will send the wrong investment signals.  
 
The EU Electricity Market Design should push for the harmonisation of electricity pricing across the EU. 
Alternatives like locational pricing, which drive the market in the opposite direction, should be avoided. 
It complicates cross-border trade and disrupts the functioning of long-term contractual arrangements.   
 
The current crisis does exemplify an extreme case of price volatility on the wholesale market, which is 
impacting consumers. Price volatility will remain a permanent fixture; future electricity short -term 
markets will account for increasingly volatile prices, resulting from increasingly variable generation  and 
demand as end-use consumption electrifies. Higher volatility is the symptom of a well-functioning market

and dispatch mechanism ; it reveals the scarcity or oversupply of energy across all end -users. Most 
importantly, it provides the right investment signals to  the clean flexibility resources which are required 
to integrate variable renewables, in addition to new electrified end-uses (electric vehicles or heat pumps). 
As acknowledged b y the  European Union Agency for the Cooperation of Energy Regulators ( ACER), 
volatile, short-term markets are insufficient to deliver investments into more renewable energy capacity1. 
The volatility leads to  higher uncertainty, translating into  a higher cost of debt. Consumers must be 
increasingly protected from the volatility of short-term markets, as far their ability to adapt allows. 
 
The revision of the EU Electricity Market Design should :  
 
• Examine how the current design is ensuring the emergence of the right price signals for 
consumers and generators, while allowing consumers and generators to protect themselves  
against the volatility of prices in the short-term market; 
• Incentivise investments in renewables by creating as many different routes-to-market as possible 
to accommodate  different investment profile; 
• Accommodate a high share of renewable generation in the energy mix. 
 
The market design revision process should provide sufficient opportunities for stakeholder feedback.  
Changes should  also not be retroactive , and respect realised investments.  The new EU Market Design 
should make rules clear, and give industry sufficient time to adapt.

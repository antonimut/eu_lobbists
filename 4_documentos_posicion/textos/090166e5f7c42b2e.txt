Electricity market design reform 2023  
 
The European energy system has many challenges ahead. Russia’s invasion of Ukraine has 
created an energy price crisis that is testing the resilience of our system. Alongside this, to 
reach the 2030 targets, the energy system requires a rapid increase in the deployment of 
renewables.  
 
Market design plays an es sential role in how the power system will respond to these 
challenges. The current framework has proved to deliver benefits including a high security of 
supply for decades and has also responded as expected to the current crisis where sudden 
and massive supply scarcity has resulted in higher prices.  This price crisis has been – and 
should be – handled through emergency measures.  
 
The long-term power market design must continue to ensure a sufficient balance betwe en 
production and consumption at any time . An efficient power system must minimize system 
costs while ensuring security of supply at a satisfactory level. Security of supply also entails 
sufficient investments both in g eneration, transmission and energy savings . Possible 
changes in market design should focus on further improving the system’s long -term 
efficiency and ability to reach climate goals. 
 
We would like to emphasize three important aspects when considering changes to the 
market design:  
 
1. Short-term emergency measures must be temporary and expire at the end of 2023 by 
the latest. 
2. Marginal pricing is fundamental to ensure a continuous adjustment of consumption 
and production , delivering affordability, security of supp ly and investments in 
renewables. 
3. The regulatory framework for l ong-term contracts should not displace commercial 
contracts for risk mitigation n or remove incentives for investments in flexibility by 
consumers and producers. This point is further elaborated in a separate memo.  
 
Separate short-term energy crisis and a long-term electricity market reform 
Statkraft believe s that it is important  to separate what constitutes a short -term crisis from 
long-term reforms. The EU has responded to the short -term crisis with strong resolve and 
speed.  
 
However, the inframarginal revenue cap and its uneven application across Europe has 
created investor uncertainty and slowed down investment. It should neither be prolonged 
beyond 2023 nor be made permanent as part of market design.  
 
In our view, reallocation mechanisms may be necessary to ensure that consumer’s costs in 
extreme situations are not unreasonably high. Still, such a reallocation mechanism should 
not reduce investment incentives  and the price forma tion should remain unchanged. Well -
designed windfall taxes  on extraordinary profits are better tools than revenue caps . Such 
windfall taxes have less impact on operational and investments decisions , avoiding a 
reduction in efficiency, an increase costs, or a weakening in security of supply. 
 
Keep the fundamentals of a functioning market going forward  
Reform of the electricity market design for the long -term should build on the strong 
foundations of the current market’s design. The internal electricity market has been 
developed over decades and  has over time delivered security of supply  benefits as well as 
good resource utilization. Cross-border trade and the major efforts undertaken to further

Market prices reflecting supply and demand are the primary t ool to achieve a continuous 
adjustment of consumption and production. Marginal pricing is fundamental to ensure this 
balance. Marginal pricing leads to effi cient utilization of resources by using the lowest -cost 
resources to meet demand. This results in lower costs for consumers over time and 
contributes to appropriate security of supply. Additionally, market prices provide signals 
about the value of new powe r, which is important for potential new investments by  both 
electricity producers and consumers. 
 
Flexible capacity needs are increasing because of the energy transition to a renewable and 
weather-based system. Marginal pricing  and price signals reflecting the system’s variable 
needs are crucial for promoting flexibility. This will trigger both new investments and ensure 
efficient utilization of flexible assets and storage  resources in the short -term market. With a 
flat price there is no incentive to save and release flexible production for times when 
intermittent resources are not available or demand peaks. Likewise, flexibility from  
consumers (large and small) will not be made available without exposure to price signals.   
 
Getting the investment environment correct  
Power Purchase Agreements (PPAs), Two-way Contracts for Difference (CfDs) and physical 
and financial f orward markets are all important long-term price contract instruments for 
improving the investment environment. These different contract forms affect both the short -
term markets and each other . Support to certain technologies or projects through  
governmental backed CfDs pass risk from market actors to the government. Extensive use of 
CfDs does not only result in high public expenditure but may also lead to an inefficient use of 
societies’ resources connected to new investments.  
 
When consideri ng incentivizing the use of PPAs, two-way CfDs and forward markets , we 
recommend that the  Commission strongly considers how they interact with the short -term 
price setting. This includes the overall ability of producers to manage their own risks instead 
of delegating such responsibility to the government.  
 
Statkraft believes that ensuring a well -functioning physical and financial forward market  is 
essential for risk mitigation and  to ensure market-based long-term investm ents. Allowing 
bank guarantees to be used as collateral will allow more participants to enter the forward 
market and thus also increase its liquidity. Statkraft’s views on long-term pricing contracts are 
further elaborated in a separate attached memo.  
 
Remove regulatory bottlenecks for European energy independence   
Speedy permits for new renewable capacity, refurbishment of existing assets  and flexibility 
together with strong investment confidence  are foundations for Europe to succeed  in 
overcoming the challenges we face. Statkraft believes tha t it is essential to facilitate faster 
permitting times by harmonizing, streamlining and simplifying permitting procedure. 
Currently, too lengthy lead times slow down the deployment at all stages of the urgent 
energy transitio n. As such, we applaud the RePowerEU legislation for faster permitting  
processes as essential to speed up the necessary buildout of new capacity as well as grids.  
 
European Unity must remain a priority 
Efficient cross-border trade is an integral part of the internal energy market. By cross-border 
trade, resources are utilized efficiently lowering total costs and increasing the overall security 
of supply.

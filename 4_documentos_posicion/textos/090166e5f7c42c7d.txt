About Thüga 
The Munich-based Thüga Aktiengesellschaft (Thüga) is an investment and consulting company. 
Founded in 1867, it is a minority shareholder in around 100 municipal energy and water 
management companies throughout Germany. The respective majority shareholders are cities and 
municipalities. Thüga as a minority shareholder provides access to specialized knowledge and the 
benefits that come from pooling activities and resources within the Thüga network.  
Together with its partners, Thüga forms the largest municipal association of local and regional 
energy and water supply companies in Germany - the Thüga Group. With more than 22,000 
employees, the Thüga partners supply about five million customers nationwide with electricity, two 
million customers with natural gas and one million customers with drinking water.

Our main Targets for the Electricity Market Design 
Municipalities and regional utilities play a decisive role in the organization and implementation of 
the energy transition on site and in achieving the climate targets. Thanks to their proximity to 
customers, local players ensure customer-oriented and practical solutions. Only by actively 
involving them and using local and regional infrastructures can the energy transition be 
implemented promptly and successfully. This applies equally to the design of the future Electricity 
Market, particularly in the context of an increasingly decentralized power supply. 
The Energy Only Market (EOM) shows clear deficits, as it does not generate sufficient incentives for 
secured and controllable generating capacities. Therefore, the existing market design needs to be 
supplemented by some elements and instruments to address these deficits and compensate for 
growing capacity deficits. At the same time, to accelerate the expansion of RES existing barriers 
must be removed (e.g. lengthy permitting processes, land provision barriers, time-consuming 
environmental assessments, long litigation paths, bottlenecks in the grid as well as in 
interconnectors). However, against the background of the negative impact of disruptive changes in 
the Electricity Market Design we see an evolutionary development of the market design as 
meaningful. 
We suggest a primarily competitive market design with low barriers to market entry, supplemented 
by regulatory components to achieve decarbonization and security of supply goals, since these 
goals are difficult to achieve via pure market mechanisms. Markets do not create sufficient 
incentives for adding capacity of renewables as well as secure and controllable conventional 
generation capacities in Germany to cover the growing demand. Ideally, these deficits can be 
compensated by near load generation and flexibility capacities that municipal utilities and regional 
energy suppliers can best provide.

Regarding the enhancement of the Electricity Market Design, we should focus on permanent and 
reliable solutions, not short-term crisis management, to create confidence in the markets and 
investment security. Only an adequate and stable regulatory framework can trigger the necessary 
investments in power generation assets. Market interventions should be as limited as possible and 
market disruptions should be avoided to generate confidence in the market and investment security. 
Any regulatory measures must take into account possible interactions with the market. 
Furthermore, the legislative approach for the evolution of the Energy Market Design should focus on 
energy sector approaches and not interfere with other policy objectives (i.e., social policy for private 
consumers, promotion of SMEs or large industrial customers). These tasks must not be reflected in 
the market design but must be carried out by the governments in the member states via the 
appropriate instruments in each policy field (i.e. through direct state aid). 
The overarching framework for the electricity market must be set at the European level. There 
should be no relapse into national solutions. The energy crisis situation last year has brought 
evidence, that the bottleneck situations for electricity (and gas) could only be overcome by a 
functioning internal EU energy market.  
Last but not least, every discussion regarding the Electricity Market design should bear in mind the 
(fast) feasibility of the suggested instruments.

Contribution to the consultation on the EU’s electricity market design 
reform 
 
thyssenkrupp Steel Europe AG | 13 February 2023 
 
 
Key Messages 
• The record-high energy prices reached over the last years are a vital challenge for energy -
intensive industries in the European Union, such as steel. On the one hand, sustained high 
electricity and natural gas prices in Europe negatively affect European co mpanies’ global 
competitiveness. As a result, many sites have already started to halt or reduce production. On 
the other hand, high electricity prices hamper industrial decarbonisation efforts requiring even 
larger volumes of renewable and low-carbon electricity in the coming years.  
• The EU must react now, especially with regard to other competitive regions, to preserve its own 
industrial base and foster its transformation towards climate neutrality. So far, all EU emergency 
measures have fallen short of offering relief to energy-intensive industries. And even though the 
EU’s internal energy market has undoubtedly generated various consumer benefits, such as 
reduced system costs, it has proven unable to ensure a globally competitive price level for 
energy-intensive industries. In many cases, other regions pursue an active location policy and 
grant local producers of basic materials and other industries additional subsidised electricity 
prices. 
• The planned reform of the EU’s electricity market is an opportunit y that should be seized to 
ensure a competitive electricity supply to European energy -intensive industries. The reform 
should, therefore, pave the way for establishing a European electricity price for the most energy- 
and trade-intensive industries (European electricity price for industry).  
• The European electricity price for industry should be the flip side of contracts for difference (Cfd) 
for supporting renewable and low-carbon electricity production. Decade-long public support for 
using mature renewable and low-carbon electricity production technologies must provide a clear 
and direct benefit for energy- and trade-intensive industries in the EU.  
• To achieve this goal, the EU should first set up a regulatory framework allowing Member States 
to guarantee a competitive price level for specific energy- and trade-intensive industries through 
an electricity price for industry. Second, in the spirit of cohesion, the EU should mobilise 
significant financial resources to support Member States with less fiscal space, e.g. through the 
announced Sovereignty Fund.

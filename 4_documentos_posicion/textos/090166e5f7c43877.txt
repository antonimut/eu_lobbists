Public Consultation: Revision of the EU’s electrici ty market design 
Comments by Falck Renewables  
 
 
 
12
th  February 2023 
 
 
Falck Renewables strongly supports the reform of the EU’s electricity market design, 
which preserves and enhances the incentives for investments in low-carbon power 
production technologies, providing investors with certainty and predictability, while 
addressing the economic and social concerns related to the high energy prices recently 
experienced. Although the actual market design has helped the deployment of 
renewables over the last decades by bringing good investment signals, it has recently 
shown some shortcomings that must be addressed. 
The route to decarbonisation requires a clear regulatory framework which makes 
electrification the first solution for both the industrial and domestic sectors and transfers 
the benefits of renewable energy production to final consumers. The market design 
reform must also differentiate between measures aimed at achieving the long-term 
targets for a zero-emission power sector at the lowest cost for society and short-term 
interventions aimed at mitigating the impacts of the current energy crisis. 
 
In response to the energy crisis, amplified by the Ukraine war, the REPowerEU plan, 
published in May 2022, has identified both the accelerated deployment of renewable 
sources and the improvements in energy system integ ration as key instruments to 
support European households and companies. In this context, national governments 
have intervened in energy markets to bring the costs down for electricity bills, in the 
absence of an EU-level harmonised response, leading to fragmented and uncoordinated 
interventions. Consequently, the principles enshrined in the current electricity market 
framework have been undermined, leading to uncertainty on revenues and ultimately 
to significant negative impacts on investments in new renewable power capacity.  
 
To reverse such worrying trends, Falck Renewables urges the need for a proper revision 
of the EU’s electricity market design, based on the following main pillars:  
•  Power Purchase Agreements and Contracts for Differe nce, when applied on 
voluntary basis and / or according to market-based mechanisms, are good tools to 
mitigate volatility of energy prices in the long-term and an appropriate incentive

•  Limiting revenues or imposing a windfall profits tax mechanism, far from addressing

and tax uncertainty and disincentivizes new investments. 
 
 
 
Specific comments 
 
Making Electricity Bills Independent of Short-Term Markets 
 
Power Purchase Agreements (PPAs) 
Falck Renewables considers that PPAs constitute an effective way to mitigate the 
impact of demand or supply shocks, thus protecting final consumers, including 
industrial consumers, who can see how their competitiveness decreases because of 
energy prices. We support the measures proposed by the EU Commission and we 
suggest introducing some reinforcements such as an obligation for suppliers to buy 
part of their electricity via long-term PPAs, incentives for industrials to cover part 
of their consumption through bilateral PPA transact ions or a centralized PPA 
market . We also believe that the following barriers currently preventing the conclusion 
of PPAs should be properly addressed: (i) credit worthiness requirements  and 
guarantee package needs, which prevent most small- to mid-sized consumers to 
participate in the PPA market and represent a constraint for the PPA bankability; (ii) 
low liquidity of forward markets in the mid-long-term resulting in low transparency 
of PPA pricing ; (iii) difficulties of producers in offsetting most of the risk profiles 
through PPA transactions with consumers because not all consumers are used to 
managing and negotiating complex PPA products with high bankability; (iv) speeding 
up and simplifying the permitting processes for renewables power plants mitigate the 
risks in the PPA negotiation related to potential d elays in commissioning .

Forward Markets 
We consider that forward markets on their own are not sufficient to support investment 
in new renewable capacity, due, in part to their low liquidity and high financial costs, 
although they represent a good instrument to mitigate market volatility risk in the 
short-term for final consumers and integrate the PPA hedging strategy of producers. 
We agree that the creation of virtual hubs for forward contracts could improve the 
effectiveness of forward markets for hedging purposes, increasing the number of risk 
profiles which it is possible to hedge through forward products and the transparency of 
pricing. In our view, the  creation of new, non-standard forward products cor related 
with the renewables production profile, new account ing methodologies for fair 
value calculation and reduction of collateral costs  would definitely support the 
development of forward markets. On the other hand, the main drawbacks that prevent 
participants from entering into forward contracts are: (i) high financial cost  
(warranties) to operate on exchange platforms such as EEX; (ii) low liquidity for mid-
long-term products; (iii) high bid/ask spread  in periods of high volatility and 
uncertainty; (iv) baseload products which are not efficient enough to hedge all risks 
of renewables power production profiles ; (v) in case of negative fair value, weight 
on company debt . 
 
Contracts for Difference (CfDs) 
In our view, two-way contracts for difference, applied to  all renewable technologies  
without discrimination, and on a voluntary basis , constitute an effective way to drive 
down the price of electricity while playing a key role in the deployment of new 
renewable capacity. This mechanism has been proven useful when hedging most of the 
risk profiles, boosting new investment in green energy and collecting financial resource 
by governments to ease the effects on final consumers in periods of skyrocketing energy 
prices. On the other hand, some risks should be taken into account when structuring 
the mechanism: (i) if CfD’s auctions are not well designed, they could end up giving 
support to projects which are at a too early stage in the development phase , with 
little probability of success, as well as geographically concentrated projects, which can 
lead to constraints in the grid ; (ii) CfDs can boost price cannibalization  in the long-
term, supporting too much capacity for the same technology; (iii) they might promote 
technologies that have poorer performance than othe rs as regards hours of 
production; (iv) size the correct amount of capacity available  for all the technology 
clusters. All these risks can be easily mitigated if we take into account some 
considerations when designing the mechanism such as  studying and defining the 
capacity allocation via CfD, considering all the incentive schemes already in place at 
national level, promoting the deployment of renewable capacity in geographical areas 
with few grid constraints or positioned closer to demand, restricting the auctions to just

Accelerating the deployment of renewables  
Some short-term measures that we believe could acce lerate the deployment of 
renewables are: (i) simplification of the authorization procedures  for renewable 
energy installations, applying the principle of overriding public interest and respecting 
the mandatory deadlines; (ii) identifying in advance the characteristics of projects which 
would allow them to avoid any environmental and landscape process ; (iii) 
simplifying and standardizing in all Member States authorization processes for 
electrochemical storage plants  combined with renewable plants. Without these 
simplifications, any adjustment of the electricity market will remain ineffective due to 
the physical shortage of renewable projects to be submitted to PPAs, CfDs and any 
other alternative contracting to the spot market. 
 
It is also necessary to design a transparent and inclusive tariff regulation framewo rk  
that gives visibility of the system's needs and reflects its costs, and that ensures stability 
to grid infrastructure operators to plan their interventions for grid development, the 
connection of new renewable capacity and smart grid management. 
 
Grid operators – both TSOs and DSOs - are currently not remunerated for investments 
in the development of new storage systems which normalize the production at specific 
points of the grid, thus preventing them from building new grid infrastructure to 
evacuate electricity. In this context, new incentives are needed for private investments 
in the installation of storage systems, considering the current risk in constructing them 
on a full merchant basis. 
 
Limiting revenues of inframarginal generators 
We consider that there are no advantages to limiting revenues of inframarginal  
generators  in terms of decarbonization and fossil fuel consumption, as it brings 
regulatory uncertainty from an industrial perspective. It is quite difficult to impose a 
methodology that fits all Member States and all technologies, and when implemented 
on a country-by-country basis, it may create market distortion. Additionally, the 
implementation of any revenue cap should also guara ntee a price floor . Financial 
resources from revenue caps applications must be allocated to the consumer clusters 
more exposed to skyrocketing prices, e.g., people with low family or individual income. 
Any support to energy-intensive industry must not disincentivize energy efficiency 
actions nor  alter how sensitive the volumes demanded are to price.

Alternatives to gas to keep the electricity system in balance 
 
We believe that having further locational and technology-based information in the 
bidding in the market would bring important advantages such as an improvement in 
price and fundamental forecast , the definition of benchmarks  on renewables 
performance with different technologies and/or locations, clear investment signals  
that would support the identification of the most interesting geographical areas and a 
reduction in balancing costs  charged to final consumers. 
 
To enhance the development of flexibility assets like storage systems, it is necessary 
to introduce a comprehensive regulatory framework from a dispatching perspective, as 
well as the storage participation in ancillary services and the promotion of new market 
instruments associated to storage features, such as products used for time-shifting. 
Long-term incentives dedicated to demand response a re necessary to support their 
spread, evaluating from the outset which operators are really able to provide flexibility 
to the electricity system. 
 
To ensure the necessary investments to complement the rising share of renewables and 
to better align with decarbonisation targets, we also urge the need for change in the 
current capacity mechanisms, which are mostly energy-based, to  “capacity-based” 
mechanisms . Obligations in offering energy can be a strong constraint for intermittent 
and non-programmable renewable energy sources. 
 
Lastly, we underline that a long-term shift of the market to more granular locational 
pricing would not be beneficial for the new market design, due to the risk of market 
cannibalization in the production segment by large companies, which are able to 
diversify their investments and more deeply analyze nodal markets thus mitigating the 
nodal price risk.

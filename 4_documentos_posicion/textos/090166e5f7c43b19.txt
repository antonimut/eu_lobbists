Position paper Electricity Market Design Reform 
Eneco is an integrated energy company, active in the Netherlands, Belgium, Germany and the United 
Kingdom. Eneco's head office is located in Rotterdam, the Netherlands. Eneco supplies electricity, 
heat and gas to business and household consumers. We develop and operate on- and offshore wind, 
solar farms, gas-fired plants, district heating networks, renewable heat sources, electric boilers, 
storage, energy management systems, heat pumps and charging services for electric vehicles. We 
have the ambition to become climate neutral by 2035, including both our own operations and our 
customers. 
By March 2023, the European Commission is expected to propose a legislative proposal on the reform 
of the electr icity market . Considering the potential implications for security of supply , economic 
prosperity and decarbonisation, Eneco believes that a cautious approach in reforming the electricity 
market is appropriate. The long-term markets and the marginal pricing of the short-term spot markets 
lead to prices which  reflect the value and av ailability of power.  In our view, the policy goals should  
continue to be availability, sustainability and affordability. Decoupling of the power and gas market 
will evolve when the share of renewable and CO2-free production increases.  
Nevertheless, recent developments have exposed flaws in the design of the electricity market, which 
require reform. During the energy crisis prices have increased sharply and many households and SMEs 
have severe difficulties to afford the price of energy. It is therefore justified that member states take 
measures against energy poverty. However, such measures should be targeted on those in need.  
The current market design also leads to volatile wholesale prices which are also reflected in the retail 
market. Volatility gives the right price signals to end-users and is not a problem as long as end-users 
have the possibility to mitigate the risk of volatility.  However, p rice vo latility does undermine 
investment certainty for renewable energy.  
Power purchasing agreements  
A higher share of renewable energy and CO2-free production in the mix will reduce the dependency of 
power prices on volatile fossil fuel prices. This will inevitably create more long-term stability. A higher 
share of renewables will lower not only forward prices but also short-term spot market prices. In the 
long run power prices are determined by the LCOE of renewable  production. In the meantime, long-
term Power Purchasing Agreements (PPA’s) for corporate users and fixed -price supply contracts for 
SME and consumers contribute to a more stable pri cing situation . Therefore we support th e 
introduction of tax benefits and bank guara ntees to enable more  long-term PPA’s . Also l ong-term 
supply contracts for SME’s and household consumers should be facilitated by the right incentives.   
Double-side Contracts for Difference 
In most cases, t he ‘market-only’ based approach is ins ufficient for the  investment certainty that is 
needed for renewable energy production. Therefore government support will be needed. In our view 
optional double-sided Contracts for Difference  (CfD) for new projects would be a n appropriate 
additional measure, in conjunction with promoting PPA ’s. A CfD  creates guaranteed income for

producers and it redistributes producer welfare to consumer welfare in case market prices increase. A 
potential disadvantage of a CfD is that, if ill-designed, it could hamper the PPA market: for producers 
there may be no reason to close a PPA if the revenue is in all cases guaranteed by the CfD. Therefore 
a CfD should be optional; it should function as a measure providing a safety net. Only if PPA’s do not 
give enough certainty, a CfD should actually be used.  
In case an asset is awarded a CfD as a result of a tender,  the Guarantees of Origin should be given to 
the producer. The will provide an incentive for producers to include the value of Guarantees of Origin 
in a bid. This will enable more PPAs and less need for public financial support. 
Eneco opposes the extension of the temporary inframarginal cap, which is exclusively directed to 
skimming off revenues. The inframarginal revenue cap undermines investment certainty in renewable 
energy.  Member states have widely diverged from the agreed 180 euro market v alue and a number 
of member states have not adequately addressed the hedging practices in the power sector. We also 
oppose imposing a mandatory Contract for Difference to existing projects. Revising already granted 
support would significantly undermine the confidence of investors. 
CO2-free flexibility options 
We acknowledge that an increased share of renewable electricity will lead to more short -term price 
fluctuations. Dynamic pricing and support for electricity storage and demand side response will be key 
instruments to match future demand and supply and stabilize short-term price volatility. 
As the share of renewable production will increase the need for dispatchable, weather independent, 
power (or demand-side response) will increase. In order to phase out the use of gas-fired and coal-
fired plants, the use of batteries, demand-side response and hydrogen-fired power plants etc. will have 
to be promoted. Therefore, more value should be awarded to CO 2-free options in the design of 
ancillary services and congestion management. A temporary waiver for the payment of grid tariffs for 
batteries should be considered in all EU countries . This is an easy and short -term measure and is 
justified as batteries can be considered as delayed feed-in of electricity. 
Additionally, Member States need Capacity Remuneration Mechanisms  (CRMs) in a market with a 
rapidly increasing share of renewables . CRMs should be limited to providing the required adequacy 
based on a system adequacy assessment and  should be designed to minimise distortive impacts on 
energy markets. Moreover, CRMs should be technology-neutral and allow domestic and cross-border 
demand side response, storage and renewable generators’ participation. They should meet an 
emissions performance standard starting from the European Investment Bank (EIB) lending policy 
standard and decreasing over time in line with reaching a CO2-free power system in 2035. 
Moreover, we call for m ore transparency on the capacity per ancillary product  of TSO’s . This  will 
improve the longer term investment willingness in batteries etc.  
Furthermore, the EU’s system for Guarantees of Origin should be gradually reformed so to create value 
for CO2-free power generation on a 24/7 basis.  
Increasing consumer welfare 
Eneco considers that different costumers have different needs. This applies to both business and 
household end-users. Some want to take charge of their own power consumption and even produce 
power themselves and want to fully benefit from the market. Other customers want to be shielded 
from uncertainty and do not want to worry about unexpected costs.

In order to empower consumers and suppo rt well-informed choices, Eneco advocates t he following 
measures: 
Measures aimed at household consumers and SMEs: 
• Allow long-term (longer than 5 years) supply  contracts in the consumer market with compensation 
for the economic loss if the contract is terminated; 
• Offer credit guarantees and tax breaks for corporate renewable Power Purchasing Agreement with 
SMEs; 
• Dynamic pricing: 
o Rules on clear communication for dynamic pricing about exposure to price volatility  
o Stimulate the usage of demand response services by SME’s and consumers; 
Measures aimed at larger businesses 
• Support business end users to close green corporate PPAs with credit guarantees  and tax 
incentives; 
• Support investments in demand-side response and batteries. 
Measures against energy poverty 
Despite the above-mentioned measures, energy poverty can occur and will increase in times of high 
prices. Government intervention should in our view: 
• In principle  be part of general social  measures because energy poverty is not a stand-alone 
problem; 
• Support for households at risk of energy proverty  should be aimed at energy efficiency , 
electrification, energy management and renewable electricity production (prosumers) 
• If specific intervention is justified because of a  crisis situation, support should be temporary and 
targeted to households with a low income and high energy bills. 
 
Further considerations on the design of an investment framework  
Prices are volatile in the wholesale market so there is investment uncertainty for renewables despite 
the high energy prices. Long-term PPAs and long-term supply contracts can help to improve investment 
certainty. The measures listed above (see section increasing  consumer welfare) would also support 
investments in renewables.  Moreover, long-term hedging adds to investment certainty  but high 
market prices lead to higher “margin calls’ and higher financial risks that many energy companies are 
not able to bear. Government guarantees or credit facilities can lower these risks but state aid for 
specific companies should be avoided and a level playing field should be maintained. 
Eneco advocates the introduction of a optional double-sided Contract for Difference for new projects 
where producers compete in a tender for subsidy when the day ahead spot price is below the tendered 
strike price and where revenues above the strike price are skimmed off. In our view there should not 
be a central buyer as counterpart for the CfD and the producer should be free to sell its power to the 
(spot) market. This means that the instrument of a double-sided CfD can be combined with PPAs.  
The double-side CfD  has several advantages: 
• The strike price is determined by pay-as-bid (LCOE plus a margin) and not by the gas price; 
• The price is competitive because of the tender; 
• The skimmed off revenues can be redistributed to consumers for energy poverty;

o Skimming off should take place on an yearly basis of average prices; 
o If returns would be skimmed off on an hourly basis there would be too much uncertainty 
about total revenue and assets would not respond to changes in supply and demand. 
• The current market design will keep on functioning efficiently; 
o The decentral dispatch stays intact 
o Power that is produced under a CfD can be sold on the (spot) market  
The potential disadvantage of a CfD is that if it is ill-designed it could hamper the long-term PPA market: 
for producers there may be no reason to close a PPA if the revenue is in all cases guaranteed by the 
CfD. Therefore a CfD should be optional and a measure of last resort. Only if PPAs do not give enough 
certainty, a CfD should be used.

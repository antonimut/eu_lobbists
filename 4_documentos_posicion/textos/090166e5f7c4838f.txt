Proxigas welcomes the consultation on the Electricity Market Design (EMD): the 
revision of the EMD is required for developing a suitable and forward- looking 
framework supporting the achievement of stable, efficient, reliable and well-integrated 
energy mark ets, enabling the European Green Deal objectives and the energy 
transition by 2050.  
The challenging target of 55% reduction in GHG emissions by 2030 requires 
interventions to enable a rapid and cost -effective decarbonisation of the energy 
system. The speed with which changes must happen requires policy makers and 
Member States to take decisive actions, outlining a credible and sustainable pathway, 
from an economic, social and environmental point of view, as well as for the flexibility, 
security and resilience of the energy system.  
The reform of the current EMD should leverage the strategic role of gas infrastructures 
for security of supply, system resilience and ability to support the system -wide energy 
transition in the most cost-effective way, enabling gas and gaseous fuels to play their 
role in the current and future energy mix (20% of the total EU energy consumption in 
2050).  
Meeting the EU’s ambitious climate and energy targets in the most cost-effective way 
requires a multi-vectored approach across all sectors to achieve carbon neutrality. It 
needs to build on an integrated energy system, that delivers energy efficiency 
improvements, growing shares of renewables and the progressive, cost -effective 
decarbonisation of the electricity and gas sector using all available technologies and 
with targeted support.  
 
Alternatives to Gas to Keep the Electricity System in Balance 
The consultation covers ways to improve the conditions under which flexibility solutions 
such as demand response, energy storage and other weather independent renewable 
and low carbon sources, can be developed and compete in the markets.  
The Commission highlight in particular that these flexibility solutions, combined with 
renewable generation and enhanced investments in grid capacity and inter -
connectivity, should contribute to reducing the role that natural gas -fired generation 
plays as a flexible source of generation and will, over time, replace, and thereby , 
phase out natural gas -fired power generation in line with the EU’s decarbonisation 
targets. 
We completely disagree with this Commission’ statement because we believe that 
natural gas and gaseous fuels  (renewable and low carbon molecules) will play a 
fundamental role in the energy mix and will be key to ensure a good market 
functioning as well as security of supply and energy system resilience, in particular in 
an energy landscape where intermittent electricity sources are destined to substantially 
increase.  
As reported by the European Commission in the Impact Assessment of the Gas and 
Decarbonisation Package, “
Gaseous fuels account for roughly 22% of total EU energy 
consumption today (including around 20% of EU electricity production, and 39% of 
heat production). According to the relevant scenarios used by the Climate Target Plan 
Impact Assessment, the share of gaseous fuels to total EU energy consumption in

2050 would be about 20%. Biogas, bio -methane, renewable and decarbonised 
hydrogen as well as synthetic methane would represent some 2/3 of the gaseous fuels 
in the 2050 energy mix, with fossil gas with CCS/U representing the remainder. In 
order to realize the increased climate ambition, the Commissio n’s is working on a 
policy that facilitates a progressive phase out of unabated use of fossil gases via 
gradual replacement of natural gas and the uptake of renewable and low -carbon 
gases.”  
Natural gas and gaseous fuels -fired power generation will ensure  sustainable and 
reliable coverage of the electricity market’s balancing and flexibility needs. 
Demand response, storage and other flexibility solutions-  to be put in place – could 
potentially be intended as reducing the role of natural gas in providing flexibility. The 
development of new products to foster demand reduction and shift energy at peak 
times might contribute to phasing out gas plants from the merit order in some 
circumstances, and incentivize flexibility when fossil fuel capacity is needed the most 
in the system. 
However, these solutions should be carefully evaluated in terms of economic and social 
costs related to demand reduction and/or related investments . We do not believe that 
increasing possibilities for collective self-consumption and electricity sharing will bring 
customers/“prosumers” – even through collective self -consumption gro ups – to 
produce all the energy they would need in an energy system where intermittency is 
destined to substantially increase, so that they could abandon  electricity produced 
from flexible gaseous fuels -fired power generation. 
Instead of fostering the se ctor decoupling, as the Commission proposes in the 
consultation, we believe that challenges for achieving the ambitious European climate 
targets, combined with the need to ensure energy system resilience and security of 
supply, impose to adopt a technology  neutral approach and to promote sector 
coupling and sector integration, with the aim to build a decarbonized and hybrid EU 
energy system.  
In order to meet the carbon-neutrality by 2050, increased electrification in all sectors 
is foreseen in the consulta tion document to be the key development. However, the 
future switch to an all-electric energy system would be neither possible nor desirable, 
for a number of reasons ranging from the difficulty to electrify the so -called “hard to 
decarbonise” sectors to th e need for massive, not sustainable, investments while 
significant other energy infrastructures are available and can be used in a sustainable 
way.  
An energy system based on an increasing share of intermittent renewable electricity 
(namely wind and solar) will need to be complemented with an equivalent increase of 
controllable and flexible resources to ensure (short-term) system reliability and (longer-
term) adequacy. Currently, for many EU Member States, this flexibility is provided by 
natural gas-fired power plants. 
Transition to a zero -carbon economy is meant to imply, along with a progressive 
electrification (where technically feasible and economically sustainable), a shift 
towards a progressively decarbonised gas sector and a closer interaction between  
integrated energy vectors (sector coupling) and other sectors of the EU economy in a 
harmonised strategic vision for a circular, carbon -neutral system. The energy system 
 
4 
integration will be fundamental to build a decarbonized and hybrid EU energy system 
and achieve the EU Green Deal’s vision for a carbon-neutral EU economy by 2050. 
In summary, the role of electricity in the energy mix and the complementary value of 
natural gas and progressively decarbonised and renewables molecules should be 
both fully recognised, so that the strategic role of gaseous fuels should not be 
undermined in the current revision of the electricity market design. In 2030 natural 
gas and gaseous fuels will still play a relevant role in satisfying European energy 
demand and supporting the need for balancing the intermittence of increasing RES 
production. This role will still be needed in the longer term and guaranteed by 
progressively decarbonised molecules, as stated by the European Commission in the 
Hydrogen and decarbonised gas market package impact assessment.  It is therefore 
necessary to keep a market design enabling natural gas and gaseous fuels to play 
their role both in the current and in future energy mix. 
 
Enhancing the Integrity and Transparency of the Energy Market 
Regulation (EU) 1227/2011 on wholesale market integrity and transparency (REMIT) 
was designed more than a decade ago to ensure that consumers and other market 
participants can have confidence in the integrity of electricity and gas markets, that 
prices reflect a fair and competitive interplay between supply and demand, and that 
no profits can be drawn from market abuse. 
Proxigas considers that REMIT worked well as a sector specific regime in the gas and 
power industry: considering the importance of the ov erall regulatory stability for the 
sector, we do not see a need of deep review. However, we consider that it could be 
worth making some adjustments.  
In general, we agree with the idea that cooperation between energy and financial 
regulators could be fruit fully strengthened, but we do not see the need to bring this 
sector specific regulation closer to financial regulation . Quite the opposite ; some 
areas of practical implementation, namely related to the management of inside 
information and physical flows deliveries, rather require that a higher consideration is 
paid to specific features of energy markets. 
Moreover, Proxigas believes that the forthcoming review should be seen as a useful 
occasion for streamlining requirements upon national markets, namely avoiding 
national initiatives that could create new obligations on data reporting and 
transparency and trying to ensure a convergence under REMIT of some existing 
obligations at national or EU level (e.g., PDE reporting in Italy, or the brand-new LNG 
reporting stream).

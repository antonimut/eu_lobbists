 
 
 13 February2023   EGEC views on the Electricity Market Design consultation  EGEC, the voice of the European geothermal industry, is a not-for-profit association representing the entire value-chain of the industry across 28 countries. It is included on the European Transparency Register number: 11458103335-07. Further information can be found at www.egec.org.  We welcome the priority focus on the electricity market design reforms. We make the following observations, clarifications and recommendations:    Summary  Europe has a gas price crisis. The volatility of gas supplies caused heating and electricity prices to dramatically increase.  The problem stems from the lack of a regulatory focus for a competitive European market for heat as well as the underdevelopment of dispatchable renewable electricity, principally geothermal.  The new Electricity Market Design reforms must adequately support geothermal electricity and reward network services such as storage, flexibility and system security to ensure the EU’s electricity system is fit for purpose to address the climate, fossil-fuel dependency and biodiversity crises.    Alternatives to the gas to keep the electricity system in balance • Geothermal provides dispatchable (baseload) electricity, flexibility, adequacy and reliability to grids. It has not received the same support as other renewable technologies. The EMD is the regulatory vehicle to correct this.  • The consultation document ignores the contribution of dispatchable renewable electricity suppliers such as geothermal and also other predictable and secure electricity sources from concentrated solar, ocean and hydro.  • Centralising capacity payments for dispatchable renewable electricity is key. These should be financed directly from the Connecting Europe Facility as they provide European grid and system balancing services.  • We are concerned that caps and segregation of markets dependent on electricity producer could undermine the effectiveness of the carbon price on pricing fossils out of the market.  • The European Sovereignty Fund must also be used to financially de-risk upfront construction costs of geothermal and other predictable and dispatchable renewable generators in all Member States to ensure each electricity network has adequate grid supporting capacity within its vicinity.

 Making electricity bills independent of short-term markets • Geothermal helps to lower electricity bills. The Italian region of Radicondoli, in Tuscany, reduced electricity prices by 30% for its residential and business consumers in January 2023 due to the competitive costs of geothermal electricity. See https://www.cosvig.it/radicondoli-taglia-i-costi-dellelettricita-a-famiglie-e-imprese-grazie-alle-risorse-geotermiche/  • Geothermal provides grid and system balancing services because it operates at a load factor above 90%, on average. This is greater than any other renewable, fossil or alternative energy source.  • Geothermal power plants can ramp up and ramp down in 15 seconds. These flexible services require an adequate financial reward. The Connecting Europe Facility can be used to purchase these services over a 12 month period to ensure system adequacy and stability.  • Two-way Contracts for Difference (CfDs) need to recognise non-price services such as adequacy, grid and system balancing and storage. They need to be sufficiently long-term in scope (beyond 15 years) to de-risk the construction of new geothermal power plant.  • Auctions should include elements concerning firmness, security of supply and system integration to capture the market welfare increase that a non-intermittent and synchronous electricity production can help realise. Geothermal electricity should also be incentivised to participate in short term and flexibility markets.  Better consumer empowerment and protection • The electricity market is increasingly replacing fossil-based heating. Heating in buildings and industrial processes is the largest source of fossil gas consumption in the EU. • Geothermal Heat Pumps (GHPs) are the most efficient source of heating and cooling. They directly replace fossil heating which brings savings to consumers but also use the least electricity input providing additional savings.  • Geothermal district heating also makes large-scale replacements of fossil heating, which has led to reductions in heating bills in winter 2022/2023. For example, the geothermal district heating system in Szeged, Hungary reduced heating bills by 50% for this winter whilst the geothermal district heating system in Ferrara, Italy, reduced heating bills by 25%.  • The EU DSO entity should be mandated to design efficient electricity heating (using a seasonal performance of 3 and above) deployment to meet the binding renewable heating and cooling target (Article 23) in the Renewable Energy Directive.  • The EU Energy Platform’s mandate must be extended, through the EMD reforms, to include the purchase of geothermal electricity plants as they are of European Strategic importance to electricity market stability.

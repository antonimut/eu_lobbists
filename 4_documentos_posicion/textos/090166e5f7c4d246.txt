Floene Energias S.A.  (Floene) Submission to the EU’s public 
consultation on the reform of the EU’s Electricity Market Design  
 
13th February 2023 
 
 
➢ Who we are 
The Floene Group is the largest gas distribution network operator in Portugal, through the participation and 
direct management of 9 Regional Gas Distribution Companies (ORD), present in 106 municipalities across the 
country. It is, therefore, responsible for managing the medium and low -pressure gas distribution network, 
under a public service regime. 
 
With a gross asset of over 2,3 billion €, more than 1,1 million of connection points and 13 700 network km 
mostly built-in polyethylene (94%) and with an average age of 16 years, Floene has one of the most modern 
and efficient infrastructures in Europe, which allows it to ensure a completely s afe supply and a quality 
service, complying with all the criteria established by ERSE and the sector-specific legislation. 
 
Floene is also at the forefront of the decarbonisation of energy consumption and the energy transition, as it 
is prepared for the distribution of renewable gases. The polyethylene network allows the transport of green 
hydrogen and non-fossil gases such as biomethane.  
 
➢ Electricity Market Reform 
Floene welcomes the public  consultation and the initiative to review Electricity Market legislation as an 
opportunity to address short term challenges facing the electricity sector across the EU and to make the 
electricity sector more resilient, reduce costs incurred by the consumers and support the energy transition.  
 
We believe that it is essential to invest in a complementary and integrated energy system (sector coupling), 
incorporating multiple energy sources (electricity, gas, heat) and integrating multiple infrastructures, thus 
promoting the best use of each solution throughout the value chain (production, storage, distribution, 
consumption). 
 
Furthermore, the reform of the EU electricity market must consider the benefits of an energy system based 
on a combination of electricity and gas allowing for: 
• Greater security of supply and energy independence, 
• Greater efficiency of the energy system through the integration of sectors, 
• Greater freedom of choice for consumers, with adaptation to different needs,  
• Development of future local business ecosystems associated with renewable gases.

Flexible and extended gas distribution networks are crucial in the decarbonisation process and vital in the 
context of sector coupling between gas and electricity.  Gas assets are crucial fl exibility and storage tools 
when intermittent energy, whose penetration in the European energy system will significantly increase, is 
not available. The energy system must be always balanced by the transmission and distribution networks to 
prevent blackouts.  
 
To achieve the transition towards a higher share of intermittent renewable energy, the energy infrastructure 
will have to ensure supply security and optimisation by maximising synergies between the gas and electricity 
systems.  The current gas infrastructure (storage, transmission, and distribution systems) is a strength in 
terms of energy storage and capacity, benefitting the energy system as a whole by supplying flexibility and 
security to the electricity side. This is especially the case in times of seasonal and daily peak demand for the 
heating of buildings. Grid infrastructure must be designed to deliver large volumes of energy at times of peak 
demand while being resilient. 
 
Energy system integration should become the cornerstone of the design of any new grid tariff to promote 
synergies between the gas and electricity systems. Indeed, the existing gas system is an energy asset in terms 
of energy storage and available capacity and is beneficial to the energy system providing flexibility and 
security of supply to the electricity system. We believe that sector coupling is the missing part of the current 
legislative reforms to implement new solutions of market integration, especially at distribution level, through 
hydrogen electrolysers, micro-cogeneration and hybrid heating. 
 
The electricity mar ket design should evolve to better foster synergies with the gas system as relevant 
investments could be avoided in the electricity grids by taking a ‘whole of the energy system’ approach to 
energy planning. A 2019 study by Afry indicates that an integrated energy system, capable of maximizing the 
use of the gas distribution infrastructure will result in potential savings - in Portugal alone - of around 9 billion 
Euros, due to the reduction in the need to upgrade electricity grids and interconnections. 
 
Existing gas infrastructures are central to Portugal’s and the EU’s energy mix and are key enablers to achieving 
energy security and climate action targets. Portugal has one of the most modern gas distribution networks 
in Europe (95% in polyethylene and with an average age much lower than other European countries) and can 
benefit from its use for the injection of renewable gases in the short/medium term, enhancing its 
development throughout the country , contributing to further penetration of renewables and to achieving 
national and European climate objectives.  
 
Aside from the short -term challenges, the European electricity sector is facing several more long -term 
challenges triggered by increasing intermittent electricity supply, decentralized generation at distribution 
grids level, decommissioning of traditional power plants  and expected growth in electrical demand jointly 
with more ambitious goals as we move towards 2050. These challenges will require ongoing policy reflection 
going beyond the scope of the current reform and we believe that energy system integration and a complete 
and technology neutral approach to energy policy will be crucial to ensure in the long term the att ainment 
of European objectives.  
 
 
Through the REPowerEU plan, the European Commission has put forward a range of initiatives to support 
the accelerated deployment of renewable energy and to advance energy system integration. Leveraging the 
value of the gas grids, alongside with decarbonising the electricity system, will be crucial in achieving these 
ambitions.  Existing gas grids can easily and immediately accommodate the expected growing volumes of low 
carbon and renewable gases without additional large-scale investments. In conjunction with existing storage 
capacities, gas grids can and will continue to  provide the necessary flexibility and back up to manage the 
increasing variability of renewable electricity supply, in a cost-efficient way, as the production of renewable 
gases and its injection in the networks increases.  Hence investment in both existing an d new gas 
infrastructure remains a priority, as we transition to a net-zero energy system , in alignment with climate 
objectives and to support the transport of renewable gases in the future.  
 
We fully support the EU’s ambitions to decarbonise the electricity sector and enhance the security of energy 
supply, particularly given the current conjecture. Nevertheless, we believe it is important that  any future 
reforms aim at an integrated and complementary energy system, as we have highlighted in the arguments 
presented above.

The Swedish Society for Nature Conservation’s (SSNC’s) 
comments on the integrated water management – revised lists of 
surface and groundwater pollutants 
 
Summary 
• The Swedish Society for Nature Conservation (SSNC) welcomes the 
Commission's proposal to revise the Water Framework Directive (WFD), the 
Environmental Quality Standards Directive (EQSD) and the Groundwater 
directive (GWD). SSNC is positive that PFAS, pharmaceuticals and pesticides 
are among the substances added to the lists of pollutants. 
• SSNC would like to emphasize that the revision is of technical nature only, 
and should not touch on basic obligations of the WFD 
• SSNC has some doubts concerning the proposed removal of WFD Article 16, 
among other things because the time frame for phasing out harmful priority 
substances is removed. 
• SSNC recommends that environmental quality standards (EQS) should be 
specified for additional groups of substances. 
• SSNC proposes that the analysis "PFAS total" should be added to the revised 
lists of pollutants in surface water and groundwater. 
• SSNC proposes that the EQS for glyphosate should be lowered. 
• SSNC is positive to the proposal that effect-based methods for estrogenic 
effects should be evaluated with the watch list and recommends that 
additional effect-based measurement methods should be evaluated for other 
types of effects. 
• SSNC suggests that the directives should be updated to enable "Non-target 
screening" methods to be included in future watch lists. This type of method is

important to be able to detect contaminants of emerging concern before they 
have already become a well-evaluated and widespread environmental problem. 
• SSNC is positive to granting the Commission delegated powers to make

article should be added on extended producer responsibility. 
 
Introduction 
To improve the protection of EU citizens, nature and ecosystems in line with 
the EU's Biodiversity Strategy and the EU's Zero Pollution Action Plan the 
European Commission presented on 26 October 2022 proposals on how the 
WFD, GWD and EQSD should be updated. The Commission's ambition is 
primarily to update the lists of pollutants that affect surface and groundwater 
so that they better respond to today's situation. The purpose is also to improve 
the monitoring of mixtures of chemical substances, and to harmonize the 
management of pollution in surface and groundwater. SSNC hereby submits its 
views on the Commission's proposal. 
 
General views 
SSNC is positive about the Commission's overall intentions when it comes to 
increasing protection for both citizens and ecosystems in line with the EU's 
biodiversity strategy and the Zero Pollution Action Plan. SSNC welcomes the 
proposals to put to a range of important water pollutants such as PFAS, 
glyphosate and pharmaceuticals to the lists of priority substances.  
However, SSNC believes that the Commission's proposals are insufficient when 
it comes to the handling of chemical mixtures and contaminants of emerging 
concern. According to the Commission, one aim of updating the directives is to 
improve the monitoring of chemical mixtures and assess combination effects. 
SSNC does not consider that the proposal fulfills these intentions because the 
directives primarily focus on individual substances, which means that the total 
chemical exposure is being underestimated. 
SSNC is positive about the Commission's overall intentions when it comes to 
increasing ability to quickly react to new risk factors, including by making it 
possible to add new pollutants to the lists with the help of delegated acts.

Specific comments 
Water framework Directive (WFD; 2000/60/EC) and structural changes  
SSNC participated in the so-called Fitness check of the WFD in 2019, and then 
argued that the directive fulfills its function well and should not be opened for 
revision. SSNC still believes that the basic objectives of the legislation are 
fulfilled in the WFD, although there is room for improvement when it comes to 
the chemical side. It is therefore important that the changes that are now made 
in the directives are only of a technical nature to improve implementation and 
compliance, as well as to be able to detect new threats that need to be handled. 
Effect-based methods (Article 2) 
SSNC is positive that effect-based methods are now included in Article 2 of the 
WFD when the concept of environmental quality standards (EQS) is defined. 
River basin specific pollutants (Article 2 & 4, Annex V) 
SSNC is positive to the proposal to include river basin specific pollutants 
(RBSPs) in the assessment of chemical and not, as today, in the assessment of 
ecological status of surface water (Article 2 and Annex V). 
SSNC is also positive for the inclusion of RBSPs in Article 4, i.e. that Member 
States shall implement the necessary measures to progressively reduce 
pollution from these substances.  
Exceptional circumstances (Article 3)  
SSNC agrees with the Commission that Article 3 should be expanded to include 
a requirement of member states to immediately inform affected neighboring 
member states if exceptional circumstances, i.e. sudden releases of pollution, 
arise.    
Strategies against pollution of water (Article 16) 
SSNC is hesitant about the Commission's proposal to remove Article 16, which 
is considered outdated. WFD Article 16 contains important proposals to 
decrease water pollution, including requirements for a time frame (Article 16, 
paragraph 6) for eliminating discharges and emissions of hazardous priority 
substances. SSNC would like to emphasize that rules for how pollution in 
surface and groundwater must be reduced are a central part of the WFD.  
The WFD will still be in force after the year 2027, and SSNC wants to emphasize 
the importance of high ambition when it comes to the implementation of the 
WFD also after the year 2027. In addition, SSNC underlines that the lowering of 
emissions required to reduce pollution from priority substances cannot be 
handled in the WFD alone, but actions also need to take place within other 
legislations such as the REACH regulation and the IED directive. In order to 
achieve a clean environment, a holistic approach is required, and the various

EU legislations have to work together to jointly lay the foundation for a toxic-
free environment. Emissions of priority substances and RBSPs should also be 
considered in national permit processes, which unfortunately does not happen 
adequately in Sweden today (Undeman 2022). 
Groundwater directive (GWD; 2006/118/EC) 
SSNC is positive about the proposal for an updated groundwater directive, 
which involves increased ambitions and stricter requirements to counter 
pollution of groundwater. SSNC is also positive to the proposal to make the 
groundwater watch list mandatory. 
Groundwater quality standards (Annex I) 
SSNC supports the proposal to add PFAS24, carbamazepine, sulfamethoxazole, 
the total amount of active pharmaceutical substance, as well as non-relevant 
metabolites of pesticides (nrMs) to the list of substances to be monitored in 
European groundwater, with EU harmonized quality standards. However, SSNC 
propose that another analysis should be added: "PFAS total", which should be 
harmonized with "PFAS total" in the drinking water directive's Annex I Part B. 
See also our comment regarding PFAS in the EQSD section below. 
SSNC considers the classification 'data poor', 'data-fair' and 'data-rich' for nrMs 
unclear and recommend it not be used. Instead, SSNC proposes that the 
directive follows the recommendations in the "European Groundwater 
Memorandum to secure the quality and quantity of drinking water for future 
generations" (IAWR et al. 2022) and establishes an 'intervention value' of 0.05 
μg/L for all substances and degradation products in groundwater. This limit 
value of 0.05 μg/L can be applied as a quality standard for both nrMs and 
individual active substances in pesticides, which today are set too high (0.1 
μg/L) and do not reflect scientific knowledge. 
SSNC interprets that the "total" monitoring in Annex I, i.e. subject line 
2. “Active substances in pesticides, including their relevant metabolites, 
degradation and reaction products - (total)” 
6. “Pharmaceutical active substances – total”  
7. “Non-relevant metabolites of pesticides (nrMs) – (total)”, 
aims to sum up all individually specified substances to the total sum, which 
must not exceed the set quality standard. SSNC believes that these total 
measurements become unclear as no individual pesticides or nrMs are 
included in Annex I, and only two drugs are listed in Annex I. In order for the 
directive to be clear regarding monitoring of "total" amounts, there must be a 
common reference framework for monitoring, as well as an agreement 
regarding which substances are to be monitored and how.

Groundwater pollutants in Annex II 
Primidone has been added to Part B (2), meaning that Member States must now 
consider setting thresholds for that drug. In addition, a Part D has been added 
to Annex II which specifies EU-harmonized threshold values (currently only 
for the sum of Trichlorethylene and Tetrachlorethylene). SSNC supports the 
addition of Primidone to Part B, but believes that the substance shoul d also be 
included in Part D.  
Environmental Quality Standards Directive (EQSD; 2008/105/EC) 
SSNC is positive to the suggested update of the list of priority substances for 
surface water and supports the inclusion of the 23 new substances as well as 
PFAS24 and the total amount of active pesticide substance. 
Since only a very small percentage of all the environmentally harmful 
substances found in our waters are listed as priority substances, SSNC 
suggests to lower all EQS values in 2008/105/EC Annex I by applying a so-called 
"Mixture assessment factor". This is to ensure that the substances, when in 
environmental settings together with substances potentially having 
synergistic effects, do not harm the environment or people’s health. 
Regulation of groups 
SSNC considers it unfortunate that most substances are still regulated one by 
one, each with its own EQS value. SSNC has the opinion that substances should 
be handled in groups to the greatest extent possible. Individual substances can 
easily be replaced by another, similar, substance when the first substance 
begins to be regulated, easily making the list obsolete. If pollutants are treated 
as groups, the total environmental load of polluting substances would still be 
detected. 
Suggested groups to use are antibiotics, estrogenic substances, bisphenols, 
neonicotinoids and pyrethroids, etc. The inclusion of PFAS as a group of 24 
substances is good, but SSNC wants to see that a measurement "PFAS total" is 
also included in the 2008/105/EC Annex I. 
Pesticides (Annex I) 
SSNC considers the EQS of 0.5 µg/L for the total amount of active pesticide 
substances to be high, but acceptable. However, it is not logical that individual 
pesticides can have higher EQS values than the total amount value. No 
individual pesticide EQS should exceed 0.5 µg/L, thus EQS must be lowered for 
Glyphosate and Atrazine.  
Glyphosate is one of Europe's most widely used pesticide proven to have 
harmful effects on both aquatic organisms and for human health (Bruggen et 
al. 2018). SSNC thus welcomes that glyphosate is now proposed to be included 
in the list of priority substances. However, SSNC consider that its main 
metabolite AMPA should also be included in the list. Since glyphosate, already 
  6/10 
 
at low doses (0.3 µg/L), has been shown to affect e.g. behavior in fish (Faria et 
al. 2021), the proposed EQS of 86.7 µg/L and 8.67 µg/L (for inland surface water 
that is not used for drinking water and other surface water, respectively) are 
unreasonably high. SSNC therefore suggests that the EQS for glyphosate and 
AMPA together should be set to a maximum of 0.1 µg/L in all surface water. 
Pharmaceuticals (Annex I) 
SSNC is positive to that pharmaceuticals are added to Annex I, and agrees that 
estrogenic substances, antibiotics, diclofenac and carbamazepine are very 
important to include. These substances can have, even at very low 
concentrations, negative effects on aquatic organisms.  
Industrial chemicals (Annex I) 
SSNC welcomes that a group of 24 PFAS has been added to Annex I with an 
EQS of 4.4 ng/L. However, SSNC suggests that another analysis should be 
added: "PFAS total", e.g. extractable organic fluoride (EOF). This is because 
there are many thousands of different PFASs on the market, with a constant 
influx of more, which risks leading to a substance being replaced by similar 
substances not listed as priority substances in the directive. "PFAS total" in 
2008/105/EC Annex I should be harmonized with "PFAS total" in the drinking 
water directive's Annex I Part B. In this recently updated drinking water 
directive, it is stated that the Commission must establish technical guidelines 
for analytical methods for monitoring per- and polyfluoroalkylated substances 
under the parameter "PFAS total" and "Sum of PFAS" (2020/2184 Article 13 (7)) . 
SSNC supports the addition of Bisphenol A as a priority substance but would 
prefer the entire group of bisphenols to be added to the list because the use of 
Bisphenol A is often replaced by other bisphenols with similar effects on 
ecosystems and human health. 
Metals (Annex I) 
SSNC supports the addition of silver to Annex I.  
River basin specific pollutants (Annex II Part C) 
SSNC supports EU harmonization of EQS values for RBSP since it reduces the 
variation in interpretation and reporting of measured levels between EU 
countries. According to SSNC, additional RBSP should be given EU harmonized 
EQS values. 
For substances classified as RBSP in a Member State, but where there are no 
EQS specified in 2008/105/EC Annex II Part C, it is now proposed through 
2008/105/EC Article 8d to make it mandatory for Member States to set their 
own EQS for RBSP. SSNC supports this proposal.  
Monitoring of ubiquitous PBTs (Article 8a) 
SSNC opposes the addition of Article 8a(2) in 2008/105/EC, which enables 
Member States to reduce the monitoring of ubiquitous persistent, 
  7/10 
 
bioaccumulative and toxic substances (so-called uPBT). SSNC believes that the 
substances concerned (e.g. PFAS, chlorpyrifos, polyaromatic hydrocarbons, 
dioxins, etc.) are important to monitor because they have negative 
environmental effects. SSNC would also like to point out that some of these 
substances, such as PFAS, are still widely used and thus continue to be 
released into nature, and it is therefore of the utmost importance that we 
closely monitor the levels of many different PFAS in Europe's waters to ensure 
that the levels do not increase. 
Monitoring 
In order for the EQSD to be able to fulfill its purpose and provide reliable 
classifications of Europe's water status, and in the long run contribute to better 
water quality, it is important that monitoring takes place in a satisfactory 
manner. SSNC would like to emphasize that the requirements for monitoring 
(2000/60/EC, Annex V, paragraph 1.3.4) must be followed by all member states 
so that levels of priority substances are measured continuously according to 
the specified sampling frequency. 
In addition, SSNC requests requirements for a minimum number of sampling 
points per surface area. SSNC would also like to highlight that since certain 
types of emissions are seasonal (e.g. pesticides), it is important that monitoring 
takes place during the season when the emissions are greatest, so that the 
monitoring does not completely miss emissions that can have significant 
effects on aquatic organisms during the emission period. 
Watch lists 
Microplastics and antibiotic resistance 
SSNC welcomes the inclusion of microplastics and antibiotic resistance genes 
in the directives so that these parameters can be included in the watch lists for 
surface water and groundwater as soon as appropriate methods are available 
(2000/60/EC Annex VIII; 2006/118/EC Article 6a; 2008/105/EC Article 8b). 
Effect-based methods 
SSNC is positive to the suggested use of effect-based methods  examining 
estrogenic effects over a two-year period (2008/105/EC Article 8a). SSNC 
suggests that more effect-based methods, for other effects, should be evaluated 
in the future. This applies to both methods in cell cultures which can indicate 
long-term effects (such as effects on hormone signaling or the ability of nerve 
cells to communicate), and short-term effects on organisms (such as embryo 
vitality, motility of water fleas and growth of algae). 
Being able to measure effects instead of measuring levels of individual 
substances gives a head start in detecting releases of new, yet unmonitored 
  8/10 
 
substances, that can harm the environment and health of organisms. Effect-
based methods also allow mixture effects to be detected (Brack 2019).  
New chemicals 
New substances are constantly being produced and put on the market, and 
some of these will be considered as environmental toxins in the future. It is 
therefore important that these toxic substances are recognized in time and 
that we do not wait for them to spread all over European waters harming for 
example aquatic life before we act. Therefore, we propose that the watch list 
permanently include a non-target screening method so that new potential 
environmental toxins can be detected in time. These screening tests often use 
methods such as mass spectrometry with the advantage that you do not need 
to know what you are looking for. 
SSNC suggests this addition to 2000/60/EC Article 2: 
‘Contaminants of emerging concern’ means substances that may 
pose an environmental or health risk in water bodies but have 
not yet been regulated as ‘priority hazardous substances’, ‘River 
basin specific pollutants’ or been added to the Watch list of 
2008/105/EC.  
‘Non-target screening’ means untargeted screening methods 
enabling detection of new, unexpected and/or unknown organic 
compounds. 
SSNC suggests this addition to 2008/105/EC Article 8b: 
As soon as suitable monitoring methods for micro-plastics and 
selected antimicrobial resistance genes have been identified, 
those substances shall be included in the watch list.  
As soon as suitable methods for non-target screening have been 
selected, an untargeted screening test should be permanently 
included in the watch list.  
Number of subjects on the watch lists 
SSNC believes that the watch list for surface water should not be limited to 10 
substances (Article 8b(1)), and the watch list for groundwater should not be 
limited to five substances (Article 6a(1)). SSNC believes that the watch lists 
should correspond to what is considered important to monitor, and thus there 
should not be an upper limit in the number of subjects. 
The watch list for groundwater contamination becomes mandatory  
SSNC supports the proposal that it becomes mandatory for member states to 
regularly monitor the substances on the groundwater watch list (2006/118/EC 
Article 6a(3)).

Delegated power  
In order to more quickly adapt the directives to current knowledge, SSNC 
supports that the proposals that gives the Commission power to list new 
groundwater pollutants and priority substances via delegated acts, as well as to 
adjust quality standards for these, every 6 years (especially 2006/118/EC Article 
8 and 2008/105/EC Article 8). SSNC also supports that the Commission is 
empowered to adopt implementing acts regarding the watch lists (2006/118/EC 
Article 6a and 2008/105/EC Article 8b), which is a good proposal since it gives 
the Commission mandate to speed up the process of updating lists of relevant 
substances. SSNC believes that it is positive that ECHA is given a central role 
in these processes. 
SSNC considers that there should be a better crosstalk between the EU's 
chemical legislation REACH and the EQSD / GWD. When a substance is entered 
on the candidate list in REACH, this substance should automatically be 
evaluated as to whether it should be listed as a priority substance, groundwater 
pollutant and/or added to a watch list. 
Funding of monitoring 
SSNC believes that the EQSD and GWD should include a new article regarding 
extended producer responsibility (EPR), in line with the "polluter pays 
principle" (PPP). SSNC believes that companies that purchase, use or sell 
environmentally harmful substances, regardless of whether it is as chemical 
substances or mixtures, or as part of a product, must pay for environmental 
monitoring. In order for this to be implemented in a good way, transparency on 
which chemicals are present in products is the key. The incentive to phase out 
environmentally harmful substances will increase if producers and importers 
must pay to put these substances on the market, which will contribute to 
cleaner water. 
A first step can be to use the candidate list of substances of very high concern 
(REACH). Since all companies that buy or sell goods with candidate list 
substances must report to the SCIP database, these companies are known to  
the authority. Similarly, all companies that purchase, use or sell candidate -
listed substances or mixtures must be registered to the authority. Therefore, 
SSNC proposes that all companies that handle substances on the candidate 
list, regardless of whether it is in the form of chemical substances, mixtures, or 
goods, should pay a proportional fee that pays for environmental monitoring 
within the EU.

Certification of Carbon Removals – EU Rules Public consultation response by Value Maritime  Introduction Executive summary: Carbon capture and utilization (CCU) plays an indispensable role in reaching climate goals. Industrial solutions such as offered by VM, provide an excellent, reliable, opportunity to avoid or remove GHG emissions (CO2, CH4, N2O) in a responsible and socially acceptable manner. The EU should set supportive and clear guidelines to enable the creation of new business models in the field of recycling/utilizing captured GHG emissions. By establishing a common standard framework for the certification of industrial carbon avoidance and removal projects the EU would support much-needed initiatives that assist in reaching the climate goals   About Value Maritime: Value Maritime is actively making the shipping industry more sustainable. Through our Filtree unit we not only filter exhausts on ships but also provide carbon capture capabilities. First half of 2023 we will have more than twenty ships sailing in North-Western Europe with our Carbon Capturing units. Making a difference now for the future! Our proven emission-reduction process is an excellent, much-needed, indispensable opportunity for the shipping industry to help reach the climate goals set for 2050.  General background The Paris Agreement is a legally binding international treaty on climate change. Its goal is to limit global warming to well below 2, preferably to 1,5 degrees Celsius, compared to pre-industrial levels. To achieve this long-term temperature goal, countries aim to reach global peaking of greenhouse gas emissions as soon as possible to achieve a climate neutral world by mid-century.  Shipping background The shipping industry, responsible for 3-5% of global GHG emissions, needs to substantially reduce GHG emissions in the coming years. With challenging goals set for the coming years up to 2050. A lot of new rules and regulations are or will be implemented for this purpose.

For a considerable part of the shipping industry the path to adapt to green energy solutions will be a long one. It is still unclear what the preferable solution for the future of shipping will be. There seem to be many thoughts and ideas, but first movers might have the disadvantage here. Considering the huge capital required, shipowners will be reluctant to move quickly. Furthermore, the lifespan of a vessel still lies between 25-40 years. A recent report by McKinsey describes the problem as follows: “Moving too early may saddle the business with an uncompetitive cost structure. The regulatory environment remains murky and uncertain. In this context, ‘wait and see’ seems more prudent.” New builds can replace barely 3% of the outstanding market on a yearly basis. Even these new builds are mostly unfit to handle ‘renewable fuels’. The shipping industry is facing a very tough challenge, but one that needs to be tackled as quickly as possible.   Carbon capture utilization and storage To reach the climate goals set under the Paris Agreement Value Maritime believes it is important to look at a broad spectrum of mitigating opportunities. carbon capture utilization is now an available technology for the shipping industry as well.   Making a Difference now for the future Our carbon capture systems on board of ships are already being used. It is an on-board solution that already helps to support reduction of GHG emission, in a cost and energy effective way. The process is focused on capturing carbon using only a minimum of energy to maximize the positive effect for the environment. Our filter unit (Filtree) for instance has a neglectable energy usage. Calculations are based on reports by objective (governmental or government linked) institutions, validated, verified, and approved by recognized institutions.   Utilization of captured carbon The Captured Carbon is or will be re-used in, for instance, the horticultural sector to help grow crop, as feedstock to produce green methanol or the carbon is stored underground. In this way we can already make a difference now, while waiting for the availability of sufficient supply of alternative fuels and suitable vessels to make the change needed to reach the climate goals.  By re-using captured carbon, we can avoid the need for extracting fossil fuels from the ground/soil/earth. The VM-way avoids the same amount of carbon to be emitted into the atmosphere. Keeping carbon in the ground (and avoid other GHG emissions), instead of burning more fossil fuels, capturing the CO2, and then storing it underground again.  Regulators are not only looking at CO2 emissions but all GHG emissions. With our emission avoidance strategy, we prevent emission of Methane and N2O as well. Limiting the footprint even further.

 Another perhaps even more promising utilization of captured carbon is using it as feedstock to produce green methanol. This is a solution that is feasible and currently available albeit at a cost that needs to be brought down, through investments, to make it commercially viable. This method can be applied in engines of current vessels with only minor adjustments. There is absolutely no need to wait for new-builds to arrive to the market. Green methanol can make a difference faster and help to reduce the emittance of CO2 at a more realistic or even faster pace.  Within the light of reducing GHG emissions in shipping, as soon as possible, our proven concept of carbon capture technology in combination with a range of avoidance and/or removal strategies should be supported by the relevant authorities.  By adopting clear regulations our proven CCU projects will be eligible to receive the certificates to support growth and thus making sure avoiding and utilizing CO2/GHG emissions can attribute to reaching the climate goals set.  Discussion  Carbon capture Considering the above-mentioned arguments, we think the regulatory bodies should have a favorable position towards technologies that can contribute to making a difference for the climate during the transition period. By allowing this type of (readily) available solutions to be included within the regulatory framework, immediate steps can be taken on the path to reach the climate goals. In recent responses to IMO by Liberia (1), South-Korea (2), Norway (3) and China (4) carbon capture is presented as an indispensable part in tackling the climate challenge. We notice that CCU is gaining more and more traction among the relevant authorities. Your support is needed to make sure this and other, current, and future technologies can further develop to support cutting emissions.   Utilization and/or storing Capturing carbon is a very useful way to prevent it from getting emitted into the atmosphere. There is an increasing number of possibilities to handle CO2 once captured. Regulators and relevant authorities tend to focus solely on storing carbon underground as the way forward. Value Maritime is of the opinion that storing captured carbon, from ships, underground is certainly a way to remove GHG emissions from the atmosphere. We see however that avoiding emitting CO2 (or establishing a circular process) is a much more logical and preferable way to deal with carbon when regulated by a robust legislative framework. Certainly, for as long as we don’t have the large-scale availability of green fuels and ships that can sail on these new types of fuels.

Well-to-Wake  When discussing GHG emissions it is vital, in our opinion, to look at it from a Well-to-Wake perspective. It is good to see discussions on this topic are included within the MEPC meetings. Where there seems to be a preference for WTW. We support this, as only then are we able to talk about a genuine level-playing field. From reports that have calculated WTW emissions for several fuels show that using regular fuels with our carbon capture technology gives the best available, realistic option in todays’ world. Making a difference now while we work on future options to cut GHG emissions even further.  Supporting innovative technologies As said, relevant authorities’ focus seems to be on storing emitted CO2 underground as can be read in most regulations/publications. Even though it seems that discussions on other options are opening, like this consultation. If the solution with carbon capture is focused solely on storing underground, this may stall existing as well as future technological solutions, impeding potentially promising innovations in this field which are or would have been able to effectively reduce GHG emissions worldwide quicker.   Solutions/requests • Support innovative technologies We would like to suggest you consider including carbon capture utilization technology in the relevant regulatory framework as soon as possible.  Should the regulators allow for a broad definition it will allow for, not only, current but also future technologies/innovations to be accepted by regulations to help/assist in reducing GHG emissions. Carbon capture utilization is an excellent opportunity to assist in reducing GHG emissions. We call upon you to not only look at storage, but actively support utilization as well, to help reach climate goals.  • Compare on basis of well-to-wake The preferred Well-to-Wake calculation method proves that this offers the most realistic, most effective way to help reach the climate goals until other options like green fuels become readily available in the future. • Include in calculations As carbon capture makes a significant impact on reducing GHG emissions, not only CO2. We suggest including this into the relevant regulations and formulas used. Value Maritime proposes to add a correction factor to GHG emission calculations to include the reduction of GHG emissions with the CCUS technology solutions.

Call to action request We call upon you to lead the way and support innovations like carbon capture utilization by including these methodologies in regulatory frameworks or facilitate for intermediate measures. We strongly advise you to include on-board carbon capture solutions for reducing GHG emissions. And give guidance on accounting, verifying, certifying, auditing, validating issues in the field of captured carbon and its usage.  Given the goals set and the ambitious timeframe, regulations, or the development of regulations, should not act as a barrier, but should promote and facilitate this range of solutions and ensure they will make a difference and help to achieve the 1,5C goal and reduce GHG emissions under the Paris Agreement. All promising measures to reduce CO2 should be considered and we ask you to support the inclusion of removed or avoided CO2 emissions in the relevant equations.  Closing statement We are looking forward to your response to this opinionating document and are available to answer any questions or remarks you may have.

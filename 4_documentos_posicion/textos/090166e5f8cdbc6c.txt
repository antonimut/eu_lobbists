Cefic views on the revision of lists of pollutants 
affecting surface and groundwaters 
 
Cefic takes note of the European Commission proposal to update the 
list of pollutants affecting surface and groundwaters  and welcomes 
the changes to the watch lists mechanism and subsequent timeframe 
for implementation.  Cefic does, however,  call for better alignment 
with EU legislations, enhanced use of standard methodologies, and a 
better framed role for ECHA. 
We believe that the proposed watch lists would improve the monitoring of substances in groundwater 
(Art 6a GWD; Art 8b EQSD) and, eventually, would contribute to improving the quality of water bodies 
and reducing pollution at source . We also appreciate that the monitoring and review cycle of the 
Watch List mechanism is set for 3 years instead of 2 years as this would give more time for monitoring, 
thorough data-analysis and help to identify the substances of emerging concern which could pose a 
risk at EU level.  
Cefic remains concerned with some of the aspects of this proposal as reflected below.  
Preserve coherence with existing legislations 
Cefic supports the prioritization process of the priority substance lists for surface and groundwater 
being based on  risk assessment , like in the previous revisions. It should be avoided that the  
prioritization is based solely on the chemical properties of a substance as this will not reflect the risk 
to the aquatic environment. The listing of a substance should entail multiple factors including: 
monitoring efforts, technical measures and a toxicological assessment; focussing on substances that 
represent a risk to or via the aquatic environment.  
Also, coherence with existing EU legislations  (like REACH, IED or the Plant Protection Products 
Regulation) should be ensured. A few substances that were extensively monitored in the past, have 
meanwhile already undergone measures to reduce the risk. Therefore, any new measures stemming 
from the new lists should not overlap with other EU-wide measures adopted over the past 15 years 
and that have been clearly delivering on preventing EU-wide risks to the aquatic environment. 
A better framed role for ECHA 
In the proposal, the European Chemicals Agency (ECHA) received a formal role in the process taking 
over the prioritization process for both surface and groundwater  which has been previously carried 
out by the Joint Research Centre ( JRC) and the Scientific Committee on Health, Environmental and 
Emerging Risks (SCHEER) . We welcome that ECHA scientific reports needs to  take into 
considerations the opinion of its Committee for Risk Assessment and the Committee for Socio -
Economic Analysis, monitoring results and the input provided by stakeholders (Art 8.2 GWD and Art 
8 EQSD).

It should be ensured that sufficient expertise is available at ECHA and in its Committees1 to carry 
out the development or revision of Environmental Quality Standards (EQS) and Watch List . In 
addition, it should be ensured that the process is transparent and that enough time  is allocated to 
assess the scientific information and for stakeholders to contribute to the process. 
More coherence in methodologies used to derive new Standards 
Concerning the methodology to identify and derive  EQS for the priority substances and pollutants , 
ECHA should use the methodology developed within the Common Implementation Strategy  and 
published in the Technical Guidance Document No. 27 on EQS. In addition,  ECHA sh ould use the 
‘Spatial Temporal Extent’ (STE) methodology 2 to define and rank the substances of EU wide concern  
applied by the JRC, which is a science-based, and systematic screening methodology for substance 
prioritization.  
The priority substances list (for surface waters) and pollutants list (for groundwater, Annex I of the 
GWD) should only include substances that are of EU-wide relevance and for which EU-wide risks are 
clearly identified through monitoring data. It must be ensured that monitoring data are representative 
for all Member States. Hence,  Annex I should not include local incidents, and/or targeted hot-spot 
data as they are of limited use for identifying the EU -wide risk  and they should be handled 
appropriately at the local level  in a River Basin Management Plan as the ‘Specific Pollutants’ 
mentioned. In addition, if there are no longer obvious cases of EU-wide risks as the chemical risks have 
been addressed, these substances should be removed from the priority list.  
In addition, the revision of the priority lists should only be done in cases where there is sound evidence 
that high EU risk still exists. These should include impact assessments of the technical evaluation  as 
well as a cost versus benefit evaluation . There must also  be clear procedures for when substances 
will be deselected as priority substances or substances in Annex I and II of the GWD.  
Aiming for an enhanced EQS-River basin interplay 
We welcome the first step to harmonise EQS of river basin specific  pollutants by introducing the 
repository of harmonised EQS in Annex II, part C of the EQS -Directive. However, it must be ensured  
that these substances listed in Annex II part C are assessed only if there is a local concern and if listed 
as River Basin Specific Pollutant by competent national authorities. It must be clarified that the list in 
Annex II Part C is not an add-on to Annex I of the EQSD. 
We are concerned about the  inclusion of River Basin Specific Pollutants in the assessment of the 
Chemical Status . River Basin Specific Pollutants and their EQS are set inconsistently across the 
Member States, which will make the assessment of the chemical status noncomparable. We suggest 
a different evaluation of River Basin Specific Pollutants and Priority Substances  is applied in the 
determination of the chemical status. For instance, this could be done  by including a more nuanced 
assessment of the chemical status of a water body  (good/not good). This would also account for the 
fact that River Basin Specific Pollutants have only been supportive elements for the ecological status 
in the past. This “supporting” status should be retained for the future. The “one out – all out” principle 
prevents the visibility of the improvements made in the chemical status during the last few decades 
and should be revised. The chemical status is also related to the ability to receive permits for projects. 
In the proposal , Articles 16 and 17 of the Water Framework Dire ctive have been deleted  on the 
procedure by which the European Commission was required to establish legislative proposals for the

• PFAS  
Cefic supports balanced regulatory measures on PFAS . We are concerned with the EQS values 
proposed for PFAS as these are setting uniform values for surface water and groundwater in line with 
the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA)’s opinion on drinking water limits. The objectives and the 
exposures of drinking water for human consumption and mere presence in the environment  are 
different. For those rivers which are not classified for drinking water use, we recommend to refer to 
the EQS freshwater/biotas, which are designed to protect the environment and humans exposed via 
fish consumption. This was also concluded in the SCHEER opinion on PFAS : ‘for surface water as a 
source for drinking water use, an AA-QS for water (QSdw, hh) should be derived using the EFSA’s TWI 
as TLhh, which would correspond to a provisional QSdw, hh of 4.4 ng.L-1(0.0044 μg.L-1).’ In addition, 
SCHEER endorsed limits for the AA-EQS eco (freshwater) for rivers which are not used as a source of 
drinking water in the SCHEER Opinio n. However, while this difference was not included in the 
proposal, it  was applied for other substances . Hence, for robustness of the scientific case,  we 
recommend that this distinction should be also applied for PFAS. 
As expressed in the  SCHEER opinion, we share concerns  on how data is extrapolated to the other 
end points. The  EQS  for human health and biota is  based on TWI  (Tolerable  Weekly  Intake)  derived  
from EFSA on 4 long chain PFAS (PFOA , PFOS, PFNA and PFHxS) for which the endpoint is the effect 
on human immune  system  depression. This  value  is  then  used  to  compare  the  limits  after  the  
application  of  the Relative Potency Factor (RPF) which is derived on the basis of hepatotoxicity, and 
the reference substance is only 1 compound - PFOA. It  is  not clear  if  other  PFAS  are  characterised  
by  immunological  effects  and  how  this  effect  is comparable with the liver toxicity.  There is not 
enough data in the EQS dossier to assess the reliability of the value.  
We observed that the proposal does not consider the availability of analytical methods and 
feasibility of implementing the proposed values. More specifically there are concerns that the  Limit 
of Quantification (LOQ) of analytical methods are not consistent with the proposed values  for biota 
and water (groundwater/surface water). The analytical issues and the interpretation of the values of 
the analyses needs to be clarified before final values are endorsed. Due to the very low proposed limit 
values the issue of the effective detectability of the PFAS substance in water and in other matrices 
must also be addressed.  Given the extremely low limits, in particular in the case of “sum of PFOA 
equivalent”, LOQs at the same order of magnitude as the limits could make the compliance with the 
limits impossible: When the analytical values for several PFAS is less than LOQ, if each “ non-detection” 
is accounted in full (=LOQ) or even partially (=LOQ/2 ), the sum of all these contributes might even 
exceed the limit itself, even in “analytical” absence of the PFAS. Hence, we consider that clear rules 
must be defined to manage the case in which the quantity of substance under analysis is lower than 
the LOQ  associated with the analytical method used . It is further understood that at this time , 
widespread, commercially available techniques of laboratory analysis across the EU cannot achieve 
the desired LOQ’s in one test method.

plausible that current background concentrations of some PFAS-compounds in major European

• Bisphenol A (BPA) 
We are concerned about the limit values proposed for BPA as these were set based on a draft EFSA 
scientific opinion. The proposed amendment of annex 1 to directive 2008/105/EC foresees to add a 
surface water AA-EQS of 0.000034 µg/L for BPA. This proposal is based on the draft EFSA opinion on 
BPA published in December 2021, that proposed a Tolerable Daily Intake (TDI) which is currently under 
revision by EFSA . In fact, European and International Authorities and Institutes expressed 
disagreement with the basis and the conclusion  of the draft EFSA opinion on BPA  from December 
2021. In addition, SCHEER stated in its final scientific opinion5 that the critical EQS should be revised if 
the underlying draft TDI is not confirmed. Cefic supports this way forward and considers that a  
proposed legislative threshold such as an EQS should not be based on a draft scientific evaluation. 
Taking the fact that the EQS for BPA is supported by a controversial DRAFT opinion of EFSA, Cefic 
opinion is that this EQS should be withdrawn and the Commiss ion proposal be re-assessed based on 
recent scientific development. 
Cefic looks forward to sharing its ideas and exploring potential policy solutions with the institutions  
in due course.

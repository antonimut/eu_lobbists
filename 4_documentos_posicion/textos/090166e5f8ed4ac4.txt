Proposal for a Directive of the European Parliament and of the Council on 
ambient air quality and cleaner air for Europe 
General 
The revision of the Air Quality Directive and its daughter directives aims to merge them into 
one directive in order to bring EU air quality standards more in line with WHO 
recommendations, further improve the regulatory framework and provide more support to local 
authorities in their efforts to achieve cleaner air. The proposal is thus intended to contribute to 
the implementation of the zero-pollution objective and the targets of the Zero Air Quality Action 
Plan for the protection of health and the environment. 
At the same time, against the background of the goal of greenhouse gas neutrality, the steel 
industry in Germany is determined to make a success of the transformation towards climate-
neutral and sustainable steel production. To achieve this, the steel industry's production 
facilities must be completely restructured, planned, newly approved and built in large parts. In 
addition, plants for the generation of renewable energies and hydrogen as well as the 
necessary transport infrastructures must be built. The transformation will thus take place in 
stages. The ambitious climate goals require a strongly accelerated time frame. If possible, the 
first plants should go into operation as early as the middle of the decade. 
Assessment 
Air quality in Europe has not only improved greatly in recent years due to ambitious measures, 
but will continue to improve due to ongoing measures. Improving air quality is necessary, but 
revising the current directive at this stage is not. 
A large number of legislative projects that the EU is already pursuing simultaneously will have 
a direct and indirect positive impact on air quality. These are, in particular, the regulations on 
climate protection, which will lead to a strong reduction in the use of fossil fuels and thus also 
to a decrease in particulate matter and nitrogen oxide pollution in the air. With its ongoing 
transformation, the steel industry will also contribute massively both to climate protection and, 
in the long term, to air quality by eliminating entire process stages and plant components. 
However, these measures will only have a concrete mitigating effect in the later course of the 
transformation. Further regulations with regard to the emission behaviour of vehicles, the 
electrification of the European vehicle fleets with strict limits on the placing on the market of 
vehicles with combustion engines, as well as the requirements for the energy efficiency of 
buildings will additionally improve air quality in the coming years. 
The proposal for a directive pursues the interest of protecting health as far as possible without 
carefully balancing this with other objectives of the Union such as ensuring mobility, 
maintaining competitiveness and jobs, and ensuring sufficient financial strength of the

municipalities. It can therefore be assumed that the proposed amendments would exacerbate 
the already existing enforcement problems in the transition from the beginning of the 
transformation to its end and the removal of the corresponding old plants. Accordingly, EU 
clean air plans would be required in many places as early as 2030, which the emitters

Against this background, the directive consultation should be suspended for further impact and 
proportionality assessment. The new and significantly lowered limit values must generally not 
apply before 2040.  
It is therefore imperative to take into account that a successful transformation of industry is the 
prerequisite for an effective plant-related emission reduction and corresponding air pollution 
control. Therefore, the already initiated transformation process must not be burdened by 
tightening limit values at the wrong time. An adapted air quality directive with strict limit values 
could build on the above-mentioned regulatory projects and transformation as a catch-all 
directive to complete the air quality effort in the EU. 
Critical points in detail 
If the Commission, the Council and the European Parliament want to stick to the legislative 
project and the current timetable, the following points of the draft directive are to be criticised: 
 A regulatory codification of the WHO rules and regulations is fundamentally rejected. The 
EU has no access to the WHO regulations and no influence on the future content of these 
regulations. The WHO guidelines merely reflect the scientific findings without any claim to 
assess the feasibility of implementation in practice. A general proportionality test is 
completely missing. 
 With the draft directive, the limit values would also already apply in the non-exceedance 
areas when the directive enters into force, which would significantly complicate the ability 
of Member States to act and implement projects. The further fuzzy obligation on Member 
States to maintain "the best ambient air quality and a high level of environmental and human 
health protection in line with the air quality guidelines published by the WHO" not only 
creates legal uncertainty, but thus also delays or prevents projects in non-surplus areas. 
This must be avoided. 
 All limit values of Annex 1 should only be complied with from 2040 at the earliest. Even 
2040 is a very ambitious date against the background that the member states must apply 
the directive in 2026 at the earliest. The implementation deadlines for the Air Quality 
Directive should rather be oriented towards the implementation of measures such as 
electromobility, hydrogen transformation, as 2030 is not the time to foresee successes with 
regard to air quality. A timely application of the values in Annex 1 should at best be regulated 
by target values in order not to miss an implementability. 
 Exemptions must be extended to disproportionate measures in exceedance areas with a 
clear specification of exceedance periods, such as 10 years.

 Measures in clean air plans may only be directed at those polluters who also make a 
significant contribution to the exceedance. This must be regulated more concretely. 
 Once the directive comes into force, there will be a de facto obligation to draw up clean air 
plans, as the future limit values would be exceeded across the board in almost all areas of 
Europe. The resulting massive legal uncertainties in the approval of projects throughout 
Europe must be prevented. A new and convincing concept is urgently needed on how to 
approach compliance with the limit values without generating negative impacts on the 
economy, mobility and infrastructure in the EU (extended do-not- significant-harm 
approach). 
 For installations within the scope of the Industrial Emissions Directive, investments in 
industrial sites must not be jeopardised with a view to the industrial transformation 
necessary for climate protection. Remediation plans in exceedance areas, agreed with the 
competent authority and linked to the transformation plans, could ensure a reduction of air 
pollution so that further measures based on the air quality directives as well as costly 
procedures to obtain exemptions are excluded. Accordingly, they must already be fully 
taken into account in clean air plans. 
 Compensation for injury to health and also an extension of the possibilities for legal action 
should be deleted, as they will lead to further uncertainty on the part of the authorities and 
will have the effect that measures with the consequence of incorrect decisions will not be 
sufficiently weighed up merely to avoid compensation. The proposal of regulations by which 
natural and legal persons in particular can be prosecuted with very substantial penalties is 
rejected. There is no evidence that natural or legal persons are significantly responsible for 
the failure to meet air quality objectives. The existing regulations are sufficient, but should 
not be restricted.

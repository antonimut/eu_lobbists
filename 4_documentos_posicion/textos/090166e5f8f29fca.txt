Position paper  
Proposal for an EU Directive harmonizing certain 
aspects of insolvency law   
 
The EOS Group is one of the leading international providers of receivables management. It is EOS’ 
goal to ensure that defaulting payers become debt -free and that our clients receive payments 
on their  overdue claims as quickly as possible. With over 6,000 employees in Europe, EOS offers 
20,000 customers worldwide financial security through tailor -made services in the B2B and B2C 
sectors. Ou r core business is supplying companies with liquidity, improving the stability of the 
economic cycle and by doing so, securing investments and jobs.  
 
In view of the persistent divergences between national insolvency regimes  across the EU , 
creating obstacles to cross-border investments and to the free movement of capital, EOS Group 
welcomes the European Commission’s proposal of December 7 th, 2022 for a Directive 
harmonising certain aspects of insolvency law . From a creditor’s point of view, a 
harmonisation of  insolvency regimes can significantly improve the management of non-
performing loans , which are at the core of our business.  
 
As the proposal refer s to corporate insolvencies, EOS Group is interested in providing input on 
the Directive from an operational industry  perspective. Most of the provisions in the draft are 
corresponding to our business in key sectors such as banking, utilities, real estate, and e -
commerce. Above all , we perceive positively that the Directive foresees easier access to 
account registries and national asset registers for insolvency practitioners. Based on our 
experience, this might apply particularly to the access to registers that may contain information 
on "assets" such as real estate property which are held by individuals in another country. Likewise, 
we welcome the implementation and link of auction platforms  across Member States .  
 
However, EOS Group sees a need for fine-tuning the proposal  regarding the clarity of 
certain provisions and potential loopholes provided therein :  
 
1) Title II - Avoidance Actions 
 
Regarding the declaration for invalidity of a legal act  (Article 6, (2) (b)), EOS Group would like 
to bring to your attention that this provision might expand the possibilities for avoidance actions . 
Article 6 (2) (b) stipulates that a legal act can be declared void, if – in addition to further 
prerequisites – a creditor knew, or should have known , that the debtor was unable to pay its 
mature debts or that a request for the opening of insolvency proceedings has been submitted. In 
several EU jurisdictions  it is not sufficient for the creditor to only “should have known” that the

expand the possibilities for avoidance actions and the risk of avoidance actions for creditors.  The

Article 9 , stipulating general c onsequences of avoidance actions , foresees in paragraph 4 a 
possibility to assign claims to obtain full compensation  to a party other than the insolvency 
administrator. Regarding the possibility to assign claims to obtain full compensation pursuant to 
paragraph 2, first subparagraph to a creditor or a third party (Article 9 (4)), it seems questionable 
whether these third parties act exclusively in the interest of (all of) the creditors. In our view, it 
would be preferable for the insolvency administrator to assert these claims exclusively.  In the 
interest of safeguarding all creditors’ r ights and interests equally, we recommend not to 
allow an assignment of claims to any third party  unless in cases where all creditors agreed 
to such an assignment in writing.  
 
2) Title VI – Winding-up of insolvent Microenterprises  
 
Considering the Directive’s main objective of harmoni sing insolvency regimes across the EU, EOS 
Group would like to underline that the term “insolvency estate”  in Article 48 is not sufficiently 
clear. Despite the definition of the insolvency estate in Article 48 (2), Article 48 (3) stipulates that 
the competent authority or, if appointed, the insolvency practitioner specifies which assets are 
excluded from the insolvency estate . Prerequisites for an exclusion are not regulated.  In the 
interest of all creditors, we recommend to add the prerequisites for an exclusion  to avoid 
that different interpretations of the scope of an insolvency estate are applied by the competent 
authority or the insolvency administrator.  
Regarding the discharge from debts in simplified winding -up proceedings, it is currently not 
clear from the proposal (Article 56) whether e.g. entrepreneur debtors  are fully discharged from 
all their debts (business and private). In the interest of harmonisation of insolvency regimes  
and especially to avoid several proce edings, we recommend to regulate the scope of 
discharge. 
 
3) Title VII – Creditors’ Committee 
 
Article 63, stipulating working method s of the creditors' committee , foresees in paragraph (1) 
that the creditors’ committee lays down a protocol of working methods within 15 working days 
following the appointment of the members . Furthermore, the protocol of working methods of the 
creditors’ committee should address among other things the matter of  conflict of interests  
(Article 63 (2)). In the interest of more efficient and predictable proceedings, we 
recommend to reduce the time period for laying down the protocol of working methods  
(specific suggestion: five working days)  and to define the term “conflict of interests ” 
more concretely .

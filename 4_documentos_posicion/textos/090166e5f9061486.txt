The proposed new directive is characterized by the fact that it is the most vulnerable and burdened 
areas that set the standard for what measures are to be implemented. As the directive is a minimum 
directive, this may have unfortunate consequences in that unnecessarily stringent requirements are 
imposed on several areas. 
  
The Directive should to a greater extent refer to the Water Framework Directive so that, on the basis 
of the Water Framework Directive, strict requirements are set where necessary, and less stringent 
requirements where the recipient has a high tolerance. 
  
The directive imposes unrealistic demands on deadlines.  This is especially true for areas where there 
is a deadline for the first 50% facilities. 
  
The directive imposes unrealistic and unnecessary requirements for sampling. For facilities over 
100,000 pe, daily samples must be taken (365 daily mixed samples or 52 weekly mixed samples). This 
implies a zero tolerance for errors in sampling. The measure is both expensive and unnecessarily 
extensive. There is a lack of a good scientific justification for setting such extensive requirements.  
 
Consideration should be given to whether requirements for all parameters should be set on an annual 
basis, rather than a specific number of annual daily tests that must comply with requirements. As it is 
now, samples from single days can lead to cleaning requirements not being met, despite good annual 
cleaning performance.  
 
Alternative emission control and documentation must be permitted so that sampling requirements do 
not prevent the use of nature-based solutions, such as infiltration. Alternative methods for 
documenting the degree of abatement should also be permitted, such as on-line meters or the use of 
other operating parameters. This will reduce the need for conventional sampling. 
 
Article 11 of the Directive proposes the achievement of energy neutrality at national level for facilities 
larger than 10,000 pe. It must be made clear how the energy balance is calculated, so that, among other 
things, energy produced outside the treatment plant itself, on the basis of sludge from the treatment 
plant, also is included. 
  
There are also increased requirements for sampling for heavy metals and environmental toxins. 
Already today, there is a lack of capacity for accredited laboratories to carry out such analyses. The

proposal in the directive hardly makes it easier. Experience has also shown that many of these 
parameters have concentrations that are below the quantification limit for commercially accredited 
laboratories. As an alternative, a solution should be proposed by taking analyses from the sludge for 
many of the substances in question. 
  
The proposal to increase the nitrogen purification requirement to 85% is not sustainable as a general 
minimum requirement. For many recipients, this is unnecessarily strict. For urban wastewater, 
nitrogen purification takes place exclusively with the help of biological processes.  And in a cold 
climate, such as the Norwegian one, this will entail a sharp increase in the volumes of the treatment 
plants and in the consumption of external carbon sources.  Our treatment plant is located in the 
southern part of Norway (Tønsberg). Although we have a mild climate according to Norwegian 
conditions, the average temperature of incoming wastewater is 11-11.5 degrees C over the past 6 
years. This means that biological processes become very demanding. 
  
Micro-contaminants are planned to have a cleaning effect of 80% for selected substances. In many 
cases, the inlet concentration will be very low. Then 80% is difficult to achieve. The requirement 
should be set in the same way as for organic matter, in that one must satisfy an outlet concentration or 
a % requirement. It is important that the presumption that the producer must pay is maintained by not 
introducing the abatement requirement until the national regulations for collecting costs from the 
producers are in place. 
  
A requirement to reduce emissions via overflow to less than 1% based on dry weather runoff is 
considered unrealistic and should not be included in the directive.

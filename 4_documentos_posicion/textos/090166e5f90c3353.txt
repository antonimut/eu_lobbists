Response to consultation on the revised list of surface pollutants under the Water 
Framework Directive (WFD) 
Summary  
• Silver is currently proposed for inclusion in WFD's list of priority substances (the PS list).1  
• There is however strong evidence that: 
A. the significant costs associated with including silver in the PS list will far outweigh 
its benefits, rendering the proposal disproportionate; and 
B. the proposal to include silver in the PS list is unscientific and contains critical errors. 
• Of critical concern is the Impact Assessment2 (IA)'s manifestly unsound conclusion that the 
high costs of the proposal can be justified due to an alleged benefit from the prevention of 
antimicrobial resistance (AMR). 
• Including silver on the PS list , without having demonstrated a serious risk, means major 
costs will be incurred without bringing any real benefit to the aquatic environment or the 
health of EU citizens. This is disproportionate and an unjustifiable use of resources. 
 
Recommendation:  
The inclusion of silver in the PS list and the EQS Directive3 should be omitted from the WFD proposal. 
A more proportionate and practical step would be to provide for the monitoring of any 
development of microbial genes resistant to silver in the next Watch list (the possibility of which is 
now provided for under Article 8b of the proposed EQS Directive). 
 
Accompanying appendix 
In addition to the points below, the attached appendix includes a technical critique demonstrating  
that: (a) the risk that surface water concentrations of silver will lead to increased AMR is 
exaggerated and that; (b) the IA justifying inclusion of silver as a PS contains significant errors.

• There is no evidence that silver can  actually induce AMR at the extremely low concentrations 
found in the environment. Any concern is speculative – it is based on extrapolating the effects 
achieved in laboratory experiments exposing microbes to high concentrations.12  
• The threat of AMR was not used to set the EQS for silver13  and has instead been inserted very late 
into the process and without proper justification.  
• Measuring silver in surface water to monitor EQS compliance  is not appropriate for the AMR

a better use of resources to measure any concentrations of resistant genes in the environment as

The IA already notes that the costs of inclusion outweigh the benefits.17 But inextricably it still judges 
them to be comparable.18 Once the supposed benefits for AMR are excluded, that position becomes 
untenable. It highlights just how clearly disproportionate the inclusion of silver in the PS list would be.  
B. The proposal to include silver as a PS is unscientific and contains critical errors.  
1) Robust scientific and technical data prove that there are insufficient grounds for silver to be 
added to the PS list, because it poses only a low risk to or via the aquatic environment.  
• In accordance with the relevant legislation, only "substances found to pose a significant risk" 
should be considered for inclusion on the Priority Substances list.  
• The criterion for shortlisting candidate priority substances based on risk classification is a 
Spatial, Temporal and Extent of PNEC exceedances (STE) score of ≥ 1.8. JRC has concluded that 
based on the available monitoring data the revised STE score for silver is 0.9 19, and would 
according to JRC's own guidance, this is considered "low risk".20 
• Recent published reports also show that the PNEC/EQS21, and the assessment of the available 
monitoring data used to derive even this revised STE score22, were conservative.  
Selecting priority substances for which a high risk has not been demonstrated means major costs will 
be incurred while bringing little to no benefit to the aquatic environment. 
2) An alleged concern related to AMR cannot be used to justify the proposal.  
• The alleged concern related to silver related AMR is only discussed very briefly in the silver 
EQS dossier and is insufficiently investigated.  
• As explained above, there is no evidence supporting the conclusion that silver can actually 
induce AMR at the extremely low concentrations found in the environment.  
• Also AMR effects attributed to nanosilver are used in the IA to justify the inclusion of silver as 
a PS for surface water, but this is not scientifically supported. Individual nanosilver materials 
have unique properties, which are different from silver.  
3) The proposed EQS of 10 ng/l is not scientifically justified and will lead to practical problems 
in monitoring such data. 
The dataset used to derive the current proposed EQS exceeds the requirements of  the relevant 
guidance by covering more test species than required.23

Moreover, the analytical methods are not sufficiently sensitive to allow concentrations of silver to be 
monitored at the low concentrations required by this very conservative EQS. Commission guidance24 
requires a method to have limits of quantification (LOQ) of equal to or below a value of 30% of the 
relevant EQS. Silver monitoring data reported In the MEC section in the EQS dossier for silver  shows 
that less than 20% of non-quantified samples were reported with an LOQ below the proposed EQS of 
10 ng/L, highlighting the practical difficulties in applying such a standard.  
4) The IA assessment is deficient 
• The stated increase in the use of silver25 is not supported by the available data and is not reflected 
in the monitoring data used to set the EQS. Temporal trends based on monitoring data show a 
decline in surface water concentrations with supply and demand for silver static over the same 
periods.26 
• The inclusion of silve r as a priority substance for surface water is based on a limited and highly 
selective interpretation of the available data concerning AMR. No consideration was given to 
studies reflecting a scarcity of reported silver resistance in order to develop a comp lete and 
balanced picture of the contribution of silver to AMR 
• Several statements in respect of the AMR concern for silver , including the level of the threat 
posed and their comparability with pharmaceuticals, are baseless (see Appendix for examples).  
Recommendation:  
The inclusion of silver in the PS list and the EQS Directive should be omitted from the WFD because of 
the absence of a significant risk and because the costs of inclusion are disproportionate bearing in the 
mind the possible benefits.  
A more proportionate and practical step would instead be to provide for the monitoring of any 
development of microbial genes resistant to silver in the next Watch list (the possibility of which would 
now be provided for under Article 8b of the proposed EQS Directive).

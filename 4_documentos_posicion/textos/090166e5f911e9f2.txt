European Biochar Industry Consortium’s (EBI) contribution to the EU public 
consultation on the proposal for the Urban WasteWater Treatment Directive 
Revision (COM(2022) 541 final) 
The European Biochar Industry Consortium (EBI) fully endorses the Commission ’s proposed revision 
of the Urban Wastewater Treatment Directive, which aims to better protect the environment and 
human health from wastewater pollution. By aligning with the European Green Deal's broader 
objectives, the Directive Revision  also addresses the current climate crisis and environmental 
degradation. 
As the Directive is laying the groundwork  for an enhanced and more sustainable sewage sludge 
treatment (Article 20 of the COM(2022) 542 final) , EBI calls for the Commission's consideration of 
pyrolysis as a sewage sludge treatment option for valorization and circularity. EBI urges policymakers 
to consider the potential of pyrolysis and biochar application to enhance the transition of the 
wastewater sector, in terms of sustainab ility improvement, in the areas of resource recycling and 
preservation, pollutants removal, energy neutrality, climate mitigation and adaptation strategies1. 
Biochar, a porous carbonaceous material that stores carbon as a long -term carbon sink, has a wide  
range of applications from agriculture to climate -friendly materials. Its benefits make it a cost -
effective technology solution that shows significant potential for waste and nutrient recovery, energy 
efficiency, and pollution control. Furthermore, it con tributes to climate change mitigation by storing 
carbon permanently while, if used in agriculture, it enhances soil properties. 
Turning sewage sludge to biochar for a better nutrient management 
Sewage sludge pyrolysis results in phosphorus-rich biochar, thus offering a nutrient recovery solution 
and recycling of such waste streams. All this, following the aim of Article 20 of the revised Directive, 
stating that “sludge will have to be treated, recycled and recovered whenever appropriate” . In this 
context, minimum reu se and recycling rate r equirements for nitrogen in sewage sludge might 
represent a barrier to such a P-recovery opportunity, according to the fact that during sludge pyrolysis, 
a large amount of nitrogen can be lost as N 2. An alternative, permitting P-recovery, would be setting 
out cumulative general targets for N  recovery in the wastewater sector . Nitrogen should be thus 
recovered before sewage sludge treatment.

Sewage sludge pyrolysis and biochar benefits to water contaminants mitigation 
Sewage sludge biochars application to land reduces excessive nutrient release from agricultural soils, 
one of the main causes of  eutrophication. Sewage sludge pyrolysis eliminates a variety of 
contaminants, including organic micro -pollutants of emerging concern specified by the Directive 
recast.  
The Directive also focuses on the need to regulate the contaminants level during wastewater 
treatments and expresses the "need to t ake action to address the issue of micro -pollutants". Within 
the scope of Article 4 of the Directive, which establishes the quaternary treatment as mandatory, 
biosolids biochar shows great potential to remove water contaminants.  
Sewage sludge biochar for energy neutrality and climate targets 
The pyrolysis process supports the goal of energy neutrality in the wastewater sector by 2040. 
Industrial-scale plants can achieve energy neutrality and in certain cases, energy positivity, generating 
up to 5 times more energy than they consume . The in-loco addition of a pyrolysis plant module to a 
wastewater facility can also reduce energy use for the transportation. Additionally, anaerobic 
digestion (AD) for biogas production and subsequential pyrolysis treatment of the digestate can 
provide further synergy.  
With the established measures to reach energy neutrality, GHG emission reductions are estimated to 
happen. Currently, the UWWTD Revision outlooks for a 37,32% emissions reduction (of the avoidable 
sector emissions). The production of biochar from sewage sludge allows to reduce GHG emissions 
from sewage sludge handling methods and at the same time supports climate -positive EU goals . In 
fact, biochar is a permanent carbon sink, which stores recalcitrant carbon in soil and products 
depending on its application. 
Economic benefits from a more sustainable and circular wastewater sector 
The Directive revision also considers the economic benefits of a more sustainable and circular 
wastewater sector. This is exactly the case with new business opportunities from the use of biochar 
and the valorisation of sewage sludge through pyrolysis, as well as a reduction in sludge disposal costs. 
Pyrolysis plants can support decentralized sludge treatment and thus transportation mitigated costs 
and emissions as well as generating significant GHG emission reductions from sludge storage and use 
on farmland. 
The European Biochar Industry Consortium warmly supports the UWWTD Revision’s purposes and 
remains at the Commission’s complete disposal for further clarifications and questions.

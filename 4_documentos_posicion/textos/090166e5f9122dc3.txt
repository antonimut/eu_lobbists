Towards a coherent policy that will help industry and regulators to assess the effects of 
recent and upcoming REACH restrictions of BPA in the environment 
Background: 
 Since 2000, the Water Framework Directive (WFD) has been the main legal instrument for 
water protection in Europe, supported by its “daughter Directives”, which includes the 
Environmental Quality Standards Directive (EQSD)1.  
In its proposal for a revision of the EQSD, the Commission has, inter alia, set new limit values 
for several substances in its Annex 1 2 , including Bisphenol A (BPA). The Commission 
proposes a new environmental quality standard (EQS) for BPA in surface water at 0.000034 
μg/L.  
 
What we know on BPA in surface water and how it is addressed: 
BPA is a substance currently used mostly as a building block (monomer) to manufacture 
polymers, namely polycarbonate and epoxy resins. It has historically been used in other 
applications, e.g. as a colour developing additive in thermal paper (cash receipts) and as an 
additive in other polymers. Polycarbonate plastic is the main use for BPA, accounting for about 
70% of global BPA production.  
During polymerisation BPA is chemically converted into the resul ting polymer polycarbonate 
which is a new chemical substance. In this polycarbonate, except for technically unavoidable 
traces of unreacted residual monomer, BPA is no longer present in its original chemical form.  
Because of its unique combination of properties, such as clarity, durability, versatility, heat and 
shatter-resistance, polycarbonate is selected for applications where no alternative polymers 
are suitable, like for instance, safety glazing, spectacle lenses, or medical equipment. It is also 
used in battery casing for electrical vehicles and LED lighting, amongst others.

Why BPA does not belong in the priority hazardous substance (PHS) list: 
The European Commission proposed to identify BPA as a priority hazardous substance, which 
the PC/BPA Group believes is unjustified for the following reasons:  
1. The proposed EQS for BPA suggested by JRC and SCHEER opinions is based on a draft opinion of 
the European Food Safety Authority which is likely to be changed.

The JRC and SCHEER opinions have based their assessment on a threshold derived for 
human exposure to BPA. The threshold (so called Tolerable Daily Intake – TDI) is still a draft 
and is currently being controversially discussed amongst renowned regulatory authorities.  
In its opinion the SCHEER5 highlights that “Should this TDI not be confirmed, the critical EQS 
would require to be re-evaluated”. Since the publication of the SCHEER opinion on BPA, EFSA 
has communicated in a recent meeting open to observers that the draft TDI would be raised 
in the final EFSA opinion (expected in February/March 2023). Furthermore, the final TDI may 
still be considered as very conservative as it is based on an i ntermediate endpoint, which is 
acknowledged by EFSA.  
Based on the SCHEER’s own assessment, and the latest developments at EFSA level, the 
EQS value should be revised. A revised (higher) EQS value would also lower the distance to 
the measured levels of BPA in the environment, which is one key criterium for the identification 
of a substance as a PHS.  
2. BPA is not a bioaccumulative substance  
Bioaccumulation refers to the buildup of the substance in any living organism, in the context 
of the EQS in aquatic organisms (fish etc.). The SCHEER final opinion on the EQS value uses 
a very conservative, worst case bioconcentration factor derived from an unreliable study with 
severe weaknesses, notably as it is based on a non -standard organism. However, a fully 
reliable guideline study exists which concludes to a low bioaccumulation potential of BPA.6  
BPA is not classified or identified as a bioaccumulative substance under the EU regulatory 
framework for chemicals (REACH & CLP).  
This should be taken into account, as  bioaccumulation is another of the main criteria to list a 
substance as PHS. Furthermore, the very precautionary conclusion on bioaccumulation by 
JRC and SCHEER led to the very low EQS, as only in case of bioaccumulation potential a 
human health based threshold can be used for EQS derivation.  
3. The level of BPA in the environment is overestimated: existing and upcoming REACH restrictions 
are not taken into account.  
More fundamentally, the current proposal is based on monitoring data carried before 2019 
which do not reflect the latest regulatory status of BPA and trends of decreasing BPA presence 
in the environment. One of the main sources of BPA in the environment, thermal paper 
entering the waste paper stream and thus the paper recycling loop, is banned since 2020. 
Hence the presence of BPA in the environment is expected to decrease since then. Other 
sources are currently being addressed through a REACH Restriction proposed by Germany 
which will look at all the main contributors of BPA in the environment,  with the aim to further 
reduce BPA levels in the environment.  
Furthermore, various assessments under the EU regulatory framework (REACH & CLP) on 
BPA concluded that BPA is readily biodegradable. Therefore, the current and future lowering 
of BPA releases will directly lead to lower environmental concentration.  
The impact of those regulatory developments is still to be determined. An identification of BPA 
as PHS at this point in time might lead to severe consequences before the existing and 
upcoming REACH restrictions come into effect. Instead, what is needed is the ge neration of

Towards the inclusion of BPA in the priority substance list: 
It is important to systematically monitor the presence of the substance in the environment. This 
is to verify that already adopted regulatory management measures, such as substance - and 
product-specific restrictions under the REACH  Regulation etc., have a downward effect on 
concentrations in surface water.  
Two fundamental changes need to be included in setting an EQS value for BPA:  
- The EQS value needs to be revised following the recommendation of the SCHEER to amend 
the value when the EFSA opinion becomes final. It is expected that the final EFSA opinion on 
BPA will be published in March 2023.  
- BPA could be listed as a priority substance but not be identified as priority hazardous 
substance. A priority substance listing would a llow to get quality monitoring data on the 
presence of BPA In the environment and on the impact of the existing measures aimed at 
addressing those issues.  
A proportionate approach on the presence of BPA in the environment: 
The main pathways of BPA in the environment are already being addressed  and effects are 
predicted to be seen in the coming years through a reduction of BPA releases into the 
environment as a consequence of the REACH restrictions.  
A low EQS together with a PHS listing is likely to disproportionately affect uses of BPA which 
are safe and only marginal sources of BPA in the environment.  There are currently four 
polycarbonate production sites in Europe. As an example, the Ramboll study results showed 
that in Germany production of BPA, polycarbonate and epoxy resin contribute less than 0.5% 
to BPA in the environment. 
 A PHS listing has as a consequence that all emissions to the environment have to be ceased, 
which would put at risk the manufacturing of polycarbonate in Europe. Polycarbonate is a high 
tech material which can only be made using bisphenol A as a building block. The PHS listing 
could put at risk the permits of industrial installations which have a demonstrated safety record 
and contribute only marginally to the issue of BPA in the environment. 
Furthermore, a  REACH Restriction proposal  has been introduced by the German 
Environmental Agency (UBA) in order to  address risks posed by the presence of BPA in the 
environment, especially in surface waters. In its proposal, UBA intends to reduce all emissions 
as far as possible, meaning to reduce the environmental concentration to a maximum of 
0.01µg/L6. This would be at the lower end of the limit of quantification for BPA in surface waters 
as currently applied in most EU member states  and by leading laboratories. The aim of the 
PC/BPA group of Plastics Europe is to ensure that the EQS value proposed is attainable and 
enforceable. It appears that the value of 0.01µg/L is an ambitious, yet an enforceable target. 
It would also be in line with the upcoming REACH Restriction objectives.

Bisphenol A and the environment
Bisphenol A (BPA) is the basic building block for the production of poly-
carbonate and epoxy resins. It can be measured in small amounts in 
the environment, especially in surface waters. Authorities, as well as BPA 
producers strive to reduce BPA in the environment as far as possible. 
BPA is readily biodegradable in surface waters with a half-life of only 
a few days. BPA is also largely degraded in sewage treatment plants. 
In order to trace the input pathways of BPA to the environment the pro-
ducers carried out systematic analyses. The outcome: new, reliable 
study results with clear conclusions on the entry pathways and sources 
of BPA in the environment.
Around 99% of the BPA produced in Europe is used directly for the in-
dustrial production of polycarbonate and epoxy resin. In the chemical 
reaction, BPA is consumed and becomes a tightly bound, integral com-
ponent of the resulting polymeric materials. Free unbound BPA is present 
only in minimal residual amounts in these materials. 
Polycarbonate and epoxy resins are extremely stable and durable. The-
refore, they contribute very little to the BPA emissions into surface waters 
- despite their production in large quantities. Also recycling of polycar-
bonate articles, whether by thermal, mechanical or chemical processes, 
is not expected to result in environmental releases of BPA.
According to the studies, the majority of environmental di-
scharges of BPA is attributable to products in which BPA is 
freely present in unreacted form, such as in thermal paper, 
where it served as a color developer until 2020.

1 Background 
Bisphenol A (BPA) is an industrially manufactured chemical used to produce 
numerous products and consumer articles. Despite the demonstrated ready 
biodegradability, BPA is found (albeit mostly at low levels) in various 
environmental compartments and organisms, e.g., in surface water and 
sediments as well as in plants and animals. For decades BPA has been under 
regulatory and scientific scrutiny because of its high production volumes, 
ubiquity in the environment and indications of weak estrogenic activity. 
Recently, the focus of regulation in Europe has been particularly on 
environmental concerns.  
With increasing regulatory pressure to prevent the presence of BPA even at the 
lowest levels (nano and picogram range), Ramboll is commissioned by 
PlasticsEurope to undertake research into currently applied and technically 
feasible limit of quantification (LOQ) levels of BPA in the environment.  
To determine these levels, existing (public) surface water monitoring 
programmes were screened for BPA and, if included, their LOQ was determined. 
In a second step, a literature search was carried out using appropriate search 
terms to establish an understanding of the technically feasible LOQ of BPA in 
aquatic medium. In a third step, laboratories were contacted and inquired about 
the minimum LOQ for BPA measurements in surface water and the related costs 
(if different LOQ can be offered). 
The main aim of the project is to provide an overview of the LOQ for BPA 
measurements in surface waters which are currently established in the EU. 
2 Executive Summary 
A web search for databases containing EU-wide BPA measuring campaigns was 
conducted, with a focus on EU agencies conducting research. The search 
resulted in eight monitoring campaigns found in the timeframe of 2008 – 2022. 
The campaign search revealed that the BPA detection methods established in 
EU have a range of LOQ values for measuring BPA in surface waters from 10 to 
100 ng/L (0.01 to 0.1 µg/L). The lowest LOQ value found was 10 ng/l 
(0.01 µg/l) in Danube Hazards m3c project in Bulgarian surface water. This was

the established LOQs. 
3 Methods 
For identification of European-wide measurement campaigns the European Commission’s website was 
investigated in order to evaluate the centralized information. Narrowing down the research to European 
associations and agencies such as the European Environment Agency led to abundant literature on BPA,  
although without mentioning LOQ. Next, measuring campaigns were investigated at member states 
level. At every level, the search terms “LOQ, BPA, European monitoring, surface water” were used to 
arrive at the data summarized in Table 1 below. Finally, a database from the Norman network 
(EMPODAT database) was a useful tool to obtain concrete information on LOQ values in EU measuring 
campaigns. The EMPODAT dataset allowed to identify two EU projects where a LOQ of BPA can be 
assessed: the Joint Danube Survey (JDS) and the Environmental Monitoring in the Black See (EMBLAS) 
project. Many EU countries are involved in the data collection within these two projects. For instance, 
JDS includes all countries where the Danube is flowing. This includes Austria, Croatia, Bulgaria, Czech 
Republic, Germany, Hungary, Romania, Serbia, Slovakia, Slovenia and Ukraine. Finally, individual 
reports from France, the Republic of Moldova and Montenegro could provide additional LOQ, but no 
specific project names were associated to it. 
In a second step, a literature search was conducted to investigate publicly available scientific articles 
and reviews papers on the LOQ of BPA in European surface waters published in the last 10 years (scope 
2013- present). The scope of the scientific literature search was limited to the concept and terms 
established in the contract agreement. Publicly available literature as well as specific journals for which 
Ramboll has access were used. The following search terms have been used for the literature research: 
LOQ/ Bisphenol A/ bpa/ BPA/ surface water/ water / Limit of quantification/ detection limit/ monitoring/ 
environmental monitoring/ Europe. The following search engines have been used: PubMed, SpringerLink

4 Results 
4.1 Applied LOQ of BPA measurements in European monitoring campaigns 
Measurement campaigns in Europe 
The JRC EU-wide water monitoring campaign is a program conducted by the Joint Research Centre 
(JRC) of the European Commission to monitor and assess the quality of surface water across the 
European Union (EU). The campaign involves collecting water samples from rivers, lakes, and 
transitional and coastal waters in all EU member states, as well as Norway and Switzerland. The 
samples are analyzed for a range of parameters, including BPA concentrations, to determine the overall 
ecological status of the water bodies. The data collected through the campaign is used to support the 
implementation of the EU Water Framework Directive, which aims to achieve targeted ecological status 
of all EU water bodies by 2027. The BPA number of samples collected may vary between campaigns to 
ensure representative coverage of the EU's surface water bodies, with the limit of detection (LOD) of 
BPA surface water determined to be 5 ng/L according to the 2010 data set (no LOQ was available). 
However, as the LOD is in general lower than the LOQ, it can be assumed that no LOQ lower than 5 
ng/L is applied. 
 
The NORMAN EMPODAT dataset is a publicly available collection of environmental monitoring data for 
organic micropollutants, which is developed and maintained by the NORMAN network, a network of 
European research institutes, regulatory bodies, and industries that aim to improve the assessment of 
emerging pollutants in the environment. The EMPODAT dataset provides information on the occurrence, 
concentration, and distribution of organic micropollutants, such as BPA, in European environmental 
samples, including surface waters, sediments, soils, and biota. The data has been collected from various 
monitoring programs across Europe, and the dataset is updated regularly with new data. As mentioned 
in the Methods section, the EMPODAT dataset allowed to identify two EU projects where a LOQ of BPA 
can be assessed: the Joint Danube Survey (JDS) and the EMBLAS project. Further, with NORMAN 
EMPODAT it is possible to extract BPA measurement values that have been collected within the 
database. Of the total number of 54563 values, only 11 indicated a LOQ lower than 10 ng/L. 510 
measurements had a LOQ of 10 ng/L, 44570 20 ng/l, 6947 50 ng/l and 1806 100 ng/L

The JDS is an advanced scientific survey that results from the EU Water Framework Directive, which

countries was 100 ng/L. 
 
The EMBLAS project supports Georgia, Ukraine and the Russian Federation to improve Black Sea 
protection, and specifically to enhance countries’ ability to perform marine environmental monitoring. 
The key involved actors are the relevant national authorities, as well as research, scientific and

The Water Research Journal is a peer-reviewed scientific journal that publishes original research articles,

published by the International Water Association (IWA), a global network of water professionals, 
researchers, and institutions that aims to promote sustainable water management practices worldwide. 
The journal published a study in 2010 indicating that BPA was detected most frequently above the 
European ground water quality standard for pesticide, which is 100 ng/L. Even though no LOQ was 
provided, it is not likely that a LOQ lower than this exists in the campaign.

Measurement campaigns in Germany 
The Hessian State Office for Conservation, Environment, and Geology (HLNUG) is a governmental 
agency in the German state of Hesse that is responsible for monitoring and managing various aspects of 
the environment, including water quality. A HLNUG report published in 2019 found that the LOQ for BPA 
using the standard analytical method SPE-LC-MS/MS (solid phase extraction coupled with liquid 
chromatography and tandem mass spectrometry) was 10 ng/L for surface water. 
 
The Water Monitoring program in Saxony involves regular sampling and analysis of water quality 
parameters such as BPA concentration. The program is coordinated by the Saxon State Office for 
Environment, Agriculture and Geology (LfULG) and involves collaboration with other governmental 
agencies, research institutions, and stakeholders. The program also supports the implementation of the 
Water Framework Directive in Saxony and contributes to the development of policies and regulations for 
water management and protection. The 2018 report on the implementation of the WFD in Saxony 
indicates a LOQ for BPA was 10 ng/L for surface water. 
 
ELWAS (Electronic Water Information System) is a software application developed by the German 
Federal Environment Agency (UBA) to support water management and protection in Germany. ELWAS 
provides a central platform for collecting, storing, and managing water-related data and information 
from various sources, including federal and state agencies, water supply companies, and research 
institutions. ELWAS is used by various governmental and non-governmental organizations involved in 
 
 
6/8  
 
water management and protection, as well as by researchers, policymakers, and the public. The LOQ 
found in a 2017 report was 5 ng/L.  
 
The FGG Elbe monitoring campaign is a research project focused on groundwater and water protection 
in the Elbe River basin in Germany, which aims to develop a sustainable and integrated approach to 
water management and protection. The project involves collaboration between various stakeholders, 
including federal and state authorities, research institutions, and industry partners. The LOQ found was 
10 ng/L from sampling data up to 2019. 
 
The Trace Substance Inventory of Rivers in Baden-Württemberg is a project that aims to monitor and 
evaluate the presence of trace substances in the rivers of the German state of Baden-Württemberg. The 
project is carried out by the State Institute for Environment, Measurements and Nature Conservation 
Baden-Württemberg (LUBW) and involves the sampling and analysis of water samples from various 
rivers in the state. The LOQ for BPA in surface waters was found to be 5 ng/L in a 2015 report.

4.3 Laboratory quotes 
 
Laboratories were contacted and asked to provide a quote and if/how they determine LOQ of BPA in 
surface waters. It is worth noting that different laboratories and analytical methods may have different 
LOQs for BPA, and the LOQ may also depend on factors such as sample matrix, instrument sensitivity, 
and sample preparation procedures. Of the ten that were contacted, three responded with details, 
whereas the remaining seven could not provide a timely answer or simply did not respond. One 
laboratory offered to be commissioned for the development of a method for BPA LOQ determination, 
with an indicative cost of 13.846 € for the method development and a further cost of 120 – 160 € per 
sample. A German institute provided a quantification method for BPA in drinks. However, the expert 
mentioned that the LOQ would be matrix-dependant and the method should be consolidated on 
occasion. The LOQ would vary from 10 µg/L to 100 µg/L in that case. So far only one laboratory 
developed a sound method with a LOQ of 5 ng/L, with a corresponding price per sample of 170 €.

Feedback of the European Association of Hospital Pharmacists to the “Proposal for a Directive on urban wastewater treatment”  Protecting the environment with the highest standards is important, but safeguarding patients' access to their therapies is crucial. The European Association of Hospital Pharmacists (EAHP) consequently welcomes the European Commission’s proposal for a Directive on urban wastewater treatment but would like to share some points for consideration.   EAHP notes that the proposal foresees the application of the ‘polluter-pays’ principle so that producers pay the additional treatment required to remove the micro-pollutants from urban wastewater that result from the manufacturing activities of their products (recital number 13, page 26 of the proposal). Amongst such products are pharmaceuticals (annex 3, page 12 of the annexes of the proposal).  EAHP stresses that this principle is a step in the right direction, but it should be ensured that the ‘polluter-pays’ principle does not jeopardize patients' access to adequate treatments. For that, this principle should not impede getting medicines for patients by hospital pharmacists. Therefore, environmental and patient interests need to be balanced.  Hospital pharmacies dispense medicines to patients and according to the proposed definition of ‘producer’ (article 2(18) of the proposal; page 38), could be considered distributors. Hospital pharmacists on a very small scale may compound medicines to attend to specific patients’ needs, but they are not distributors. Consequently, there is a need to exempt pharmacies from being considered distributors since these are the intermediary between manufacturers and pharmacies.  Furthermore, nowadays there is the burden of medicines shortages, often of essential ones. There must be safeguards so that the implementation of the Directive will not lead to additional shortages that impact patients getting access to the therapies they need.  The cost of healthcare is increasingly becoming a burden to national budgets. An impact analysis of the Directive should take place so there will be an appropriate insight into the cost consequences of the implementation.  In the health sector, it is also paramount to complement the ‘polluter-pays principle’ with transparency safeguards. It is essential to promote transparency across the different pharmaceutical supply chains to allow a complete mapping of them. Since many pharmaceutical plants are located outside the EU there is a need to promote more and better information regarding global supply chains to allow the traceability of the Active Pharmaceutical Ingredients (APIs)’ origin. International cooperation between the World Health Organization, the EU, and the pharmaceutical industry is essential to achieve standards to regulate the emissions from pharmaceutical production.

Air quality 
Revision of the EU rules 
 
France Chimie contribution 
 
 
France Chimie does not take a position on the objectives of the directive, but wishes to share the 
efforts made by industrial companies to reduce their air emissions and thus contribute to improving 
air quality. 
 
The chemical industry is a major player in the French, European and global economy. The impacts of 
its activities are real: most production units release polluting substances through their air emissions. 
 
However, for many years now, thanks to major investments, regulatory pressure and voluntary 
commitments by companies to protect the environment, the management of chronic risks related  to 
production has been greatly improved. 
The French legislation s and regulations relating to the impact of industrial facilities on their 
environment, generally derived from the EU, is very constraining and currently among the most 
challenging. At both national and EU levels, it is constantly progressing: 
▪ 
  The Industrial Emissions Directive is currently under revision; 
▪ The Brefs are revised at regular intervals according to the evolution of technologies and 
practices: we are completing a first revision cycle, the second revision cycle will start soon;  
▪ French regulations are regularly adapted accordingly. 
As a result, the requirements, particularly in terms of air emissions, which are becoming increasingly 
stringent, have led to significant reductions in emissions, which are continuing.  
 
This logically leads to a reduction in pollutant concentrations in the environment, in line with the 
contribution of the chemical sector to environmental degradation, it means  an improvement in air 
quality. 
This is reflected in the air quality assessments in France and in Europe where we observe a decrease 
in the annual average concentrations for the main pollutants and in particular the primary ones, thus 
maintaining the trend for about 20 years.

Europe’s non-ferrous metals industries’ feedback to the consultation. 
We welcome the opportunity to provide feedback on the recent Commission proposal to amend the Water 
Framework Directive, the Groundwater and the Environmental Quality Standards (EQS) Directives.  
We noted with great interest that the proposal assigns a central role to ECHA for scientific support in the identification of  
water pollutants and the derivation of EQS value s in the future. Furthermore, we take note of the following statement 
‘ECHA should also ensure better coordination between various pieces of environmental law through increased 
transparency as regards pollutants on a watch list or the development of Union wide or national EQS or thresholds, by 
making relevant scientific reports publicly available’. 
We welcome and fully support the intention to make the prioritisation processes, the setting of quality standards and 
threshold values more transparent, more efficient and aligned with other principles such as the One Substance, One 
Assessment principle, and existing substance specific EU framework legislations to make the best use of available 
expertise and data. 
We have closely followed the Water Framework Directive, the recent ‘priority substances list’ revision and EQS derivation 
process. Based on this experience, we would like to provide a series of recommendations: 
 
• Increased and formalised dialogue is essential between EU Commission/ECHA (formerly JRC) and experts in the 
working groups under the Common Implementation Strategy for the WFD (Working Group on Chemicals). 
• The EU approach will also be strengthened by looking at examples of multi -stakeholder dialogue and clear and 
transparent as sessment frameworks in other jurisdictions, which have proved successful in prior European 
regulatory processes (i.e., under the Existing Substances Regulation) in delivering scientifically robust and more 
consensual outcomes. Such approaches typically als o positively contribute to expertise and capacity building 
amongst the different stakeholders, and we would be pleased to share information on such alternative 
approaches, and the key success factors that characterise them.  
• The use of an unambiguously risk-based approach in the derivation of EQSs must be ensured. 
• Ensure that robust socio-economic impact assessments are carried out to properly evaluate the costs, benefits 
and consequences of adding substances to the priority list and revising established EQ Ss for existing Priority 
Substances.

• Retain Article 16 of the WFD to provide a clear structure and predictability to the review process. 
• Maintain the ordinary legislative procedure for the revision of the list of priority substances to ensure a fair and 
balanced decision-making power sharing between the European Commission, the EU Parliament and the EU 
Council. 
• Retention of the 'river basin specific pollutants' part of the definition of 'ecological status' to ensure that the specific 
chemical characteristics of each water body are properly considered, thus ensuring the scientific relevance of the 
associated environmental thresholds. 
 
Overall, we continue to emphasise the commitment of the non -ferrous metals industry to the achievement of 
good ecological and chemical status of EU waters and to the continuous improvement of the risk assessment of 
metals in water using the latest scientific knowledge and methods. 
 
Thank you for the at tention you will give to our comments, we welcome any opportunity for us to provide 
additional information.

Nickel Institute’s feedback on the revision of the Nickel Environmental Quality Standards 
 
The Nickel Institute 1 (NI) welcomes the opportunity to provide feedback on the proposed revision of 
the Environmental Quality Standard (EQS) Directive and the freshwater nickel EQS. The NI does not 
support the revised EQS values proposed by the Commission and has the following remarks on process. 
 
Scientific concerns with the proposed revised nickel EQS: 
- The scientific dossier upon which the EC proposal is based is not complete. The NI has consistently 
urged that, should the Commission decide to revise again the nickel EQS, it was critically important 
to consider all scientific information generated since 2011 when the discussions on the existing 
EQS were closed. Unfortunately, this has not been the case and the revised nickel EQS dossier did 
not take into account relevant peer -reviewed scientific information (including new ecotoxicity 
data, bioavailability modelling updates, supporting data, and water chemistry monitoring 
information) made available to the Commission and the JRC since 2012.  In our views, t his is a 
significant gap that puts into question the scientific basis of the proposal. 
 
Very limited discussions with Member States experts and stakeholders: 
- Over the last years, the consistent message from the Commission was that a review of the nickel 
EQS would be discussed within an expert subgroup with interested Member States and 
stakeholders. However, no expert subgroup was convened until very late in the  process, in June 
2022, and only following repeated requests from the NI. In addition, it consisted of only a 1 -hour 
conference call to discuss the Ni EQS dossier which the JRC had already drafted . The discussion 
was incomplete and it did not cover all the aspects of the revised EQS dossier. In our view, this was 
completely insufficient to ensure an inclusive process and sufficient scientific discussions  with 
affected Member States and stakeholders. 
 
Timeline of the impact assessment process: 
- The study supporting the Impact Assessment was launched at the beginning of 2021, well before 
the JRC finalised the nickel dossier in July 2022. It is unclear how the study could properly evaluate 
the potential impacts of the revised nickel EQS, if the EQS values were not yet finalised and  they 
had still to be examined by the scientific committee (SCHEER). In our view, the Impact Assessment 
study should be conducted once the dossier is finalised and the scientific committee has adopted 
its Opinion. 
 
Legislative proposal published before the SCHEER consultation was finalised: 
- The Commission’s legislative proposal was published while the public consultation on the 
preliminary SCHEER Opinion on nickel (and other substances) was still ongoing, without taking into 
consideration possible additional comments from stakeholders. This seems inconsistent with the  
Commission’s stated procedure and with due process. 
 
Regulatory uncertainty: 
- This is the  third time, since 2008, that a nickel EQS has been proposed  under EU law. While we 
acknowledge the need to review EQS values periodically to keep the legislation up -to-date and

Directive in 2013, which set a protective and state-of-the science standard for nickel. 
- A recent review 2  of available scientific information showed that nickel do es not present a 
continental-scale risk to European surface waters  and, therefore, does not fulfil the criteria for 
classification as a Priority Substance under EU law.  
- A decision to revise the nickel EQS ignores the opinions of many Member States, includ ing those 
reflected in the Impact Assessment targeted stakeholder consultation in 2021, where the existing 
freshwater nickel EQS of (4 µg/L bioavailable) was considered as “correct” by the vast majority of 
respondents. This is also acknowledged by the EC Impact Assessment Report (Annex II, page 111). 
- The outcome of the expert survey confirmed that Member States and stakeholders did not 
consider a revision of the nickel EQS as a priority under current review cycle of the Directive. 
- Ensuring the correct implementation of the existing standard and avoiding unnecessary changes 
should be the priority to protect the environment while providing regulatory certainty for Member 
States and businesses, in line with the EC Better Regulation principles. 
Socio-economic considerations: 
- The EC Impact Assessment Report acknowledges that there would be “ significant costs to ensure 
compliance” for nickel . In addition, the Report (page 199) notes that in the case of nickel the 
proposed revision “is likely to create a new wave of exceedances, with potentially an extensive 
package of measures needed to achieve good chemical status”. The Report also states that: “For a 
smaller set of substances, the impact assessment draws a neutral result […]. Based on the analysis 
of substances in the neutral category, the most uncertain will be nickel. Given the potential 
uncertainties involved, this may be the one substance where, depending on the specific measures 
implemented, the costs outweigh the benefits”. 
 
- Given this finding and the uncertainties outlined in the Impact Assessment , we consider that the 
proposed revision is not justified and it is not an effective and efficient policy option.  
Conclusion: 
For all these reasons, we consider that the revision of the nickel EQS should be postponed and 
resumed when it is possible to follow a process that takes all available information into account 
and respects all the regulatory steps required by the Water Framework Directive and the Better 
Regulation guidelines.

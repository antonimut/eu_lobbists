COSMED INPUT on revision of the Urban Wastewater Treatment Directive  
 
First of all,  COSMED support this initiative leading to the improvement of urban water quality and 
wastewater treatment.  
A quaternary treatment would indeed be necessary if some pollutants remain unremoved after the 
first three treatments. However, it is necessary to clarify which residual pollutants are not treated by 
the current treatments  and which need to be treated further.  The current definition of "micro -
pollutants" is too broad, and totally inconsistent with this notion of additional specific treatment. The 
definition must therefore be clear, unambiguous to clearly identify the concerned pollutants, and be 
applied in a harmonized way across the EU.  Including in the directive a list of micropollutants would 
therefore be supportive. 
The i mplementation of an EPR system is totally justified to support the additional cost of this 
quaternary treatment, and t he contributors to the pollution must participate in the cost of this 
additional treatment. However, it is essential that all contributors contribute to the cost. The selection 
of only pharmaceutical and cosmetic sectors is unjustified, especially since the notion of micro -
pollutants is not targeted. The cosmetics sector must contribute to the quaternary treatment of the 
micro-pollutants it generates, and this principle must apply to each sector accordingly. The calculation 
method for released micro -pollutants must be clear, relevant and adapted to the respective 
contributions of each sector. 
Specific consideration should be provided for SMES who will be the more impacted by the EPR system. 
The proposal for a directive defines exoneration according to the amount of products placed on the 
market. We stress that the threshold currently defined of 2 tons per year would concern only few 
cosmetic SMEs and must be refined. Considering the full formula and the water content, which is the 
most contributor to the total weight of a cosmetic product, a quantity of 2 tons per year is far too low, 
even for SMEs.   
 
The practical implementation of this EPR, defined by each Member State, is still undetermined, but due 
to the free circulation of products in Europe, the implementation of this EPR for products placed on 
the EU market must be clearly defined, particularly with regard s to the calculation and amount  of 
contributions.  
 
Thus, we insist on  
- A clear specification of the type of micropollutants to be subject to quaternary treatment.   
- The implementation of an EPR for the different contributors, implemented in a proportionate and 
equitable way in relation to the level of discharge. 
- Specific consideration of SMEs that would be the more impacted, especially reconsidering the level 
of exoneration.

About COSMED 
 
COSMED is the French cosmetic association for SMEs, a non-profit seeking association with more than 
1000 members, in France and Europe. Cosmed sit at the European Commission as the voice of cosmetic 
SMEs. 
COSMED is involved in the development of cosmetic regulations, as well as in national and international 
standardisation bodies. In this way, COSMED contributes to the development of a regulatory  
framework for the marketing of safe and effective cosmetic products.

Comments on the draft revision of the Ambient Air Quality Directive  
Art. 1/Annex I  
• the new 2030 standards (especially for PM2,5) are too ambitious; the proposal should rather 
focus on gaps and effective measures needed to achieve the highest achievable air quality 
level; 
• financial impacts to reach the EQS should take into account the energy crisis, inflation, etc.; 
• probability of reaching the targets is under high uncertainty burden (energy mix prediction in 
current situation, emission reduction development in countries outside EU, etc.);     
• goals for 2050 – the proposal does not mention what level of pollution should be considered 
absolutely fit for health and environment (as many pollution substances are of non-threshold 
nature in this sense); 
• the EC assumption that the costs to reach the new standards will be covered by financial 
savings from the medical expenses and ecosystem expenses stemming from the pollution is 
not realistic – this is purely hypothetical (spread of these expenses in public-private budget 
vs. concentration of cost for the concrete measures against pollution); 
• effective flexibility instruments should be introduced and ready to be utilized by MSs when 
they are facing objective barriers (technological, overestimations of effectivity of the current 
EU policy framework, etc) in concrete time to reach the new goals. For this purpose, 
amendment of Art. 16 and 18 would be appropriate. 
Art. 3  
• MSs will have to prepare the Air Quality plans to reach the new EQS for several years ahead. 
The proposed short-term period when the EQS might be updated could easily devalue the 
investments spent to reach the „previous“ EQS (e.g. if the new/another technologies are 
required to reach the new standards); therefore, the text should at least ensure that MSs 
should not be responsible/accused for the investments enabling reaching the previous 
standards; 
• ideally, the proposed 5-year period should be prolonged at least to 10 years within which the 
EQS should not be changed. In 5 years the MSs have no chance to prepare and implement 
the strategies; 
• the goal of the AAQD revision should not be to set the strictest possible number/value but to 
evaluate to what level the achievability threshold can be moved. 
Art. 7 and 8 and Annex III  
• low ambitions of the revised Directive in terms of increase of the quality of the AQ 
description is in big contrast with very ambitious AQ standards; 
• Thus, revision of the common framework on minimum monitoring stations which would be 
valid for each MS is needed to increase their number as the proposal requirements cannot 
be sufficiently effective to minimize uncertainties, for modelling, identification of hidden 
problems of AQ, etc. 
Art 11 and Annex V and VI  
• efforts to unify the AQ modelling and quality criteria are welcomed as well as quantification 
of the measurement uncertainty; 
• however, it is not clear if and to what extent the uncertainty will play role in EQS compliance 
assesment (Example: measurement 11 units, EQS 10 units, uncertainty 3 units); this could 
lead to unnecessary infringements. The utilization of uncertainties in practice has to be 
explained

• Annex V – provision to report and use also the data for the AQ assessments which do not 
meet the quality requirements is misleading and high-risky as it could lead to under/over-
estimations and irrelevant info for public, for modelling, etc. 
Art 15 
• effectivity of short-term action plans (see Art. 20 – almost not amended by this proposal) is 
questionable as e.g. the smog situation could not be effectively solved by them, especially 
not at the level of individual MS (according to Copernicus the extraordinary pollution mass 
has origin in several regions in EU at the same time; dispersion conditions dependance; etc.); 
• the short-term plans can have very negative effects and huge impacts e.g. on industry as it 
can e.g. limit or stop the blast furnaces production with all the consequences, while, in the 
meantime, the situation could become normal (no smog). Moreover, the proposal in this 
sense does not reflect quite a long time needed for ceasing/shutdown of operation of the 
sources. 
Art. 16 
• better specification of the rules for deduction of natural factors in meeting the EQS is 
appropriate in the proposal, however, transboundary pollution deduction aspect is 
completely missing and should thus be covered too – it is out of responsibility of given MS. 
Art. 18 
• more transparency of the EC assessment of the MS requests for derogation (prolongation) is 
needed - almost no progress is contained in the proposal in this sense; 
• it is not clear why the 5-year period for prolongation is set arbitrarily – e.g. natural conditions 
leading/contributing to incompliance (e.g. dispersion, orographic or climate conditions) may 
persist, while the MS has no chance to reduce their effects;  
• thus, possibility to grant an exception (the derogation) repeatedly should be allowed. 
Economic and social reasons should also be considered (AQ plans could contain methods 
how to solve those problems). 
Art. 19 and Annex VII 
• according to the Directive, the AQ plans are in fact perceived as the only instrument how to 
solve the non-conforming AQ, however, despite a negative evaluation in the Fitness Check, 
the proposal does not change the things again (except some content of the plans, it does not 
look at their quality); 
• the draft revision also does not tackle the legal form of the AQ plans, however, such form is 
crucial for their effectivity. The plans should be obligatory and comparable using the same 
methods; 
• the proposal leads to higher administrative burden in terms of AQ plans making as it 
introduces two more types of plans at NUTS 1 level which should be made by MS and which 
can overlap with the National emission reductions plans (also NUTS 1); this should be 
avoided; 
• general provision of 4-year period for all MS to ensure remedy in case of exceedance is not 
fair as there are very different conditions and starting points in individual MS in the EU 
(population, AQ, local conditions, financial sources, etc.); this should be taken into account;  
• updates of the AQ plans should not be mandatory but should be made only if is necessary.  
  
Art. 21 
• transboundary pollution aspect is still insufficiently tackled in the proposal (in fact, only the 
rule that by 3 months the given MSs should cooperate and react is introduced); clear 
obligation to deeply cooperate after finding a common solution (plans) on pollution 
reduction process is missing; 
• clear central and moderating role of the EU institutions in case of transboundary pollution 
should be introduced; 
• individual MS and polluters cannot be responsible for pollution from abroad – thus, at least 
exoneration measures must be introduced. 
Art. 22 
• requirement to set identical indexes across the EU does not make sense – national index 
must be perceived as indexes reflecting the specific circumstances and problems of individual 
states. Thus, the possibility to set the national index should further persist. 
Art. 27 
• setting such public rights will probably lead to more frequent cases of confrontations – 
therefore, it would be appropriate to identify other (extrajudicial) ways allowing the remedy 
in shorter time (such as EC assessments of the plans); 
• in that sense, the text should ensure proper and expert assessment of the plans by the EC 
once they are reported. 
Art. 28 
• for the reasons mentioned below the article should be deleted; 
• it is not possible ensure 100% health via setting the limit values – thus, the introduction of 
compensations does not make much sense. For example: What should the individuals be 
compensated for by MS if the negative health consequences cannot be prevented/avoided?;  
• furthermore, it is not clear, how it could be proven that the given health consequence is 
related to the time the Directive is in force. The health problems could start well before or 
can be caused by other factors, such as living style; 
• to the above-mentioned point, the reverse burden of proof concept should be avoided as it 
brings a lot of burden and uncertainties and rather goes against the goals of the directive – it 
takes a lot of time and energy spent on proving that the authorities are not responsible for 
the health impacts; instead, that time could be spent for AQ remedy/improvement of 
measures, etc.; 
• in addition, the proposal ensures that public have a full right to enter the process of AQ plans 
(and also remedy actions) preparation – this is another reason why the concept of 
compensations is questionable; 
• for some potential compensation receivers the article could be perceived as an attractive 
opportunity how to easily get to financial sources – this is unacceptable. 
Art. 29 
• the main pollution (according to the data) often comes from public (household heating), 
while the industrial pollution has been deeply decreased and is rather additional – therefore, 
it would be appropriate, instead of sanctions, to introduce effective granting mechanisms; 
• moreover, sanctions against the industry have already been applied in CZ, however, there is 
a serious problem with transboundary pollution aspect and thus fairness of the sanctions as 
it is difficult (impossible) to recognize pollution from local industry and from abroad; 
• thus, the targeted support to apply concrete measures to limit the pollution from 
given/selected sources is much more effective and sustainable way than the sanctions.

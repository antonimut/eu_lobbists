Executive Summary  
Air Pollution is a critical point to address  and SUEZ supports the new revision and its alignment with 
the WHO recommendations.  
Reducing air pollution helps to put the EU on a trajectory to achieve zero pollution for air at the latest 
by 2050, in synergy with climate -neutrality efforts. Air pollution is also the principal environmental 
threat to health, with chronic exposure to air pollution, and above all particulate matter, such as fine 
particulate matter (PM 2.5), increasing the risk of heart diseases, stroke, lung cancer and respiratory 
diseases such as a sthma, bronchitis, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) and respiratory 
infections.  
Air pollution is very costly for society, estimated around €231 to 853 billion per year, including €8 
billion for lost workdays. Consequently, improving Air Quality will reduce the costs associated with air 
pollution and it will contribute more to the overall ecological transition. 
SUEZ recommends maintaining the ambition set out in the directive proposed by the European 
Commission.

2. SUEZ’s recommendations  
In general, the European Commission has integrated in the revised directive many positive aspects 
which will help to tackle air pollution. In complement to the proposal by the European Commission, 
SUEZ recommends the following:  
Reducing air pollution levels by incorporating treatment technologies  
• The revision of the Ambient Air Quality Directive only addresses the reduction in pollution 
levels, but it does not  incorporate provisions  for treating air pollution in specific 
circumstances. SUEZ recommends incorporating within the Directive the possibil ity for 
Member States to use specific treatment methods. In this sense, the European 
Commission could propose guidelines on innovative screening, quantification, capture 
and treatment solutions (e.g., natural pollution sinks using micro -algae) to control point 
and diffuse emissions of contaminants to the air. Please see the Case Study below. 
 
• In line with our recommendation to incorporate treatment within the Ambient Air 
Quality Directive, another example is treating air quality in  confined spaces and 
connected outdoor/indoor areas , especially in areas with high pedestrian traffic and 
elevated particulate pollution concentration ( example: tailored air filtration process 
eliminating fine particle mat ter, aerosols, NO2, VOCs in airports, malls, metro stations, 
underground car parks, roadway tunnels… ), with a focus on most fragile populations . As 
they have a less mature respiratory system than adults and are particularly sensitive to air 
quality, children must be protected as a priority (e.g., in day nurseries or school yards).

CASE STUDY  
In France, 75% of children breathe polluted air1. A figure corroborated internationally since 91% of the 
world’s population breathes air that contains a high level of pollutants . Because children have a less 
mature respiratory system than adults,  and build their immune defense for adulthood, they are 
particularly sensitive to air quality and must be protected as a priority. As part of its regional plan 
“Changeons d’air” aiming at improving air quality, the French Ile-de-France region decided to advance 
technological progress and rely on science to find innovative solutions to improve air quality, notably 
in schools.  
Building on public/private partnerships between the region, SUEZ and  Fermentalg, an innovative 
device creating a “bubble of clean air”, with a broad spectrum of treatment (PM10, PM2.5, NO2, VOCs 
as a priority) by combining several cutting -edge technologies such as micro -algae, was placed in the 
schoolyard of a school in the city of Poissy.  
 
 
 
Incorporate requirements for greenhouse gas emissions capture  
SUEZ recommends that in addition to more ambitious ambient air quality policies (national plans, WHO 
limits, monitoring and modeling and even treatment as proposed  by SUEZ) and the establishment of 
dedicated EU funds (e.g., Horizon Europe), it is necessary to incorporate the requirement to capture 
greenhouse gas at the same time. 
 
Promote for specific cases Public / Private Partnerships  
Public/private partnerships provide a secure framework to test innovative air quality solutions. An 
example is cited above of the Ile de France regional plan “Changeons d’air”. This program co-financed 
innovative air quality treatment solutions in sensitive and very dense public areas such as metro

3. SUEZ supports the European Commission’s Proposal  
In line with our recommendations, SUEZ supports in particular the following propositions:  
➢ Public access to information. 
➢ The decision to set more stringent limits with additional pollutants to measures. 
➢ The increased requirements for monitoring and modelling.  
• Most European countries are already covered by a network of air monitoring stations with 
daily or weekly collection, consolidation and publication of data, complemented by models 
for area -wide air q uality assessment and forecast. SUEZ supports in particular a wider 
adoption of the unified air  quality index covering sulphur dioxide, nitrogen dioxide, 
particulate matter (PM10 and PM2.5) and ozone and to make it available through a public 
source providing an hourly update and a forecast for the upcoming days 
• It is indeed important to support the deployment of integrated solutions and 
technologies (connected command centers, high -tech micro-sensors, data platforms, 3D 
modelling, predictive models an d real-time monitoring…) with the objective of reducing 
overall air pollution.  
• Considering the costs associated with air pollution and its impact on city attractiveness, 
investment in smart management and remote -control solutions and services for cities 
also contribute to reducing pressure on the municipal budget . On this point, SUEZ 
recommends that financing to support the implementation of good quality real-time air 
monitoring and modelling solutions should be made available.  A good example which 
could be fostered is the 1,500m€ NextGenerationEU Funds obtained by Spain in order to 
finance its Low Emission Zone monitoring (traffic, air quality etc.)   
➢ More stringent penalties and access to justice rights.  
➢ Air Quality Plan proposals . SUEZ supports t he submitting of short -term action plans from 
Member States to the Commission two months after the adoption of th eir Air Quality Plans  
and communication to the public and to appropriate organizations such as environmental 
organizations, consumer organizations, organizations representing the interests of sensitive 
population and vulnerable groups.  
➢ The establishment of a review of state of science on pollutants and their effects on human 
health and the environment.

4. Conclusion 
We believe initiatives, which combine digital innovation and reduction in air pollution, will find 
resonance with the European Green Deal (Zero Pollution Action Plan, Preserving Europe’s natural 
capital) and strategies for Shaping Europe’s Digital Future. Success in achieving good air quality is also 
dependent on the level of ambition applied in other legislative acts (Industrial Emissions Directive, 
etc.).  
First and foremost, monitoring and modelling is crucial to understanding the quantity and types of 
air pollution present. Therefore, SUEZ fully supports the numerous and detailed provisions tabled in 
this revision.  
Air treatment solutions also help tackle GHG emissions, thereby contributing to the EU’s ambitions on 
emissions reduction by 2030 and carbo n neutrality by 2050. While the revision to the Air Quality 
Directive does not tackle air treatment solutions,  it is nonetheless a complementary and necessary 
solution for specific circumstances : for example, locations where air pollution is signific ant and/or 
locations where people are particularly sensitive to air pollution for health reasons  (metro stations, 
school yards in densely populated cities). 
As air pollution is a crucial health and environmental matter , we recommend maintaining the high 
ambition proposed in the revision during the trialogues.

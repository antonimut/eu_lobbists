ETUC input to the European Commission call for evidence  
on the EU Talent Pool 
 
The ETUC remains highly critical on the development of the EU Talent Pool , which seems to take us 
back to earlier models of labour mig ration designed for, and driven by,  employers, which the ETUC 
clearly rejects. However, the ETUC welcomes the call for evidence in order to assess the possible policy 
options. 
One important element for such an instrument  is its governance and accoun tability, including the 
involvement of trade unions in both origin and EU destination countries at all relevant levels and in all 
stages from design and negotiation to implementation, monitoring and evaluation.  
From the trade union per spective, a key aspect is who and how the working and emplo yment 
conditions will be checked. This has been a point being raised before and after the implementation of 
the Talent Pool Pilot. It needs to be underlined that it is not only about matching skills but about the 
possibility for migrant workers to access decent and good quality jobs based on the principle of equal 
treatment.  
It is said that the Talent Pool m ay support the implementation of the Talent Partnerships . ETUC 
remains critical and concerned  about the trend towards the use of bilateral labour mi gration 
agreements and partnerships with third countries as a means of labour migration governance.  Both 
the Talent Pool and Talent Partnerships  should address the need for transparency, information and 
involvement of social partners at relevant levels , coverage of r ecruitment fees and travel costs by 
employers, as well as access to information for workers , including labour and trade union rights, 
complaints and redress mechanisms. The Talent Pool should provide reliable information to migrant 
workers at all stages, including the available support from trade unions and civil society organisations. 
Fair recruitment standards, including bans on abusive practices and recruitment fees must be uphold. 
This should build on the  fair recruitment policies and principles of the ILO and the WHO Global Code 
on the Int ernational Recruitment of Healthcare workers, including social partner initiatives such as 
the EPSU-HOSPEEM Code of Conduct .  Tools such as  the ITUC Migrant R ecruitment Advisor  that 
allows migrant workers to review recruitment agencies should be considered.  
The Talent Pool should consider the validation and recognition of skills and qualifications, which is a 
practical barrier that migrant workers experience. Workers’ skills and qualifications should be valued, 
assessed and swiftly recognised, as necessary, whether or not documentation is available.  
The ETUC is on the view that the Talent Pool should be open to all  workers, across sectors and skills 
levels, a nd in a non-discriminatory manner. Both European and national labour migration policies 
continue to focus on ly on  ‘highly skilled’ workers . This instrument should be an opportunity to 
encourage access to decent labour migration pathways across different occupations and skills.  
Data protection considerations, such as how the data will be collected and processed, should be taken 
into consideration . There should be strict data protection safeguards for the migrant workers 
concerned.

Background information 
 
All countries in the EU have severe problems with labour shortages especially in the technical fields 
and these problems will only worsen due to demographic developments. Indeed, the quickly 
tightening labour market across Europe today is the number one problem for companies that put the 
lack of human resources on top of their agendas. 
 
MET companies do not escape this reality and repeatedly state that labour shortages are 
increasingly hampering their competitiveness and their ability to innovate and provide quality jobs.   
STEM and ICT professionals, for example, are needed in many fields, including emerging ones such 
as the electric vehicle production. Furthermore, workers with a STEM background are crucial for the 
MET industries to continue to move forward. Our companies have also a pressing need of Data 
analysts, Engineers, Cybersecurity specialists, ICT specialists, as well as workers with the adequate 
analytical skills and system design skills. All these professions and skills sets are indispensable to 
ensure the success of the green and the digital transformation that the industries are undergoing, 
especially in critical sectors such as the automotive and aerospace industries. 
 
Labour shortages are due, amongst others, to the demographic situation with less active people and 
the ageing of the European population that result in a reduced pool of available workers. Against this 
background, Ceemet calls on the European and national policy makers to develop and implement a 
well-planned and coordinated strategy that includes a combination of measures that address 
migration, skills, employment and labour market related issues. We are, as a matter of fact, of the 
firm belief that implementing well designed legal migration policies is certainly an important tool to 
address labour shortages in the EU.  
 
It is for these reasons that the tech & industry employers welcomed the Commission” Skills and 
Talent package” that was released in April 2022 and that contains a mix of legal and operational 
measures intended to address labour market shortages in the EU. 
 
As regards the two legislative proposals aiming at revising the “single-permit directive” and the “long 
term residence” directive, Ceemet calls for their fast adoption. We agree to any measure focused at 
putting in place an efficient, simplified and less bureaucratic legislative framework, in order to 
improve legal migration to the EU. 
 
Within its operational pillar, the Commission announced that it had planned to launch an EU talent 
pool by mid-2023, in order to facilitate the match between Third Country Nationals (TCN) and job 
opportunities in EU businesses. Ceemet welcomes this initiative as it will facilitate the recruitment of 
the (qualified) workers and talent that EU companies are looking for.

• The tech and industry employers agree with the overall objective of this  initiative aimed at 
providing the Member States with an EU-level tool to facilitate international recruitment and 
yield opportunities for qu alified TCN to work in sectors of EU interest. The envisaged tool 
would enable the EU to attract skilled workers from abroad in a targeted manner, to address 
labour shortages and support the EU’s transition towards a green and digital economy.   
• We also agree to the Commission overall target of avoiding ‘brain drain’.  
• We agree with the Commission approach that labour migration can be part of the response 
to tackle demographic changes and meet future labour market demands. 
• We also share the Commission´s analysis on the challenges that are identified in relation to 
international recruitment, such as the difficulties about the genuineness and comparability of 
foreign qualifications, difficulties in identifying, selecting and recruiting interested TCN due to 
the uncertainty and incomparability of foreign qualifications etc. 
The proposed policy options 
 
As regards the proposed policy options, Ceemet would favor options 2 and 3.  The tech and industry 
employers consider that option 2 can be quickly implemented as it will not entail a legislative 
instrument and the tools to attract TCN are already in place. However, at the same time, we need to 
get option 3 started so that we will have broader and better possibilities, EU-wide, to attract the 
international talent we need. Indeed, the tech and industry employers are of the opinion that options 
2 and 3 do not rule each other out. For this reason, we favor to get both options started.  
 
Ceemet considers that option 2 of the call for evidence (building on existing EU tools) is the 
best option in the short term as, in spite of the fact that its impact will be limited, immediate 
implementation is possible.  
 
The advantages of developing an EU talent pool through a web portal is that it would enable 
candidates to express their interest in working in the EU in a simple manner. Further, the biggest 
benefit of using a talent pool based on a web portal is the time saved. Instead of lengthy and 
expensive recruiting measures, suitable talent would be found with just a few mouse clicks. Filter 
options that lead to pre-selection of candidates or to specific target groups (based on skills 
shortages) could be easily installed in the web portal. In order to find good, suitable applications, it 
is important that the web portal is easy to use and easily accessible to all: companies and potential 
talents.  
 
As the platform will be built upon an extensive database, a methodological approach and adequate 
structure of the data is needed.  
 
In terms of policy tools, “the web portal talent pool” could be developed through a non-binding 
instrument – a Recommendation. This will facilitate its rapid adoption and implementation. 
 
Ceemet also welcomes the possibility of upgrading and modernizing the EU immigration portal in 
order to provide with accessible, interactive and user-friendly information to TCN interested in 
moving and working in the EU on relevant migration schemes and requirements, as the portal will 
function as a complementary tool to the EU web portal. 
 
Together with the web portal, Ceemet believes that dedicated job matching events in specific 
sectors/occupations aimed at facilitating the match between EU employers and TCN in specific 
sectors/occupations could also serve to attract talent to the EU, but again in a limited manner. 
However, we also support the promotion of such events since the logistics and the costs involved in 
their organization are limited.  
 
As said above, Ceemet favors option 2 for the short term and option 3 for the medium and long term.

Indeed, a real talent pool open to all Member States for targeted migration purposes (as laid down 
in option 3) will have a larger impact as creating an EU talent pool will avoid that Member States 
have to set-up their own platform at national level. Further, employers will be able to access a larger 
pool of talent and TCN will be able to access more job opportunities across all the EU.  
 
It is for these reasons, that we also call for option 3 to get started as soon as possible, as the 
policy instrument required for the development of such a tool is legally binding and it would 
thus require lengthy discussions amongst the Member States before the legislative act can 
be adopted. 
 
The EU talent pool, as laid down in option 3, will address specific labour shortages, and from the 
sectoral approach it would be beneficial as it could include more medium/low qualified workers that 
are also indispensable for the MET industries. 
 
We also welcome that the EU talent pool builds on certain elements of EURES (without extending 
the EURES objectives) notably the interoperability with national recruitment portals. A real talent 
pool that facilitates automatic matching (in the same way as it works on the EURES portal) is certainly 
a key to success. 
 
The interoperability with national systems/platform/recruitment portals as well as the technical details 
and design of the pool are yet other critical factors to ensure the success of the talent pool. 
 
We agree with the Commission that this option remains a voluntary system to the Member States. 
 
Finally, Ceemet would like to highlight that this pool will only be a successful initiative if it is easily 
accessible for SMEs and TCN as well as designed in a user-friendly manner in order to facilitate the 
recruitment of talents. 
 
It is of the utmost importance that the Commission launches targeted communication campaigns to 
promote this “talent pool” amongst SMEs once it is put in place.

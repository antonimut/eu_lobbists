 
EURONEXT CLEARING’S POSITION PAPER TO THE 
COMMISSION’S EMIR 3.0 PROPOSAL 
Euronext Clearing 
  
1. On December 7th, the European Commission published its proposal for the EMIR review. 
With this proposal, t he Commission is pursuing two objectives : (i) the need to address 
the potential risks to EU financial st ability, stemming from the continued overreliance on 
systemic non-EU (UK) CCPs and (ii) the need to build more attractive, resilient and robust 
EU clearing infrastructures, to strengthen the CMU and increase EU CCPs competitiveness, 
also on an international stage. 
 
2. Euronext Clearing , a multi -asset clearing house, provides proven risk management 
capabilities on 14 markets, across a range of trading venues including Euronext Milan, 
MTS, BrokerTec and Hi -mtf. Asset classes cleared include equities, ETF s, Closed -end 
Funds, Financial Derivatives, Commodities (Agricultural & Energy) and Fixed income (Cash 
and Repos markets).  
 
3. As part of the Euronext Group, the CCP will become Euronext’s CCP of choice for its 
cash equity, listed derivatives and commodities markets, providing a harmonised 
clearing framework across Euronext venues. In this context, to support this consolidation 
project (the Clearing Migration Programme), Euronext is scaling up the capabilities of 
Euronext Clearing, addressing the c urrent fragmentation of the European post trading 
landscape. 
 
4. This strategic ambition will allow Euronext to directly manage this key client service and 
provide a harmonized and reliable clearing framework across all Euronext trading 
venues, providing addi tional efficiencies at post -trading level, and addressing 
fragmentation1. Although currently not in scope of the clearing migration programme, 
Euronext Clearing continues to monitor the evolutions within the OTC clearing space, in 
light of the possibility of extending, at a future stage and depending on market demands, 
its service offering also towards OTC asset classes, to provide an additional solution in the 
European space.  
 
5. Clearing infrastructures are the backbone of the European financial economy and are 
expected to play a pivotal role in the context of European growth and recovery. Following 
Brexit, Europe deserves resilient, safe, and efficient clearing infrastructures in order to 
foster growth and gain further momentum for the fulfilment of the Capital Markets Union. 
Set out below are our views on the Commission proposal. 
 
EMIR 3.0 – Euronext Clearing Positioning 
General remarks  
6. Euronext welcomes the Commission’s initiative . In order to overcome the structural 
financial stability concerns related to overreliance on third country CCPs, it is first and  
foremost necessary to improve the attractiveness of clearing in the EU, by supporting the

development of EU domestic capacity.  As highly regulated entities, to remain (globally) 
competitive and able to react to market circumstances, clear and efficient approval 
processes are critical . Also, an efficient and effective supervisory framework should 
enable small incremental innovations,  ensuring market needs can be met with in an

Amendments to EMIR article 15 & 49 authorisation procedures 
 
7. When CCPs develop  additional services or make significant changes to their risk 
management models, the EMIR authorisation procedures 2 play a key role in ensuring 
that CCPs are able to pursue and implement innovation, addressing industry 
needs in the context of a dynamic  global market environment. Clear and efficient 
approval processes are therefore critical in order to ensure that EU CCPs can, in the 
medium to long term, compete at arm’s length, with their international, non-EU peers.  
 
8. In order to deliver the post-trading infrastructure that Europe deserves, CCPs need legal 
certainty, in the form of clear procedures and clearly defined timelines for 
obtaining the necessary approvals. We note that these features are currently lacking 
in the present EMIR framework. For this reason, we welcome the Commission’s proposal 
to introduce defined timelines and criteria within the level 1 text  for the application 
of EMIR Articles 15 and 49 authorisation procedures. This will deliver the necessary legal 
clarity, that market actors seek, while also further promoting convergence of supervisory 
approaches. 
 
9. In addition to the lack of clarity on timelines, the current framework has discouraged to 
a significant extent small incremental innovations, which also play a key role in 
ensuring the resiliency of a CCP . Innovation sometimes occurs in “small” steps, 
consisting in fine tunings and refinements of existing models. The current framework also 
presents a level of uncertainty whether small incremental innovations should be subject 
to an Article 15 or 49 procedure. This uncertainty complicates the business development 
at CCPs.  
 
10. Therefore, we support the proposal for 10-day non -objection procedur es for the 
approval of both products and services and changes to risk models  which are 
deemed of lesser significance from a risk perspective. This will enable CCPs to implement 
small incremental changes and non-material variations of its service offering, under a 
more proportionate supervisory framework.  
 
11. We note that the effectiveness of these proposals will depend on the harmonised approach 
set by Regulatory Technical Standards, which will specify documents, format, and content 
of the applications to be submitted by the CCP. Today, the content of the applications  
under Articles 15 and 49  are not clearly defined , leading to uncertainty and potentially 
divergent approaches across the EU. To promote the global competitiveness, it is essential 
the market can rely on pre-defined timelines with a clear understanding of the documents 
needed to prepare for regulatory scrutiny.

12. While we promote supervisory convergence and a clear description of documents to be 
submitted and timelines to be followed, this should not result in an overly prescriptive and 
disproportionate approach, which could outweigh the benefit of the new framework.  
 
13. This also applies to non-objection procedures introduced under EMIR Articles 17a and 49, 
paragraph 1 , second subparagraph , with respect to the scope and level of detail ed 
information requested to demonstrate the presence of the conditions for applying the non-
objection procedure(s): a proportionate and efficient approach  will be a key element for 
the effectiveness of this new tool.3 
 
Supervisory arrangements  
 
14. The Commission  proposal introduces several changes, intended to streamline the 
interaction between supervisory authorities. These include  the set-up of new supervisory 
structures, such as Joint Supervisory Teams (JSTs) and a Joint Monitoring Mechanism 
(JMT). 
 
15. From our experience,  the present EMIR framework is indeed at points characterised by 
overlap of tasks and responsibilities (between NCAs, ESMA, College), which has at times 
lead to (unintended) duplication of efforts by both CCPs and relevant authorities. This was 
also acknowledged by the Commission in its impact assessment4.  
 
16. Concerning JSTs, we understand that the Commission’s intention is to improve 
cooperation between relevant authorities to achieve clearer responsibilities and avoiding 
unnecessary duplicative work.  While we support  the Commission’s objective, from a 
practical perspective, it is important to ensure that the institution of JSTs will not 
result in a further duplication of supervisory structures and responsibilities, i.e., 
on top of the responsibilities already allocated to the CCP’s NCA(s).  
 
17. The proposal also includes the creation of a new Joint Oversight Mechanism , a 
technical-political monitoring forum, composed of several EU authorities and agencies, 
with tasks related to the monitoring of the implementation of EMIR 3.0 and the broader 
development of the EU industry. Also in this case, it is important that this new supervisory 
structure does not distort the current governance  framework and allocation of 
responsibilities. 
 
18. Lastly, we support proposals  strengthening ESMA’s role in promoting coordination 
amongst NCAs, ensuring a level playing field across the industry, which is a precondition 
to competition.

19. The Commission aims to further specify membership requirements in a way that prevents 
CCPs to becoming Members of another CCP. 
 
20. While understanding the risk rationale behind this proposal, we would like to highlight the 
need for additional clarity in respect to interoperability arrangements . 
Interoperability arrangements are an important asset of the European post trading. These 
arrangements legally rely on a special membership status awarded by an interoperating 
CCP to its peer. Therefore, we would suggest clarifying within the legislative process, that 
the proposals preventing membership do not affect interoperability agreements as defined 
in EMIR Title V. 
 
Intraday Variation Margin pass on – new Article 41 
 
21. The Commission proposal intends to amend EMIR Article 41, by adding that a CCP shall 
strive to the best of its ability not to hold intraday variation margin calls after all payments 
due have been received. We understand the scope of said proposal is limited to futures 
products. We understand this is to prevent unwanted liquidity pressure on the position of 
the CCP’s participants. In this context, we note that in 2020 , the ESRB made a 
recommendation of similar nature, leading to significant discussions within the industry 5.  
 
22. Although we understand the Commission is introducing a best effort obligation on CCPs, 
we have concerns on this initiative . From our experience during the COVID-19 market 
stress, we have seen that the payment of intraday variation margins in the context 
of high volatile markets,  with no clear directional trend, could expose a CCP to 
additional significant risks and further aggravate the liquidity position of the 
participants: a rapid change of trend could dissolve the gains on a position, with ensuing 
need to reprocess margin cal ls. This would create further liquidity drag, in a moment of 
already volatile market circumstances 
 
23. In this case, a nother downside of intraday variation margins pass on, is the potential 
additional (and unnecessary) liquidity pressure on those participants having excess 
non-cash collateral deposited at the CCP that could no longer be used to cover variation 
margins (thus forcing a cash call from the CCP that have to pay variation margins to who 
is on the right side of the Market). Currently, Euronext Clearing allows the coverage with 
non-cash collateral as there is no intraday variation margin pay out. 
 
24. Also, a proposal that is  limited to va riation margins on futures could lead to  the 
unintended consequence of aggravating the liquidity positio n of certain 
members vis -à-vis the CCP,  particularly those dealing with certain specific hedging 
strategies, which characterize their trading behaviours.

As an example : in order to hedge an exposure on a long -term future on a fixed income security, 
market participants can make use of short-term options, which are rolled forward (usually quarterly) in 
order to match the duration of the originating position. Under the Commission’s proposal, increases in 
the value of the hedge position would however not be immediately passed through, as with the future 
position, with the risk of creating a liquidity mismatch for the participant, depending on the market’s 
direction.  
 
25. The proposal would also add complexities, as  the implementation of an instant pass -
through mechanism for variation margin calls, would be legally, in addition to 
operationally, challenging, requiring  extensive reframing of  the market’s legal, risk, 
technical and operational existing arrangements concerning margin collection and 
redistribution and associated management of liquidity exposures. 
 
26. Lastly, noting that the various constraints and considerations listed above do not depend 
on the CCP’s ability, we fear that the wording chosen by the Commission (grounded on 
the concept of CCP’s ability) might deliver a misleading message to the market, giving 
rise to expectations on erroneous best practices. For all these reasons, we would welcome 
the deletion of the above described provision.

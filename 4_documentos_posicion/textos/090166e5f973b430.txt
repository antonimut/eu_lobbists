Indigo welcomes European Commission`s legislative proposal for a regulatory framework for the 
certification of high integrity carbon removals 
The goal of limiting global warming to 1.5C is only just alive and we need to draw on all the available 
solutions, including nature-based carbon removals, if we are to have any chance of hitting that 
target. Indigo believes that certified carbon removals can make a meaningful contribution to climate 
change mitigation and enable agriculture to be part of the climate solution, but certification must be 
backed by science, high-quality standards, and rigorous methodologies. The move to establish an EU 
regulatory framework for carbon removals is therefore a good step forward, but there is detailed 
technical work yet to be done to realize its full potential.  We strongly believe that it is possible to 
scale carbon removal solutions in agriculture enabling it to not only play its part in tackling the 
climate crisis but also establishing a durable new revenue stream for farmers and making both their 
land and EU food systems more resilient in the face of a changing climate.  
We recently announced1 the second crop of high-quality agricultural soil carbon credits, five times 
more than the first, validating agriculture as a meaningful solution to climate change and a recurring 
source of additional income for farmers. This second issuance demonstrated that scientifically-
rigorous crediting for ag soil carbon projects is not only possible, but also scalable and repeatable. 
The ability to accurately quantify and verify the amount of carbon removed has been a key obstacle 
preventing agriculture from scaling up this natural carbon sink. To solve this problem, we have 
developed an internationally recognized and proven model, that combines science, technology and 
data to establish the implementation of sustainable on-farm practices, mobilize new financial 
resources to incentivize  farmers to change practice and a credible, nature-based climate solution for 
businesses. 
We welcome the Commission's framework proposal to raise the quality bar for carbon removals. 
This is a no-regret measure that will benefit the atmosphere and European farmers through high 
quality carbon removals, improved soils and environmental co-benefits. 
We are honored to join the EU Expert Group on Carbon Removals as an observer and look forward 
to contributing our expertise and experience in agricultural soil carbon crediting projects. In doing 
so, we would like to share our feedback on the main points of the proposal that we believe are 
relevant to establishing a robust EU Certification System and scaling up high quality carbon 
removals:  
Carbon Farming / Definition of land-based carbon removals: Focused exclusively on carbon 
removals, the definition should be broadened to include the full suite of climate benefits of changes 
in agricultural land management (including non-CO2 GHGs) through practice change. Not only as a 
co-benefit, but also as the value of the soil carbon credit to reward farmers' sustainability efforts. 
However, this should not come at the expense of soil-related carbon benefits, which should be 
required to be demonstrated on all projects.  
Methodology: Established protocols and methodologies should be taken into account in the 
development of the EU standard. The Climate Action Reserve (CAR) Soil Enrichment Protocol and 
VERRA VM0042 Methodology for Improved Agricultural Land Management, are proven to ensure 
high quality carbon removals in the voluntary carbon market by ensuring removals meet the key 
tests of realness, additionality, permanence, avoidance of leakage and no double counting.

Moreover, key to successful implementation of both of these methodologies is the required 
adherence to strict criteria (e.g., VCS VMD0053) for the calibration, validation, and calculation of 
uncertainty when biogeochemical process-based models are employed in the quantification.  
Quantification: The equation for quantifying net carbon removal benefits is sound, but would 
benefit from replacing the term "CRtotal" with "CRproject" or "CRactivity" for clarity.  
Baseline: While the proposal to use a standardized baseline offers some advantages, in our 
experience a dynamic, individualized baseline using actual historical practices and data provides the 
most robust estimates of carbon removal. If periodically reviewed, we recommend that the baseline 
be reassessed every 10 years and validated during the subsequent verification. If standardized 
baselines are to be developed, we urge caution and deep engagement with the scientific 
community. Some improved forest management protocols used in the U.S. are built around a 
reference carbon stocking rate (tC/ac). This approach has led to selective enrollment by highly 
specialized forest carbon project developers who understand how to target lands already well above 
the common practice metric. In turn, this has led to some significant criticism in recent years. In the 
case of agriculture, regardless of the technical approach to developing baselines, additionality 
should require some change in land management practice(s). 
Additionality: The principle should be that it goes beyond Union statutory requirements, allowing 
for the stacking of public and private incentives to change practices, as long as the resulting 
environmental benefits are uniquely claimed. Focusing on the farmer, we believe that a combination 
of voluntary public and private incentives can best motivate farmers to change and sustain practices, 
with CAP eco schemes providing an effective kick-start.  
Permanence: Soil carbon credits should also be considered permanent. There should be no 
discussion of temporary crediting mechanisms. 100 years has been established as a reasonable 
compromise between atmospheric benefit and timescales that are practical for human 
decisionmaking. In scaling up high quality carbon credits, the key to managing permanence risks is to 
zoom out from the single field view to the aggregated, or grouped, project level. Most importantly, 
liability should remain with the registry or the provider, not the farmer. This again highlights why it 
is critical that trusted, independent and established registries such as the Climate Action Reserve or 
VERRA are included in a robust EU certification system to ensure quality standards such as 
permanence with conservative credit issuance and security buffer pools to manage permanence 
risks. 
Certification bodies: For cross-border and multi-country projects, EU-recognized certification 
schemes should be allowed to designate a single certification body to minimize additional 
administrative burdens and to support the scaling up of high-quality carbon removals. 
Certification schemes: We recommend looking to the ICROA certification standards, ICAO CORSIA 
program criteria, as well as the California Air Resources Board requirements for compliance Offset 
Project Registries for additional guidelines that should be required for certification schemes. It is 
imperative that schemes be thoroughly vetted for competence, governance, conflict of interest, 
stakeholder engagement practices, adherence to ISO standards, and proven project processing 
capabilities.

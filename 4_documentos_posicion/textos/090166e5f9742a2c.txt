Commission's proposal COM(2022)698 to review EMIR (Regulation 648/2012) 
including targeted amendments to the CRD, IFD and the UCITS Directive as 
well as the CRR, and the Money Market Funds (MFF) Regulation 
Finance Norway welcomes the proposed amendments and are generally supportive of measures to  
make markets more efficient while improving market security and robustness. Finance Norway is 
supportive of measures to make CCPs situated in the internal market more attractive and robust, 
however, we are not convinced that the benefits of moving clearing by force into the internal market 
exceeds the cost of doing so. Finally, Finance Norway also share the objective of enhancing safety, 
robustness, and efficiency in post-trading arrangements.  
  
Finance Norway has followed the discussion around the need to establish clearing capacity inside the 
internal market to secure the function also in stressed market conditions. We acknowledge these 
arguments. The presented proposals contain many elements that will benefit the internal market for 
derivatives clearing. Finance Norway welcomes the initiatives that bring new products quickly to 
market and meet demands from clearing members and clients faster. More transparency and 
increased competition are important to have a well-functioning clearing market in the EU.  
 
However, we have some concerns related to: 
• requirements on active accounts  
• the additional Pillar 2 measures for concentration risk.  
• the margin requirements for single stock equity options  
• index options and transparency of margin models for clients. 
 
Active accounts 
The structure of the market for derivatives clearing was created through the enforcement of EMIR 
and was established before the London global financial marketplace left the internal market through 
Brexit. The ambition to now force a movement of clearing activities to CCPs established in the internal 
market, through the proposed Article 7a will have a significant impact on clearing costs for EU banks 
and especially for medium sized and regional banks. By forcing banks to relocate some of the clearing 
activities and by splitting operations to more CCPs and exposures to several default funds, we also see 
a potential risk for market fragmentation.  
 
It should also be noted that the majority of NOK related financial derivative products are cleared 
outside the EU. As per today, the liquidity in NOK on EU based CCPs is very modest. 
 
Finance Norway favor improvements in cross-border initiatives, such as the supervisory framework 
where third country CCPs are supervised by ESMA under EMIR 2.2. It is important to have a sound 
competition among the CCPs that are serving the EU markets. The participants must have access to 
the best liquidity pools and thereby efficiently hedge risk for clients and keep the costs low. Splitting 
up portfolios could also reduce market efficiency and increase the overall risks in the market.  
 
This is a further step on the path that in practice has been followed since Brexit. However, the 
potential benefits of the proposed measures are abstract and should be weighed against the very 
concrete costs the banking industry will be forced to take on to meet with the proposed obligations.

Pillar 2 capital measures 
The proposed amendment of Article 104 (1) of the CRD give competent authorities discretion in 
relation to the financial institutions’ concentration risk in use of central clearing counterparts. Banks 
and other entities use derivatives to manage their risks, and subsequently to reduce systemic risks.  
 
The obligation to clear with central counterparties, and to pose collateral for this activity is set by the 
legislator. The concentration risk targeted through this proposal is a systemic feature of the way the 
clearing obligation is crafted. Moving a small portion of the clearing from the deep and liquid 
international market for clearing will not substantially influence the risk concentration in the large 
counterparties. Therefore, it seems not justified and not so clear why this Pillar 2 tool is added to the 
prudential framework.  
 
Replacing the institutions choice of allocation between different CCPs with supervisory intervention 
powers marks the introduction of a new principle in institutions’ handling of derivatives. The measure 
is unnecessary from a stability point of view, and we rather perceive it as a move to increase potential 
business and liquidity for EU CCPs. 
 
For clearing cross-border we understand ESMA under EMIR 2.2 has a wide toolbox, and further 
supervisory solutions are a better way forward in order to increase competitiveness among the CCPs 
that are serving the European markets.  
 
Single stock equity options and index options 
Finance Norway strongly supports the joint ESAs proposal to extend the temporary exemption from 
margin requirements for single stock equity options and index options in article 38 of the RTS on the 
timing of when certain risk management procedures will start to apply for the purpose of the 
exchange of collateral. Indeed, we would welcome an exemption for these products from the margin 
requirements as long as they are not in place in all relevant jurisdictions.  
 
Transparency of margin models 
We support all measures to increase transparency in the clearing market. The feasibility of disclosure 
relies on information being supplied by the CCPs to their clients. Under Article 38 in EMIR, CCPs and 
their clearing members shall disclose prices and fees. Therefore, the transparency in the proposal 
needs to be supported by the CCPs to their members. Please note that in the Nordics the CCPs are 
calculating the margin down to the client level as well.

22nd March 2023 
 
JBCE’s input on the proposal for the regulation of Carbon Removal Certification 
 
Introduction 
Japan Business Council in Europe (JBCE) appreciates the opportunity to provide comments 
on the Commission proposal1 for the regulation  of Carbon Removal Certification . JBCE 
believes the proposed regulation is an important step in stimulating the markets for carbon 
removals and achieving the goal of carbon neutrality.  
Nevertheless, we would like to feedback as detailed in the two points below. 
 
(1) Flexibility for the definition of “long-term”  
There are multiple CDR (Carbon Dioxide Removal) measures in the world such as BECCS, 
DACCS, carbon farming, biochar, CCU products , etc. , and they all have different carbon 
storage periods and different risks of releasing CO2 into atmosphere again. 
On the other hand,  in Article 6 of the  regulation of  Carbon Removal Certification , the 
Commission says the following: 
“An operator or group of operators shall demonstrate that a carbon removal activity aims at 
ensuring the long-term storage of carbon.”  
 
However, JBCE believes that stipulating a specific, minimum period to be observed by each 
CDR measure will be particularly challenging since it will exclude CDR measures for 
temporary carbon storage such as carbon farming and some CCU products, which are useful 
measures to achieve the goal of carbon neutrality. 
 
JBCE hopes that the Commission will consider and develop  (1-a) dynamic LCA and 
(1-b) e-fuels, whilst listening to stakeholders’ opinions that offer different possibilities. 
 
(1-a) dynamic LCA

In order to include temporary carbon storage in the product as a certified CDR measure in 
this regulation, more flexibility is required in  how to comply with “ long-term”. One of the 
countermeasures for this issue will be developing the methodologies of dynamic LCA. 
Dynamic LCA is suitable for accounting for both temporary and permanent carbon storage  
as referred to in the JRC technical report2. 
 
(1-b) e-fuels 
E-fuels made by CCU, such as RFNBO (Renewable Fuels of Non-Biological Origin) and 
biomass fuels, tend to be combusted soon after production, so the period that the carbon is 
stored is very short . Therefore, there is a possibility that e-fuels cannot be  regarded as 
certified CDR measures. 
However, JBCE believes that e-fuels have great potential for displacing the existing fuels 
made from petroleum and due consideration should be given to this contribution to reducing 
CO2 . 
 
Methodologies for CCU products are still rarely seen in the world, even in voluntary schemes, 
but we can refer to one example drafted by Verra3. The main objective of t his draft is to 
provide the methodologies for CCU plastics, but the approach to displacement offers us the 
following insights. 
“Emissions associated with the production of virgin plastic through traditional processes that 
are displaced by the plastic material manufactured by the project must be accounted for in 
the baseline scenario.” 
 
(2) Harmonisation with other CDR measures 
JBCE welcomes the Commission’s engagement in this proposal and believes that further 
harmonisation with other CDR-related schemes, including the voluntary market, is required 
in the future.  
“To ensure a cost -effective certification process, those technical harmonised rules on 
certification should also have the objective of reducing unnecessary administrative 
burden for operators, or group of operators , in particular for Small and Medium 
Enterprises (SMEs), including small farmers and foresters.”

JBCE hopes that the Commission will in particular harmonise and set direction with 
regard to (2-a) carbon credit and (2-b) MRV (Monitoring, Reporting and Verification) as 
follows, whilst listening to stakeholders’ opinions that offer different possibilities. This 
will help to reduce more burdens on CDR operators, especially for SMEs. 
 
(2-a) carbon credit 
Carbon credits are important aspects when considering expected incentives obtained from 
CDR markets and their plans for investment and business model. However, carbon credits 
have not yet been sufficiently harmonised.  
For example, the Clean Development Mechanism  (CDM) defined in the Kyoto protocol  
introduced carbon credits for carbon farming removals  called CERs (Certified E mission 
Reductions), but CERs are not allowed to be traded under the EU ETS. 
 
(2-b) MRV (Monitoring, Reporting and Verification) 
MRV would be one of the burdens for CDR operators, therefore clear methodologies would 
make it smooth to start or keep their business. 
There are multiple CDR measures such as BECCS, DACCS, carbon farming, biochar, CCU 
products, etc., but methodologies for MRV are still unclear in some CDR measures.  
For example, CCS-related technologies such as BECCS and DACCS can apply the existing 
methodologies like the CCS directive4 and Monitoring and Reporting Regulation (MRR)5, but 
the MRV methodologies of CCU products, etc. are still unclear. 
JBCE believes that the Commission should develop and establish MRV methodologies for 
these unclear CDR measures in the future.

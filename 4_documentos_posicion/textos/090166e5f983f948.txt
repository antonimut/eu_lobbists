E.ON feedback on the EU Commission’s proposal for carbon removals certification 
E.ON welcomes the European Commission ’s proposal for a Regulation  establishing a  certification 
framework for carbon removals. As confirmed by the IPCC, carbon emissions reduction alone will not 
be sufficient to limit global temperature increase to 1,5°C. Thus c arbon removals will play a n 
indispensable part to reach global climate goals.  
The EU Commission’s proposal provides a  framework allow ing permanent carbon removal 
technologies to reach economic feasibility, while being monitored by a robust government system . 
Technologies of carbon dioxide removals are already well advanced, but  in order to unlock the 
commercial development of those technologies the certification of carbon removals is the crucial first 
step 
Action is needed swiftly and in a robust manner 
To scale up the deployment of  carbon removal technologies  and to meet the climate goals , 
investments are needed already today. The proposal is the important first step towards establishing a 
certification for carbon removals . E.ON acknowledges that putting up an certification system is both 
difficult and complex. Nevertheless, it is important that the process embraces the current momentum 
of carbon removals. The expert group should therefore be granted all means to advance swiftly, but 
nevertheless in a robust manner . Ideally the certification framework would be in operation by 2025 
with some planning conditions announced beforehand. This timeframe is important to give investors 
security and foresight to scale up carbon removal activities until 2030 , with first potential carbon 
removal project up and running in 2026. Especially projects with longer lead time and big investments, 
such as BECCS and DACCS, need to have the certificate framework in place swiftly.   
Definition of market conditions  underpinned with clear methodologies  and carbon removal 
subcategories 
A clear definition for each different carbon removal activity which differ strongly by their nature and 
environmental impact  should be included . Carbon removal activities with a permanent  geological 
storage have a significant longer duration with a high environmental offset, but also higher economic 
deployment and risks than other types of carbon removals. Hence the methodologies need to include 
counting, monitoring, verifying and additionality to cater for different types of carbon removals  and 
their duration.  Consequently, w e encourage the EU Commission to consider including in the 
certification framework different sub -categories of certificates, which better reflect the diversity of 
carbon removal solutions and their characteristics , with a special attention to their  level of 
permanency and with respect to the return of investment. 
Special attention shall also be given to BECCS activities, where both fossil and biogenic carbon can be 
captured in the same unit without being able to physically separate the two fractions (e.g. waste-to-
energy power plants and cement sites). In those cases, the methodologies must provide monitoring 
and sharing regulation to differ between those fractions and ensure the correct  allocation to EU-ETS 
certificates and carbon removal certificates. 
Once established the framework for the certification, it is important to make sure that carbon removal 
certificates are used in a manner coherent with EU’s climate policy and do not undermine CO2 
emission reduction goals. The current proposal does not provide any indication of how they may be 
valorized. It is important to create a market by setting quotas for carbon dioxide removals which 
incentivizes the purchase of high value certificates with longer duration and thus higher environmental 
value. Therefore, it will be crucial that carbon removal certificates can be credited to reach the set

quotas for carbon dioxide removals and allow trading mechanisms of those certificates. In addition, a 
credible and transparent system should be established in order to carry out the necessary audits on 
registries and avoid the double counting of such carbon removal certificates. 
Predictable establishment of baseline to provide investor security 
E.ON welcomes that the quantification of carbon removal activities will be a periodically update d in 
line with  the market evolvements. Nevertheless, in order to provide security for investors it is 
important to clearly defined how the baseline will be calculated and which periodic update that will be 
targeted. To incentivize the market development of carbon removal activities the baseline deduction 
mechanism should be only in force after e.g. 2030. Furthermore, it needs to be clearly defined what is 
included in the term “GHG increase”, giving special attention to the GHG emissions evoked by longer 
transport routes and their discriminatory nature which may result in increas ed rural industrial 
migration. 
Interconnection with other legislative files and national policies 
The sustainability of biomass is already ensured via the EU Taxonomy regulation and the Renewable 
Energy Directive. Nevertheless, when it comes to sustainability criteria, the current provisions of Article 
7 seem be substantially stricter than the equivalent requirement s in the Taxonomy . This may cause 
major barriers for any industrial-scale carbon removal project. It is therefore important to ensure that 
the CRCF proposal is in line with existing legislation.

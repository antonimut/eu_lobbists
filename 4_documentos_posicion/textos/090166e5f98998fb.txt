To the European Commission  
 
Assonime’s Position Paper on the EC Consultation on Listing Act – MAR and Prospectus 
We would like to thank the E uropean Commission for all the huge work done with the proposals published 
last December; they represent a great effort of simplification in line with the goals of Capital Market Union  
and reflect the suggestions of several Expert groups (HLF Report CMU, Oxera Report, TESG Report, Report of 
the Next CMU High Level Group).  
The reform of the EU rules on capital markets is of paramount importance  as one of the factors that may 
contribute attracting investments and improving confidence.  We therefore support many of the proposed 
amendments as we think that these go in the right direction of simplification, and we hope that the legislative 
process will be speeded up. However, we include some specific suggestions in the text below.  
 
Market Abuse (hereinafter “MAR”) 
MAR is an important piece of legislation, and we welcome most of the proposed amendments in this area  
which will simplify the burdens for listed issuers. In particular: 
We strongly support: 
- the narrowed disclosure obligation set forth in art. 17.1  which allows the communication of more 
mature information. 
The main proposed amendment pertains to art. 17.1 where intermediate steps in a protracted process are 
now excluded from the disclosure obligation; this amendment has the great merit to ensure tha t issuers do 
not have to disclose information that are not sufficiently mature, as only the final events should be 
communicated to the public, as implied in recital 58.  
We think that the above-mentioned amendment will solve the current fragmentation of approaches in the 
Member States  regarding the frequency of disclosure of inside information  and the use of the delay, as 
reported in the EC’s Impact Assessment . This amendment, together with the non -exhaustive list to be set 
forth by the European Commission  defining the future event in a protracted process that is subject to 
disclosure under art. 17.1 and the timing for disclosure, could bring more clarity.     
- the clarification of the condition to delay the disclosure (article 17.4 b). We appreciate the deletion of 
the general condition that the delay “is not likely to mislead the public” which was quite problematic and 
its replacement by examples drawn from ESMA’s earlier guidelines and Q&A.

- the clarification of market sounding regime (article 11) which is clearly defined as an option in order 
to benefit from the safe harbour ensured by art. 11 MAR; 
- the extension of the permanent insider list to all listed issuers (art. 18) 
- the higher threshold for the notification of managers’ transactions (art. 19). 
 
However, we would like also to suggest some modifications to: 
- the new procedure for the notification of the delay to the National Competent Authority  (hereinafter 
“NCA”) (article 17.4) 
According to paragraph 4, the notification to the NCA has to take place immediately after the decision to 
delay disclosure is taken, instead of immediately after the information is disclosed to the public. Indeed this 
change will not reduce the administrative burden for issuers who will still have to document their  decisions 
of delaying and during a strict time frame also communicate to the NCA. 
We suggest keeping the status quo; the notification to the NCA should be made only after the information is 
disclosed to the public and not before (also to avoid delay notifications of inside information that will not be 
published having lost meanwhile the nature of inside information, see ESMA MAR Q&A 5.2).  
- rumour (art. 17.7) 
Art. 17.7 states “‘ This paragraph includes situations where a rumour explicitly relates to inside information 
the disclosure of which has been delayed in accordance with paragraph 4 or 5, where that rumour is 
sufficiently accurate and reliable to indicate that the confidentiality of that information is no longer ensured”. 
We think that adding the word “reliable” can be misleading and confusing and should be eliminated from the 
text.  
- regime of sanctions (art. 30) 
For issuers a maximum administr ative sanction for infringements of article 17, 18 or 19 based on annual 
turnover, as envisaged by the proposal,  could be disproportionally high. To mitigate such sanction, we 
suggest reintroducing a fixed cap for both SMEs and other issuers.   
In particular, regarding infringements of articles 18 (insider lists) and 19 (managers’ transactions), which have 
no impact on market integrity as they are only burdensome bureaucratic tasks, the Commission’s proposal 
even introduces new turnover-related maximum sanctions.  
The Commission proposal also does not consider any modification on sanctions for natural persons (see article 
30(1)(i)). We suggest providing for a more proportionate framework also in this case. Administrative sanction 
for natural persons shall be based on annual compensation (instead of the total annual turnover) for 
infringements of article 17(1); moreover, we recommend to strongly reduce sa nctions for infringements of 
articles 17(4) (delay) (currently  1 million Euro), 18 (insider lists) and 19 (managers transactions) (both currently  
500,000 Euro). One tenths of the actual maximum amount seem appropriate to punish inadvertent breach of 
MAR.

We welcome a lighter regime for SMEs but we hold that the definition included in article 30 shall abandon the 
notion of the Commission Recommendation 2003/316/EC introduc ing a definition based on market 
capitalisation (500,000,000 or 1 billion Euro would be more appropriate), as suggested by the TESG Report and 
HIGH Level Forum CMU Report. 
 
We would like to suggest tackling some other issues regarding managers’ transactions (art. 19): 
 
- modifying art. 19.8 on the method for calculating the threshold for the managers’ transactions as to 
provide that once the threshold (e.g. 20.000 Euro) has been reached, the calculation of the threshold 
should restart from zero until a new threshold has been reached again (meaning that all the following 
amounts must be summed up until they reach again the threshold); in this case a manager will inform 
the market only when a new threshold is reached (e.g. 20/40/60.000) and clarify that transactions on 
different securities shall be calculated separately (art. 19.8); 
- excluding from the notification obligation of gifts, inheritances and donations , which were not 
included among the transactions to be notified under the old MAD, because they are completely 
passive from the PDMR’s point of view (see art. 10.2 let. (k), EU Delegated Regulation n. 2016/522), 
have no signalling value since the price field in th e notification form of managers’ transactions for a 
gift, donation or inheritance shall be populated with 0 (zero) (see ESMA MAR Q&A 7.4) and 
considering the difficulties to calculate the value of a donation, a gift or inheritance (see ESMA MAR 
Q&A 7.4); 
- clarifying that where performance shares are allocated to PDMRs as part of a remuneration package, 
the obligation to notify arises only when the PDMR sells the shares  as this is the moment in which 
there is a real signalling value for the market; as the ESMA Q&A (see 7.51) does not clarify the issue, 
we think that the moment in which shares are granted for free to PDMRs (meaning the moment in 
which shares are credited in the account of the PDMR) should not be notified (there  is no discretion 
by the PDMR who is passive and there is no signalling value for the market) while when the shares are 
sold there should be a notification. A  different interpretation would imply a duplication of 
notifications, more work to be done by the issuer’s staff and the increase of indirect costs. For 
phantom stock, the notification duty should be excluded as the PDMR has only the right to receive 
cash. The above-mentioned clarifications could be included in the ESMA guidance.

Prospectus Regulation 
Prospectus Regulation is also an important piece of legislation in force whose burdens may discourage from 
accessing capital market; we therefore support most of the proposed amendments.  
However, as a general and preliminary remark, we think that even if the use of a Regulation intends to achieve 
maximum harmonisation, the situation across Member States is very fragmented, as reported in the last 
ESMA Peer Review in 2022 regarding the scrutiny and approval process.   
This would suggest changing the general approach: i) exploring the possibility of an option for all issuers to 
publish a ‘European prospectus’, approved by ESMA to overcome the fragmentation created by different 
national approaches, ii)  entrusting ESMA with a wider range of direct supervisory powers to have a truly 
European (network) System of Financial ‘Supervisors’ with ESMA at the centre and NCAs in Member States. 
ESMA could accordingly be attributed supervisory competence also with reference to the approval of 
prospectuses.     
Another issue that could deserve more harmonisation is the liability of the NCA which varies across Member 
States, and which is one the factors that may slow down the process of approval of the prospectus, as stated 
in the above mentioned ESMA Peer Review; this also urges a change in the domestic law. 
Also the function of the prospectus is changing over time considering that in some Member States the retail 
offers are disappearing and offers are more and more oriented to institutional investors only , who do not 
need the kind of protection ensu red by the rules on prospectus , or, in case of transfer from an MTF to a 
regulated market, only an admission to trading takes place (this is the case of Italy).  
➢ We support the new exemptions the Commission is proposing to introduce: 
- the extension of the exemption from the obligation to publish a prospectus to offer of securities fungible 
with securities already admitted to trading on a regulated market or an SME growth market, provided 
that the securities offered represent, over a period of 12 months, less than a determined percentage of 
existing securities (art. 1); 
- the increase of the percentage mentioned above, for both offer and admission to trading of securities, 
from 20% to 40% of the number of securities already admitted to trading on the same market, calculated 
over a period of 12 months (art. 1); 
- the introduction of a new exemption from the obligation to publish a prospectus for offer and admission 
of securities fungible with securities that have been admitted to trading for at least the last 18 months,

- the increase of the threshold to 12 million euro for the offers of securities exempted from the obligation

➢ We also support: 
 
- the use of the English language for the offer and admission to trading only in the Home Member

- the mandatory use of incorporation by reference which could simplify and shorten the prospectus 
even if it could hamper flexibility (art. 19). 
 
➢ We do not support: 
 
- the standardised sequence of information in the different types of prospectus which goes against 
the flexibility ensured by the current regime; art. 24.4 of the EU Delegated Regulation 980/2019 
allows, where the order of the information is different from the order in which that information is 
presented in the Annexes to the Regulation, to provide a list of cross references indicating the items 
of those Annexes to which that information corresponds, where required by the NCAs. This allows 
using information already provided in an international offering circular in case where an offer is 
previously made to institutional investors (which is the most common situation). 
 
➢ We think that additional changes are necessary: 
 
- the increase of the threshold (for the exemption of the prospectus) of 150 persons to 500 natural or 
legal persons in order to allow companies to raise funds more easily and to align with the US regime 
following the adoption of the JOBS Act (art. 1.4b); 
̵ the possibility to choose the Home Member State also for the approval of the prospectus for equity

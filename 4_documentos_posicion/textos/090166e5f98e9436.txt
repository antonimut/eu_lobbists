Statement of Alzchem Trostberg GmbH on Proposal for a revision of 
the Regulation on classification, labelling and packaging of chemicals 
(CLP) 
 
Alzchem Trostberg GmbH has the following comments and concerns regarding the current 
Commission proposal for revising the CLP regulation: 
 
General comments 
 Amendments in CLP Regulation concern several further regulations: The legal 
consequences of the proposed changes for other chemical regulations must be 
considered and are far -reaching. Furthermore, the impact assessment  lacks in 
significance as it does not take into account a large part of the consequential costs caused 
by the CLP revision(s). The CLP revision is only the first step for implementing new hazard 
classes, further regulations including REACH have to be adapted with regard to new and 
additional testing methods and new requirements. In addition, national chemical laws will 
have to be aligned . Neither for the new hazard classes nor for the additional elements , 
like multi constituent substances (see below), a draft on proposed methods or guidance 
documents is already available and will be presented later while the transition phases are 
already running. Yet, without clear definition of the testing conditions and rules no testing 
can be started (e.g. OECD methods, testing requirements per annual volume in REACH 
Annexes VII to X  that have to be agreed with ECHA before doing animal testing ). 
Uncertainties in regard of classification criteria for the new hazard classes should be  
solved. 
 Missing harmonization with UN GHS:  The unilateral introduction of the new hazard 
classes in the EU via a delegated act in parallel to the start of discussions about 
introduction of those hazard classes into the UN GHS will result in a transition period of 
several years with non -harmonized requirements in the EU whilst final harmonization is 
not at all ens ured. EU CLP should be aligned with the UN GHS process before  
implementation not vice versa, this contradicts the approach of the harmonized 
classification system all members have agreed on. Adding the new hazard classes to CLP 
does not lead to an additional benefit for public health and environment and creates 
unnecessary double regulation: EU REACH already provides adequate mechanisms to 
regulate all substances that would be covered by the proposed new hazard classes under 
CLP. For example, a dverse effects resulting from endocrine disruptors are already 
covered by existing CLP hazard classes (STOT, CMR) and result in appropriate risk 
management measures.  
 General Hazard Approach: Focusing almost solely on potential hazards of a substance 
instead of evaluating the actual risks associated with the use and handling of chemicals – 
as it is done in many other regions as the USA – will lead to substantial reduction of 
available substances if use as intermediate and safe use with acceptable risks are not 
allowed anymore.

Comments on new CLP elements 
 
Multi-constituent substances 
The introduction of “Multi-constituent substances” will lead to legal uncertainties in the treatment 
of substances and mixtures. The proposed definition of the term “multi-constituent substance” is 
not aligned with the requirements on substance identity under REACH. Currently, the substance 
definition1 under CLP and REACH includes necessary additives and impurities from the 
production process. This addition  is not needed and the definitions of substances (‘mono -
constituent’) and multi -constituent substances should clearly be aligned between REACH and 
CLP. 
Harmonised Classification and Labelling (CLH) 
The Commission intends to streamline and facilitate the CLH process for what we want to share 
a critical view on the following changes: 
- Initiation of CLH: In case of a CLH proposal initiated by the Commission itself, the initiator 
and the assessing instance will be identical. Thus, we see a risk of inadequate pol itical 
pressure, e.g. politically motivated substance bans, on a process that should be purely 
science based.  
- Automatic Classification for new hazard classes: It is agreed that substances with the new 
hazard classes should be given priority in a harmoni zed classification. However, we 
strongly disagree that substances listed under

- Substances or substance groups:  We have objections against the harmoni zed 
classification of groups of substances. Clear and scientifically sound criteria for grouping 
of substances should be used. Otherwise, grouping may lead to unjustified classification 
of a substance in a group, if the scope is not well defined. Overestimation of hazards leads 
to wrong decisions and further narrows the range of substances that can be produced and 
used in the EU (see general comments). 
 Labelling 
- New requirements for labels: With new requirements in labelling formatting, especially 
for the adapted font sizes, the Commission is creating an administrative burden and high 
costs for industry without any recognizable benefit for hazard communication. It 
complicates material handling for all comp anies and automatically leads to increased  
product stocks due to more packaging variants because of labels in different languages

- Fold-Out labels: The use of fold -out labels should be further facilitated as they are a 
necessity to enable the free movement of goods between  EU countries and are also 
beneficial for non-native speakers in the member states. But for this purpose it has to be 
clearly stated that the sequence of languages in such labels is irrelevant, unlike current 
demands by  some member states  who want  to see  their language to be on the first 
position. 
- Digital Labelling: We support the introduction of digital labels. However, the presented 
introduction for digital labels offers little incentive to actually introduce them widely . It 
rather leads to confusion as it would be usable only for additional label information, while 
regular labelling is still mandatory . This leads to double work without any benefit  for the 
downstream user. First, the data format should be defined and only then the digital label 
be introduced into the regulation. 
 
Complimentary to our remarks we fully support the position and comments brought forward by 
the German Chemical Industry Association (VCI) and the European Chemical Industry 
Association (CEFIC).

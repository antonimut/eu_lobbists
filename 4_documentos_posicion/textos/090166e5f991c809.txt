 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
The European Insulation Manufacturers Association (Eurima) supports the development of ambitious 
policies fostering the achievement of a sustainable built environment based on significant emissions 
reduction targets for buildings and the broader construction ecosystem. Eurima recently published it 
carbon neutrality roadmap where we look at how our industry’s commitment to reduce its own emissions 
can be leveraged through the right policies. In this sense, we recognise the role of carbon removals as 
one of the levers to achieve net zero, the design of which is however essential to deliver a correct impact 
and avoid negative interferences with other levers. This is where the certification approach plays a big 
role. In other words, carbon removals can a ct as a good lever to  facilitate the reduction of CO2 
emissions while ensuring the sustainable use of natural resources , provided its interaction with other 
levers and with the wider sustainability picture is well accounted for. 
 
In this context , by partic ularly referring to the possibility of certifying carbon storage in long -lasting 
products and materials it is essential that a level playing -field is guaranteed. This is why Eurima 
underlines that the following should be achieved in the development of carbon removal certification:  
 
1) Better define and prioritise long -term storage over temporary storage and make the certification 
conditional upon proof of ecological /biodiversity related practices (e.g. sustainable forestry 
management);  
 
2) Secure carbon a ccounting methodologies that take into consideration all the environmental impacts 
along the whole life cycle of products and materials;  
 
3) Avoid any market distortions for construction products. 
 
 
Eurima appreciates the Commission’s proposal to establis h four criteria to ensure a sound and robust 
EU certification system. In this respect, we stress that the compliance  of those criteria should be strictly 
applied and assessed by particularly providing a more precise definition of “permanent storage” and 
ensuring its prioritisation over “temporary storage ”. The same level of clarity should also be applied to 
the definition of “long -lasting products and materials” in order to avoid confusion on the actual carbon 
storage potential of certain solutions  which store carbon at the early phases of their life cycle but have 
significant environmental impacts at their End-of-Life stage. For this reason, to be of actual relevance in 
contributing to the overall carbon emission reduction objectives and in addressing the c limate crisis, 
carbon storage in products and materials should last as long as possible.  Addressing the end -of-life is 
important, and it is essential not to underestimate the risk of simply shifting the emissions burden to next 
generations by over-relying on carbon storage and not acting today on carbon reduction. In this sense, 
the Commission should firmly ensure that only authentic long-lasting products with permanent storage 
potential and respecting the cascading principl e are certified under the EU cert ification framework . 
Furthermore, by taking into consideration the sustainability crit erion laid  down by the Commission, 
Eurima believes that, in order to preve nt counterproductive effects on the environment and biodiversity, 
certification should be only i ssued upon proof of ecological ly sustainable management practices as a 
mandatory requirement. 
 
At the same time,  the efforts to keep providing solutions to reduce both operational and embodied 
emissions associated with buildings and create a sustainable built environment must be sustained by a 
solid and sound level playing field when it comes to assessing, disclosing and reducing carbon 
emissions. In the context of the a bove, Eurima strongly endorses the EU Level(s) framework and its

(EN15804) and buildings (EN15978) . This is why  alternative methodologies to account for temporary 
carbon storage  should be carefully evaluated . Indeed,  prioritising the specific role of carbon storage 
attributed to certain solutions cannot distort  the overall sustainability assessment of other buildin gs 
solutions. In this sense, the dimension of carbon  removals needs to be acc ounted for in a separate 
manner in order to not distract from the priority to reduce overall carbon emissions.  Common and 
internationally scientifically agreed European LCA methodologies such as EN15804 or EN15978 
account for every carbon emitted in a similar way,  regardless of when emitted . Careful consideration is 
needed as alternative LCA approaches, such as the Dynamic LCA set in the French Regulation 
RE2020, create an artificial reality whereby a large part of carbon is "saved" or "stored" for products that 
contain sequestrated carbon early in the life cycle, but downplaying the fact that these products have a 
high End -of-Life environmental impact, due to the related emissions. A s explained above, such a 
“discounting” approach will simply shift the emissions and impacts to future generations . Moreover, 
applying these methodologies would also reduce the benefit of recycling and reusing construction 
products, driving the building sector towards solutions that have high impacts a t their end -of-life and 
products that cannot be recycled or reused. All this could than cause a reverse effect on the objective of 
carbon removals and negatively impact the realization of EU climate targets for 2050 and beyond. 
 
Finally, there are a lot of efforts to ensure that carbon emissions reduction accounting is harmonised, 
structured and transparently documented. In this scenario, carbon removal certifications should not 
jeopardise the existing sound-science level playing field and create market distortion by promoting 
specific solutions over others. On the contrary a well-designed carbon removals certification can be a 
positive incentive for the EU market by ensuring a level  playing field for many different solutions that 
could contribute to carbon neutrality.

About the European Insulation Manufacturers Association (Eurima) 
Eurima (TR ID number: 98345631631 -22) is the European Insulation Manufacturers Association, representing the 
interests of all major European mineral wool insulation producers. 
Our industry members produce a wide range of mineral wool products for thermal and  acoustic insulation, providing 
fire protection of domestic and commercial buildings and industrial facilities while offering innovative growing media 
and green-roofing solutions. 
We are a science and research -driven organisation, communicating the benefit s of mineral wool insulation while 
assisting our members in fields such as product standardisation and EU -focused issue monitoring and management, 
helping them to stay informed and contribute to EU affairs relevant to mineral wool insulation products and t he 
industry's licence-to-operate.

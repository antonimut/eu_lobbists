Comment on the public consultation - EBI On a Union certification framework for carbon removals  
In addition to emission avoidance and reduction, carbon removal remains the last 
chance to reach net zero within the timeframe envisaged by the EU. It is mandatory 
to compensate for hard-to-abate emissions and reduce the greenhouse gas 
concentration in the atmosphere to a level that preserves human habitats. The EU 
is leading the way by laying the groundwork for a carbon removal framework. If well 
elaborated, this framework will be the base for Europe to strengthen its position as a 
leader for climate protection technology, creating jobs and an attractive environment 
for young talents.  
The European Biochar Industry Consortium (EBI) strongly supports the elaboration 
of a union certification framework for carbon removals. We are honored to participate, 
as a member of the European Commission´s Carbon Removal Expert Group, in 
defining appropriate methodologies for verifiable, high-quality carbon removals. 
Biochar Carbon Removal (BCR) is an already established concept for result-based, 
high-quality carbon removal certificates, fostering the scaling-up of technological 
carbon removals on a EU level. Due to the incentives provided by carbon removal 
certificates, BCR will sequester 150.000 t CO2e in 2023 within the EU1.  
In order to start scaling durable carbon removals to climate relevance in a timely 
manner, we urge the commission to focus on the following points: 
1. Defining permanence by timescale rather than by location. 
2. Setting a more ambitious industrial removal target of at least 10 Mt CO2e by 
2030. 
3. Providing a roadmap to scale carbon removals over a given timeframe. 
4. Preventing the creation of barriers for existing removal technologies and 
concepts, while supporting additional sustainability aspects 
5. Developing liability frameworks and MRVs for existing and future carbon 
removal technologies.

1. Defining permanence by timescale 
We urge the European Commission to define permanent carbon removal by 
timescale rather than by type and location of storage. In the current version of the 
proposal for a Union framework for carbon removal, permanent carbon removal is 
defined as ³a carbon removal activity that, under normal circumstances and using 
appropriate management practices, stores atmospheric or biogenic carbon for several 
centuries2´. Although the description is technology agnostic, only the possibility of 
permanent carbon storage in geologic formations is specifically mentioned3. To our 
understanding, this is based on the existing legal framework for geological formations, 
namely Directive 2009/31/EC.  
We support the framework envisaged for this particular storage option, but encourage 
the Commission to establish similar frameworks for other technologies (see chapter 
4), subsequently leading to a time-based definition of permanence. A well-known 
and already well-established method for permanent carbon removal is based on the 
carbon-preserving application of biochar (BCR)4. BCR converts carbon dioxide from 
the atmosphere into a chemically inert solid form. Any application that prevents the 
oxidation of biochar results in long-term storage of carbon. Biochar applications 
leading to permanent storage include but are not limited to, the use in soils or long-
lasting building materials, e.g. concrete. 
Permanent carbon removal should be classified as the highest quality of carbon 
removal. Curtailing this category to a specific storage type will limit the member states 
that can work in this area. Opening it to further, scientifically proven storage options 
will lead to more options for member states to contribute to the (8¶V goals.

2. Setting an industrial removal target of at least 
10 Mt. CO2e 
We ask the European Commission to reevaluate the currently set goal of 5 Mt CO2e 
industrial carbon removal by 2030. From our understanding, the goal was set mainly 
due to the limitation of suitable geological storage sites within this timeframe. Bearing 
in mind that Biochar Carbon Removal is an industrial carbon removal, BCR could 
contribute to an impressive scale-up of industrial carbon removal within the EU.  
BCR produced from sustainable feedstock like residues from agriculture, food 
processing, and forestry alone can deliver 6-7 Mt CO2e permanent removal yearly, 
by 2030. Existing projects already sequester 90.000 tons of CO2e annually (2022) 
within the EU. Moreover, the projects demonstrate additional social and 
environmental synergies e.g. by supporting the local economy, building resilient 
soils or improving long-lasting building materials.  
Carbon removal in addition to emission abatement and reduction remains the last 
chance to reach net zero within the envisaged timeframe. Therefore, we urge the 
European Commission to set an ambitious but realistic goal for industrial carbon 
removal of 10 Mt by 2030. 
 
3. Providing a roadmap to implement carbon 
removal 
The absence of transparent plans for the deployment of carbon removals restraints 
potential further investments in this urgently needed sector. Therefore, we ask the 
European Commission to provide a more detailed roadmap for carbon removal for 
2030 and beyond. This should be continuously adapted to recent scientific and 
technological developments. Project developers and investors are observing the 
development of the carbon removal sector with high interest. Providing guidance on 
the planned development of the needed carbon removal sector increases trust and 
helps mobilizing public and private investment. Hence it accelerates the growth 
of the European carbon removal industry and supports (8¶V technology leadership 
within the global community.  
We suggest at least the following parameters to be included: 
x Availability: Prioritising technologies with high technology readiness level 
(TRL) and market acceptance/shares for timely scale-up, while supporting the 
development of technologies with lower TRLs. 
x Sustainability: Due to the advanced climate change, creating a more resilient 
society and environment is becoming more important. Therefore, especially in

the early stage of deploying industrial carbon removals, these aspects should 
be taken into consideration. This should lead to a prioritization of technologies 
with positive impacts on society and the environment. Therefore, we strongly 
support the article on sustainability.  
x Scalability: Scalability for different technologies is by nature depending on 
various factors. Technologies with a high potential to scale, that are not limited 
by infrastructural constraints, should be the focus of the first implementation 
phase.  
 
4. Preventing the creation of barriers  
We encourage the European Commission to establish a framework for carbon removal 
that supports and strengthens existing carbon removal technologies and 
concepts. While several aspects of the proposal are consistent with this, the current 
concept of additionality may prove to be a barrier to existing carbon removal 
technologies and approaches.  
From our perspective article 5 subpoint (b) leaves room for interpretation, moreover, 
it is not clear, how this will be implemented in practice. 
As clearly outlined in recent academic work on the State of CDR, the current 
deployment of ³QRYHO´ carbon removal is far behind what is foreseen in pathways 
achieving the Paris temperature targets.5 Therefore, in our view, all efforts to 
address this shortfall should be valued, by designating all technological carbon 
removals (fulfilling all sustainability criteria) as additional. 
Creating a precise, non-bureaucratic, and pragmatic approach to ensure scaling of 
high quality carbon removal to climate relevance will be key.

5. Developing liability frameworks 
Liability frameworks for carbon removal technologies are key to ensuring a positive 
impact on the stabilization of human climate. While liability aspects are already well 
covered within the Directive 2009/31/EC for Bioenergy with Carbon Capture and 
Storage as well as for Direct Air Carbon Capture and Storage, similar frameworks are 
missing for other technologies and approaches like Biochar Carbon Removal. Well-
defined liability frameworks may include insurance concepts and will lead to more 
trust and safety for further investments. While missing liability frameworks will 
drastically lower the speed for implementation in the near term, moreover could lead 
to market distortions. Therefore, providing a clear framework in the short term will 
be critical to the success of CDR technologies. 
Hence, we encourage the European Commission to develop liability frameworks 
for high-potential carbon removal technologies and offer our support for Biochar 
Carbon Removal.  
 
Summarized, with the implementation of the CRC-F, the Europen Commission is 
taking leadership to develop a trusty and impactful framework to scale the needed 
high-quality CDR sector. During the review of the actual proposal, we found some key 
aspects which remain still unclear or could lead to delayed implementation of CDRs. 
Hence, we encourage the European Commission to address the points above within 
the further development of the CRC-F. 
We are thankful and honored for the opportunity to elaborate actively on the CRC-F 
and remain at your disposal in case of any questions and queries.

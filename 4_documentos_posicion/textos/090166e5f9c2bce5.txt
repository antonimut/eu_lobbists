RESPONSE TO THE EC’s LISTING ACT CONSULTATION ON FINANCIAL RESEARCH 
 
We would like to draw your attention to the proposal to amend Directive 2014/65/EU with specific 
reference to the intention to increase the market capitalization threshold for small and medium -sized 
listed enterprises ("SMEs") to 10 billion euros, below which financial research fees and trading fees may 
revert to a "re-bundled" regime. 
 
The introduction of the unbundling regime with MiFID II destroyed the market. Despite a very marginal 
reduction in costs for mutual funds subscribers, there was a significant reduction in research spending 
budgets. Those who produce and sell their own research services had to adapt to profound changes in 
their business model, resulting in a lack of independent research and reduced market coverage. This is 
particularly true for small and medium capitalization stocks, resulting in a weakening of the European 
capital markets. Discussions are underway in the UK to consider the abolition of unbundling. 
 
With regard to the proposal, the Commission itself recognizes that the previous revision of the directive, 
approved under the Capital Markets Recovery Package  and which introduced the 1 billion euro 
capitalization threshold, did not achieve  the desired goal of supporting research production on SMEs 
because it did not address the problem correctly. The main reason for this is that investment firms have 
opposed the introduction of two separate billing systems, preferring to maintain an unbundling approach 
for all customers. 
 
However, the current revision again risks not addressing the problem correctly as research continues to 
be seen as an incentive, and while raising the threshold to 10 billion, leaves the remaining part of Article 
24, paragraph 9-bis unchanged, which requires that:  
a. before the provision of execution services or research services, the investment firm and the research 
service provider have concluded an agreement that identifies the share, within the combined charges or 
joint payments for execution and research services, that is attributable to research;  
b. the investment firm informs its clients of the joint payments, for execution and research services, 
made to the third-party research service provider. 
 
These two obligations, together with the fact that investment firms would still need to maintain two 
separate billing systems, will continue to represent obstacles to the development of research on SMEs. 
 
Brokers will continue to be unable to contact clients to send research, even for free, unless an agreement 
has been reached with managers and investment firms. This will continue to discourage the production 
of independent research for small and medium capitalization stocks. Managers should also inform 
clients of th e implicit costs related to bundled research services, resulting in brokers being forced to 
quantify those costs separately. However, the presence of research on SMEs has a much greater value 
that cannot emerge from the sole highlighting of the costs neces sary to produce it. The absence of 
research represents a barrier to the entry of new investors and therefore does not favor the development 
of the European market, whose fabric is mainly composed of SMEs.

A first proposed solution could, therefore, be to definitively exclude research (even only on SMEs with 
a market capitalization below 10 billion EUR) from incentives. By removing research below 10 billion 
from incentives, a healthier and more organic development would be favored, and a more in -depth 
approach to investment choices thanks to a wider choice of research providers available. To do this, the 
current proposal could be modified by replacing Article 24, paragraph 9 -bis in its entirety with the 
following: 
 
9-bis. The provision of research services b y third parties to investment firms that provide portfolio 
management services or other investment services or ancillary services to clients is considered compliant 
with the obligations set out in paragraph 1 if it concerns issuers whose market capitalizat ion has not 
exceeded EUR 10 billion as expressed by year-end quotations for the 36 months preceding the provision 
of research services or their equity for the financial years in which they were not listed. 
 
However, this proposal may not be sufficient as investment firms and managers may decide to continue 
paying low fees for order execution services on small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) securities, 
thus not incentivizing research effectively. Therefor e, in our view, it is also necessary to add an 
additional modification to the proposal described above to expressly include research as one of the 
evaluation criteria for transaction execution quality for the purposes of best execution . This would 
redirect orders for companies with a market capitalization of less than 10 billion EUR to brokers who 
invest in research on those securities, thus promoting its production. To do this, it would also be 
necessary to intervene in Article 27, paragraph 1, which could be modified as follows: 
 
1. Member States require investment firms to adopt sufficient measures to obtain, when executing 
orders, the best possible result for their clients, taking into account the price, costs, speed and likelihood 
of execution and settlement, the size and nature of the order, the availability of research (in the case of 
issuers whose market capitalization has not exceeded EUR 10 billion as expressed by year -end 
quotations for the 36 months preceding the provision of research services or by equity for the financial 
years in which th ey are or were not listed) or any other consideration relevant to the execution. 
However, whenever there are specific instructions given by the client, the investment firm is required to 
execute the order in accordance with such instructions. 
 
These two ch anges, together, would have a greater chance of achieving the objective set by the 
Commission to improve the visibility of SMEs, facilitating the development and production of research. 
Brokers could resume contacting investment managers and investment firms to send research, providing 
free trial services and facilitating the entry of new investors, thus promoting the further development of 
the European capital market. 
 
However, we would like to make it clear that the decision to pay for research services, even under a re-
bundled regime, remains the responsibility of managers and investment firms. It would be appropriate, 
therefore, for them to be encouraged by European institutions and regulators to value it adequately.

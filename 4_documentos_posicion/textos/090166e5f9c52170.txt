Assonime comments on the EC proposal amending Directive 2014/65/EU and repealing Directive 
2001/34/EC 
Listing requirements and repealing of the Listing Directive 
We support the EC proposal of repealing the Listing Directive, as it has been already substantially 
replaced by other EU legislation such as the MiFID, Prospectus Regulation and the Transparency 
Directive, and of incorporating its remaining  provisions regarding market capitalisation and free 
float into MiFID. 
As to the listing requirements, we share the proposal of lowering the threshold of free float to 10% 
as it could make public listing more attractive and listing rules more competitive toward other non-
EU markets. Nevertheless, we are concerned with the proposed wording as it appears to set a rigid 
threshold of free float and ensures only partial flexibility in its concrete application (see  art. 51a, 
par. 5). In particular, the proposed wording is not entirely clear with regard to the monitoring of the 
10% free float requirement, the consequences of falling below it and the concrete intervention in 
lowering the 10% threshold, when needed . In order to enhance the 10% threshold as a measure 
that can contribute to the competitiveness of EU listing rules, we suggest considering the application 
of the threshold only at the moment of listing and ensuring flexibility to the market operato r in 
monitoring the issuers’ free float and taking decisions thereof . On this second aspect, we suggest 
preserving the current Listing Directive approach – to be incorporate into MiFID – according to 
which the minimum free float requirement can be derogated in cases where the market can operate 
properly with a lower free float percentage. 
We are concerned  with the provision that entrusts the European Commission with the power of 
changing the minimum free float threshold “when the applicable thresholds impede the liquidity on 
public markets taking into account the financial de velopment”: the process and the practical 
implications of this delegated power are not clear; in particular, it further reinforces our doubts on 
the flexibility issue explained above. 
Investment research on SMEs 
We support the EC proposals that aim at enhancing the visibility of listed companies, especially 
SMEs, with regard to the research coverage. In particular, we share the proposal of increasing the 
current market capitalisation threshold of EUR 1 billion up to EUR 10 billion below which re-bundling 
trading execution fees and research would be possible.  As a matter of fact , the introduction of 
unbundling rules have impaired the overall availability of research, especially for small and medium 
capitalisation companies, and the first amendment to these rules  that provided an exemption for 
the investment research covering issuers whose market capitalisation does not exceed EUR 1 billion 
did not help in supporting the production of research for SMEs, making this further legislative review 
highly advisable.

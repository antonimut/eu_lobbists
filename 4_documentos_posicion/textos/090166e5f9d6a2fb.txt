 
 
 
 
CLP REGULATION 
  
Revision of the EU CLP Regulation 
Eurogypsum’s feedback  
 
 
29 MARCH 2023 
 
 
Eurogypsum, the association representing the interests of the European industry extracting 
and processing gypsum, welcomes the revision of the EU legislation on hazard classification, 
labelling and packaging of chemicals (CLP Regulation) . Gypsum is a cost -efficient, low 
carbon, sustainable mineral that has been used for centuries to produce construction 
materials such as plaster, stucco or plaster-based products. Eurogypsum’s main objective is 
to promote a sustainable built environment for Europe through the environmental, social and 
economic benefits of gypsum. 
 
 
The gypsum industry and CLP 
 
The EU already has one of the most comprehensive and safest regulatory frameworks 
for chemicals in the world.  The CLP Regulation is the core piece of Union legislation for the 
hazard assessment of chemicals. It sets out the hazard classification of chemicals and how 
to communicate those hazards to consumers and workers. 
Eurogypsum supports the European Commission’s goal of achiev ing a higher level of 
protection of citizens and the environment against hazardous chemicals . We believe that 
gypsum products are aligned with these criteria.  
The introduction of new hazard classes  and corresponding criteria (such as endocrine 
disruptors (ED), and persistent, bioaccumulative and toxic  (PBT), very persistent very 
bioaccumulative (vPvB), persistent, mobile and toxic (PMT) and very persistent very mobile 
(vPvM) substances) should be in line with the United Nations’ Globally Harmonized System 
(GHS). Therefore, we hope for the imminent introduction of the new hazard classes in the 
UN GHS, in order to minimise discrepancies of implementation for the companies involved in 
the classification, labelling and packaging of chemicals. 
Eurogypsum welcomes the requirement for the introduction of pragmatic rules related to 
labelling on small and multilingual packaging  and supports  further development of  
digital solutions for accessing relevant labelling information. 
We thank the Commission and European institutions for taking these comments into account 
in the roll-out of the Regulation on Classification, Labelling and Packaging of Chemicals.

Eurogypsum is a European federation of national associations of producers of gypsum products (i.e. plaster and 
plasterboard). It is one of the few fully integrated industries (from cradle to cradle) within the construction 
products field. The companies which mine gyp sum also process it and manufacture the value -added products 
and systems used extensively in construction and other industries. 
With a turnover of EUR 7 billion, the European gypsum and anhydrite industry operates some 160 factories and 
154 quarries and ge nerates employment directly to 28,000 persons and indirectly for 300,000 persons. The 
Gypsum industry provides jobs to 1,100,000 plasterers and plasterboard installers. It trains around 25,000 
persons per year across Europe.

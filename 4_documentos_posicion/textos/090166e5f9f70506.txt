Citeo is the French company in charge of Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) for household packaging 
and graphic papers. EPR is an application of the polluter -pay principle: producers and companies responsible 
for placing products on the market are required to finance or organize the waste management at the end of life 
of these products. In France, most companies have decided to collectively organize themselves under the 
responsibility of EPR organizations accredited by public authorities. 
Citeo sent feedback to the European Commission and Eunomia during the previous consultations on their work 
on the revision of the Packaging and Packaging waste Directive. Citeo would like to provide its analysis on the 
Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (PPWR) proposal to contribute to the coming discussions in order 
to understand in a pragmatic way the impact of the proposed measures.

General remarks  
The global economy is now 7,2% circular in 2022 and this rate drops every year, from 9,1% in 2018, 
to 8,6% in 2020 1. Our current economy model nee ds to be reconsidered and be aligned with the 
environmental and climate challenged . We need to do much better and reduce first the amount of 
material, maximise their use and then boost the management of their end -of-life to ensure that  the 
amount of leakage is rationalised.  
Citeo believe s the European Commission is setting the path of  ambitious legislative 
measures to address packaging challenges by repealing the Packaging and Packaging waste 
Directive (PPWD) which was outdated in relation to today’s society and environmental pressure.  
We have seen rising numbers of national legislative proposals, the German Circular economy law 
on packaging in 2019, the French Circular Anti-waste and Circular Economy Law a year after,2, or 
more recently the Spanish law on Circular Economy 3. Given the globalisation of the  economy, the 
worldwide issue of building more sustainable policies, it has become necessary that the EU becomes 
a key player and builds a strong resilient Internal market.  
To do so, the European Commission published a new  proposal on packaging and packaging waste 
(PPWR) by harmonising with the help of a regulation legislative tool EU rules on packaging to develop 
a more circular economy.  
Citeo welcomes the ambition of the proposal and shares the Commission willingness of a 
complete harmonisation. The example of the Single Use plastic Directive4 shows discrepancies in 
terms of transposition between Member states and length of procedure to implement the measures 
in national law. A regulation will answer to those challenges by ensuring a complete harmonisation 
throughout the internal market.  
In addition, the proposal of the Commission is setting general obligations while technical measur es 
will be entailed in secondary legislation.  In building this secondary legislation, it is critical to ensure 
a purpose -driven collaboration between the public and the private sector to ensure reachable 
incentivising targets . A Packaging Forum, committee consulted during the writing of the 
secondary legislation, composed of representatives of the whole value chain (producers, 
PROs, NGOs) is necessary to achieve a consistent European legislation applicable 
throughout Member States. 
1. Prevention measures 
1.1. Reduction measures on Member States 
On article 38, Member States are entitled to reduce the amount of packaging waste generated  per 
capita, as compared to the packaging waste generated in 2018. 
To this end, Member State shall implement  measures and use instruments to prevent the generate 
of packaging waste and provide incentives. The proposal refers for instance to the adoption of waste 
prevention plan to be adopted by producer responsibility organisation. 
Citeo would like to emphasis the French Anti Waste and Circular Economy law (“AGEC law”) which 
require to entities subject to EPR to develop and implement a prevention and eco -design plan with

On article 9, the proposal required to minimize packaging and limit the empty space as necessary to 
ensure its functionality. Citeo welcomes this measure which will ensure  the ecodesign of 
packaging.  
 
Citeo supports the proposal in terms of restriction on use of certain packaging format s and 
the introduction of exemption for company complying with the definition of micro-company. It enables 
the use of packaging to be rationalised to what is necessary. 
2. The harmonized recyclability definition 
aligned with Citeo’s position (article 6) 
In our opinion, a harmonized definition of recyclability is a prerequisite to encourage upstream eco -
design at the packaging level by sending a clear signal to the stakeholders of the packa ging value 
chain, while giving a chance for innovative material. In this perspective, you will find below our 
analysis and recommendations pursuant the Commission’s proposal. 
2.1. Recyclability definition  
2.1.1. Recyclability definition criteria  
On §1 of Article 6 Citeo welcomes the ambition of the Commission to ensure that all packaging is 
recyclable (§1 of the proposal), aligned with the Circular Economy Action Plan entailing the objective 
of packaging put on the EU market to be reusable or recyclable by 2030. 
However, the proposal doesn’t contain any  reference that packaging shall be recyclable by 2030 . 
Packaging shall only comply with the design for recycling criteria from 1 January 2030. Therefore, it

article 65 of the regulation.  
In addition, while it is aimed that reusable packaging is also recyclable on the long run, it could be 
relevant that the recyclability methodology of reusable packaging follows a different approach since 
the system is not necessarily the same . In fact, in France, packaging that is not returned to a reuse 
system can be returned for two main reasons:  
• Either it is kept by the consumer and therefore  with the possibility to be discarded in the 
sorting bin later. In that event, the reusable packaging should be recyclable like single -use

• Or it is taken out of circulation by the industrialist because it is too damaged for instance. In 
that event, it should be recycled in the industrial system. 
Therefore, Citeo calls:  
• As it is not technically feasible to have all packaging recyclable at the entry into force 
of the legislation, Citeo recommends amending §1 to refer to January 1, 2030, as the 
applicable date of the measure; 
• Citeo recommends taking into consideration that reusable packaging may answer to 
a different recyclability approach.

allows the recyclability assessment according to the current technologies available and not those

2.1.2. Design for recycling guidelines and recycling performance grades 
On §3 of Article 6, given the objectives of the PPWR and the need for the industry to have a 
complete view in terms of timing, the Design for Recycling guidelines (DfRs) are a prerequisite 
to ensure the compliance with the 2030 target.  
Therefore: 
Citeo suggests reintroducing the 2027 effective deadline to adopt the delegated acts 
entailing the design for recycling guidelines  as mentioned in the first leaked draft of the 
proposal within the paragraph 3 of article 6. 
On §4 Article 6, on what the DfRs shall consider, Citeo asks for clarifications.  
First, the term “consider” is too vague and gives room of interpretation for across the Union territory. 
Citeo would suggest referring to the terminology used in §8 “be compatible” which is more restrictive. 
Second, regarding the terminology “state of the art of collection, sorting and recycling processes”, it 
should be put in context with the recyclability definition  and the requirement that it has to only take 
into account the current technologies  widely available and not those available in the future  or 
representing only few facilities in Europe. 
Regarding delegated acts precising the recyclability performances grades , from A to E [95 -
70]%, and by reference to its Tree methodology, Citeo believes it should take into consideration an 
intermediate recyclability level (see category “green light” and “orange” in Citeo’s Tree methodology 
in the diagram below).

2.1.3. Recyclability at scale  
Pursuant §2 (e) and §6 of the article 6, packaging shall be recyclable at scale from 1 January 2035 
on the basis of defined elements.  
First, Citeo would like to stress the side effect of the “recyclable at scale” definition based on 
the collection, sorting and recycling infrastructure and processes covering at least 75% of the Union 
population, including packaging waste exported from the Union.  
Due to the fact that habits of consumption are different across Europe (different types of products 
and/or distribution channels and/or consumption patterns), packaging used may differ from a country 
to another. In addition to having different collection and sorting scheme, Member States might thus 
also have different recycling streams within their territor y reflecting consumption specificities.   The 

 
 
 
 Citeo | 24/03/2023 |  Restricted distribution   8/36  
    
application of the “at scale criteria” would jeopardize the development of specific recycling schemes 
reflecting the reality of one internal market, which could be inexistent in the rest of the Union. 
For instance, consumers may prefer to consume UHT milk or fresh milk. transparent bottles  are 
best suitable for storing fresh milk, while cartons, HDPE bottles and opaque PET bottles are used 
for UHT milk packaging because they protect the food from light, which in turn helps to protect food 
flavour against light-induced lipid oxidation.  
Citeo developed R&D project 8 to improve the  circularity of opaque PET and create a recycling 
scheme, Citeo worked on the ecodesign of the packaging to reduce the opacity with the aim to reach 
a bottle -to-bottle circularity. Grey recycled bottles for milk are available since 2020 in French 
supermarket. In addition, Citeo launched a call for tenders to generalize bottle to bottle recycling by 
20259. 
The recyclability at scale definition could prohibit the use of opaque PET because it would  not be 
collected by 75% of the population of  Member States (but only because it does not exist on most 
markets). A switch away from this packaging could lead to higher environmental impacts and/or more 
difficulty to reach recycled content objectives.  
Another example is the use of polystyrene (PS) resin. France is a strong market in terms of yogurt 
consumption with a packaging mainly composed of PS. PS is also used for other applications such 
as meat and fish trays. 100,000 tonnes of PS packaging are consumed each year. While there are 
not enough recycling solutions for PS yet, the forecasted timeline for the development of PS recycling 
facilities in France has been the following: decision of investment in 2020, pilots in 2022, industrial 
facilities in 202510. 
A consortium of food manufacturers called “PS25” represented partly by Citeo, has agreed in January 
2022 to finance a recycling scheme for polystyrene in order to recycle yoghurt pots and meat or fish 
trays, packaging that has hardly ever been recycled in France until now. In March 2022, Citeo 
launched a call for tenders to select companies that will recycle PS packaging sorted by the French  
population through  the yellow bin.  In particular, recyclers will have to take over the share of PS 
packaging that will be managed by them as of January 2023  with the aim to have an operational 
recycling scheme by 202411. Many initiative to develop PS recycling facilities take place in Europe, 
for example Indaver in Belgium launched the construction of their facility last September.  
The recycled at scale definition could jeopardize the development of such recycling schemes, 
which answer to a particular Member State’s way of consumption and not found elsewhere in 
Europe, since it does not fall under the 75% criteria of recycled at scale.  
It is necessary to create a legislative framework which leaves room for the development of innovative 
recycling schemes, which are mostly aligned with national consumption habits. 
If it is not, it would call into questions massive investments which have al ready been made by the 
industry to develop new recycling schemes.  Social and economic impacts have to be taken into 
account and this is not enough considered in the impact assessment.  
Last example on innovative resins  by being forward-looking, in the event in 2036 a new material 
has been developed , with very efficient functionalities for the circular economy, a company would 
produce first tons of the material, then find a market in one or two EU countries. It would have to

2.2. Integration of the work on standardisation 
Given the Commission is empowered to adopt delegated acts  to develop design for recycling 
guidelines, Citeo calls the Commission to take the current ongoing work on standardisation 
into consideration.  
The European Commission ’s DG GROW published on 1 December 2021 a request for a 
standardisation proposal on the recyclability of plastics and the integration  of recycled plastic. This 
project is a direct result of the Circular Plastic Alliance (CPA), of which Citeo is a member, which as 
a reminder is an initiative under the European Strategy for Plastics (2018), in particular Annex III on 
voluntary industry commitments. The objective of the CPA is to stimulate the European market for 
recycled plastics in order to reach 10 million tonnes of recycled plastics by 2025.  
In the context of this request for standardisation, Citeo has been appointed by AFNOR to represent 
France on the SRAHG, which is a committee defining the operating rule s and the various 
standardisation groups working on standardisation projects.  
Citeo works in this framework on the writing of standards concerning recyclability. The aim is 
to find a consensus on methodology throughout the stakeholders of the value chain.

More precisely, the Ecodesign team participated in the 7 th meeting of the SRAHG dedicated to the 
mandate of DG GROW concerning the launch of the work dedicated to the 3 standards concerning 
recyclability13. On behalf of Citeo, a member of the Ecodes ign team has been appointed convenor 
on the working group about on design-for-recycling guidelines for plastic packaging products. 
There is no reference in the PPWR of the current work on standardisation. Given that the industry is 
working on voluntary design criteria to ensure harmonised recyclability definition of plastic packaging,

Therefore, Citeo calls:

• To Extend the work on standardisation to all materials and not just plastics.  
2.3. Creation of a European technical committee 
Citeo supports the creation of a European Technical Committee by the Commission  
representing the value chain (which could include the COTREP and CEREC for example in France, 
as well as equivalent committees in Europe) in charge of facilitating the standardized Design for 
Recycling Guidelines update which entails criteria for each packaging and material type. This would 
ensure the optimal recovery of the material without any perturbation as the design of packaging 
would follow the guidelines of the technical committee. 
The creation of the European Technical Committee by the Commission should result of a mandate 
from both the DG ENV and the DG Growth. 
 
As regarding to the DfR guidelines developed by the Technical Committee, it should precise a set of 
criteria for each material and packaging, in order to ensure the compatibility of a specific material 
and packaging with recycling streams. 
However, given the lack of harmonisation regarding collection and sorting systems, the Technical 
Committee should consider national specificities when one material is recyclable in one Member 
State whereas it is not in another one. This would prevent any confusing information for consumers. 
Alongside the creation of a Technical Committee, Citeo supports the development by the 
Commission of guidance on what constitutes effective collection/ sorting systems  a.

3.1. For a limited scope of compostable packaging 
Article 8

ultimately enshrined the possibility for other packaging to be compostable since by 2030 all 
packaging should be recyclable. 
Recyclability should take priority over composting. 
By way of inspiration, the French legislative Order  of March 15, 2022  entails a  listing  of 
compostable, methanizable and biodegradable packaging and waste that can be collected together 
with biowaste that has been sorted at source.

8. Extended producer responsibility  
8.1. Providers of online platforms 
On article 40§3, the Regulation provides for a legislative framework for packaging put on the market 
through online sales platforms. 
According to Citeo, the legislative framework proposed by the Commission is not sufficient 
to ensure that packaging put on the European market through marketplaces is falling under 
the scope of the extender producer responsibility.  
Indeed, the proposal only requir es the registration of producers in the national register proposed in 
article 39 and a self-certification that the producers comply with the EPR obligations.  
There are neither compulsory requirements on the producers nor responsibility of online platforms to 
ensure the fulfilment of EPR obligations.  
The responsibility is at the core of the EPR system and online platforms should take up their share 
of responsibility.   
The French legislation is a good example of good practices that have had proven effects on the 
responsibility of online platforms.

Pursuant the AGEC law 35, producers selling their products via a marketplace must prove to them 
that they are contributing to a PRO and thus comply with EPR scheme . Producers shall provide to 
marketplaces a Unique Identifier Number (UIN) given by the PRO when they register. In the event a 
producer does not provide a UIN to the online platform , it is the responsibility of the online platform 
to declare and pay the EPR fees to the PRO in the name of the producer.  The law provides for 
financial sanctions in the event EPR obligations are not fulfilled. 
The nationwide implementation of this measure is a success for the enforcement of EPR scheme to 
all sellers on online platform. Since the measure was implemented in January 2022, Citeo has seen 
its client portfolio double in less than a year. From 13,000 in January 2022, the number of producers 
who contribute to the financing of EPR scheme to the increase up to 35,000 clients in total in March 
2023. 
Therefore: 
• It should  be ensured that all packaging put on the market, including through 
online platforms, comply with EPR scheme. A self -certification required to online 
sellers from online platforms isn’t sufficient enough to enforce EPR.  
• Online platforms play a key role in the placing of packaging on the market and 
need to be made accountable. 
• The French legislation has been a successful driver to ensure the compliance to 
EPR by having been made online platforms responsible for declaring household 
packaging and graphic paper from third-party sellers who do not have registered 
to a PRO.

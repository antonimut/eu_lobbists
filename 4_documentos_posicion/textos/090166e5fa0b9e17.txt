Industry Position on the Proposals for API Regulations on 
• the collection and transfer of advance passenger information (API) for enhancing and 
facilitating external border controls, amending Regulation (EU) 2019/817 and Regulation 
(EU) 2018/1726, and repealing Council Directive 2004/82/EC - COM(2022) 729 final and 
• the collection and transfer of advance passenger information for the prevention, detection, 
investigation and prosecution of terrorist offences and serious crime, and amending 
Regulation (EU) 2019/818 - COM(2022) 731 final 
 
March 2023 
 
I. Industry Position 
 
The industry supports  the goals of the  above-mentioned proposed Regulations, referred to 
729 and 731, respectively. Our primary objective  in applying the current and future API 
framework is to contribute to strengthening the EU’s border security.  
 
Furthermore, according to international standards, adopting an API program shall limit the 
operational and administrative burdens on aircraft operators to the greatest extent possible 
while enhancing passenger facilitation (e.g. expediting border control procedures, reducing  
airport congestion, reducing delays to travelling public, etc.). Worth mentioning that as per the 
nature of API, it  is not information  that carriers need for their operations but to comply with 
Government requirements and for which they assume extensive costs related to the collection, 
formatting and submission of the data. 
 
We welcome the ambition to set up a unique EU ‘’single window’’ for carriers to submit API data. 
In due course, we expect this ‘’single window’’ concept to be used for PNR data . We value EU 
efforts to standardise the data set and timing for API data submissions across Member States 
(MSs). The expected benefits of all these positive developments  will only be  realised with 
effective and standardised implementation. Currently, we lack enough supporting information 
to conclude that it would be such a seamless and integrated implementation. 
 
There are some elements of the proposed regulations that deeply concern the industry: 
 
• Intra EU s cope. The airline industry opposes the proposed requirements  mandating API 
collection on intra -EU flights  (proposal 731) . This  obligation unilaterally and 
disproportionally burdens air travellers and carriers , placing them at a competitive 
disadvantage, considering that other modes of transport are not subject to these 
requirements even if they represent a very significant share  of people’s movements within 
the EU. These additional burdens will negatively impact passenger experience and increase 
the pressure on ground operations despite the efforts made by the aviation industry to 
optimise airport processes over the past decades , while eroding Schengen’s free 
movement.  Moreover, this heterogeneous approach across all transport modes operating 
intra-EU weakens and undermines  the entire EU border security framework, which is the 
primary goal of the proposals. At the same time, the mandatory collection of API data intra-
EU will erode much of what Schengen stands for. Therefore, we urge the EU not to introduce 
mandatory API collection requirements for intra -EU flights. If it considers the requirement 
to collect API data for intra -EU traffic necessary, this obligation  must be equally  and

simultaneously applied to all modes of transport  to achieve the security objectives of the 
proposed measure, not just aviation. 
 
• Methods for data collection . The proposed approach to only collect API data by 
automated means is unbalanced and disproportionate. Likewise, such an approach 
prevents carriers from executing their prerogative on how to run their own business. No  
other precedents worldwide mandate carriers by law how they have to collect API data. 
Furthermore, in the case of intra -EU travel (proposal 731) , this approach will most likely 
result in a cumbersome check -in/boarding process , mainly for travellers but also for 
carriers. This will negatively impact airport flows and traveller experience, while those 
travelling intra-EU using other modes of transport will not be subject to it.  We call for a 
flexible legislative framework which enables  both manual and automated data collection . 
Likewise, authorities must assess the actions they can also take to address data quality (e.g. 
enhancing government systems capabilities to validate travel document information) 
 
• Legislative framework patchwork. At the time of implementation, the two proposed API 
regulations will overlap with  other leg islations, i.e.  EES, ETIAS and PNR (as API is a PNR 
element). This overlap must be considered upfront  to ensure a holistic , standard and 
effective implementation for the benefit of authorities, carriers,  and travellers. By reading 
API/EES/ETIAS Regulations, such an approach is not evident, especially on inbound flights. 
There may be a potential overlap of key elements such as data sets, frequency of 
submission and transmission modes.  Hence, we call  on the EU to conduct a holistic 
assessment of the regulations that impact the flow of travellers’ data (API/PNR/EES/ETIAS), 
regardless of their purpose, to ensure a standard and homogenous approach at the time of 
implementation. This must also be evaluated in the mandatory evaluations of both 
proposals.  
 
Finally, some aspects of the proposed regulation require  further clarity  for the industry  to 
understand the impacts of these regulations and , if applicable, contribute to any necessary 
improvement. This is the case, for example, of the scenarios under which carriers are expected 
to correct/complete/update/delete data. Likewise, further detailed and/or technical 
considerations must be discussed in time, subject to how the proposed regulations evolve 
during the legislative process.  
 
To contribut e to an effective and practical revised EU API legislative framework, a deta iled 
summary of the above- mentioned key concerns from the industry can be found below . It 
includes our solutions  to address them in the proposed Regulations and/or in the (to be 
developed) Implementing Decisions, as applicable.   
 
We hope you consider the above and below industry input to ensure an effective API legislative 
framework. It shall be efficiently and consistently  integrated with other applicable legislative 
frameworks (e.g. EES, ETIAS or PNR)  without creating an unnecessary burden on the air 
transport mode and the air travellers but ultimately securing the entire Schengen security and 
connectivity. 
 
We remain at your disposal for any further information and look forward to receiving your 
feedback.

II. Key Considerations 
 
1. Flights in scope – Intra EU 
A. The issue 
• The inclusion of intra -EU flights (selective approach) in the scope of 731, and the 
fact that this obligation will impact only air transport, is highly concerning. It 
imposes a disproportionate operational burden and competitive disadvantage on 
air transport/travellers, considering that other transport modes are not subject to 
those requirements, even if they represent a vast majority of people’s movements 
within EU. This also has security implications for the whole Schengen/EU area. 
• At the same time, this heterogeneous approach across all transport modes in the 
EU weakens and undermines both proposals' primary “security” objectives . 
Moreover, even in case a passenger flies  from Paris to Frankfurt by plane but 
decides to take the train back to Paris from Frankfurt, this passenger would still be 
in Germany, according to the system. This shows that unless all transport modes 
are obliged to capture API data, the obligation for air carriers to collect API data for 
intra-EU flights is not effective. 
• A relevant outcome of the public consultation conducted via an EU survey in 2019, 
was that a large majority of respondents either  strongly agree or agree that 
collecting API for other modes of transport would improve border control and the 
fight against irregular migrat ion, as well as would better support law enforcement 
authorities.  
• We recognise that the additional costs/burdens imposed on air carriers due to 
intra-EU API data collection have been considered ( e.g., exponentially increased 
volume of data to be transmitted, modification of systems enabling automated data 
collection or operational changes etc ). Still, the proposals incorporate the 
obligation of air carriers to collect this data. However, the reason why  this 
requirement is imposed only on the air transport mode but not the others remains 
ambiguous. 
• The assumption that the additional costs imposed on carriers related to intra -EU 
data collection/submission will be fully mitigated by an automated system and  the 
set-up of the central API router is questionable. It has not been demonstrated at 
this stage.  
• The industry fully supports the principle behind the central API router. However, the 
exact benefits for carriers can only be assessed once there is full clarity on all the 
practicalities of how that API router will operate in combination with EES/ETIAS, 
which is not the case today. 
• To date, the industry’s experience setting up the EES/ETIAS ‘’carrier interface’’ (CI) 
has been suboptimal. As the CI is to evol ve into the API router, this experience 
increases our concerns on how the API router will eventually operate in 
combination with EES/ETIAS (and in parallel with the PNR transmission to EU 
Member States) and whether this process will be efficient and streamlined as it shall 
be due to the inherent and expected benefits of the ICAO Annex 9 standard on 
‘’Single Window’’. 
• Furthermore, the mandatory collection of API data intra-EU will erode much of what 
Schengen stands for due to the additional obligations impos ed on travellers and 
airlines. It will undoubtedly impact the passenger experience and passenger

B. The impact 
• Airport check-in/boarding times for intra -EU flights will be increased  due to 
additional data being collected. This applies especially to the gate procedures if 
passengers are unprepared for travel. Many passengers travel with cabin baggage 
only and go directly to the gate where the check will be performed.   
• Passengers will need to go through additional ‘’touchpoints’’ to complete their 
journey ( i.e., providing their travel document /info) while there is no tangible 
facilitation benefit for them ( e.g., faster/seamless clearance at the border), 
especially when for intra-EU, there are no border checks. 
• Airlines automated check-in channels will have to be modified (web, App, others). 
• Exponential increase in the cost of collecting/transmitting data estimated on one-
off total costs amount to EUR 125 million and recurrent net costs to EUR 36,32

• Extra burden to the gate staff as the gates for intra Schengen flights are 
considerably smaller, with less equipment and staff, compared  to the gates for 
extra Schengen flights, where travel documents / API data checks have to be 
performed for passengers for whom the checks have not been performed. 
 
C. The solutions 
• We urge the EU not to introduce mandatory API collection requirements for 
intra-EU flights . If it considers the requirement to collect API data for intra -EU 
traffic truly necessary,  this obligation must be equally and simultaneously 
applied to all modes of transport, not just aviation.  
• The negative practical impact of intra -EU data collection on travellers’ free 
movement/carriers’ operations as described above but not limited to them, must 
be considered and addressed (e.g. should there be scenarios where a passenger 
is not to be boarded, assurance must be given to airlines on their exemption from 
paying c ompensation to travellers for denied boarding  (EU Regulation 
(261/2004)). 
 
2. Methods for data collection 
A. The issue 
• The i ndustry has strong reservations about the proposal to mandate by law the 
method by which carriers should collect API data (i.e., via automated means only). 
Furthermore, the legal basis, within and outside the proposed Regulations, that 
assists the EU in imposing carriers such a mandate is unclear.

• The choice of method should be the carriers’ prerogative for reasons including but 
not limited to differences in stations’ infrastructure across the globe, carriers’ IT set 
up and/or interline agreements. 
• Automated means is the industry’s preferred API data collection and transmission 
method. H owever, on the one hand , it can still occur that data cannot be read 
correctly by automated means. On the other hand, the reality is that automated 
data collection cannot be 100% guaranteed in all cases  (e.g. automated readers 
not available at some airports). This shall not be neglected and/or considered only 
as ‘’exceptions’’ to the general rule of automated data collection mandated by law. 
 
B. The impact 
• Refer to section 1B 
• A relevant percentage of travellers  buy their tickets via  travel agents for which 
airlines cannot guarantee the data entered or its  collection mode. Hence, this will 
increase bottlenecks at the airport due to the additional  API data 
collection/verification to be done by the airline. 
  
C.The solution 
• Data capture obligations to remain flexible in  the regulation, meaning that data 
collection should be possible by both automated and  manual means. See 
proposals for corresponding articles.

III. Other considerations 
 
3. Coexistence of API/EES/ETIAS/PNR Regulations 
A. The issue 
• The industry supports the EU’s intention to ‘’ standardise’’ the frequency of API 
submission and data elements across EU Member States. However, it is worth 
noting that data sets still vary between the two proposed Regulations, including for 
inbound flights that are in the scope of both.  
• Inbound fligh ts into EU and Schengen countries are in the scope of the API 
proposed Regulations and EES/ETIAS Regulations . T he practicalities of the  
coexistence of all these legislative frameworks must be considered. A holistic, 
homogenous, standard and efficient approach is a must. 
 
Page 7 of 8 
 
• When reading above mentioned  Regulations, such an approach is not evident . 
There is no clear consistency on crucial elements for operators to set up their 
systems and procedures (i.e., data set, timing, transmission mode).  
• Likewise, the coexistence of PNR and API legislative framework is relevant as API is 
a PNR data element (to be transmitted as long as collected). 
 
B. The impact 
• An overlap of data sets and/or frequency of submission and /or transmission 
modes for the same API data of the same inbound flights will result in an 
unnecessary ad-hoc system set up by carriers with subsequent inefficiencies and 
extra costs. 
• The duplication of channels to transmit API (i.e. API router plus national authorities 
collecting PNR) will lead to additional costs for implementation on carriers. 
 
C. The solution 
• The same and standard data elements, frequency and transmission modes shall 
apply across any API transmission  (regardless of the purpose by which API is 
collected, i.e. border management, law enforcement, EES/ETIAS) as per below:  
- data elements:  standard MRZ and flight data.  Additional data elements which 
may be required if collected by carriers (e.g. seat, baggage) it will be included in 
batch only 
- frequency: check-in + flight closure  
- submission mode: interactive API at check -in + flight  close-out message at 
flight closure (or batch API) 
• Above approach should be reflected in the proposal for Regulation and the (to be 
developed) Implementing Decisions.  
• Assess the coexistence of the API/EES/ETIAS regulations ; the  evaluation of the 
different regulations and their interplay is a must. The evaluations (as per Article 20 
(731) and Article 38 (729)) of both proposals must therefore assess and analyse 
this.  
• As API is a PNR data element, there shall be a standard approach between API and 
PNR legislative frameworks  on the above -mentioned elements (data set and 
frequency). 
• Transmission of PNR data for charters intra -EU, which includes only name and 
surname, seat allocation, and perhaps baggage, should be completely replaced by 
the API router , and there should be no need to retain PNR transmission in such 
cases. 
 
4. Data Correction/Completion/Update/Deletion  
A. The issue 
• There is a lack of clarity i n 731 about the scenarios under which carriers are 
supposed to correct, complete or update or delete the API data. 
• Rules for API data deletion by carriers after submission differs from COM 729 and 
731 (48 hours vs after submission, respectively).

5. Penalties 
A. The issue 
• This section should be read in conjunction with section 2, under which the industry 
calls for the methods of data collection to be flexible by regulation. 
• Proposed regulations have not changed the existing EU API Directive approach to 
penalties. Hence there is a continued lack of clarity/consistency across  Member 
States regarding how ''data quality'' should be measured.  
• Addressing API data quality requires a multi- faceted approach involving both the 
airlines and authorities, given the variety of causes leading to data inaccuracy 
which goes beyond airlines’ processes. 
• Penalties shall be used as a ‘’last  resort’’, while a  cooperative and educational 
approach should be taken rather than punitive.   
• Carriers should not be liable for the cases in which travellers’ checks-in and provide 
the API data based on a valid travel document, which is not the one they finally 
presented at the border on arrival. 
• The fines issue is even more cumbersome on proposal 731 and the intra-EU flights 
for which and according to the Schengen border code, no systematic border 
checks are performed by authorities nor carriers to run additional checks. 
 
B. The impact 
• The existing penalty regime has led to a heterogenous approach across Member 
States. Furthermore, some have set up disproportionate and non- effective fines 
regimes (e.g., issuing ‘’standard’’ fines to carriers regardless of the fine trigger , i.e. 
non-submission, late submission, spelling).  
• In such cases, a carrier operating a reasonable number of flights into a country with 
a punitive approach, maybe assuming around 800k EUR fines yearly. 
  
C. The solution 
• Set up a clear, standard, agreed-upon framework for assessing API quality. This 
will help carriers and authorities to have a framework for improvement. Carriers 
should have the prerogative to decide how to address the identified quality issues.  
• Work on an 'error threshold' for inaccurate data , ensuring a sensible and 
tolerable error level within which carriers can operate. Otherwise, the requirements 
can be completely unreasonable (e.g., sanctions for one letter wrong in a name). 
• Such a clear and balanced framework should be incorporated into the existing Art 
30 of 729 and Art 16 of 731.

About the German Association for Reusable Packaging  
The non-profit organisation was founded in 2022 and consists of 70 members, mainly companies and 
organisations that 
● are system operators of reusable packaging solutions for food, E -commerce, take -away or 
body- and homecare articles, 
● fill food, body- and homecare and other products in reusable packaging, 
● investigate or research and/or consult the topic of reusables, and 
● offer solutions in material selection, design, cleaning, logistics or sorting.  
Our common goal: avoid packaging waste and close material cycles to not  lose valuable materials and 
resources. The clear focus of the association is on reusable packaging systems (in German 
“Mehrwegsysteme”). 
The German Association for Reusable Packaging fosters networking, the exchange of experience and 
collaboration between members and external actors from business, civil society and science. Together 
we are working to make reusable packaging an ecologically and economically advantageous option in 
as many areas as possible. 
We initiate and support practical projec ts to develop the physical and digital reusable infrastructure as 
well as projects to standardise and improve interoperability between reusable systems. We support 
scientific studies as well as public relations and capacity building for different target gr oups.

Our position at a glance 
The obligation to offer reusable packaging for take-away food (‘Mehrwegangebotspflicht’) as part of the 
amendment of the German Packaging Act in place since 1st January 2023 proves that legislation can 
significantly support innovative solutions and the necessary infrastructure to reduce packaging.  
Above all, formulating clear targets and necessary framework conditions while avoiding over-regulation, 
supports innovation and provides investment security. 
The German Association for Reusable Packaging therefore considers the proposal to amend the 
Directive (94/62/EC) on packaging and packaging waste, first issued in 1994, and its transformation 
into a regulation as an important initiative for a sustainable change in the packaging industry. 
The German Association for Reusable Packaging particularly welcomes the fact that the draft contains 
reuse as an option for packaging, including concrete targets. At the same time, the steering effect in 
the current draft is limited by essential points. 
The German Association for Reusable Packaging therefore proposes: 
● The clear and appropriate use of definitions of terms for reusable packaging, including a clear 
differentiation between reusable packaging or systems and refillable items in consumer 
possession ("bring your own" or "refill"), as well as the use of the wide used and common term 
"Mehrweg" in the German version of the Regulation; 
● A realistic (practice oriented) description of reusable systems, replacing the currently 
inadequate and inaccurate distinction between "open and closed loop systems" in Annex VI of 
the draft regulation; 
● A clear distinction of the concepts of reusable packaging and refilling in the targets as laid down 
in Art. 26, an increase of the target for take-away/ready-prepared food, intended for immediate 
consumption, as well as the introduction of targets for other FMCG product groups;  
● The exception of reusable packaging already in use as regards the specified minimum recycled 
content for packaging as laid down in Art. 7 , as well as  temporary exemptions for newly 
reusable packaging placed on the market, in case there are insufficient quantities of recycled 
material available on the market; 
● To state explicitly the option for Member States to set more ambitious targets than those set 
out in the Regulation - e.g. to be allowed to set higher reusable targets; 
● Not to include a mandatory obligation for the introduction of serialised labelling of reusable 
packaging, as it can be read from Art. 11(2) and (4); 
● Include a clearly regulated, general take-back obligation for reusable packaging.

Proposal in detail 
Clear definition and distinction of reuse concepts 
The term reuse refers to various concepts for packaging reduction, which must be clearly defined and 
distinguished from each other: 
1. Reusable packaging in the frame of reusable systems (described in the draft Regulation as 
"reusable packaging within a syste m for re -use"). This packaging is (supported by an 
appropriate incentive) circulated within a process (e.g. cleaned and/or repaired) and then made 
available to be used for the original purpose (as defined in the German Packaging Act 
(VerpackG) §3 (3)). 
2. Refill (bring-your-own): This is the possibility that suitable packaging is brought by customers 
for filling food or drinks. The containers are typically in the property of the customer, and 
customers are responsible for cleaning and storing the containers.  
The draft of the Regulation defines in Art. 10  reusable packaging, as packaging that has been 
conceived, designed and placed on the market with the objective to be re-used or refilled; (Art. 10 (1)). 
As a result, the definition contains a cross reference with regard to reusable packaging and carries the 
risk of misinterpretation. In addition, the terms are not used consistently - both in English and German 
versions of the draft. 
For a legally secure EU Packaging Regulation, it is necessary that terms as def ined in Art. 3 are used 
clearly and consistently, to make clear always which reuse concept is meant. The cross reference 
described above currently leads to massive inaccuracies in the draft regulation:  
● Art. 11, 23, 24, 44  and 50 refer to "reusable packagin g" only. We assume that "reusable 
packaging within a system for re -use" is meant here, since otherwise customer -owned 
packaging would need to be tagged with digital data carriers in the meaning of Art. 11 or could 
be returned via deposit and return systems; 
● The English version uses the terms 'reuse' and 're -use', whereby a difference in terminology 
only becomes apparent on repeated reading. The term 're -use' or 'system for re -use' (with 
hyphen) obviously refers to reusable packaging (in German ‘Mehrwegverpackung’) or reusable 
systems (in German ‘Mehrwegsysteme’), in contrast to 'refill'. The term 'reuse' (without hyphen) 
or 'reusable packaging', on the other hand, seems to correspond as a generic term (in German 
‘Wiederverwendung’), but is not applied consis tently in the draft. Here, too, we see a great 
danger of misunderstandings and misinterpretation.  
For more clarity and to avoid misinterpretation, the German Association for Reusable Packaging 
therefore recommends, to use in the German version of the Regulation 
● use in Art. 3 (22) and (26) the established terms "Mehrweg" or "Mehrwegverpackungen" and 
"Mehrwegsystem" instead of "wiederverwendbarer Verpackungen im Rahmen eines 
Wiederverwendungssystems" and "Wiederverwendungssystem"  
● instead of the current definition in Art. 10 (1)  “Verpackungen gelten als wiederverwendbar, 
wenn sie die folgenden Bedingungen erfüllen: a) sie wurden mit dem Ziel konzipiert, entwickelt 
und in Verkehr gebracht, wiederverwendet oder wiederbefüllt werden zu können”;  
use the following definition: “Verpackungen gelten als wiederverwendbar, wenn sie die 
folgenden Bedingungen erfüllen: a) sie wurden mit dem Ziel konzipiert, entwickelt und in 
Verkehr gebracht, als Mehrwegverpackung verwendet zu werden oder wiederbefüllt werden zu 
können;...”,

Set clear, measurable and ambitious targets for reusable packaging 
The German Association for Reusable Packaging supports the fact that the draft regulation contains for 
the first time concrete targets for the use of reusable packaging in different sectors. We consider the 
legally binding targets as a very powerful and directing instrument.  
All targets given in Art. 26 (2) to (6) are joint targets for reusable packaging and for packaging which 
are owned and can be refiled by customers ('re -use and refill targets'). The targets as laid down in Art. 
26(2) to (6) however can only apply to reusable packaging within a system for re -use 
(“Mehrwegverpackungen”) for the following reasons:  
● A target for reusable packaging contains a clear regulatory addressee, producers or retailers, 
who can be obliged to comply with the target in the regulation subject to sanctions; 
● On the other hand, in the case of customer-owned packaging for refilling, the addressee of the 
regulation can only be obliged to provide the offer, but not to comply with a target.  
● In addition, there is a risk that compliance with the target is not comprehensible and more fairly 
documented in the case of customer -owned packaging for refilling. There is a risk of misuse 
and misreporting. 
Since the refilling of customer-owned packaging of course contributes to waste prevention targets, this 
type of reuse should be promoted, but not by being included in the targets, but for example by stipulating 
that the filling of these packaging must be made available in principle, if the product allows it. In addition, 
there could be targets to sell defined product categories via refill stations. 
The German Association for Reusable Packaging estimates the level of the targets  in the EU -wide 
context as realistic. We are convinced, however, that in some countries with already established 
reusable systems, higher targets can be achieved. Although the introductory part of the draft legislation 
states that EU Member States may adopt requirements that go beyond the minimum targets set out in 
this Regulation ( recitals 92), there is no specific wording in the according articles. From the point of 
view of the German Association for Reusable Packaging, it is imperative to enable the Memb er States 
to formulate more ambitious goals that go beyond the targets of the draft regulation. In Germany, for 
example, a reusable target of 70 percent for beverage packaging has been in force since 2019. This 
must not be weakened by EU requirements under any circumstances. 
In the case of  ready-prepared food, intended for immediate consumption  (take-away) the German 
Association for Reusable Packaging considers the target for 2030 (10%, Art. 26 (3a)) as too low, but 
justifiable in view of the EU-wide implementation. However, in the opinion of the association, the target 
for 2040 (40%, Art. 26 (3b)) is too low for three reasons: 
1. It is expected that by 2040 exemptions for packaging subject to the reuse targets are formulated 
by the Commission (correspondingly Art. 26 (16b and 16c) ); this will lead to an immediate 
increase in the reusable target achieved. 
2. After the introduction phase and the associated transition effort, experience has shown that an 
expansion of the reusable systems will proceed quickly, as scaling effects occur especially after 
the initial introduction in reusable systems, which lead to lower unit costs and reduced other 
expenses.

3. The restriction on the use of single-use packaging for food and beverages filled and consumed 
within the premises in the HORECA sector (Art. 22 and Annex V) will further promote the use 
of reusable solutions for out-of-home consumption. 
From the perspective of the German Association for Reusable Packaging, following these three 
justifications, the same target for 2040 should be set  in the food sector as for take -away beverages 
(Art. 26 (2b)).  
The restriction of the targets for food and beverages filled and consumed only within the premises in 
the HORECA sector (Art. 26(3) ) should be deleted. The provision for take -away food needs to be 
extended to all kinds of final distributors (similar to the provision for take -away beverages, Art. 26(2)), 
as e.g. food retailers, bakeries, cinemas and events also produce significant amounts of single -use 
packaging waste. 
The Commissi on's power to impose binding targets on further products and packaging formats as 
included in  Art. 26 (16a)  enables future expansion of reusable systems - this is also very much 
welcomed by the German Association for Reusable Packaging. Based on decades of  positive 
experience in Germany, further targets for packaging of milk and dairy products should be set. Plus, 
experience from other FMCG product groups can serve as an incentive for the European Commission 
to introduce further targets - these include, for example, reuse systems in the following areas:  
1. Foods, such as spreads, canned foods, sauces, edible oils, cocoa and coffee, for which the 
first reusable systems have been established; 
2. Non-food products as cosmetic products, detergents and cleaning agents for which first 
reusable packaging solutions are applied. 
The German Association for Reusable Packaging offers the European Commission to supplement the 
list of products and packaging i n Art. 26. We are happy to share details about positive experiences of 
reusable packaging systems being developed and the necessary infrastructure.  
Overall, the targets give Member States and companies investment certainty and have the potential to 
support the development of reusable packaging systems. The exceptions in Art. 26 (16b and c) (further 
exemptions for economic sectors and packaging formats possible) however, counteract this potential.  
The German Association for Reusable Packaging therefore proposes in summary, 
● in Art. 26 (2) to (6) set targets for reusable packaging within a system for re-use solely;  
● state under Art. 26 explicitly that EU Member States can set more ambitious targets; 
● increase the target for take -away food in reusable packaging in Art. 26 (3b) to 80% for 2040 
and extend the provision to all final distributor for take-away food (not only for HORECA) 
● introduce targets also for product groups mentioned in the previous paragraph (including dairy 
products, spreads, preserves, edible oils, cocoa/coffee, cosmetics), e.g. in line with the targets 
for alcoholic beverages in the form of wine; 
● All decisions about exceptions pursuant to Art. 26 (16b and c) should be made fairly, justifiably 
and transparently, e.g. by a balanced expert committee.

Clear labelling and traceability 
The German Association for Reusable Packaging advocates the use of a harmonised EU-label or  reuse 
logo ( Art. 11 (5 )) in order to make it easier for consumers to distinguish between single -use and 
reusable packaging. However, for reusable packaging that is already in use, and to which 
manufacturers/system operators have only limited access, sub sequent labelling would entail 
considerable additional effort and costs. 
Furthermore, the German Association for Reusable Packaging supports the marking as reusable 
packaging at the point of sale (Art. 11 (2)). There is no specification included, how to do this. Under the 
German Packaging Act, the installation of signs directly on the shelf ("shelf marking") is required, which 
could serve as orientation.  
The German Association for Reusable Packaging supports the reporting of detailed information about 
reuse cycles (trips and rotations) to guarantee ecological advantage. This can be done via various 
methods. Statistical statements based on inventory data are state of the Art. Art. 11 para 2 in 
conjunction with para 4 could be understood as serialised labelli ng of reusable packaging. However, 
the practice of reusable packaging in the beverage industry - established in Germany for more than 5 
decades - proves that these systems can be controlled highly efficiently without appropriate labelling 
and achieve high return rates. Mandatory serialised labelling would increase the operation costs of the 
systems without any added value. This would weaken reusable packaging systems in competition with 
single-use packaging.  
Serialisation is a good method to collect significant data about a system. The process offers additional 
benefits such as full track & trace of the exact number of trips and rotations and tracking of the value 
chain. It thus provides well-founded data for assessing the sustainability of reusable packaging systems. 
Based on this, we support the voluntary introduction of serialisation. 
In summary, the German Association for Reusable Packaging proposes 
● that reusable packaging which is already in use at the time of entry into force of the Regulation 
is exempted from the labelling requirements in Art. 11 (2); 
● specify how marking is to be done at the point of sale (Art.11 (2)); 
● to accept other methods to prove reuse cycles (trips and rotations) ( Art. 11 (2) and (5)) and to 
delete the obligation for serialised labelling of reusable packaging 
Make return as easy as possible 
When setting up reusable systems, it is extremely importan t to make the return as convenient as 
possible for consumers in order to achieve high response rates. The German Association for Reusable 
Packaging therefore welcomes the approach to encourage the Member States to use take -back 
infrastructure applied for t he single-use packaging also for reusable packaging ( Art. 44 (6) ). and the 
approach that return of reusable packaging should be as convenient for consumers as the return of 
single-use packaging ( Art. 44 (8)). 
The requirements of Art. 44 , however, are insu fficient to ensure convenient return conditions for 
reusable packaging. Analogous to the general take-back obligation for single-use beverage packaging 
made of plastic and metal as laid down in Annex X, an obligation shall be included for the take-back of 
reusable packaging of the same type.  
The German Association for Reusable Packaging  therefore demands that take-back points shall 
be obliged to accept both single-use packaging with deposit refund and reusable packaging. Each take-
back point should take ba ck at least the packaging categories sold at the site, so that the return of 
reusable packaging is possible independent from systems ('return anywhere' concept).

This can be achieved by supplementing paragraphs 1-10 of Art. 26  by a subparagraph (c), which 
reads as follows: 
(c) from 1 January 2030, the final distributors shall take back all reusable packaging placed on 
the market of a Member State of the product categories referred to in the first sentence. By 
participating in a clearing system for reusable packaging or the i ntegration in reverse logistics, 
such as wholesalers, the final distributor ensures that reusable packaging which is not 
distributed by himself is brought back in the use chain. 
According to the intention of the German Association for Reusable Packaging, t his addition ensures 
that 
● consumers can return reusable packaging conveniently and without additional effort, thereby 
strengthening the acceptance of reusable packaging; 
● The acceptance of reusable packaging within a system for re -use by producers and last 
distributors is strengthened and the number of individual reusable systems is reduced. This 
strengthens the ecological and economic performance of reusable systems.  
In addition, the German Association for Reusable Packaging sees not only the return at the  points of 
sale as necessary, but also the development of return infrastructures using e.g. public space.  
Reuse above Recycling  
As the German Association for Reusable Packaging we support closed resource cycles and the use of 
recyclates. Due to abrasion and damage, reusable packaging also reaches a state where it can no 
longer be used and has to be sorted out and recycled. However, reusable packaging is designed to 
remain in the use phase for a long time - for some packaging (e.g. transport crates) the use phase lasts 
decades. The minimum recycled content for 2030 and 2040 as stated in  Art. 7(1)  and (2) should 
therefore not apply to packaging already on the market. They would have to be ejected, destroyed and 
re-produced in order to meet the requirements of Art. 7.  
In addition, reusable packaging usually requires more material than single -use packaging in order to 
have a higher stability that ensures the highest possible number of reuse cycles. If there will be a 
significantly increased demand for recyclates suitable for the use of contact-sensitive filling goods from 
2030 onwards, this could significantly hinder the introduction of reusable packaging.  
Against this background, the German Association for Reusable Packaging requests:  
● an exception to the provi sions of Art. 7 (1) and (2) on minimum recycled material content for 
reusable packaging already in use;  
● an opening clause for Art. 7 (1) and (2) in the event that recyclate is not sufficiently available; 
● speeding up EFSA's authorisation procedures for recycled materials in direct contact with food, 
e.g. rPP 
Deletion of differentiation between reusable system types 
According to the German Association for Reusable Packaging, the terms "closed loop system" and 
"open loop system" introduced in Annex VI do not reflect the reality of the market and the existence of 
various systems for reusable packaging. The transfer of ownership is not a suitable criterion for defining 
reusable systems, since it must be an essential characteristic of a reusable packaging system that the 
packaging remains the property of the system operator. 
The German Association for Reusable Packaging considers therefore that the differentiation between 
closed and open loop systems is not necessary considering the following statements on the governance 
of reusable packaging within a system for re -use. In cas e the differentiation is maintained, we

The German Association for Reusable Packaging considers the requirements for reusable packag ing

● remove the specification of closed and open loop systems in Annex VI  and establish all

1-3) as general requirements for the systems. 
 
Outlook 
The German Association for Reusable Packaging is ready to support the EC constructively and in the 
sense of sustainability in the revision of the draft legislation and to share collected, published studies 
and reports, as well as practical experience from existing and emerging reusable systems. 
Thank you very much for the attention with which the proposals are certainly handled. We wish success 
in revising this important and groundbreaking legislation.

 
 
FIAB POSITION ON PACKAGING AND 
PACKAGING WASTE REGULATION 
COMMISSION PROPOSAL  
INTRODUCTION  
The Spanish Federation of Food and Drink Industries welcomes the European Commission's 
proposal for a Regulation on packaging and packaging waste (PPWR).  
The sector shares the Commission's ambition to continue moving towards packaging 
sustainability, innovating in eco -design to improve its recyclability, promoting the prevention 
and reuse of packaging, and increasing the content of recycled m aterial, thus favoring the 
market for secondary raw materials.  
Over the years, the food and drink industry has constructively worked for the enshrinement of 
recyclability and prevention goals in legislation supported by an holistic view in the context of 
the European Green Deal. The sector is committed to the concept of "circular packaging", which 
meant to be a circular, sustainable and waste -preventing alternative to traditional throwaway 
models, that could even be a better option than only reuse.  
However, before imposing obligations that are extremely restrictive, it is necessary to carry out 
a thorough evaluation that allows analyzing the feasibility of the measures in order to determine 
which is the best option in each situation, from an environmental, technical, economic and social 
perspective, without jeopardizing food security and giving companies sufficient time to adapt to 
possible changes that entail relevant impacts on production processes and high investments. It 
is important to provide  long ter m guidance, predictability and  a margin of flexibility to 
companies to be able to comply with the objectives, as well as try to minimize bureaucratic 
burdens.  
This regulation should also ensure the integrity and proper functioning of the single market, 
avoiding the proliferation of national or regional legislation that entails legal uncertainty for 
businesses.  
Our guiding principles:  
• Contribute to the Green Deal objectives and align with the United Nations Sustainable 
Development Goals; 
• Ensure the proper functioning of the EU single market;  
• Set demanding but realistic goals that can be met and allow sufficient flexibility to allow 
industry to decide how best to achieve these objectives.

• Be coherent and consistent with other packaging-related legislation, such as the Waste 
Framework legislation, the Single- Use Plastics Directive and the Food Contact Materials

FIAB COMMENTS ON KEY ISSUES  
RECYCLABILITY (ART. 6, ANNEX II)  
In order to facilitate the pathway to more circular and sustainable packaging, it is important to 
support industry in its commitment to making packaging 100% recyclable.  
Redesigning packaging and comply with a new system at scale requires time and investments 
and human resources.  If all packaging will need to be compliant with Design for Recycling 
guidelines, sufficient transition time should be granted. The delegated acts should be published 
within 12 months from the adoption of the Regulation. Also, the Regulation should grant at least 
5 years to comply with the design for recycling. During the time the criteria are developed, the 
Regulation should clarify that current standards apply to help economic operators comply with 
the legislation.  
To achieve that goal, the following suggestions must be considered:  
• Sufficient transition time should be granted. 
• Assume a material, end-of-life pathway and technology neutral approach  
• Foster a system that helps innovation and safeguard forward-looking policies. 
• Improve sorting and collection rates (e.g. through infrastructure investment  by 
competent authorities; harmonised minimum requirements for EPR, DRS) 
• Clear guidelines for recyclability evaluation, considering new and innovative rec ycling 
technologies. 
• Support harmonised labelling (incl. digital solutions) and sorting instructions across all 
MS. 
RECYCLED CONTENT (ART. 7)  
To meet their recycled content obligations laid down in the SUP Dire ctive (and in the future 
PPWR), producers need to have access to a sufficient amount of affordable, high -quality 
recycled food-grade plastic. Currently, there are difficulties in ensuring access to food -grade 
recycled material.  
 
 
Recycled PET is highly demanded by other sectors to incorporate it into lower quality 
applications, such as textiles or tires, where it will generally no longer be recyclable. To avoid 
this downcycling, producers – and any other sector that must be subject to mandatory minimum 
recycled content targets – should have priority access to a fair share of the recycled materials 
they put on the market. 
In the case of r -PET from beverage bottles, it should be noted that it is demanded by a wide 
range of food and non -food sectors, due to the numerous attributes of PET (high recyclability, 
suitability for contact with food, etc.) and is intended for other applications. This means that 
beverage PET is no longer used for the same purpose, indirectly increasing the need for virg in 
plastic for the beverage industry. This situation limits beverage packers' access to their recycled 
materials, when they are currently the only ones subject to the EU's mandatory r-PET use targets 
(SUP Directive).  
On the other hand, the targets lay down in Article 7 apply for the plastic part of the packaging, 
and after clarification by CE, any part of plastic packaging, no matter how much there is of it, 
will need to comply with these targets. However, so far, PET is practically the o nly recycled 
plastic generally authorized for use in food contact material. It is expected that EFSA will be 
authorizing new processes for other kind of plastics, but there is no timeline so , currently, the 
only real market for recycled plastic suitable for contact with food is PET. This provision “for the 
plastic part in packaging” should only apply when EFSA  not only assessed  the processes of 
recycling for other plastics as safe, but authorized it, and these plastics will be available on the 
market. 
In addition, the following suggestions must be considered:  
• Make sure targets consider availability and costs of the recycled material, as well as the 
quality of infrastructure and technology available. In that sense, targets for 2040 should 
be reduced.  
• Targets should apply after 5 years f rom the adoption of the implementing act laying 
down the methodology for the calculation.  
• It is also imperative that targets are set per economic operator as an average of their 
product portfolio placed on the market, in a manner consistent with the SUP Directive, 
rather than per unit of packaging. 
• Unlock (early authorisation of) new and innovative recycling technologies such as 
chemical recycling, in order to have food-grade recycled material available. For this, 
we want to promote this more quickly, taking into account that the creation of "fuel" 
would not be counted as recycled, as established by Spanish legislation, and support 
the balance of masses and the credit method. 
• All operators of the food and beverage sector with mandatory recycled plastic targets 
should be granted priority access to recycled material  from packaging placed on the 
market (Right-of-first-Refusal), thus avoiding downcycling. 
• Plastic parts in non -plastic dominated packaging , like paper and metal , should be 
excluded from the recycled plastics targets. 
 
 
• Packaging for baby food should be given special considerations due to the vulnerable 
consumer group and stricter specific migration limits than for general food (like for 
medical devices). 
• The implementing acts establishing the methodology for the calculation and verification 
of the percentage of recycled content and the format for the technical documentation  
must be published within 12 month s, to allow time for packaging to comply with the 
obligations.  
• Ensure a high level of food safety and consistency with other EU legislation. 
• Avoid packaging functionality from being undermined. 
REUSE AND REFILL (ART. 10, 23 -28, 45, ANNEX VI)  
We consider it essential during this process to insist on the fact that reuse should only be 
considered when demonstrably beneficial for the environment and cost-efficient.  
Before proposing target s per sector, it is very important to analyze the specificities of the 
different categories: for example, their volume, characteristics of the distribution channel that 
can make the reverse logistics process very complicated, location of the factories, siz e of the 
companies or the density and organic content of the product itself.  
In this sense, it is also very important to note that reuse packaging is not better from an 
environmental and economic point of view by default. Therefore, we believe it is neces sary to 
include the concept of “ circular packaging ” as a circular, sustainable and waste -preventing 
alternative to traditional throwaway models. Instead of focusing only on re -use as the unique 
alternative, circular packaging is a wider concept that includ es single -use and 
refillable/returnable packaging as valid solutions in terms of waste prevention and 
environmental impact minimization.  
Circular packaging is that which is collected separately at a high rate, refilled or recycled multiple 
times; when rec ycled, it should contain a high percentage of recycled material, thereby 
contributing to material resource efficiency and litter prevention. 
The aim is to reflect that single-use circular packaging is an option technically, economically and 
environmentally useful, effective and feasible (even more than reusable packaging for long 
supply and distribution chains) to minimize the use of virgin materials, to reduce the 
environmental impact of packaging and to prevent packaging waste generation.  
The definition of “circular packaging” should be included on the list of definitions of article 3 and 
then, to introduce a reference in the text so to count the circular packaging as a way to reduce 
the placing on the market of virgin materials and prevent packaging wast e. “Single-use circular 
packaging” should be included as a feasible and allowed alternative to achieve the targets set in 
the article 26  “Re-use and refill targets” for that sectors or economic actors that can 
demonstrate that this is the best alternative from an environmental, technical and economic 
perspective.  
 
 
Furthermore, reuse systems already in place which have been working for years should not be 
subject to additional red tape and burdens. To mitigate the risks of reducing the effectiveness of 
well-functioning, existing deposit systems by introducing new requirements, the already existing 
systems for reuse must be excluded for all below requirements (Annex VI).  
Finally, it would be critical  to recognize the strategic importance of packaging differentiation 
design. In the competitive environment which companies operate, packaging plays a critical 
role. Quality, service and cost standards of the companies are key, as well as their strategies 
from several aspects: needs of logistical efficiency and  needs of commercial and marketing 
differentiation. 
The following considerations must be taken into account:  
• Circular packaging/flexibility:  
o Apply a broad definition of reuse that can help all sectors to be part of the green 
transition. 
o Consider reuse when demonstrably beneficial for environmental , underpinned by 
consumer acceptance and cost-efficiency. 
o It is necessary to ensure flexibility and innovation in terms of packaging format. 
• Objectives:  
o Ensure realistic, evidence based and proportionate targets with clear definitions 
and scope. 
o The attainment of the targets should allow flexibility so that the calculation may 
be based on the volume of beverages or the number of units of sales. 
o In case mandatory targets per producers are maintained, they should not be 
imposed per individual producer but on a sectoral basis, without due regard to 
the actual environmental benefit they bring, as they may contradict the EUs 
broader decarbonisation target. Moreover, in that case, many SMEs located in 
rural areas would be at risk of disappearing.  
o Reuse targets should be complementary to recycled content targets and should 
not undermine recycling efforts and investments. 
o Where the objectives are maintain ed at producer level, provision should be 
made for an exemption for SMEs, not only for micro-companies. 
• Cross-industry solutions, standards since reuse targets for transport packaging will have 
cross-industry implications (transport, retail etc). 
• Recognize the strategic importance of packaging differentiation design. 
• Guarantee infrastructure and funding to ensure smooth (reverse) logistics. 
• In any case, provision should be made for a sufficiently long transition period to facilitate 
the transition of sectors obliged to re-use. 
 
 
LABELLING (ART. 11, 12)  
In order to ensure a smooth functioning of the single market, i t is necessary to s upport 
harmonised labelling and sorting instructions across all Member States. 
Consumers should not be misled  by too much and possibly contradictory information on how 
to correctly handle empty packaging. The deposit logo provides sufficient guarantees of the 
righteous handling of the packaging once the consumer has handed in the packaging. 
The additional labelling requirements will increase greenhouse gas emissions as bigger labels 
and more ink (washing off detergents, etc…) will be required. 
For ease of access to solely packaging related information, it may be preferred to have the 
deposit logo as the digital information carrier for packaging information. Equally, not all 
European manufacturers recourse to off -label communication means and therefore cannot 
prevail themselves of a present on-line digital carrier. 
Including data  or in formation to facilitate the tracking of the packaging and the number of 
rotations for each packaging is an excessive burden at an economic and administrative level, 
since it would require an individualized QR code for each packaging 
RESTRICTIONS ON PACKAGING (ART. 22, ANNEX V)  
Article 22 establishes restrictions on the use of certain packaging formats (annex V), for example, 
single use grouped packaging such as collation films or shrink wraps , or single use packaging in 
HORECA sector . Switching to alternatives to those single-use plastic packaging formats  
requires an appropriate transition period  to do customer trials, stability tests, ensuring the 
convenience for retailers, etc. A too short transition period may a lso lead to shortages in the 
market for the alternative solutions or materials. 
It is important to note the need to have material with adequate mechanical strength and elastic 
limit to perform in applications that require stretching of the material. It is therefore necessary 
to ensure the stability and protection required, both in its distribution process, handling during 
trade as well as by the final consumer. 
Companies cannot afford to change plastic by other materials to group packaging completely 
because other options imply drastic changes in packaging lines, sometimes not affordable 
economically nor viable due to the lack of space or the characteristics of the material. Moreover, 
the weight of other materials could be much greater and would affect the e rgonomics of load 
handling because plastic is waterproof and not permeable to liquids, gases and vapours 
protecting the products during transport. 
Measures in this case should focus on incorporating recycled plastic to the grouped packaging 
(the threshold fixed at the present technically possible). This would be more consistent with 
measures already taken by some national authorities and would reduce the placing on the 
market of virgin material. 
 
 
Additionally, it is necessary to consider the requirements of food safety and hygiene (allergens, 
cross-contamination…), quality, food waste, etc., for many of the packaging formats mentioned 
in Annex V, especially paragraph 4 used in the HORECA sector.  
COMPOSTABLE PACKAGING (ART. 8, ANNEX III)  
The draft Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (PPWR) creates a mandate on 
compostability for all coffee or tea system  single-serve unit in 2026 - within 24 months of the 
entry into force of the future Regulation (Article 8(1) of the proposal).  
Although we welcome the consideration of packaging for all coffee or tea single- serve unit, we 
oppose the requirement that they must be compostable. The entire Spanish food and beverage 
industry, including the coffee  and tea  sector, demand the free choice of materials by the 
operators. 
Based on the current environmental data, the potential econ omic impacts, the adverse effect 
on innovation and the challenge of short-term implementation, we are convinced recycling and 
composting must both be considered as valid End-of-Life options for coffee or tea single- serve 
unit. Any proposal that mandates the use of solely one specific material is counterproductive.   
It is imperative that packaging have access to recycling and composting streams and that the 
appropriate investment is made in infrastructure to allow consumers to participate.  
Furthermore, we believe that the proposed Regulation should include a reference to a specific 
standard or certification for compostable packaging (Article 8 and Annex III), such as the UNE -
EN 13432:2000 Standard. 
About home compost: It is a reality technic ally, commercially and in regulation too, at least in 
Spain (Ley 7/2022, de 8 de abril, de residuos, y suelos contaminados para una economía circular; 
Real decreto 1055/2022 de 27 de diciembre de envases y residuos de envases  ) and Spanish 
regions (Andalucía: Proyecto de Ley de Economía Circular );  and it must be considered in this 
Regulation in any way. Therefore, we request that home composting be contemplated in the 
proposed Regulation and that a specific standard be developed at European level, taking existing 
models as a reference (e.g., AFNOR NF T 51-800). 
OBLIGATIONS OF MANUFACT URERS (ART. 13 , 15, 16, 20 ) 
A high number of documents must be requested by the manufacturer, analysed and kept for 
quite a number of years so that compliance can be undertaken. Many of the documents 
duplicate information already requested in the context of other European legislation, including 
the food contact materials legislation. 
So, conformity and obligations posed on manufacturers with regard to its assessment should be 
streamlined across existing legislation. 
Indication of the contact details of manu facturers on labels goes far beyond Regulation 
1169/2011, whereby, according to article 9.1. (h) it is the name or business name and address 
 
 
of the food business operator responsible which should be mentioned in the label (and it may 
be other than the manufacturer).

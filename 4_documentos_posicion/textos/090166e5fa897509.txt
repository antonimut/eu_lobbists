 
 
 
 
 
Nordic Ecolabelling feedback on the EU Commissions proposal for a regulation 
on packaging and packaging waste 
 
Thank you for the proposal for a regulation on packaging and packaging waste and the possibility to 
give feedback.  
 
Nordic Ecolabelling supports the EU Commission’s Green Deal and the circular economy action plan, 
including the proposal for a regulation on packaging and packaging waste. Packaging is a key 
environmental concern. We think it is positive that the Commission is proposing to raise the level of 
ambition considerably and, in particular, that uniform methods will be established for documenting 
material composition and recycling rates. This is needed if we are to achieve the goal of significantly 
reducing the climate impact of packaging.  
 
Nordic Ecolabelling has 30 years of experience with ecolabelling of products and services. We 
manage the Nordic Swan Ecolabel, which is the official ecolabel of the Nordic countries. The 
national offices are also competent bodies for EU Ecolabel. Based on this we have some comments. 
They are summarized here and more details are given below: 
 
 The three main objectives of the proposal are in line with the work done by Nordic 
Ecolabelling. We set requirements for the packaging of Nordic Swan Ecolabelled products 
based on the following principles: resource efficiency, design for reuse and recycling, use of 
recycled material and sustainable sourced renewable resources in packaging.  
 Concerning labelling of packaging it should be made explicit in Article 11 that Type I 
ecolabels will continue to be permitted, as they are not misleading, but a trusted and well-
known standard for greener choices. 
 Nordic Ecolabelling recommend that as a starting point for the increased use of recycled 
plastics, the Commission should adopt implementing acts establishing strict quality 
requirements to recycled material to ensure its safe use in packaging. 
 Nordic Ecolabelling supports all initiatives to establish joint guidance, rules, tests, 
measurements and calculations, including requirements on traceability to verify compliance 
of packaging with the regulation. These are of utmost importance for successful 
implementation of the regulation. 
 Considering Nordic Ecolabelling’s concerns about the potentially harmful substances 
originating from recycled plastics in packaging, we support to establish databases including 
information on the toxicity or danger of packaging materials (Article 51).

Ecolabels can help push development 
Ecolabels such as the EU Ecolabel and the Nordic Swan Ecolabel already place demands on many 
types of packaging, both focusing on the inclusion of recycled material and the design of packaging 
in order to promote a circular economy. This may mean, for example, restrictions on what kind of 
plastic you can mix together and how it should be handled. This is implemented for product groups 
where we have criteria both for the product and the packaging (e.g. laundry detergents, toys, 
furniture) and for product groups where the packaging is the product group (e.g. packaging for 
liquid foods). Therefore, we greatly appreciate developing uniform requirements for documentation 
and methods for calculating the recycling rate. This can also support that we can raise the 
requirements further (for example level of recycled content), and thus achieve a very good 
interaction between legislation and ecolabels. 
 
Article 5 - Requirements for substances in packaging 
Nordic Ecolabelling has long experience working with substances harmful to environment and health 
and at the same time set requirements striving to increase use of recycled material in the Nordic 
Swan Ecolabelled product and its packaging. Availability and quality of recycled material is often the 
limiting factor, mainly due to evolving market. There is also a lack of methods to guarantee safety 
of recycled material. Recycled material may contain harmful substances, impurities and residues 
that can migrate from the packaging to the product. Demands for the quality of recycled material 
also differ depending on the product type, e.g. demands for recycled plastic in packaging of toys or 
in food contact materials are quite different from those used to pack e.g. furniture. Therefore, 
requirements should also take into account the product type. 
 
Article 7 - Minimum recycled content in plastic packaging 
Nordic Ecolabelling totally agrees that increasing the use of recycled plastic in packaging is highly 
profitable to promote the circular economy. Use of recycled material in packaging should not, 
however, happen at the expense of packaging quality. The proposal highlights generally the need 
for high quality post-consumer recycled material but there are, however, only a few articles aiming 
to ensure high quality recycled material.  
 
Nordic Ecolabelling has for many product types set requirements for recycled content, depending 
on the product and its packaging. Availability and quality of recycled material is, however, often the 
limiting factor, mainly due to evolving markets and missing methods to guarantee purity of recycled 
plastics. Demands for the quality of recycled plastic also differ depending on the product type in 
question, e.g. demands for recycled plastic in packaging of toys or in food contact materials are 
quite different from those used to pack e.g. furniture. Requirements for recycled content should 
take into account quality requirements and the product type in question and guarantee its safe use, 
including harmful substances or impurities not migrating from the packaging to the product. 
 
 
 
 
It is highly desirable that the methods, for calculation and verifications shall be developed as quickly 
as possible. Today, there are no standardized methods available, thereby causing confusion and 
decreasing reliability. Nordic Ecolabelling recommend that as a starting point for the increased use 
of recycled plastics, Commission should adopt implementing acts establishing strict quality 
requirements to recycled material to ensure its safe use in packaging. This includes focus on 
developing e.g. guidance, measures and routines regarding e.g. origin and traceability of recycled 
plastics as well as accepted test methods for verification of impurities and potential migration of 
harmful substances.  
 
Article 8 - Compostable packaging 
In principle, it is reasonable to require compostable packaging for products where the packaging 
and labels are disposed of together with the product and treated with biowaste. However, 
techniques and processes to handle biowaste vary from country to country as well as the 
instructions regarding what can be included in the process. This should be taken into account when 
setting requirements for compostable packaging.  
 
Article 9 – Packaging minimisation 
We support the proposed requirements for packaging minimisation. Requirements for material 
efficiency and design for recycling are core requirements set for packaging in ecolabelling criteria. 
For example, there is a requirement for packaging volume for ecolabelled toys where empty space 
between the product and outer edges of packaging is limited to maximum of 2 cm.  
 
For several Swan ecolabelled products there is a requirement for weight-utility ratio (WUR). The 
purpose of the WUR is to reduce the amount of packaging, thus, helping to ensure a reduction in 
the unnecessary transport of packaging and air, and thereby lower the CO2 emissions. WUR is a 
measure of the amount of primary packaging used to deliver an amount of product with a certain 
performance (functional unit). 
 
Article 10 - Reusable packaging 
Nordic Ecolabelling supports the objective that use of reusable packaging shall be promoted. This is 
also in line with our principles.  
However, its use has to be environmentally justified, based on verified scientific data and lead to 
environmental benefits. 
 
Article 11 - Labelling of packaging 
We believe it should be made easy for the consumers to choose the environmental best products. 
This, however, does not mean that more information and more labelling is always the solution. On 
the contrary we risk overwhelming and confusing the consumers. Type 1 ecolabels like the Nordic 
Swan already include environmental aspects of the products lifecycle including packaging.  
 
 
 
 
It is important that officially recognized Type I ecolabels also must be permitted in the future. There 
is no reason to disband already functioning and trustworthy labels. We therefore propose a slight 
addition to article 11, paragraph 7 to ensure that there is no doubt about the future of official 
ecolabels. 
Our suggestion in yellow:  
 
Article 11 (7) 
Without prejudice to requirements concerning other harmonised EU labels, economic operators 
shall not provide or display labels, marks, symbols or inscriptions that are 
likely to mislead or confuse consumers or other end users with respect to the 
sustainability requirements for packaging, other packaging characteristics or 
packaging waste management options, for which harmonised labelling has been laid 
down in this Regulation. These restrictions do not comprise the EU Ecolabel and other officially 
recognized Type I ecolabels (ISO 14024). 
 
Joint labelling for sorting already exists in the Nordic Countries and can be used as a good example 
for establishing a harmonized label,  see closely  https://www.avfallsverige.se/in-english/common-
symbols-for-waste-sorting/ 
 
Article 22 - Restrictions on use of certain packaging formats 
We support this. Nordic Ecolabelling has already ban on disposables such as cosmetics, hygiene and 
toiletry products of less than 50 ml for liquid products or less than 100 g for non-liquid products in 
hotels as stated in the Annex V. There is also a ban on disposable items in the criteria for restaurant 
premises. 
 
Article 51 – Packaging databases 
Considering Nordic Ecolabelling’s concerns about the potentially harmful substances originating 
from recycled plastics in packaging, we support to establish databases including information on the 
toxicity or danger of packaging materials. 
 
Article 57 – Green public procurement 
Article 57 in the new proposal gives the Commission mandate to establish mandatory requirements 
for public procurement. We fully support the goal of ensuring that public procurements have strong 
criteria for packaging and packaging waste. Seeing many member states already use ecolabels as a 
tool in this regard, we would recommend that any new requirements also include official Type 1 
ecolabels.

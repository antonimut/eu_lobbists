Revision of Council Directive 98/24/EC and European 
Parliament and Council Directive 2004/37/EC concerning 
OEL and BLV for lead and its inorganic compounds and 
diisocyanates 
The EU Commission has put forward proposals for new limit values for lead and its inorganic 
compounds and for diisocyanates. The Confederation of Swedish Enterprise believe that it is 
important that limit values are kept up to date based on new knowledge about the health 
effects of chemical substances and social and technological development and welcomes the 
Commission's proposal. 
Our view is that the process of drawing up new directives on working environment, in this 
case CAD and CMRD, must be protected and followed. The Advisory Committee on Safety 
and Health (ACSH) has an important role. The ACSH is composed of the three parties of the 
labor market. The parties have an important role in the implementation and compliance of the 
directives. It is of great importance that the opinion of the ACSH is reflected in new 
directives. In this way, the trust in the process is strengthened. 
The ACSH proposes limit values based on science but also based on technical and socio-
economic factors. If the European Parliament put forward amendments in the directives that 
are not in line with the ACSH, there is a risk of undermining the process and the 
implementation of the directives might become more difficult. 
Limit values (OEL and BLV) are one of several measures that need to be in place in order to 
have a safe working environment. Other important measures are knowledge, routines, 
instructions, training, premises, equipment, PPE etc. 
Lead and its inorganic compounds 
The ACSH was not unanimous in its view on the proposed new limit value (OEL) and 
biological limit value (BLV) for lead and its inorganic compounds. Swedish Enterprise 
supports the view put forward by the employer party in the ACSH, 0.05 mg/m3. This is half of 
today's Swedish limit value. The Commission's proposal is 0.03 mg/m3.

The Commission's proposal for a new biological limit value (150 ug/l) is approximately half of 
the current Swedish biological limit value (approx. 311 ug/l) and a significantly greater 
reduction of the current European biological limit value (700 ug/l). The proposed new limit 
value follows the opinion of the government and employer side in the ACSH. 
Our view is that when the limit values, OEL and BLV, are reduced to a much lower level, an 
extended implementation time is required. However, this is not proposed by the Commission.  
Without a longer implementation time and a gradual reduction of the limit values, companies 
are not given reasonable conditions or possibilities to change their operations. The 
companies concerned are found in the entire value chain from extraction/mining, processing, 
manufacturing and recycling. The change involves, for example, remodeling of existing or 
investment in new premises and ventilation systems and the development of new 
manufacturing processes and alloys. To meet the new requirements, large investments and 
time are required. Investments that for many companies, for example the smelters, involve 
multi-million sums. 
With a gradual reduction of the limit values, OEL and BLV, the possibility for companies to 
reach the very low limit values increases. Limit values that cannot be complied with serve no 
purpose. The consequence of a limit value that cannot be complied with might be that 
companies are either forced to relocate their business or purchasing their products outside 
the EU in countries that might have a poorer protection against occupational exposures, risks 
in work environment are exported. 
Time is also necessary to be able to physiologically see the effects in the blood of the 
employees who have been exposed in work. 
Regarding the occupational limit value, OEL, we want to see a step-by-step reduction. We 
propose: 
75 ug/m3 (8hr TWA) with a 5-year transition after inclusion in CMRD 
50 ug/m3 (8hr TWA) with an 8-year transition after inclusion in CMRD.  
The current Swedish biological limit value (BLV) came into force almost two years ago and 
was then a reduction of 25%. At that time concerns from some certain special foundries were 
expressed. Now when the limit value is proposed to be reduced even further, it is of the 
utmost importance that the industry is given sufficient time to adjust so that the limit values 
can be complied with. We propose: 
200 ug/l with a 5-year transition after inclusion in CMRD 
150 ug/l with an 8-year transition after inclusion in CMRD. 
It is important to understand that a low limit value is not the only solution to protect workers 
from exposure. It is at least as important that there is knowledge of and good routines on 
how the work should be done to minimize exposure and the contamination in the work 
premises. 
Diisocyanates 
Swedish Confederation of Enterprise supports the Commission's proposal. This is in line with 
the opinion of the ACSH.

Unlike the EU, Sweden has for a long period of time had limit values for diisocyanates. 
However, these are expressed in a different way than those currently proposed by the 
Commission. If the Swedish limit values are recalculated, some end up above others below 
the proposed limit value, according to information from the Swedish Work Environment 
Authority. Our view is that Swedish companies are used to handling diisocyanates and are 
well prepared for the change. 
In addition to limit values for diisocyanates, Sweden also has regulations that require other 
measures to reduce exposure. For a long time, we have had requirements for training and 
statutory medical checks. These and other measures together with limit values ensure safe 
handling and low exposure.

FNADE’s position on Packaging and Packaging Waste 
Regulation 
 
April 2023 
FNADE, the French association for waste management and environmental services welcomes the 
proposal of the European Commission for a revision of the packaging and packaging waste directive. 
Overall, this new regulation on packaging and packaging waste repr esents a step forward for the 
waste management sector and FNADE globally supports its objectives. 
FNADE supports the European Commission’s proposal on packaging and packaging waste in order to 
reduce the environmental footprint of packaging, enhance recycling and promote a more circular 
economy. This proposal goes in the right direction. 
Nevertheless, FNADE would like to take the opportunity to make some comments and point out some 
concerns in the proposal. 
 
Delegated and implementing acts 
 
FNADE understands the fact that  not everything can be proposed through the regulation and that 
further impleme ntation of delegated acts will be needed . However,  FNADE would like to ensure 
stakeholders and experts of recycling and packaging will be involved as much as possible in the further 
process of drafting delegated acts.  
Indeed, too many important topics  will be completed through delegated and implementing acts, 
creating uncertainty as these future decisions cannot be evaluated and taken into account in the 
analysis of the entire text at the moment, although they are very important for FNADE’s members. 
Therefore, in order to achieve a smooth recycling market and to better evaluate the technical issues 
and what is really feasible in operational conditions, it is absolutely necessary to involve the 
stakeholders in the further work on delegated acts.  
The proposal considers several delegated and implementing acts to establish criteria and technical 
aspects that influence investment choices in the waste management sector. We feel it is important to 
provide below a list of the acts that have the greatest impact on the activities of our members: 
- In Article 6, the criteria for the design of recycling and the methodology to assess if packaging 
is recycled at scale will be established in delegated acts to be adopted by the Commission. 
- In Article 7, the Commission will adopt an implementing act to establish the methodology for 
the calculation and verification of the percentage of recycled content  recovered from post-
consumer plastic waste and the format for the related technical documentation. Moreover, 
the Commission is empowered to adopt delegated acts to revise and provide for derogations 
from the scope, timing or level of minimum recycled content, and amend the mi nimum 
percentage of recycled content recovered from post-consumer plastic waste, “where justified 
by the lack of availability or excessive prices”. 
- In Article 8, the Commission is empowered to adopt delegated acts to amend the list of 
packaging that must be compostable. 
- In Article 11, the Commission is empowered to, by implementing acts, establish harmonised

labelling requirements  and formats for packaging and waste receptacles  as well as for 
identifying the material composition of packaging by means of digital marking technologies. 
- In Article 26, the Commission may adopt delegated acts laying down more specific re-use

- In Article 27, the Commission shall adopt implementing acts establishing detailed calculation

- In Article 50, the Commission shall adopt implementing acts establishing rules for the

packaging waste generated, and the format for the reporting.  
All these aspects, which will be found in specific acts adopted by the Commission, are of primary 
importance for waste management companies. All those decisions in the hands of the Commission in 
delegated and implementing acts could jeopardise the business  plans, due to the lack of the 
possibility to carry out a prior risk assessment. 
Our main concern is linked to the uncertainty that can be found in paragraph 10 of Article 7, which 
empowers the Commission to adopt delegated acts to adjust the minimum per centages of recycled 
content when justified by the lack of availability or excessive prices of recycled plastics.  
Even though FNADE has always strongly asked for minimum recycled content that allow to substitute 
virgin materials with recycled ones thus re ducing costs, dependencies and negative environmental 
impacts linked to the use of primary raw materials, we fear that without defining “lack of availability” 
and “excessive prices”, the Commission creates the conditions to change, or even block the recycl ing 
market. In doing so, the risk is to send a controversial and contradictory message to all stakeholders 
involved, who may consider the recycling market too uncertain and volatile when, on the contrary, the 
important work done in mechanical recycling has led to excellent results. 
In conclusion, FNADE suggests to delete this proposal, at least if this proposition should remain in the 
regulation, FNADE strongly requests stakeholders and in particular the recycling industry to be part 
of the drafting and to be involved by the Commission as a stakeholder.  
 
Recyclable packaging and recyclability 
 
Introducing design criteria for recyclable packaging and performance scores for recyclable packaging 
based on these criteria is a major step forward. However, FNADE would propose that these criteria 
shall be defined with all stakeholders (packaging industr y, including representatives of the waste 
treatment industry, packaging manufacturers and suppliers, distributors, retailers, importers, SMEs, 
environmental groups, and consumer organisations). Sharing their knowledge and expertise would 
help to define whether or not a packaging is recyclable. 
Recyclability is a needed step to encourage and promote recycling. But recyclability cannot be imposed 
nor dictated. It should be a collective responsibility that can only be achieved with the implication of 
all the actors in the value chain.  
Recyclability should be defined through relevant common criteria applicable to all products. Europe 
should also set up standardised rules, to define the environmental requirements and performance of 
products within the Europe an single market, without Member States being able to derogate from 
them.

FNADE, together with French authorities, producer responsibility organisations, industry, and many 
other stakeholders, has already started to work on a methodology determining basic criteria that 
consider three minimum priority requirements for establishing packaging recyclability: easy collection, 
sorting and reincorporation . Therefore, we strongly support the requirements for recyclable 
packaging laid down in Article 6: collection a nd sorting ability, recyclability at scale, quality, 
downstream market, and the exclusion of the compostability criterion from the framework so as not 
to associate recyclability with compostability. Recyclability at scale is a major objective for packaging  
and its application could be brought forward to 2030 rather than 2035, to enhance recycling channels 
for all packaging. 
In addition to these prerequisites, some other complementary criteria should be taken into 
consideration such as sustainable markets, easy communication, ability to meet the standards and not 
to hamper innovation, and last but not least the fact that all relevant operators in the value chain (from 
design to end-of-life) agree on those criteria.       
A scoring on recyclability should also be set up to help consumers to choose the better product. 
France has been working for several months on a criteria assessment tool that is currently being tested 
by several large French companies in real operating conditions. FNADE would be happy to  share the 
first results of this study with members of the European Commission and the Parliament. 
Finally, to ensure the monitoring of the packaging recyclability, FNADE would propose that the 
manufacturers self-declaration of conformity shall be replaced  by a third-party certification. In order 
to facilitate the reporting process , this certification could be a “grouping” certification for similar 
packaging placed on the market by several manufacturers (e.g. one single certification for all clear 
mono-PET trays without labels). 
 
Minimum recycled content in plastic packaging  
 
FNADE welcomes the introduction of mandatory minimum recycled content targets. Indeed, since the 
implementation of this measure for single -use plastic bottles, rPET has become the on ly recycled 
plastic whose price is  disconnected from virgin raw materials, creating a sustainable market for this 
material. In this respect, FNADE fully supports the 2030 targets for contact sensitive packaging other 
than PET which takes into account operational realities and also allows to maintain the R&D and secure 
the outlets for the recycling of these materials.  
FNADE would also suggest extending the minimum recycled contents to other materials such as 
paper/cardboard and metals. Indeed, even though these packaging already have good recycling rates 
in Europe, they still have a great recycling potential, and such a mandatory measure would help to 
boost secondary raw material markets in Europe. To do so, FNADE would propose to reduce the 8-year 
period currently contained in the Commission’s draft ("8 years after the date of entry into force of this 
Regulation, the Commission shall review the situation regarding the use of recycled packaging 
materials in packaging other than plastics ") to a 2-years period. Indeed, this 8 -year period would be 
definitely too long to have a real impact on these other materials.

Information on the packaging /QR code  
 
FNADE fully supports the obligation to provide technical information on the packaging. This is really a 
major step that will empower citizens in their purchasing act. But it could also imply further 
development regarding collection , sorting, recycling an d why not deposit refund system s but also 
improve traceability. Therefore FNADE is of the opinion that this information should be attached and 
specific to each individual packaging as it is technically possible today to produce a unique QR code 
per packaging (e.g. on medicine packaging under Directive 2001/83/EC).  
A unique identifier would make it possible to know whether a packaging has been correctly collected, 
sorted and recovered. This knowledge would enable recyclers to increase packaging recycling and to 
adopt further targeted measures according to data collected through these QR codes on the life cycle 
of the packaging. This QR code should be affixed directly on the product so that it cannot be dissociated 
from the packaging. It could also allow the implementation of a digital deposit refund system for plastic 
bottles and cans, as proposed by the Commission. 
 
Compostable packaging  
 
FNADE welcome s the Commission’s position as set out in its Communication on biobased, 
biodegradable and compostable plastics. Indeed, it greatly clarifies the framework for the use of those 
plastics in the EU and is similar to the current French regulation. Overall, th e development of these 
materials can only be considered if their properties allow them to be efficiently recovered with current 
composting technologies and if they do not represent a potential contamination for conventional 
plastics intended for mechanical  recycling. FNADE therefore supports the Commission’s proposal to 
limit the use of compostable plastics to some packaging, in order to ensure efficiency and high quality 
of secondary raw materials. 
However, FNADE regrets that the Commission has n ot clearly expressed in its Communication that 
plastics should be first recyclable in order to stay in the material loop as long as possible. 
In the same vein, FNADE’s members regret that the proposal for a regulation, even though it sets a 
new definition of “compostable packaging”, does not require in its Article 8 that compostable 
packaging should never jeopardize the production of high quality compost or induce any negative 
impact on soils. 
 
Mandatory collection targets 
FNADE would like to recall  that for achieving high recycling targets and thus more circularity, high 
collection targets are absolutely needed. Waste collection and in particular waste packaging collection 
is clearly the starting point of recycling. What is most missing on the Commission’s proposal is probably 
mandatory collection targets.  
 
Deposit Return Systems  
 
The implementation of a deposit return system (DRS) for the collection of single-use plastic bottles and 
single-use metal containers has proven its efficiency in some Member States. However, FNADE would 
oppose the obligation of implementation of  a deposit return system for the collection of packaging. 
Indeed, we assume DRS should not be the one-size-fits-all solution to increase the collection rate.

The collection efforts must focus on all packaging put on the market and not only on single-use plastic 
bottles and single -use metal containers if we want to reduce waste in the environment. Therefore, 
FNADE would propose collection targets for recycling and minimum recycled content for all packaging 
rather than implementing a deposit return system. Furthermore, France identified that DRS is 
definitely not the only path to i mprove packaging collection : among other , the introduction of 
incentive pricing for selec tive collection, the development  and generali sation of out -of-home 
collection, or the sorting of bio-waste at source are powerful levers for improving the collection of all 
packaging. 
If a deposit return system were to be implemented, FNADE, as a waste man agement professional, 
would be willing to share expertise  to make the system as relevant as possible. This system should 
therefore be fully integrated into the public waste management services  (service of general interest) 
of the Member States without destabilising them unnecessarily, and the materials sorted for recycling 
should imperatively remain in a free and open market. 
Therefore, the system operator of DRS should not be an independent, non-profit legal entity because 
it could disrupt the public waste management services of the Member States. FNADE advocates a high 
degree of flexibility for  each Member State to  organise its own system, whether conventional or 
"smart/digital", based on an impact assessment at national level, and by eva luating how to improve 
existing collection systems. DRS, if necessary, can be adapted to the local context and be a non -
disruptive complementary collection system for packaging. To that extent, all stakeholders of the value 
chain should be involved in th e governance and the decision making process  of this system. 
Downstream of the collection, requirements on material sales procedures must guarantee fair access 
for recyclers and transfer of ownership without any “right of first refusal” given to specific industries 
for the material output of the vending machines.  
As each Member State has its local specificities, each Member State should be able to set up the 
system that brings the best result.  
 
Reusable packaging 
 
FNADE approves the reuse targets included in the proposal for a regulation as it allows to keep the 
material in the loop. However, FNADE would like to point out that high reuse targets may prevent the 
achievement of recycling targets. Indeed, the regulat ion must ensure industrial coherence and 
compatibility between its different targets. Reuse and refill targets are both necessary but, if too 
ambitious, they may involve technical and economic choices that are not compatible with industrial 
applications, for both manufacturers and recyclers.  
FNADE also wishes to emphasise two main concerns: 
- At this stage, no study has proven that reuse has always a better environmental impact than 
recycling. In some situation, recycling may be more efficient than reuse.  
- As the PET bottle is one of the most eco -designed and recycled packaging, FNADE does not 
really understand the willingness of the proposal to propose high reuse targets on this material 
(non-alcoholic beverages packaging). It would be more interesting to focus on efforts to reuse      
packaging whose recycling performance is currently poor.

Circular packaging 
 
To highlight and encourage the achievement of significant eco -design and recycling performance on 
all types of packaging, FNADE proposes to introduce a definition of “circular packaging”.  
The circular packaging will meet these three requirements: 
- An A or B recyclability performance grade as defined by Article 6 and Annex II; 
- An overall collection rate of 90% or above;  
- A recycled content of 50% minimum or 20% above the minimum recycled content set out in 
Article 7, the highest of the two criteria being applied. 
The circular packaging, which meets demanding criteria in terms of sorting, collection and recycling, 
and whose percentage of recycled content exceeds the targets of the regulation, should benefit from 
exemptions from the reduction objectives.

Comment 
on the European Packaging Regulation 
 
 
 
Ref. Ares(2023)2834570 - 21/04/2023
 
 
 
 @VDA_online 
 Verband der Automobilindustrie 
The German Association of the Automotive Industry (VDA) consolidates about 650 manufac-
turers and suppliers under one roof. The members develop and produce cars and trucks, 
software, trailers, superstructures, buses, parts and accessories as well as new m obility of-
fers. We represent the interests of the automotive industry and stand for modern, future-ori-
ented multimodal mobility on the way to climate neutrality. The VDA represents the interests 
of its members in politics, the media, and social groups. We work for electric mobility, climate-
neutral drives, the implementation of climate targets, securing raw materials, digitization and 
networking as well as German engineering. We are committed to a competitive business and 
innovation location. Our industry ensures prosperity in Germany: More than 780,000 people 
are directly employed in the German automotive industry. 
 
The German Automotive Industry welcomes the initiative of the European Commission to har-
monize the legislation on packaging and packaging waste in Europe. Particularly the harmo-
nizing of consumer waste sorting instructions is an important step to achieve a boost of recy-
cling without impeding cross-border trade within the EU Internal Market. 
 
Nevertheless, the German Automotive Industry recommend adapting the current proposal re-
garding the following aspects: 
 
1. Definitions of packaging and manufacturer versus producer (Ar-
ticle 3) 
The definitions of (6) “making available on the market” and (7) “placing on the market”  as 
well as the use of these terms in the definitions of (9) “manufacturer”, (10) “producer” and i n 
Art. 13 are contradictory. 
 
To prevent misinterpretation, the VDA proposes to further clarify the definitions of manufac-
turer and producer as physical manufacturer and first distributer of packaging with their differ-
ent obligations. The term “has packaging designed or manufactured and uses that packaging 
[…] under its own name” (Art.3 (9)), should belong to the definition of producer.  
E.g., ordering a packaging with the own trademark cannot lead to full manufacturer obliga-
tions like e.g., conformity. 
2. Minimum recycled content in plastic packaging (Article 7) 
The recycling content targets are very ambitious, especially because of the restriction to post -
consumer plastic waste. Furthermore, there could be a conflict with other upcoming regula-
tory requirements, e.g. the review of the End of Life vehicles legislation where targets for the 
minimum recycling content in plastic products are proposed. The Commission proposal 
leaves the possibility in Art. 7 point 10 to adjust the minimum percentages e.g. in case of lack 
of availability or excessive prices. We would like to point out that the small packaging manu-
facturers will have a disadvantage with respect to access to such material already long before 
such lack of material/excessive prices will be corrected by adjustments of the Commission in 
a delegated act. We suggest to reconsider the targets. 
3. Compostable packaging (Article 8) 
There is an urgent need to establish and implement a standardized/harmonized recycling 
process for biobased, biodegradable, and compostable plastics. This must be comparable to 
(ideally integrated into) the existing recycling process of conventional plastics. If this is not 
done, these raw materials are added to the thermal exploitation.

4. Requirement of minimization of packaging (Article 9) 
The selection of packaging according to the size of the content and the associated obligation 
to reduce empty contents in packaging is reasonable. However, this will limit the flexibility to 
use the same packaging for different products. 
 
The huge variety of products/spare parts in the Automotive Industry sometimes demands the 
use of standardized packaging in order to prevent a great amount of investments into differ-
ent tools and print clichés on the side of the packaging manufacturer. Taking this into ac-
count, a further complexity increase in the supply chain would be counterproductive for the 
intended environmental benefit. 
5. Labelling of packaging (Article 11) 
The proposed pairing of the labelling of the packaging with the labels on the waste recepta-
cles (see also recitals 44 and 45) combined with repealing Commission Decision 97/129/EC 
(recital (50)) is a very efficient approach.  
 
In order to prevent misinterpretation we suggest to use the same term in article 11 (1) (“label 
containing information on its material composition”) and article 12 (“labels that enables the 
separate collection of each material specific fraction”) in order to differentiate from “materi al 
composition” in Art. 11 (6).  
 
Every additional label contradicts the environmental idea because of additional process steps 
using energy and increasing CO2 emissions. Furthermore, the soiling of the material has a 
negative impact on the quality of the reusable materials. We therefore propose to consider 
integrating the sorting instructions for the consumer in the QR code of the packaging or at 
least leave this option open for the future. Such a solution could be accompanied by public 
awareness campaigns on the separate collection of different packaging fractions.  
 
It must also be taken into account that there are packaging materials which, from a technical 
point of view, cannot be marked or which can only be marked individually and manually at a 
later stage (e.g., very small packages, shrink foils, etc.). 
 
Article 11 (4). The state of the art of technical methods of labelling is always under develop-
ment. As a result, it is necessary to avoid that methods of marking, such as “printing” or “en-
graving”, are determined individually. In addition,  other methods, such as needling, emboss-
ing, punching or lasering exist. The technical implementation of the labelling should not be 
limited due to individual operational and economic factors.   
 
Article 11 (6)/recital (50)c: Six different plastic groups are classified in accordance with the 
current Directive 97/129/EC. However, plastic alternatives, such as biobased, biodegradable 
and compostable plastics, cannot be clearly assigned to a group. The group "07-Other Plas-
tics" are used as is customary in the industry for the marking of other plastics (PA, PUR, PC). 
However, this group has no European legal basis. The unambiguous labelling of biobased, 
biodegradable and compostable plastics and other plastics is recommended. Otherwise, they 
cannot be added to the recycling stream and are instead ending up in energy recovery. 
 
Furthermore, packaging for spare parts is usually produced on a parts-specific basis and 
stocked for up to 10-15 years. The planned transition periods are not sufficient to eliminate 
old stocks. Premature destruction before the packaging is used would not be resource effi-
cient. The existing parts label can be used to prove when the part was packaged and that the 
packaging was already in stock before the regulation came into force. In order to ensure the

optimal use of old stocks, these should remain unaffected by the EU regulation if they were 
already in the stock when the regulation came into force. 
 
We would also like to stress the point that Article 4 point 4 and 5 is leaving an option for an 
additional labelling for identifying the responsible producer responsibility scheme or certain 
deposit and return systems. Such an additional label would lead to a proliferation of disparate 
national labels with subsequent confusion of the costumer and would clearly contradict the 
overarching goal of this Regulation to harmonize the labelling. Therefore, we propose to de-
lete this option.  
 
Another important aspect would be to leave the possibility to coordinate the labelling with the 
QR Code with other important trading partners (e.g., USA/Asia). 
6. Obligations of manufacturers (Article 13) 
In order to standardize and simplify labelling, multiple QR codes or other digital data carrier 
codes on the packaging should be avoided. It is recommended to consolidate the information 
(material composition, manufacturer information, packaging type number) in a uniform data 
carrier code standard. 
7. Instant implementation of re-use targets for transport packaging 
(Article 26) 
Transportation between company sites and every national transport between economic oper-
ators is affected 100% without transition period. It is not possible to reach this objective be-
cause of the complex network for transportation of goods.  
Due to process optimization in the transport sector, reusable packaging is not always prefera-
ble and more environmentally friendly compared to non-reusable packaging. Distance, 
transport mode and weight should also be considered. 
8. Documentation on packaging, suppliers and manufacturers and 
EU declaration of conformity (Articles 14, 20, 34) 
In order to avoid misinterpretations, the issue of the technical documentation and the proof of 
conformity of the packaging must be issued in English and optionally in the national lan-
guage. 
9. Multi-supplier strategy (Article 20) 
The procurement of packaging materials can be carried out in different ways.  
▪ Scenario 1: The packaging material is procured and sourced from one supplier. The 
identification of the supplier is clearly assigned via the type number of the packaging.  
▪ Scenario 2: The packaging material is procured and sourced from multiple suppliers 
at different shares. The identification of the suppliers cannot be clearly assigned via 
the type number of the packaging.  
Scenario 2 is not described in Article 20 and therefore must be taken into account in order to 
ensure the free market economy and competitiveness. 
10. Obligation related to excessive packaging (Article 21) 
The mandatory requirement is problematic for special product ranges (example: hazardous 
goods). There, more protective means and thus a higher proportion of empty space are often

required to comply with the dangerous goods regulations. Today, there are no quotas that 
have to be met, and in our view, this is not necessary, as it is in the interest of the economic 
operator to keep the proportion of empty space as low as possible. If a quota is retained, it 
should in any case be included as an exception if compliance is technically or legally not fea-
sible. 
11. EU declaration of conformity (Article 34) 
Currently, EU declarations of conformity for packaging are not in use or under development. 
Regarding the goal of simplifying processes, this increases the administrative burden and 
complexity. Furthermore, the packaging manufacturer/supplier must be responsible to control 
the manufacturing process independently depending on the individual economic situation.  
12. Register of producers and extended producer responsibility 
(Articles 39 & 40) 
Articles 39.2 and 40.2 should be further specified as regards the words 'each Member State'. 
Corporate companies are active across several national borders. For the application of the 
packaging in the corresponding regions, it is recommended that the individual national com-
panies of the company carry out the registration independently. This could be solved by 
proper definitions in article 3 of (6) “making available on the market”, (7) “placing on the mar-
ket”, (9) “manufacturer”, (10) “producer” (see our comment nr 1)). 
13. Member States can make Producer Responsibility Organiza-
tion (PRO) mandatory (Art. 41 point 1) 
The Automotive Industry clearly supports the current approach of the Waste Framework Di-
rective which is giving a producer the choice to organize his waste management indivi dually 
or collectively in form of a PRO (see recital (14) of Directive 2018/851/EC). A mandatory PRO 
would be an intervention in the free play of the market forces, that should only be possible 
under very restrictive conditions with a very good justification. 
14. Exemption for packaging produced before enactment of the 
Regulation 
The Automotive Industry is obliged to ensure availability of spare parts for a long period of 
time (e.g. >15 years). These spare parts are stored in packaging produced before the enact-
ment of the Regulation (e. g. all-time stockpiling of products). Therefore, the packaging has 
already been produced and fitted to protect the products. Subsequent removal of packaging 
materials can cause damage to sensitive products when they are unpacked and repacked in 
order to implement subsequent packaging requirements. In addition, it would be a waste of 
material without any further benefit for the environment. Also, the information on minimum re-
cycled content may not be available for the packaging used at the time when the packaging 
has been stored. Therefore, we ask the Commission to take this into account by introducing 
exemptions for such packaging, analogous to the so called “repair as produced” principle for 
legacy spare parts. 
15. Harmonization of other waste sorting instructions 
The harmonization of sorting instructions of packaging waste is the only way to improve recy-
cling and at the same time allow free circulation of packaged goods in the Union. However,

the ultimate goal should remain the harmonization of all separate waste c ollection/labelling 
across the EU. France e.g., is introducing waste sorting instructions for 22 product groups – 
even for EEE and batteries, despite the EU-wide obligatory label with the crossed-out 
wheeled bin. Other national legislators will follow, which would lead to enormous challenges 
to the free circulation of goods within the EU Internal Market. 
Furthermore, Labelling of packaging shall also be harmonized with other leading regions (CN, 
US, JP) for symbols and content, to set global standards and avoidance of multiple labelling 
and customer confusion.

Freshfel Europe response to draft Proposal for a Regulation on packaging and packaging waste, 
amending Regulation (EU) 2019/1020 and Directive (EU) 2019/904, and repealing Directive 94/62/EC 
 
Freshfel Europe supports the ambition to increase the sustainability of packaging through enhanced 
reusability and recyclability of packaging as well as the commitment to tackle over -packaging, plastic 
packaging and waste generation, which is also refle cted in the Circular Economy Action Plan. EU 
packaging ambitions must be matched with harmonised EU rules, especially from an operational and 
economic perspective.  
While much effort has been made by the EU fresh fruit and vegetable sector to address packa ging 
through innovative solutions of packaging designs, materials and reductions in overpackaging, there 
remains room to further boost the sustainability and harmonisation for the sector in this area. Freshfel 
Europe therefore supports the European Commiss ion’s proposal for a new Regulation on packaging 
and packaging waste, amending Regulation (EU) 2019/1020 and Directive (EU) 2019/904, and 
repealing Directive 94/62/EC.   
While this initiative is necessary to move towards sustainable packaging solutions in all sectors, and 
to guarantee a cost -efficient levelled playing field, Freshfel Europe is highly concerned about the 
proposal’s discrimination of the fruits and vegetable sector of Article 22 Annex V, point 2, where no  
 
Summary:  
• Freshfel Europe, the European fresh produce association, supports t he efforts to 
increase sustainable packaging solutions and the harmonisation the proposed regulation 
would bring to the packaging market in Europe. 
• Considering the aim of the proposal is to limit packaging and packaging waste  in all 
industries, the fres h produce sector is highly concerned about the unfounded, 
discriminative targeting of fresh produce in Article 22, Annex V, point 2.  
• The fresh produce sector is already making substantial efforts to move away from 
unnecessary packaging in a safe manner. Almost half of fresh fruits and vegetables are 
already sold loose, and packaging for fresh produce only accounts for around 1,5% of all 
plastic packaging used for food in Member States. For paper and cardboard, the ratio is 
estimated to be similar.  
• Freshfel Europe warns against the unintended consequences a ban on packaging would 
bring in terms of food safety, quality, information transparency and food waste 
prevention. A ban on packagin g would also have detrimental effects on consumption 
and providing consumer information.  
• The proposal risks duplicating Member State efforts already undertaken under the 
Single-Use Plastics Directive.  
• Freshfel Europe calls on the European institutions to reconsider the discriminative ban 
on packaging for fresh produce, and allow the sector to continue its pursuit of best 
practices and joint commitments to phase out the use of excessive packaging in line with 
sustainability ambitions and societal concerns.

Packaging for fresh produce constitutes only around 1,5% of all plastic packaging put on the market

Freshfel Europe believes that under the Regulation no sector should be singled out and be subject to 
additional bans, as the purpose of the proposal remains to reduce packaging in all industries and uses.  
Moreover, Freshfel Europe emphasizes the need for full and detailed analyses of the impact of the 
proposed changes to packaging legislation, which will have both direct and indirect im plications for 
the fresh fruit and vegetable sector and could lead to unintended consequences detrimental to the 
sector’s sustainability efforts and fresh produce consumption.  
Today, a majority of European citizens are consuming well below the WHO recommendation of 400 g 
per person per day. This contributes to increased obesity and risks for non -communicable diseases, 
having serious effects on human health and on European health care systems. It is vital to ensure 
accessibility and convenience of fresh produce, and to guarantee the quality and freshness of the 
products from “farm to fork”.  
Bans within the scope of this proposal should only be justified by objectives related to packaging and 
packaging waste reduction and should not target a sector where it will not only have a limited impact 
on the total use of packaging in Europe, and where packaging is highly functional and has high added 
value for the perishable products they contain both throughout the supply chain and in the home. The 
use of packaging means a cost for operators. These added costs are weighed against their added value 
for quality, marketing opportunities and consumer convenience. The sector is continuously striving to 
reduce the use of unnecessary materials without putting food safety or consumption at risk.  Freshfel 
Europe reiterates some of the numerous essential functionalities of packaging in the fresh  fruit and 
vegetable sector, namely: 
• Product containment : Fresh fruit and v egetable products are inherently small -sized loose 
products, with some being very small multi -item products. For effective and efficient 
operations and logistics throughout the chain packaging products are used to contain these 
small-sized loose products i n large (logistics, including national, intra -EU and international 
trade) and small (consumer) quantities. This extends for example from multi -use (wooden or 
plastic) pallox-boxes, open pool (plastic crates), standardized plastic and paper-based boxes

and palettes used in operations, to retail packaging including food containers for the sale of 
small multi-item fruit and vegetables sold by weight such as berries, small -sized apples and  
cherry tomatoes and packets for the sale of fresh cut salad. This is both more sustainable and 
cost efficient. 
 
• Extend product shelf life : Fresh fruit and vegetables are highly perishable and during each 
stage of the supply chain from production to consumption must be protected so that they 
arrive at the consumer in good condi tion as per marketing standards. In this way , packaging 
extends the shelf life of products by limiting minor damage to products that eventually may 
increase product perishability. Furthermore, many packaging types used in the sector include 
innovations to specifically extend the shelf life of products by maintaining product freshness, 
such as by limiting drying-out and/or improving in-package atmosphere conditions, e.g. sealed 
cucumbers or broccoli. Such packaging types are essential for the supply of products travelling 
longer distances intra-EU, and to ensure the good functioning of the Single Market.  
 
• Prevent food spoilage and waste: By extending the shelf life of products, protection provided 
by packaging used across the fresh fruit and vegetable supply chain prevents food waste by 
limiting product damage and maintaining freshness at each stage of the chain and in the 
home. For example, berries must be contained in packaging containers as spoilage rates are 
extremely high when sold loose.  
 
• Prevent microbiological contamination & maintain hygiene: Fresh produce packaging can be 
used throughout the supply chain to prevent microbiological contamination of products and 
maintain hygiene. Packaging preve nts fresh fruit and vegetable products from contact with 
other surfaces, which may be the cause and/or facilitate microbiological contamination. 
 
• Product information display: As inherently non-packaged and non-branded products with no 
product information, packaging offers the sector possibilities to communicate to both supply 
chain actors and consumers with obligatory and essential product information. This 
information is offered via a large variety of packaging from paper -based palettes, to 
containers, wra ppers and small labels. Legally obligatory information includes but is not 
limited to variety, traceability, country of origin, organic distinction from conventional 
products and fraud detection. Essential information for the benefit of trade and consumers  
includes among others colour, branding, PLU codes, barcodes, quality, storage and/or 
preparation messages (for example ‘ready to eat’). More generally, packaging remains a 
medium for informing consumers about progress and product segmentation initiatives,  
whether they are official signs of for example quality and origin (PDO/PGI/ST) or collective and 
private quality approaches.  
 
• Consumption promotion:  As a non -branded product packaging facilitates consumption 
promotion for fresh fruit and vegetables. In the EU the average daily consumption of fruit and 
vegetables per capita is 365g per day, which is well below the minimum 400g recommended 
by the WHO fo r good health. In a food market with an extremely large array of branded 
products, packaging offers an opportunity for consumption promotion through text and 
graphics.

• Consumption facilitation: Actors in the European fresh produce sector along with Membe r 
States and the European Commission are continuing efforts to achieve increased at-home and 
out-of-home consumption of fruit and vegetables to stimulate overall consumption above the 
WHO recommended level. This includes increasing the availability of a di verse range of 
products and varied portion sizes for consumers to suit their changing lifestyles. Packaging 
facilitates these efforts by providing different mediums of fruit and vegetable consumption to 
suit different demographics’ lifestyle and consumptio n patterns, which are continually 
evolving. For example this includes convenience packaging for those wanting to eat ‘on -the-
go’ and meal kits with fruit and vegetable products for 1-2 portions of a recipe for those living 
alone and/or those looking to learn how to cook with fresh produce.  
 
• Consumer confidence:  Packaging gives confidence to consumers in guaranteeing both 
product safety and quality. Particularly during health crises, such as the recent COVID -19 
pandemic, packaging provides food safety, including hygiene control, and quality certainty to 
consumers, which facilitates continued consumption levels vital for the good health of 
citizens. 
In light of the above-mentioned functionalities of fresh produce packaging, Freshfel Europe warns the 
European Commission that Article 22 Annex V point 2 may lead to severe direct and indirect negative 
consequences regarding food safety and quality, food waste, unfair competition on the Single Market 
and labelling requirements among others. Freshfel Europe therefor e wishes to draw your attention 
to:  
• The direct and indirect negative consequences on food safety and quality  that arise from a 
complete ban on single -use packaging of fresh fruits and vegetables as stipulated by Article 
22, Annex V, point 2. In effect, this will result in decreased consumption, putting the health of 
European citizens at risk and increasing financial costs and workload of healthcare institutions 
and social welfare systems.  
 
o Being a perishable and sensitive product, fresh produce runs a heightened risk of 
physical damage and microbiological contamination  when deprived of the 
functionalities of packaging. Packaging protects the product throughout the supply 
chain and prevents visible and non-visible damages that can be both off-putting and, 
more importantly, dangerous for consumers. In combination with the limitation of 
labelling and displaying of in formation deriving from the lack of packaging, this will 
have a negative effect on consumer confidence  and lead to decreased consumption, 
having detrimental effects on human health.  
  
o Removal of packaging increases the risk not only of micro-biological contamination 
but also of cross -contamination between products . This will be most noticeable 
regarding the possible cross -contamination between organic and conventional 
products, causing non-compliance and disturbance for the EU Organic Action Plan.  
 
o Packaging contributes to increased fresh produce shelf-life. Removing packaging 
decreases the life span of fresh produce which will mainly affect the last stages of 
the supply chain, namely in supermarkets and households. Indeed, this is where the

EU Platform on Food Losses and Food Waste through the  EU Food Loss and Waste 
Prevention Hub ha s already identified the largest amounts of food waste occurring,

• The lack of proper impact assessment to legitimise the proposed ban on single-use packaging 
for fresh fruit and vegetables, resulting in consequences conflicting with EU ambitions of the 
EU Green Deal and the Farm to Fork Strategy: 
 
o Having no packaging would result in increased food waste. As mentioned above, a 
removal of packaging on fresh fruit and vegetables would increase food waste 
throughout the supply chain and serve counterproductive to EU actions against food 
waste. It also contradicts the Recommendations for Action in Food Waste Prevention 
of 2019 by the EU Platform on Food Losses and Food Waste which stipulates that one 
of the main areas of opportunity to prevent and reduce food waste is “/…/finding 
innovative packaging solutions, offering a diversity of portion sizes, raising consumer 
awareness on date marking /…/”. All these examples require the presence of 
packaging. The Recommendations also mention  the responsibility of food 
manufacturers to “take full account of critical role of packaging ensuring food quality, 
safety and preventing food waste” and to seek packaging solutions that allow shelf -
life extension. A ban on packaging would impair these efforts.  
 
o Removing packaging interferes with legal labelling requirements  and creates unfair 
competitive advantages amongst products  resulting in unfair competition on the 
Single Market . Removal of packaging means removing accessibility to product 
information to consumers which puts competitiveness at risk. First of all, it interferes 
with legal labelling requirements of traceability , origin labelling, distinction between 
organic and conventional products, and storage and preparation messages,  among 
many others . Second, voluntary labelling on susta inability and nutritional value, 
where fruits and vegetables have amongst the best records, will be made impossible 
to convey to the consumers, thus affecting their empowerment to make informed 
choices.  Moreover, by allowing for product exemptions as declared in Annex V, point 
2, some  products might be favoured above others and gain unfair competitive 
advantages. It would cause a distortion on the Single Market and create an unlevel 
playing field. This in turn could lead to a production shift towards produ cts that are 
allowed to be packed which would limit the selection of fresh fruits and vegetables 
enjoyed in the EU today. 
The fresh produce sector has proven commitment to its strive for sustainable solutions and 
environmental efforts, continuously balanci ng sector needs with sustainability ambitions and 
consumer safety. In this spirit, Freshfel Europe urges decision-makers to take the following points into 
consideration: 
• Reconsider the explicit ban of Article 22, Annex V, point 2 on single-use packaging of fresh 
fruits and vegetables, acknowledging the limited impact and wider negative implications of 
such a ban. A complete ban on the packaging of fresh produce would not only put food safety

the EU would mor eover be limited. Firstly  because of the aforementioned small percentage

• Focus on infrastructure for recyclability and waste management to decrease packaging 
waste and make it easier for consumers to engage with proper waste management. Since fruit 
and vegetable packaging rarely is the cause of littering but more often is consumed in the 
home, recyclability and well -functioning waste management structures are key to reducing 
packaging waste from the fresh produce sector. 
 
• Promote innovation and research on sustainable and efficient packaging solutions  and 
materials, for instance coatings . There is a lack of policy coherence, as work on packaging 
alternatives is slow -moving. Encouraging development in sustainable packaging solutions,  
would ensure minimal packaging waste without putting food safety, quality, or production at 
risk.     
 
• To identify relevant areas for packaging and packaging waste reduction for the fresh produce 
sector, without putting food safety, human health and environmental sustainability at risk, 
further impact assessments must be undertaken . Such impact assessments  must consider 
food security and food waste , implications on labelling, traceability , consumer information  
and consumption, as well as clarify how this proposal aligns with the ambitions of the Green 
New Deal, the Farm to Fork, or the EU Food Waste Reduction targets.  
 
• Any interference in the allowed use of packaging materials and packaging formats must be 
developed by the sector that possesses the operational knowledge. Sector commitments 
must be  based on operative expertise and implementation realities to  avoid any ban or 
derogations becoming bureaucratic decisions. A gradual withdrawal of packaging of fruit and 
vegetables to comply with sustainability efforts and societal concerns must be based on joint 
commitments and sharing of best practices . Such actions are already underway through 
various initiatives, and already today around 50% of fresh fruits and vegetables in Europe are 
sold loose . To guarantee a level playing field on the Single Market, there must be no 
restrictions until a sufficient timeline, including on any list of exemptions, has been 
established. Should a complete packaging ban on fresh fruit and vegetables  be established 
without any indication on what products would be exempted or when, this will result in a 
period when some fres h fruit and vegetable products will not be able to be sold on the EU 
market.

Freshfel Europe welcomes Article 8 (1) on the compostability of sticky labels on fresh fruit and 
vegetables and the harmonisation of fruit and vegetable stickers it brings. Over the past three years, 
the fresh produce sector has worked intensively on compostable solutions for  fruit and vegetable 
labels to provide viable industrially and home compostable label solutions for all products. This 
extensive effort has been challenging, with many products such as those with long-transit times, such 
as imported produce from third countries, and high exterior moisture content still proving difficult to 
label with compostable products. In these instances, labels begin the degradation process or fall of 
the product before it reaches the point of sale.  
Freshfel Europe is concerned that Article 8( 1) lacks labelling quality specification s. No specific 
standard is mentioned in Article 8( 1) for industrial compostability, such as EN13432. Additionally, 
Article 8(1) does not mention any requirement for certification. Freshfel Europe strongly recommends 
that labelling quality specification is included in Article 8( 1) to ensure verification of labelling quality. 
Furthermore, industrial compost able requirements in the proposed Regulation for fresh produce 
labels should be clear that the product’s construction must meet the compost standard and not just 
the product components individually. For example, in the case of fresh produce labels the 
construction, namely the face stock, adhesive and ink, should meet the compost standard and not just 
these components on an individual basis. Freshfel Europe is happy to assist the Commission in finding 
favourable solutions to this.  
Freshfel Europe requests that Article 16(1) on the obligations of importers is given further operational 
clarity regarding the non-inclusion of the provisions of Article 22, Annex V point 2. The fresh fruit and 
vegetable sector, especially for produc t stock management planning and packaging stock managing, 
operates in quite advance planning and preparation to ensure full compliance. Imported fresh fruits 
and vegetables into the EU are often pre -packed at the origin to guarantee that the products meet 
the strict quality specifications an d to maintain maximum quality and freshness for EU consumers 
while preventing food waste even after long transit times of several weeks. Some examples include 
table grapes and kiwifruit. By having different restrictions and allowances for importers of fresh fruit

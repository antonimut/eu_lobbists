Cummins feedback on the proposed Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation. 
 
Cummins is working towards our PLANET 2050 goal of using natural resources 
sustainably. By 2030, we have committed to generating 25% less waste as a percentage 
of revenue in facilities and operations. We also aim to reuse or responsibly recycle 
100% of packaging plastics and eliminate single-use plastics in our dining facilities, 
employee amenities and events. Cummins will continue to explore opportunities to 
optimize and reduce packaging waste, increase the use of reusable packaging, and 
eliminate processes that needlessly generate packaging waste.  
 
Cummins broadly supports the review of the Packaging and Packaging Waste Directive 
and in particular the effort to harmonize requirements such as EPR and labelling.  
 
Harmonized approach to EPR and labelling 
• Cummins welcomes the harmonised approach to EPR across all members 
states as this should reduce variability and decrease complexity. 
• We encourage labelling via a QR code or a website as this allows for timely 
packaging updates across Member States. Cummins has a global supply chain 
where goods are shipped across the world. Labels from other regions may 
make it into Europe, therefore there should be allowances for professional 
packaging to prevent the repacking of goods leading to unnecessary 
environmental impact and packaging waste. This also includes the ask of 
Member States enforcing different labelling including that of the EPR schemes. 
The possibility of each Member State enforcing different labelling systems will 
cause fragmentation, a barrier to free movement of goods and will not be 
feasible.  
Packaging reuse 
• Cummins has and will continue to support the reuse of items such as 
pallets and crates. The tracking of reusables can be facilitated with the use 
of technology. However, with stretch wrap and wooden pallets this is likely 
to be challenging, as when stretch wrap is removed from the pallet it is 
often cut meaning it is unviable to reuse.  As for pallet reuse given that we 
do not use tracking technologies on wooden pallets tracking will be difficult, 
if the expectation is to track the materials with accuracy.  
• The ask of using only reusable transport packaging for economic operators 
delivering products to another economic operator within the same Member 
State may not always be feasible. Whilst we agree with the idea in principle 
there are times when this is detrimental to the environment or not 
economically viable.

Design for recycling and waste infrastructure 
• Packaging can be designed for recycling, in theory, but the current 
inadequate waste and recycling infrastructure needs investment to ensure 
packaging can be recycled in practice. The proposed measures will not 
tackle this issue in its entirety. Investment in the waste and recycling 
infrastructure across each Member State must happen to facilitate 
development and allow recycled content goals to be met.  
• Design for Recycling guidelines must be developed with voices throughout 
the value chain and cross industry representation. To avoid confusion, 
guidelines and assessment criteria must be clearly communicated and 
standardized across all Member States. Clarity is needed on recyclability 
assessments and how to provide evidence of packaging material 
recyclability across Europe. 
 
Packaging innovation 
• Going forward there must also be room for packaging innovation. Packaging 
materials must be selected based on holistic criteria including the ability to 
protect and contain the product, ensure safe and effective transportation as 
well as environmental impacts. In addition, environmental impacts must not 
only include recyclability or recycled content but also consider the overall life 
cycle of the packaging.

COMMENTS ON PACKAGING AND PACKAGING WASTE REGULATION PROPOSAL 
ASSOMET, the Italian non Ferrous Metals Industries Association 
 
Key messages 
 Assomet welcomes the objectives of the proposal for a Regulation on Packaging and Packaging 
Waste (PPWR) of increasing the sustainability and circularity of  packing products. As a permanent 
material, aluminium may be a key contributor to these objectives  based on the material 
properties and the excellent results achieved in recycling and waste reduction in the EU and in 
particular in Italy. 
 We call on the Commission to duly value the materials’ properties when defining the policy on 
packaging. The use of packaging made by permanent material  and effectively collected and 
recycled, such as those in aluminium , should be allowed and encouraged . In Italy , the aluminium 
packaging’s recycling targets set for 2030 have extensively been overcome. 
 The mandatory requirements on compostability for coffee and tea capsules (a rt. 8 , par.1) would 
prevent the usage of aluminium for the production of coffee and tea single serve units, with severe 
impacts on the market without a real benefit for the environment . The aluminium capsules are 
100% recyclable and designed to optimize and reduce the waste related to the packaging material 
and its content, preserving the quality of the beverage for a long time. Therefore, we do ask to 
maintain the neutrality of the choice of material for the production of beverage capsules and the 
neutrality of choice of the end-of-life solutions (recyclability or compostability). 
 A general ban for single use applications is a very severe form of market restriction  and need to 
be carefully assessed on a product -by-product case considering the properties of the materials, 
their recy cling rates and the potential negative impacts that a ban could have on food waste, 
consumer behaviour, food safety, hygiene and the environment.  Therefore, we urge the 
Commission to delete article 22. 
 Assomet agrees to the need of reducing the generation o f packaging waste. However, we are 
extremely concerned that reuse targets, in particular the ones for food and beverages, lack of solid 
scientific analysis on the environmental impacts on a product -by-product level as well as impact on 
hygiene, food safety and food waste. We believe that the most balanced and suitable approach for 
optimizing the environmental sustainability of the  use of packaging is to give Member States the 
choice of the best solution between reuse and/or  recycling on a case by case basis . Therefore, we 
recommend deleting the Article 26 and, in particular, the reuse obligations envisaged, by 2030 
and 2040, for take-away packaging referred to in paragraph 3.

Introduction 
Assomet represents the Italian producers and processors of non -ferrous metals: aluminium, lead, copper, 
zinc, precious metals, etc 
Overall, the sector is made up of 1.000 companies employing 26.000 people with an annual turnover of €28 
billion euros. 
The aluminium foils production and aluminium packaging sector is very relevant among Assomet’s 
membership as well as in Italy as a whole. 
According to 2021 data, the aluminium packaging sector in Italy is constituted by 28 companies with a total 
turnover of €3.6 billion, with an output of 249.000 tons1. 
 
PPWR Proposal: general remarks 
Assomet agrees to  the objectives of the proposal for a Regulation on Packaging and Packaging Waste 
(PPWR) of increasing the sustainability and circularity of packing products. We fully support the objective to 
reducing the total environmental impacts of production and consumption of packaging and packaged goods 
and to reducing the waste production in line with the EU Green Deal goals. 
Consequently, the PPWR proposal aims to make the packaging more recyclable and to address the increase 
in packaging waste generation. It also aims to help Member States achieve recycling targets as agreed in 
the 2018 waste legislative package in a cost-effective way. 
As a permanent material, aluminium may be a key contributor to these objectives based on the material 
properties and the excellent results achieved in recycling and waste reduction  in the EU and in particular 
in Italy. 
 
 Sustainability and circularity of aluminium packaging 
Aluminium is the ideal solution for the production of sustainable packaging being 100% and infinitely 
recyclable whilst keeping all its characteristics as malleability, resistance to impacts and corrosion and its 
ability to guarantee a “barrier effect” that protects from light, air, humidity and bacteria.  
Secondary aluminium has a very high value and a consolidated and well-functioning market already in 
place2.  
Moreover, aluminium allows, in each recycling phase, an average energy saving capacity of 95% comparing 
to the use of primary metal.

In Italy, on average, more than 70% of aluminum packaging is recycled, going well beyond the EU targets of 
50% by 2025 and 60% by 2030. 
In particular, the aluminum can is the most recycled beverage container in the world and today in Italy the 
recycling rate of beverage cans alone is over 90% of the ones put in the market (compared to the European 
average rate of 76.1%3). 
In light of the above, Assomet calls on the Commission to duly value  the materials’ properties when 
defining the policy on packaging  and the advanced results already accomplished by the aluminium 
industry in the reduction of packaging waste and the circular economy. 
 Legal basis 
The PPWR has also the general objective to ensur ing a well -functioning internal market through fully 
harmonised rules on packaging while tackling negative impacts on environment and health from packaging 
and packaging waste. 
Assomet welcomes the opportunity to ensure a uniformed regulatory scenario throughout the EU allowing 
economic operators to achieve ambitious objectives of sustainability an d circularity at European level:  
promoting the free movement of goods within the single shared market, simplifying operational activities, 
thus giving medium and long -term visibility to economic operators and allowing them to invest in the 
development of new sustainable, recyclable and effectively recycled packaging solutions, taking advantage 
of economies of scale. 
In order to achieve t he harmonization between Member States, it is paramount  to make sure  that 
packaging is subject to the same labelling and information, for example in accordance with the 
alphanumeric coding provided for by Decision 129/97/EC, for which companies have already i nvested 
considerable economic resources. Any further national label ling should therefore remain only on a 
voluntary basis and should not affect the possibility of the free movement of the product (with its 
packaging) on the domestic market. 
A robust EU single market is key to preserving the free movement of packaging raw materials and packaged 
goods. Therefore, Assomet fully supports  the choice to maintain an internal market legal basis (Article 
114 TFEU) and to be preserved during the legislative process, paying attention not to penalize possible 
virtuous experiences  of each country . At the same time, the provisions that allow Member States to 
maintain or introduce further specific requirements at national level  (for example art. 4.4 and art. 45) 
should be erased from the text. Should an environmental legal basis be introduced according to Article 192 
TFEU (environmental protection)  the potential for harmonisation would be weakened by a patchwork of 
national packaging legislations, to the detriment of consumers, environmental protection and the 
competitiveness of the European industry.

 Definition of recycling 
It is important to refrain from only defining recycling as closed -loop recycling (i.e. product to  product 
recycling). It is fundamental to leave the possibility to use recycled material from aluminium  packaging in 
non-packaging applications and vice-versa. 
In Europe, about half of the aluminium produced originates from recycled materials. The market for  
recycled aluminium works and it is efficient due to its intrinsic high value. However, it is not feasible to only 
envisage closed -loop recycling for aluminium packaging and use only recycled content coming from  
aluminium packaging.  
To help fostering even more the market of aluminium recycled content, it is critical to support proper  
collection, sorting and recycling of used products. For this reason, it is important that all aluminium foil  
packaging is included in separate collection systems in all Member States. 
 
Comments on article 8 
Article 8 par. 1 provides that the following are included among the packaging for which the compostability 
requirements are mandatory: 
art.3 
f) tea or coffee bags necessary to contain a tea or coffee -based product and intended to be used and 
disposed of together with the product; 
g) single -serve unit of the coffee or tea system necessary to contain a coffee or tea -based product and 
intended to be used and disposed of together with the product; 
These provisions would prevent the usage of aluminium for the production of coffee and te a single serve 
units, with severe impacts on the market without a real benefit for the environment.  
As a matter of fact, we do not agree that in direct contact with food, organic recycling is always the optimal 
form of recycling. 
The aluminium capsules are designed to optimize and reduce the waste related to the packaging material 
and its content, preserving the quality of the beverage for a long time and being 100% recyclable. 
Therefore, we do ask to maintain the neutrality of the choice of material for the production of beverage 
capsules and the neutrality of choice of the end-of-life solutions (recyclability or compostability).  
Aluminium is emerging as the most popular option for capsules due to its high barrier properties and high 
recyclability of the  material – since it can be recycled indefinitely without losing its original quality. By 
providing a full barrier, the alumin ium capsules also avoid the need for additional protective packaging per 
unit thus contributing to achieve the minimization of the packaging.

Maintaining the freedom of choice is key to achieving the best results for consumers and the environment. 
Furthermore, limiting the production of coffee and tea capsules to only compostable materials would not 
only severely disrupt the beverage  capsule market, but also undermine the massive investments in the 
circularity of aluminium beverage capsules and the potential for innovation of the industry.  
For years, coffee producers using aluminium capsules have been tapping into collection and recycling 
systems in the main European markets often using the networks and skills of packaging EPRs. Cost-effective 
recycling technologies for aluminium capsules are available and well distributed on the market while 
composting plants still need to be fully and effectively implemented. 
In Italy, a collection and recycling system for alumin ium capsules and coffee powder has been put in place 
since 2011. There are currently over 163 collection points in 77 cities. In 2022, over 1,700 tons of c offee 
capsules were collected and recycled.  
Thanks to the National Consortium for the Collection and Recycling of Alumin ium Packaging (CIAL), in Italy 
100% of the collected aluminium coffee capsule are recycled to make new aluminium goods or to be used 
as a secondary raw material for coffee capsules, while the coffee is sent to composting plants and used as a 
fertilizer. 
Therefore, we support the amendments proposed by EAFA (European Aluminium Foil Association). 
In particular, we  call on the Commission to clarify the definitions of the different types of packaging used 
for the consumption of coffee and tea: the permeable ones which operate by means of filtration (such as 
tea bags) and those which have a stiffer structure that makes them suitable for recycling.  
In the latter case, which includes the aluminium capsules, it is necessary to guarantee the neutrality of 
the material and to exclude these applications from the provisions of article 8, paragraph 1. 
 
Comments on article 22 
The restrictions for the items listed in Annex V are not justified by any impact assessment and do not take 
into account either the properties of the materials or the results of some Member States that have already 
established themselves as leader in  recycling systems and sustainable management of the packaging even 
for single-use applications. This approach risks nullifying the efforts and investments made by the most 
virtuous industrial sectors and countries which have led to overcome the European packagi ng recycling 
targets, to date. 
The 95% of the aluminium packaging is single -use, with 90% of applications in the food sector. However 
aluminium is a permanent material and single use applications should not be associated with the concept 
of disposal, since aluminium is well suited for an infinite usage. The 75% of all aluminium ever produced is 
still in use.

Aluminium is also a lightweight material  compared to other s. N evertheless, the global and Italian 
aluminium packaging industry, in the last 20 years, has pursued a continuous improvement in reducing the 
weight of packaging with a significant saving of resources and CO2 emissions. For example, the weight of a 
33cl beverage can has dropped from 14g in 2000 to 12.2g today, a drop of 12%. 
A general ban for single use applications is a very severe form of market restriction and need to be carefully 
assessed on a product-by-product case considering the properties of the materials and their recycling rates 
and the potential negative impacts that a ban could have on food waste, consumer behaviour, food safety, 
hygiene and the environment. The Commission’s impact  assessment completely fails to justify such 
measure on a product-by-product level. 
Therefore, we urge the Commission to delete article 22 
 
Comments on article 26 
Industry agrees  to the need of reducing the generation of packaging waste.  However, we are extremely 
concerned that reuse targets, in particular the ones for food and beverages, lack of solid scientific analysis 
on the environmental impacts on a product -by-product level as well as impact on hygiene , food safety and 
food waste, especially as the proposal envisages such high target percentages and short-term deadlines. 
The introduction of any reuse objectives should only be envisaged where it is clearly demonstrated that this 
is justified  from an environmental and ecological point of view based on verifiable and comparable 
technical-scientific evidence. 
We believe that based on scientific evidences, the reuse objectives proposed by the Regulation should be 
deleted, holistically taking into  account the packaging requirements for their daily use, i.e. with respect to 
the hygiene, health and food safety and preventing food waste. 
Life Cycle Assessment should always be used  to analyze the cost/opportunity of reuse comparing to 
recycling. If following this analysis there are no net environmental benefits but more negative impacts, the 
strict application of the waste hierarchy will have negative effects on both the environment and the 
economy. 
Aluminium sector can demonstrate based on LCA studies  that f rom an  environmental point of view  
aluminium foil  performs no worse than the reusable boxes considered for the analysis since it has less 
negative impacts in most relevant environmental categories and equal in the remaining ones. 
Moreover, prescribing reuse obligations, regardless of the material with which the packaging is made, its 
recyclability and the ir current recycling rates would jeopardise the results obtained by the collection 
systems of each Member State  and the efforts that packaging companies have put into circularity and 
recyclability of their products without any beneficial for the environment.

In conclusion, we believe that the most balanced and suitable approach for optimizing the environmental 
sustainability of the use of packaging is to allow Member States to balance the choice of the best solution 
between reuse and/or recycling on a case by case basis  
In fact, by ensuring the necessary flexibility in choosing one or the other solution both the main objective of 
the Regulation and the vocation - including infrastructural - of the individual Member State, could be 
preserved. 
Therefore, we recommend deleting the Article 26 and, in particular, the reuse obligations envisaged, by 
2030 and 2040, for take-away packaging referred to in paragraph 3. 
The use of completely recyclable containers and other packaging should be allowed and encouraged, in 
accordance with the UNI EN 13430 standard, s uch as those in aluminium which are effectively collected 
and recycled and in Italy have already overcome the recycling targets set for 2030.

VDMA position paper 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Proposal for a Regulation on packaging and 
packaging waste 
 
With this position, VDMA, the Machinery and Equipment Manufacturers 
Association, is commenting on the most important aspects of the proposal for 
for a Regulation on packaging and packaging waste (EU Packaging Regulation).   
 
The VDMA welcomes the objectives of the proposed EU Packaging Regulation and the 
change from a directive to a regulation. EU-wide harmonised and thus uniform 
packaging law requirements contribute significantly to a sustainable circular economy 
and the efficient functioning of the EU internal market. The new EU Packaging 
Regulation can thus replace the current "patchwork" of specific packaging regulations 
in the EU Member States.  
 
In order not to jeopardise the objectives of the new EU Packaging Regulation and to 
ensure the functioning of the EU internal market with regard to packaging law 
requirements, the EU Commission should ensure that no further individual national 
measures with regard to packaging law regulations are implemented in the EU Member 
States. The EU Commission should also work to ensure that existing national 
packaging regulations that undermine the EU internal market are repealed. 
It must be ensured that the new requirements of the EU Packaging Regulation are 
implemented quickly, completely and verifiably in all EU Member States. This is the 
only way to achieve EU-wide harmonisation in the area of packaging law requirements 
and to prevent competitive disadvantages. 
 
Mechanical and plant engineering as enabler of the circular economy  
 
In the view of the mechanical and plant engineering industry, climate neutrality is only 
possible with a future-oriented and efficient circular economy. With its innovative 
technical solutions, mechanical and plant engineering makes a significant contribution 
to reducing CO2 emissions and avoiding food and packaging waste. In this context, the 
discussions of recent years have focused primarily on the topic of plastics. This has led 
to extensive new regulations (EU Single-Use Plastic Directive). The sustainable use of 
plastics requires a functioning circular economy and the careful use of resources1 .   
 
As an enabler of the circular economy, the mechanical and plant engineering sector 
sees more opportunities than risks in the EU Packaging Regulation and offers itself as 
a transparent and open discussion partner to all stakeholders for in-depth discussions. 
The following points can be used as a basis for the discussions. It must be taken into 
account that individual articles of the proposed regulation allow for a wide margin of 
interpretation. The unclear requirements inevitably lead to uncertainty and thus to 
planning insecurities for all affected market actors; they also cause costs and hinder 
and prevent future sustainable innovations.

On the proposal for an EU Packaging Regulation in detail: 
 
Article 5 - Requirements for substances in packaging   
 
According to Article 5(1) of the proposed EU Packaging Regulation, packaging shall be 
manufactured in such a way as to minimise the presence and concentration of 
substances of concern as constituents of the packaging material or any of the 
packaging components. 
Discussions on the topic “substances of concern” are currently underway and must be 
seen in the context of the revision of the EU chemicals regulation REACH. Against this 
background, VDMA believes it is premature to include regulations on substances of 
concern in the EU Packaging Regulation. Consistency and coherence with the EU 
chemicals regulation REACH must be ensured.  
 
Article 6 - Recyclable packaging  
 
"Design for recycling" criteria must be given high priority in the proposed EU Packaging 
Regulation and in the delegated acts. Only if recycling is considered from the beginning 
can correspondingly high recycling rates be achieved at the end of life.   
 
The mechanical and plant engineering industry therefore welcomes the assessment of 
the recyclability of packaging on the basis of uniform EU-wide "Design for recycling" 
criteria.  
 
In the EU, there are already numerous guidelines on the topic of "Design for Recycling" 
for both plastic and fibre-based packaging (see Annex). These should be incorporated 
into a uniform catalogue of criteria. Attention must be paid to ensuring that these 
criteria and the disposal and recycling options, which are currently still very different in 
the EU member states, are compatible with each other by law.  
 
Before criteria are set on the basis of delegated acts, stakeholders from the entire 
packaging value chain should be consulted. The VDMA would therefore advocate the 
formation of an independent expert group with stakeholders from the entire packaging 
value chain to support the EU Commission in the development of these criteria.  
 
Article 7 - Minimum recycled content in plastic packaging  
 
VDMA generally welcomes the definition of minimum recycled content in plastic 
packaging. In recent years, the use of recycled material, some of which is costly to 
produce, has failed due to reservations on the part of users and, above all, time and 
again due to the lack of price parity between primary and secondary plastics. This 
obstacle should be removed with the minimum percentage of recycled material per 
packaging unit obtained from post-consumer plastic waste provided for in Article 7 of 
the EU Packaging Regulation. 
 
However, the mechanical and plant engineering industry expressly appeals that the 
design of the quotas should be gradual and dynamic, starting from simple applications 
outside food packaging where standard plastics (PE, PP, PET, PS and PVC) are used.

From a technical point of view, VDMA is critical of the mandatory use of recycled 
content for contact-sensitive packaging. The prerequisite for use in this area is suitable, 
i.e. approved recycling technologies. Here, the industry still sees a considerable need 
for action and, above all, innovation in order to be able to realise the ambitious goals of 
the minimum share of recycled content. 
 
According to section 2 of Regulation (EU) 2022/16162 , "A pre-requisite to any increase 
in recycled content in food packaging and other food contact materials remains the 
need to secure a high level of protection of human health. However, wasted plastic 
materials and articles, even when originating from food use, may contain contaminants 
incidental to that use which potentially compromise the safety and quality of recycled 
plastic food contact materials and articles." 
 
In this context, it should be taken into account that as of today, according to Annex I, 
Table 1 of Regulation (EU) 2022/16163 only two recycling technologies for PET have 
been approved so far: On the basis of the evaluations done by the EFSA (European 
Food Safety Authority) of the applications for authorisation submitted in accordance 
with Regulation (EC) No 282/2008 (no longer in force), mechanical PET recycling and 
product loops in a closed and controlled chain may be considered as suitable recycling 
technologies to recycle plastic waste into plastic meeting the requirements of Article 3 
of Regulation (EC) No 1935/20044.  
 
The use of recycled plastics in contact-sensitive plastic packaging, must comply with 
EU regulations for food contact materials5 .  
 
For non-PET contact-sensitive plastic packaging (e.g. for food), there are currently no 
recycling processes evaluated by EFSA and thus approved by the EU, so it can be 
assumed that the guarantee of a high level of protection for human health when using 
post-consumer plastic waste in new contact-sensitive plastic packaging is currently not 
given.  
 
The biggest challenge in the recycling of post-consumer plastic waste are the so-called 
multilayer films. These will continue to be necessary in the future in order to be able to 
package food hygienically and safely. In order to be able to return this material to the 
cycle in the best possible way in the future, it is necessary to expand the established 
recycling processes. From the point of view of mechanical and plant engineering, 
chemical recycling is a supplement to established mechanical recycling processes, as 
it involves breaking down the plastics into its basic chemical components. However, 
this useful addition is not yet recognised under the EU Waste Framework Directive. 
Furthermore, the currently existing plants for chemical recycling are not yet large-scale 
plants capable of producing the corresponding quantities. In this way, the required 
minimum percentages of recycled material cannot be realised even in the medium

Article 22 - Restrictions on use of certain packaging formats  
(Annex V, Position 2 and 4)  
 
The main task of packaging is to protect the product along the entire logistics chain: 
From the place of its manufacture to the place of its use or consumption. In doing so, it 
protects valuable resources, because with each step in the value chain of a product, its 
value increases due to the resources used in the form of labour, energy, water, etc. 
Thus, the product itself causes a far greater carbon footprint than its packaging.  
 
Packaging is essential for a safe and hygienic food supply. Packaging that is matched 
to the product reduces food loss and waste.  
 
A ban on packaging of all packaging materials for fruit and vegetables (Annex V, 
Position 2), as well as a general ban on disposable packaging in the HORECA sector 
(Annex V, Position 4), can lead to contamination and cross-contamination of food, an 
impairment of consumer health, as well as to spoilage of food and thus to increasing 
food losses. This would undermine the "Farm to Fork" strategy propagated by the EU 
Commission. One of the central goals of this strategy is precisely to reduce food losses 
and waste.6   
 
Disposable packaging in the HORECA sector that is generated at the respective 
facilities does not end up in the environment, but is collected and can then be properly 
disposed of or recycled.  
 
Bans on certain types of packaging are therefore not expedient and require a thorough 
evaluation with a focus on function, life cycle assessment and eco-efficiency. 
Preferential treatment or discrimination of individual packaging materials should be 
avoided.  
 
Bans on certain packaging and materials affect all links in the value chain. For the 
Packaging Machinery Industry, an enabler of sustainable packaging solutions, this 
would have profound consequences. Business models established over decades would 
be at stake and with them many jobs. 
 
Article 26 - Re-use and refill targets  
 
Food packaging must undergo extensive cleaning processes before it can be reused or 
refilled. Repeated contact with food and the chemicals and temperatures required for 
cleaning can cause material degradation of the packaging, depending on the frequency 
of reuse cycles. This can lead to cross-contamination between different uses, so that 
hygiene, safety and quality of products packaged or filled in reusable packaging cannot 
always be guaranteed compared to single-use packaging, and food loss can increase.  
 
The targets for reuse or refilling of packaging should therefore be reviewed in terms of 
consumer safety and health as well as actual environmental benefits.

The transport of empty packaging resulting from a reusable system obligation must be 
questioned above all with regard to ecological aspects.  
 
On the specific reuse targets under Article 26, paragraphs 12 and 13:  
Transport packaging in the industrial goods industry is a special case in the B2B 
sector. This packaging is usually individually adapted and manufactured for the 
respective product (machine, component, spare part, etc.). This individuality and 
specificity must be reflected in the legal text of a new EU Packaging Regulation. 
Exceptions should therefore apply with regard to the required reusability. 
 
Article 46 - Recycling targets and promotion of recycling  
 
The mechanical and plant engineering sector advocates ambitious but achievable and 
technically possible recycling targets.   
 
The proposed EU Packaging Regulation defines specific recycling targets for the 
various waste fractions. In the view of VDMA, these are very ambitious and can only be 
achieved if the corresponding infrastructure in the form of suitable sorting and recycling 
facilities is available throughout the EU. The feasibility of the recycling targets should 
be verified as part of an appropriate and careful assessment by an independent expert 
group of relevant stakeholders.   
 
 
The VDMA – Machinery and Equipment Manufacturers Association 
The VDMA represents more than 3,500 German and European mechanical and plant engineering 
companies. The industry stands for innovation, export orientation and SMEs. The companies employ 
around 3 million people in the EU-27, more than 1.2 million of them in Germany alone. This makes 
mechanical and plant engineering the largest employer among the capital goods industries, both in the 
EU-27 and in Germany. In the European Union, it represents a turnover volume of an estimated 770 
billion euros. Around 80 percent of the machinery sold in the EU comes from a manufacturing plant in 
the domestic market.

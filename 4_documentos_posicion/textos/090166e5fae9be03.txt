Copa-Cogeca feedback: 
Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation 
2022/0396(COD)   
Introduction 
In view of the publication of the Commission’s proposal for the Packaging and 
Packaging Waste Regulation, Copa and Cogeca – who represent 22 million 
farmers and their families, and over 22,000 cooperatives – would like to express 
their concerns as regards some of the extremely ambitious targets and measures 
that are included in the proposed regulation of 30 November 2022, as well as their 
expected impact on key agricultural sectors.  
Context  
Copa and Cogeca support the sustainability objectives set in the European Green 
Deal and the importance to continue the transition to a more circular economy, 
which includes ensuring sustainable packaging. Our members have been 
engaged and orienting their investments in this direction, for years. To move 
forward this change, an appropriate regulatory framework is needed.  
However, it is paramount that all three pillars of sustainability (economic, social 
and environmental) be given equal weight and the socio-economic 
circumstances (high production costs, challenges regarding the 
availability/affordability of raw materials), as well as the health and safety 
considerations, be taken into account and factored into new legislative proposals. 
It is through this very delicate balancing of principles, as well as an evidenced-
based and cost-effective approach, that the set objectives can be attained. 
Moreover, the Commission should foresee a proper and reasonable timeframe 
and transitional period for implementation. 
Taking the aforementioned into account, we would like to share our 
responses to specific aspects of the proposal:  
  
1. Targets for recycled plastic packaging 
As far as the specific provisions are concerned, European farmers and agri-
cooperatives are extremely worried about the impact of the proposed targets for 
recycled plastic packaging content and reuse/refill, as well as the absence of 
downcycling measures and harmonised labelling.

With respect to recycled plastic packaging, we consider that the definition for 
plastic packaging is concurrently wide and very restrictive, as it neither refers 
to types of packaging nor does it encompass innovation in terms of material. To 
ensure clarity and a definition that is future-proof, the EU-level definition should 
cover aspects such as the type of packaging (containers, bags, ties etc.) and the 
types of plastic and materials allowed (i.e., bio-based, biodegradable or 
compostable).  
Furthermore, these targets and requirements do not consider the challenging 
socio-economic context, the efforts made by producers and their cooperatives 
(who have made heavy investments to implement the use of ecological 
packaging that have not yet been amortized) and the repercussions for the latter. 
The war in Ukraine has exacerbated the difficulties with access to packaging 
material due to reduced availability and affordability of raw materials. This is 
compounded by the surging costs of inputs, energy and food, as well as supply 
chain disruptions.  
The proposed regulation does not contain any measures to prevent 
downcycling of food packaging used for non-food packaging. We believe that 
high-quality plastic used as food contact materials should be recycled in a way 
that ensures its continued use in the same product category with minimal losses 
in terms of quantity, quality or function. Measures to prevent downcycling will 
render possible the establishment of closed-loop systems. We hereby 
encourage, in line with the EU circular economy strategy, the inclusion of 
measures to prevent recycled plastic from being downcycled and non-food 
sectors’ unrestricted access to food-grade materials.  
The classification as "recyclable packaging" should also take into account the 
quality of secondary materials and infinite recycling. The same applies to the 
closed-loops not considered in the current draft in this respect. Furthermore, the 
criteria for recyclability should not be developed separately for each packaging 
material in a delegated act, as this could lead to different classifications for 
recyclability. This point is also of relevance to sectors relying on glass, 
including the wine sector.  
 
Although the proposal sets ambitious targets for the use of recycled material, the 
availability of such materials that comply with food safety standards are not yet 
available in the desired quantity. Indeed, in the event of implementation of these 
targets in the near future, European packaging manufacturers would not even 
have the capacity to supply all operators.  
While we take note of the “derogations” set out in Art. 7, paras 9 and 10, we do 
not consider these sufficient to consider the issues raised above. Therefore, as a 
solution, Copa and Cogeca call for the targets for contact sensitive plastic 
packaging (food packaging) to be set by manufacturer and not by unit, and 
corresponding amendments to Art. 7, paras 1 and 2.  
The proposal for a regulation also envisages a series of obligations, not only for 
the packaging manufacturer which would penalised due to the technological

2. Provisions applicable to the fruit and vegetables sector 
Copa and Cogeca support efforts to make fruit and vegetable packaging more 
sustainable and welcomes harmonisation across the EU in this area. However, 
Packaging and labeling of fruits and vegetables ensures their traceability, which 
is important for ensuring product safety. Therefore, the proposed ban on single-
use packaging is disproportionate, does not consider the reasons why a 
product is packaged and risks leading to undesirable effects such as food 
waste and potential food safety hazards.  
In general, measures in this regulation with regard to the fruit and vegetables 
sector are discriminatory and repeat the problematic approach taken by the SUR 
regulation, that is a focus on bans instead of practical recommendations for 
solutions.  
Given that reduction in food waste is also a priority of the Commission, setting 
ambitious targets on packaging for fruit and vegetables will require setting 
an appropriate transition period until suitable new generations of packaging 
materials and technological solutions are available at affordable prices and in a 
form that can replace packaging current and provide the same shelf-life. If the 
new packaging’s protection level is insufficient, it will be important to consider a 
list of exemptions for the fruit and vegetables most at risk in terms of safety, 
integrity, and alteration of their organoleptic quality.   
Additionally, proposed measures aimed at reducing packaging should be 
based on strong and reliable scientific analyses while packaging does make a 
major contribution to a longer shelf life and thus to reducing food waste. 
Scientific analyses could be based on Life-cycle Assessment (LCA) 
calculations, if they meet high scientific and reliable standards.

Today, our producers are lacking technically effective and economically viable 
alternatives to single-use packaging and non-compostable labels, especially 
considering the heterogeneity of the products concerned, their perishability and 
fragility, the needs of its management in the points of sale and the needs of fruit 
and vegetable producers to satisfy export demand. Given the environmental 
impact of food waste can be considerably higher than the impact of single 
packaging, it is important that this is taken into consideration. Furthermore, 
given that packaging is used to protect fresh produce in transit from the farm, 
the elimination of single-use packaging would not only increase food waste from 
resulting damage to produce, but also increased costs to producers, which is 
unacceptable.  
Regarding specific provisions, this proposal would ban the use of non-
compostable stickers on fruit and vegetables in Art. 8. and the use of single use 
packaging for less than 1.5 kg fresh fruit and vegetables in Art. 22 and annex V, 
“unless there is a demonstrated need to avoid water loss or turgidity loss, 
microbiological hazards or physical shocks”.  
Here we note that single-use packaging aims not only to prevent “water loss or 
turgidity loss, microbiological hazards or physical shocks”, it seeks other functions.  
Therefore, its elimination in the manufacturing lines would harm the commercial 
activity and viability of our producers, cooperatives and traders, and the ability of 
single use packaging to adequately supply the EU market and add value to the 
product through, for example, differentiation, quality, convenience, marketing, 
extension of the life of the product, provision of information to consumer 
about variety, quality, the production or recommended use. The Commission 
should also take into account that increasing the value of the products is one of 
the main objectives of the CAP, especially for fruit and vegetables, and that this 
continues to be the case within the sectorial interventions of the new CAP 
through operational programs of fruit and vegetable producer organisations.  
Stickers and labels attached to fruits and vegetables carry important 
information, in particular origin labelling in certain countries. They can be easily 
removed from the product and can be recycled as plastic. Here, the material and 
technological investment required to change the entire raw material production 
and manufacturing technology is not proportional with the benefits that can be 
achieved.  
Finally, it is unacceptable that the fruit and vegetable sector is specifically 
targeted in the proposal, given that this sector is not the only one to use 
packaging in food and indeed is one of the sectors that need it most. This is 
discriminatory and will certainly not contribute to increasing the consumption of 
healthy fresh fruit and vegetables and is counterproductive to EU policies aimed 
at: promoting healthy diets; reducing food waste across the EU; boosting safety, 
quality and traceability of EU production; recording and controlling the origin of 
products such as through “made-in” labels for products made in the EU or a 
Member State.

Based on the above, Copa and Cogeca strongly call for a revision of the above 
provisions which concern sticker labels and a ban on packaging for fruit and 
vegetables under 1.5kg and to focus not on prohibitions but instead 
incentives and a workable strategy which may include promotion ecological 
labelling or improving packaging management. More specifically, as a 
compromise solution, and taking into account the heterogeneity of fruit and 
vegetables, the regulation should be oriented towards:  
­ Reducing the use of packaging, particularly superfluous ones; 
­ Promoting the use of environmental friendly packaging (such as 
degradable or compostable paper or cardboard packaging), where 
technically and economically possible; 
­ Respecting the cases in which the use of plastic packaging is essential and 
irreplaceable today, without loss of quality given current technological 
possibilities. 
­ improving packaging waste management 
 
In any case, any strategy for  increasing the use of alternatives should take into 
account the following factors:  
• Market compatible additional costs or fair cost burden in the marketing 
chain  
• sufficient availability of materials 
• No loss of the positive effects of the packaging  
• Demonstrated significant benefits for the environment 
• Not generate discriminatory conditions for certain products or regions or 
distort the circulation of goods within EU market 
On the measures regarding stickers and labels, the focus should be on food 
education and information rather than new production technologies. 
Communicating the role of labels on fruit and vegetables and their recycling as 
selective waste could be part of the communication strategy on healthy and 
environmentally conscious diets. The labels would not require composting but 
given that most of the green waste generated by households is processed as 
household waste, a compromise could be reached (as with PET bottles) to include 
labels in the recycling process, but with minimal technological and financial input 
compared to the current proposal.  
Additionally, Copa and Cogeca call on the Commission to block the initiatives 
of certain Member States that are unilaterally regulating in an uncoordinated 
manner in this regard, thus creating barriers to the free movement of fruit and 
vegetables within the EU single market.

For the wine sector, we consider rigid deadlines and targets as set out in the 
proposal to be unproductive to achieving a system that is both ecologically and 
economically suitable. Copa and Cogeca instead call for further impact 
assessments with the aim of achieving a packaging mix for this sector that is 
evidence-based and sustainable.   
 
Regarding obligations for deposit and return systems, we welcome the exclusion 
of wine from this obligation in principle. This is a step in the right direction, for it 
takes into account the realities on the ground and the specificities of the 
products which would render compulsory reuse targets impossible to achieve.  
Not only are wine products consumed across the globe, but also the bottles are 
often used by consumers for different purposes which make collection, cleaning 
and reuse a very energy-, fuel- and water-intensive exercise. Moreover, any 
mandatory reuse targets for all the aforementioned products would run counter 
to the recycling objective to achieve packaging circularity. On top of this there is 
the logistical complexity of organising mandatory collection and transportation, 
as well as the lack of adequate infrastructure.  
However, we note that the proposal sets mandatory reuse/refill targets for wine 
in Art. 26, para 5. Besides wine produced in the EU being exported and enjoyed 
across the globe, which renders bottle collection as very hard to achieve, the 
mandatory reuse/refill targets also pose significant hygiene risks.   
With bottles often being used by consumers for  different purposes, collection,  
cleaning,  and  reuse  of bottles constitutes  a  very  energy-,  fuel-  and  
water-intensive exercise that also increases the risk of entry and 
development of pathogens. It is for this reason that reuse is not recommended 
in the  reference  health  guide  of  the  wine  sector. Food safety is a primary issue, 
which cannot be compromised due to environmental protection goals, so we do 
not consider it appropriate to set reuse or refill goals for wines either. 
Additionally, we note that not all bottles are suitable for this purpose. For 
example, the thin neck opening required for a screw top can compromise the 
bottle's transporting strength, so manufacturers must use heavier bottles and use 
cork closures, at greater cost. 
It should be added that the bottles also represent marketing value, with their 
diversity they contribute to the fact that consumers can better distinguish 
between individual products. If refilling is required, it would be the responsibility 
of the consumer to prevent food adulteration, which is not feasible. In addition, it 
should also be resolved that the bottles of the individual wineries are returned to 
themselves. On the other hand, the proposal does not make the deposit and 
return system mandatory for these products, which is illogical and 
counterproductive in this context.  
Moreover, any mandatory reuse targets would run counter  to  the  recycling  
objective  to  achieve  packaging circularity. In light of the above and assessed 
against the background of hygiene, health and safety requirements, the extent to

certain stage of the production cycle.

5. Provisions applicable to the dairy sector  
Copa and Cogeca note that this proposal foresees a marketing ban single-use

and Annex 5. Both butters and single-serving grated cheese sachets would be

6. Need to ensure harmonisation of measures  
To protect the EU common market and avoid fragmentation and competitive 
distortion, as it is currently occurring with several Member States either adopting 
or envisaging adoption of national legislation on this issue, a harmonised EU-wide 
approach should be pursued.  
However, it must be noted that the regulation as proposed will allows packaging 
included in an extended producer responsibility scheme or covered by a deposit 
and return system to be identified by a specific national symbol on the 
packaging. As most packaging will be covered by a producer responsibility 
scheme this will allow for member states to require national labelling on most 
packaging products thus restricting and impeding the placing on the marked of 
packaging that otherwise comply with the regulation. 
On Art. 4, which covers free movement, Copa and Cogeca therefore call for the 
deletion of para 5, to prevent distortions of the internal market.  
On labelling of packaging, instead of allowing a national symbol to identify the 
extended producer responsibility scheme or a deposit and return scheme it 
should be required that member states use a harmonised symbol in the same 
way as the harmonised deposit label in Art. 11, para 1. To this end, we call for the 
amendment of Art. 11, paras 1 and 8, and Art. 12 to specify that the label on the 
packaging shall be easily understood and without the need for a national text.  
In support of the aim of this regulation to protect the integrity of the internal 
market and reduce barriers, and also to ensure certainty for producers, it is 
imperative that the aforementioned provisions be revised to ensure 
harmonisation across the EU is realised.

8. Impacts on economic operators using transport packaging  
We note that Art. 26 of the proposal would impose minimum reusable packaging 
requirements relevant to transport, such s through replacement of transport 
cartons by plastic crates.  
Copa-Cogeca emphasise that industrial development in recent years has led 
producers to equip their end-of-line facilities with automations involving the use 
of cartons. Replacing them with collapsible plastic crates would be contrary to 
the requirements by the trading partners of producers (e.g. retailers) relating to 
tray type, number and shelf display. Such trading partners (especially those in the 
discount world) would be obliged to revise the way products are presented on 
the shelf, as they would no longer be able to use the display carton. 
This would also have a significant exacerbation of palletisation which would 
result in increased costs for transported pallets and logistical costs, with the 
negative effects on the efficient management of the checkout flow, in particular

Conclusion 
In fine, European farmers and cooperatives are committed to and constantly 
working on improving the sustainability and circularity in their production 
methods and products. To continue this path, they need a supporting regulatory 
framework that is fair and feasible, which means it does not compromise the 
products’ safety and takes into account the specificities of the different products. 
Targets must be underpinned by robust scientific and empirical evidence in 
order to be realistic. Such research should be carried out where such evidence is 
lacking (life-cycle analysis and viable alternatives). Lastly, flexibility and a 
sufficient transition time, which is aligned with the technical and economic 
feasibility of alternatives, are essential for enabling innovation and adaptation.

Joint letter from French, Italian and Spanish F&V Cooperatives concerning: 
DRAFT EU REGULATION regarding Use of Plastics in Fruit and Vegetable Packaging  
(replacing Directive 94/62/EC on Packaging and Packaging Waste) 
 
Mr. Virginijus Sinkevičius, Commissioner Environment, Oceans and Fisheries 
Mr. Janusz Wojciechowski, Commissioner  Agriculture 
Mr. Stella Kyriakides, Commissioner Health and Food Safety 
C/C: Florika FINK-HOOIJER, Wolfang BURTSCHER, Sandra GALLINA 
Brussels, February 9th,  2023 
Dear Commissioners,  
 
We are writing to you as representatives of the Fruit and Vegetables cooperatives in France, Italy, and Spain. 
We would like to express our concerns and proposals in relation with the “Draft EU Regulation Packaging and 
Packaging Waste”, that was presented by Commission on 30 November 20221 and will replace Directive 
94/62/EC. 
That proposal,  includes a specific provision on the use of packaging for F&V: it would ban the use of non- 
compostable stickers on F&V (art.8) and the use of single use packaging for less than 1.5 kg for fresh fruit and 
vegetables (Art. 22 and annex V point 2), “unless there is a demonstrated need to avoid water loss or turgidity 
loss, microbiological hazards or physical shocks”. So that, with respect of FV, it is not about reducing or 
promoting alternatives, or improving management, but directly about prohibiting any kind of single use 
packaging! 
We would like to notice, although it is obvious, that FV are not the only food that employs single use packaging, 
neither the sector were plastic or single use packaging is the more used… Not to compare with other non-food 
goods!! In fact, Commission is considering the ban on packaging F&V before tackling other much more 
superfluous uses of packaging or plastic.  We find no reason why the regulation is specifically primed with our 
sector in such a DISCRIMINATORY WAY! 
It should also be remembered that F&V sector has been engaged for some time in the evolution and 
improvement of the use of environmentally friendly packaging where they have found it feasible. This is 
evident if we look at the new forms of presentation introducing alternative materials and formats that one 
can find in FV retailers.  
Furthermore, nowadays there is still, for many FV products, a lack of technically effective and economically 
viable alternatives to single use packaging and of compostable labels, especially considering the number and 
heterogeneity of the FV concerned, the perishability and fragility of most of them, the needs of its 
management in the points of sale and the FV export vocation.  
Anyway, it would seem that something very important is being overlooked:  single use packaging doesn’t aim 
only to prevent “water loss or turgidity loss, microbiological hazards or physical shocks”, it seeks other 
functions that should be considered by UE when raising exceptions. Functions such as:  differentiation, 
convenience, marketing and boosting commercial brands, differentiated quality, extended useful life of the 
product, providing information to consumers about  variety, quality, production system, recommendations 
for use…  So, elimination of packaging would harm the commercial activity and viability of our producers, 
cooperatives and traders  and its ability to adequately supply the EU market and add value to the product 
in favour of the consumer. Let's remember that increasing the value of products is one of the main objectives

of the CAP, especially for fruit and vegetables. It has been so until now and it will continue to be so within the 
sectoral intervention of the new CAP, through operational programs of FV producer organisation.  
In addition, the ban on the use of single use packaging would go against several other strategies that the 
Commission itself is currently promoting: it strongly increases the risk of producing food waste; it could harm 
the quality, organoleptic conditions, food safety and traceability; it could affect FV consumption (e.g: if it is no 
longer possible to offer the consumer certain easy-to-eat presentations).  Finally, sale in bulk will make it 
difficult to guarantee and to control the identification of the country of EU-origin and other provisions of the 
EU Marketing standards and labelling rules. 
For all these reasons, we strongly oppose any European wide ban on the use of single-use packaging in fruit 
and vegetable, without first foreseeing -and remedy- the immediate impact on the sector, on the consumer 
and on the environment and we ask the Commission:   
 To focus alternatively on a step-by-step approach to boost the use of ecological packaging and to  
improve the single-use packaging management systems (by improving collection and recycle systems 
in addition of promoting the reduction of use of packaging) so that the ecological transition is 
compatible with the adaptability of the sector. 
 To pursue an approach centred on achievable, realistic, and economically sustainable targets with a 
reasonable timeframe for implementation and based on an impact assessment covering the economic 
and environmental effects.  
 To guarantee the harmonized application of any regulation on packaging throughout the Union.  
 To provide sufficiently clear application provisions to avoid legal uncertainty, which defines concepts 
such as “packaging”, “fresh FV”, “demonstrated need”… 
In the short term, we have asked single Member States not to regulate independently in this regard in order 
to protect the proper functioning of the EU common market. Thus, French regulation (Article 77 of “Loi 
AGEC”) and its application Decree  which is currently under discussion , and the one that has already been 
approved in Spain (article 7.4 of “Real Decreto 1055/2022 de Envases y Residuos de Envases”)  should be 
suspended to favour a timely, balanced, non-discriminatory and common European regulation for all F&V 
operators in the EU market. 
When it comes to the French situation, and since the French Ministry of Ecological Transition has already 
communicated on the publication of a new decree, the draft of which was notified2 to the Commission on 
14/12/22 within the framework of Directive (EU) 2015/1535, we urge the Commission to block this draft, as 
long as an harmonization work at European Union level is already underway in the same field (after the  
above mentioned “Draft EU Regulation Packaging and Packaging Waste” has been adopted by College of 
Commissioners). 
We trust that you are sensitive to our concerns and proposals.

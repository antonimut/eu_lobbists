Position paper of the Foreign Investors Council (FIC) on the European Commission’s proposal for a 
Regulation on packaging and packaging waste, amending Regulation (EU) 2019/1020 and 
Directive (EU) 2019/904 and repealing Directive 94/62/EC 
 
We, the Foreign Investors Council (FIC) and its members, support the objective of the European Commission’s 
proposal for a  Regulation on packaging and packaging waste, amending Regulation (EU) 2019/1020 and 
Directive (EU) 2019/904 and repealing Directive 94/62/EC  (the "Regulation") to contribute to the efficient 
functioning of the internal market for packaged products, while preventing or reducing the negative impacts 
of packaging and packaging waste on the environment and human health. 
The choice of a Regulation as the legal instrument is appropriate to ensure the efficient functioning of the  
internal market and to enhance the potential for regulatory harmonization across Member States.  
However, there are several concerns about the provisions of the Regulation. 
For example, certain provisions, such as those in Articles 4(4), 11(7), and 45 of the Regulation, allow Member 
States to introduce or maintain specific national requirements , which may lead to divergent measures that 
jeopardize the free movement of packaging and packaged products. 
At the same time, certain provisions of the Regulation could affect the prior ities of the beverage industry  in 
Romania, and more broadly, at the EU level. The beverage industry in Romania has innovated and invested 
over time to ensure that packaging becomes increasingly sustainable, which is why we support the overall 
objectives of the Regulation to reduce packaging waste. However, we are concerned that the provisions of the 
Regulation could be extremely burdensome, even for a sector that has managed to maintain a signif icant 
proportion of reusable packaging in its portfolio, such as reusable bottles and kegs for serving beer by the pint 
or home reusable solutions developed by the soft drinks industry.  
In order to support the  Romanian competent authorities in developing national positions to be promoted 
within the legislative process for the adoption of the Regulation , w e present below the main concerns 
regarding the Regulation and the r elated proposals we kindly ask the European Commission to take into 
consideration as part of the feedback that the European Commission will present to the European Parliament 
and Council with the aim of feeding into the legislative debate in relation to the Regulation. 
A. In brief, the concerns relate to the following provisions of the Regulation: 
• minimum recycled content targets for plastic packaging; 
• labeling requirements; 
• re-use and refill targets of sales packaging; 
• targets for packaging waste reduction; 
• deposit and return systems. 
 
B. Detailing the issues on which there are concerns and proposals: 
 
1) Minimum recycled content targets for plastic packaging (Article 7 of the Regulation)  
We support high minimum recycled content targets for plastic packaging if the beverage industry has 
priority access to recycled content. We believe it is important that the Regulation includes requirements to

waste and is obliged to use recycled material in new packaging placed on the market. 
In this regard, we request that the Regulation be amended to include a right of preemption in favor of the

recycling loops and avoiding the  loss of high-value PET by converting it to low-value plastic. In addition to 
avoiding free-riding, priority access would encourage investment in the different loops of other industries and 
create a larger scale, which is currently not the case. 
We, therefore, agree with the proposal that Member States should ensure that systems are in place for the 
return and separate collection of all packaging waste in a way that facilitates preparation for re-use and high-
quality recycling (Article 43(1)). 
However, the Regulation does not include a definition of high -quality recycling or any measure that would 
ensure that high -quality recycled materials suitable for food use are used primarily for food purposes . This 
means that high-quality materials (meeting EU food saf ety requirements) may be reduced and used for non -
food purposes that do not require the same level of quality. 
There is also no requirement to ensure that the obligated industry has priority access to the recycled materials 
required for food contact packaging. Moreover, it is unrealistic to assume that increasing separate collection 
requirements and targets will ensure the necessary quantities and quality of recycled raw materials. This could 
lead to apparent competition, which could generate a scramble for raw materials and an increase in the price 
of the material s, which would be contrary to the Regulation objectives, without being the result of the 
competitive strategies of individual companies. 
Moreover, we support calculating the minimum recycled content as an average for all such packaging placed 
on the market on the territory of a Member State and not per unit of packaging, as proposed in Article 7 of 
the Regulation. Whereas Directive (EU) 2019/904 on the reduction of the impact of certain plastic products on 
the environment (transposed into Romanian legislation by the Government Emergency Ordinance 6/2021  on 
the reduction of the impact of certain plastic products on the environment) proposes that the 30% recycled 
plastic content target be calculated as an average for all packaging placed on the market in  a Member State, 
the Regulation proposes to calculate the recycled content per packaging unit. This is a contradiction that 
needs to be corrected. 
There is no environmental benefit to calculating recycled content per unit of packaging . The amount of 
recycled content an operator can use on average in its portfolio remains the same. In addition, an average 
approach helps the producer to manage the use of raw mater ials according to availability and the seasonal 
demand for products and other market realities. 
Calculating recycled content, on average, at the Member State level, protects the EU’s circular economy from 
unpredictable external geopolitical events that can impact value chains.

b. Article 43(1) of the Regulation should be amended to mandate Member States to ensure the

essential to have transitional revisions to offer some predictability to the industry in the event of 
undesirable delays. 
 
2) Labeling requirements (Article 11 of the Regulation) 
To ensure the optimal operation of the deposit and return system, the logo of the deposit and return system 
must be given priority on the packaging label over any other instructions on how to handle the packaging. 
These instructions may distract the consumer from the correct  return of the  packaging through the deposit 
and return system.  
The labeling of p ackaging using a QR code, or any other type of digital data carrier should not be made 
mandatory, as there are insufficient technologies for all types of packaging (e.g. , tin cans) /  they are  not 
compatible with all beverage packaging formats. The labeling using a QR code should be, at most, optional. 
Moreover, we would like to point out that it is not possible to label each package with a unique QR code, if 
this is required by secondary legislation. 
We would like to point out that no assessment has been carried out so far on using QR codes in any existing 
packaging return system. If they were to be used, millions of new codes would be generated daily on the 
market, which would require real-time reporting and close coordination between producers, retailers, and the 
packaging return system. Therefore, we believe that a detailed analysis of the impact and feasibility of using 
QR codes in the deposit and return system is necessary before deciding in this regard. In any event, if such an 
obligation is confirmed, a sufficient transitional phase is required, particularly regarding the implementation 
of the QR code (for example, for all new containers purchased from 2030 onwards).

Beverages producers and retailers in Romania are committed to making significant efforts to implement the 
deposit and return system , to reduce the environmental impact of waste and to support sustainable 
development. At the same time, we believe  that there should be a parallel phasing of environmental and 
sustainability approaches (the Regulation and the Deposit and return system). It is essential to find a balance 
between the need to protect the environment and the responsibilities of producers so that a sustainable and 
prosperous future can be built for all. 
Proposals for the European Commission to present to the European Parliament and the Council: 
a. The Regulation should be amended to require that the logo of the deposit and return system be given 
priority on the packaging and shall be placed before any other instructions on handling the packaging 
in order to highlight the correct way of returning the packaging through the deposit and return system; 
b. The Regulation should be amended to make the provision on QR labelling optional, as the technologies 
for labelling all packaging with QR codes are limited and incompatible with all types of packaging. 
 
3) Re-use and refill targets (Article 26 of the Regulation) 
We agree that reusable packaging is part of the solution to reduc ing plastic pollution and greenhouse gas 
(GHG) emissions. At the same time, re-use targets should be set in such a way as to take account of 
geographical, environmental, economic, and infrastructure factors. That is why it is important to have a wide 
range of re-use as defined by the Ellen MacArthur Foundation1. 
We believe that re-use targets should also include refill-at-home and refill-on-the-go solutions, which are 
included in the Ellen MacArthur Foundation’s definition of re-use. This will encourage manufacturers to offer 
solutions oriented towards not only HORECA or retailers  but also solutions for consumers at home, which 
would lead to widespread re-use on a large scale. 
The re-use and refill targets should be the responsibility of each operator and each Member State in relation 
to the industry in each Member State to avoid free -riding and the competitive disadvantage of the first 
operators who implement. 
We express our concern about the provisions of the Regulation establishing mandatory national re-use and 
refill targets for the categories of beverages referred to in Art icle 26(2) of the Regulation, namely 20% by 
2030 and 80% by 2040. These goals are unrealistic, and re-use and refill of packaging are not always the best 
options from a climate and environmental perspective. 
We would also like to point out that the current provisions of the Regulation can be considered 
discriminatory towards the EU’s beer sector. In this context, we are concerned that the beer sector is being 
treated unfairly , which could negatively impact  the beer industry at the EU level . It is important that 
regulations are fair and do not unduly favor or disadvantage certain sectors.

Proposals for the European Commission to present to the European Parliament and the Council: 
a. The Regulation should be amended to require re-use and refill targets only after impact studies have 
been carried out, considering an analysis of the entire product life cycle. 
b. The Regulation should be amended to require adopting of the methodology for calculating the re-use 
and refill targets by 2025 and not by 31 December 2028 as set out in the Regulation. 
c. The Regulation should be amended to c larify that kegs are reusable packaging and that such should 
be taken into account when calculating the achievement of the re -use targets. This is necessary to 
establish a clear and concrete legislative framework for their position in respect of the re-use targets. 
The beer industry uses a significant number of KEGs, which have a long-life span of decades. They 
contribute substantially to reducing packaging placed on the internal market, which is why this 
contribution must be considered in calculating the achievement of the re-use targets. 
Note: the kegs are designed to be used unlimitedly, depending on the market situation (often hundreds 
of times a year). Kegs are usually used for more than 10 years and can be repaired, recycled, and 
reused. The volume of the kegs varies from 13 liters to 50 liters. A 50 liters kegs counts 100 uses if we 
take 0.5 liters cup as a reference unit. 
d. The Regulation should be amended to include refill-at-home and refill-on-the-go solutions (fountain 
beverages or dispensers) in calculating the achievement of re -use and refill targets. Many of these 
solutions are already being used or tested in certain markets, with excellent results among consumers.  
e. The Regulation should be amended to require the same targets for all alcoholic beverages, without 
exceptions for certain categories of beverages. 
f. The proposed targets should not distinguish between sparkling wines, spirit drinks, and wines, as many 
of these products come in standardized bottles , are consumed locally, and are not exported. In this 
regard, the Regulation should be amended to  set targets of 5% and , respectively,  15% for all 
beverages, not just alcoholic beverages in the form of wines. 
 
4) Targets to prevent the generation of packaging waste (Article 38 of the Regulation) 
We support the targets for preventing the generation of packaging waste proposed in the Regulation for each 
Member State by reducing packaging waste generated per capita by 5% by 2030, 10% by 2035, and 15% by 
2040, as compared to the packaging waste generated per capita in 2018, with a clear methodology and a 
predictable timetable in place.

Proposal for the European Commission to present to the European Parliament and the Council:  
a. The Regulation should be amended to include requirements for the adoption of a methodology at the

5) Deposit and return systems (DRS) (Article 44 of the Regulation)   
In general, we support mandatory deposit and return systems and minimum requirements for efficient and 
effective operation thereof across the EU. Th ese include improving the minimum requirements for the 
retention of unredeemed deposits by the system administrator, the VAT exemption on unredeemed deposits, 
and the ownership of collected materials by the system administrator.  
According to Article 44(2)(a) of the Regulation, the obligation to establish the deposit and return system does 
not apply to packaging for wine, aromatized wine products, and spirit drinks. 
Packaging re -use rules must apply  to all products without discrimination  based on products . Competing 
beverage sectors should not receive preferential treatment through EU -imposed exceptions to reuse targets 
and obligations in relation to the deposit and return systems. Therefore, the principle of non -discrimination, 
a general principle of EU l aw, should be respected , and products should only be treated differently for  
objective reasons. 
In this regard, we consider that the exemption provided in Article 44(2)(a) for  the packaging of wines, 
aromatized wine products , and spirit drinks is not justi fied, as it is not compatible with the objective of 
ensuring equal and non -discriminatory treatment for all producers and the requirements of Article 44(1) 
should not be limited to certain categories of beverages only. In addition, the possibility of exten ding those 
requirements to other types of packaging or categories of beverages in the future should be included. 
Given that the environmental policy purpose is based on a horizontal strategy that prevents and reduces 
waste, there is no justification for gr anting a priori exemptions to categories of alcoholic beverages, such as 
packaging for wine, wine-derived products, spirit drinks, milk, and dairy products as listed in part XVI of Annex 
I to Regulation (EU) No 1308/2013 of the European Parliament and of the Council, as specified in the proposal 
for a Regulation COM (2022)677 from 30.11.2022. 
The Regulation provides no justification for the differentiation between wines, spirits , and other alcoholic 
beverages. In addition, this differentiation is contrary to the Regulation’s environmental objectives, which 
include reducing as much as possible the total amount of packaging placed on the EU market and, by 
extension, the resulting waste. Under this regulation, excluding certain sectors from implementing re -use 
targets is unfair unless it would negatively impact the environment. 
Proposals for the European Commission to present to the European Parliament and the Council:  
a. To build strong deposit and return systems in the EU, the Regulation should be amended to apply to 
all beverage packaging, including wine and spirit drinks. In Romania, these packaging are already 
included in the deposit and return system by law. Moreover, other systems in the EU that have much 
higher collection and recycling performance, include wine and spirit drinks packaging. 
b. All deposit and return systems should include the access to material mechanism described above to 
close the loop in a truly circular economy. At the same time, the minimum requirements for operation

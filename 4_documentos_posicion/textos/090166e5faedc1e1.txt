F.E.E.D.M. Statement 
EU proposal for a revision of the EU legislation on Packaging and Packaging Waste 
F.E.E.D.M. welcomes this public consultation and the opportunity to provide comments on the 
Commission’s proposal for a revision of the EU legislation on Packaging and Packaging Waste. 
F.E.E.D.M. represents the interests of the European honey packers and distributors, mainly 
small and medium -sized enterprises (SME). The priority of the association is to defend and 
promote honey as a pure and natural product. The association supports good apiculture 
practices and aims at improving the quality st andards of the product. The members of 
F.E.E.D.M. commit themselves to the integrity and quality of the products, which are subject 
to their business. 
 
Planned ban on single -use portion packs in the HORECA sector (hotel, restaurant, 
catering): 
F.E.E.D.M’s proposal is for a continued allowance of single-use honey portion packs, 
especially in sensitive areas (e.g. hospital, military). Reasonable transition periods 
against food waste are essential. 
 
 
Cost increase and logistical disadvantages  
The Commission’s proposal to ban single-use packaging in the HORECA sector would have a 
negative impact on companies and consumers. Additionally, it would require major changes in 
the supply chain. New recycling schemes, for example small reusable glass jars, would lead 
to a significant increase in c osts especially for SMEs, which will then be passed on to the 
consumers. 
Compared to plastics, the transport of glass containe rs is much more space  and energy 
consuming. T he transport weight is many times higher, even higher than the food portion 
contained. Additionally, there is a risk of breakage with glass packaging, which - added on the 
risk of injury - can lead to high losses during transport. The increased energy required for the 
production of small glass packaging and the high energy costs for cleaning reusable containers 
must also be taken into account. Mandatory use of reusable containers made of glass, could 
also lead to difficulties in availability and thus to problems in the supply chain. 
Single-use portion packaging already consists of materials with a high recyclable content up 
to 100 % - without the use of composite films - and can therefore already be integrated into the 
recycling loop. In case of individual packaging, the ratio of packaging weight to the food portion 
is usually much more favourable than for larger packaging. This makes transport even more 
efficient.

Limitation of the variety of honey products 
In case of a ban of single-use packaging, the option of choice would be to move towards larger 
honey dispensers with a pump. Dispensers would be hygienic and also easy for consumers to 
use. However, this would result to the use of solely liquid-form honey in the HORECA sector 
since some more solid formats of honey could not be placed in this type of dispenser. This 
would then lead to discrimination against the honey packers that provide a more solid form of 
honey. 
 
Food safety and food waste concerns 
Single-portion packaging in the HORECA sector develops a high standard of hygiene that 
cannot be maintained in case of larger packaging. It ensures moisture stability and contributes 
to microbiological safety. It helps to keep the freshness and quality of the honey as it is sealed 
and protected from external contamination sources such as air, moisture, and light. This helps 
to prolong the shelf life of the honey and also ensures that it retains its natural aroma and 
flavour. The single-portion packaging also makes a clear lab elling for each portion possible 
and thus contributes to a transparent flow of information for consumers. Additionally, and most 
importantly, single -use portion packs support portion control, which prevents excessive 
portioning and thus unnecessary food waste. 
 
Need of single-use portion packs in sensitive areas 
Single-use portion packs are essential, especially in areas such as hospitals and the military 
field. In the field operation, the use of glass containers is not feasible due to transport weight 
and risk of breakage. When supplying patients in hospitals, the high hygiene standards must 
be maintained, which can only be guaranteed by single-use portion packs. F.E.E.D.M. 
therefore demands that single-use portion packs are continued to be allowed in sensitive areas 
such as hospitals and the military field. 
 
Transition periods 
In case of a ban of single -use packaging, F.E.E.D.M calls for a reasonable transition period 
allowing stocks of single-use portion packs to be used up . Companies should also be given 
time to gradually adapt to the new regulation. Furthermore, with respect to sustainability goals, 
a constant (re)evaluation of the status quo is needed in order to monitor if the outcome of the 
regulation results in not only decreased packaging waste but increased food waste.  If this 
would be the case, adjustments should be made possible.

Conclusion 
In conclusion, F.E.E.D.M. embraces the Commission’s proposal to decrease the amount of 
packaging and packaging waste . However, s ingle-use packaging for honey offers crucial 
benefits for companies and consumers in the areas of food safety, cost-effectiveness, control 
of food waste and guaranteed hygienic standards in sensitive areas . Therefore, F.E.E.D.M. 
calls for the continued allowance of single-use portion packs for honey, especially in sensitive 
areas. In case of a ban of single-use packaging , F.E.E.D.M stresses the importance of 
providing a reasonable transition period, allowing single-use portion pack stocks to be used 
up. This would give companies the time to align with the new regulation and avoid wa ste of 
packaged products that are not compliant anymore.

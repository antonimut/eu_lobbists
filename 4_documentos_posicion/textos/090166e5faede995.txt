Proposal for a Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation – Recycling of XPS packaging shows that a 
‘negative list’ would be an obstacle to realising the circular economy 
 
Brussels, April 2023 
 
The EU co -legislators are currently discussing the above -mentioned (PPWR) proposal of the European 
Commission (EC). We fully support its objectives, including preventing or reducing adverse impacts on the 
environment, and in particular increasing recycling and the use of recycled materials  while reducing other 
forms of recovery and disposal – after all options to prevent waste have been exhausted. To these ends, our 
industry has been working towards all packaging being economically recyclable by 2030 or earlier. 
 
An unofficial leaked draft of the proposal included a pre-determined “negative list ” with disparate 
“characteristics” purportedly preventing or inhibiting recycling. The EC had not formally and comprehensively 
consulted on this list, and its original source and logic have remained unclear. The EC has observed that 
"[t]here is currently no evidence that these packaging design features/ materials will not become recyclable 
by 2030 as considerable efforts are being made by industry to enable this" ( impact assessment, part 2 , p. 
469). We welcome that, after receiving evidence in response to the leak, the EC decided not to include such 
a list in its proposal – for good reason, as also the example of extruded polystyrene (XPS) shows.  
 
XPS is commonly used to produce foamed food trays for raw meat, fish and tofu. Its blanket inclusion in a 
negative list  would discriminate against a material that  is recyclable, already recycled in practice  and 
generally sustainable  (please see below)  – without sufficient justification and proper process. The mere 
assumptions apparently underlying the negative list are clearly outdated in light of recent progress.  
 
This progress is part of the increasing recycling of PS in general. It starts with the design for recycling, which 
is underpinned by RecyClass guidelines for multiple key PS packaging formats. Tests are ongoing to develop 
a guideline for XPS food trays this year. As other styrenics, PS and XPS can be efficiently sorted with existing 
technology. McKinsey found in a survey of mechanical recyclers  across Europe in 2020 that 33% of them 
process PS (figure 2). In 2022, the Joint Research Centre (JRC) of the EC documented a midrange recycling 
rate of 40% for PS (and expanded PS; p. 21). As national examples, a bout two thirds of the PS household 
packaging waste is already sorted for recycling in Germany, according to a report produced in 2021 for the 
Federal Environment Agency  (UBA; p. 132). French producer responsibility orga nisation (PRO) Citeo 
announced the establishment of a recycling channel  for PS household packaging waste in December 2022. 
For the Netherlands, a 2021 report for the municipal waste association NVRD  observes that PS can be 
assessed as well recyclable when its chemical recycling starts in 2023 (p. 23).  
XPS food trays are already, or will be, separately collected at scale  for recycling. For example, the French 
Ministry of Ecological Transition anticipates achieving 100% recyclability in the meat packaging sector by 
2025, also based on increasing PS recycling (p. 16). Corepla, the consortium responsible for the recovery of 
plastic packaging in Italy, has started collecting and sorting XPS food trays (next to PS) for recycling this year, 
after demon strating the their closed-loop recyclability  in 2022 . Spanish PRO Ecoembes also separately 
collects yoghurt pots and PS food trays with a view to recycling. Belgium has included XPS trays in its separate 
collection for recycling in the ’extended blue bag’, and shows just how economical the recycling o f PS and 
XPS is, by demanding the second lowest fee for them among all plastic packaging in 2023 (after transparent 
colourless PET bottles), based on the so-called net cost principle. Swedish Plastic Recycling will start collecting 
and sorting all household plastic packaging waste for recycling, including foamed PS, in mid-2023 to 2025. In 
Portugal, PS and XPS packaging are also separately collected.  Austria will include PS yoghurt pots and XPS 
trays in its separate collection for recycling from 2023  and has just broken ground for the matching sorting 
capacity. Ireland has also included typical XPS applications like meat trays in its separate collection.

Furthermore, XPS food trays have already been commercialised with up to 50% post -consumer recycled 
content.  Their – indispensable – safety has been demonstrated and recently corroborated in a peer-reviewed 
study. This is facilitated by the intrinsic properties of PS , which make it very insensitive to processing (e.g. 
extrusion), allowing for many mechanical recycling loops, and ‘ low diffusion’, which has been confirmed by 
a peer-reviewed study and means that it absorbs and releases little contaminant during use and recycling . 
The new EU Regulation 2022/1616 allows the use of safe  recycled PS  as food contact material ( FCM) 
produced with so -called novel recycling technologies , after completing a notification and while complying 
with stringent monitoring and reporting obligations.  
 
In light of all this, pre-determining a negative list based on assumptions and without independent technical 
analysis and proper consultation would inadvertently contradict the general regime under the PPWR to 
ensure recyclability, the beneficial effects of the  ongoing standardisation work that the EC has mandated , 
until August 2025, including for PS trays,  as well as broader EU policy. Even if the PPWR in theory allowed 
rebutting presumption of non -recyclability of a such negative list , its chilling effects  in practice would 
jeopardise all the already ongoing work and investments  in design, collection, sorting and recycling , 
inadvertently leading to less recycling as a self-fulfilling prophecy. For similar reasons, over 60 associations 
from the packaging value chain have opposed it. 
 
Moreover, singling-out XPS would lead to regrettable substitution. A 2019 peer-reviewed life-cycle analysis 
(LCA) by Fraunhofer found that mono -material XPS trays have the lowest environmental impact compared 
to viable alternatives – even without considering the significant, more recent progress with their recycling.  
 
Therefore, we welcome that the ENVI Rapporteur has not proposed such a negative list in her draft report, 
and call on all co-legislators to refrain from introducing any such list. We appreciate that the EC has publicly 
explained the compelling reasons against including it in its proposal  (at 53:00) and ask the EC to continue 
defending its right decision, if necessary.  
 
Rather, the general rules should apply without discrimination. The PPWR should provide the EC with a clear 
timeframe for its delegated acts, which gives packaging producers and users a reasonable transition period, 
whilst ensuring that packaging is recyclable as quickly as possible. The signal sent by the general regime of 
the PPWR will already have a positive effect by encouraging ongoing and further increasing recycling efforts.

Metsä Group’s position on Packaging and Packaging Waste 
Directive revision 
Metsä Group welcomes the Circular Economy Action Plan and the aim to contribute to the circular 
economy through the review of the requirements for packaging and packaging waste. We believe 
it is important to ensure that the provisions set out measures that are meaningful, achievable and 
support the functioning of the internal market and free movement of goods while preventing the 
possible negative impact of packaging and packaging waste on the environment and human 
health. 
In particular, we believe that recyclable single -use and reusable packaging should be seen as 
complementary solutions, and that materials’ life-cycle impact assessments should be use as the 
basis to choose the best packaging solutions from environmental, end -use and context point of 
view case by case. The leg islation should also guide Member Stats in the development of 
ambitious collection and recycling systems, which can be efficient alternatives to the 
Commission’s proposed bans.  
 
Fibre-based packaging supports circular economy, European resilience and climate 
change mitigation 
• The recycling rate of paper and cardboard packaging in the EU is high (> 80%)1. It is possible 
to increase it even further: the key would be to develop and harmonize the waste collection 
infrastructure so that in all EU countries packaging is collected separately per material. 
• A recent study confirms that the current paper-based packaging used in the quick service for 
takeaway offers significant environmental advantages. Switching to reusable alternatives 
would doub le the CO2 emissions and increase freshwater consumption by 50% while 
demanding five times more mineral and resource extraction2. 
 
Recycling and reuse are complementary solutions  
• Recyclable single-use and reusable packaging should be seen as complementary solutions: 
o Recyclable fibre -based packaging is made from renewable raw material and can 
replace fossil based materials and solutions. 
o Detailed and binding reuse targets for various packaging solutions are partly 
unrealistic and favour non-renewable materials which are difficult to recycle.  
• Businesses and operators should have the possibility to choose the best packaging solution 
from environmental, end-use and context point of view case by case, based on material’s life-
cycle impact assessment.  
o This is an overarching principle in the EU waste framework directive.  
o There is no one size fit all solution. Actors should have the freedom to select case-by-
case the best fit for purpose packaging having the lowest environmental footprint 
throughout the life cycle.

o For example, a recent study by the Fraunhofer Institute indicates that the value chain 
based on fresh fibre-based pulp has superior LCA results. One of the end products of 
this value chain is paperboard used in packaging3. 
o Further restrictions should always be proportionate and aimed at encouraging the 
options that deliver the best overall environmental outcome. 
 
Collaboration is key: industry actors should be involved in defining the criteria for 
recyclability 
• It is positive that the recyclability of packaging is approached through the Design for Recycling 
criteria. Industry actors should be deeply involved in defining the Design for Recycling criteria: 
o The performance classes for recyclability ranging from A to E (where E would be 
banned from the market ) can lead to true development of recycling systems 
o Producer responsibility fees for packaging material grades based on recyclability is a 
good incentive to develop and improve recycling. 
 
Product safety and consumer protection is non-negotiable  
• Product and food safety cannot be compromised.  
o Single-use packaging has a key role in preventing food contamination and reducing 
the risk of cross-contamination between users. The increase in the number of reusable 
containers will therefore directly result in an increased risk of food-borne illnesses4.  
• Mandatory recycled content requirement for the plastic part of packaging should not be 
extended to materials which already have high recycling rates and a well-functioning recycling 
system and market.  
 
Ensure uniform Europe-wide regulation on collection and sorting   
• Instead of bans the regulation should guide to the development of collection and recycling 
systems. 
o Separate collection of paper and board is the most effective way to increase the 
circularity of the product and its recyclability potential by keeping the materials clean. 
o Ensuring that materials end up in the appropriate recycling operation  increases the 
valorisation of secondary raw materials and can support more efficient EPR schemes. 
o Mandatory take back scheme have proven to be very efficient in countries where they 
were implemented with very high collecting, sorting and recycling rates (France, UK).

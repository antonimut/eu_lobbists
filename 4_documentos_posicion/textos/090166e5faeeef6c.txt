 INTRODUCTION 
 
Farmindustria thanks the Commission for this opportunity to provide its contribution on 
such an important issue for the sector. The position expressed below is in line with and 
implemented in Confindustria position. 
 
Farmindustria is the Italian National Association of Pharmaceutical Companies, which has 
approximately 200 member companies operating in Italy, both italian-owned and foreign-
owned companies, with over 120 manufacturing sites all over the country and over 68,000 
highly qualified employees. It is member of Confindustria, the European Federation of 
Pharmaceutical Industries and Associations (EFPIA) and the International Federation of 
Pharmaceutical Manufacturers and Associations (IFPMA).  
 
THE PHARMACEUTICAL INDUSTRY 'S COMMITMENT TO ENVIRONMENTAL 
SUSTAINABILITY 
 
The pharmaceutical industry is committed to ecological transition , also thanks to 
important investments aimed at improving the efficient management of resources: water 
treatment, reduction of waste and use of plastic and reduction of energy 
consumption.  In the last ten years the pharmaceutical sector in Italy has reduced energy 
consumption by 44%, a percentage that rises to 51% for consumption related to atmospheric 
emissions.  According to Istat data, it is the sector with the highest percentage of companies 
to have introduced (that introduced?) innovations for reducing the consumption of 
materials or water per unit of product. These results come from growing investments in 
green technologies. In 2015, the Eco-Pharma Stewardship (EPS) was created, which 
is the European program for the intelligent and sustainable management of the 
environmental impact of the medicines throughout their life cycle.  
Back in 1980, Farmindustria – together with the supply chains of both private and public 
pharmacies (Federfarma and Assofarm) and of pharma distributors (ADF and Federfarma 
Servizi) – established Assinde, a centralized system to guarantee the correct 
disposal of expired medicines , considered according to current legislation, ‘medical 
waste requiring special management systems’ 1. This makes Italy the first country in Europe 
to have such organization. 
In 2016, Assinde, with the Ministry of the Environment, signed a framework agreement 
for the management of waste produced in the pharmaceutical manufacturing and 
distribution cycle, that includes all types of waste present in the manufacturing sites. Since

The pharmaceutical industry shares the goal of making Europe the first climate-neutral

SPECIFIC REMARKS FOR THE PHARMACEUTICAL SECTOR AND PROPOSALS  
 
1. RECYCLABILITY OF IMMEDIATE PHARMACEUTICAL PACKAGING- ARTICLE 6  
 
Article 6 of the proposal for a Regulation foresees that immediate pharmaceutical packaging 
(i.e. packaging directly in contact with the medicine) must be recyclable, designed for 
recycling and recycled at scale from 1 st January 2035. It also proposes that packaging can 
be recycled so that the resulting secondary raw materials are of sufficient quality to 
substitute the primary raw materials. 
 
In this respect, the derogation to 2035 in the legislative proposal for immediate 
pharmaceutical packaging is welcomed, but we believe that this derogation should be 
made permanent . In fact, we question the feasibility of such stringent recyclability 
requirements for pharmaceutical packaging for the following reasons:  
 
x Immediate pharmaceutical packaging is subject to specific and detailed 
regulation. The pharmaceutical sector is one of the most regulated in Europe and 
worldwide, operating effectively within an evidence-based regulatory framework. 
Through regulation, from European Medicine Agency (EMA), it is required that the 
selected packaging proves to support and maintain product quality, safety, efficacy, 
sterility, purity, identity. Any changes to the current regulatory framework must 
be aligned with existing regulations and guidelines governing the packaging

x Immediate pharmaceutical packaging must be approved by the Medicines 
Regulatory Agencies. Technical description of the immediate packaging is part of the 
pharmaceutical quality documents (Module 2.3 and 3) of the Marketing Authorisation 
Application (MAA) to be submitted to and assessed by the Competent Health 
Authorities. Any change in the pharmaceutical packaging would lead to the submission 
of a considerable number of applications for variations of the Marketing Authorisation 
(MA) for pharmaceutical products marketed in Europe, with a huge bureaucratic 
burden for the EMA and for all the National Regulatory Authorities (AIFA in 
Italy) with potential slowdowns in activities related to the protection of public health 
(i.e., evaluation, approval and supervision of aspects of quality, efficacy and safety of 
medicines). 
 
x Immediate pharmaceutical packaging is contaminated with the medicine and 
can compromise waste stream.  
In some cases, the immediate packaging is ‘contaminated’ with the medicine (i.e., the 
vial or bottle when the medicine is a liquid). In such cases, placing the packaging in a 
defined waste recycling stream would contaminate the stream and compromise 
the recyclability of the other waste.  Additionally, the direct contact between the 
immediate packaging and the active substance/biological product leads to 
contamination of the immediate packaging by residues of active 
pharmaceutical ingredients or impurities.  Secondary raw materials resulting 
from possible recycling of materials may not be of sufficient quality to substitute the 
primary raw materials of the immediate packaging. This poses a real threat of 
contamination, and this is especially relevant in case of highly potent or mutagenic 
substances such as cytotoxic agents. 
 
x The development of a new (or modified) immediate  pharmaceutical packaging 
is a long and complex path. 
The normal process for introducing a new immediate packaging material for medicinal 
products already on the market would likely follow the process outlined in Figure 1. To 
satisfy the evidence requirement that demonstrates that there is no negative impact to 
the product quality, safety and efficacy, a 10-year development is usually required for 
immediate packaging, whereas a 5-year (or less) accelerated pathway for outer packaging 
could be provided for. The immediate packaging must: prevent contamination by 
external agents; not have any characteristics that may alter the medicinal product; 
effectively withstand external environmental conditions (light, humidity, etc.) and 
ensure adequate protection during product handling. For this reason, numerous tests are 
required for the development of pharmaceutical packaging, including, for example, 
aspects related to stability, permeability, cytotoxicity, biocompatibility, and suitability of 
container closure, etc.

x Pharmaceutical packaging may have non-recyclable components. 
The proposal for a Regulation states that each individual part of a packaging must be as 
recyclable as any other to compose a recyclable packaging.  
However, pharmaceutical products, due to the technical requirements such as cold chain 
requirements, sterile packaging, etc., use particular material like rubber stoppers and 
composite materials providing essential functionality to the packaging, and which are 
often difficult to recycle (e.g.: borosilicate glass used in vials because of the purity and 
high chemical resistance, stainless-steel needles attached to or provided with a syringe, 
flip-off caps used to allow easy recognition of the contained product during production 
and when administering to the patient). Consequently, the presence of these non-
recyclable materials/components in immediate pharmaceutical packaging would 
preclude their marketability, putting at risk patients' access to effective and safe 
medicines. 
 
Finally, the criteria that pharmaceutical companies should follow for the implementation of 
recyclable immediate packaging are not yet known, as they will be  defined by the EU 
Commission by means of Delegated Acts for which a date of publication has not been 
indicated yet.    
 
 
Therefore, for the reasons set out above, in order to avoid the risk that patients are denied 
access to safe and effective medicines because their packaging is not recyclable, a 
permanent derogation for pharmaceutical immediate packaging from the 
application of the recyclability requirements set out in Article 6 would be desirable.  
 
 
 
2. MINIMUM RECYCLED CONTENT OF PLASTIC PACKAGING –  ARTICLE  7   
 
Article 7 of the proposal for a Regulation indicates the minimum percentage of recycled 
content in plastic packaging. Paragraph 3 of Article 7 states that immediate pharmaceutical 
packaging and certain types of external pharmaceutical packaging (i.e., those with specific 
requirements to preserve the quality of the medicinal product) are exempt from the 

 
 5 
obligation of recycled content and paragraph 9 states that this exemption is to be revised by 
1 January 2028. 
 
Furthermore, this revision raises concerns since, given the high quality and purity 
requirements of medicines, there is no evidence of the use of recycled plastics in 
the immediate packaging of the same . In addition, the current FDA guideline 
‘Guidance for Industry Container Closure Systems for Packaging Human Medicines and 
Biologics’ also explicitly prohibits the use of recycled plastic in the  production of 
immediate pharmaceutical packaging.   
 
 
Therefore, given the reasons set out above, it is necessary to make  the 
exemption from the minimum recycled content requirement  in pharmaceutical 
packaging permanent and to eliminate the possibility of revising the derogation 
in 2028. 
 
 
 
 
3. LABELLING OF PACKAGING - ARTICLE 11 
 
Article 11 of the proposal for a Regulation foresees that packaging shall be marked with a 
label containing information on its material composition. Specifications for the labelling 
requirements and format will be defined by delegated Acts and will apply without 
distinction to all packaging.  
 
As highlighted, medicines are subject to specific, strict, and detailed regulations coming 
from the implementation of Community legislation (Italian Law Decree 219/2006 and 
subsequent amendments), which also provide indications on the information to be reported 
on the immediate and outer packaging, including those on the disposal of the medicinal 
product. In particular, Article 73, paragraph 1, letter l) of Law Decree 219/2006, foresees, 
among others, that the following particular should appear on the outer or immediate 
packaging, ‘..., special precautions to be taken for the disposal of unused medicinal 
products or waste derived from medicinal products, as well as reference to any 
appropriate collection system in place’.  
Even the package leaflet (art. 76 of the aforementioned Law Decree) contains information 
regarding the disposal of the medicine (such as ‘ D o n o t t h r o w a n y m e d i c i n e i n t o 
wastewater and household waste. Ask your pharmacist how to dispose of medicines you 
no longer use. This will help protect the environment’). 
 
The inclusion of a new environmental symbol/pictogram in the packaging of medicines 
would entail the submission of a considerable number of applications for variations to the 
labelling and packaging leaflet for all pharmaceutical products marketed in Italy and 
E u r o p e , w i t h a bureaucratic burden on the activities of the regulatory bodies 
responsible for approving the variations (AIFA  for medicines authorized with 
national, Mutual Recognition and Decentralized procedures, EMA for those authorized 
with centralized procedure), as well as on companies  
 
 
 6 
In this regard, the Italian Ministry of the Environment  and  Energy Safety  - 
recognizing that medicines are subject to stringent and specific rules -  has established  
that medicines are exempted from the obligation of environmental labelling,  which 
in Italy came into force from 1 st January 2023 and applies to all packaging ( Clarification 
Note Nov 2022).  
 
In addition, as regards immediate pharmaceutical packaging, the introduction of new 
labelling requirements may not be technically feasible for small packages such as ampoules 
and small blisters which must contain the information required by the current regulatory 
framework (such as, for example, the name of the product, batch number and expiration 
date) that must be readable for patients.  
 
 
We therefore believe, on the basis of the reasons laid out above, that pharmaceutical 
packaging should be excluded from the scope of Article 11 and thus exempted from 
the application of environmental labelling in line with existing national legislation.

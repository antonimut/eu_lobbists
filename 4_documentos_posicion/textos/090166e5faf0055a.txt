Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation: 
Creating an ambitious policy framework to drive circularity of packaging while safeguarding 
the free movement of goods. 
 
Position Paper 
 
The LEGO Group aims to make a positive impact on the planet our children will inherit and is committed 
to creating more sustainable and circular products and packaging, reducing our environmental footprint 
and inspiring children to learn about sustainability while co-creating solutions with and for them. As part 
of our sustainability efforts, our company embarked on an ambitious journey to  phase out single -use 
plastic packaging and transition towards paper -based packagin g. We have chosen the paper -based 
solutions to ensure that our materials come from responsible and renewable resources and to improve 
the recyclability of our packaging. 
 
The LEGO Group, therefore, welcomes the proposal for a Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation, 
which should create a policy framework based on robust scientific evidence and data. Such a framework 
should allow companies to innovate and find more sustainable solutions, and recyclers to collect more, 
improve efficiency and create more valuable recycled materials. The biggest challenge, however, will be 
to drive the circularity of packaging through material-specific measures and greater EU harmonisation. 
The imposition of national-specific requirements has created barriers to the free movement of goods and 
has led to negative environmental effects such as increased material needs for additional labelling and 
additional waste produced.   
 
We therefore invite the European policymakers to consider the following policy recommendations. 
 
1. The new legal format should support the free movement of goods in the EU. 
At the LEGO Group, we welcome the change of the EU packaging legislation from a Directive to a 
Regulation. Changing the legal format is the only way to address existing fragmentation of the EU Single 
Market and to tackle the diverse and contrasting measures put in place by Member States, which ha ve 
created barriers to the free movement of packaged goods. The n ew Regulation will thus contribute to 
setting clear and harmonised rules, ultimately creating a level playing field and providing legal certainty 
for economic operators across the EU.  
2. Involvement of industry in the development of Design for Recycling criteria should be anchored 
in the Regulation. 
We welcome the proposal to develop EU Design for Recycling criteria to measure the compliance of 
packaging with recyclability requirements. We strongly believe that material- and format-specific criteria 
that reflect the actual recycling infrastructure will be the most appropriate and effective tool to assess

recyclability. The involvement of industry in the development of these criteria will be key to ensure their 
feasibility and enforceability. This involvement should be explicit and anchored in the legislation. 
3. Fibre-based packaging should be tested using a harmonised methodology. 
All fibre -based packa ging whose recyclability is in question, such as coated papers, alternative fibre 
packaging, or paper cups and tubes, should be tested using an agreed laboratory methodology, that is 
material specific, representative of the industrial paper processing and harmonised across the EU. The 
European Commission should look towards well-established and emerging methodologies and standards, 
such as the CEPI Recyclability Laboratory Test Method, on which to build the new criteria. We would also 
welcome clarity and certainty on an evaluation methodology that provides clear guidelines for 
interpretation of results.  
Some packaging types, such as corrugated boxes and uncoated or clay-coated carton boards, that do not 
come into contact with contaminants such as food, dirt and oil could be generally categorised as recyclable 
and should not require testing. 
4. Labels should be harmonised, simplified and digital. 
National disparities in sorting instructions and labelling requirements for packa ging create a major 
challenge for any company operating across multiple markets. The LEGO group works with a global supply 
chain and does not design packaging specific to each market , which h elps cut down on waste by 
maximising efficiencies and streamlining logistics to support our sustainability ambitions.  
We strongly advise against Member States mandating their own labelling systems in the areas covered 
by the new Regulation to safeguard the free movement of packaged goods, driving economies of scale 
and greater competitiveness across Europe . The LEGO Group re lies on the European legislators to 
implement a coherent approach to mandatory and voluntary product labelling and to advance the 
transition to digital labels.  
As such, we support  the ambition to implement a Digital Product Passport  established under the 
Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation. A streamlined digital label would allow us to incorporate 
detailed and up-to-date instructions for collecting, sorting, reuse, and recycling of our packaging, as well 
as to provide information on local Extended Producer Responsibility schemes in multiple languages and 
formats without a need to design packaging specific to each market. 
For labels that need to continue to be provided in a physical f ormat – for consumer information and 
accessibility – we call on the European legislators to simplify labelling requirements with pictograms  
which could be complemented by further information to be provided through digital means.

About LEGO’s transition to a paper-based packaging solution: 
In recent years, the LEGO Group has been called on by children around the globe to move towards 
sustainable solutions and contribute to the achievement of climate and environmental objectives. Co -
creating with children, the LEGO Group has developed an alternative to single -use plastic bags, namely 
recyclable paper-based pre-pack bags. Our company is carrying out significant investments to change our 
packing lines in our European factory in Kladno (Czechia) and soon in our other factories around the world, 
to make the transition to paper -based packaging happen. Our paper -based pre-pack bags are tested by 
external and independent laboratories to ensure their recyclability, and are verified as recyclable in the US, 
Canada and the EU. We chose to invest in a paper-based solution because it is a highly recyclable solution, 
it is more likely to be recycled across the globe than other materials, it is made from renewable resources, 
and it is one of the safest options a vailable to meet the stringent safety standards necessary to protect 
toys to be safely played with by children.

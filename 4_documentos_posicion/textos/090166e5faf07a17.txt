FruitVegetablesEUROPE (EUCOFEL) is the European private sectoral Association 
representing the European Fruit and Vegetables producers and exporters at the EU 
level. FruitVegetablesEUROPE’s members are national and regional Federations and 
Associations (mainly PO and APO) and companies from the main European producing 
countries (France, Germany, Greece, Italy, Poland, Portugal, and Spain). 
FruitVegetablesEUROPE’s direct members account for more than 4,500 companies.  
The European Commission presented on the 30th November, the draft Regulation of the 
European Parliament and of the Council on packaging and packaging waste, amending 
Regulation (EU) 2019/1020 (on market surveillance and product compliance) and 
Directive (EU) 2019/904 (on reducing the impact of certain plastic products on the 
environment) and repealing Directive 94/62/EC (on packaging and packaging waste). By 
2030, all packaging should be re -used or recycled, according to the new Circular 
Economy Action Plan. In order to achieve this objective, all packaging materials should 
be valued in the pursuit of the circular economy goal, with an emphasis on eco-design, 
current and future recycling technologies, and strong measures to combat dispersion. 
A feedback period on the proposal has opened on the 1st December 2022 until the 24th 
April 2023 to which FruitVegetablesEUROPE and its members wish to express their key 
considerations as follows: 
FruitVegetablesEUROPE and its members are intensely committed and involved 
in the objectives of the European Green Deal and the new EU circular economy 
action plan, to achieve sustainable and circular solutions while strengthening the 
competitiveness of the Fruit and Vegetable sector. 
FruitVegetablesEUROPE and its members supports the Commission's view on 
harmonised rules, avoiding the complexity of diverging legislation between Member 
States and ensuring the proper functioning of the EU single market by emphasising 
harmonised labelling, packaging, and sorting without compromising food safety and 
hygiene standards in all Member States. 
FruitVegetablesEUROPE and its members believe that the implementation of the 
projected measures should not be cross-cutting but should reflect the specificities 
of individual sectors and Member States, particularly the Fruit and Vegetables 
sector.

FruitVegetablesEUROPE and its members stresses that new rules represent new 
costs for producers. Producers need to make investments for the transition to new 
packaging solutions, ensuring security of product supply and coheren ce with other 
packaging-related legislation, such as the Waste Framework legislation, the Single-
Use Plastics Directive and the Food Contact Materials legislation. 
FruitVegetablesEUROPE and its members have concerns about the impact on the 
environment, consumers and competitiveness of the EU packaging regulation 
and stress the importance of supporting cooperation between all actors in the 
packaging value chain, including packaging producers, packaging users, retailers, 
waste management operators, public authorities, and of course, consumers. 
FruitVegetablesEUROPE and its members have HIGHLY CONCERNS AND 
DISAGREES WITH THE DISCRIMINATORY APPROACH TOWARDS THE FRUIT 
AND VEGETABLES SECTOR. Under the new proposal, art.22 para 1 «Restrictions 
on use of certain packaging formats», states that «1. Economic operators shall not 
place on the market packaging in the formats and for the purposes listed in Annex V», 
which through Annex V (2) restricts the use of packaging formats for fresh fruit and 
vegetables for less than 1.5 kg: «2. Single use packaging for less than 1.5 kg fresh 
fruit and vegetables, unless there is a demonstrated need to avoid water loss or 
turgidity loss, microbiological hazards or physical shocks». 
We see no justification to this discriminatory approach without any plausible 
justification by the European C ommission to fruit and vegetables, the only sector 
subject to such restrictions , as there are many biodegradables, recycled or 
compostable alternatives, and other new sustainable alternatives will emerge in the 
future. 
FruitVegetablesEUROPE and its members call for a revision  or removal of the 
above provisions, as the absence of a list of Fruit and Vegetables covered may lead 
to different interpretations and divergences in the application of the Regulation i n 
Member States. 
FruitVegetablesEUROPE and its members raise awareness about the role that 
packaging plays in the EU food economy and in the Fruit and Vegetables supply 
chain. Packaging plays a central role in extending Fruit and Vegetables shelf life 
preventing significant product damage, loss, deterioration, or food losses and 
consequently preventing the waste of resources associated.  
FruitVegetablesEUROPE and its members highlights the importance of packaging 
for a fair competitive European market , allo wing a commercial and marketing 
differentiation. Packaging provides characteristics on the products marketed, such as 
indication of the origin, food safety, traceability, quality, sustainability, and production 
methods - characteristics that allows the producer to differentiate their products and 
the consumer to make their purchasing choice clearer. 
FruitVegetablesEUROPE and its members shares the concerns of the European 
Economic and Social Committee on the impact on the environment, consumers 
and industrial competitiveness of the EU plastic and packaging reforms , shared 
during the public hearing held on 9 December 2022: “the packaging used to preserve 
something usually represents significantly fewer  resources and has a far 
lower intrinsic value than the product it is protecting, for example, in the food business, 
30% of all food produced worldwide is lost or wasted along the supply chain: as a 
result, product loss due to poor packaging is likely to have much more negative

FruitVegetablesEUROPE and its members urge the European Commission to reflect 
the needs of the EU Fruit and Vegetable sector  by using functional  and 
environmentally more beneficial packaging rules  that ensures food waste 
reduction targets, without compromising the supply of Fruit and Vegetables with high 
freshness, safety, and hygiene standards  to EU consumers for healthy and 
sustainable diets. 
FruitVegetablesEUROPE and its members believe that  EU packaging and 
Packaging Waste regulation should result in positive outcomes, supported by 
continuous environmental footprint analyses.  
FruitVegetablesEUROPE and its members call  for realistic timelines  and 
consistency with other EU legislations to allow a sufficiently long and smooth 
transition period for the application of new rules along the packaging value chain, 
facilitating the transition of sectors to a new packaging model. 
 
Conclusion  
FruitVegetablesEUROPE and its members urge policymakers to take fair and non -
discriminatory decisions to the EU Fruit and Vegetables sector. EU Fruit and Vegetables 
producers are under of excessive regulation with the implementation of the new CAP 
(2023-2027), environmental regulations under negotiation ( Packaging and Packaging 
Waste, Nature Restoration and Sustainable Use of Plant Protection Products proposals), 
upcoming regulations ( Soil Health Law, expected Q2 2023, and the Legislative 
Framework for Sustainable Food Systems, expected Q4 2023), the Revised Waste 
Legislation from 2018 - plus new legally binding food waste targets for EU member states 
later this year, and other national regulatory developments.  
The sector is facing a sensitive period that puts European agri-food producers under 
excessive pressure, being also affected by adverse weather conditions (droughts and 
floods ), the Russia's invasion of Ukraine, the high energy and commodity prices, and 
high inflation, making 2023 and the time ahead a difficult period for adapting this major 
legislative package to the reality of production and the EU market, at the same time as 
the sector faces a period of food insecurity and difficulties in supplying EU markets. 
FruitVegetablesEUROPE and its members are committed to achieve the EU's climate 
neutrality target by 2050, advocating that the new EU packaging law should maintain the 
competitiveness of the European agri-food market with science-based solutions and an 
economic and social impact assessment with real results for sustainability. We will 
continue to actively pursue the proposal on packaging and packaging waste through the 
European Parliament and the Council under the ordinary legislative procedure.

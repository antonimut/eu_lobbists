The position of Ball Beverage Packaging   
on the EU Packaging & Packaging Waste Regulation (PPWR) 
 
Ball Beverage Packaging welcomes the European Commission's Packaging and Packaging Waste 
Regulation (PPWR) published on November 30th 2022 and recognizes the effort to help make Europe less 
dependent on primary resources and external suppliers and put the packaging sector on track for climate 
neutrality by 2050. 
At Ball, we are committed to a circular economy and are actively working towards fostering closed product 
loop - bottle-to-bottle and can-to-can – recycling, whose annual positive environmental impact increases 
exponentially year after year and will significantly contribute to the EU climate targets.  Our goal is to 
achieve at least 90% recycling rates and  increase the recycled material content in our aluminium 
packaging to 85% by 2030 , in the regions we operate . In Europe, our industry already committed to 
achieve near 100% recycling rates for aluminium beverage cans by 2030 . We cannot achieve this alone 
and welcome the chance to collaborate with EU policymakers to make this proposal more ambitious , 
science-based and fit to contribute to the EU Green Deal targets. 
 
Ball urges EU policy makers to: 
 
1. REUSE QUOTAS: 
Exempt highly circular beverage packaging from the calculation of reuse quotas if it is collected at 
least at 90% in a DRS  in clearly separate waste streams and it achieves high mandatory recycled 
content rates (e.g. 80% recycled content) 
WHY: Obliging every producer, including SMEs, to switch to a potentially more environmentally 
damaging business model, is unproportional and might undermine (investments in) highly circular 
packaging collected in a DRS  and achieving high recycled content within 3 years of implementing 
mandatory DRS across the EU. 
2. DRS:  
a. Support mandatory all-inclusive deposit return schemes (DRS) for drinks containers of all 
materials.  
WHY: Limiting mandatory DRS t o plastic beverage bottles and aluminium and metal  beverage 
containers will create a discriminatory and disruptive economic and environmental impact on the 
market and potentially more single-use glass bottles in the EU with increased carbon emissions. 
b. At a minimum, set a 90% separate collection for recycling target by 2029 for beverage container 
packaging of all materials , regardless of whether collected in a DRS or in an EPR system, and 
adopt DRS in Member States whose collection performance fails to meet interim milestones 
needed to attain the 90% target. 
WHY: The 90% target will ensure that each country introduces a robust separate collection system 
focused not only on attaining the targets and reducing litter, but also at re -circulating the materials 
into closed loops, thereby preventing waste and suppl anting virgin inputs with secondary recycled 
content several times over.

c. Include provisions for a variable deposit and maintain a retailer take-back obligation in 
the Minimum Requirements for DRS. WHY: Variable deposits prevent a producer and consumer 
shift to larger volume containers with unintended consequences. A retailer takes back obligation 
ensures that a large share of beverage containers put on the market in a Member State will be 
returned at or closely to the point of sale of beverages. 
 
 
1. REUSE QUOTAS 
Ball acknowledges the Commission’s intention to reduce packaging waste by promoting reusable 
packaging, at least where it makes environmentally and economically sense. However, we don’t think that 
mandatory reuse targets as stipulated in article 26, especially when obliging single economic operators, 
i.e. both beverage producers and final distributors, are the right policy tool  to achieve the best 
environmental outcome in an economically viable manner.  
Policy proposals for mandatory DRS by 2029 and then for reuse by 2030 are not aligned and undermine 
industry efforts towards closed loop recycling with minimum 90% collection and high recycled content 
rates, e.g. 80%. 
Obliging each individual producer to shift its business model to reuse by 2030 will not only undermine the  
DRS set up and investments, but also put an excessively high economic burden on producers, espec ially 
SMEs, without any guaranteed environmental benefit. 
DRS-operators in Europe ensure that beverage packaging included in the DRS is already among the best 
performing packaging categories in terms of collection rates and high -quality recycling1. Once DRS is 
implemented across the EU, the potential for very high recycled content rates can be used and full 
circularity be achieved, which will be an important contribution to decarbonization. 
According to article 44 (1)  of the PPWR Member states are ob liged to launch DRS for single use plastic 
beverage bottles and single use metal beverage containers by 2029, if collection rates of 90 % for these 
packaging categories are not achieved through existing collecting systems. The establishment of DRS for 
recyclable beverage packaging in member states will support and strengthen the existing closed ‘bottle 
to bottle’ and ‘can to can’ loop within the EU, which is why we strongly supports the obligation.  
However, the mandatory reuse targets for the same type of beverage packaging will undermine the 
required DRS set -up and put an unreasonable financial burden on the producers as well as affecting 
existing and well -functioning sustainable material loops. Today , refillable and single -use packaging 
typically coexist within the scope of DRS , and flexibility is an essential prerequisite to breweries’ and 
importers’ existence on small and medium sized markets.  
In addition, there is no scientific evidence that reuse systems perform per se better for the environment. 
Reuse is no goal in itself . Without weighing the environmental and economic impact of re fillable versus 
highly recycled  beverage packaging and determi ning the conditions under which reuse results in net  
environmental benefits through comprehensive and scientifically sound impact assessments, one cannot 
assume an automatic benefit.

Instead it is about driving circularity and ensuring that we bring the ecologically most optimized 
packaging on the EU market, both single-use and reusable, to decarbonize materials and contribute the 
EU climate targets under the EU Green Deal. 
Reuse systems should complement highly circular and recycled beverage packaging, as both have their 
advantages in different system conditions, including different consumption and distribution situations.  
Our proposal  is therefore to exempt beverages packaged in highly circular containers such as the 
aluminium beverage can from the calculation of reuse quotas if collected at minimum of 90% in  a DRS 
and achieving mandatory high recycled content rates, e.g. 80%. 
  
2. DRS 
Ball firmly supports the requirement of mandatory DRS for plastic bottles and metal and aluminium 
beverage containers by 2029 as stipulated in article 44, because its implementation in member states will 
reinforce the closed "bottle to bottle" and "can to can" recycling loops already in place within the EU.  
While mandatory DRS is essential to pave the way towards 100% collectio n and closed  product loop 
recycling with very high recycled content for aluminium beverage cans, there is no provision in the PPWR 
proposal for the most carbon intense container material – single-use glass. In addition, packaging for 
wine, aromatised wine, and spirit drinks, predominantly packaged in single-use glass bottles, are exempt 
from this obligation. 
This gives single-use glass bottles a competitive advantage over better performing single-use containers 
such as cans, as well as refillables, and could lead producers to shift materials to single-use glass across 
Europe – at high environmental costs –  thus leading to market distortions. 
As of today, 45 out of 50 existing DRS worldwide  include single-use glass bottles, with highly effective 
examples in Denmark, Estonia and Finland2. Reloop’s Target 90-Report clearly states the benefits of DRS 
plus a separate collection goal of 90% for beverage containers of all materials in terms of reduced litter, 
a lower carbon footprint, and exponential growth of resource efficiency for the EU3. Incorporating glass 
into the mandatory DRS provision would also support the packaging shift towards refillable glass bottles.  
A proportional mandate for DRS should not discriminate against any materials or create a cost advantage 
for certain materials. Instead, it should align with other policy provisions in the proposal, including article 
26 on reuse and refill quotas. 
Therefore, our proposal is to extend the mandatory DRS for single -use plastic beverage bottles and 
metal containers to beverage containers of all materials and sizes . At a minimum  90% separate 
collection for recycling targets should be set by 2029 for beverage container packaging of all materials, 
regardless of whether collected in a DRS or in an EPR system. There should be no exemptions for wine, 
aromatized wine products and spirits. DRS should then be adopted in Member States whose collection 
performance fails to meet interim milestones needed to attain the 90% target.

Ball welcomes the provision of minimum requirements for new DRS  to harmonize the implementation 
of schemes across Europe. However, some key requirements for an effective and efficient DRS are 
missing. The first is a requirement to include beverage containers of all materials, as explained above.  
The second is a requirement to adopt variable deposit levels in a DRS, based on different beverage 
volume sizes. This is key to prevent a shift by producers and consumers to large volume containers, even 
where it’s not desirable, e.g. for portion control and food waste prevent ion. Analysis from Reloop also 
shows that DRS countries with variable deposits perform higher. 4 The third is a retailer take back 
obligation, as proposed by the Commission. This is key to provide consumers with a convenient and highly 
accessible return location and to ensure the take back of a large volume of beverage containers sold in a 
member state. Existing systems that use a return -to-retail approach, where retailers selling drinks are 
legally responsible for redeeming empty containers from consumers and refunding their deposit, have the 
highest return rates globally5.  
Ball Beverage appreciates the opportunity to engage with EU policymakers to share our global experience 
from countries with DRS, where our company operates, to support the design of an efficient scheme that 
will help achieve the European Union's goals of boosting recycling rates, reducing litter, combating climate 
change, and establishing a circular economy.

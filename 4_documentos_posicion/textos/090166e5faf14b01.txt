 
Position Paper on the revision of the Packaging and Packaging Waste 
Regulation (PPWR)  
 
PCEP (the Polyolefin Circular Economy Platform), PCEP brings together actors from 
across the polyolefin value chain, united in our common mission to drive the transition 
to a circular economy for polyolefins - the most commonly used family of plastics. 71.4% 
of plastics packaging in Europe is made of polyolefins, accounting for 71% of collected 
plastic waste. This collected material goes on to be transformed into 84.7% of post-
consumer recycled polyolefins, used today in European products across building and 
construction, packaging, agriculture, automotive, electronics, and other sectors.  
 
Key messages 
• PCEP welcomes the proposal from the European Commission on the revision of 
packaging rules in the EU. PCEP embraces the EU Green deal and agrees and welcomes 
the overall approach to reduce the environmental impact of packaging, without 
forgetting the importance of the internal market for the EU’s competitiveness and 
prosperity. 
• PCEP’s mission is to keep the maximum amount of polyolefin material in the circular 
economy, at its highest value, for as long as possible, which is not necessarily 
application specific (i.e., packaging to high quality non packaging products).  
• PCEP welcomes that the PPWR is recycling technology neutral.  We recognise the 
valuable role that mechanical, physical, and chemical recycling technologies can all play 
when used strategically and in a complimentary fashion. 
• Highly recyclable streams of polyolefin packaging waste should be made available for 
mechanical recycling first, with chemical recycling focusing on the more challenging 
waste streams for mechanical recyclers. 
• The lack of an agreed mass balance methodology for calculating recycled content is 
creating uncertainty and holding back investment decisions. A credible and robust mass 
balance accounting system should be in place by the end of 2023.   
• PCEP support the fuel exempt mass balance calculation methodology to determine 
recycled content.   
• Design for recycling criteria should be adopted in delegated acts before the end of 
2026 so that industry has the time to act ahead of the 2030 deadline that all packaging 
placed on the market must be recyclable. 
• Recycling at scale criteria for the different packaging types should be based on access 
to separate collection systems and sufficient sorting and recycling infrastructure.

• PCEP support the ambitious recycled content targets but believes that setting recycled 
content targets per SKU is unnecessarily restrictive and that targets should be set as an 
average across the plastic packaging placed on the market by an economic operator.   
• The 2040 recycled content targets should be reviewed by the end of 2034, by when 
additional recycled content targets will have been set for other plastic products. This 
will allow for an assessment of the amount of plastic needed to meet the 2040 targets. 
• We believe 100% targets for reuse in Article 26 should be avoided as there will always 
be situations where reuse may not be either technically possible or environmentally 
beneficial.   
• Reuse targets for intra economic operator / linked or partner enterprises need clear 
geographical boundaries setting which limit their scope to the EU. 
• There needs to be cross border compatibility of reuse systems within the EU due to the 
high level of trans-frontier movements that will be involved. 
• The reuse target for pallet wrappings should be removed to reduce the risk of negative 
environmental outcomes due to the lack of viable reusable alternatives.    
• Single-use plastic grouped packaging provides an important packaging function to 
allow the consumer to safely transport items from the place of purchase to home 
without spillage or breakage.  We therefore do not support a restriction on its use. 
• We support the inclusion of a complimentary target in the PPWR for the use of 
sustainably sourced bio-based feedstock in packaging, where this can bring 
environmental benefits by lowering of greenhouse gas emissions. 
Recyclability of packaging 
Design for recycling of packaging is a prerequisite for achieving a circular economy for 
polyolefins, as it is for other polymers and material types.  We therefore welcome the 
requirement that all packaging placed on the market after 1st January 2030 should be 
recyclable.  However, packaging producers need time to adjust their packaging once 
the design for recycling criteria is laid out in the delegated acts.  We ask that a date be 
inserted into the legislation for the adoption of these delegated acts, which should be 
at the latest by the end of 2026.  Recycled content targets for reusable packaging 
should only apply to such packaging placed on the market after 1st January 2030.  This is 
to avoid the unnecessary removal of functional reusable packaging from the market in 
2030. 
The objective of packaging being recycled at scale by 2035 is also welcomed.  Whilst 
the definition in Article 3 is clear, the text in Article 6 (6) is less so.  We propose that 
requirements for recycled at scale relate solely to the required infrastructure to 
facilitate the collection, sorting and recycling of all packaging types listed in Table 1 of 
Annex II.  The requirement for recycling at scale should be that 75% of the Union 
 
population has access to separate collection systems and that there is sufficient 
infrastructure to sort and recycle the collected waste packaging.  In terms of the 
definition, it should be adapted to make it applicable also for commercial and industrial 
packaging and we would recommend the removal of state of the art as this is open to 
interpretation.  Any specific and quantified targets for recycling, and the necessary 
collection and sorting that will be required to meet them, should remain in Article 46.  
Any reporting requirements to monitor targets should be in Article 50.  These two 
articles along with Article 6 should work together to achieve the desired outcomes of 
achieving recycling at scale. 
We would highlight that whilst Table 1 in Annex 2 provides suitable packaging type 
categories for assessing design for recycling, these categories are not suitable for 
reporting on recycling rates as packaging types, in particular for polyolefins, are often 
mixed in the output streams for recycling.  Requiring reporting of wastes against the 
packaging types shown in table 1 in Annex II would require extensive sampling of sorted 
wastes with the data being of little practical use in monitoring recycling or achieving 
circularity. 
We welcome the work within CEN CENELEC TC261 on a harmonised approach to 
recyclability evaluation initiated by the European Commission and resulting from the 
work done within the Circular Plastics Alliance (CPA).  We support the guidelines 
produced by the CPA and recommend that the use of the CEN CENELEC standards 
within the delegated acts should be kept under consideration.  We would encourage 
ongoing engagement between CEN CENELEC and DG Environment to facilitate their 
use in the delegated acts. 
 
The delegated acts proposed in Article 5, on requirements for substances in packaging, 
and related to SoCs should be limited to substances in packaging that negatively affect 
re-use and recycling.  This is to avoid duplication and possible inconsistences being 
created with other legislation, in particular Regulation (EC) No 1907/2006 and 
Regulation (EC) No 1935/2004.  Relevant industry stakeholders should be consulted 
during the preparation of any delegated acts. 
Collection and sorting 
Central to achieving many of the legislative targets set out in the draft PPWR are higher 
rates of collection across Europe, greater capture rates of target materials in sorting 
centres and more granular sorting to facilitate the transformation of baled recovered 
waste polyolefins into high quality recycled polyolefin pellet are needed.  Currently 
there are significant differences between members states.  PCEP believe that well 
designed EPR schemes are best placed to not only provide financial resources but also 
 
to play a key coordination role in the development of a circular economy.  Ensuring that 
the different parts of the supply chain act in a coordinated fashion will be key in 
achieving the desired policy goals. 
PCEP believe that separate collection is the best mechanism to divert waste polyolefin 
packaging for high quality recycling and that achieving high performing separate 
collection uniformly across all member states should be a key strategic goal within the 
EU.  This in turn will facilitate the achievement of many of the other targets and policy 
objectives set out in the PPWR.  However, after best practise separate collection has 
been established across the EU, we recognise that there is likely still to be a need to 
capture any remaining polyolefin packaging from residual waste prior to energy 
recovery or final disposal.  
The facilitation of shipments of waste for reuse and recycling within the EU must be 
ensured to allow the smooth functioning of the circular economy.  This should include 
the trans frontier shipment of mixed packaging fractions for sorting and other interim 
operations as well as sorted fractions moving between EU countries for recycling. 
We welcome the work in CEN CENELEC TC249, initiated by the European Commission 
request following work in the CPA, related to the standardisation of sorted plastic 
waste specifications, including for polyolefins. 
Recycling Technologies 
PCEP welcomes that the PPWR is technology neutral in relation to recycling.  We 
recognise the valuable role that mechanical, physical and chemical recycling 
technologies can all play in achieving circularity for polyolefins when used strategically 
and in a complimentary fashion.  Due to the lower CO2 impact, highly recyclable streams 
of polyolefin packaging waste should be made available for mechanical recycling first, 
with chemical recycling focusing on the more challenging streams for mechanical 
recyclers.  For example, residual and by-product streams from sorting centers and 
mechanical recycling operations.  However, when choosing a recycling route, demand 
side technical requirements for the recycled plastic must also be considered, such as 
food or near food contact requirements.   
Recycled content targets 
PCEP welcome the use of minimum recycled content targets to increase demand for 
recycled plastics.  We support a polyolefin-to-polyolefin definition of circularity, rather 
than product-to-product or sector-to-sector, as these do not always result in the best 
environmental outcomes.  This is consistent with how circularity is viewed for other 
materials.  PCEP’s mission is to keep the maximum amount of polyolefin material in the 
 
circular economy, at its highest value, for as long as possible, which is not necessarily 
application specific. 
PCEP believes that setting recycled content targets per SKU is unnecessarily restrictive 
and may impact on companies’ ability to meet recycled content targets.  We would like 
to see targets set as an average across the plastic packaging placed on the market by 
an economic operator.   
We share the Commission’s ambition for stretching recycled content targets. This gives 
a clear signal to industry and supports investment in recycling infrastructure.  We 
believe the targets set for 2030 are challenging but achievable with the right changes in 
the supply chain and investments in infrastructure.  We assess the aspirational targets 
for 2040 as being at levels close to or at the theoretical maximum achievable.  Due to 
this extremely high level of ambition and the associated paradigm shift in the sector 
required to meet the targets, we support the measures set out in Article 7 paragraphs 
10 and 11 to adjust the targets, if this is required.  In Article 7 (10) we would recommend 
the inclusion of a definitive review of the 2040 targets by the end of 2034 to assess 
whether the targets require adjustment based on the criteria set out in this paragraph. 
Significant chemical recycling capacity will be required for polyolefins to meet the 
contact sensitive targets being proposed.  A credible and robust mass balance 
accounting system should be agreed and implemented through legislation as soon as 
possible, preferably before the end of 2023.  The current date of having a delegated act 
in place by the end of 2026 is too close to the 2030 targets. The lack of an agreed mass 
balance methodology is creating uncertainty and holding back investment decisions 
that must be made now if the required infrastructure is to be in place by 2030.   In 
addition, the absence of a mass balance methodology means it is not possible to assess 
the feasibility of meeting the contact sensitive targets for polyolefins as it is unclear 
how much polyolefin waste is needed to produce the required level of recycled 
polymer suitable for this application.  This in turn makes it difficult to assess other 
recycled content targets for polyolefins as there is a finite supply of possible feedstock 
for all recycling technologies.  PCEP support the fuel exempt mass balance calculation 
methodology to determine recycled content.   
It is important that there is sufficient collection, sorting and recycling of polyolefins to 
meet the mandatory recycled content targets.  The impact assessment appears to work 
off the basis that all packaging waste will be available to meet the packaging recycled 
content target.  This is not currently the case.  PCEP commissioned research indicates 
that whilst nearly 85% of polyolefin recyclate originates from packaging only 25% is 
used back in packaging applications.  The construction, agricultural and automotive 
sectors are long term users of recycled polyolefins generated from packaging waste 
 
with key manufacturers in these sectors having invested heavily in the recycling supply 
chain, sometimes setting up their own recycling operations.  Recycled polyolefins 
provide an important alternative to virgin in these sectors and generate significant 
environmental benefits.   
Bio-based plastics  
We support the Commission’s aspirational target that at least 20% of carbon used in 
plastic products should be from non-fossil sources by 2030, as set out in the 
Communication on Sustainable Carbon Cycles.  With this in mind, we would support the 
inclusion of a complimentary target in the PPWR for the use of sustainably sourced bio-
based feedstock in packaging, where this can bring environmental benefits by lowering 
of greenhouse gas emissions. 
Reuse 
PCEP supports the establishment of reuse targets in the proposal and recognises the 
important role that polyolefin plastics can play in achieving them.  We recognise the 
importance of reuse in systems where they have a proven environmental benefit over 
single use alternatives.  
Articles 26 paragraphs 12 and 13 need sufficient time to be put in place.  Currently some 
requirements in paragraph 12b enter into force as soon as the legislation applies and the 
remainder by the end of 2025.  We recommend that these paragraphs apply from 2030 
to give industry time to prepare.  This also aligns with other reuse targets, the recycled 
content requirements coming into force and is logical in that the design for recycling 
criteria will be known.  In addition, these paragraphs require 100% reuse to be achieved.  
We believe that aiming for a 100% target is unrealistic as there will always be situations 
where 100% reuse may not be either technically possible or environmentally beneficial.  
Finally, these paragraphs need clear geographical boundaries setting which limit their 
scope to the EU.  This is to avoid having to set up reuse systems over large distances 
and in different continents where companies may have operations or linked enterprises.  
Setting up global reuse systems is extremely unlikely to result in the best environmental 
outcome.  We would also highlight the need for cross border compatibility of reuse 
systems within the EU due to the high level of trans frontier movements that will be 
involved.  
We recommend the removal of the reuse target for pallet wrappings and propose that 
Article 26 (9) focuses only on straps as we are unaware of fully functional reusable 
alternatives for pallet wrappings.   We are also concerned that this requirement may 
drive solutions with poorer environmental outcomes that require, on average, more 
plastic per trip.  As well the significant potential for environmental downsides, there are 
 
also technical, hygiene and economic barriers to introducing reuse options for pallet 
wrappings.    
Restrictions on use of packaging formats 
We do not support the restriction in Annex V of single-use plastic grouped packaging 
and recommend the this is removed.  Single-use plastic grouped packaging is not only 
used to encourage consumers to purchase more than one product.  It provides an 
important packaging function to allow the consumer to safely transport items from the 
place of purchase to home without spillage or breakage.  It sometimes also carries out a 
hygiene function.  This restriction in likely to lead to some unintended negative 
consequences, such as additional packaging used for protecting individual items.  In 
addition to the important functionality, it should be recognised that this type of 
packaging is easily mechanically recyclable, widely recycled, and can include high levels 
of recycled content.  This is also a material specific ban, which PCEP do not support.

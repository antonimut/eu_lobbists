Executive Summary 
McDonald’s supports the overall goals of the European Green Deal - in particular that 
of achiev ing a circular economy. It is for this reason that we  have grown increasingly 
concerned over the last few months about the proposal on the Packaging and Packaging 
Waste Regulation (PPWR). We are especially worried about the unintended 
consequences for the environment, the economy, food safety and consumers. 
 
McDonald’s welcomes the revision of the EU Packaging and Packaging Waste Directive 
(94/62/EC) and the overall ambition in the proposed PPWR. However, we believe that the 
current proposal will not ensure a just and fair transition for European citizens, nor would 
it deliver on the Green Deal and Circular Economy Action Plan.  
 
Several independent studies clearly show recyclable fibre -based foodservice 
packaging has the greater potential to benefi t the environment, economy, food 
safety and consumers than reusable foodservice packaging.  These are detailed 
reports, conducted by independent experts and commissioned by leading companies 
like McDonald’s and industry associations who share the EU’s climate goals.  
 
As such, we believe any law should take into account the specific needs of the complex 
Informal Eating Out (IEO) business sector, and focus on the right foodservice packaging 
solutions for Europe. We believe this means allowing a mix of solutions. 
 
Our concrete recommendations to ensure that the PPWR allows packaging to perform 
its core functionalities (protecting, containing and delivering products safely) while 
ensuring food affordability and security, the best overall environmental performance , 
and being suited to sector-specific business models, include: 
 
● The EU should pause, reflect and conduct a full impact assessment study  before 
moving ahead. Moreover, Member States should not unilaterally introduce legislation 
- this will further complicat e an already complex sector, and will have a negative, 
fragmentary impact on the single market. In that regard, the proposal should also 
remain a Regulation and not shift to a Directive – as varying implementation of the 
law will be detrimental to having a  harmonised EU -single market, which is key to 
ensure Europe remains competitive. 
 
● There needs to be equivalence of treatment in legislation between recycled and 
recyclable (fibre-based) single-use packaging and reusable tableware for dine-in.

Introduction: McDonald’s commitment to packaging waste reduction 
McDonald’s is one of the biggest actors of the Informal Eating Out (IEO) Sector in the 
world, with 6,409 McDonald’s  restaurants in the EU. Together with our franchisees we 
employ nearly 300,000 people across Europe, and feed millions of EU citizens daily. 
At McDonald’s, we care deeply about our impact on the world. Our purpose is To Feed and 
Foster local Communities. We are always striving to use our influence and scale to make 
a positive impact on the planet and in communities we serve across Europe and globally. 
We are on a journey to help implement and accelerate solutions to keep waste out of 
nature and valuable materials in use.  
In the EU our packaging is primarily sourced from European fibre packaging suppliers and 
we have set goals to source 100% of our primary guest packaging (fibre and plastic) from 
renewable, recycled or certified sources. For now, 92.8% (by w eight) of McDonald’s food 
packaging in Europe is wood fibre and 99.4% of that fibre packaging comes from recycled 
or certified sources. We are focused on implementing global and local solutions to 
expand reduction and recycling of guest packaging by the en d of 2025, and we believe 
recycling has an important role to play in creating truly circular packaging systems and in 
reducing waste. Throughout Europe our packaging is designed to be  collected and 
processed in paper recycling systems. 
We support the overall goals of the EU Green Deal and are on a journey to help implement 
and accelerate solutions to keep waste out of nature and valuable materials in use. 
Europe has historically fallen short of achieving circularity by adopting legislation that 
does not fully consider sector -specific business models, operational requirements, and 
consumer needs and well-being. While packaging and packaging waste are big issues at 
stake when it comes to Europe’s transformation towards circularity, w e at McDonald's 
believe that it is necessary for the European Commission to take a step back and assess 
the sector-specific needs to make sure there is a level -playing field for all stakeholders 
that does not negatively impact the environment and European consumers.  
 
Specificities of the IEO Sector 
The IEO sector plays a key role in the European economy, employing hundreds of 
thousands of Europeans and generating billions of Euros a year. The sector has a unique 
set of business, operational, and consumer characteristi cs: it is highly fragmented, with 
small and medium enterprises (SMEs), the majority of whom are not exempt from the 
legislation, making up more than two -thirds of the sector, and with more than 320,000 
individual business owners. IEO consumers also have a very different set of demands 
compared to those in the broader restaurant sector: they expect speed, affordability (the 
average price of an IEO meal in the EU is approximately €4), and above all convenience. 
Take­-away consumption accounts for more than 70 percent of sector revenue, and this 
figure continues to grow.  
These unique characteristics mean that adopting effective circularity solutions at scale 
presents multiple challenges, including identifying an effective mix of circularity 
solutions for a se ctor dominated by SMEs with packaging that must meet food -grade 
standards and safety requirements. In some European regions, underdeveloped waste 
infrastructure to collect, sort, and effectively dispose of waste presents limitations. Other 
regions have bui lt well -functioning collection and recycling systems achieving 
significant recycling rates – this is notably the case for Italy.  
The IEO sector is also categorised by consumer behaviours that are difficult to influence 
and change (particularly in takeawa y and delivery, where visibility into, and control over, 
consumer behaviour is highly limited). Estimates suggest that a reusable cup needs to be 
reused between 50 and 100 times to make it environmentally preferable to a single -use 
cup from a plastic waste  generation point of view. 1 Yet, early pilots are showing that 
return rates for reusable options have been very low, which significantly impacts reuse 
rates. In France, for instance, where reusables are available for dine -in only and should 
not leave the restaurant, McDonald’s is seeing a loss rate of around 15% due to customers 
taking the items from the restaurant. Because of this we are not currently achieving the 
desired reuse rate, or a positive environmental outcome. In Germany, despite customers 
paying a deposit of 2€ per reusab le item, 70% of reusables are not being returned to the 
restaurant.  
 
The impact of the current PPWR proposal 
Based on independent data and our experience with reusables in France and Germany,  
we are concerned that the bans and reduction targets on single  use (fibre -based) 
packaging in the proposed PPWR will likely be counter-productive to the overall EU Green 
Deal objectives and will be damaging for the economy, environment, food safety and 
consumers. 
Several high-level independent studies, conducted by highly recognised experts such as 
Kearney, Ramboll, and McDowell into the implications of the PPWR clearly show 
recyclable fibre-based packaging has the greater potential to benefit the environment, 
economy, food safety and consumers. Additionally, scaling  recycling solutions and 
infrastructure should be the priority for dine -in, and reusables should not be 
implemented for takeaway and delivery. No single circularity solution on its own can 
solve the sector’s waste footprint. Europe needs to identify a mix of solutions with the 
greatest potential to work across specific sectors, compared to ‘blanket’ solutions and 
targets.  
The studies show that a rollout of reuse models across all packaging types and channels 
will lead to higher packaging waste volumes and  a sharp increase in plastic waste . 
Proposed PPWR 100% reuse targets could dramatically increase the EU’s plastic waste by 
up to 300% for dine -in and up to 1500% for takeaway. 2 In fact, reuse models often use

Recommendations 
Therefore, we want to offer the following recommendations that the Commission as well 
as the Parliament and Council should take into account as they continue their 
deliberations on the PPWR proposal.   
● The EU should pause, reflect and conduct a full impac t assessment study  before 
moving ahead. Moreover, Member States should not unilaterally introduce legislation 
- this will further complicate an already complex sector, and will have a negative, 
fragmentary impact on the single market.  
● In that regard, the proposal should also remain a Regulation and not shift back to a 
Directive. Varying implementation of the law will be detrimental to having a 
harmonised EU-single market, which is key to ensure Europe remains competitive. 
● There needs to be equivalence of t reatment between recycled and recyclable 
(fibre-based) single-use packaging and reusable tableware for dine-in. 
● Reusable models should not be implemented for takeaway and delivery, given that 
single use is demonstrably better for the environment, economy, food safety and 
consumers.  
● The EU should scale existing circularity solutions and know -how, such as 
recycling. Circularity solutions in the IEO sector must consider the significant  
importance of takeaway consumption and delivery.  Improving recycling shows the 
best environmental outcomes in the short term, especially for takeaway and delivery, 
where we project reuse models will lead to negative environmental outcomes.

Conclusion 
Once again, we would like to thank the European Commission for its work and reiterate 
our support in favour of packaging circularity in the European Union, and the br oader 
goals of the Green Deal. 
Yet, as we have demonstrated, a mix of solutions is what is necessary for the IEO sector 
in Europe to really meet its environmental targets while not being detrimental to 
consumer well-being and businesses.  
We are therefor e asking for the Commission to pause, assess the increasingly 
significant data, and reflect upon the best way forward. We are also asking that 
member states not press ahead with their own legislation as this will lead to even more 
complication for businesses and consumers, and could risk f ragmentation of the single 
market.  
McDonald’s is committed to being a constructive partner with policymakers throughout 
the revision of EU packaging and packaging waste legislation and we look forward to 
continuing this debate.

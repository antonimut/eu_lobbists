Position paper on the lightwooden packaging industry on the Packaging and 
Packaging Waste Regulation Proposal (PPWR) 
Context 
The European Green Deal has imposed that by 2030 all packaging placed in the EU market 
need to be reusable or recyclable. The revision of the PPWD is assessing what are the new 
essential requirements in order to place packaging in the EU market aiming  to make this 
target a reality. The discussion will be focused on what is considered to be ‘reusable’ or 
‘recyclable’. Several European standards cover recyclability and compostability and some 
are being reviewed to make them compatible with the PPWD. 
Grow international welcomes the PPWR proposal but would like to highlight  the following 
specific topics and we hope these elements will be considered. 
Key messages 
1- Overall, we think that it is crucial to recognise the packaging renewable and circular 
materials’ contribution to climate neutrality . Renewable materials can help to 
reduce the use and promote the phase out fossil - based products. It is important, 
whenever possible, to incentivise products made from renewable and sustainably 
managed resources  considering they have a reduced environment al footprint and 
contribute to a low carbon circular economy. 
 
2- We have to collectively think about new solutions  and propose new ways for the 
collection, sorting and treatment of ‘ renewable’ or bio -based packaging This could 
include mechanical recycling, organic recycling ( composting) and energy recovery 
when no other alternative is available.  
 
3- Packaging restrictions on specific packaging need a thorough assessment based on 
robust science and the requirements to b e exempted from the ban need to be clear 
in order to have legal certainty for economic actors. 
 
4- A clear and realistic definition of recyclability  as well as  high quality recycling,  and 
compostability should be developed with the aim  to have legal c larity. In addition, 
the definition of transport packaging and sales packaging should be clarified. 
 
5- Reusable packaging may not be the right solution for all materials  and should be 
complementary to recyclable packaging . It will depend on the absolute 
environmental impact for the type of packaging measured by an LCA . 
 
6- Need to have harmonised labelling requirements  to avoid the current situation 
where the market is fragmented with divergent national requirements.

Detailed messages 
1- Its crucial to recognise the positive impact of the  use of renewable resources in 
packaging in the PPWD revision  and renewable materials’ contribution to climate 
neutrality. It is also necessary to reduce the use of packaging with a high 
environmental impact. The conclusions of the LCA conducted by the French Agency 
for Ecological transition (ADEME) 1 comparing carton, plastic & wood packaging 
shown that (as shown below) single use wooden packaging is the best solution  for 
fruit and vegetables. By comparing a multi-rotation plastic packaging with two single 
use packaging made from renewable materials, under no circumstances can we say 
that reusable is  better than single use in terms of environmental impact. It will 
depend on the material. Therefore, a renewable  material, if managed sustainably, is 
ALWAYS better for the environment than a fossil -based material, regardless of the 
meritorious efforts made to reduce the use or the negative impacts of the latter.  
 
 
Promoting reuse targets is justified only for materials from fossil fuels such as plastic but not 
for renewable materials like wood. They do not make any sense for packaging transported 
for long distances and for export purposes. 
 
According to Directive 2018/852, one of the objectives is to protect, preserve and improve 
the quality of the environment by improving waste management, and ensure a prudent and 
efficient utilisation of natural resources. Then it is very important to manage natural 
resources sustainably and efficiently  for the next generations. The European wood 
packaging industry manages its forests sustainably and is increasing tree planting 2 via 
voluntary programmes 3. This directly contributes and has an impact on the re duction of 
total annual greenhouse gas emissions  which is a n EU target and a reduction of the EU's 
dependence on imported natural resources.  
Wood packaging can help the food industry to reduce the use of plastic , especially in the 
case of plastic in contact with food products. Wood packaging for food products comes from 
sustainable forest management and emits less CO₂ in its lifecycle . Furthermore, they are

2- It is necessary to think about new solutions and propose new ways for the separate 
collection, sorting and treatment of ‘ renewable’ or bio -based packaging. The 
industry cannot be made solely responsible for the correct  management of this 
valuable resource as the already contribute with EPR fees and local authorities/ EPR 
organisations have the competence to deal with it. The PPWR will not reach its  
ambitious targets by only making packaging producers responsible. It has to be a 
collaborative effort promoting the investment in recycling infrastructures via EU and 
National funding and good collection and sorting. 
It is also crucial to recognise the cascade -uses of wood including energy production. This 
should be developed in a specific EU waste -wood legislation (including the obligation for 
municipal or private collection systems to separately collect wood waste  and ban ning 
landfill).  
3- Packaging bans and restrictions on specific packaging such as food contact 
packaging need a specific and thorough assessment . The proposed ban of 
restricting all packaging for fruits and vegetables (less than 1,5 kg) risks leading to an 
increased food waste in the distribution chain inducing an increased environmental 
and social burden.  In any case, the hygiene and safety must not be jeopardized 
specially for food con tact materials (FCM) considering the FCM legislation is 
currently being review. Both legislations should be examined in parallel.   
 
Reducing the use of wooden packaging means that the wood would directly be used by the 
construction and furniture sector or  as biomass without a first cascade -use as packaging. 
Indirectly, this is not be the best way to use natural resources and has a bigger 
environmental impact (as other materials with a bigger environmental impact will be used). 
 
It is adamant to consider that food packaging must respect very strict phytosanitary 
measures and  “The t ransfer of foodborne disease remains a clear and present hazard to 
consumers and that  there are greater risks of cross contamination within “circular” reuse 
systems, than in the current “linear” single use systems.”5 
 
4- We ask for  the development of a harmonised, clear and realistic definition of 
recyclability and high-quality recycling at EU level  to ensure legal clarity.  Art 6 says that it 
will be considered as recyclable packagin g that can be recycled  so that the resulting 
secondary raw materials are of sufficient quality to substitute the primary raw materials . 
For instance, in Italy  wood waste that is currently collected becomes the raw material for 
the panel industry and therefore for the furniture industry.

This can really be problematic as there is no clear definition of what is to be considered 
“sufficient quality”.  However, the impact assessment considers that “ wood tends to be 
downcycled for use  as feedstock for the panel board industry, animal bedding, surfacing 
(e.g. equestrian, play areas, landscaping) and biomass fuel” which means the main uses of 
woodwaste will be downcycled and will not qualify as recyclable packagi ng. Recycled 
woodwaste used by the panelboard industry or construction sector extends the life of wood 
waste into long lived products as requested by the European Forestry strategy.  
 
Therefore, we insist greatly on the necessity to define wood from packaging waste as 
recycling and in no way as downcycling , since woody biomass used for the panel board 
industry is used for innovative carbon storing materials and solutions for packaging, 
furniture and construction. In this respect, medium density fiberb oards (MDF) with paint/ 
plastic coating/ urea formaldehyde cannot be considered as packaging that may inhibit 
recycling as mentioned in the impact assessment. 
 
In addition, the recyclability requirements laid down in Article 6 that will be developed in 
secondary legislation will have to take into account the uniqueness of wood as a 
sustainable, renewable, low carbon and environmental footprint material (including energy 
and water use). 
There is already a definition  of separate collection and recyc ling in the WFD but they need 
further clarification. Existing definitions in European standards and legislation are 
ambiguous or obsolete and they should adapt it based on product type categories in order 
to consider the purpose, use , collection schemes and lifespan of the product. In addition, 
it should  maintain note A.3.1 6 from European standard EN 13430  on Requirements for 
packaging recoverable by material recycling.  This note mentions that it should be 
considered as recyclable ma terials those new materials which are in the process of 
establishing a recycling process as it is recognised their development may take a period of 
time. This will allow the industry to set up recycling schemes during the transition period 
before compliance.  
Separate waste collection  is a requirement to consider if a product is recyclable. Wooden 
packaging is recyclable and it can be separately collected at household level . It could be 
collected in the bag / container with other packaging (co-mingling in the yellow/ blue bag 
or container ). Considering the price of virgin wood has increased significantly in the last 
years, it is very important that all waste wood is collected as several industries are 
demanding this resource and the price of  waste wood is also increasing. For instance, Ikea 
has adopted an internal target to produce panelboards 100% from recycled wood in Europe 
by 2030 thus all collected wastewood will be recycled.

Alternatively, light wood packaging could be collected  with biowaste or gardenwaste  in 
order to make compost as it brings the required structure thanks to the lignocellulose7. In 
any case, the collection should take into account the local situation  which identifies the 
treatment facilities where the waste will be actually treated. This will determine where 
waste wood is best collected. 8 In any case , it should clearly be identified where the waste 
wood sh ould be disposed o f so consumers know how to sort  lightwooden crates.  For 
instance, i n Germany there is no solution for consumer lightwood packaging and it is not 
allowed in the yellow (paper/cardboard bag) although packers and fillers pay since many 
years EPR contributions. 
Currently, lightwooden packaging is being tested to check compliance with standard 13432 
on industrial compostability a nd materials which are bio -based are accepted as 
biodegradable. We support a revision of th is standard  as it is 20 years old and it does 
probably not allow renewable materials such as wood to fully decompose during the 9 0 
days. I f the PPWD is reviewed it makes sense that the Standard is also adapted as it 
currently almost impossible to comply with it.  
In addition, in article 3 of the PPWR the definition of ‘sales packaging’ ‘grouped packaging’  
and of ‘transport packaging’ should be clarified. It does not include the reference to tertiary 
packaging in the transport packaging definition that is now mentioned in recital 10. We 
believe this sho uld be included in article 3 to avoid different interpretations as Members 
states have different definitions of what transport or sales packaging is. In the PPWD , 
transport packaging is considered tertiary packaging or packaging conceived so as to 
facilitate handling and transport of a number of sales units or grouped packagings in order 
to prevent physical handling and transport damage. 
5- Reusable packaging may not be the most adequate solution for all materials  as it will 
also depend on the geographical, economic, environmental and social conditions to set up 
a reuse system. Only when it makes sense for the specific material as there is no one -size-
fits-all solution. Therefore, we support the adoption of policy measures that are evidence -
based and take into account a lifecycle approach showing the absolute environmental 
impact of the adopted measure. The production of lightwood packaging uses very little 
energy as the process is li ke sharpening a pen and therefore has a very positive 
environmental impact (a negative carbon footprint , limited use of non-renewable energy or 
low emissions of greenhouse gases). 9  
When comparing the recycling process of one material which in a closed loop (cardboard) 
and the other in an open loop (wood), it cannot be scientifically proven that the closed loop 
is better. Imposing a  recycled content on wood packaging will be unrealistic because they 
need to comply with strict hygiene standards and regulations as a food contact material.

Furthermore, reusable packaging, in order to comply with EU legislation must be sanitised 
and be properly washed before being reused. In addition , it requires the use of detergents 
and solvents, as washing also requires a significant amount of water. Waste water resulting 
from cleaning p lastic containers , contains detergent residues,  and it may also contai n 
microplastics that will be released during the washing itself.  If water is then not then 
properly cleaned it could further pollute rivers, sea and aquifers. 
 
6-Divergent national packaging requirements are a clear obstacle to the single market and 
require the redesign of all packaging, with signific ant costs, and negative impacts on the 
environment. We fully  support the introduction of harmonised labelling requirements to 
put an end to the proliferation of national marking obligations that have challenged the free 
movement of packaging in the EU  and create confusion in the market . L eaving it up to 
Member States to introduce further labelling requirements would be contrary to the spirit 
of harmonisation that justifies this review. We therefore believe that Member States should 
not be allowed to introduce further labelling requirements for the purpose of identifying 
EPR schemes, as prescribed in Art. 4 (5) and Art. 11 (8), and Art. 11 (7) should explicitly 
prevent the introduction of requirements on packaging sustainability labels. 
 
About GROW International 
 
GROW International is the European organisation of wooden packaging manufacturers . It represents the 
interests of wood packaging with regards to the environment, quality, hygiene, food safety, etc. It is an 
organisation created as a result of the cooperation between European organisations in the wood packaging 
sector in Spain, France, G ermany and Italy . It represents more than 150 companies that manufacture 800 
million boxes for fruit and vegetables  annually, and 400 million boxes of other types, representing a business 
of more than 500 million euros.  For further information, please do n ot hesitate to contact us:  www.grow-
international.eu

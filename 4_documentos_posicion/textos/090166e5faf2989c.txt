Ensuring a Sustainable Future for Packaging: Sappi's Perspective on the PPWR 
Proposal 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
➢ Increasing t he Role of Innovation in Achieving the EU Environmental Objectives by Innovation 
Principle Check 
As the EU considers the Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (PPWR), it is crucial to ensure that proposals 
align with both the Green Industrialisation Plan and the Net Zero Industry Act. These initiatives drive research 
and innovation towards ecological objectives while maintaining European industry competitiveness. To achieve 
this balance, proposals must be evaluated based on the Innovation Principle, not solely on their env ironmental 
impact. 
 
It is especially critical to consider economic factors, given that European companies operate in both the Single 
and global markets. Proposals must carefully balance the cost of supply and production to ensure that achieving 
ecological targets does not come at the expense of economic sustainability. Thus, it is essential to scrutinize 
whether the PPWR proposal achieves this crucial balance.  
 
➢ Considering National Progress and Different Reuse and Recycling Markets & Moving Back  to a 
Directive  
Reuse and recycling markets across the EU Member States have significant variations in their conditions. 
Therefore, it is crucial to consider national specificities and different approaches to packaging waste while 
formulating the PPWR. Such considerations are essential to avoid any potential setbacks and ensure that the 
already established national progress in waste management is not undermined.  
 
Sappi Europe 
Sappi, a leading provider of renewable fibre-based products, supports the goals of the Packaging and Packaging 
Waste Regulation (PPWR) proposal. However, we offer the following suggestions to improve the proposal:  
➢ We recommend that the proposal be evaluated against the Innovation Principle to increase the role of 
innovation. 
➢ We advise the consideration of national conditions for re -use and recycling while maintaining the 
format of a directive, which would provide greater flexibility for Member States to adopt the measures. 
➢ We suggest placing the emphasis on reuse and recycling as complementary rather than mutually 
exclusive options. 
➢ We recommend avoiding the use of mandatory recycled content requirements for products that 
already have a well-functioning market with high recycling rates.  
➢ We call for transparency and involvement of the Industry in drafting secondary legislation. 
By implementing these suggestions, we believe the PPWR will be better positioned to achieve its ecological 
objectives while maintaining competitiveness and innovation in the industry.

Given this context, adopting a directive instead of a regulation as the PPWR instrumen t seems preferable. A 
directive would provide greater flexibility for the Member States to adapt the proposed measures to their specific 
contexts and incorporate existing practices. This would enhance the likelihood of successful implementation and 
contribute to achieving the environmental objectives of the PPWR. 
 
➢ Placing Reuse and Recycling as Complementary Solutions 
The PPWR's preference for reuse over recycling risks marginalizing fibre -based packaging, as it may replace 
renewable and recyclable packaging types with fossil-based alternatives. Fibre-based packaging is crucial for the 
circular economy, with an 81.6% recycling rate in Europe, owing to a well -functioning market for fibre -based 
secondary raw materials.   
 
We recognise that recyclable and reusable options are complementary towards a commo n goal of achieving a 
circular economy in the EU. Policy decisions (including reuse targets) should be founded on an evidence -based 
evaluation of the lifecycle impact of packaging, its system, and the evaluation of the economic and technological 
implications, and consequences for human health and food security. Importantly, fibre -based packaging has a 
critical function in protecting and preserving goods throughout the value chain, extending food li fespan,1 and 
preventing product and food waste2 which contributes to resource efficiency.3 Crucially, it safeguards consumer 
safety in logistics and on the shelf. In addition, it provides nutritional information and tells consumers how to 
store and prepare food safely. Paper -based products, particularly, contribute to expanding the shelf -life period 
of products often from hours to many days.4 Furthermore, packaging maintains the quality of sensitive products, 
such as milk and juices, which provide consumers with essential vitamins and nutrients. 
 
Therefore, all measures should be based on a lifecycle impact assessment of the specific application. Single -use, 
recyclable fibre-based products should not be penalized, and a case-by-case approach considering each material 
stream's recycling and environmental performance would help prevent unintended consequences of the reuse 
approach. In some sectors (e.g., in food service systems or logistic systems) recyclable fibre packaging has a 
better environmental performance than reusable options 5. 
 
Moreover, optimal solutions should be backed by data that proves desirable economic, environmental, and social 
impacts while enabling industry-led product innovation. The PPWR should not hamper the progress made by the 
industry in investing in product innovations and recycling infrastructure. Reuse and recycling should be 
complementary, with an emphasis on the most suitable approach based on the specific application . 
 
➢ Avoiding Mandatory Recycled Content Requirements for Products that already Have a Well-
functioning Market with High Recycling Rates, such as Fibre-based Packaging  
Setting recycled content thresholds for fibre-based materials risks distorting a well-functioning market with high 
recycling rates. Mandatory thresholds would compromise food safety and potentially lead to an increase overall 
in packaging mass. They would also limit the use of fibre -based packaging for food contact  applications.  With 
the fibre -based industry actively pioneering innovative fibre -based packaging solutions that offer barrier

➢ Involving the Industry in Drafting Secondary Legislation and Providing the Necessary Clarity to 
Encourage Investment 
Clarity regarding the content and preparation of the upcoming Delegated Acts is crucial for the industry to adapt 
and comply with the new packaging and packaging waste rules. As such, it is important that the EU takes into 
account feedback from industry st akeholders, such as Sappi, who are developing expert -led solutions. When 
drafting the Delegated Acts, it is essential to ensure that the regulations support rather than harm the industry 
and its competitiveness. 
 
We welcome the proposed, actionable and forward-looking, definition of recyclability applicable to all packaging. 
However, the recyclability of the packaging must be assessed while taking into account material specificities via 
Design for Recycling (DfR) guidelines which provide technically sound guidance for recycling. The DfR should be 
based on expert judgment and consultation with targeted stakeholders, including recyclers, waste management 
operators, and technology providers. Thus, policymakers should ensure that stakeholders from the industry are 
consulted in a transparent and systemic manner in the drafting process of DfR guidelines. The paper and board 
recycling, manufacturing , and converting industry, which has already invested in and developed recyclability 
guidelines,6 has the knowledge and the expertise to support the development of DfR guidelines which will ensure 
recyclability by considering the packaging composition, functionality , and suitability for recycling in existing 
streams and with existing technologies. A process within the independent standardisation body, European 
Committee for Standardisation (CEN), would allow for open cooperation and exchange of expertise to set 
ambitious and realistic requirements on packaging recyclability. 
 
Moreover, the PPWR proposal restricts the use of certain packaging formats, including single-use packaging used 
for individual portions or servings of condiments, preserves, sauce, coffee creamer, sugar, and seasoning in the 
HORECA sector, except for takeaway purposes. Amendments and new additions to this restriction could be made 
via delegated acts, making it crucial for the industry to have clarity on the content and preparation of secondary 
legislation.

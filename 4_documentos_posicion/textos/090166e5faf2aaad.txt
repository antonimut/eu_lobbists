Sustainable packaging with open pallet pooling  
EPAL statement on the proposal for a EU Packaging & Packaging Waste Regulation 
 
  
 
Introduction 
EPAL supports the European Commission's proposal for the EU Packaging & Packaging Waste 
Regulation (PPWR), which was published on 30.11.2022. The objectives of preventing and reducing 
packaging and promoting the sustainability of packaging by introducing and harmonising provisions 
for the reuse and recycling of packaging are identical to the objectives that EPAL has been pursuing 
since 1991 in the open EPAL Euro Pallet Exchange Pool. 
 
EPAL has been an important part and a recognised role model for the Circular Economy in the 
packaging industry for more than 30 years. The reuse, exchange, repair and recycling of transport 
packaging in the form of EPAL wooden pallets and EPAL Box pallets has set standards for the 
sustainable use of resources. The EPAL Euro pallet is at the centre of this. With a production figure of 
more than 1.5 billion pallets since 2000, the EPAL Europallet is the backbone of European pallet 
logistics. The open EPAL Europallet exchange pool has been living Circular Economy for more than 30 
years, as it is to be introduced for other sectors of the packaging industry with the PPWR proposal. 
 
This document is limited to a statement on the most important contents of the PPWR proposal from 
EPAL's point of view. 
  
 
Executive summary 
The PPWR proposal is fully supported by EPAL in terms of objectives, methodology and most of the 
content. In the further adaptation and design, the well-functioning conditions of the open EPAL Euro 
pallet exchange pool should be taken more into account in order to avoid a step backwards with regard 
to the reuse and exchange of pallets. 
It is particularly important to take more and better account of the special conditions of wooden pallets 
and the special conditions of transport packaging (B2B). This includes considering not only reuse and 
recyclability but also other aspects such as the use of renewable resources, the relatively low energy 
consumption and the long service life before final recycling. 
In the further legislative process, the instrument of a regulation should be retained. The development 
of the delegated acts must be transparent and with the active participation of all stakeholders.

EPAL recommendations 
 
1 
The PPWR proposal focuses on the establishment of new reuse systems and favours closed deposit 
and pool systems. This fails to recognise the functionality and importance of open and free reuse 
systems and thus jeopardises the achievement of the sustainability goals. In the further legislative 
process, the conditions of already existing reuse systems in general as well as the advantages and 
importance of the open EPAL Euro pallet exchange pool in particular must therefore be better taken 
into account. 
 
2 
The PPWR proposal focuses on the reduction and avoidance of plastic packaging. 
The special features of wooden packaging are not always fully and accurately taken into account. This 
should be corrected in the further legislative process. 
 
3 
The PPWR proposal focuses on the reduction and avoidance of consumer packaging. 
The special features of transport packaging (B2B) are not always correctly assessed and taken into 
account. This must be corrected in the further legislative process. 
 
4 
The PPWR proposal almost exclusively considers the aspects of reuse and recycling of packaging as a 
benchmark for the sustainability of packaging. Other aspects that are also of great importance for the 
assessment of sustainability, such as the renunciation of fossil and finite resources, the low use of 
energy for production and repair or social aspects of decentralised production in structurally weak 
regions are not or not sufficiently taken into account. This should be corrected in the further legislative 
process. 
 
5 
The harmonisation of the regulations for packaging and packaging waste is right and important for the 
development and use of sustainable packaging in the common market. This harmonisation must not 
be restricted by national opening clauses, regardless of whether these opening clauses allow the 
provisions to be tightened or weakened. The instrument of the regulation must be implemented 
consistently in order to avoid an impairment of the free movement of goods. 
 
6 
The PPWR proposal lacks the necessary detailed regulations in many places and instead refers to 
delegated acts that do not yet exist. The PPWR must be developed through delegated acts with the 
greatest transparency and with the early and comprehensive participation of the companies and

associations concerned. This is the only way to avoid undesirable developments that jeopardise the 
achievement of the sustainability goals and the acceptance of the PPWR. 
  
 
 
 
In detail: 
 
1 Strengthen existing reuse systems, not limit them 
The international EPAL Euro pallet exchange pool, which is open to all companies using pallets, is one 
of the most successful circular economy systems in the world. This success results from the 
standardization of the load carriers, which is guaranteed by the constant and independent quality 
assurance of the EPAL. This enables EPAL Euro pallets to be used by all industrial sectors, retail and 
wholesale and logistics. The type and duration of use are not restricted, just as there are no other 
access and use restrictions in the open EPAL Euro pallet exchange pool. 
The open and free access to participate in the pallet exchange is the main reason for the success of 
EPAL Euro pallets, in addition to the possibility of using EPAL pallets across companies and industries. 
This is generally recognized in the PPWR proposal. The description of open loop systems in Annex VI 
lit b) of the PPWR proposal describes almost exactly how the open EPAL Euro pallet exchange pool 
works. 
However, individual detailed regulations contradict this (e.g. reporting regulations - Annex VI, Part A, 
No. 1k), just as unclear requirements for a governance structure do not take into account the 
functioning of an open reuse system. Both of these are obviously due to the fact that the PPWR 
proposal is primarily based on low-cost packaging, the reuse of which offers no direct economic 
advantages or is even associated with disadvantages. This does not take into account the 
circumstances of the EPAL Euro pallet exchange pool, in which the EPAL pallets represent economic 
goods for the participants whose value is only realized through reuse and exchange. 
 
2 Consideration of the special conditions of wooden packaging 
EPAL pallets (with the exception of EPAL mesh boxes) are made exclusively from wood. EPAL is 
therefore part of the wood packaging industry and a member of the European Federation of the Wood 
Packaging Industry FEFPEB. The FEFPEB association comments on the PPWR proposal with its own 
statement. This statement of the FEFPEB association is fully supported by EPAL. 
This EPAL statement only adds the aspect of using wooden pallets in an open pool system. 
 
Restricting the material of EPAL pallets to boards (made of wood), blocks (made of wood or wood 
chips) and nails (made of metal) is a prerequisite for zero-waste recycling at the end of the average 7-
year service life of EPAL Euro pallets. As a renewable resource, wood has significant advantages over

plastic. The use of wood as a material for packaging is in line with the Commission's objectives to avoid 
and reduce plastic packaging. 
This must not be thwarted by the fact that the PPWR proposal contains regulations that cannot be met 
by wood packaging. One example is the requirement in Art. 6 d) that the secondary raw material should 
replace the primary raw material. Unlike the blocks of wooden pallets, which are made of wood chips, 
the boards of wooden pallets cannot be made of wood chips without limiting or excluding the safety 
and in particular the reusability of the pallets. What is needed is a supplementary provision, 
comparable to the provision in Art. 9(4) (at the end), which explicitly mentions that the functioning of 
wooden packaging is taken into account when assessing it as recyclable packaging, especially when it 
comes to reusable packaging such as EPAL pallets, whose long period of use is preferable to short-term 
recycling. 
In the further design of the PPWR, care must be taken to ensure that the provisions do not favour 
plastic packaging over packaging made from natural and renewable raw materials. This would run 
counter to the goals of packaging sustainability. 
 
3 Consideration of the special conditions of transport packaging (B2B) 
Transport packaging and other tertiary packaging (B2B) such as EPAL pallets have to fulfil different 
requirements than those applicable to consumer packaging. This needs to be taken into account in the 
Design for Reuse and Design for Recycling provisions, especially when it comes to the weight and 
volume of packaging. 
EPAL Euro pallets are used in large numbers to store heavy loads in high-bay warehouses. The 
standardisation and quality assurance of EPAL ensure the necessary quality and safety for this. Any 
restriction of the high quality through restrictions on weight and volume would impair the possibility 
of use and reuse and, in particular, the free movement of goods. 
This must be taken into account when drafting the provisions on Design for Reuse and Design for 
Recycling. 
 
4 Consideration of all aspects of packaging sustainability 
The PPWR proposal focuses on the aspects of reuse and recycling of packaging. These aspects are 
without doubt among the essential prerequisites for the sustainability of packaging. At the same time, 
however, it should not be misunderstood that the renunciation of fossil-based materials in favour of 
the renewable material wood is an equally important aspect for the assessment of the sustainability 
of packaging. The same applies to the relatively low energy input required for the production and 
repair of wooden packaging. The production of EPAL Euro pallets also frequently uses energy from 
biomass, which is obtained by using waste wood from sawmills and pallet production. Equally 
important is the cascade use of wood, as associated with reusable EPAL pallets, which best meets the 
Commission's requirements for a long life cycle of wooden packaging before it is recycled.

These and other benefits of using wood as a renewable material must also be taken into account when 
assessing sustainability and promoting the use of reusable wooden pallets such as EPAL pallets. 
 
5 Harmonisation through regulation 
The full harmonisation of the contents of the PPWR proposal is essential for a functioning internal 
market. 
The birth of EPAL was the opening of the borders to Eastern Europe, which made an internationally 
uniform standardisation and quality assurance of EPAL Euro pallets necessary in order to avoid quality 
losses and the associated restriction of the exchangeability and reuse of EPAL pallets. The same applies 
to the requirements for reuse and recyclability of packaging in general. 
The already audible demands to retain the regulatory form of a directive instead of a regulation should 
therefore be resisted. At the same time, the PPWR should not contain any opening clauses for national 
regulations. The different contents of the current national packaging laws hinder progress towards a 
circular and sustainable packaging economy and are associated with many disadvantages in the field 
of logistics. It is important to avoid both opening clauses for intensification and weakening of the 
provisions on reuse and recycling. Both would impair the free movement of goods in the member 
states - nationally and internationally. 
 
6 Transparent development of delegated acts with the participation of all stakeholders 
In many places, the PPWR proposal contains references to delegated acts that do not yet exist. This is 
understandable, as the PPWR has to replace the provisions of the numerous national provisions that 
will be replaced by the change to a regulation. On the other hand, this is associated with great legal 
uncertainty and, in particular, planning uncertainty. This shortens the time for the affected companies 
to adapt the currently missing provisions. This must be taken into account when determining the date 
of entry into force and when assessing transition periods. 
Even more important is the transparent development of the delegated acts with the participation of 
the affected sectors, associations and companies. The proposal of the PPWR already shows that some 
special conditions of individual markets as well as the open EPAL Euro pallet exchange pool have not 
been evaluated completely and in some cases also not correctly. This must be avoided at all costs in 
the development of the delegated acts by informing and involving all stakeholders in a timely and 
comprehensive manner.

About EPAL: 
 
As an international association, the European Pallet Association e.V. (EPAL) is responsible for 
organising the EPAL Euro pallet exchange pool. EPAL Euro pallets are the cornerstone of industrial and 
retail supply chains in Europe. The EPAL brand stands for quality, safety and sustainability in logistics. 
In 2021 and 2022, more than 100 million EPAL euro pallets were produced each year. There are 
currently more than 650 million EPAL Euro pallets and approximately 20 million EPAL Box pallets in 
circulation making the EPAL Euro pallet exchange pool the biggest open pallet exchange pool in the 
world. More than 1,600 EPAL licensees produce and repair EPAL Euro pallets and other EPAL load 
carriers.  
 
As a not for profit organisation, EPAL does not pursue any commercial interests. Founded in 1991 as 
the international association for exchangeable Euro pallets, EPAL is currently active in over 35 
countries through national associations and representatives. The central focus of EPAL’s activity is the 
organisation of the international EPAL Euro pallet exchange pool and the quality assurance of the 
production and repair of EPAL Euro pallets, EPAL Box pallets, and other EPAL load carriers. 
The EPAL Euro pallet exchange pool, with its principles of reuse, exchange, repair and recycling, has 
been an exemplar for a sustainable circular economy for more than three decades. Wooden EPAL Euro 
pallets make a significant contribution to protecting the climate. They store CO2, prevent waste and 
improve the carbon footprint of users in industry, retail and logistics through reuse, exchange, repair 
and recycling.

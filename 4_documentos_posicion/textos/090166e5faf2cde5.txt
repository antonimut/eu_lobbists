Opening statement 
 
The elimination  of waste and the creation of a tru ly circular econo my hav e been on the 
agenda of European brewers since well before the European Union defined this as one of 
its flagship initiatives. With respect to packaging, deployed efforts cover a range of different 
initiatives that span production, marketing, dist ribution, u tilisation and disposal stages. 
They include, where feasible and without prejudice to beer safety and quality:- 
▪ designing packaging to minimi se use of materials, use lighter and rec ycled 
materials, and to utilise packaging materials that ensure recyclability; 
▪ reusing packaging and rationalising product ranges to reduce packaging; 
▪ distributing products in bulk whilst also reducing secondary and tertiary packaging; 
▪ supporting and enabling efficient returnable and recycling collection systems. 
 
The Brewers of Europe therefore un equivocally shares  the overall objectives o f the 
proposal. However, brewers are concerned that it violates the principles of law, resulting in 
policy measures that a re not proportionate and fa ir, whilst jeopardising the good 
functioning of the Si ngle Market. They believe that these concerns  can be addressed, 
without comprom ising on  sustainability and even supporting furt her the overall 
environmental objectives. 
 
 
Discriminatory treatment of the brewing sector needs to be lifted 
 
 Legal arguments 
It ha s first to be emphasised that, according to well-established case -law, alcoholic 
beverages and in particular , though not e xclusively, wine an d be er are competing 
products vis-à-vis the consumer, and are, at least partially, substitutable with each other 1. 
Therefore, their respective treatment should not  introduce any distortion of competition as 
regards any measure which may have an impact on c onsumer perception and/or choice2. 
They should n ot be subject to dif ferent treatment which impacts either production or 
distribution costs and, more generally, whi ch puts them at a  disadvantage vis à vis the 
other operators in a similar situation3 . 
 
The principle of non-discrimination, which is a general principle of EU Law applicable to all 
measures taken by the inst itutions, allows difference of treatment for dif ferent situations 
and, in case of s imilar products, only if  the difference of treatment is ju stified by objective 
reasons. As to the justification for measures introducing differences of treatment in similar 
situations, th e European Court of Justice  has la id down general p rinciples accordin g to 
which those differe nces of treatment should be adequate, i.e. justified in the li ght of the 
purpose of the measure itself4.  
 
In the present case, the objective of the packaging and packaging waste legislation is to 
move from a linea r to a circular ec onomy. This is a general  requirement laid down and 
applicable to all beverages, whethe r or not the product is subject to vertical legislation. It 
has also to be noted  that t he motiv es of the draft regulation do not explai n such a 
difference of treatment, w hich is not  motivated at  all. Whereas clause n ° 104 merely  
mentions “given the nature of the products and differences i n their produc tion and

 The Brewers of Europe asks  
✓ Reuse targets 
The scale of the on-trade (including kegs) / off-trade split and the packaging mix hugel y 
varies across Eu rope, from countries with two thirds of consumption to those with bar ely 
ten percent of c onsumption in the h ospitality sector, from countries with high percentages 
of recycled metal cans to countries with high percentages of returnable glass bottles. 
 
The targets set for beer in both on- and off-trade would be manageable if applied at EU 
level. According to  GlobalData inform ation, the perc entage of  reusable beer glass 
packaging represents already more than the target set for 2030. This does not even  take 
account of the use of reusable beer kegs and beer tanks, which prevents extra packaging 
from being put on the market. As such, one may even wonder whether the introduction of a 
reuse target for beer is proportionate.  
 
It is also unjustified to set a lower target for wine and grant a full exemption for spirits, 
particularly when the 2040 target is still more than fifteen years off and there is no objective 
reason why a certain percentage of the glass bottles used for these products could not be 
reusable when already two thirds of glass beer bottles are reusable. 
 
Europe’s brewers therefore request that: 
▪ all beverages categories be subject to the same target; 
▪  economic producers have the option to implement the target across their full 
product portfolio rather than per category of drink. 
 
✓ DRS 
The a priori exemption from DRS for  wines and spirits is legally not justified and also not 
warranted as glass is out of scope. There is no objective reason to treat wine and spirits in 
cans and PE T, whether or not impo rted in the European, differently from be er in cans or 
PET, equally whet her or not imported i n the European U nion. An a priori exemption  of 
higher str ength beverages from de posits, making these products less expensive to 
consumers than lower alcohol beverages at point of sale also runs counter alcohol policy 
objectives. The application of a deposit on a low alcoholic d rink, beer in this case,  but no 
deposit on higher str ength beverages makes wines and sp irits the better o ption for 
consumers from cost and handling perspectives. This clearly is in viol ation of the non -
discrimination principle. Several deposit schemes both within and outside of the European 
Union include also wine and spirits. It makes more sense to define, across all beverages,  
objective reasons for exclusion, or  inclusion of co ntents, rather than naming individual 
beverages, or cate gories of beverages : e.g. hygiene condi tions can be a reason  for 
excluding dairy products.

Proportionality principle should be better respected 
 
 Drinks 
European brewers welcome that th e European Packaging and Packaging Was te proposal 
pursues a compreh ensive policy for all types o f packaging. Drinks p ackaging, however , 
seems to be singled out. The responsibility and duty of reaching the legislation’s objectives 
in terms of packaging prev ention and packaging minimisation must  be bor ne by all 
packaging, not only by one packaging segment. 
 
Packaging minimisation 
The ban upon entry into force of widely used packaging materials is unrealistic: it takes on 
average 18 months after order to have the necessary machinery delivered. This time span 
does not even include the necessary testing and piloting that will need to occur before the 
equipment is fully operational. 
 
In addition, it runs counter to the objectives of the legislation that the draft proposal  bans 
recycled and recyclable packaging materials such as plastic films and foils. Even more so 
if they have recycled content. 
 
 Labelling 
Increasing the information available to consumers can be beneficial, but there should also 
be limits thereto.  
 
Additional sortin g mentions/log os/pictograms on pa ckaging that is already part of an 
allocated recycling stream and has the logo for that purp ose would be confusing and must 
be avoided. Such  packaging must not be considered as household was te, let alone as 
waste. It represe nts a valuable material resource, whose value  is exploit ed first t hrough 
reuse and then in its recycling. 
 
The affixation of mu ltiple packaging handling instructions, which  risk being contradictory, 
goes again st The Brewer s o f Europe commitment to simplify  the message to the 
consumer. The deposit log o already conve ys the message of both reusability and 
recyclability of the packaging. In addition, the  deposit approach guarantees a proper 
handling of the packaging either through reuse or in the rec ycling scheme. There is no 
need for duplication of logos/mentions that risk ultimately having the effect of reducing the 
effectiveness of well-functioning, existing systems, with consumers forfeiting their deposit.  
 
Equally, Europe’s brewers see no need for a harmonized deposit label at European level: a 
further change would c onfuse and might discourage consumers to ensure separate 
collection and re turn of the packaging . Equally, a harmonized deposit label wo uld b e 
misleading the cons umer: with national sch emes in force defining national amounts of 
deposits, he/she could not expect to receive the dep osit back if he wis hes to leave behind  
his beverage container in a country different to t he one he /she bought it from. Europe’s 
brewers, who have taken a le adership role in the de velopment of deposit sche mes in the 
Member States, thus share the ask of The Association of Deposit and Return Systems  for 
harmonisation at national level, not at European Union level. 
 
All returnable/reusable packaging currently on the market which are not indelibly marked 
for, a mongst other s, tracking  purposes would need to be unnecessar ily replaced, well 
before the end of their life. This would generate a huge amount of packaging rat her than 
reduce packaging.

✓ Compliance 
A high number of documents must be  provided so that  compliance checks can be

The Brewers of Europe therefore re quests that the required documentation and its content

✓ Reuse 
Equally, ef ficient and effective  beer packaging reuse sy stems put in place since many

Brewers in particularly seek to do away with the re d tape that is suggested in terms of

Legislation must be enforceable 
 
Legal certainty 
Policy meas ures must be supported by clear  criteria and de finitions, allowing for a clear  
understanding by both operators and compliance authorities. A unifo rm int erpretation 
allows a level playing field amongst operators and provides them with legal certainty.  
 
For several of the proposed measur es, further impl ementing acts to clarify e.g. s cope, 
exemptions and testing prot ocols still need to be developed. This raises questions on the 
credibility of the impact assessment tabled. 
 
 Entry into force 
Targets for which secondary legislation still needs to be drafted should only enter into force 
5 years after the publication of the relevant delegated acts. Operators need the lead-in time 
to replace packaging equipment and/or to change distribution systems. Equally, the set-up 
of deposit return schemes requires several years, as evidenced by the systems set up or in 
the process of being set up in the meantime.

 Re-usable packaging targets 
✓ No one-size fits all approach 
Producers operating solely on the basis of non-reusable packaging should not be pus hed 
out of the market, in particular if their packaging is recyclable and will be recycled at a very 
high percen tage, whether or not  as part of deposit return systems . The exemptions  
currently identifie d in the draf t text are falling short of the European Union ’s ambition to 
support small and medium -sized enterprises. A producer with only one single use bottling 
line may not have the re sources to inve st into reusable packaging lines, may not be on 
time with tho se investments, may not ha ve the area needed to invest in additional space 
required for operating reusable productio n lines or may no t receive the production perm it 
(Industrial Emissions Directive) , as a switch to reusable packaging  is having  more 
environmental impacts than a single use packaging line.  
 
✓ Kegs must be accounted for 
Furthermore, European brewers question the need for reusable targets on beer.  With 
about one third of beer sales through on -trade, which is characterised by the use of 
reusable packaging – i.e. kegs, the voluntary reuse systems set up in many countr ies and 
the strong involvement in national deposit systems, the brewing s ector i s exerting 
leadership in sustaina ble packaging. These kegs are sales packaging according to the 
Food Information to Consumers legislation. Equally, contra ry to the transport packaging 
definition, a keg does not contain measurable single units. Consequently, if beer – whether 
alcoholic or non-alcoholic – is to be covered by targets, these should be calculated against 
total volumes of beer put on the mar ket in reusable packaging. In that respect, it is crucial 
that a convers ion methodology takes the amount of s ervings per beer keg into account to 
calculate the re-use target achievement. 
 
✓ Aggregated re-use targets for all beverages 
Breweries often produce both alcoh olic and n on-alcoholic beers. In addition, many 
breweries also put soft drinks or waters on the market. Hence, it should be possible for a 
beverage company to assess compliance with the reuse targets holistically across  product 
portfolio instead of per product category. 
 
DRS – priority access to recycled materials 
The DRS must provide for an o ption for beverage pro ducers/ importers to purchase a fair 
share of t he collected material in proportion to the containers they place on the mar ket, 
allowing them to be recycled into new containers . This will avoid down -cycling and ensure 
that recycled content targets are met. 
Product safety should not be endangered 
The proposal holds a number of provisions which ris k endangering the prod ucts’ integrity 
and safety: e.g. 
▪ Pallet wrappings, straps and securing straps are par t of the load securin g system 
and must exe rt the necessary holding forces on the load to prevent it from slipp ing 
or being damaged. Re usable securing devices cannot guarantee this and therefore 
represent an incalculable safety risk. 
▪ A crown cork needs virgin plastic to ensure that the container is 100%  sealed and 
the l iquid in the containers remains sa fe and retai ns its gustatory charact eristics. 
Metal cans are typically coated with an organic layer that protects the integrity of the 
can from effects of the  food/drink and preve nts chemical reactions bet ween the 
can's me tal and t he food/drink. This does not prevent them from being 100% 
recyclable. No viable alternative is yet available on the market for virgin plastic bags 
used in beer tanks.

The Single Market at risk 
 
The Brewers of Europe welcomes the mo ve from a Directi ve to a Regula tion. This should 
in principle enhance the legal certainty for the economic operators.  
 
Member Stat es, though, should not be en couraged to create stricter rules and in crease 
burdens for econom ic operators . The Single Market w ould be weake ned rather than 
strengthened. Where current national rules are more ambitious  then the forthcoming rules, 
Member States should be allowed to pursue them. 
 
 
Conclusion 
 
Whilst me eting the targets and obligations laid  down in any final legis lation will be a 
challenge, the brewing sector’s comm itment to a sust ainable fut ure for beer i s real. We  
recognise the responsibility we must deliver, within a good functioning E uropean Union 
Single Market, on an ambitious European Union-wide environmental sustainability agenda, 
including for packaging and packaging waste. 
 
The Europe an brewing sec tor is com mitted to collaborating with the leg islator and the 
packaging supply chain to ens ure that the forthcoming packagi ng and packagin g waste 
Regulation will contribute to the wider European Union sustainability agenda.

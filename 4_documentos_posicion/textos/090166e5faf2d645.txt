 
Proposal for a Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (PPWR) TOMRA feedback on the European Commission’s proposal 
Summary 
TOMRA, the leading solutions provider for the collection and sorting of waste for reuse and recycling, welcomes the Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation proposal by the European Commission, which sets ambitious but achievable goals during a timeframe to enable investment.  Harmonisation across the European Single Market is needed to provide the necessary predictability for investors to scale up needed capacities and we support the proposal to replace the Directive with a Regulation with direct effect. In particular, we welcome the following measures: • Waste prevention and reusable packaging, as it is essential that we implement measures higher up on the waste hierarchy, as defined under the Waste Framework Directive, in our efforts to reduce waste.  • Recycled content targets for packaging containing a plastic part, which will not only increase the uptake of secondary raw materials but will provide recycling players with the legal certainty they need to scale up needed infrastructure.  • Recyclability criteria will help ensure that we meet the objective of packaging that is recyclable both by design and in practice – we believe this should be achieved by 2030 as set in the Circular Economy Action Plan. The delegated acts and the methodology to assess recyclability (at scale) should be available in due time to allow the industry to adapt with the new rules.  • Strengthened separate collection requirement and, in particular, the introduction of well-designed Deposit Return Systems (DRS) for plastic and metal beverage containers which are proven to increase capture rates, reduce littering, and increase circularity of materials. At the same time, given the intention for all packaging to be recyclable by 2030, (energy) recovery and landfilling of recyclable packaging materials should no longer be tolerated.  Welcoming the overall direction of this new proposal, we do however note that several significant aspects of the Regulation are left to secondary legislation. Given the strong impact this Regulation will have on stakeholders all across the value chain, it will be of key importance to include a wide variety of stakeholders in the elaboration of said delegated acts. Similarly, despite the  complexity of the file, TOMRA insists on the importance of having it finalised in time by mid-2024 to create the legal certainty necessary to kick-start the foreseen changes.

Below is a more detailed summary of our key proposals for further improving the Commission’s proposal to achieve the goals of reducing packaging waste and improving packaging circularity. 
Key proposals  
1. Prevention/ Reuse  Given the growing amounts of packaging and associated generation of waste, TOMRA welcomes the ambition of the PPWR proposal of moving up the waste hierarchy, by introducing targets for the prevention of packaging waste and reuse of packaging. Such binding targets provide the necessary legislative framework to reverse the current trend. Further, the introduction of targets for reusable packaging sends a strong market signal and creates the appropriate environment to enable long-term investment. This is particularly true for the food and beverage take-away sectors, where the proposed targets could have a strong transformational effect, and greatly reduce waste generation.  At the same time, TOMRA believes that it is important that reuse targets be focused on the sectors where they have the highest potential for proven environmental benefits, using an evidence-based approach.  The regulation sets, for the first time, quantitative targets on the top two layers of the waste hierarchy – prevention and reuse. Although reuse does contribute to waste prevention, it is important to keep the targets consistent. In particular, reuse needs to be differentiated from refill: as outlined in recital (65), refill is a prevention measure and should therefore count towards the prevention targets set under Art. 38, it should not be included in the reuse targets under Art.26 paragraphs (2) to (6).  With the current Directive Member States have the possibility to include reuse towards the achievement of their recycling targets, accounting for up to 5 percentage points. However, given that the Regulation now proposes to introduce separate and specific reuse targets, it is no longer justified to account reuse towards recycling targets – or at least not for those packaging types for which explicit targets are set under Art.26. Therefore, the text under Art.48 (1) needs to be adjusted accordingly for the 2030 targets. The proposal stipulates that the rules on how the reuse targets will be measured are to be defined in implementing acts by end 2028. We stress that such a timeline would leave only one year until targets need to be achieved and is too late. The rules on measurement must be available much earlier to allow sufficient time to industry to adapt their packaging and put in place efficient reuse systems and necessary infrastructure. 2. Recycled content  TOMRA strongly supports the targets set on recycled content for all packaging containing a plastic part, as they will create the necessary conditions for the development of a functioning secondary raw materials market. These ambitious targets will not only increase 
 
the uptake of secondary raw materials (and thereby decrease reliance on virgin feedstock and consequently fossil fuels), they will also create the necessary conditions for long-term investment in recycling infrastructures as they provide the certainty recyclers need via stable demand for recycled materials. We also welcome the differentiation of targets according to categories of packaging which reflect the current regulatory and technical challenges of integrating recycled content into PET vs non-PET and contact-sensitive vs non-contact sensitive applications.  Apart from the category of “single-use plastic beverage bottles”, placing the targets for the other categories on a per-unit basis is overly restrictive and in some cases technically unfeasible in the short to mid-term. A more flexible – yet robust – approach is therefore recommended to balance the possibility of using varying levels of recycled content in different applications while still keeping the intended overall market demand for recyclates. The intention should be to keep the maximum possible amount of plastic materials at their highest value, for as long as possible – therefore circularity should not necessarily be limited to the packaging sector once the quality is not suited for that sector anymore. TOMRA supports a resource hierarchy whereby materials are turned back to the same or similar application, employing the least energy and resource-intensive processes (mechanical recycling as priority: food contact – other contact-sensitive – non-contact sensitive – durable plastic applications; chemical recycling for degraded/ contaminated plastic waste).  Exemptions from recycled content targets should not be granted to compostable plastics – apart from those packaging formats for which compostability is mandated under Art. 8 (1) – as this could be a loophole for avoiding the recycled content requirement and might lead to material substitutions. Compostable plastics are required to go for material recycling according to Art. 8 (3) and can thus generate recycled content to be integrated in new products.    While we fully support the explicit focus on plastic packaging for recycled content targets given the challenge of low uptake for that material, we would recommend a material-neutral wording when it comes to general provisions related to recycled content.  In particular, the modulation of EPR fees based on the percentage of recycled content should not be limited to plastic packaging only. To avoid unnecessary material substitutions and market disruptions, and at the same time expand the benefits of wider use of recyclates, we would advise an assessment of the situation and suitability of setting targets on other materials earlier than the 8-year timeline proposed under Art.7 (11).  Finally, it is important to stress that the recycled content provisions are very closely linked to both recyclability (packaging designed to be recycled provide and uptake high-quality recycled materials) and to the provisions on collection and sorting (in order to ensure producers can access the necessary secondary raw materials to comply with the legislation, it is crucial to ensure enough material is captured and effectively recycled to the required quality).  
 
3. Recyclability  In line with the objectives of the Circular Economy Action Plan, TOMRA strongly welcomes the introduction of requirements on packaging recyclability and linking those to EPR fees through eco-modulation. In our view, to be considered recyclable by 2030, packaging needs to fulfil both criteria – being designed for recycling and effectively recycled in practice (“recycled at scale”) by 2030. We understand that the details of how the two requirements are to be operationalised is a complex issue and it is proposed to address them at a later stage through delegated acts establishing design for recycling and performance grading criteria and methodology to assess “recyclability at scale”. However, the timeline for when this secondary legislation should be available is not defined – yet it is critical to provide this clarity well in advance to allow the industry to adapt to the new rules. Today, important standardisation efforts are ongoing and mature design for recycling criteria and guidelines already exist and can be relied upon. We urge to base any harmonised EU-wide criteria as intended by the Regulation proposal on already existing guidelines/ standards to accelerate the overall process and provide clarity and direction for the industry as soon as possible.  Once packaging is designed for recycling, collection and sorting it is a prerequisite for it to be effectively recycled in practice and at scale. Knowing that all packaging placed on the EU market will be recyclable by design by 2030 and in application of the EU waste hierarchy, it should be ensured that as of 2030 no packaging ends in incineration or landfill without the chance of it being sorted out and recycled. Therefore, the requirements for separate collection should be further strengthened to ensure all (recyclable) packaging gets sorted out even from non-separately collected/ mixed waste and is sent for recycling instead of incineration or landfill.  4. Separate collection/ DRS  Without significant increases in collection of all types of packaging, the recyclability of packaging, as well as the achievement of ambitious recycling and recycled content targets, will be compromised.  TOMRA strongly supports the increased emphasis on separate collection under Article 43 and the further provisions under Article 44 to introduce DRS as the ultimate form of separate collection and the proven most effective way to achieve high collection rates for plastic and metal beverage containers. High collection rates coupled with high levels of closed loop recycling – as typically achieved through well-designed DRS – have the potential to significantly increase circularity of the targeted materials.  In that sense we find it important to link the DRS to high performance of 90% separate collection as defined in the proposal. While we agree that it should be possible for a Member State to apply for an exemption from the DRS requirement, the conditions for a possible exemption need to be strengthened, and more transparency and verification on reported performance needs to be established.  
 
- The current wording is inconsistent and opens for loopholes: o Art.44 (1) point (c): while the Member States need to file for an exemption by 1 January 2027 based on data they need to provide for 2026 and 2027 o Art.50 (7) point (a): the rules for the calculation, verification and reporting of data on separate collection are to be set in an implementing act to be adopted 24 months after entry into force (2024/2025) which would mean around mid-2026/2027 o Art.50 (3) point (b): and the data is only required to be reported starting from 1 January 2028 (and made available electronically within 19 months after the end of the reporting period according to Art.50 (4));  Directive (EU) 2019/904 (“The Single-Use Plastics Directive”) only requires reporting on single-use plastic beverage bottles and the Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2021/1752 provides methodology for the calculation of only those. Timing is not fit for the purposes of an exemption under the PPWR:  o The first reporting period is the calendar year 2022;  o Data to be reported electronically within 18 months of the end of the reporting year for which they were collected (i.e. for 2022 – data would be available by Jul 2024; for 2023 – by Jul 2025; for 2024 – by Jul 2026, for 2025 – by Jul 2027…). The setting of minimum requirements in Article 44 and Annex X are an important step to ensure that DRS are designed to function to the best of their capabilities and prevent distortions of the internal market through uniform application across Member States. Based on TOMRA’s 50 years of experience with design and operation of more than 40 deposit systems globally, we have gathered learnings from the world’s highest performing systems in a whitepaper on well-designed DRS1. We welcome that many of the key elements we have identified are taken up in the proposed Annex X. At the same time, it is necessary to acknowledge that most existing DRSs in Europe are already well designed and highly performing, yet some of them might not be 100% designed according to the requirements of Annex X. In such a case, we would suggest for a possibility of a derogation for existing systems achieving the required level of performance as defined in Art.44 (3) (i.e. separate collection of the targeted materials above 90%). Finally, we would like to note that the setting up of well-designed DRS for single-use beverage containers also contributes to creating the preconditions and partly the infrastructure for well-functioning take-back of reusable beverage bottles. 5. Secondary legislation Although TOMRA welcomes the ambitious provisions set out in the European Commission’s proposal, we note that many key parameters would need to be defined at a later stage through secondary legislation. This includes for example calculation methods for the share of recycled content, reporting and labelling requirements, or the recyclability criteria under Article 6.  This secondary legislation will determine how the Regulation is implemented in practice, it will have a significant impact for the industry. At the same time, it is also the actors across the value chain that have a key expertise and knowledge in practicality. Therefore, TOMRA wishes to stress that stakeholder consultation and engagement in the process of developing secondary legislation will be crucial.   1 https://circular-economy.tomra.com/resources/drs-0  
 
6. Conclusion Overall, TOMRA welcomes this proposal for a Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation, which has the potential to be a game changer and create the necessary framework for circularity in the packaging sector, which still represents a significant share of waste in the EU.  The provisions on recycled content, separate collection, waste prevention and recyclability can have a significant impact on the circularity of packaging, by ensuring packaging that is designed to be recycled is effectively captured and treated once it has been used by consumers, to feed once again into new products.  Here, it is key to underline that although this file is particularly complex, TOMRA insists on the importance of having it finalised in time by mid-2024 to create the legal certainty necessary to kick-start the foreseen changes.  TOMRA is committed to constructively contribute with our expertise, and we remain fully available for any questions.

Art. 43 allows Member States to derogate from the obligation for separate collection, essentially where this does not affect the potential of the packaging waste to undergo recycling and results in an output of comparable quality to that achieved through separate collection. Packaging for which Member States make use of this derogation, under the stated conditions, should be considered as equally recycled at scale as packaging whose waste is separately collected, as the conditions ensure an equivalent environmental impact. Art.43 §2 Member States may allow derogations from paragraph 1 provided that collecting packaging or fractions of packaging waste together or together with other waste does not affect the potential of such packaging or fractions of packaging waste to undergo preparing for re-use, recycling or other recovery operations in accordance with Articles 4 and 13 of Directive 2008/98/EC and generates output from those operations which is of comparable quality to that achieved through separate collection.

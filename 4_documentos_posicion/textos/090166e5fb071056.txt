 
 
 
Vattenfall response to the Consultation on the EU Electricity Market Reform  
(Proposal for a regulation - COM(2023)148) 
 
26/04/2023 
Vattenfall is a European energy company with approx imately 20,000 employees. For more 
than 100 years , we have electrified industries, supplied energy to pe ople's homes and 
modernized our way of living, through innovation and cooperation. Our goal is to make fossil-
free living possible within one generation. Every thing we do and the decisions we take shall 
lead to this goal. This is the basis of Vattenfall’s strategy, and we advocate for a regulatory 
environment that makes this transition possible – in the energy sector and beyond in transport, 
industry, and other sectors. 
With regard to the European Commission proposal for the EU electricity market reform, we like 
to share the following views: 
 
We welcome that the European Commission has chosen an evolution of the power 
market legislation instead of a revolution 
• Given the short preparation time for the reform, fundamental changes would have risked 
fossil free investments into the EU power market at times where these are needed more 
than ever.  
 
• One-sided revenue caps for existing and/or new assets  need to be avoided . Revenue 
caps can limit investment in the energy sector and reduce the competitiveness of the 
market.  
 
• Additionally, any proposals that seek to regulate wholesale prices or split the market could 
also have negative consequences for the sector's growth and further jeopardize security of 
supply.  
 
We support that the reform leaves room for national implementation 
• Last year has shown, that the situation in each of the EU countries differs  – be it a 
different degree of decarbonization of the power system, a different preference for 
technologies or a different industry landscape.  
• We welcome that the prop osals allow different options to governments  on how to 
decarbonize and drive investments going forward. 
• The proposed reform should prioritize the development of a robust market framework that 
incentivizes investments in new fossil free energy production.  This will help to bridge the 
supply-demand gap and reduce reliance on fossil fuels.

• Commercial investments need to be kept possible and financial support schemes 
for clean electricity generation, flexibility or capacity by governments need to remain 
voluntary. 
• The wording of the Contracts for Difference (CfD) for new investments nee ds 
clarification to ensure that it is clear and unambiguous.  
 
Consumers should be protected and empowered 
• We share the interest of consumers and decision makers to make our energy system future 
proof and ensure mechanisms are in place to protect (vulnerable) customers from 
sustained periods of soaring prices as seen in the crisis.  
• Suppliers should advise customers on variable tariffs how to avoid "bill shocks" in case 
prices increase  
• While it is reasonab le to monitor supplier risk management , it is equally important to 
avoid prescriptive measures that could hinder the ability of market participants to hedge 
effectively – we therefore do not support the potential obligation on suppliers to enter PPAs. 
• Consumers need to be well informed and having competitive contract choices, 
including their duration and appropriate levels of protection.  
 
Grid development needs to go in tandem with the market reform 
• To deliver affordable and clean power to the consumers, we need a forward-looking tariff 
methodology for the electricity grid. 
• Additionally, the promotion of long -term transmission rights  will help to improve the 
competitiveness of the market by making sure sold capacity has guarantee d transport to 
the end-user.  
• Virtual hubs are a novel concept that should be assessed by ENTSO-E before being 
integrated into the market framework , to avoid unnecessary complexity – we do not 
need solutions that make the market design more complex than it currently is. 
 
Overall, the proposed reform should aim to complete and simplify the current market design, 
restoring consumer and investor confidence in the market while achieving climate goals at 
affordable prices and maintaining our standard of living. Done correctly and swiftly, the reform 
will create a sustainable and secure energy future for Europe.

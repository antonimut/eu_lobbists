On 13 February 2023. The European Commission (EC) published a draft directive, in which 
it included its proposals for new Occupational Exposure Limits (OELs) and Biological 
Material Limits Value (BLVs) for lead. These are: 
• OEL - 0,03 mg/m3 (a value of 0.05 mg/m3 is currently in force in Poland, which is 
currently one of the strictest standards in the EU Member States); 
• BLV - 15 ug/dL (the current value in Poland is 50 ug/dL). 
 
The European Commission foresees a period of two years for Member States to comply 
with the new legal requirements. The proposal does not provide for an additional 
transitional period for the implementation of the new standards, which would adequately 
take into account the specificities of the industries in the Member States (as was the case, 
for example, for the exhaust gases of from diesel engines in the mining industry). In 
motivating such r adical changes, the Commission points out in the first instance, the 
health protection of the industry's employees and, in its view, the small social and 
economic cost. 
 
In our view, the proposals put forward not only represent a significant cost of adjustment 
investments, a sharp increase in operating costs, and, as a consequence, the bankruptcy 
of many smaller and medium -sized enterprises and and group redundancies in large 
enterprises, but also the need to actually cease lead production in Poland. All this will also 
result, inter alia, in an increase in the mass of stored lead-bearing waste, which is currently 
managed in production processes. In practice, therefore, instead of intended to protect 
the health and economic security of Europeans employed in lead production and related 
industries - the new regulations will directly hit the most basic interests of a significant 
group of workers.

The current Polish OEL value of 0.05 mg/m3 is one of the of the strictest hygiene standards 
found in EU Member States . Adapting production processes to the required standard 
required (and still requires) industry to implement many modern solutions and significant 
investments in reducing the absorption of lead by the human body in the in the 
workplace. It also involved th e consistent implementation of many organisational and 
educational programmes to protect the health of our employees, both related to work 
and non-occupational activities. As a result of the actions taken, today we do not have any 
employees at KGHM Polska Miedź S.A. with blood lead levels above the BLV value 
applicable in Poland. It should be noted, however, that achieving such a result was the 
result of a long-term process (it took more than 10 years to gradually descend to values 
currently recorded below the level of 50 ug/dL), which was conducted with the 
participation of Polish medical communities. 
In the case of KGHM Polska Miedź S.A., i.e. one of the few European producers of lead 
from waste arising during the refining of polymetallic concentrates, the fact that all the 
lead-bearing materials we produce are generated as a by -product of the main cop per 
production process remains a fundamental issue. Currently, as part of the 
implementation of the Closed Circuit Economy, lead -bearing materials are processed to 
produce a more environmentally friendly product. Discontinuation of this activity would 
lead to high fixed costs associated with the need to maintain and continually expand 
landfills for hazardous waste, such as lead -bearing materials. In turn, the possible 
necessity to cease their production would require the cessation of our basic activity 
(which, apart from many other consequences, would also be an action contrary to the 
provisions of the Polish geological law, i.e. an unreasonable development of a polymetallic 
copper ore deposit in the form of abandoning a portion of its exploitation) or at le ast - 
requiring outlays of PLN hundreds of millions – a complete encapsulation of the current 
production line (including the construction of new elements of the production line). As a 
result, we would have to deal with a situation which would fundamentally alter the current 
market position of KGHM Polska Miedź S.A., which employs almost twenty thousand 
people, and of the recipients of lead produced here, including primarily entities from the 
domestic automotive industry (our country is currently one of Europe's largest producers 
of lead-acid batteries). 
We would also like to emphasise that KGHM Polska Miedź S.A. conducts continuous 
activities aimed at implementing modern solutions with respect to protecting the health 
of our employees, local communities and the natural environment. We can boast of many 
investments in the health protection of our employees, the organisational and 
educational programmes carried out, as well as the implementation of specific solutions 
to reduce lead absorption in the workplace. These solutions have been in place in the

In acco rdance with the Strategy of KGHM Polska Miedź S.A., investment and 
modernisation processes carried out in the process line area focus on improving working 
conditions through automation and the encapsulation of installations. The largest 
activities in this area in the last 10 years included the Project of modernisation of

which, apart from its technological and environmental effects, also contributed to an 
improvement in working conditions for employees employed there. 
Since 2019, KGHM Polska Miedź S.A. has been working on adapting the installations 
operating at the company's smelters to the requirements of the BAT (conclusions. In the 
years 2019-2022, investment expenditures related to the implementation of these tasks 
amounted to PLN 328 million. In the following years, further investment activities will be 
carried out in line with, among other things, adaptation to the new OEL standards. Thanks 
to systematic modernisation, and sometimes changes in the technology used, as well as 
encapsulation of production processes, we were able to meet the restrictive requirements 
of BAT regulations. All of this gave us reason to believe that the technological and 
organisational effort and the investment outlays made will allow us to operate confidently 
for the next 15-20 years. 
Effective adaptation by KGHM Polska Miedź S.A. to the new OEL and BLV values for lead 
will take time. Further changes in the technologies used, organisational pr ocesses and 
further significant financial outlays are challenges which neither we - one of the largest 
companies in Poland, nor other, much smaller lead producers and processors in the 
current macroeconomic situation will not be able to meet. As a conseque nce of the 
proposed changes, KGHM Polska Miedź S.A. – Europe's leading producer of copper, the 
world's first producer of silver and a leading producer of other metals – will overnight be 
relegated to the group of plants which do not meet key regulatory standards. 
That is why, when taking part in last year's public consultation, we submitted a reasoned 
position paper to the European Commission in which we indicated the fo llowing BLV 
values and the time horizons for achieving them:  
• 35 ug/dL with a transition period of 5 years from the entry into force of the Directive 
on chemical agents (no earlier than 2028);

It should also be underlined that, during the setting of new OELs and BLVs for lead, there 
were significant differences of opinion within the European Commission's advisory body, 
the Advisory Committee on Safety and Health (ACSH), and that the social partners 
(workers', governmental and employers' representative s) were not able to reach a 
consensus opinion on this issue. 
We also have a number of concerns about the Impact Assessment document 
accompanying the draft directive. In our opinion, it is not an objective analysis of the 
impact of the new regulations for the OEL and BLV values for lead on the EU economy, its 
member states and the condition of workers and companies that will have to create the 
conditions for implementing the new regulations. Our comments on some of the 
provisions of this document are presented below.  
To conclude, we would also like to draw attention to an extremely important aspect: the 
security of Europe's raw materials. Its importance is particularly highlighted now, in the 
political and economic reality of the war in Ukraine. In our view,  this conflict should – 
according to the ensuing facts – significantly change the perception of having one's own 
European raw material base. Regrettably, in this document this perspective has not been 
taken into account even in the smallest degree.

KGHM Polska Miedź S.A. comment 
It is very important to highlight the differences related to working  conditions and 
technological constraints between lead producers from metal concentrates and lead 
recyclers and processors. 
Metal producers that process ores and concentrates containing dusty lead currently 
employ significantly more workers with blood lead levels above 15 ug/dL than do recyclers 
and other sectors. Therefore, while a BLV of 15 ug/dL may ultimately be technically 
feasible for most companies, at the same time it may also prove to be a significant and 
disproportionate challenge for primary source lead plants. 
The smelting of feedstock from virgin sources, requires more production steps than the 
production of lead from recycled materials. The technological process of smelting lead 
from primary sources also leads to the generation of waste materia ls that carry harmful 
chemical compounds. Production from primary sources is associated with a multi -stage 
refining process involving separation from other metals with similar physical and chemical 
properties.  
Discontinuing the processing of lead -bearing materials would result in their storage, 
which would would consequently lead to significant environmental damage. 
 
Relevant passage 
Pg.84 - "In terms of international competitiveness, only three countries outside the EU 
currently have a BLV for lead. However, most of them have an OEL that would correspond 
to a BLV of 30 μg/100ml. Therefore, the impact on companies working with lead should 
be moderate." 
KGHM Polska Miedź S.A. comment 
In our view, this demonstrates that countries outside the EU have a more pragmatic 
approach to the OEL / BLV issue. While the OEL is an unambiguous parameter, referring 
to the scope of the employer's health and safety activities, the BLV value is not fully under 
the employer's control - this parameter is also influenced by environmental factors and 
the employee's life habits, which are beyond the employer's control (e.g. eating habits, 
use of stimulants including tobacco). For this reason, outside the EU, t he issue of BLV is 
not categorically regulated. The recommended BLV value of 15 ug/dL, will be 2 times more 
stringent than the values adopted outside the EU. Such a situation is incomprehensible 
and must lead to a deterioration in the competitiveness of co mpanies doing business 
within the EU.

Relevant passage 
Pg.111 - "The feedback from businesses and business organisations is in line with the 
position of the employers' organisations presented during the consultation of the with 
the EU social partners. In the case of lead, they recognise that both OELs and BLVs need 
to be revised. However, the revised limits must be proportionate to the risk and take into

We fully agree with this position. The Commission/Consultants, in this part of the impact

Relevant passage 
Pg.133 - "Employers will also gain cost savings through their employees being less 
exposed to occupational diseases." 
KGHM Polska Miedź S.A. comment 
The authors do not clearly demonstrate such a correlation and base their theses on 
conjecture and general quibbles.  
It is clear that lowering hygienic exposure risk thresholds can affect the social costs of 
protecting workers' health. However, it should be noted that any programmes aimed at 
improving the health of populations are, by definition, long -term in nature. We do not 
treat occupational health and safety issues in terms of costs. In line with our internal 
commitment, we will bear the higher costs of preventive health care associated with the 
reduction of norms, regardless of the final decision of the regulator. All we want is - as is 
the case in the health sector as a whole - for the adjustment processes to be spread over 
a realistic, all-inclusive timeframe.  
For example, the European plan against cancer, adopted in 2022, provides, among other 
things, for a 5% re duction in tobacco use per population by 2040, i.e. over a period of 8 
years. This is a reasonable approach, but inconsistent with the plan for changes in 
biological norms for lead. In the context of a two -year adjustment period for this metal, 
the European Commission should consequently ban tobacco use in the population of lead 
workers 20 years ago, as this is how long it takes for lead to be released from the smoker's 
hard tissue.

CRM Alliance Position Paper on the Critical Raw Materials Act 
Introduction 
The Critical Raw Materials Act is an important step for Europe’s ambition to secure a competitive domestic value 
chain for critical and strategic raw materials. The CRM Alliance welcomes the proposal, and the ambition Europe 
has for the raw materials industry sector. However, more action is needed to achieve these objectives. 
 
Securing the sustainable access and supply of both critical and strategic raw materials will not be achieved by 
2030 within the context of the CRM Act and Communication alone. Following a careful and regular assessment 
of the CRM Act, the CRM Alliance has propose d the following recommendations that we believe could further 
support the achievement of the objectives of the regulation. 
 
To summarise this paper, the CRM Alliance proposes: 
 
• Early socio -economic impact assessments should automatically be triggered in all legislation aiming to 
restrict the use of critical and strategic raw materials.  
• All uses of critical raw materials serving applications and technologies deemed strategic for the EU shall be 
considered to meet the Essential Use Concept. 
• The Commission and Member States should provide greater clarity as to how State Aid rules would be used 
and introduce grant, loan and tax credit mechanisms to support the creation of new facilities in the EU and 
third countries.  
• The Commission should provide de -risking mechanisms to support strategic/critical raw materials  
investments of strategic importance for the EU realised in third countries, in the same way as projects 
realised within the EU. 
• The Commission should protect European strategic raw materials’ production from unfair trade practices by 
maintaining and strengthening trade defense measures, in order to ensure a level playing field. 
• Future strategic and critical raw materials lists may also be accompanied by an “im pact assessment” of 
existing EU legislation and the impact it has on materials on those lists. 
 
Recommendations 
 
• Integrating the critical and strategic raw material concepts in broader legislation and creating tailored 
regulatory regimes  
The absence of a harmonised legislative approach to critical raw materials has led to the unnecessary targeting 
of these materials under several and different regulatory frameworks and to the creation of regulatory barriers 
negatively impacting the supply of these materials , the competitiveness  of the EU industry and  access to 
investment.  
 
For instance, suggested OEL values, the Essential Use Concept and forthcoming classifications of substances in 
the value chain could limit the production and use of certain materials. Moreover, REACH and OSH rules have 
the potential to impact the ability of European industry to develop supply for these materials in a major way.  
 
The CRM Alliance calls for more consistency between the existing and ongoingregulations under revisio n by 
acknowledging legal grounding to the terms “strategic raw material” and “critical raw material”, to ensure rules 
established under these pieces of legislation are compatible with political ambitions.  
 
Additionally, early socio-economic impact assessments should automatically be triggered in all legislation aiming 
to restrict the use of critical and strategic raw materials. Indeed, the status of these materials should require 
additional assessment on the impact regulatory provisions would have on the investment climate and 
competitiveness of the targeted materials, in line with the better regulation principles.

deemed strategic for the EU shall be considered to me et the Essential Use Concept laid down in the Taxonomy

• Creating a favorable investment environment in the EU 
In order to meet the ambitious benchmarks included in the CRMA, businesses will need to invest in mining, 
processing and refining, and recycling capacity. These are capital-intensive projects and therefore, whether self-
financed or financed externally, require detailed business plans and forecasts to be produced, before sites or 
locations are chosen. This includes an assessment of the d ownside risks from regulatory changes and financial 
incentives in place. 
 
By implementing a harmonised approach to critical and strategic raw materials, coupled with consistent 
legislation and industry incentives, a  stable and coherent legislative environ ment for raw materials would be 
created, providing businesses with predictability and certainty for their investments. The CRM Alliance recalls 
that the cost of energy and access to green energy are essential factors of the competitiveness of companies in 
the raw materials sector and that an ambitious and proportionate European energy policy for all Member States 
is urgently needed to the success of the objectives of the CRM Act. 
 
Moreover, the EU should prioritise establishing WTO-compliant incentives to ensure a level playing field globally. 
These could take the form of consumer incentives  for sustainable European raw materials  or support to 
manufacture more advanced facilities. 
 
The Commission and Member States should provide greater clarity as to how State Aid rules would be used and 
introduce grant, loan and tax credit mechanisms to support the creation of new facilities in the EU and third 
countries. These should be focuses on both operational and capital expenditure. 
 
• Providing a flexible framework supporting EU competitiveness   
The CRM Alliance is concerned that the benchmarks  in terms of sourcing, processing and recycling  are too 
general and unrealistic, and may result in unintended and negative consequences. 
 
Indeed, the CRM Act should allow for a certain  level of flexibility to best reflect the uniqueness of the value 
chain of the raw material targeted, since each material has specific properties and challenges associated with its 
sourcing, processing and recycling.  It should focus on maintaining existing capacities and supporting them.  An 
open and constant dialogue between industry and policymakers should be encouraged to identify benchmarks 
that are both technically and economically feasible, as well as in line with the EU’s objectives.  
 
Moroever, when introducing  obligations in terms of monitoring, certification schemes, environmental footprint, 
circularity measures, etc., a balanced approach is necessary to allow industry to comply with these obligations 
while guaranteeing a level playing field between EU and non-EU companies. Any regulation applying burdens to 
strategic and critical raw materials should be subject to a “check” on economic viability. 
 
The benefits of these provisions should be carefully considered against the economic and technical difficulties 
associated with their implementation and the risk of overburdening EU industry, in particular SMEs. Market -
based measures proven to be feasible, compatible with EU objectives and implementable by industry without 
excessive overburden should be preferred.  
 
• Strengthening Strategic Partnerships and de-risking investments 
Strategic Partnerships should be reinforced in the CRMA and include a much stronger global strategy for securing 
responsible imports, which avoids an overdependence on single suppliers and addresses trade distortions. These 
should be based on “win -win” agreements that support the developm ent of producer countries, including 
support for them to move down the value chain and secure more of the value-addition from their natural mineral 
wealth. This supports EU access to raw materials and sustainable, responsible economic growth in emerging 
nations. Examples of this can include critical infrastructure like road and railways, which boost growth in 
developing nations and make downstream refining and processing more commercially attractive (which are also 
more value-adding) but also make it easier for the EU to access the raw materials being created.  
 
The Commission should provide de -risking mechanisms to support strategic/critical raw materials investments 
of strategic importance for the EU realised in third countries, in the same way as projects realised within the EU 
(fast track and easier access to financial mechanisms such as loans, loan guarantees, grants, tax credits).  Trade

• Introducing a transparent assessment procedure for strategic materials  
Critical raw materials are assessed through a criticality assessment based on a specific methodology which 
includes a long and thorough validating process with the assistance of external experts, industry representatives 
and research institutes. On the contrary, the identification of strategic raw materials was not based on a 
published methodology nor did it include previous consultations. Incorporating strategic raw materials in to the 
critical raw materials list (Annex 2) implies that all of materials have met the thresholds set by the CRM 
methodology, which is misleading.  
 
It would be beneficial to have a transparent and clearly defined methodology for the assessment of materials to 
be included in the strategic raw materials list, including through the use of technical factsheets, similar to the 
methodology applied to the critical raw materials list. 
 
Future strategic and critical raw materials lists may also be accompanied by an “impact assessment” of existing 
EU legislation and the impact it has on materials on those lists. 
 
Conclusions 
The CRM Alliance encourages the co -legislators to consider the above-mentioned recommendations in the 
frame of their assessment of the CRMA with the aim to develop a regulatory framework fit to secure access and 
use of the raw materials needed to support EU industry and societal wellbeing. 
 
About us 
 
The Critical Raw Materials Alliance (CRM-Alliance) represents primary producers, traders and associations of raw 
materials that the European Commission has determined to be critical to the EU economy (CRMs).  Critical Raw 
Materials (CRMs) are those raw materials which are economically and strategically important for the European 
economy but have a high -risk associated with their supply. Used in environmental technologies, consumer 
electronics, health, steelmaking, defence, space exploration, and aviation, these materials are not only “critical” 
for key industry sectors and future applications, but also for the sustainable functioning of the European 
economy. 
 
Thus, the CRM Alliance stresses the need of a unique approach in regulation and policymaking when addressing 
CRMs to avoid overregulation, innovation barriers and loss of EU competitiveness and societal well-being.

A3M's position on the draft Regulation on Critical Raw Materials 
(CRM) published on 16 March 2023 
Paris, The 28th of April 2023  
 
A3M, which represents the metals mining, processing, and recycling industry, wishes to express its 
strong support for the draft European Union (EU) Regulation on Critical Raw Materials, aimed at 
ensuring EU's strategic autonomy. 
A3M is convinced that this regulation is crucial to ensure the resilience of th e European industry. The 
text should enable responsible sourcing of raw materials to ensure the achievement of Europe's 
strategic objectives, such as the energy transition in line with the Net Zero Industry Act, the 
development of the Union's strategic val ue chains, including the battery and defence industries, and 
by promoting a circular economy. These issues have been fully identified in the Commission's proposal 
and we welcome this. 
The draft regulation does indeed lay the foundations for a European resources policy by setting targets 
for extraction, transformation, recycling and diversification of EU supplies and within a regulatory 
framework favourable to the development of European strategic projects.   
However, to fully achieve these goals, the ambition of the regulation needs to be further increased , 
in order to ensure the level playing field, which is essential to compete with non -European players 
and to access the dedicated funding necessary to ensure the viability of CRM projects.  
As underlined by the Commission, this text focuses on the identification of cri tical,  strategic metals 
and strategic projects, while aiming at the acceleration of procedures. Nevertheless, A3M believes that 
this regulation must be as ambitious and precise as possible, to allow the development and 
deployment of responsible metal supp ly projects in the service of European sovereignty, while 
ensuring the articulation and coherence with other structuring European policies (energy, industrial, 
chemicals, circular economy and waste transfer). In this respect, A3M recalls that the cost of energy is 
an essential element of the competitiveness of companies in the sector.  
A3M also recalls that the diversification of raw material supplies for strategic European industries 
requires a policy of support for extra -European extraction, within the fr amework of identified 
projects, involving European players and in countries sharing our values. 
In this perspective, A3M is in favour of the adoption of the regulation before the European elections 
of 2024 and wishes to take full part in the discussions until then.

1. Effective control of environmental and social risks to protect the reputation of the 
value chain  
 
Achieving a level playing field is a prerequisite for the development of a sustainable European value 
chain. However, the provisions foreseen in the draft regulation are not sufficient.  
Indeed, A3M underlines that the alignment with ambitious Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) 
standards, which European companies are already adopting on their own, is currently not recognised 
by the European regulator as a precondition for access to the EU market. We deeply regret this. This 
lack of consensus on an appropriate CSR framework puts European offers in an unfair competitive 
situation with other actors that do not re spect the same rules, to the detriment of social and 
environmental standards. 
Article 29 of the Regulation provides that the Commission will be empowered, through implementing 
acts, to recognise certification schemes, covering for example environmental protection, social rights 
and corporate transparency (Annex IV). A3M supports this proposal  allowing to certify at European 
level, existing responsible mining standards. However, A3M believes that the regulation should detail 
more precisely the requirements n eeded for a standard to be recognized and thus, only allow the 
recognition of the most ambitious mining standards to date, such as IRMA and ICMM.  
In this way, Annex IV must be strengthened  by including more detailed requirements regarding 
biodiversity and damage to habitats, fauna, flora, and ecosystems, including a ban on deep sea tailing 
placement (DSTP).   
Finally, A3M calls for the CRM Act to impose on all actors in the value chain the same due diligence 
obligations and verification systems as those provided for in Art. 45 of the Batteries Regulation, which 
deals with the "obligations of economic operators with regard to due diligence policies", thus ensuring 
a true level playing field in environmental and social matters.  
A3M supports the provisions of article 30 aiming at the implementation of a carbon footprint 
obligation. However, it is necessary to remain vigilant as to the methodology used, in order to establish 
consistency with the  provisions of the ESPR , without jeopardizing the competitiveness of European 
companies through this obligation. 
 
2. Recognition of strategic projects and associated funding  
A3M welcomes the introduction of a process to identify strategic projects in the Union for extraction, 
processing and recycling,  which enhance the EU's security of supply for strategic raw materials . 
Compliance with relevant EU legislation and/or participation in a recognised certification scheme is 
considered sufficient.

A3M supports the notion of public interest in order to facilitate the authorisation of projects carried 
out under sustainable conditions; but nevertheless, regrets the vagueness surrounding many 
implementation points.  
 
The concrete process for selecting these projects remains to be specified, in particular the arbitration 
procedures and the role of the CRM Committee (board). The assessment criteria identified in the text 
need to be clarified in order to guarantee transparency and provide greater visibility for  operators, 
especially :  
- The notion of significant contribution to the EU's security of supply in strategic raw  materials; 
- The realisation within a reasonable period of time, production volume estimated with sufficient 
confidence; 
- The benefits of the projects at EU level, in particular for the downstream sectors; 
- The mutual benefit for the EU and third countries when the project is carried out in the countries 
concerned.  A3M supports the fact that strategic projects can also be defined in third countries  
and asks that this provision remains unchanged.  
 
The operational scope of this "strategic projects" labelling must also be specified:  
- On the technical maturity of projects: what minimum TRL level is required? 
- On the objectives of "financial support and development support" of the Critical Raw Material   
Board: what use is it if no financial tool can support such projects? 
- On the articulation with other regulations: the regulation specifies that the status does not exclude 
the application of the general requirements of international, EU and national regulations. 
 
Eligible recycling projects are not sufficiently identified (which metals and associated processes, what 
is the scope of funding). Article 25 of the Regulation remains to be clarified. In its current wording, this 
article targets "critical" materials. A3M considers that it should make it possible to identify projects of 
public interest, and wonders whether it could be applied to projects for recyclin g metals and alloys 
containing critical metals (e.g. nickel). Its scope should be clarified to also include strategic metals, such 
as scrap metal.  
 
We also note that for strategic projects, the Commission is setting up a system facilitating the 
conclusion of off-take agreements for strategic projects. A3M could support such a mechanism as long 
as these agreements remain at the initiative of the project leaders. 
 
On the lack of dedicated funding for projects: Article 15 provides that the CRM Committee should give 
recommendations on the possibilities for funding projects and supporting their development. No new 
dedicated funding for projects is provided for in the regulation, either for strategic projects or for 
projects relating to CRMs. This gives rise to fears of ambition without any real means associated with 
it.

A3M is in favour of a package of financing tools to facilitate the effective implementation of projects: 
it has high expectations for the rapid implementation of an ambitious European sovere ignty fund, 
which should be presented next summer , but above all it is aiming for the creation of a permanent 
European fund dedicated to critical/strategic raw materials (metals) within a maximum of one year 
after the adoption of the CRM act. In addition, the establishment of a PIIEC dedicated to strategic raw 
materials (upstream value chain and recycling) should be available, as well as the possibility of loans 
from EIB  type orga nisations. A3M also believes that the Global Gateway initiative could be 
strengthened, to finance raw materials projects. 
Finally, A3M notes and welcomes the Commission's recognition of the need to include mining and 
metallurgical activities in the taxonomy. These are essential to support the objectives of a sustainable 
supply of raw materials to the EU. The Commission's communication provides a first anchor that must 
be followed up before the European elections next year. A3M asks the Commission to take a position 
before the next elections on a timeframe within which mining activities should be integrated into the 
taxonomy. 
3. Setting ambitious targets in line with the raw materials targeted  
3.1- Objectives 
A3M supports the objectives set out in Article 1 of the Regulation which are the basis for a European 
resources policy. However, it is necessary to specify these objectives, as the current wording is still 
vague because they are not broken down by metal pr oduct. For example, 10% seems rather low for 
the extraction of certain metals where Europe has deposits/projects (but perhaps difficult for others, 
such as cobalt). It is therefore essential to support responsible European -owned mining projects 
outside Europe. 
For some metals, metal-specific targets could be considered based on accurate data. The flexibility 
of numerical targets is also important for recycling (example of low grade alloying elements in very 
small parts).  
Finally, in order to create ambition, long-term visibility and coherence with other regulations (example 
of the battery regulation), A3M is in favour of defining targets for 2040 and 2050. 
3.2- The metals concerned 
The draft regulation provides for two lists of metals and minerals: 
- Strategic raw materials (SRM): a short list of materials defined by their use in low -   
carbon/digital/defence energy technologies, characterised by a significant potential gap between 
global supply and expected demand and where an increase in production capacity is relatively 
difficult (see Annex I); 
- Critical raw materials (CRM): a broader list of other raw materials with a risk of supply        disruption 
(see Annex II).  
A3M sees this dual list as positive, as it allows the development of strategic projects to be targeted and 
prioritised, while at the  same time making it possible to anticipate the challenges of securing supply 
for a wider list of metals.

However, A3M encourages the Commission to extend the classification of nickel beyond just battery 
grade nickel, as nickel is key in many applications both as a primary and secondary raw material.  
(recycling of used steel into stainless steel). 
 
In addition, A3M notes that the list of CRMs in the US version is broader than that proposed in the 
European regulation1.  
 
 Furthermore, A3M notes the absence of zinc used for example in batteries but also in the 
infrastructure of photovoltaic panels and wind turbines. The energy crisis linked to the war in Ukraine 
has led to the temporary closure of many European sites. Finally, it should be noted that in France, the 
Zinc value chain also allows the recovery of Indium - which has been removed from the updated list of 
CRMs -, whose usefulness for the nuclear industry (e.g. control rods) is known. In fact, the steel mill 
dust recycling value chain is one of the elements of accessibility to two important metals, Zinc and 
Indium.   
Finally, A3M notes that the main metals in tension for rare earths composing permanent magnets are 
Neodymium, Praseodymium, Terbium and Dysprosium and suggests that Gd, Sm, Ce be removed from 
the list of strategic rare earths for magnets as they do not present availability problems.  
Concerning the timeframe for updating the lists, A3M noted that Article 3 of the Regulation provides 
for a review every 4 years. As the stress tests on the sector are scheduled every 3 years, it seems 
appropriate to align these periods in order to carry ou t a revision of the list in a way that is more 
consistent with the reality of the needs.  
A3M also points out that an ad hoc revision of the critical and strategic lists may be necessary in the 
event of exceptional circumstances and proposes the integration of an urgency criterion allowing the 
Commission to be asked to revise the list in such a case. 
Finally, no specific provisions are planned for situations where investments have been made due to the 
critical or strategic nature of a material, but the material is removed from the list on the occasion of a 
review. A3M therefore requests that consideration be given to the establishment of a transitional 
regime for the benefit of the project leader in this situation.  
1 Establish strategic international partne rships with the best equipped regions to ensure the 
short-term supply of strategic minerals 
This is an essential mechanism to ensure the EU's strategic independence objectives in raw materials. 
   
Europe must include chapters on responsible raw materials in all free trade agreements (FTAs) and 
develop strategic partnerships with countries that have the capacity to supply large volumes of critical 
raw materials over the next three to five years. 
- Strategic partnerships are to be signed with Indonesia for nickel and with Latin American 
countries for lithium. For example, A3M hopes that 2023 will see the finalisation of the free 
trade agreement with Indonesia, and the conclusion of a strategic partnership with Chile, 
following last year's FTA.

This is why A3M supports the mission that will be given to the Critical Metals Committee (Board), to 
arbitrate which states should be given priority in the signature of strategic partnerships on critical 
metals, in order to rapidly launch negotiations. Never theless, A3M requests that this arbitration be

Finally, A3M calls for chapters on environmental and social safeguards to be included in these

appropriate CSR framework. This is the only way to differentiate the European offer from that of 
competitors. These agreements must take into account the respect of WTO rules. 
 
 5. Simplification and acceleration of project procedures 
 
A3M welcomes the provisions on administrative simplification of projects and fully supports the 
coordination at national level of procedures and the shortening of authorisation periods, as well as 
the creation of a European Council for Critical Raw Materials. 
 
We note that the regulation provides for all CRMs: 
- Advice on financing and development by the CRM Committee; 
- The creation of one competent authority per country as a single point of contact for       
authorisation requests related to all CRMs; 
- Planning and zoning for project development. This may prove to be a constraint as zoning is often 
subject to environmental assessment and public consultation, this provision should be taken with 
caution and evaluated. 
 
For strategic raw materials, the regulation specifically provides :   
- Contentious and litigious procedures to be dealt with urgently by the Member States; 
- Permit applications not exceeding 24 months for extraction and 12 months for recycling and    
processing; 
- Validation of file documents within 1 month by the competent authority; 
- Public consultations carried out within a maximum of 3 months. 
 
A3M asks to clarify the operationality of this ambition in relation to the general requirements of 
international, EU and national law.

6. Strengthen the provisions on the circular economy 
The CRM Regulation provides that each state has 3 years after entry into force to put in place the 
following provisions:  
- Increase the collection of waste with a high potential for recovery of critical raw materials; 
- Increase the reuse of products and components with a high potential for recovery of critical raw 
materials; 
- Increase the use of critical secondary raw materials in manufacturing; 
- Increase technological maturity in recycling and promote material efficiency; 
- Ensuring that the workforce is equipped with the necessary skills to support circularity in the critical 
raw materials value chain. 
 
The provisions of Article 25 are very vague. The ambition needs to be raised by linking it to other 
regulations to achieve these objectives: ESPR, Waste Shipment Regulation, Chemicals Regulation, 
Batteries Regulation, DEEE... 
 
A3M underlines the fact that this article should in particular allow the promotion of projects to 
improve existing recycling (in particular of scrap metal) in order to achieve the objectives set out in 
Article 1 of the Regulation.  For example, several steel manufacturers are engaged in vertical 
integration on the collection/recovery side to ensure the sustainability and quality of their supply of 
raw materials, thus avoiding the departure from the continent of large quantities of unprocessed scrap 
metal. This activity has a positive externality (better sorting). To optimise this positive balance, support 
is needed (research programmes, investment support, partnerships, etc.) to improve the recovery rate 
of non-ferrous metals (copper in particular), but also of metals containing large amounts of ferro -
alloys (nickel). Full exploitation of the potential of recycling would thus reduce the pressure on 
extraction. 
 
7. Creation of a specific regulatory framework for permanent magnets (article 27 and 28) 
A3M welcome s the fact that the regulation includes obligations regarding permanent magnets: 
labelling and consumer information on the materials of the magnet as well as on its recyclability. 
Furthermore, A3M welcomes the specific consideration given to the recycling of rare earth -based 
permanent magnets by the delegated act and calls for the creation of a dedicated sub-group within 
the Critical Raw Materials Committee  (CRM Board). A3M will be vigilant on the outcome of the 
legislative process. In this sense, A3M stresses in particular that Article 28(3) must be strengthened in 
a more binding sense concerning the adoption of delegated acts relating to the requirements for the 
integration of certain metals in permanent magnets. 
 
Furthermore, the provisions on the introdu ction of a "product passport" are a step in the right 
direction. In order to allow manufacturers to anticipate the future obligations that will be introduced 3 
years after the entry into force of the regulation, A3M invites the European Commission to adopt  as 
soon as possible the implementing act specifying the format of this product passport.   
 
8 
 
 
Concerning the articulation with the Sustainable Products Regulation (ESPR), A3M underlines the fact 
that the provisions presented in the draft CRM Regulation are sources of regulatory uncertainty as they 
can be replaced by delegated acts stemming from the ESPR Regulation. Furthermore, a delegated act 
could be adopted from 2031 onwards, which delays investments. Thus, the delegated act adopted 
under the CRMA should be given priority over the delegated act under the Ecodesign Regulation and 
the European Comm ission should be invited to adopt all delegated acts relating to permanent 
magnets before 2030. 
 
 
8. Strong and transparent governance  
 
A3M is in favour of the creation of a Critical Raw Materials Committee composed of representatives 
of the Member States and the Commission in charge of coordinating actions at the EU level empowered 
with the following missions : 
- To make recommendations on the financing and development of projects related to all CRMs; 
- To advise the Commission and the Member States on where to exploit, use and recycle raw 
materials; 
- Monitoring and prioritisation of agreements with third countries on all RMCs; 
- Monitoring of raw material stocks; 
- Evaluation of Member States and companies actions. 
 
A3M considers that the scope of responsibilities of this Council seems well dimensioned. However, 
the link with the European Raw Materials Alliance (ERMA) must be clarified to avoid duplication 
(identification of projects, support role, etc.). 
 
Moreover, the role of the States in the arbitration process must be ensured by a reinforced 
representation within this body. A3M stresses the fact that the possibility of involving experts should 
be fully exploit ed. Finally, a balance must be found between the transparency of decisions and the 
confidentiality of discussions. 
 
 
In conclusion, A3M considers that the draft regulation on critical raw materials constitutes a solid 
basis for ensuring the transition towards EU independence in raw materials, but that its effectiveness 
will depend on the ability to integrate, during future discussions, within the text :  
- Strong CSR requirements to differentiate projects with European capital; 
- Dedicated funding for CRM -related projects, which appears to be the missing component of the 
draft text; 
- Consistent and complementary objectives between projects under the MRC regulation and other 
regulations (ESPR, waste shipment regulation, battery regulation). A3M draws attention to the 
possible duplication, in particular with other emergency instruments such as the EIMS (reporting, 
alert mechanisms, etc.) 
- Rapid adoption and implementation deadlines, particularly for CSR and permanent magnets.

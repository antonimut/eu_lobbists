Cross-border Enforcement – EPA response to EC consultation 
 
The European Parking Association (EPA, https://www.europeanparking.eu/) takes note of the publication 
by the European Commission on 1 st March 2023 of its Proposal for a Directive amending Directive (EU) 
2015/413, facilitating cross-border exchange of information on road-safety-related traffic offences. 
We welcome the expansion of the current Directive’s scope of traffic offences covered, notably with the 
inclusion of a reference to “dangerous parking”. However, we regret that the Commission decided to limit 
this long-awaited focus on cross-border parking infringements by adding in the definitions of Article 3: “(q) 
‘dangerous parking’ means parking the vehicle in a way that infringes the applicable rules on dangerous 
parking in the Member State of the offence. Failure to pay parking fees and other similar offences shall not 
be considered dangerous parking”. 
The parking and  urban mobility sector has long been calling for an effective mechanism at EU level to 
pursue the (increasing) failure to pay for parking, both on- and off-street. On-street, public authorities are 
grappling with foreign license plate holders that do not respect their parking management policies, 
especially in border regions. While off-street, many parking operators are moving to a free flow (barrier-
less) model and are as such experiencing more and more failures to pay by foreign license plate holders as 
well. 
While perhaps difficult to gauge at first sight, we feel there is definitely a link between failure to pay (all) 
parking infringements and the current road safety situation in cities – not in the least because parking 
offenders are more likely to generate road safety threats , hence the Commission’s inclusion of the 
reference to “dangerous parking”.  As cities strive to create the conditi ons for smooth and sustainable 
urban mobility, both public and private parking operators have been calling for an efficient and effective 
information exchange mechanism at EU level  to facilitate cross -border enforcement of parking 
infringements. We feel that the proposal for a revised Directive is the ideal instrument to achieve this 
important objective to ensure both safe and sustainable urban mobility , and it would certainly be a 
missed opportunity if it fails to do so! 
For the above reasons, we suggest deleting the last sentence of article 3q :  Failure to pay parking fees 
and other similar offences shall not be considered dangerous parking 
Mirroring this deletion, we invite EU decision makers to reflect on the best way to integrate failure to 
pay parking fees as an offence leading to the exchange of cross-border information. Indeed, Directive 
2019/520 on the interoperability of electronic road toll systems and facilitating cross-border exchange of 
information on the failure to pay road fees in th e Union does not cover the situation of parking in urban 
areas.  
The EPA will therefore engage in further discussions with the EU institutions in the hope to remove this 
restrictive definition related to parking offences. We remain available for any furthe r exchange of views 
and information that would substantiate our request.

NORPARK position :  
NORPARK, the Norwegian Parking Association, whose members are primarily composed of local 
governments (municipalities) that oversee and enforce public parking regulation, and private companies 
that oversee and enforce private parking regulation in accordance with national acts/regulations, echo the 
sentiment expressed by POLIS (Belgium). We are also convinced that this is a good initiative, but believe 
that that the scope of the revision would be better off if it were to be widened to include parking offenses. 
Cross-border enforcement against such offenses is cumbersome and not cost -efficient for neither the 
motorist nor the authority, and we believe both motorists and authorities would benefit if such procedures 
were simplified through EU legislative. 
 
FNMS position  (together with GART  (French transport authorities)  and AITF (French territorial 
engineers):

decriminalised failure to pay . The reform also granted  local authorities with new tools to serve their

mobility and parking policie s, which aim to reduce car traffic and consequently improve road safety. 
Decentralisation and decriminalisation reforms for on -street parking had already been introduced in 
several Member States at that time. 
This reform is reflected in particular by the fact that the absence or insufficiency of payment for parking is 
no longer comparable to an offence (decriminalization). We are now talking about a post -parking fee 
(Forfait de Post-Stationement or FPS), which allows the user to pay his fee only at the end of his parking, 
on a flat-rate basis, the amount of which is freely set by each city at stake (decentralization or devolution). 
Unlike the 1st class contravention, the  National Agency for the Automated Processing of Offences (Agence 
Nationale du Traitement Automatisé des Infractions or ANTAI), responsible for sending by post the notice 
of payment of the post-parking fee and the case of the enforceable title in the event of non-payment of the 
post-parking fee within 3 months, cannot, for lack of European or bilateral legal framework between two 
States,  France - Switzerland for example, send the FPS to the non -French holder of the registration 
certificate.  Consequently, and since 1 st January 2018, ANTA I does not process post -parking packages 
concerning foreign vehicles because it cannot recover them outside the French borders. 
This impossibility consequently leads to inequality between French and non-French holders of registration 
certificates with regard to the obligation to pay their on-street parking fee. 
It also penalises local authorities, and in particular municipalities on a border with another State or with a 
high tourist activity for which a significant proportion of motorists may not pay the parking fee, in a feeling 
of total impunity undermining the credibility of the parking policy.   
These communities are also deprived of the effects already observed by the reform on the quality of life in 
the city and sustainable mobility. The amount of FPS cancelled also represents a considerable shortfall for 
these communities. 
Finally, this impossibility deprives communities and police forces of the means necessary for the 
surveillance and control of vehicles parked on the street in terms of road safety or national security. 
To illustrate this situation that penalizes communities, it should be noted that: 
1. Between 1 and 2% of parking checks in non-border local authorities involve non-French vehicles 
2. Nearly 50% of parking checks in local authorities near a border involve non-French vehicles;  
3. in Annemasse, for example, nearly 14% of established FPS involve vehicles registered in Switzerland  
4. some local authorities, discouraged by the lack of cross -border information, stop issuing FPS to 
vehicles with foreign plates, which creates a vicious circle. 
Consequently, the associations Groupement des Autorités Responsables de Transport (GART), Association 
des Ingénieurs Te rritoriales de France (AITF) and Fédération Nationale des Métiers du Stationnement 
(FNMS) call for the establishment of a European legal regime, introduced in the draft revision of the  
Directive on Cross Border Exchange of Information, which would allow local authorities to recover the post-
parking fee in the various countries of the European Union.

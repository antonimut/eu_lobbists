Response of ESTA on the public consultation 
on strengthening the emissions targets of heavy-duty vehicles.
(Com(2023)88 final)
Transparencyregister number: 850612124598-41
10 May 2023
Summary
Cash in Transit (CIT) – armoured vehicles should be considered as “special purpose vehicles”
under Regulation 2018/858/EU and be exempted of CO 2 targets under the draft Regulation
amending Regulation (EU) 2019/1242 strengthening CO₂ emission performance standards for
new  heavy-duty  vehicles.  Emission  targets  should  not  interfere  with  security  regulations
impacting on health and safety of crews of CIT vehicles.
Response to the Commission public consultation
ESTA understands the imperative of reducing emission from heavy-good vehicles.  
The draft legislation aims at seriously reducing emissions from heavy-duty vehicles as part of
the follow-up of the Paris Agreement. It proposes seriously reduced thresholds from 2030
onward with 5-year incremental steps, aiming at zero-emission vehicles.
CO2 emissions of vehicles are a function of different factors, but they primarily depend on the
fuel  consumption  of  a  vehicle,  which  in  turn  depends  on  their  weight  and  their  speed.
Regardless of any given engine/frame technology, the CO 2 emission level will increase with
weight and speed. 
EU Regulation  (EU) No 1214/2011 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 16
November 2011 on the professional cross-border transport of euro cash by road between euro-
area Member States provides for different types of transport of cash cross-border, which are
specified in its Articles 16, 17 and 18. These provisions provide for the transport of cash in
“cabin-armoured” or “fully armoured vehicles” and defines cash-in-transit (CIT) vehicles as
“vehicles used for the professional transport of cash” under its Article 1, point (j).
ESTA wishes to raise the very peculiar issue of cash-in-transit vehicles, compatible with the
transport arrangements described under article 16, 17 and 18 of EU Regulation 1214/2011,
whether used for cross-border or domestic professional cash transport, in or outside the euro
area, and other security transport vehicles which are equipped with strongly armoured cabins
and cargo compartments designed to resist violent, possibly armed, attacks from robbers and

the armouring of the cabins and cargo compartments is bound to increase significantly the
weight of the vehicle and therefore the level of its emissions. Because of their purpose, it is
necessary that the armouring is not reduced to allow the equipped vehicles to meet emission
standards, and that a reasonable speed is also permitted, as security/CIT vehicles would be
endangered if their speed was reduced only for the purpose of meeting emissions levels. In
fine, it is not only a measure aiming at protecting the valuable cargo in the vehicles, but also
the health and safety of its crews.
The derogation requested for security transport/CIT vehicles would not, due to the specificity
of the vehicle concerned and their number, challenge or put in jeopardy the aims pursued by
the draft Regulation. According to ESTA’s latest (2022 data) annual attacks and loss report,
the number of such vehicles in circulation by ESTA’s Members in the EEA is 8,994 vehicles,
i.e. a tiny fraction of the 500,000 annually produced new heavy goods vehicles, as stated in
the explanatory memorandum of the draft amending Regulation.
ESTA therefore requests that CIT vehicles are considered in the Regulation as “ special
purpose” vehicles under article 3, point (31) of regulation 2018/858 EU on vehicles type-
approval  and  qualifies  for  exemption  of  CO2 targets  under  article  3a  of  the  draft
Regulation.
ESTA would point to the fact that the justification for this exemption is already considered in
the draft regulation under its recital 15, which justifies an exemption for “ coaches and lorries
for critical security and safety applications ” on one hand; and under its  recital 21  which
provides for exemption of “ special purpose vehicles ” or, in its last paragraph on “ vocational
vehicles".
ESTA also requests the Commission to take appropriate provisions in the Regulation to
ensure that emission targets remain compatible with overarching needs for security and
therefore  compatible  with  existing  security  regulations,  as  the  draft  legislation
acknowledges it in the case of public security services. This also serves the purpose of
health and safety protection of CIT crews operating the vehicles.

About UNIFE 
 
Operating in Brussels since 1992, UNIFE represents European train builders and rail suppliers. 
The association advocates for 119 of Europe’s leading rail supply companies – from SMEs to major 
industrial champions – active in designing, manufacturing, maintaining and refurbishing rail transport 
systems (trains, metros, trams, freight wagons), sub systems and related equipment. UNIFE also brings 
together national rail industry associations from 12 European countries. 
 
UNIFE’s position 
 
UNIFE welcomes the Green Deal Industrial Plan, and more specifically the European Commission ’s 
initiative on a Net Zero Industrial Act (NZIA). Bearing in mind the initiatives taken by the United States 
(i.e., Inflation Reduction Act – IRA), Japan (i.e., Green Transformation Programme) and China (i.e., latest 
Year Plan), UNIFE acknowledges the importance of the NZIA to accelerate the transition of the EU's 
industrial sector towards climate neutrality by 2050.  
 
UNIFE believes that the NZIA must be a comprehensive roadmap that supports and boosts key and 
strategic industries for the decarbonization of the economy. It represents an historic opportunity that 
must be seized to create jobs and generate economic growth, while ensuring a greener Europe for 
generations to come. 
 
As proposed, the NZIA is a positive start of a necessary  holistic and long -term strategy plan detailing 
specific financial and regulatory support to secure an EU-built industrial ecosystem and ensure a tailor-
made strategic autonomy for every key sector identified. However, UNIFE deeply regrets the absence of 
a value chain approach and therefore an incomplete scope of the NZIA, which should encompass rail 
products and trains.  
 
 
Extending the list of technologies and scope (Annex I) 
 
The Commission proposes a list of eight technologies or areas labeled ‘Strategic Net Zero’, based on three 
criteria. The draft Regulation therefore integrates critical technologies on which the EU is not sufficiently 
or no longer autonomous, or emerging technologies on which the EU wants to position itself . However, 
UNIFE expresses concerns at freezing a list of technologies; as in the medium term, the EU’s analysis of 
strategic technologies will evolve and Europe might become dependent on other critical technologies for 
the Net Zero objective in the future.

Although UNIFE welcomes the inclusion of batteries and fuel cells as Strategic Net Zero Technologies since 
they are key to the rail sector and supply industry , our association remains concerned about the lack of 
recognition of the entire net zero-emission downstream mobility value chain. 
 
As far as the transport sector is concerned, rail is the greenest mode of mass transportation, responsible 
for less than 0.4% of transport -related GHG emission s. Yet, the modal share of rail remains largely 
insufficient to tackle the decarbonization challenge of the transport sector, which accounts for 
approximately 25% of the EU’s greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions. Two important elements should also be 
emphasized as well: 
- the ambitious EU objectives of tripling high-speed rail traffic and doubling freight rail by 2050 call 
for boosting the rail sector in the forthcoming years; 
- the strategic nature of rail infrastructure, acknowledged in the  revision of the  Network and 
Information Security Directive (NIS2) and in the Foreign Investment Screening Mechanism and 
further accentuated by the Russian aggression against Ukraine. 
 
It is clear that the rail sector’s environmental and energy-efficiency performance is key to navigating the 
transition to a carbon -neutral economy and ensure the energy efficiency of industries and industrial 
supply chains.  Therefore, UNIFE calls for the inclusion of the rail supply industry  in all its dimensions 
(rolling stock, infrastructure, signalling, services) in the scope of the Net Zero Industry Act, as  ‘Net Zero’ 
or in a new category of critical sectors for which Europe is not in a situation of excessive dependence on 
third countries.  It should also be  ensured that projects concerning ‘Strategic Net Zero ’ technologies  
integrate the needs of the downstream European sectors (e.g. battery projects should notably take into 
account the needs of the rail sector).  
 
 
Boosting financing for rail 
 
The European Rail Supply Industry  needs significant EU funding to ensure a pan -European investment 
capacity through the different Member States. In this sense, the future European Sovereignty Fund must 
be established in close coordination with European industries to ensure its cost -effectiveness, added-
value and complementarity with existing EU funds  and programmes. Rail products should be a priority 
under the scope of the Sovereignty Fund given their strategic importance from a sovereignty and 
decarbonisation point of view, especially those with  a clear anchor on the twin digital and green 
transitions, including digital rail technologies such as the European Rail Traffic Management System 
(ERTMS) and the Future Railway Mobile Communication System (FRMCS), Digital Automatic Coupling 
(DAC) and thos e relating to alternative rail propulsion such as hydrogen and battery -powered trains to 
phase out diesel trains. 
 
Therefore, if the European Commission would like the ambitious revision of the TEN -T to succeed and 
accomplish the objectives of the European  Smart and Sustainable Mobility Strategy, f unding should 
support much needed rail infrastructure investment and modernisation , as well as on rolling stock 
retrofitting and acquisition, going beyond financial mobilisation through the existing funding 
opportunities (Connecting Europe Facility, InvestEU, Recovery and Resilience Facility, Horizon Europe and 
Cohesion Policy programmes).

This initiative also provides an opportunity to reflect on how to allow further flexibility for Member States 
to grant public aid – limited to carefully defined areas and on a temporary basis. The so-called Temporary 
Crisis and Transition Framework (TCTF), which was adopted on 9 March and which will be applicable until 
the end of 2025, is an positive step and UNIFE particularly supports further relaxation of public aid ‘for the 
acquisition of clean vehicles or zero-emission vehicles and for the adaptation [retrofitting ] vehicles’. This 
should inspire a parallel revision of the State-aid guidelines for railways which could also provide a more 
favourable framework (i.e. increase the threshold of aid intensity) for key rail technologies such as ERTMS. 
 
 
Strengthening EU public procurement 
 
Public procurement should be a role model for the end-consumer and prioritize goods which components 
originate in the European Union, especially in case such products are of strategic importance as it is the 
case for rail supply. 
 
The draft Regulation has specific sustainability and resilience criteria that public authorities must take into 
account in public procurement procedures, in addition to price or cost. Public authorities shall give the 
tender’s sustainability and resilience contribution a weight ranging between 15 % and 30% of the award 
criteria.  
 
UNIFE has been a strong proponent of going beyond mere (low) price award criteria and  has been 
fostering such a step change across rail procurement through various initiatives. Therefore, UNIFE fully 
supports this provision which is aligned with the Most-Economic Advantageous Tender (MEAT) principle 
understood as the Best Price Quality Ratio (BPQR). The Regulation should remain general as to the criteria 
to take into account since this field is covered by European Directives (and therefore national frameworks 
that are not very harmonized) and a diversity of sectors. 
 
However, the exceptions provided for by the proposal for a Regulation in Article 19(4) seem excessively 
broad and could make the provision ineffective. UNIFE w ould here suggest to draw inspiration from the 
scope defined in the International Procurement Instrument (Regulation 2022/1031), and in particular in 
its Articles 6 and 7: 
- Application to all public contracts above a certain threshold; 
- Exemption only for small public authorities (contracting authorities with a population of less than 50,000 
inhabitants). 
 
Going beyond the NZIA,  it is key that any upcoming revision of the public procurement Directives from 
2014 makes such targets binding across all utility sectors.

Proposed amendments to the regulation amending the electricity regulation and directive 
 
 
Executive summary 
 
ENTSOG amendments to the regulation amending the electricity regulation and directive are focused on specific proposals laid down by the EC in order to:  
• ease the approval process of Capacity Mechanism and make it more permanent, 
• make clear that electrolysers and gas-fired power plants, in other words, sector integration,  are part of the solution to fill the flexibility needs of the 
power sector, valuating the flexibility offered by the gas system, 
• involve all relevant network operators into the assessment and preparation of a report on the need for flexibility and 
• provide locational investment signals for dispatchable new generation and for electrolysers. 
In ENTSOG's view such clarifications will ensure that the system to be developed is designed taking into considerations all possible obstacles and opportunities 
(such as constraints of intermittent energy and positive aspects of sector integration) and based on most efficient solutions available (also existing infrastructure 
for both gas and electricity) and therefore is future-proof. As a clarification, it is also proposed to remove “non-fossil” since it is not defined and does not apply 
to demand side response, only to production side.

Rationale 
 
1/ Remove “non-fossil” and add “sector integration” 
The future will also have large periods where there is little or no power generation from solar and wind, and in those periods, we need alternatives if we are to 
maintain the EU as a significant economic hub. Demand Side Response can be a solution up to a certain point. Once this point is reached it would be detrimental 
 
  
 
 
Page 6 of 9 
 
to the economy and to the citizen lifestyle. Furthermore, the cost needs to be manageable. Hence the EU also needs to look at how enough dispatchable power 
generation capacity is ensured. To  make sure sufficient production capacity is always available in the future, this could be through gas -fired power plants or 
other dispatchable technologies to come. 
 
That is why incentives to ensure Capacity Mechanism for dispatchable generation are still available and ready to make up for intermittent renewable generation, 
especially at peak hours, should also be provided. The proposed amendments should ease the approval process of Capacity Mecha nism and send long-term 
investment signals that reflect the need for integrating capacity mechanisms as a more structural part of the energy market. 
 
In addition, increase in the intermittent energy sources generation makes flexibility tools such as Demand Side Response and Storage even more important. 
Electrolysers are a tool for Demand Side Response and/or Storage. The proposed addition “sector integration” is to make clear that electrolysers and gas-
fired power plants are covered and that the value of the flexibility offered by the gas system is recognised. 
Furthermore, we propose to remove “non-fossil” since it is not defined and does not apply to demand side response or storage, but only to the supply side. 
To integrate vast amounts of electricity generated from renewable sources, the electricity system needs flexibility not only from the demand side, but also from 
the supply side. Furthermore, it only makes sense to refer to the origin of the energy when desc ribing the supply side. Hence there is a need to distinguish 
between supply, storage and demand side flexibility and only use the term “non-fossil” to describe the supply side.  
2/ Involve gas and hydrogen TSOs and ENTSOG&H in the assessment of the flexibility needs 
The Regulation foresees NRAs to assess and produce a report on the need for flexibility every two years (demand response and storage) in the electricity system 
for a period of at least 5 years based on data provided by TSOs/DSOs. Given that hydrogen, gas and electricity network are likely going to be integrated and 
there is some benefit in cross-cooperation in relation to the identification of optimal location sites for sector coupling solutions (including electrolysers) it is 
appropriate that all TSO operators take a consultive role in the definition of the periodic NRAs report on electricity systems flexibility. 
 
The Regulation foresees that ENTSO-E defines a common methodology that electric TSOs should follow when determining flexibility needs. Given that flexibility 
needs should be assessed on a system level in order to deliver optimised outcomes it is necessary that ENTSOG&H is also involved – that way also the value 
of decarbonised gas solutions could be represented in the spirit of delivering efficient investment outcomes.

Therefore, ENTSOG&H should be involved when ENTSO-E and the EU DSO entity will assess the flexibility needs since ENTSO-E and ENTSOG(H) have developed 
joint scenarios on the use of gases by the power sector and will cooperate in this direction also in the future, ensuring a proper coordination of the respective 
members. 
 
3/ Recognise further the need of Capacity Mechanisms (CM) 
As more European countries rely on wind and solar power, ensuring adequacy will be increasingly challenging in periods of low wind and solar output. This calls 
for integrating Capacity Mechanisms as a more structural part of the energy market. To reflect the structural need for CMs it must be possible to employ 
them on a more long-term basis. Capacity Mechanisms should be more easily accessible in order to foster investments in dispatchable generation. Otherwise, 
these dispatchable generation means may be dismantled, creating a security of supply issue and an increase in power prices.  
 
Locational investment signals for these dispatchable new generation means are needed to reduce power grid reinforcement. Requiring capacity mechanisms 
to provide location dependent incentives will help reducing the need for power grid reinforcement by ensuring that the required investments take place in the 
right locations.

Rationale 
 
Electricity grid reinforcement may become one of the main bottlenecks in the energy transition, as it will be costly and time -consuming. To reduce the need 
for power grid reinforcement, a market reform that is future -proofed and able to deliver net zero must be implemented and rely on more granular locational 
investment signals for the location and dispatch of new generation and demand. As increasing shares of renewable electricity is integrat ed, it will ensure 
improved matching of market and physics without increasing the need for grid reinforcement.  
Benefits of more granular locational investment signals: 
1) Reduced overall energy system development costs, as larger shares of the European energy consumption will be transported i n hydrogen pipelines instead 
of power lines. Transporting energy via hydrogen infrastructure is several times cheaper than transporting it via power lines, as hydrogen pipelines can transport 
10-20 times more energy than electricity cables. This underlines the socio -economic benefits, i.e., lower investment cost, of hydrogen pip elines that will be 
able to evacuate huge amounts of renewable electricity in a more efficient way. Furthermore, pipelines enable connection to hydrogen storage facilities, which 
increases the value of renewable energy and electrolysers by providing the opportunity to store energy.  
2) Reduced need for electricity grid reinforcement due to improved matching of market and physics. Thereby the pace of grid developments may become less 
of a bottleneck in the energy transition, and the overall system costs will be reduced. 
3) Competitive hydrogen production costs, as hydrogen producers will benefit from lower input costs in the form of electricit y costs when located close to  
congestion. 
The proposed amendments give the legal basis for these locational investment signals and for any changes in tariffs that would reduce redispatching cost.

 
Repsol's comments on the proposed revision of Regulation (EU) 
2019/1242 for the strengthening of emission reduction targets for new 
heavy-duty vehicles  
 
We welcome the revision of the regulation on CO 2 emissions for new heavy -duty vehicles as a 
fundamental tool for the decarbonization of the transport sector. 
However, in relation to the proposal launched by the Commission on February 14, 2023, we miss 
the recognition of the contribution of CO 2-neutral f uels as a complementary way to 
electromobility, so that the decarbonization of road transport is carried out in an agile, resilient, 
safe and socially just way. 
To ensure a successful energy transition, it is essential to guarantee the competitiveness of 
industry as an essential pillar of the European economy. To this end, regulation must enable 
technology development and deployment of all carbon neutral technologies while ensuring 
that European industry can compete on a level playing field compared to other regions that do 
not have the same climate ambition. 
Therefore, we consider it is essential to recognize the contribution of  CO2-neutral fuels 
(CNFs) for the decarbonization of road transport in this regulation for the following reasons: 
 
1. THEY GUARANTEE THE REDUCTION OF CO2 EMISSIONS AND CONTRIBUTE TO 
CIRCULAR ECONOMY , in accordance  with the sustainability and footprint 
reduction criteria of the Renewable Energy Directive (RED) and in compliance with 
the waste management hierarchy. 
The Renewable Energy Directive (RED) establishes a clear and harmonized methodology at 
European level to estimate the reduction of CO2 emissions. According to this regulation, to be 
classified as renewable, fuels must reduce their emissions by at least 65% -70%1 compared to 
fossil fuels. Depending on the technologies and raw materials used, this reduction could reach 
90% and even negative CO2 emissions. 
Therefore, and in coherence with the RED and the rest of the Fit for 55 package, it is necessary 
that this regu lation takes into account CO2-neutral fuels as part of the solution for the 
decarbonization of heavy transport. 
On the other hand, the production of CO2-neutral fuels is largely based on the use of waste that 
is neither reusable nor recyclable and which otherwise would end in  incineration or landfill.  
Enabling technologies to produce these renewable fuels provides a better solution to non -
recoverable waste in accordance with the management hierarchy established by the EU, while at 
the same time reducing fossil fuel imports. 
 
2. CO2 EMISSIONS REDUCTIONS ARE BROUGHT FORWARD, since they are already 
available and compatible with the circulating vehicle fleet and refueling 
infrastructure.   
The use of CO2-neutral fuels entails the following advantages: 
- Reducing the investment needed in distribution and supply infrastructure.  
- Reducing the necessary investment in the transformation of the automotive industry.

- Allowing the transformation of the current fuel production industrial sites, guaranteeing 
their continuity and development and, consequently, employment.  
 
3. THEY ARE A LEVER TO BOOST  RENEWABLE HYDROGEN CONSUMPTION IN 
EUROPE, without having to wait for the development of hydrogen engines.  
According to the "Hydrogen Roadmap Europe"  the manufacturing fuels accounts for more than 
50% of total hydrogen consumption in Europe2. Currently, this hydrogen is produced by reforming 
of natural gas. Setting the right regulatory framework would lead to the complete replacement by 
renewable hydrogen. 
Moreover, CO2-neutral fuels will incorporate additional renewable hydrogen for their manufacture, 
thus favoring demand and thereby boosting the development of this energy vector. In other words, 
the development of the renewable hydrogen market could be brought forward without having to 
wait for the development of hydrogen engines to be consolidated. 
If the EU only recognize electrification as the only tool to decarbonize heavy-duty transport, it will 
jeopardize these investments and the opportunity to rapidly develop the renewable hydrogen 
market. 
 
4. THEY ARE NECESARY TO PRESERVE  THE COMPETITIVENESS OF EUROPEAN 
INDUSTRY: they are an optimal solution for carrying out  the decarbonization of 
heavy transport in the most cost-efficient way possible. This is especially relevant 
in Spain as the share of  road freight transport is significantly higher than in the 
rest of Member States. 
According to the most recent Eurostat data (2020), 77% of freight transport in the EU is carried 
out by road. In the case of Spain, according  to the same database, 1.5 million tons of goods are 
transported each year in the country, 96% of which are transported by road, while only 3% and 
1% are transported by ship or air respectively. 
Heavy-duty vehicles included in the scope of the proposal bel ong to very diverse segments 
(buses, trucks, vans, tractors...), each of which has different performances (payload, travel, 
fueling/refueling time and location...) and there is no single optimal technology for all of them. 
Their decarbonization will therefore require the development of a broad portfolio of technologies 
adapted to this diversity. 
 
Repsol envisions the future of this decarbonized sector thanks to the participation of multiple 
technologies, including CO 2-neutral fuels and electricity, which wi ll make possible the 
decarbonization of heavy transport in the most efficient way and at the lowest possible cost.  
 
 
5. THEY MAXIMISE OPTIONS TO DECARBONI ZE AVIATION AND MARITIME 
TRANSPORT: the exclusion of  CO2-neutral fuels from heavy -duty vehicles 
regulation would threaten the viability of essential investments for the 
development of sustainable fuels for aviation and maritime transport. 
 
The regulatory proposals of the Fit for 55 Package  related to aviation and maritime transport 
recognize the importance of renewable fuels for the decarbonization of these sectors and 
therefore include specific targets until 2050. It is important to note that in the production process

One of the main pillars of d ecarbonization is the principle of efficiency in the use of resources ; if 
there is no market for CO2 neutral fuels to be used in road transport, then we will waste valuable 
resources going against this principle. 
Currently, the fueling manufacturing processes result in an 85% of the fuels aimed at the road 
transport market, 10% at the air transport and the remaining 5% at the maritime transport. In the 
current and future business models of fuel production industrial sites, the exclusive production of 
fuels for aviation and maritime transport is hardly viable.  
If the EU does not recognize  heavy-duty vehicles running on CO 2 neutral fuels as an additional 
tool to decarbonize road transport, this will put at risk the viability of the investments and, at the 
end, it will jeopardize the transformation of the current fuel production industrial sites. 
Based on the foregoing, the approach of using CO2 -neutral fuels exclusively in aviation and 
maritime transport  would encourage the relocation of  fuel production  industrial sites to other 
regions, where these fuels could be marketed while fuels for aviation and maritime transport could 
be ex ported to Europe.  Thus, this approach adversely affects the development of European 
industry and its competitiveness. 
 
6. JUST TRANSITION : t hey guarantee industrial transformation plans , promoting 
employment and the development of depressed rural and industrial areas , 
ensuring investment and the capture of value for European and national GDP, in 
sum, avoiding the relocation of industry. 
The circular economy is one of the best opportunities for reindustrialization in the EU . Fostering 
initiatives for its deployment is not only a key lever for achieving a sustainable future, but also a 
great opportunity for the development of rural and industrially depressed areas. We will avoid 
imports of fossil raw materials such as oil and gas  by using non-recyclable waste as a feedstock 
to produce CO2 neutral fuels. 
As a conclusion, the recognition of the contribution of CO2-neutral fuels to the decarbonization of 
heavy-duty road transport is crucial to boost the investment in these technologies along the EU. 
This will allow to capture value for both national and European GDP, preserving current jobs while 
fostering new jobs creation and ultimately, avoiding the industrial relocation towards other regions 
outside the EU which are promoting  clear and predictable  investment support measures  in 
renewable fuels. 
 
7. THEY CONTRIBUTE TO STRENGTHEN ENERGY SECURITY OF SUPPLY IN 
EUROPE: the production of CO2 -neutral fuels from non -reusable and non -
recyclable waste makes it possible  to replace the import of crude oil  from other 
regions. 
In Spain, the energy dependency is particularly high (73% in 2017) due to the high share of fossil 
fuels in our energy mix that must be entirely imported. Therefore, in order to guarantee the energy 
supply in both Spain and Europe, it is imperative to increase the diversity of the sources of energy 
supply.  
RePowerEU Plan specifically recognize the contribution of CO2 neutral fuels to diversify the 
energy sources. These fuels , made of non -reusable and non -recyclable domestic waste, 
supersede oil imports and they can be stored the same way as conventional fuels, so they 
positively contribute to the security of energy supply.

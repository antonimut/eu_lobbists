Proposal for a directive on common rules promoting the repair of 
goods (Right to Repair) 2023/0083 (COD) 
Suomen Yrittäjät – the Federation of Finnish Enterprises (FFE) is an inter-
est and service organisation for small and medium-sized enterprises 
(SMEs) for all fields around entire Finland and their owners. As Finland’s 
largest organisation, representing 115.000 enterprises, we work every day 
to support free entrepreneurship and an entrepreneur-friendly society.  
We thank for the opportunity to express our opinions and views on the 
given proposal for a directive Right to Repair. 
1. General remarks 
We support the reasons and objectives of this proposal promoting repair of 
goods, green transition and sustainable consumption. Consumption causes 
negative environmental and climate effects. These effects should be re-
duced by supporting various operating models of circular economy and by 
offering repair services and used goods to consumers.  
This directive proposal does not however set any obligations or incentives 
to consumers for them to have their goods repaired instead of getting a 
new one. In most cases in Finland the repair of goods costs more than pur-
chase of a new one due to high cost of labour. Therefor we are somewhat 
sceptical about the true effects of this directive proposal in meeting the 
goals of green transition. 
2. Obligation to repair (art. 5) and impact on SMEs 
During the negotiations it should be assessed thoroughly that the obligation 
to repair does not cause unreasonable administrative burden to business. 
According to the impact assessment of the Commission the administrative 
burden to small enterprises is estimated to be greater in relation to their in-
come than for bigger enterprises. Overall, the proposal is estimated to ben-
efit SMEs, as SMEs engaged in the repair field benefit from the proposal as

companies operating in manufacturing industry and retail are put in some-

Ultimately the obligation to offer and arrange repair services is imposed on 
the seller. During the negotiations It should be clarified thoroughly that this 
obligation does not become unreasonable for individual seller of goods. 
We are worried that in real life the producers will transfer the obligation to 
repair to sellers through business-to-business contracts. Although the pro-
ducer is primarily responsible for the obligation to repair, in reality the obli-
gation may turn into an unreasonable burden to SMEs. It should also be 
noticed that the obligation to repair should not exceed the availability of 
spare parts. 
3. European Repair Information Form (art. 4) 
It is not appropriate to regulate, that companies should provide consumers 
the European Repair Information Form even on consumer request. This is 
due to a very little added value it provides consumers and the administra-
tive burden it causes to companies.   
Paragraph 1. of the article 4. 
The sales systems of SMEs do not most probably support provision of the 
form and the transition period of 24 months is not that long. In addition, the 
companies have already a very extensive obligation to inform consumers. 
Therefor we see that the administrative burden caused by the form is 
greater than its benefit to consumers.  
A challenge of the Repair Information Form is also that even today the con-
sumers are not able to describe the problem in the product correctly and 
this causes errors in repair offers. This means that the information on the 
form regarding the price and other terms of repair (spare parts, time…) may 
be incorrect and lead to differences of opinion with consumers. 
Paragraph 5. of the article 4. 
The information on the form should be valid for 30 calendar days unless the 
repairer and the consumer have agreed otherwise. This also includes the 
price and estimation of the time needed for the repair. SMEs do not nor-
mally have extensive spare parts warehouse and the need to order spare 
parts separately. The availability and price of spare parts may change rap-
idly. Also, the time needed for repair may change according to the availabil-
ity of spare parts, workload of repairer, information given by the consumer, 
etc.

The repairer and the consumer may agree otherwise about the validity of 
the form. We presume that it is possible to agree about a shorter validity. 
The word “agree” indicates though, that the repairer cannot reduce the va-
lidity of the offer unilaterally. How would this “agreement” work in real life? 
Which consumer would agree on a shorter validity? The recital or reasoning 
of the directive proposal do not offer any explanation or clarity on how this 
provision should be interpret or applied.  
4. Online platform for repair and goods subject to refurbishment (art. 7) 
It should be estimated if the obligation to ensure that at least one online 
platform exists is reasonable considering the resulting costs and on the 
other hand the benefits for the consumers and enterprises.  
The directive proposal requires that the repairers inform consumers exten-
sively, among other, about the time needed for repair and availability of 
spare parts. The logistics of spare parts needs to be reliable. As for compa-
nies the registration on the platform is voluntary and in most likely the com-
panies will have to pay a fee for their registration (as the use of the online 
platform shall be free of charge for consumers), we highly doubt if this plat-
form provides any benefits either for consumers or for companies. On the 
other hand, we also see that the sales promotion and marketing of repair 
companies does not belong to member stats. That is the job of the market 
economy.  
We are also concerned how the quality of repair companies operating on 
the platform is ensured and how their operation according to quality stand-
ards can be ensured. Is it possible for a company to tell they are an author-
ized repairer of a certain trademark? 
5. Amendment do Directive (EU) 2019/771 on certain aspects concerning contracts for 
the sales of goods (SGD) (art. 12) 
The directive proposal proposes a change to the SGD that the seller of 
goods shall repair the goods to bring those goods in conformity if the costs 
for replacement are equal to or greater than the costs for repair. We sup-
port this change and find it important as it supports the Union goals in 
green transition by setting the repair as the first alternative instead of re-
placement. The promotion of sustainable consumption during the whole life 
cycle of the product, the reduction of waste and carbon dioxide emission 
and the promotion of repair industry requires participation of consumers in 
to meet these goals. We also find important that this change would correct 
the legal position of enterprises as consumer would no longer be able to

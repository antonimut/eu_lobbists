EDISON’S CONTRIBUTION TO THE EX-POST PUBLIC CONSULTATION ON THE REVIEW OF EMISSION 
STANDARDS FOR HEAVY-DUTY VEHICLES 
Edison welcomes the opportunity to share its assessment on the European Commission’s proposal for 
a review of Regulation (EU) 201 9/1242 which sets stricter CO2 emissions standards for heavy -duty 
vehicles (hereinafter “HDV Regulation”).  
As part of the company’s strategy, Edison  is fully committed in promoting the decarbonization of 
transport via a multi faced approach rooted in the deployment of pragmatic solutions both from an 
infrastructure and alternative fuels perspective. Edison is in fact a player in the deployment of urban 
e-mobility solutions through the installation of LDV recharg ing stations, while contemporarily 
developing an innovative small scale LNG network for both maritime and heavy-duty vehicle 
transportation, ready to shift to sustainable bio-methane and in the medium run to synthetic methane. 
Indeed, in November 2021 Edison inaugurated its first small scale LNG depot , a project expected to 
save around 6 million tons of CO2,  contributing directly to the  decarbonization of heavy -duty 
transportation in Italy.    
The HDV Regulation plays a paramount role in directing investments and in guiding original equipment 
manufacturers’ (OEMs) in defining company strategies aligned with the European Green Deal’s 
objectives for the sector.  The Proposal identifies the role hydrogen fuel cell and electric powertrain 
technology will play in decarbonizing HDVs. The aforementioned systems will eventually take up a 
conspicuous part of the future market when it comes to  medium to longer term  new vehicle 
registration. Edison indeed will also invest in some of these solutions having for instance received CEF 
funding for its HYMOT project for the deployment of three hydrogen refueling stations for HDVs.  
Nonetheless, we notice that the HDV Regulation review proposed by the Commission distances itself 
from the pivotal principle of technology neutrality while it should better take into account the variety 
in applications and operating conditions of heavy-duty vehicles. 
Promoting Technology Neutrality 
Considering the longer time frame for the deployment of new technology for the sector  and possible 
challenges related to heavy loads over long distances, the revised Regulation must ensure that all HDV 
uses can be met with the most cost -efficient technology. The tailpipe emissions approach would not 
only disregard the role bio -methane and other renewable fuels can have in decarbonizing the sector 
but would also ignore the potential for decarbonization of the majority of new short-term HDV 
registrations. Edison envisions a role for progressively decarbonized molecules in the future H DV 
sector which would also contribute to eliminating SOx and NOx by up to 90%. 
Through the promotion of technology neutrality, the HDV Regulation would ensure the fastest 
decarbonization of HDVs by allowing all existing technologies to contribute to the EU’s net zero goal. 
In order to do so, the regulation should abandon a strict tailpipe only approach and move towards a 
more representative well-to-wheel approach which would promote the actual GHG footprint of fuels 
used by HDV. The possibility to explore such option was noticeably present in the 2019 regulation. 
Edison would like to underline how the full accounting of  the GHG footprint for fuels on a well-to-
wheel basis is in full alignment with the Renewable Energy Directive, the EU ETS and also the Fuel EU

Maritime regulation approach, while AFIR recognizes the role progressively decarbonized gases can

Considering that most new registrations in the short term  will be for gas -powered vehicles, a vision 
shared by  the EC in its  own modelling in the impact assessment, it is crucial that this regulation 
recognizes the benefits of biofuels and synthetic methane, bearing in mind that LN G assets enable a 
switch to the use of these fuels . Allowing for this  possibility would  send positive market signal s, 
aligning with the vision set in the Net Zero Industry Act which lists sustainable biogas/biomethane as 
“strategic net-zero technologies”.  
Proposals  
The HDV Regulation must account for the real GHG performance of existing powertrain technologies. 
This can be done w ithout having to drastically redesign  the EC’s proposal. The  co-legislators should 
evaluate the option of a carbon correction mechanism that would allow for the correct representation 
of the real contribution of renewable fuels to the sector.  Such option had been floated  as a Carbon 
Correction Factor (CCF)  by the EC  in the ex -ante public consultation, receiving considerable support 
from 110 stakeholders and 90 scientists.   
The inclusion of a Carbon Correction Factor (CCF) would  establish a methodology that would 
acknowledge the contribution  to decar bonization of the use of renewable fuels when assessing 
compliance with the CO2 Standards of new registered HDVs. At the basis of the CCF is the application 
of a factor to the formula used for calculating vehicle emissions that would offset a specific amount of 
CO2 emissions based on the presence of such fuels in the EU’s mix. Such methodology would allow for 
the respect of the technology neutrality principle while promoting decarbonization objectives sending 
a clear signal to biomethane producers, facilitating the uptake of renewable fuels as substitute to their 
fossil alternatives.  
However, in order for a CCF to correctly function, a clearly specified definition of “Carbon Neutral Fuel” 
must be laid out in the Regulation to ensure investment certainty in the market. Considering its 
functional role in appropriately assessing the GHG footprint of a fuel on a well-to-wheel approach, we 
would suggest using as a basis for such definition the one for fuels contained in the Renewable Energy 
Directive (2018/2001). Carbon Neutral Fuels are to be considered fuels whose emissions are to be net 
zero, meaning that the CO2 equivalent of the fuel used has not been emitted into the atmosphere or 
has been captured.

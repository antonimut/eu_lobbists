Edison’s response to the consultation on the revision of the REMIT Regulation  
 
As a leading player in the gas and electricity sectors , Edison fully recognizes the crucial role that integrity 
and transparency play in energy markets, supporting market participants  in operating in a safe and 
efficient market.  
 
The REMIT Regulation over the past decade has proven to be an effective tool for increasing transparency in 
EU markets. We believe that some aspects of the Regulation need to be revised to streamline and simplify 
the current processes, without revolutionizing the structure of REMIT. Although the ultimate objectives of 
the changes proposed by the Commission are shared , the feasibility of some of the proposals should be 
carefully assessed. 
 
Generally, many of the changes introduced by the Commission are taken from financial market regulation. 
Edison stresses the importance of keeping in mind that energy markets are physical markets with specific 
characteristics that make them fundamentally different from financial markets. Throughout the legislative 
process, therefore, it will remain important to recognize th ese differences in the passages where financial 
market concepts are introduced in the regulation. 
 
One aspect that we consider a priority in the context of this review is clarity when it comes to definitions. 
For example, the concept of inside information is a pillar of the REMIT and it is of utmost importance to 
reach a definition that is clear and prevent misinterpretation. In this case the proposal introduces the idea 
that any information that a market participant (MP) might use to base a trading decision must be published. 
Such a broad definition could also be interpreted to include information that is not part of the scope of the 
regulation (e.g., MPs' business plans and trading strategies). 
 
An additional definition that the proposal amends is that of "person professionally arranging (or executing) 
transactions" This definition, to which the term "executing" is proposed to be added, would then include all 
types of MPs and not just those professionally arranging transactions. This change, inspired by financial 
markets, would extend the scope of the definition, and consequently of a number of onerous obligations 
(e.g., oversight activities), to entities such as final customers who use energy to conduct their business and 
who are considered MPs by the Re gulation (large industrial customers) or small energy producers (wind, 
photovoltaic, biomethane producers) who enter into transactions in wholesale markets for the sole purpose 
of selling the energy produced by their facilities. 
 
In order to maximize trans parency and integrity within gas markets, Edison believes the extension of the 
inside information publication requirement to facilities and infrastructure operated by LNG, storage and 
transmission operators is necessary . In the current REMIT text, liquefac tion and storage services are not 
considered wholesale energy products, so LNG terminal operators are not defined as "market operators" and 
are therefore not subject to the disclosure of inside information obligation. Especially in light of the growing 
importance of LNG in the European supply mix, information held by LNG terminal operators should also be 
disclosed because it can have significant relevance for market functioning.  
 
Finally, Edison believes that  LNG benchmarking provisions should be left within the scope of Council 
Regulation (EU) 2022/2576, an emergency regulation with temporary application. The provision of 
information used to establish the benchmark is unrelated to efforts to counter manipulation and 
transparency, unlike other information that companies are legitimately required to disclose under REMIT.

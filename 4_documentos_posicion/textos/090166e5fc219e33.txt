Commission proposal to reform the Electricity Market Design  
Essenscia position  
 
Context  
The current energy crisis and its resulting soaring electricity prices have pushed the electricity market design high 
on the political agenda. Such context gives a strong mandate to address the deficiencies in the existing electricity 
market design. However, a hasty approach should be avoided as the biggest challenge of the market design is not 
the current energy  crisis, but rather making the design fit-for-future in view of an increasing penetration of 
intermittent low-carbon energy in the electricity mix towards 2030 and 2050.  
 
Access to competitive and low -carbon electricity is crucial for the  chemical and life sciences industry , a 
sector at the backbone of our European economy and providing the solutions to transition towards a climate-neutral 
society. Facing international competition, the elevated European energy prices have put stress on the sector’s  
competitiveness. It is essential to anchor and expand the existing chemical and life sciences industry in Europe by 
ensuring competitive prices , while  providing access to sufficient low-carbon electricity  allowing the sector’s 
transition towards climate-neutrality.  
 
Over the past decades, Europe’s electricity market integration and liberalization process have brought 
significant socio-economic benefits by enhancing the efficiency in the power sector. The functioning of the 
market should be enhanced and made fit for the future challenges rising from the changing energy mix.   
 
 
Proposed recommendations  
In its proposal to reform the Electricity Market Design, the European Commission aims to (1) boost renewable 
energy investments, (2) better protect and empower EU consumers, and (3) enhance the competitiveness of EU 
industry, amongst others, by promoting the use of long term -instruments such as  Power Purchase Agreements 
(PPAs) and  Forward Markets  (FMs). We welcome  the increased importance of long-term market-based 
instruments as important investment enablers.  
 
As the role of the EMD is to promote market functioning, regardless the origin of electricity and taking into account 
that both the ETS and RED directives are regulating emission reductions and spurring renewable investments, we 
question the electricity segregation based on its origin in several proposals as this will disturb efficient market 
functioning. The proposals in the EMD directive on public support measures are blurring a clear framework on 
State Aid and State Aid financing  as the se instruments are regulated in the State Aid  Guidelines and the 
signalling function of the market . We also question  the impact of shifting surveillance on parts of the market 
instruments away from the TSOs to Member States on the coherence of market functioning. 
 
We invite European policy makers  to assess proposals to truly transform to a European Electricity Market  that is 
fit-for-future. We recommend the following: 
• Provide additional guidance on the promotion of voluntary market -based instruments such as 
Power Purchase Agreements and Forward Markets. 
• Consider security of supply, competitive prices, and the integration of low -carbon energy on an 
equal footing 
• Target the main complexities of a future low -carbon power system  with increasing shares of 
intermittent production. 
• Improve the functioning of the current electricity market and avoid market distortions.

Provide additional guidance on the promotion of voluntary market -based instruments such as 
Power Purchase Agreements  and  Forward Markets.  
We welcome the European Commission’s initiative to promote voluntary PPAs and FMs. We believe 
guarantee schemes can help secure more PPAs and, in turn, enable more investments in low-carbon electricity. In 
addition, where not already available, member states should  promote the creation of marketplaces for (cross -
border) PPAs as this wo uld improve transparency and stimulate competition.  Furthermore, promotion of PPAs 
should always preserve technology-neutrality and the voluntary character of uptake. Regarding FMs, creating virtual 
trading hubs could be an interesting path to foster forward market liquidity. However, a deeper impact assessment 
is justified, including the link to the impact on the availability of physical cross -border interconnection capacity.  
 
 
Consider security of supply, competitive prices, and the integration of low -carbon energy on an 
equal footing.  
The current EMD reform proposal focusses on the promotion of a low-carbon and renewable electricity mix through 
the further promotion of flexibility mechanisms, long-term instruments (PPAs and FMs) and national public support 
instruments (CfDs). However, the impact of public support on the cost of the power system and on the resulting 
electricity prices remains unaddressed. As the electricity mix approaches higher renewable penetration levels, 
power system costs (grid costs, balancing, …) are expected to rise, mainly due to the increased need for (sub)-
hourly, daily, weekly and seasonal flexibility. Therefore, we invite the European Commission to thoroughly 
assess the impact of all proposed measures on power system costs.  
 
 
Target the main complexities of a future low -carbon power system  with increasing shares of 
intermittent  production .  
The technical complexities linked to increasing shares of intermittent production are not really addressed in the 
proposed market reform.  
 
A first complexity relates to the short-term market . In today’s day -ahead energy market , the marginal pricing 
approach ensures an efficient dispatching of power plants. However, this system also has its limits in case of 
diverging marginal costs of units, especially when some units have a guaranteed return through subsidies. This 
approach has also led to excessive inframarginal rents driven by the high gas prices over the past year . On top, 
CO2-prices artificially mark-up inframarginal rents. Therefore, we invite the European Commission to thoroughly 
assess all potential alternatives  for short -term price formation and functioning of short - and long -term 
markets in order to return the benefits of low-cost generation (often CAPEX subsidised  and financed by 
consumers) to the end consumer.  
 
Secondly, the current proposal aims pushing renewables through long-term market-based instruments such as 
PPAs and forward markets. We strongly support this initiative. However, we believe that the proposal could further 
address the profiling costs. In practice, the profile of renewable PPAs often does not align with the consumption 
profile of an off-taker. While closing a renewable PPA could  provide the off-taker protection against price volatility  
on the short-term market, expensive gap-filling contracts will remain necessary. Although to a lesser extent, a similar 
reasoning applies to forward markets.  
 
  
 
 
 
 
 
Improve the functioning  of the current electricity market and avoid market distortions  and 
technology specific measures . 
We strongly encourage  European policy makers to pursue market-based solutions , as the European 
Commission rightfully pushed for during past reforms.  
 
Public support instruments, such as CfDs, should be avoided  for mature low -carbon power generation  and be 
reserved for a limited amount of new innovation projects with low TRLs . CfDs are state aid instruments which 
are already regulated in the CEEAG in which more guidance on the financing, the conditions for use and 
the design of the mechanism  is included. Although CfDs aim to work two -sided to avoid excessive windfall 
profits, they shield the operator from market signals matching production with demand, eroding flexibility signals for 
production and innovation incentives to solve the profiling gap of intermittent generation. This will increase system 
costs.  
 
For the sake of coherence and efficiency, flexibility should be embedded in the existing market  with system 
operators in a facilitating role, instead of creating another stream of public support with a responsibility born by the 
member states. A similar reasoning applies for the demand side for which a market approach is preferred. 
Below-cost regulated prices should be avoided, especially if they selectively benefit certain consumer categories.  
 
Apart from a market -based approach, technology-neutrality should be a nother cornerstone of the EMD 
instead of the segregation of electricity base d on its origin as can be observed in several technology specific 
proposals. With regards to flexibility , the focus is on non-fossil flexibility only and in the proposed peak shaving 
product only demand response is targeted. This product can be activated during a peak hour which is defined in 
function of demand and electricity generated from renewable energy sources . This technology-specific 
definition will lead to non-disclosure of grid costs linked to renewable generation  and as such it does not 
accurately reflect system costs nor the need for cost efficient solutions. We urge the European Commission 
to avoid technology specific choices that may undermine the balance between the competitiveness of power prices, 
security of supply, and the transition towards a low-carbon power sector.

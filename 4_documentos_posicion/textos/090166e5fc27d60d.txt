CEMA position on 
the NRMM road circulation Regulation 
 
 
 
Introduction 
CEMA represents the agricultural machinery industry which includes many non-road 
mobile machinery, also called self-propelled agricultural machinery that use occasionally 
the road. It concerns machinery, covered by the Machinery Regulation for occupational 
safety, to perform a particular agricultural function like spreading, spraying, mixing, 
harvesting, etc. These machines are characterised by their low volumes and many types 
to accommodate for the different applications, and particular needs of farmers and 
contractors. The functionalities and the economic realities of agricultural production dictate 
their design which often results in dimensions and masses that are considered 
exceptional.  
 
 
A balanced legislation 
CEMA welcomes the initiative of the harmonisation of technical requirements in Europe 
for road circulation of non-road mobile machinery. It has been a difficult exercise which is 
reflected in the fact that the file has been brought on and off to the European level for 
discussion for the past 20 years. Of course, it relates to machinery for many different 
sectors and application of which agriculture is only one. 
 
From the proposal it is clear that the Commission has shown great insight resulting from 
the many discussions with Member States and industry. The proposal provides the 
necessary flexibility for economic operators while at the same time keeps the focus on 
simplified conformity assessment procedures relevant for the sectors involved. CEMA 
welcomes especially the inclusion of procedures for exceptional vehicles, due to 
dimensions and masses, which affects the agricultural machinery industry 
disproportionately. By this inclusion it is ensured that the decision to allow an extra heavy

The wide range of different testing options from testing by the manufacturer to full testing

burden for manufacturers. 
Furthermore, the clear split between this Regulation and the Machinery Regulation on 
many points is much appreciated. 
The transition period and the timeline, for the end of validity of existing EU type approvals 
when new requirements are introduced, will avoid discussions on such timelines with each 
technical revision. 
 
Proposal for further improvement  
 
There are some remaining points where CEMA would like to advise of changes that will 
improve the text even more and make it more suitable for the defined scope. 
 
• Definitions – many points added to the definition of type and variant do not have 
any added value and should be deleted, so to avoid valueless administrative 
burden. 
• Article 4 related to exceptional vehicles: though we cannot stress enough how 
important the inclusion of this procedure is, more flexibility would be welcome to 
what is “exceptional” in relation to machinery masses. For example, reference to 
axle loads and ground contact pressure should be added as they are the most 
relevant parameters to assess potential road damage.  
• Article 15 list of technical requirements:  
• It shall be an option to create classes or categories in the delegated acts 
but it should not be mandatory. Within the text  it is referenced as optional 
(article 4) but here it seems rather depicted as mandatory. Additionally it 
must be avoided that such classes or categories, if any, are linked to the 
fitting of technical systems or technical requirements, like power source or 
speed.  
• We call for further deletion of topics that are sufficiently covered by the 
Machinery Regulation. Examples are the manual that is now already 
containing all relevant information, vehicle structural integrity, heating 
system, and sharp edges on the exterior of the vehicle. 
• Article 22 related to the COP:  It would be a relief of burden, in particular for 
SMEs, if the Certificate Of Production only needs to be requested once and is 
further used as verification by other MS. If Certificates of Conformity can be 
accepted among the various Member States, why not also the COP?

• Article 26 on the end of validity for EU type approvals: this new text is much 
welcomed as it gives once and for all clear preconditions and time period for 
termination of validity. However, for the precondition in point b), being the 
introduction of new requirements, if should only relate to the placing on the market 
which is the first action to happen (for some sectors the registration can happen 
much latter and that should not invalidate the approval). Also in relation to this 
point b) the period for termination of validity, linked to this placement on the 
market, should be extended to 24 months, to take into account the seasonal 
nature of agricultural machinery sales. 
• Article 52 Transitional Provisions: It is of utmost importance that such transition 
period, to move from the many national approval to an harmonized EU approval, is 
decided upfront, given the many machinery types that will be required to undergo 
such new mandatory EU type approval.  However, the transition period will only be 
effective and not create additional burden if no significant changes to the national 
requirements happen in this period. In the past, after the entry into force of 
Regulation 167/2013, we witnessed the take-over of EU requirements into national 
law for towed agricultural machinery in various countries without much 
consideration of the effects on the R&D and homologation efforts from 
manufacturers, and which as a result created a large burden for industry. Such 
repetition should be avoided. It might be something difficult to be officially 
transposed in legal EU text, but an agreement between Commission and Member 
States would be needed.  
• Article 53 on entry into force and application: We welcome very much the 36 
months prior to the implementation, which also obliges EU Commission, technical 
services and authorities to be ready by that date. However, there should be the 
option to use the Regulation also earlier after entry into force, introducing an early 
application clause. As soon as delegated and implementing acts will be available 
and some technical services and authorities will be ready, manufacturers willing to 
do so could start to seek for EU type approval and benefit from the EU single 
market.

CEMA represents the European agricultural machinery industry which comprises 
about 7,000 manufacturers, most of which are SMEs, producing more than 450 different 
types of machines with an annual turnover of about €40 billion (EU28 - 2016) and 150,000 
direct employees. CEMA companies produce a large range of machines that cover any 
activity in the field from seeding to harvesting, as well as equipment for livestock 
management.

BEUC preliminary assessment 
 
BEUC welcomes the proposal for a Directive on common rules promoting the repair of goods  
(COM(2023)155). However, in our view the propos ed measures will be insufficient to ensure 
that a proper right to repair is introduced for the European consumers. Several improvements 
are still needed. 
Repair within the legal guarantee scheme 
The Sales of Goods Directive 1 is an instrument with a great potential to create incentives to 
make products more durable and repairable. Unfortunately, the current proposal is a missed 
opportunity in this respect  as many promising policy options, considered during the public 
consultation process, were disregarded.  
 
The proposal introduces only one change to this  Directive by limiting consumers choice to a 
remedy, in case of  good’s non-conformity with the contract,  by prioritising repair o ver 
replacement (if it is cheaper or equally expensive). BEUC is against this fundamental change 
which we consider to be disproportionate. It would  reduce existing consumer rights  and 
contradict the principle of contractual justice . It also creates significant inconvenience for 
consumers without any certainty that it will help in achieving the goals set forward in the 
proposal. In practice, it will lead to a strong imbalance between the consumer and the trader, 
with the latter being the only party able to assess the costs of repair  and no proper way for 
consumers to contest it. The wording of the provision itself might also trigger issues with the 
interpretation of the art. 13 of the Sales of Goods Directive. 
 
On the other hand, BEUC has advocated for decades to expand the legal guarantee periods 
for durable products  (e.g. white goods or electronics) 2 and we regret to see that this 
recommendation was not included in the Commission proposal. The current 2 years of legal 
guarantee period are not appropriate for more durable goods and should be therefore expanded 
in line with the Ecodesign requirements of durability.  Concretely, product-specific guarantee 
periods should be established via the Ecodesign implementing measures on the basis of their 
lifespans. This measure, combined with introducing a possibility for consumers to bring their 
claims directly to the producer (joint producer-seller liability) and aligning the reversal of 
the burden of proof with the length of the legal guarantee period – have the best potential 
to create a proper incentive for producers to make their goods more durable and to improve 
consumers’ access to repairs.  
 
Repair beyond the legal guarantee scheme 
The proposal puts a strong focus on introducing new measures  that address defects that do 
not fall under the scope of the legal guarantee scheme. BEUC has doubts about this approach.

In our view the existing legal guarantee scheme would be much better suited to achieve the 
sustainability goals of promoting the repair and reuse of products. We also question the fact 
that after the initial 2 year guarantee period is over , the defects linked to the product’s poor 
design (and therefore liming its durability) will be treated in a same way as defects caused by 
consumers themselves. Concretely, o n the basis of the current proposal, in both cases 
consumers will be asked to pay in order to repair their goods, with no rules in place on how 
this price should be calculated. 
 
The proposal brings however also certain fundamental improvements. First of all, it 
acknowledges that the responsibility for products’ defects falls most of the time on the producer 
and that the producer is also best placed to repair the good. The direct producer liability should 
be however triggered not only for defects beyond the legal guarantee but also within. Second 
of all, we recognise that, for the first time , the proposal creates a link between the EU sales 
law and the product policy legislation which should be even further expanded.  
 
For what concerns concrete measures included in the proposal, we fear that the new 
obligation for producers to repair goods might have a limited practical impact. First of all, 
because it will only apply, for the time being , to a handful of product s. Moreover, since the 
producers will be free to set a price for these repairs as they see fit , it will be quite e asy for 
producers to escape this obligation by simply setting the price unreasonably high which will 
lead consumers to turn elsewhere.  
 
Finally, in our initial assessment, we consider that the Commission proposal to improve 
consumers access to the repair information via dedicated national online platforms and the 
European Repair Information Form might bring an added value for consumers. 
 
Measures to make repairs more affordable 
High costs are the most important barrier to repair reported by consumers 3. Unfortunately, 
none of the pending legislative initiatives introducing the right to repair tackles this problem 
sufficiently. In the explanatory memorandum to the proposal on common rules promoting the 
repair of goods, the Commission stated that its different measures will lead to a more 
competitive repair market, which will then result in more affordable repair prices. BEUC has 
doubts of whether this will be the case. We would suggest that the Council considers introducing 
more targeted measures that would make repairs more affordable. Many interesting initiatives 
introducing different forms of financial incentives for repair4 are currently being developed 
on the national level and could serve as an inspiration. 
 
 
BEUC is currently preparing a more detailed assessment of this proposal which should be 
published in the next weeks.

Achievingauniversal “consumer right torepair”
The “right to repair” will only be achieved if devices are designed andmanufacturedwithrepairin mind. While there has been some progress over recent years, we are still a long way fromwhere we need to be. Designs have become more intricate and complex, and manufacturersare making it more difficult for independent service providers to operate. European legislationshould entrench a universal consumer right to repair regardless of warranty limits, wherebyevery consumer should have the choice to seek repair of any of their devices by theprofessional of their choice, at afair andaffordableprice.
Amending consumer legislation cannot be a safety net on its own. Obligationtorepair over replacement
● Within the warranty limits - Eurefas welcomes the suggestion to amend thehierarchyof remedies by placing repair over replacement. This was along-requestedamendmentthat would have a consequential impact on consumers’ behaviours. We also welcomethe exemption carved out when the repair is more costly or impossible. This will beparticularly relevant for refurbished devices where oftentimes repair can be impossible(eg. the device is too old or beyond economical repair) or simply too costly. Asindependent service providers, we very much depend on the availability of parts andtheir price. Repairs can become exponentially costly for independent service providers,especially when OEMs imposepart bundlingwhichforcesthemtobuyandchangemorethan the part that needs to be repaired making theprocessmorecomplicatedandmorecostlythanit should.
● Outside of warranty limits - For the obligation to have consequential benefits onconsumer behaviours, we strongly believe that the provision should refer to a“reasonable and proportionate price”. We need to ensure that consumers are notdiscouragedbyunaffordablerepairs.
The obligation to repair should also apply indistinctly to all products (new orsecond-hand) regardless of where they have been bought or previously repaired. It isunfortunate to see an increase in cases where OEMs refuse to repair their owndevicessolely because they have been refurbished or repaired by an independent serviceprovider, outsidetheir control.
● Warranty periods - Eurefas does not believe amending the warranty limits is eitherdesirable or necessary at this point. An extension of the warranty period would on thecontrary be harmful to the environment and to the European refurbishment and repairindustry as long as the lawdoes not ensure a level playingfieldwithmanufacturers: wewould not be able to service devices longer and at a reasonable price if we don’t haveaccess to parts or if repair limiting practices continue to be used by OEMs. As a result,for most products, replacement will always be cheaper than repair, and the mostcommon, andenvironmentallydamaging, optionduringwarranty.
Onlineplatform

We warmly welcome and support the idea to set up and promote national platforms toconnectconsumers to professionals (repairers or sellers/distributors of refurbished goods). We areconfident this would help bring greater visibility to professionals and incentivise consumers toseek quality from registered professionals. The only way to educate consumers about theirrepair options and effectively promote the platformwould be tomandateanobligationtoinformconsumers at the point of sale (or in advanced marketing materials) by distributors of newdevices.
Our preference would be to make the platformEuropean-wide to allowany consumer to seekrepair regardless of the place of sale or country of residence. However, should the legislatorsdecide to leave it up to each Member State to set up their own platform, we recommendagreeing on a set of limited common criteria to ensure equivalent qualityof theregistrantsontothe platform. Those objective criteria should a minima include business identification, proof oftechnical competenceinthecountrywhereit operates, VATnumber, proof of insurancecoveringliabilitiesfromtheir activities, etc.
We also are pleased the Commissionplanstoenablethedevelopment of avoluntaryEuropeanquality standard for repair services that could boost consumer trust across the Union. Suchstandards should be as open as possible, and limitedtocriteriathat arerelevant toconsumers,such as the ones cited in recital 27. In order to foster competition and avoid manufacturers’market lockdown, it should be based on non-discriminatory criteria that can also be met bySMEs. Eurefas stands ready to bring its expertise to building this standard and stronglyrecommendstoconsult our sector toensureacomprehensiveoutcome.

 
 
 
ITI Response to the European Commission’s Open 
Feedback on Common Rules Promoting the Repair of 
Goods  
 
The Information Technology Industry Council (ITI) welcomes the opportunity to respond to th e 
European Commission’s feedback on Common Rules Promoting the Repair of Goods Directive. ITI 
represents 80 of the world’s leading information and communications technology (ICT) companies 
from all corners of the technology sector. We are the premier global  advocate for technology, 
representing the world’s most innovative companies. We promote public policies and industry 
standards that advance competition and innovation worldwide. Our diverse membership and expert 
staff provide policymakers with the broadest perspective and thought leadership from technology, 
hardware, software, services, and related industries. We firmly believe that the interests of our 
companies and industry are fundamentally aligned with those of the economies and societies in 
which we operate. 
ITI supports the Commission’s objectives to promote the sustainable consumption of goods and 
reduce waste. We appreciate the consideration and incorporation of our comments submitted to the 
Commission’s Open Consultation on the Sustainable Consumption of Goods – Promoting Repair and 
Reuse in April 2022. Ensuring the durability, safety, and security of the products consumers use every 
day is a top priority for our members, which is why our industry builds products with the wellbei ng 
of consumers at the forefront of design and manufactur ing. Products are designed in compliance 
with current international regulations and are built to last and keep consumers safe. Authorized and 
qualified repair services ensure that a product needing repair can be fixed in a manner that does not 
compromise built-in safety protections. It’s critical that regulatory measures regarding repairs don’t 
jeopardize consumer  safety. Products consist of parts and pieces that , if taken apart  and re -
assembled incorrectly or if exchanged by an inferior or counterfeit part , may pose a serious safety 
risk to not only the repair er but also to the end user. The proposed Common Rules Promoting the 
Repair of Goods from the Commission is both ambitious and pragmatic and can help to deliver our 
shared aim of promoting the sustainable consumption of goods and increase the repair  of eligible 
devices.

Please see our feedback below on the Common Rules Promoting the Repair of Goods Directive: 
 
 
Article 1 – Subject Matter, Purpose and Scope 
• The proposal for repair  would operate outside of the legal guarantee period and is aligned 
with established EU laws and regulations by the explicit references (in Annex  II) to the 
existing Ecodesign Regulations.  We welcome this approach, which allows specific tailored 
requirements based on discrete product categories.  This approach will allow for repair 
mandates to align with both the product technologies and the associated user environments 
which can be radically different. ITI welcomes this approach as it will avoid the introduction 
of new horizontal requirements that may be  duplicative, contradictory, or unreasonable 
based on product-specific factors.  In order to provide legal certainty to manufacturers, we 
support alignment with the appropriate Ecodesign regulations ( e.g., alignment with the 
timeframe required to provide spare parts).  We would urge caution in applying a common 
approach, which may result in a negative  impact on the criteria laid down in Directive 
2009/125/EC. 
• Our understanding is that the proposed Directive will only address products which are sold 
to consumers. We emphasize the need to clarify the scope of ‘goods’ to exclude Business to 
Business (B2B) products. We support this application because B2B transactions are often 
conducted under different types of commercial repair arrangements. There will always be 
instances where a small minority of consumer products are purchased by a 
business/enterprise, e.g. mobile phones for business use. Currently, as is, the directive could 
be interpreted to include such products (e.g. servers and data storage products according to 
Commission Regulation (EU) 2019/424). Small Scale servers are already included within Lot3, 
which distinguishes them from their more complex variants. We ask for  clarification that 
products typically sold to businesses are not in scope of the Directive and its Article 5 
(Obligation to repair). 
 
Article 3 - Level of Harmonisation: 
• ITI supports a full harmonization approach across the EU . A lack of harmonization among 
member states leads to difficulty complying with regulations.  We further emphasize th at 
national level transpositions need to align to avoid any future fragmentation. 
Article 4 - European Repair Information Form 
• The proposal requires a ‘European Repair Information Form’ to be provided to consumers on 
a durable medium. In many instances this information will already be provided to consumers 
online or via QR codes, so it is not necessary to be in the mandated format. In order to reduce 
the administrative burden on manufacturers and repairers,  as well as benefit the 
environment by saving paper, we suggest the proposal require certain types of information 
be provided to the customer via the Europ ean Repair Information Form ‘or in another 
suitable manner’ i.e., online or QR code and on a durable medium.

Article 6 – Information on Obligation to Repair  
• As emphasized under Article 3 above, we strongly encourage the Commission to harmonize 
requirements among all member states  for information on obligation to repair.  To more 
easily facilitate compliance  for producers  as well as  create a clear , unified  system for 
consumers, there should be a specified method for producers to inform consumers.  ITI 
recommends the specified method to be online.  
Article 12 - Amendment to Directive (EU) 2019/771 
• We support the proposal to require repair as the primary remedy within the legal guarantee 
although encourage more flexibility to offer the best solution both in terms of sustainability 
and consumer convenience. We suggest the repair mandate could be further elaborated to 
address the possibility - at the sellers’ or manufacturers’ discretion - of offering refurbished 
products as part of the service strategy. In addition, the repair mandate should only apply to 
new devices, not to refurbished devices. The Sale of Goods Directive (EU 2019/771) already 
recognizes that secondhand goods should not be expected to have  the same potential 
lifespan as a new device and therefore limits to the ability to repair refurbished goods should 
be recognized. 
Legal Guarantee: 
• The proposal is in line with ITI’s position, submitted to the Commission’s Open Consultation 
on the Sustainable Consumption of Goods – Promoting Repair and Reuse in April 2022, which 
cautions against restarting the legal guarantee after repair, as envisaged by the Commission 
Inception Impact Assessment, as it can create uncertainty and could effectively result in legal 
guarantee extension in perpetuity. Similarly, the proposal and impact assessment are in 
alignment with ITI’s position that a longer legal guarantee would have marginal benefits. The 
majority of consumer claims happen in the first two months after the purchase and that, as 
mentioned in the European Commission Impact Assessment, 96% of defects happen in the 
first two years of purchase.  Extending the statutory warra nty period would not affect the 
expected lifetime of a product  and would not change the number of defects identified but 
could instead drive-up product prices. As such, the approach taken by the Commission in 
relation to warranties is welcomed.  We do sugge st that manufacturers and sellers should 
have the possibility to replace defective products with refurbished ones  within the legal 
guarantee period. 
Online Repair Information Platform  
• Manufacturers support the safe and quality repair of their products. We recognize that brand 
image could be impacted by the durability and effectiveness of the repair. We are concerned 
that without an effective means to qualify the repair and refurbishment organizations listed 
on the platform, the platform will not serve the function of guiding safe, reliable , and 
effective repairs. We ask that  the European Commission expand on its intended approach 
with regards to how the platform will ensure the quality of repairs or refurbishment.

• To ensure safe, secure, and reliable repair for consumers, these online platforms should limit 
the registration to repairs performed by Original Equipment Manufacturers (OEMs), 
Authorized Service Providers (ASPs), and qualified independent repair providers. This is 
essential to provide customers with repair services that ensure the high quality of repairs, 
safeguard customers’ privacy and security, and protect customers from injury. This would 
also support OEMs legitimate efforts to protect their intellectual property and/or the security 
of their ecosystems.

 
 
 
The Federation of Norwegian Industries' comments to the draft 
Regulation on Critical Raw Materials 
 
About the Federation of Norwegian Industries 
The Federation of Norwegian Industries represents industry branches such as oil and gas 
contractors, onshore petroleum activities, aluminium, biotechnology, cement, chemical 
industries, electro and energy equipment, furniture, glass and ceramics, machine and hardware 
industry, maritime industry, aquaculture and aquaculture suppliers, graphic arts and 
communication, metals, mining, paints and coatings, paper and pulp, pharmaceuticals, plastics, 
recycling, facility services and textiles. We represent 3,100 member companies with approx. 
130,000 employees. 
 
We welcome the new proposal for a Critical Raw Materials Act. Reaching the targets of the 
European Green Deal and the accelerated ambitions of REPowerEU will require a significant 
increase in the use of minerals and metals. Going forward, improving Europe’s access to critical 
minerals and metals will be decisive for securing strategic autonomy and delivering the green 
transition. Norway is well positioned to contribute, with experienced extractive and processing 
industries as well as reserves of metals and minerals which are indispensable to the green 
transition. Norway’s mineral policy must be developed in close cooperation with the EU and 
contribute to securing competitiveness and increased self-sufficiency in Europe. Likewise, the EU 
can consider Norway among its closest partners in developing its critical raw material policy. 
 
List of strategic raw materials 
Europe’s Critical Raw Materials Act is an important first step forward for Europe’s raw 
materials and industrial policy. It recognizes that the EU must act urgently to avoid unhealthy 
dependencies or supply bottlenecks for the metals and minerals required in its energy and 
digital transitions. The list of strategic raw materials listed in Annex I, Section 1 is an 
important measure to secure access to raw materials of strategic importance to energy and 
digital transitions and an identified potential supply concern in the next decade. Shortlisted 
raw materials are eligible for accelerated permitting measures and extra financial support.  
 
We recommend the following improvements to this list.  
• Aluminium should be added to the list of strategic raw materials. Aluminium, throughout the 
whole value chain from unwrought aluminum to finished products, is an enabling material in 
the green transition as a component of nearly all technological solutions listed in the Net Zero 
Industry Act. The Commission’s impact assessment and strategic foresight report identify 
aluminum as fulfilling the definition of a strategic raw material (with its ore bauxite already on 
the critical raw materials list), due to strong rising transition demand, an eroding EU 
production base, and the growing role of China and Russia in European supply.  
• "Natural Graphite" should be replaced with "Graphite" on the list of both Strategic and Critical 
Raw Materials. Such a change would incorporate Synthetic Graphite in the scope on the new 
Critical Raw Materials Act. Graphite is used as the anode material in most lithium-ion 
batteries and makes up 50 % of the battery weight. Natural and Synthetic Graphite can be 
used interchangeably in batteries. If produced using renewable energy, the carbon footprint 
of Synthetic Graphite is reduced by more than 90 % compared to current standard material 
from China. Today, more than 90 % of all battery-grade anode material is produced in Chane

• "Lithium battery grade" should be replaced with "Lithium" (all grades) in the Strategy raw 
materials list contain. Some processes for cathode material can cope with different technical 
grades. Furthermore, different technical grades for lithium can be converted into battery 
grades by refining. So, focusing on all Lithium grades allows Europe to be more flexible. 
• The main metallurgical process for accessing listed by-products should also be strategic. 
Several strategic raw materials are only mined or recycled as byproducts. This means their 
production is only viable from bigger operations focused on the primary commodity, such as 
zinc for germanium. The Critical Raw Materials Act’s individual material focus does not reflect 
this necessary integration. The primary commodity operations necessary for accessing 
strategic byproduct raw materials should also automatically qualify as strategic. 
• Finally, Silane should be added to the list of Strategic Raw Materials. Silane is a silicon-
containing gas that is made from silicon. Silane may replace and/or complement graphite as 
the basis for future anode materials, e.g. to be used in electrical vehicles. However, there is 
no European capacity for producing or refining Silane and, at the same time, Silane is an 
expensive gas to transport to the EU markets. Currently, investments in anode production 
based on Silane are drawn to USA or China. Adding Silane to the list of Strategic Raw Materials 
will provide a strong signal and incentives to secure access future anode materials in the EU. 
 
Accelerating permitting 
The draft Critical Raw Materials Act makes important proposals to speed up the permitting 
process for strategic mining, processing, and recycling projects. Regulatory uncertainty related to 
permitting is experienced as a significant barrier for new mineral projects in Europe. This 
uncertainty concerns both the timelines and the results of permit applications, and it represents 
significant risks and costs for the mineral industry. We support the Commission’s proposals for a 
one-stop shop, permitting deadlines, and other streamlining measures. We underline that these 
proposals are important to reduce long timelines, while assuring that from an industry 
perspective they should not come at the expense of environmental checks and community 
involvement.  We recognize that Member States will need to test the practicality of these 
measures against their own legislation and procedures. In addition, shipments of intermediates, 
by-products, waste, etc. containing critical raw materials within Europe should be simplified. 
 
Recycling provisions 
We welcome the value chain approach of the CRM Act and the fact that in addition to mining and 
processing, recycling is also covered. In order to strengthen provisions on recycling, we propose to 
introduce separate reporting on pre-consumer and post-consumer scrap and waste containing 
critical raw materials (for example under Art. 1). With such transparent reporting, policymakers 
and the industry will get a clear picture of the extent of which recycling is supporting a genuine 
circular economy by recycling products at the end of life (post-consumer recycling).  
 
Link the Critical Raw Materials Act to EU level financing

projects, with Member States instead given new state aid flexibilities under the Temporary Crisis

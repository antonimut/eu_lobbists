EBIC comments on the Proposal for a Regulation of the European 
Parliament and of the Council amending Regulation (EU) 
2019/1009 as regards the digital labelling of EU fertilising products 
[2023/0049(COD)] 
 
 
The European Biostimulants Industry Council (EBIC) welcomes the  opportunity to provide 
comments to the European Commission’s proposal on the optional use of digital labelling for 
EU fertilising products (including plant biostimulants), adopted on 27 February 2023 and open 
for public feedback until 29 May 2023. 
As communicated by EBIC in the past (‘20220217-EBIC-DigitalLabelling-Final.pdf’ sent during 
the public consultation in February 2022), the voluntary use of digital labelling is considered to 
be very beneficial for the plant biostimulant s industry. The FPR introduces numerous 
mandatory labelling requirements for plant biostimulants, so physical product labels are often 
“overloaded” with information to be FPR-compliant.  
The possibility of digital labelling based on a common framework at the EU level has been 
awaited and is very much welcome by EBIC members.  The introduction of digital labelling 
would notably limit packaging needs, reduc ing waste of such materials. It would introduce 
greater flexibility (e.g. to quickly update information on labels ), it would reduce the 
administrative costs for companies, and would allow for more tailored advice to farmers. 
Considering this, EBIC would like to provide the following feedback on the proposal: 
 
I – EBIC calls for the introduction of a new key criterion in the proposal, i.e. the distinction 
between professional users and non-professional users 
To EBIC, t he biggest issue in the current proposal is that there is no distinction between 
professional and non -professional users. Instead, there is a distinction between economic 
operators (e.g. blenders, retailers, wholesalers , importers or distributors) and end-users (e.g. 
farmers), and between products supplied with  packaging and products supplied without 
packaging (in bulk).  
EBIC strongly encourages the introduction of a clear distinction between professional and non-
professional users , so that products supplied with packaging to professional users 
(independently of whether those professional users are economic operators or end-users) are 
subject to more flexible rules th an products supplied to non -professional users . Another 
possible way of differentiating could be to introduce – in the notion of end -users only – the 
criterion of professional end -users versus non -professional end -users, and to keep the 
provisions targeting non-end users – or economic operators - as they are in the proposal. We 
take note that the EU FPR currently defines ‘economic operators’ only and not ‘end-users’ yet. 
By defining ‘end-user’ in the FPR and introducing the concept of professional end-user versus 
non-professional end-user, digital labelling could be far more flexible for professional than for

non-professional end-users. We note that the so-called ‘Blue Guide’ on the implementation of 
EU product rules 2022 defines end-users1 and mentions professional end-users. 
In fact, most plant biostimulant products are not sold to professional end-users (farmers) 
directly, but through wholesalers and retailers. It is far from reality to assume that wholesalers 
or retailers will prepare physical labels for the products they get from manufacturers with a 
digital label. Therefore, in practice , manufacturers will keep providing the physical labels as 
they do today to those wholesalers and retailers. EBIC members are unanimous about the fact 
that, as the proposal stands now, changing labels and putting in place digital labels is not worth 
the investment. 
EBIC acknowledges the fact that the FPR currently does not distinguish professional from non-
professional users. However, in the context of digital labelling, such a distinction is key . 
Professional users  already routinely check products online before their purchase , and our 
members are convinced that they will not have any trouble in adopting digital labels.  
Studies in recent years are all pointing out to the progressive digitalisation of the EU agricultural 
sector (Kernecker et al., 2020). In a study conducted in 2016, 58,5% of 817 German farmers 
interviewed owned a smartphone (Michels et al., 2020). A study from 2020 showed that 82% 
of farms in Germany already use digital technologies or applications (Bitkom, 2020). Those 
that are particularly prevalent include GPS-controlled agricultural machinery (45%), intelligent 
feeding systems (46%), mobile applications (40%), and the intelligent and site -specific 
application of crop protection agents or fertilizers (32%)  (Bitkom, 2020). To give an idea of 
digitalisation in other agricultural sectors, a study conducted in 2019 with 63 dairy farmers in a 
mountainous area in the North of Italy showed that 69 ,84% used a smartphone often or very 
often for checking weather forecasts, and 44,45% for Internet browsing (Caffaro and Cavallo, 
2019). Finally, in a study conducted among 34 dairy farmers in the Northwest of Spain in 2021, 
97% declared that they used the Internet , apart from being proficient with  new technologies 
(Vázquez-López and Marey-Perez, 2021). We can only expect those numbers to be higher in 
2023. 
Moreover, one of the specific objectives stated in the Commission proposal is to facilitate the 
management of labels by economic operators and to achieve cost -efficiency for the industry. 
By not recognising the specific case of professional users, EBIC members believe that the y 
will not opt for digital labelling and, therefore, this objective will not be attained. 
In addition, EBIC members had expected this digital labelling initiative to facilitate market 
access in countries with smaller markets , where the investment to develo p specific physical 
labels would be eased by the possibility to introduce  information on the  digital label. As the 
proposal stands now, this will not happen, and market access barriers will not be overcome.

If the professional user criterion is introduced, EBIC would advocate for more labelling 
elements to be provided only digitally when product s are supplied with packaging to 
professional users (whether such professional users are end-users or not). 
 
II – About the current requirements of the proposal for products sold to end -users (e.g. 
farmers) 
As stated above, plant biostimulant products sold to end -users (with or without packaging) 
represent a very limited case in practice. Most products are not sold to end-users directly. 
In the case of products made available on the market in packaging to end-users: 
The information that may be provided digitally for the end -user label when the product is 
supplied with packaging is too little compared to the abundant information that has to be 
provided on the physical label  (see comparison table below, prepared with the mandatory 
labelling elements for PFC 6 Plant Biostimulants only). In EBIC’s opinion, the proposal for the 
digital provision of data is far too conservative. As reported by members, it is not worth the 
investment changing all labels. Companies would simply refrain from using digital labelling.

If the end-users/non-end users distinction is kept as such ( i.e. without the introduction of the 
key criterion of distinguishing a professional from a non-professional user), EBIC would call on 
the following labelling element to bear an asterisk in Annex III : ‘instructions for intended use 
including those concerning application rates, timing and frequency, and target plants or 
mushrooms’ – so it can be on the digital label only if desired (i.e. does not need to be duplicated 
on the physical label). This labelling element takes a significant part of the space allocated to 
labelling on physical labels.  The way the proposal is currently phrased is far too exclusive: 
‘instructions for intended use other than  those concerning application rates, timing and 
frequency, and target plants or mushrooms’. According to our members, there are no other 
instructions for intended use (or very limited ones). 
EBIC generally agrees that information on hazard and safe ty, or some general basic product 
identification/information (quantity, physical form, product function, expiry date) , remains on a 
physical label affixed to the packaging of products sold to end -users. However, for use 
instructions and storage instructions (i.e. correct handling and use after purchase), EBIC does 
not agree with the proposal to keep them on physical labels. EBIC would welcome those on 
digital labels. 
Besides the labelling element ‘instructions for intended use including those concerning 
application rates, timing and frequency, and target plants or mushrooms’ which in our opinion 
should bear an asterisk, EBIC calls for another labelling element of Annex III part I point 1.(e) 
to bear an asterisk in the proposal: ‘recommended storage conditions’. 
 
III – Products sold in bulk 
For products sold in bulk (i.e. with no packaging), whether to end-users or not, the requirements 
for digital labelling are generally acceptable. But this is not a major part of the market of plant 
biostimulant products. According to EBIC members,  bulk products are not a problem today  
labelling-wise. A leaflet usually accompanies the bulk product. 
 
IV – Products sold to non-end users 
Products sold to non-end-users - to economic operators - are relevant thinking of wholesalers, 
retailers or blenders. However, the issue described above remains: physical labels will remain

the choice for most EBIC members who, in the vast majority, sell their products to wholesalers 
and retailers. The proposal states that the data carrier on the digital label must be accompanied 
by the warning ‘a physical label must be provided […] before the product is made available on 
the market to end -users in a packaging’. In reality, th e physical label will de facto already be 
provided by the manufacturer selling to the non-end user. 
 
In addition, blenders (also non-end users), are, according to our members, not the biggest part 
of the plant biostimulant products business. 
 
The mandatory requirements for digital labelling, as stated in Article 11b of the COM proposal, 
are generally acceptable and do not call on specific comments  except from the follow ing two 
remarks: 
As regards Article 11b , point 3(b)  of the proposal , we would like to suggest the following 
amended text (what we propose to be added has been underlined): ‘The digital label shall be: 
[…] (b) easily and directly accessible including via downloading and/or installing an application, 
without a need to register in advance , to download or install applications  or to provide a 
password; accessible to all potential users in the Union;’. Rationale: an application on a smart 
phone is essential to read a product label electronically through e.g. 2D data matrix codes. 
Practically, farmers will likely download an application for convenience-purposes.  
In addition, as regards Article 11b, point 3(e) of the proposal, EBIC would welcome more details 
on the fact that the digital label must be available for a period of 5 years from the moment the 
product is placed on the market. How would product changes be handled concretely vis-à-vis 
this digital label? For instance, could that mean a new QR code? Or a code linked to the batch 
number? 
 
 
V – When digital labelling is chosen, EBIC would appreciate further clarity in the proposal 
on how to provide the digital information 
Even if EBIC understands that the COM would adopt supplementing technical requirements 
for the digital label via delegated acts, until the Regulation starts applying, EBIC members 
would appreciate further details about how the label information presented via IT solutions is 
going to be organised. Businesses and users need an easy system, not to the detriment of the 
quality of the information being provided digitally. 
For instance, some EBIC members would support a decentralised organisation, in a database 
operated individually by each manufacturer, following standardised templates or guidelines. 
Overall, members call for a flexible organisation system f or the digital label, leaving them 
freedom, as it would imply costs and would depend on their logistical structures on a case-by-
case basis.

Conclusions: 
EBIC welcomes the Commission proposal on optional digital labelling. EBIC calls for the 
introduction of a criterion for professional users versus non -professional users, so that 
digitisation for products intended for professional users can become a reality.  As it stands, 
the proposal on digital labelling does not go far enough , and most EBIC members would 
not invest in cr eating digital labels. As a second best, EBIC calls for the mandatory 
labelling elements ‘instructions for intended use including those concerning application 
rates, timing and frequency, and target plants or mushrooms’ and ‘recommended storage 
conditions’ to bear an asterisk in Annex III of the FPR. 
 
 
EBIC is willing to contribute to  the proposal as it gets further discussed by the EU decision -
makers during the ordinary legislative procedure. 
We thank you very much and remain at your disposal for further details.

ABOUT EBIC 
 
The European Biostimulant s Industry Council (EBIC) 
promotes the contribution of plant biostimulants to make 
agriculture more sustainable and resilient and in doing 
so promotes the growth and development of the European 
Biostimulant Industry. Our mission is to ensure biostimulant 
technologies are valued as integral to sustainable agriculture, 
while securing an enabling regulatory framework for all of 
them.

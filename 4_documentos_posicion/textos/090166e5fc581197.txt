FNA's response to the European Commission's Consultation on Driving Licences Directive 
The Fédération Nationale de l'Automobile (FNA) has been the professional organisation representing companies in 
the automotive, motorcycle and mobility services sector since 1921. It supports entrepreneurs and craftsmen in the 
automotive and mobility, in particular driving schools represented by the Union des Indépendants de la Conduite  
(UNIC). This organisation ensures the defence of local businesses ’ interests that are  essential to learning about 
mobility. Active in many French departments, UNIC aims to be the first driving school in France, t he first and the 
only training centre. 
1. French market 
Today there are approximately 12,248 driving schools for 25,000 employees. These are local businesses that are 
essential for learning about mobility, whether they are independent or affiliated with a network. 
The Government aims to simplify acces s to mobility and driving licences to facilitating  the employment of young 
people. To this end, several measures are being studied and discussed: 
Lowering the age for obtaining a driving licence to 17 or even 16 under, but only certain conditions emphasized by 
UNIC representatives, 
Removal of the obligation of 20 driving hours necessary to obtaining the driving licence, 
Removal of the 3 months of trai ning necessary to switch from the B licenc e on an automatic gearbox to a licenc e 
on a manual gearbox 
Right of driving schools to issue a restrictive driving C ertificate, to which FNA and UNIC representatives oppose, 
because this measure is  completely deadly for enterprises  and this ability  to deliver a certificate  must remain a 
sovereign mission of the States through their inspectors. 
 
2.   FNA and UNIC Position on the proposal of modified Directive presented by the European Commission 
FNA and UNIC representatives  thank th e European Commission for this C onsultation on D irective 2006/126/EC 
about a major revision of the Union rules related  to driving licences. The present reform is a response to Member 
States' request to strengthen the EU road safety legal framework to ensure that fewer people die in road accidents. 
 
2.1 Choice of strategic option B and the compulsory minimum age to getting the class “B” driving licence 
FNA and UNIC welcome this initiative with satisfaction, for the following reasons: 
 Policy option B, chosen by the European Commission, reinforces policy intervention in road safety. To reduce the 
number of accidents caused in Europe by young people, the Commission proposes to “fix at Union level the minimum 
ages of applicants for the dif ferent categories of driving licence.” It even authorizes  Member States, “in order to further 
enhance road safety, to raise the minimum age required to drive certain categories of vehicles. ” The mandatory minimum 
age throughout Europe for obtaining a B licence is therefore maintained at 18 by the Europe an Commission. It is 
only in "exceptional circumstances" which the Commission defines as "national situations, in particular to allow the 
driving of vehicles used in the context of fire and public order services or pilot projects linked to new technologies for 
vehicles" that the age could be lowered by the Member State to 17 years. 
 
2.2 Preference for a less strict strategic option B which does not stigmatize seniors 
Solution B preferred by the European  Commission to option C is to be welcomed, because stricter rules on 
physical and mental fitness to drive would be tantamount to penalizing eld erly people, whereas  support for 
seniors is preferred for the following reasons: 
Statistics show that drivers between 16 and 17 years old are more likely to be involved in car accidents than drivers in 
any other age group. In addition, these teenage drivers cause more injuries and fatalities than other drivers.

Motorists between the 60 and 70 years old are safer than any other group. The analyzes carried out observe that, on 
the whole, driving licence holders become more careful drivers as they get older and in particular from their sixties 
onwards. Many seniors come to UNIC enterprises  for an upgrade and do not hesitate to take hours on t he initiative of 
their family or so metimes of the doctor who declines to give his opinion  on a disab ility because of negative  social 
consequences for the disabled person. 
Driving licences remain the only chance of mobility  in many rural areas where people are older. All facilities must be 
offered to avoid excluding from mobility people who cannot do anything without their driving licence. 
Therefore, when drivers reach the age of 70, if health problems begin to interfere wi th their driving abilities, it 
is better to encourage them to carry out a self -assessment of their physical and mental fitness to drive, as 
proposed by the European Commission for all drivers, whatever their age, in Appendix III, page 44. Scienti sts 
attest that from 45 years old the various abilities of motorists tend to decline: sight, hearin g and coordination of 
movements. The incidence of these deficiencies requires frequent monitoring. 
But should we therefore reduce to five years or less the period of validity of the licence of drivers when they 
are 70 years  old? In France, even the Minister of the Interior, Mr. Christophe CASTANER, in 2019, spoke out 
against this approach: safety is not linked to the age of the driver but rather to his state of health and his sight.  
He considered that regularly checking the health of all drivers is more effective and less stigm atizing with regard 
to seniors. To make this imperative of checking the medical fitness of motorists more equitable and non -
discriminatory, making the check subject to the renewal of the licence should then be generalized as follows: 
The period of validity of the licence is 15 years, as is the case for the European licence created in 2013 
After these 15 years, there are additional periods of five years for the renewal of the driving licence, as it is already the 
case in some countries of the European Union 
Moreover, in the presentation of the reform of the Directive, on page 3, the European Commission explains 
that "the ex -post evaluation has found that the current age -based screening is no longer perceived as the most relevant. 
Although there is evidence about potential physical deterioration due to age, some studies have concluded that specific 
medical conditions, such as substance abuse, mental health illnesses, epilepsy and diabetes, heart conditions and sleep 
apnoea are not necessarily connected with age. They are however more important factors than age when it comes to medical 
fitness to drive.” 
FNA and UNIC rep resentatives therefore invite the European Commission to review the content of Article 10 
paragraph 2, because the hereafter provisions point the finger at seniors: 
“Member States shall reduce the periods of administrative validity set out in the first subparagraph, points (a) and (b), to f ive 
years or less for driving licences of holders residing on their territory having reached the age of 70, in order to apply an 
increased frequency of medical checks or other specific measures, including refresher courses. This reduced period of 
administrative validity shall only be applied upon renewal of the driving licence.” 
FNA and UNIC representatives  call on the European Commission to clearly set out concise reasons for the chief 
provisions of the se enacting terms , because justification is mandatory. This is all the more essential to legal 
certainty as there appears to be an inconsistency between the approach developed in the general presentation of 
the reform and that applied to Article 10  paragraph 2  of the proposed Directive: on the one hand, age is not 
considered to be a determining criterion, on the other hand the age of 70 constitutes a limit to the period of 
validity of th e permit. To remove  any confusion and a lack of consiste ncy, a clarification is , thus,  needed. 
Jurisconsult Hubert LEGAL, Head of the Legal Department of the Council of Ministers and of the European Council 
noted in this respect in 2015 that the recitals m ust be precise on the conditions on which the provisions are 
based, because it is a question of " giving the possibility the parties to a dispute to defend their rights, and the Court of 
Justice of the European Union to exercise its control1. »

2.3 Choice of strategic option B and the compulsory minimum age to getting the class “B” driving licence with ACC 
France has implement ed the “Advanced Driving Learning ” (ACC) for many years and it is a success. The  
following results speak for themselves: 
In 2019, the AAC resulted in the issuance of 213,898 B permits (i.e. + 8.5% compared to the previous year) 
The proportion of permits issued in AAC represents 24.8% of all B permits issued. The B permit success rate for these 
candidates is 74.24%; which represents 17 points more than the overall success rate and 21 points more than the non-
AAC success rate 
FNA and UNIC representatives  are therefore very much in favor of this initiative. They are pleased that the 
European Commission has followed the recommendation presented by FNA in  previous consultations: the 
Commission has understood the reasons for the opposition expressed by the FNA in these previous consultations 
to starting training at age 15: only 2 out of 10 pupils are on average sufficiently prepared for this training. 
The two professional organizations confirm their vigilance with regard to obtaining the  permit before the 
minimum age required. This should only be  possible through AAC and with restrictions such as the prohibition to 
drive during certain hours. 
The European Commission has accepted this approach in the explanatory memorandum to the Directive : recital 
n°28, pages 22 and 23, explains that in this case,  for reasons of road safety, "the use of those driving licences should 
be subject to being accompanied by an experienced driver. In such situations, Member States should be allowed, for reasons 
of road safety, to define stricter conditions and rules within their territory concerning the driving licences they have issued.” 
As for the extensi on of the AAC to the heavy goods vehicles, UNIC representatives endorse the position of the 
European Commission, which is motivated by considerations of young people employment in a sector where the 
shortage of drivers is problematic. The se are the reasons which led French Parliament to adopt the Article 99 of 
the new law n° 2019-1428 of December 24, 2019 on the o rientation of mobility (LOM law), which amended the 
provisions of Articles L211-4 and 5 of the Highway Code as follows: 
Article L211-4: "Anyone aged at least eighteen years can follow an apprenticeship in supervised driving of light vehicles, 
under the constant and direct supervision of a guide, after validation either of their initial training or of skills minimum 
during the practical driving license test defined by order of the Minister responsible for road safety. » 
Article L211 -5: "Persons undergoing vocational training with a view to obtaining a national education diploma or a 
professional title issued by the Minister responsible for employment allowing the issuance of a driving license may 
practice supervised driving, on a light vehicle or a vehicl e of the heavy group, under the constant and direct supervision 
of an accompanying person fulfilling the conditions set by the decree mentioned in Article L. 211-7. 
Supervised driving is accessible from the age of sixteen to students who have completed the training prior to obtaining a 
license to drive light vehicles or vehicles in the heavy group. »

UNIC representatives regret that the system of AAC provided for in article 14 of Directive is not inspired by 
French provisions, in particular as regards the obligation of training and the level to be acquired. The objecti ve of 
road safety requires, in fact, that the training program be harmonized in Europe. The risk is, as observed by UNIC  
enterprises, that holders of a class “ C” driving licence from other countries of the European Union have not 
reached the required level. UNIC teachers have noticed a difference in theory and practice. The level of training is 
not as required . Some foreign candidates have their  class “C” driving permit, but if they had taken  it in France, 
they would not have got it because they are not conversant with the necessary knowledge. Their level is still well 
below that of a French candid ate who is preparing for a “B”  driving licence. UNIC representatives, therefore, call 
on the European Commission to introduce into the provis ions of the Directive this training obligation at a more 
demanding level and to determine the training program in more detail in appendix II dedicated to th e "minimum 
requirements for driving tests and knowledge, skill and behaviour for driving a power-driven vehicle (pages 30 to  
43).” For road safety reasons, it is necessary to lay down the minimum requirements for granting a driving licence. 
This involves an upward harmonization of standar ds for driver testing and licencing. The European Commission 
highlights in recital n°18, page 21 that “the knowledge, skills and behaviour connected with driving motor vehicles should 
be specified, the driving test should be based on these concepts and the minimum standards of physical and mental fitness for 
driving such vehicles should be specified.”  But these details are still lacking. Measures must be taken to prevent 
differences in learners from the European Union, not only because many of them  are coming  to exercise their 
work as drivers in France to address shortages, but also because the objective of improving road safety is the key 
element at stake. 
 
2.4 Choice of strategic option B and adaptation to new technologies and societal changes 
UNIC representatives support  the Eur opean Commission's initiative to issue “mobile driving licences”, ie in 
digital form. However, this new modality should not b e done to the detriment of the physical driving licence. For 
the purpose of legal certainty, the  Commission must clearly make explicit  the distinction and complementarity 
between the two forms of driving licence. 
UNIC organisation welcomes with great satisfaction that the European Commission obliges drivers to "have at all 
times the necessary knowledge, skills and behavior, in order to be able to have sufficient knowledge of risk factors related to 
micro mobility means;” (Appendix II page 43). This is, in fact, the new trend that is increasingly widespread in cities 
and even in the countryside: micro -mobility is soft mobility that is easily transported, light and practical. Among 
the modes of transport concerned, there are machines belonging to the following families: scooters, skateboards, 
bicycles (often folding), balance bikes or electric scooters. 
 
3. FNA’s Position, in particular of the recovery-towing sector on the extension of the B permit to 4.25 t 
FNA representatives note  the constant increase in the weight of vehicles and draw the relevant conclusions.       
They approve the European Commission's Position on mobility aimed at reducing emissions of pollutants. 
 
3.1 Observation of constant vehicle weight increase 
The constant vehicle weight increase concerns both passenger and utility vehicles 
The present SUVs (Sport Utility Vehicles) fad, SUVs being known for their large loading capacity, as well as for the 
electric car (EV), whose battery i s increasingly heavy, and  the numerous comfort and safety equipment  are at the 
origin the increased weight of current vehicles. Observed d uring tests carried out in 2022 , this growth is changing 
rapidly. The independent international rating organisation, which is the reference in the purchase of vehicles,  the 
Euro NCAP (“European New Car Assessment Programm”), presents a comparative table, attache d below, which shows the

average curb weight increase, from 2010 to 2020 and from 2020 to 2022. The EVs weight tested in 2022 is listed in 
the third column (“Battery Electric Vehicle, BEV”). 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
The proliferation of mandatory safety systems, the heaviness of EV batteries, connectivity growing steadily and the 
“Advanced D river Assistance Systems” (ADAS)  are highlighted to explain this progression. As this whole set of 
elements (“the safety pack”) is part of the rating criteria used by Euro NCAP, the vehicle receives a bad rating if one 
of them is lacking. To increase their sales, vehicle manufacturers are taking measures to ensure that this pack can 
be easily checked: configuration is specified and the amount of kilos is well detailed. Certainly safer for  their users, 
these heavier vehicles  are nevertheless pointed  out by the rating organization, because of the risks they pose to 
motorists who drive lighter, o ften older ones; severity of the crash  is evidenced  by the damage caused , for 
example, when a recent vehicle collides with a city car being no more than ten years of age. 
Commercial vehicles are often overweight and the consequences are just as alarming as for passenger vehicles. 
This is the case, in particular, of the breakdown and towing vehicle fleet, the overloading of which is recognized and the 
impact on road safety highlighted: premature wear of the breakdown vehicle, deterioration of road holding, lengthening 
of slowing distances, det erioration of braking components and suspension as well as tires with danger of bursting, 
inefficiency of the electronic stability control and maintenance device. Accidents can cause the overturning of the 
machine, the projection of materials or even the fall of the towed vehicle. 
Commercial vehicles are used for the delivery of food  products in the city centre; for example, for the catering trades, 
professionals use small refrigerated trucks limited to 3.5 tonnes: equipped with isothermal boxes and compressors, they 
are easily overweight si nce they already weigh 3.3 to 3 .5 tons. Driving with a B licence is subject to fines; this forces the 
enterprises to limit loading and increase the number of deliveries; the result is an increase in financial costs and, from an 
ecological point of view, a significant increase in pollution. In Germany, drivers benefit from different  regulations for the 
transport of goods for these same vehicles: a tolerance brings to 4.25 t (i.e. 3.5 t + 750 kg) the maximum authorized laden 
mass of vehicles (without trailer) which can be driven with licence B. 
 
3.2 Consequences for road safety and changing circumstances of economic development 
Road safety and local and regional economic development unde r equal conditions for enterprises  throughout 
Europe plead for an adaptation of Directive 2006/126/EC to current reality.  
 
The following 8 proposals are presented: 
3.2.1. Compulsory training in the control of vehicles that have become heavier 
The establishment of initial training, followed by compulsory continuing training, is essential: 
To make drivers more aware of the serious consequences for road safety involved in the use of heavier vehicles and 
To enable them to master their driving 
The Commission seems to have adopted this FNA recommendation for initial driver training,  whose minimum 
knowledge requirements are defined in Annex II (page 43), updated to take into account: 
“- the need to recognise and anticipate traffic dangers and hazards and assess their seriousness 
- the requirement to have sufficient knowledge of safety related to the usage of alternatively fuelled vehicles and knowledge on 
the usage of advanced driving assistance systems and of other automation aspects of a vehicle.” 
However, a clarification should be made in these provisions to further emphasize the risks posed by increasingly 
heavy passenger or commercial vehi cles to users of micro -mobility, as well as to  drivers of older and less heavy 
vehicles that characterize today's car fleet.

3.2.2 Proposals for a class “B” driving licence at 4.25 t 
The proposal to increase the maximum permissible total weight to 4.25 tonnes for class “B” driving licence holders, 
subject to the training mentioned in point 1, has been a requ est from FNA representatives since  many years, in 
order to take into consideration the increase in the weight of vehicles on the market. This will al so make it easier 
for t he recovery and towing enterprises  currently recognized to be overloaded when towing take the current 
heavier vehicles. 
 
3.2.3 Simplification of access to the driving licence for light commercial vehicles (bus, truck) 
The FNA is in favor of strengthening the supervision of light commercial vehicles (LCVs), which benefit in France and 
in Europe from a flexible regulatory framework. These veh icles are driven with a class “B” licence for the transport 
of goods throughout Europe. Simplifying access to heavy goods vehicle ( HGV) licences is a good initiati ve, if the 
following measures are also taken: 
- In terms of compulsory minimum initial training (“Formation Initiale Minimale Obligatoi re – FIMO”) and compulsory 
continuing education (“Formation Continue Obligatoire – FCO”), and 
- Technical inspection compulsory are to be scheduled after the driving licence, and 
- More tougher sanctions are to be taken in the event of breaches of the Highway Code provisions 
 
3.2.4 Instructors’ training 
Instructors must have continuous training adapted to the evolution of both vehicles and learners. The educational 
and psychological approach must be focused on the vulnerabilities of today's students and on the security risks 
linked to technolo gical developments in vehicles, i.e. electrification of the current fleet, handling  of driving 
assistance systems, etc. 
 
3.2.5 Learners’ training  
The content of the training must be enriched without impacting the cost of the licence. In this area, driving schools 
note significant shortcomings in vehicle control. The initial training must be reinforced on the fundamentals, fa ce-
to-face or remotely for Highway the C ode, but essentially on the practice a nd the reflexes to be acquired to 
preserve road safety while driving increasingly powerf ul vehicles in terms of weight and speed; additional course s 
must be planned in post -licence training on broader themes which would allow either to recover points, or t o 
obtain advantages, in particular for the payment of  the insurance. FNA representatives are in favor of the proposal 
for refresher courses on eco-driving and knowledge of new vehicle technologies. 
 
3.2.6 Practical driving test in real conditions 
FNA organisation is opposed to the use of driving simulators during the e xamination of the driving licence, because 
the control of the reflexes must be done in real situation. It is a question of both feeling the power of the vehicle 
and experiencing traffic with other motorists in real time: these are essential prerequisites for better mastering 
driving and acquiring reflexes. The spontaneity of the reaction to danger is best verified under these condit ions. 
Driving simulators provide applicants with a less stressing state  of mind, as  they know  that there are no real 
consequences on the safety of road users. 
 
3.2.7 No lowering of the age to 18 for class “D” licence 
FNA is against  lowering the age to 18 for class “ D” license, for the purpose  of road safety and safeguarding the 
physical integrity of transported persons. 
 
3.2.8 Probationary period limited to driving light vehicles (urban cars) 
Young drivers aged 18 to 24 accounted for 18% of road deaths in 2017; they are involved in 32% of accidents. This 
is why FNA proposes that during the probationary period of three years, (which is reduced to two if the motorist 
carries out a post-licence course or to 1 year and a half when he benefits from an AAC ), the type of vehicles driven

should be limited by a power/weight ratio, for example to the use of city cars.  The proposal of Directive provides 
this possibility in paragraph 3 of Article 15, which states that “Member States may establish additional rules applicable 
on their territory to novice drivers during the probationary period to improve road safety. They shall inform the Commission 
thereof.” Still for road safety reasons, Articl e 15 of the Directive require  "the holder of a driving licence of a given 
category issued for the first time shall be considered a novice driver and shall be subject to a probationary period of 
at least two years .” FNA representatives call on the European Commission  that awareness of the seriousness of 
accidents caused by driving current passenger cars and LCVs,  which are increasingly heavy, should also be included 
among the limitations for novice drivers. That is to say that  their daily driving during this probationary period 
should be limited to small urban cars, in order to implement step-by-step the practices and reflexes acquired  and 
better master their car. 
 
3.3. Furthering knowledge and promoting the mobility contributing to the reduction of pollution emissions 
FNA representatives are supporting the European Commission ’s Position  and the latter approves that the 
statement that FNA presented during previous consultations is justified. 
 
3.3.1. FNA supporting the European Commission’s Position on reduction of pollution emissions 
FNA encourages me mbers of driving schools to teach the best driving to reduce pollutant emissions by providing 
learners with the means to limit their impact on emissions and the appropriate driving style to reach this objective. 
Mastering driving zero -emission vehicles sho uld be thorough and complete . M oreover, because these zero -
emission vehicles are those who have an increasingly heavy weight. FNA shares the Sustainable and Smart Mobility 
Strategy implemented by the European Union to significantly improve sustainability o f mobility and tra nsport. 
Learners must be made aware, as the Commission points out  in recital 12 , that  “emissions from the road transport 
sector include greenhouse gases emissions, air pollutants, noise and microplastics from tyre and road wear. Driving s tyle 
influences these emissions, with possible negative impacts on the environment and human health. Therefore, driving training 
should equip drivers to reduce their impact on emissions as well as to prepare them to drive zero-emission vehicles.” 
 
3.3.2 Appreciation for the European Commission’s Position in support of FNA's Position on the “B” licence extended to 4.25 t 
To encourage road users, especially professionals, to equip themselves with vehicles that respect the environment, 
the Commission is incl uding new provisions on progressiveness and equivalence between categories of driving 
licence. Article 9, paragraph 2 sub-paragraph h) of the Directive (page 31 -32) provides the possibility for a class “B” 
licence to drive heavier vehicles: “two years after a driving licence, granted for category B, was issued for the first time it 
shall be valid for driving the alternatively fuelled vehicles referred to in Article 2 of Council Directive 96/53/EC13 with a  
maximum authorised mass above 3 500 kg but not exceeding 4 250 kg without a trailer.”The European Commission is, thus, 
giving support to FNA position, already developed in the first part of this consultation to hi ghlight the constant 
increase of the weight of passenger vehicles and LCVs. By doing so, the European institution cleverly kills two birds 
with one stone , i.e.  including new rules on staging and equivalences between categories and increasing the 
maximum mass of most zero-emission vehicles in class “B” driving licence. The Commission's strategy is to facilitate 
the use of these "green" vehicles, in order to contribute to the achievement of the ob jectives of European Green 
New Deal  and achieve the "zero pollution" objective and also to align European enterprises  on an equal footing. 
This practice, as we have already written, is common in Germany where companies have been driving powerful 
LCVs for years with class “ B” licences to the cent re of towns, whereas it is prohibited in France. It is, however, 
necessary to add the other essential c ondition to ensure that the preservation of the environment is not done to 
the detriment of that of road safety, i.e.  to make continuous road safety training compulsory for the control of 
these vehicles, whose powerful weight inc reases the risk of death on  traffic roads. For the purpose  of legal 
certainty, this requirement should be clearly stated in the modified Directive.

 PRIMIGEA COMPLEMENTARY AMEDMENT SUGGESTIONS Preliminary comment The text of the regulation incorporates a series of measures that the sector fully supports, insofar as they will have positive effects on the raw materials industry.  Thus, the measures to speed up deadlines and procedures (Articles 8, 9, 10 and 11); planning and zoning (some) (12); online accessibility to administrative information (17); the implementation of national exploration programs (18); measures to avoid supply crises (19-22); measures to promote circularity (25-28); recognized certification systems (29); measures to ensure free circulation, conformity and market surveillance (section 3), are considered to be very positive with some exceptions that are analysed in this report and with the main point that some of them should be extended, with the necessary adaptations or exceptions, to all MPMs, including all their value chains.  That is why it is essential to broaden the scope of strategic raw materials or to define a third category (which would include all other RMs), for example, essential MPMs, to which most of the articles would not apply, but which would include some measures to improve access to resources and a less ambitious time limit for the processing processes. In addition to solving this serious problem that invalidates the proposal for 99% of the European RMs industry, the following needs for improvement should be mentioned: § The CRM Regulation is more a declaration of intent towards Member States than a Regulation with concrete and effective measures, with a very high-level wording and not very concrete. It is clearly a soft regulation, more akin to a Directive than a Regulation. Thus, the approach of the Regulation makes it weaker and less robust than the Net Zero Industrial Act, which makes it appear to be a subsidiary regulation. § The proposal seems to forget that it must be applied in 27 member states and more than 325 regions, with different legislations in force, different distribution of competences between states, regions and municipalities. In addition, it is very important to note that the territories are not virgin and that there is a multitude of pre-existing extractive rights in force, of rights in process, of limiting land use planning, etc.  § The administrative structures that would have to comply with Regulation are already saturated and overwhelmed by interminable permitting procedures. The prioritization of some projects over the rest would cause further delays for those excluded. This point is particularly important, since for this reason and the one described below, the proposal ceases to be neutral for 99% of the industry and becomes detrimental to it, which makes it unacceptable. There is a precedent derived from the prioritization of renewable energy projects. § Another critical problem derives from the lack of mechanisms to equitably resolve overlaps between pre-existing rights or with the permitting processes already initiated and new projects derived from the Regulation. § Both issues could be a source of conflicts in the territory that would be detrimental to the very objectives pursued by the Regulation due to the conflict judicialization that could be undertaken by the companies harmed in the exercise of their legitimate rights. § The proposal omits private or public-private research, does not offer any clear framework for it, and does not include measures against speculation, nor to adapt to the different legal frameworks of ownership of RMs resources in the different Member States, or to their internal organization and distribution of competences.  § Nor does it include mechanisms for compatibility with regulations that prevent access to resources (nature protection, ecological restoration, soil health, etc.).

§ It is not clear why some of its provisions are limited to strategic projects and do not also apply to critical RMs. § Similarly, there are raw materials that should clearly be integrated into the published lists. § In addition, there are issues to be improved linked to the positive aspects described above, such as some of the planning and zoning measures (12), implementation and reporting (14), financing (15 and 16), downstream risk preparedness (23) or joint purchasing systems (24).  § Another important aspect is that the protection of the confidentiality of sensitive data should be reinforced to protect confidential information from malicious third parties or direct competitors, as opposed to the proposal spirit of transparency. § There are no mechanisms to prevent distortion of competition between EU countries, disruption of the single market and the aid framework. The vagueness of many parts of the text points in that direction. § It also lacks a unified sanctioning regime (it is left to the Member States), which should be a single European one and apply to the Member States (actors of most of the actions of the proposal), to avoid additional distortions of competition, starting from the two extremes (Emissions Trading Scheme (European) and Industrial Emissions Directive (national)). § The financing mechanisms are unclear, as it is ambiguous the reference to the Taxonomy or the calculation of the environmental footprint. § Finally, public procurement should focus on third country RMs, with mechanisms that avoid the risk of downward pressure on the price of European RMs. Specific proposals are made below in relation to the above. Proposal on article 3 (3)  Regarding article 3 paragraph 3 “The Commission shall review and, if necessary, update the list of  strategic raw materials by [OP please insert: four years after the date of entry into force of this  Regulation], and every four years thereafter”  Proposal  “The Commission shall review and, if necessary, update the list of strategic raw materials by [OP please insert: four years after the date of entry into force of this Regulation], and every 4 four years thereafter the second year after entry into force of this Regulation and every two years thereafter.  Para 4 [new] By derogation from Paragraph 3 of this Article, the Commission shall add additional raw materials on the list upon request of the Board if the Board or the Commission detects while exercising the monitoring, stress testing and information obligations under Articles 19.1, 19.2 and 20 of this regulation changes in supply and access to specific raw materials that risk endangering the targets set out in Article 1.2.”  Justification:  If a supply risk arises, there is a need to be able to react quickly.  To take account of possible technological and economic changes, the list of strategic materials should be periodically reviewed and, if necessary, updated. In addition, this list should be updated on an ad-hoc basis if the Board and the Commission determine in exercising their monitoring obligations an immediate supply risk, so as to facilitate projects when suddenly the supply situation unforeseeably changes (i.e. wars, conflicts, natural catastrophes etc.).

IV.  Implementation of Strategic Projects Article 7. “3. The Member State whose territory is concerned by a Strategic Project shall take measures to contribute to its timely and effective implementation.”  Proposal "In the event that the Strategic Project comes in conflict with other right or pre-existing extractive activity or a non-strategic extractive project whose processing had begun prior to the Strategic one, the Member State must implement agile procedures to analyse the compatibility between both, so that, to the extent possible, efforts will be made to ensure that the Strategic Project continues, as long as it does not impair the exploitation or the oldest extractive project or whose processing began earlier. In the event that the technical analysis results in the incompatibility, the conflict must be resolved on a case-by-case basis, taking into account factors such as the amount of reserves, strategic importance of the material, as well as other economic and social factors. In the event of the pre-existence of a non-strategic extractive project and if the incompatibility and prevalence of the Strategic Project is determined, the owner of the earlier project must be compensated. However, if the prevalence of the previous, non-strategic extractive project is declared, its holder will have no obligation to indemnify the subsequent one”. Comment on paragraph 5 article 7 (page 26) Paragraph 5 of the same article states that: “The project promoter shall, every two years after the date of recognition as a Strategic Project, submit a report to the Board containing information on at least:  (a)  progress in the implementation of the project, in particular with regard to the permit granting process.  (b)  where relevant, reasons for delays compared to the timetable referred to in Article 6(1), point (c) and a plan to overcome such delays.  (c)  progress in financing the project, including information on public financial support.”  It remains unresolved what would happen if the promoter detected that the delay can be attributed almost exclusively to administrative processing problems. The Confederation here raises the question of how the Commission plans to address this type of delay, either by requesting the non-compliant State, or through sanctions, or through judicial actions. Comment on paragraph 6 article 7 (page 26) In paragraph 6, the following is said: "The Board may request additional information from project promoters relevant to the implementation of the Strategic Project at any moment.” This term, “relevant”, is legally indeterminate or non-concrete. It is necessary to limit the type of information that could be required by the Council from the promoter to avoid surprise requests. In addition, this type of requirements must be covered by the appropriate protection measures to avoid leaks of confidential or reserved information that could damage the promoter and the viability or success of the Project.

V.  One stop shop  Proposal on article 8, paragraph 3 “3. The responsibilities of the national competent authority referred to in paragraph 1 or the tasks related to it may be delegated to, or carried out by, another authority, for each critical,  strategic and/or essential raw material projects, provided that:  (a)  the national competent authority referred to in paragraph 1 notifies the project promoter of that delegation.  (b)  a single authority is responsible for each critical, strategic and/or essential raw material projects.  (c)  a single authority coordinates the submission of any relevant documents and information. (d) if a project promoter conducts more than one critical,  strategic and/or essential materials project in a Member State within the same operational step, a single authority shall be responsible for this promoter’s projects” Justification The new point (d) is added to avoid that a project promoter running more than one strategic project must deal with different single points of contact. Comment on paragraph 3 article 8 (page 27) From the point of view of the Spanish State, the linkage of this provision should be analysed so that this single national authority does not invade the competences of the Regions. Apparently, it may be delegated to other authorities, therefore, it may happen that the "distributor" or "coordinator" of the affairs was the State through the Ministry, although the competence would continue to be executed by the Regions. Another possible option would be for the regions to simply assume the competence of the sole authority in their territory, without the intervention of the national authority. In this case, the issue of jurisdiction would be respected, but the coordination task would be blurred, unless the State exercises a role of mere intermediary in terms of transferring information from the Regions to the Commission. For the purposes of the one-stop shop, City Councils are not being considered, even though, without an activity planning license, extractive activities cannot be carried out. Comment on paragraph 5 article 8 (page 27) “5. The national competent authority referred to in paragraph 1 shall take into consideration any valid studies conducted and permits or authorisations issued for a given critical,  strategic and/or essential raw material project before the project entered the permit granting process in accordance with this Article, and shall not require duplicate studies and permits or authorisations, unless otherwise required under Union law.”  According to smart regulation principles, the prohibition to require duplicate studies and permits or authorisations, must of general application. There is no reason why this provision should only apply to Strategic Projects, while other projects are left at risk of this type of unfair requirements. Proposal on paragraphs 6 and 7 article 8 (page 27) “6. The national competent authority referred to in paragraph 1 shall ensure that applicants have easy access to information on and simple procedures for the settlement of disputes concerning the permit granting process and the issuance of permits for critical,  strategic and/or essential raw materials projects, including, where applicable, alternative dispute resolution mechanisms. The Board shall share and discuss best practices including from other relevant mining regions ensuring structured and predictable formats.

VI.  Digitization In accordance with the provisions of the explanatory memorandum: "In order to allow citizens and businesses to directly enjoy the benefits of the internal market without incurring an unnecessary additional administrative burden, Regulation (EU) 2018/1724 of the European Parliament and the Council, which established the Single Digital Gateway, provides for general rules for the online provision of information, procedures and assistance services relevant for the functioning of the internal market. The information requirements and procedures covered by this Regulation should comply with the requirements of Regulation (EU) 2018/1724. In particular, it should be ensured that project promoters of Strategic Project can access and complete any procedure related to the permit granting process fully online, in line with Article 6(1) of and Annex II to Regulation (EU) 2018/1724.” (page 7) The multiple incidents of digital platforms are one of the frequent causes of delays in processing, which is why the Commission is urged to analyse the technological development of each member state, in order to determine concrete measures to improve and adapt these systems, so that they can provide citizens with an effective digital platform service on equal terms with the rest of the European promoters, either for Critical/Strategic RM projects or for Essential RM. VII.  Priority status of Strategic Projects  Comment regarding paragraph 3 article 9 (page 28): “3. All dispute resolution procedures, litigation, appeals and judicial remedies related to the permit-granting process and the issuance of permits for Strategic Projects in the Union in front of any national courts, tribunals, panels, including mediation or arbitration, where they exist in national law, shall be treated as urgent, if and to the extent to which national law provides for such urgency procedures and provided that the normally applicable rights of defence of individuals or of local communities would be respected. Project promoters of Strategic Projects shall participate in such urgency procedure, where applicable.” As has already been stated in other sections, this provision is not complemented if there are no consequences in the event of breaches or non-compliance. It seems that the article regulates conflict between pre-existing rights or permits and the Strategic ones. It indicates that an urgent procedure must be applied to resolve it,  “if and to the extent to which national law provides for such urgency procedures”. However, it could be covering any type of conflict, including those of individuals with the Administration, for any reason, in administrative appeals.

Planning Proposal Article 12 "Member States shall ensure that national, regional and local authorities responsible for preparing plans, including zoning, spatial plans and land use plans, include in such plans, where appropriate, provisions for the development of critical, strategic or/and essential  raw materials projects. Priority shall be given to artificial and built surfaces, industrial sites, brownfield sites, and, where appropriate, greenfield sites not usable for agriculture and forestry. with the objective to a priori balancing different or competing land use claims.”  Justification Land use conflicts can create serious barriers to the deployment of raw material projects, whether critical, strategic or essential. The plans must be well designed, so that they consider the potential to implement raw material projects, decreasing the risk of conflict and accelerating the sustainable deployment of raw material projects in the Union. Therefore, competent national, regional, and local authorities should consider including provisions for raw material projects when developing relevant plans. Well-designed planning, considering competing interests, including extractive interests, should be an aspiration for all management plans.

Financing Comment on articles 15 and 16 (page 31) The sector agrees with the activation of financing systems that encourage the promoters of all RMs projects, not only Critical, for the reasons already mentioned. Thus, for example, regarding the system that allows both interested buyers and promoters of Strategic Projects to indicate their purchase or sale offers, extreme caution must be exercised to avoid distortions in the market and in free competition. XVII. Penalties Article 45 establishes the following: “By 12 months after entry into force of the Regulation, Member States shall lay down rules on penalties applicable to infringements of this Regulation and shall take all measures necessary to ensure that they are implemented. The penalties provided for shall be effective, proportionate and dissuasive. Member States shall, without delay, notify the Commission of those rules and of those measures and shall notify it, without delay, of any subsequent amendment affecting them.” Comment on article 45 (page 56) It is necessary that the Member States establish effective, proportionate and dissuasive penalties in their national legislation in case of non-compliance with this Regulation, however, the nature of the penalties to be imposed is not developed nor are minimum or maximum limits established. Since there is no unity of the sanctioning regime, there will be dispersion and the effectiveness in the application of the regulation will be unequal from one territory to another. For this reason, it is proposed, without invading the exclusive powers of the Member States, that a scheme of penalties be drawn from which the states can start from the other to establish the sanctions so that a certain uniformity is achieved regarding the nature and entity of the measures to impose. Penalties should be imposed, not only on those administered, but also, under a disciplinary regime, on Member States or Public Administrations who unjustifiably fail to comply with the deadlines and regulatory procedures.

MOVING’s Position in favour of the use of simulators for education. 
 
 
 The P roposal added new contents concerning all categories of vehicles, in particular the 
inclusion of  hazard perception , risks including vehicles with advanced driving 
assistance systems and other automatic features, knowledge safety aspects related to 
alternatively fuelled vehicles and aspects in relation to the environment: including as 
regards electrical vehicles, limitat ions of emissions  (air pollutants, noise and 
microplastics from tyre and road wear etc.) 
 
In this line, we consider that the use of simulators should also be included in the Proposal, as 
an important tool t o teach  these new contents, specially hazard perc eption risks,  and even 
to add others as driving in country roads. With that aim, it would be necessary to take into 
consideration two aspects: training on the simulator must take place in a driving school with a 
feedback of the results to the driving instructor. Also we should consider that in the foreseea-
ble future, there will be no more driving school cars with gearboxes, in some countries of 
Europe as it is the case of Germany. However, learner drivers buy old cars with ma nual 
gearboxes; how are they supposed to learn to change gears without a simulator?.

In fact and according with MOVING Industry Report 2023, the use of simulators takes into 
account the planned expansion of training  around the topic of hazard perception. The aim is 
to better condition the learner drivers to recognise potential driving situations in good time, to 
avoid them and, if necessary, to react adequately. 
According with a survey of driving schools, using a simulator by the Institute for the 
Automobile Indus try (IfA), a round six percent of driving schools in Germany already used 
driving simulators in 2016 to better prepare their students for road traffic and the test .  In 
coherence with these figures,  MOVING assumes that between 1.500 and 2.000 simulators 
are already used in driving schools in Germany and around 2.100 in France. 
Today, simulators are able to depict everyday traffic situations in a realisti c and practical 
way. In this sense, various procedures can be practised without any risk and even dangerous 
and unusual traffic situations can be simulated. The possibilities of the simulator are far from 
exhausted, and the development of the software shows that the scenarios are becoming 
more and more realistic and can now also depict difficult weathe r conditions and 
imponderable hazardous situations. 
Driving simulators can help to combat the acute shortage of driving instructors, because 
neither a vehicle nor a driving instructor is needed, as the simulator works autonomously.  
A survey in 2021 of dri ving schools, using a simulator by the Institute for the Automobile 
Industry (IfA) proves the benefits that its use brings. It increases for example, the pass rate in 
the theoretical and practical driving test, trains dangerous situations and thus increase s road 
safety. 
There is another important a spect highlighetd in our green Deal P osition1 and that is 
environmental friendliness. A driving lesson in the simulator saves 85 percent CO2 
compared to a n ormal driving lesson in a Golf  7 (1,6 TDI 85 Kw)  which will contribute to 
reach the EU’s climate goal of reducing emissions by at least 55% by 2030 and make EU 
climate neutral by 2050 i.  In this line, in 2016, the Institute for Automotive Economics at the 
University of Applied Sciences in Geislingen scientifically examined the use of driving simula-
tors in driving  schools.  
It was proven that the eco -balance of the simulators is extremely positive.   An    average 
practice drive of 25km results in CO² emissions of 2675  gr (cf. Volkswagen.at). A common 
simulator consumes 187gr CO² per simulator hour (in a standard energy mix) (cf. Energiea r-
gentur.nrw). The simulator hour thus produces 14 times less CO² than a comparable driving 
lesson in a driving school car. In Germany a lone, more than 688  tons of CO² were saved in 
2018. Therefore the use of simulators in driving training is one of the best ways driving 
schools can contribute to climate neutrality.

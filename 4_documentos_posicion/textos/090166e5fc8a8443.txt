About Northvolt  
Northvolt is a European supplier of sustainable, high -quality battery cells and systems. 
Founded in 2016 to enable the European transition to a decarbonized future, the company 
has made swift progress on its mission to build the most sustainable batteries in the world for 
the transition to renewable energy.  Northvolt has high ambitions for battery recyc ling and is 
currently engaged in establishing its first full -scale battery recycling plant, Revolt Ett, 
alongside Northvolt Ett gigafactory. 
Executive Summary 
Access to and supply of Critical Raw Materials (CRMs) is crucial to the  European energy 
transition and to the EU’s broader industrial and strategic goals. For the EU’s battery industry, 
domestic supplies of relevant CRMs remain insufficient to meet expected demand and  
challenges to  cost-competitive access to these materials both in Europe and in third 
countries endure for European industrial players.  
Northvolt welcomes the Critical Raw Materials Act (CRM Act) proposed in March 2023, which 
represents a considerable step in improving the supply of raw materials needed for the green 
transition. Measures regarding eased permitting for strategic projects and the focus on 
sustainability and circularity are conducive to building a more secure and sustainable raw 
materials supply chain. If these measures are comprehensively implemented, they will 
substantially contribute to risk mitigation objectives , eliminating the necessity for market-
intervention provisions as outlined in Chapter 4. The Chapter should therefore be reduced to 
only core monitoring provisions, while removing measures on stockpiling, company risk 
preparedness, and joint purchasing (Art. 21, 22, 23 & 24).   
However, access to funding for strategic projects will remain a barrier to the achievement of 
the Act’s long-term goals. Therefore, in addition to the provisions described in this document, 
Northvolt supports the implementation of tangible financial provisions through a dedicated 
European CRM Fund.

1. Remove measures on stockpiling and joint purchasing (Art. 21, 22 & 
24) 
Direct market intervention in the form of s trategic reserve buying and policies designed 
around redistribution schemes  can inflate prices and artificially accentuate scarcity . This is 
potentially in counter action to market fundamentals , thus  negatively impacting European 
producers of value -added goods  who are  dependent on CRMs. European users of raw 
materials must already contend with higher capital and operating costs  than competitors in 
third countries ; stockpiling measures could apply addi tional and unnecessary input cost 
pressures to nascent European industry. Northvolt therefore calls for removing measures on 
both stockpiling and joint purchasing. 
2. Company risk preparedness (Art. 23)  
Northvolt does not support the introduction of regulatorily mandated provisions on company 
risk preparedness, such as stress test s, particularly when  measured against metrics 
determined by non-industry third parties . The implementation of such policies is duplicative 
to natural industry efforts to miti gate risk  and diversify supply , both increasing costs for 
European producers and potentially producing an indirect market-distorting effect.  
In particular, such  measures may trigger a “race to the door” to secure supply contracts 
against f orecasted consumption which may or may not materialize , leading to  
overcommitment, inflated commodity prices, and supply squeezes, followed by eventual price 
collapses and reduced production capacity in the medium- to long-term.  
Additionally, r eporting obligations already exist under other EU legislative initiatives (e.g.  
Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive) which act to address the impact of global 
value chains. Introducing mandatory checks in the CRM Act therefore adds an incremental  
and unnecessary burden for firms. 
3. Additional financing (Art. 15) 
Northvolt strongly advocates for the provision of additional funds for strategic projects, both 
in Europe and in third countries, in support of the Act’s goals. This can be achieved through a 
dedicated European CRM Fund for strategic projects, outlined in Policy Option 3 in the impact 
assessment.  
4. Recycling (Art. 25) 
Northvolt supports the need to harmonize applicable waste management rules for certain 
waste streams. It is essential to avoid redundant reporting to ensure targets of CRM recycling 
can be met.  To achieve these, more digital solutions to streamline processes, in particular 
permitting, as well as harmonizing waste management frameworks between Member States 
are considered important levers to reduce barriers for recycling of CRMs.

Northvolt welcomes the idea of re-use as a positive and sustainable approach to prolong the 
lifecycle of CRM -containing products . It is crucial to ensure that only suitable products are 
utilized for such applications. In the case of batteries, there are different product groups with 
varying degrees of performance and characteristics, some of which may be better suited for 
re-use and others better for recycling. Therefore, re -use in the case of batteries should be 
based on the residual performance capability to avoid valuable CRMs resting as idle capacity 
in underutilized applications that could be put to better use via recycling in a new application.    
Recycling is essential to reduce dependence on primary raw materials and a reliable 
feedstock is needed for battery recycling to achieve this goal. It is thus important to promote 
responsible and informed decision-making when it comes to re-use and recycling, considering 
the product's quality, safety, and environmental impact.  
To reduce the reliance on primary raw materials, the circularity framework through material -
efficient recycling of CRMs  needs to be promoted. Circularity implies that valuable recycling 
resources, such as end -of-life batteries, black mass and battery manufacturing waste, must 
remain available for the European recycling and battery materials industry. This is of 
particular importance for black mass  containing nickel, manganese, cobalt and lithium . 
Recognizing the strategic importance of black mass for the European battery ecosystem and 
limiting its export will boost domestic recycling interest and reduce the need for primary raw 
materials. Northvolt therefore welcomes the announcement of the inclusion of waste codes 
for Lithium -ion batteries and intermediate waste streams ( “black masses”) under the 
European List of Waste by 2024 to ensure their proper recycling within the EU, as mentioned 
in Communication COM(2 023)165 on a secure and sustainable supply of critical raw 
materials. 
5. Updated list of critical and strategic raw materials (Annex I & II) 
Northvolt supports the Commission’s approach of identifying relevant raw materials via its list 
of critical and strategic raw materials. The addition of Manganese and Nickel , both of which 
are crucial to the battery industry, is especially welcomed. Fluorspar should also be added as 
a strategic raw material.  
However, for avoidance of doubt, removing the term “battery grade” is recommended. A wide 
variety of materials may be considered “battery grade”, depending on impurity remova l 
capabilities and manufacturing process . For the principal battery metals, a more pertinent 
definition could be : 1) Nickel metal, intermediates, and salts, 2) Manganese metal, 
intermediates, and salts, and 3) Lithium compounds, intermediates, and salts, and 4) graphite 
purified to 99.5% purity. 
6. Tariff elimination for imports of battery CRMs (new) 
EU customs policy should be adapted to support the EU supply of CRMs and Green Deal 
objectives. A tariff elimination on spent  (used) cells and batteries is welcome, to facilitate 
recycling and reduce dependence on primary raw materials through circular practices. 
Imports of battery CRMs ought to be granted tariff suspensions in full and for the normal

7. Capacity benchmarks (Art. 1)  
To ensure that domestically sourced material will be used further in Europe and not 
substantially exported and processed elsewhere, incentives for mined material to remain in 
the Union for further processing can be helpful  in safeguarding local processing operations 
when needed.  
8. EU Critical Raw Materials Board (Art. 34 & 35)  
Establishing a Critical Raw Materials Board t o oversee the governance and implementation 
of the CRM Act  is welcome.  For this dialogue  to remain responsive to market realities,  
however, it is crucial to include not only public sector and policy-making stakeholders, but also 
industry representatives from across the raw materials value chain, including (but not limited 
to) major miners, refiners, and end users (e.g. battery makers).  
9. Clarify rules on environmental footprint of CRMs (Art. 30)  
Rules for the calculation and verification of the environmental footprint of CRMs must be 
further clarified . The most widely referred to impact category  is Global Warming Potential 
(Carbon Footprint, measured in kg CO 2 equivalent). This metric should there fore be 
considered as one of the most important impact categories.  
As CRMs are the majority contributor to emissions for many of the sectors necessary for 
decarbonization, the sorting of CRMs into performance classes is insufficient for determining 
their actual environmental footprint . In addition to performance classes, specific 
environmental impacts should be disclosed, in order to allow users to take informed decisions.

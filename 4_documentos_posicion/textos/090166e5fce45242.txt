 
 
 
VTT reply to the EC consultation on the Net-Zero Industry Act  
  
 
 
1. The EU’s attention to clean technologies is very welcome. However proposal focuses almost 
entirely on the investment in manufacturing capacity and measures for a fast permitting 
processes.  Too little attention is paid to the need to ensure also the development, 
maturation and scale -up of the almost read y net -zero technologies as well as the next 
generation of critical technologies. Actions to boost research, development and innovation 
should be reinforced in the proposal, beyond the establishment of the regulatory sandboxes. 
The Green Deal Industrial Pla n should be supported by a dedicated Research, Development 
and Innovation Pillar supporting the industrial objectives as done in the EU Chips Act for the 
microelectronics sector.  The impact of RDI actions will play a key role and will also support 
the long-term competitiveness of net-zero industry and assure the supply of new technologies 
and new industrial sectors whose deployment will take place after 2030. 
 
2. Regulatory sandboxes are not stand-alone entities. The regulatory sandboxes must be linked 
with technology infrastructures, e.g. test beds, pilot lines, living labs , to allow for rapid 
experimentation and disruptive innovation . Strategic investments in technology 
infrastructures are needed to facilitate the scaling up of prototypes and market upta ke of 
innovative technology solution s. Research institutes are needed to help designing the 
regulatory sandboxes for the joint experimentation, in a controlled real -world environment, 
of the Innovative net-0 technologies and identify the gaps that would re quire coordinated 
investment in technology infrastructures, e.g.:  
 
 Polymer electrolyte membrane (PEM) electrolyser and production/deliveries of 
electrolysers systems   
 Increase the pilot environment to an industrially relevant size range of new energy 
storage solutions, (e.g. for solid state batteries, flow batteries, V2G.)  
 Recycling of batteries and new energy production technologies with efficient 
circulation of critical raw materials, especially lithium, and including traceability 
technologies.  
 Manufacturing of nuclear energy solutions for small and medium nuclear reactors 
(SMR)   
 Demonstration of technical carbon sinks: bio-CCUS demonstration in an industrial 
environment (utilising the mineralisation route) and DAC value chain pilot of the 
entire value chain.  
 Multifunctional environment for testing new grid technologies and enabling peer-
to-peer energy communities and activating individual customers for energy system 
flexibility  
 Demonstration of electrified solutions for carbon intensive process industry

3. The relevant local innovation ecosystem should support the competent auth ority in the 
design and development of regulatory sandbox specific plans. This would allow for better 
integration of the testing for the scaling up of prototypes with related regulations and other 
societal issues. 
 
4. Identification of technologies that contribute to achieving the net zero target should take into 
account all the technological developments rather than be based on pre -identified ones. In 
the current proposal t he net -zero technologies are narrowly defined to include the 
manufacture of equipment and components needed for energy production and transmission, 
not more broadly the areas or technologies that contribute to the net -zero target. T he 
technologies needed for reducing energy demand as well as manufacturing technologies for 
circularity and material processing should also be considered. The contribution from digital 
technologies is crucial to achieve the goals of the Net -0 projects and strategic net -0 
projects.  The Net-zero technologies, which are critic al along all the value chain should also 
include:  
 
 Circular net-0 capacity with added value by-products  
 Digital technologies for the optimization of manufacturing, for resource and energy 
efficiency, using AI data platforms, digital twins and industrial 5G.   
 Automation technologies, such as high-level control systems, to support 
manufacturing and process optimization   
 Clean tech for material processing and products’ re-use   
 Heat recovery in industrial processes and heat storage   
 Energy Efficiency in manufacturing processes and green electrified industrial 
processes.    
 Industrial side streams utilization for sustainable production fuels and chemicals   
 Cybersecurity and smart grid management (6G, AI)   
 
 
5. It is critical to accelerate the implementation  of circularity to increase the capacity  in the 
supply chain. This includes recycling, re -use and repairing of components products and 
manufacturing machinery. The increase of capacity cannot be based onl y on creating new 
manufacturing. It would be importa nt to clarify that circular technologies are considered 
within the defined “net-zero technologies ” (Article 3).  
 
6. “Carbon usage” essential to produce synthetic hydrocarbons, should be included in the Net-
0 strategic technologies jointly with Carbon capture and storage.  The demand for carbon 
usage is high, and important to the EU net -emissions targets. It is not well justified why, in 
this perspective, the carbon storage projects have so much weight in the proposal.  
 
7. In a similar way technologies for nuclear processes with minimal waste and small modular 
reactors (SMRs) should be included in the annex  for strategic technologies for consistency

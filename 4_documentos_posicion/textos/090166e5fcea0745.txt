CNGTC response to the proposal on the digitization of company law 
 
The Conseil national des greffiers des tribunaux de commerce (CNGTC) welcomes the European 
Commission's initiative to improve digital company law , and supports the objectives of modernizing 
and improving accessibility to company information. 
Created by law no. 90-1259 of December 31, 1990 (codified in article L.741-2 of the French Commercial 
Code), the CNGTC is a legal entity charged with defending  the collective interests of the commercial 
court clerk profession. It has four main missions: representation, control, information and training. 
Commercial court clerks, public and ministerial officers appointed by the Minister of Justice and full 
members of the court, serve companies and play an active role in modernizing commercial justice. 
One of the profession's core activities is the collection, control and distribution of certified and reliable 
information on companies and their managers. Registrars are responsible for keeping legal registers, in 
particular the Trade and Companies Register (RCS), in which over 6 million companies are registered, 
representing 80% of the French economy. 
The RCS ensures the widest possible dissemination of legal information on companies. It also provides 
reliable, controlled legal information (identity of directors, registered office, status, etc.), financial 
information (filing of annual accounts) and data on beneficial owners. 
Drawing on its expertise and the digital in novations implemented by commercial court clerks, the 
CNGTC would like to share its analysis of the Commission's proposal. This document is based on three 
main points: 
• Supporting the European Commission's ambitions 
• Points of vigilance requiring clarification 
• Problematic provisions. 
 
Broad support from the CNGTC for the European Commission's ambitions 
 
The CNGTC welcomes the Commission's proposals, in particular the reinforcement of the role of 
registrars in monitoring company incorporation, a sine qua non f or confidence, especially from a 
transnational perspective. 
Cross-border access to company information presupposes close cooperation between Member States' 
commercial registers, which is essential to the smooth operation of the single market. The legality

For the CNGTC, the interconnection of the insolvency register and the register of beneficiaries through

The CNGTC also welcomes the alignment of the rules in Article 14 (bis and ter) between corporations 
and partnerships, again with a view to greater transparency, regardless of economic structure. 
Article 15, entitled "Up -to-date registers", is central to the CNGTC, which supports its wording, in 
particular the obligation to update at least once a year, as a guarantee of the reliability of regist er 
information. 
Similarly, the CNGTC supports the wording of article 16 ter, which would provide a European model for 
the registration certificate. The Conseil National had put forward this proposal in its white paper 
published in 201.0. 
The CNGTC also con siders that article 16 quinquies on the exemption from legalization and similar 
formalities and point 18.6 are necessary measures in the cross -border approach, even if more details 
are needed on how they are to be implemented. 
The CNGTC welcomes the introduction of a standard multilingual EU digital power of attorney model 
(Article 16c). As the power of attorney is established and revoked in accordance with national legal and 
formal requirements, it does not raise any difficulties in its implementation. Alignment with work on e-
IDAS is also a positive development. 
Finally, the CNGTC would be in favor of greater accessibility for the disabled, to ensure that the 
digitization of company law is synonymous with equal opportunities for all. 
 
Points of vigilance requiring clarification 
 
While the smooth operation of the single market requires mutual trust in the verification systems of 
other member states, the CNGTC draws attention to the fact that the exemption from translation 
(article 16 septies) could raise technical difficulties in processing this information, and lead to excessive 
use of the safeguards in cases of reasonable doubt (article 16 sexies), making this exceptional 
mechanism the principle. 
On reading the provisions of the directi ve, the CNGTC would particularly like to share its thoughts on 
the issue of translation for assessing a deed. To ensure legality, registrars will be able to validate a 
document transmitted by another registrar in the European Union. However, the question o f 
understanding the document received in order to verify its veracity and authenticity may pose a 
problem. The proposed directive could be more explicit on translation obligations (article 13 point 12 
pages 36 and 37). Par ailleurs, à l’article 14 b, le CNGTC souhaiterait que la Commission apporte une

Problematic provisions 
 
The CNGTC shares the Commission's view that registers should be updated within 15 days (article 
15.2.a), to improve the interoperability of information systems. 
However, the deadline of 5 days stipulated in article 15.2.b is difficult to meet when the entire legality 
check is carried out by the clerk after the documents have been submitted, particularly in the case of 
complex acts requiring numerous checks. This timeframe is also problematic for jurisdictions where 
legislation requires the retention of paper documents and the digitization of non -standardized 
documents prior to legal control. In view of these difficulties, the CNGTC is proposing to extend this 
deadline for the processing of complex files. 
In the interests of legal consistency, the CNGTC also suggests deleting the reference to "acts" from 
article 19 bis point 2, as the notion of "act" mentioned in the article does not refer to any item on the 
scrolled list. For the sake of consistency, the same change is justified in article 28. 
Finally, in article 10, the CNGTC considers it appropriate to add that the registrar is the person who 
checks the company's incorporation, with the simple principle that the person who certifies the 
information must be the entity legally responsible for the reliability of the data. 
The CNGTC is at the disposal of the European institutions to pursue its reflections and share its 
expertise, in particular by presenting the digital innovations that the profession has been promoting 
for several decades. It calls on the co-legislators to adopt the directive swiftly.

Proposal for a Directive of the European Parliament and of the Council amending Directives 2009/102/EC 
and (EU) 2017/1132 as regards the expansion and improvement of the use of digital tools and processes 
in company law. 
The Italian Association of Young Lawyers (Aiga) welcomes the text of the Proposal for a Directive in 
question, which responds well to the need of the business world and legal professionals to quickly have 
complete, updated and easily understandable information on commercial entities opera ting in the single 
European market. 
 In this regard, satisfaction can only be expressed for the prediction: 
 - an interconnection and integration in the BRIS of the data resulting from the various national commercial 
registers (collected in the same BRIS - Business Registers Interconnection System), of the data taken from 
the register of beneficial owners referred to in the anti -money laundering directive (collected in the Boris - 
Beneficial Ownership Registers Interconnection System ) and data resulting fr om the various bankruptcy 
registers of the member states (collected in the IRI – Insolvency Register Interconnection), which will 
facilitate the retrieval of information on the solvency, reliability and entrepreneurial structure of a foreign 
commercial entity;  
- the "one time only" principle - according to which companies do not have to submit the same information 
several times to the business registers of the various Member States, incurring additional costs for this 
fulfillment or for related activities (e.g. translation or legalisation) – which constitutes a concrete 
implementation of the principle of freedom of establishment and removes some of the typical obstacles of 
setting up subsidiaries or branches abroad;  
- of specific disclosure obligations for  groups of companies, aimed at clarifying the control relationships 
existing between the companies of the group.  
Nonetheless, we believe that the proposed Directive should include:  
- among the information subject to the publicity obligation pursuant to art. 14 and 14 bis, the existence of a 
certified e-mail address compliant with the ETSI EN 319 532 -4 standard defined in June 2022: this in order 
to strengthen the interoperability of eDelivery at European level; 
- in article 14 ter (Information relating to  groups of companies), that the obligation to provide information 
about the parent company, even if non -European, applies to all intermediate companies and not just to 
one of them and that this obligation can be satisfied, possibly even by simply indicating an active link to the 
data of the parent company present in the BRIS: multinational companies often set up numerous 
companies of minimum size on the national territories of the individual Member States in order to evade 
tax or labor law regulations, with  this making it very difficult to identify the parent company, the effective 
structure of the group and the responsibilities falling respectively on each of the group companies; 
 - the right of registered companies to indicate in the national register (and  therefore in the BRIS) any ESG 
certifications obtained in compliance with the directive approved on 28 November last by the European 
Council;  
- a link to the EUIPO database, which indicates the trademarks and industrial property rights owned by the 
company;  
- to protect the consumer operating within the single European market, the obligation of responsible 
subjects pursuant to Directive 25 July 1985, n. 85/374/EEC to indicate their unique identification code of 
the registered company (EUID) on the labels of the products marketed.

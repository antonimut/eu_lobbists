The NZIA aims to improve the access to net-zero technologies and ramp up the manufacturing 
industry with the necessary equipment components. This will help to address one of the many 
challenges faced today when building electrolyzers for large-scale projects.  
 
But several challenges remain unsolved in the current proposed NZIA text and the NZIA is therefore 
not as ambitious as its name suggests.  
 
Already today, Yara produces 7% of the hydrogen in the EU. Based on this and on our long-term 
experience in hands-on development and execution of both electrolysis and CCS projects, through 
investments in equipment and their use that energy-intensive industries will be able to reach 
the desired emission reduction in Europe . Additionally, it also strongly depends on acce ss to 
affordable energy (requiring investment in generating capacity and transmission, thus bringing 
electricity costs down). 
 
The urgency to act is high because of the climate emergency. Europe also faces the risk of 
investment leakage, since investing in decarbonization requires a globally competitive industry.  
As rightly pointed out by the European Commission in the NZIA proposal, "many European 
sectors, in particular the energy -intensive industries such as fertilizers, steel and cement, 
have been sever ely impacted by the energy crisis" . While this is recognized in the text, no 
adequate answer is provided. In addition, the fertilizers sector was also heavily impacted by the 
disruption of its value chain due to the war in Ukraine. The current crisis highl ighted that Europe 
needs a strong and resilient domestic industry, whilst reducing GHG emissions and tackling the 
dependency on imported fossil fuels. It is key for the EU to ensure the competitiveness of its net -
zero industry in global markets, whilst  re aching its climate neutrality goal. That is the only way for 
the road to net-zero to be fair and successful. 
Yara International welcomes the Commission's proposal for a  
Net Zero Industry Act (NZIA).

1. Yara calls on the European policy makers to add the expansion of clean hydrogen production and 
import capacity to the hydrogen technology as an explicit goal of the NZIA . This will enable the 
development of the hydrogen market in the EU, in line with the ambition of REPowerEU 1, and to achieve 
the decarbonisation of hard-to-abate industry sectors, which is crucial to achieve both the 2030 targets and 
climate neutrality by 2050. The Manufacturing of clean hydrogen should be added as net -zero 
manufacturing in the Annex, and hydrogen projects should be eligible to become 'net-zero strategic 
projects'. This is necessary to ensure that these projects will benefit from streamlined permitting, 
recognition, priority status and facilitated access to financing.   
 
2. We strongly support a CO 2 injection capacity target . Conventional ammonia p roduction generates 
significant volumes of pure CO 2, which has several applications in different sectors (incl. the food and 
beverage industry, drinking water treatment as well as in securing unbroken cold chains in key sectors such 
as the health sector and the food chain). The remaining volumes of unused CO2 are well suited for injection. 
However, the storage sites are only slowly developing, and increased CO2 transport and injection capacity 
can significantly accelerate the decarbonization of the European fertilizer industry.   
 
3. To drive the industry’s transition, the NZIA should support the integration of net-zero technologies in 
industrial settings, fostering the use of alternative feedstocks (e.g. sustainable biomethane)  and 
renewable or low -carbon energy. To integrate net -zero technologies in existing industrial  installations, 
companies today need to bear heavy investments and significant administrative burdens (primarily due to 
permitting). To facilitate this transformation, we suggest adding a new category for ‘net zero technology 
integration projects’ in the text. This will  enable the necessary transformative change to happen at the right 
speed and scale.  In addition, Yara believes that the NZIA should create a business case for European 
industry players by providing infrastructure, supporting operating costs, and addressing structural 
competitive disadvantages such as high energy and feedstock costs. Investment into the entire supply 
chain and production process is needed to achieve the desired outcome of NZIA. Otherwise, the risk is that 
– because of high op erating costs – industry would simply move out of Europe, rather than abating 
emissions. This is why the scope of the NZIA proposal must be expanded.  
 
4. The NZIA create the conditions for a more ambitious deployment of government support schemes 
for technology integration and hydrogen infrastructure projects, while avoiding distortions of the EU 
Single Market. The Hydrogen Bank and the Temporary Crisis and Transition Framework on state aid(TCTF) 
are positive steps in this direction, but the European industry  needs more targeted support, through 
simplified access and minimal administrative burden. 
 
5. A data sharing approach is necessary to develop hydrogen production and import, in addition to 
transparency around CO2 storage capacity. This can help industrial operators to prepare for conversion 
to renewable hydrogen (such as for RFNBOs) and provides knowledge about infrastructure and investment 
conditions. It would also prompt Member States to take a holistic approach for the hydrogen infrastructure 
development.

International Council on Mining and Metals (ICMM) response to the public 
consultation on the EU’s Critical Raw Materials Act  
  
London, 17 June 2023  
 
The International Council on Mining and Metals (ICMM) welcomes the European Commission’s clear 
recognition and commitment to responsible mineral supplies in the Critical Raw Materials Act, and we 
welcome the opportunity to take part in this second public consultation on the proposed regulation. We 
submitted our initial views on the Commission’s consultation on critical minerals during the autumn of 2022, 
and our response from 9th November 2022 can be found here. ICMM’s comments are primarily concerned 
with permitting & licensing, certification schemes, governance, and environmental footprint declarations.  
 
  Strategic projects 
 
The regulation differentiates between critical and strategic raw materials, and the concept of strategic projects 
is linked to this differentiation. In particular, raw material is defined as strategic when it serves supply chains of 
strategic technologies, as mentioned in the Annex to the Net Zero Industry Act. However, mining projects are 
often multi-metallic and supply a number of different metals at once. If all extracted materials contribute to 
the production of such technologies, the entire project should be classified as strategic, independently from 
the classification of each raw material extracted. 
 
Permitting & licensing   
 
With regard to overall permitting and licensing, ICMM strongly welcomes the acknowledgement from the 
Commission that there are challenges in the way new mines are being licensed. Greater degrees of 
predictability, simplification and streamlining (where appropriate) and measures such as the proposed one-
stop shop, are welcomed. However, it is important that any changes to the permitting and licensing processes 
do not come at the expense of the quality of impact assessments, and community and stakeholder 
engagement.   
 
Certification schemes  
 
We welcome the Commission’s proposal for a certification scheme to ensure that mining projects adhere to 
high ESG standards. It is at the same time important to recognise that there has been a proliferation of 
responsible mining standards over the past decade, and that the resulting standards landscape has become 
increasingly complex to navigate and implement - a fact widely recognised by governments, supply chain, 
and the mining industry alike. Against that backdrop, we would strongly discourage the creation of further 
standards and would instead strongly encourage further engagement and collaboration with various existing 
standards owners to deliver improved ESG performance. We, therefore, welcome the Commission’s current 
approach to the proposed certification schemes. Establishing a broad set of boundary characteristics - as has 
been the case - that enables for a number of different standards to be chosen from in a non-prescriptive 
fashion, whilst also setting a robust standards ‘floor’, is a sensible approach.  
 
However, we note the challenges associated with the certification schemes part of the Conflict Minerals 
Regulation, where the failure to effectively establish a process of assessing the adequacy of potential 
certification threatens to undermine the efficiency of the Conflict Minerals Regulations. To avoid a similar 
situation with regard to the Critical Raw Materials Act, ICMM would strongly advocate that a deadline be set 
for decisions on applications submitted by the scheme owner. We believe that setting a 6-month period for 
scrutiny and engagement with the scheme owner would allow for a thorough assessment to determine 
compliance with the stated criteria of the certification schemes.

Consistency 
Given the critical raw materials' integral role in numerous industrial value chains, and their unique 
characteristics at various stages - from extraction and processing to recycling, the sector is already governed 
by a comprehensive set of legislation, programmes, and initiatives. These cover ESG considerations, due 
diligence, and environmental footprint, including the EU Battery Regulation, the forthcoming Corporate 
Sustainable Due Diligence Directive, the Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation, and the EU Product 
Environmental Footprint Programme. However, to ensure consistency and effectiveness, it is crucial to 
introduce an assessment of the impact of existing and upcoming EU regulations on the security of critical raw 
materials supplies. This should be integrated into the European Commission's impact assessment for new or 
revised legislation. This approach will align the requirements of the Critical Raw Materials Act with these 
aforementioned regulations and future legislation, thereby enhancing the security and sustainability of critical 
raw materials supply chains. 
 
 Governance  
 
With respect to the governance approach outlined under Chapter 7, we welcome the proposal to allow 
experts, other third parties and representatives from third countries to participate in the Board standing and 
temporary subgroups detailed under Article 35 clause 7.  The issues to be considered by the Board are 
complex and will benefit from the insights of experts familiar with operating conditions outside of the EU.  We 
note, however, that there is no reference to the industry’s involvement other than private financial institutions 
under Article 35 clause 6(a). We hope that some provision will be made to allow industry to contribute to the 
process, where appropriate. 
 
Environmental footprint declaration 
 
When it comes to addressing the impact of critical raw materials, we note the intent to develop environmental 
footprint criteria under Article 30 considering the elements detailed in Annex V.  Ensuring such requirements 
remain well aligned with on-going and published work and that every effort is made to avoid any inconsistent 
requirements between different frameworks is essential. We welcome the commitment under Annex V 
Section 2. Scope for the calculation rules to reflect the international agreements and technical and scientific 
progress in the area of life cycle assessment, noting that any changes to the calculation rules should be 
subject to an appropriate transition period to allow businesses to adapt to new compliance requirements.   
 
We call for efforts to be made to ensure that new requirements for environmental footprint declarations, 
including the calculation rules, are developed with input from actors across minerals value chains, including 
mining and metal producing companies, and from third countries stakeholders through their inclusion in the 
consultation process outlined under Recital 59, thus giving due consideration to the ability of third-country 
suppliers to comply with environmental declaration requirements. We note the intent to involve stakeholders 
in informing the impact assessments to be undertaken for critical raw materials as is deemed necessary and 
call for this approach to be extended to encompass the calculation rules development. 
 
Article 30 clause 1 also mentions that the environmental footprint methodology should identify the most 
important impact category to be declared when the raw material is put into the EU Market. Acknowledging 
that not all life cycle impact categories have the same robustness and scientific acceptance, relevant impact 
categories should be selected among those widely accepted by both industry and the scientific community 
that have been shown to be scientifically robust.

About ICMM   
 
ICMM is a leadership organisation that is working with its members and stakeholders across the world for a 
safe, just, and sustainable world, enabled by responsibly produced minerals and metals. We represent the 
commitment to sustainable development of 26 of the world’s largest mining and metals companies, together 
accounting for one-third of the global industry across 650 sites in 50 countries. We also count as Association 
members, 39 national or commodity Associations – each of whom independently manage their engagement 
and policies.  
 
The bedrock of ICMM's vision is our ten Mining Principles and related Performance Expectations, which 
define the good practice environmental, social and governance requirements of our company members. 
Implementation of the Mining Principles is a condition of membership and supports progress towards the 
global targets of the UN Sustainable Development Goals and the Paris Agreement on climate change. 
Incorporating robust site-level validation of performance expectations and credible assurance of corporate 
sustainability reports, ICMM’s Mining Principles seek to maximise the industry’s benefits to host communities, 
while minimising negative impacts to effectively manage issues of concern to society.

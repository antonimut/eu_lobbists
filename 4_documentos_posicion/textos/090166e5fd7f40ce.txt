Grenoble, June 23rd 2023 
Response to EC Consultation on the “European Critical Raw Materials Act” 
MagREEsource company 
MagREEsource is a young permanent magnet producer whose ambition is to structure a sovereign 
industrial sector, anchored in the circular economy, and wants to open a first European MagFactory 
with a capacity of 500 tons/year by 2027 as a first step. 
Today, Europe sources more than 95% of it s rare earth elements  from outside (mainly China). To 
guarantee its own supply and the strategic autonomy of European manufacturers, MagREEsource has 
developed an innovative and clean technology for recycling end -of-life magnets with hydrogen, in 
order to recover a magnet powder that can be directly reused for the manufacture of new magnets. 
MagREEsource wants to prove that Circularity and Technology will be a game changer to regain 
Sovereignty and Sustainability in Europe, and is supported for these reasons by the European Raw 
Materials Alliance (ERMA) and EIT Raw  Materials (EIT-RM). Thus, MagREEsource fully supports the 
European Commission proposal for a Critical Raw Materials Act, which specifically includes rare earths 
magnets. 
Comments on permanent magnets in CRM-Act 
Existing complexity for identifying and disassembling permanent magnets in subsystems  is today 
limiting their recycling.  
MagREEsource is welcoming the proposed regulation for labelling the permanent magnets  (Article 
27.1) and identifying their composition (Article 28.1):  
- The 4 categories of permanent magnets (Article 27.1) is well adapted for today market but 
may be enlarged  during the next decade if new permanent magnets com es into industrial 
phase (like iron-nitrides and high entropy alloys). 
- Access to magnet information (type and location) for motor vehicles is of importance, even 
for existing internal combustion engine vehicles, as they used a lot of magnets in small sized 
motors (pumps, seats, electric windows) and loudspeakers, which are today not sufficiently 
identified and thus recycled. 
- Access to permanent magnets compositions (Article 28.1) is important for recycling processes. 
These data should not be considered as confidential as magnets described in Article 27.1 are 
already used and imported for decades. Moreover, as grain -boundary-diffusion (GBD) 
permanent magnets have variable content of heavy rare earths (HRE) in their entire volume, 
it should be clearly specified (Article 28.4) th at the composition should be the chemical 
composition of the full magnet itself, and not only the first layers close to the surface which 
could be enriched in heavy rare earths (HRE). 
- MagREEsource is proposing to decrease the total weight threshold for publicly available 
information on magnets down to 0.1kg instead of 0.2kg (Article 28.1). This measure will allow 
to identify and recycle additional permanent magnets, which are installed for example in some 
automotive alternators, e-scooter or fridge/freezer/washing-machine motors/compressors.

MagREEsource is also welcoming the unique product identifier that provides information for access 
and safe removal of all permanent’s magnets incorporated in the products (Article 27.4 and 28.5):   
- However, for electrical vehicle, permanents magnets of the traction motors are often glued or 
tightened in complex assemblies. Recovering t he magnets is complex and costly  or even 
impossible. It could be suggested to include a system of bonus for OEM which have designed 
their motors for being easily repaired and recycled.  
- Moreover, having access to the exact locations of the magnet is crucial for increasing recycling 
rates, and having only access to the removal steps of the electric motors (Article 28.5) will 
certainly limit their recycling. This article may be reinforced by the proposal above and include 
also include full procedure for magnets removal.  
To secure access to European permanent magnets production, CRM-Act is setting recycled content of 
permanent magnets (Article 28). MagREEsource fully support this position, which could be reinforced 
by:  
- Reducing the timeline for the implementing act rela ted to minimum shares of elements in 
permanent magnets (Article 28.3  and 28.6 ). Targeting 2027-2028 instead of 2032 for this 
ambitious objective will certainly speed up the reindustrialization of magnet industry in 
Europe. 
- Setting European incentives to use permanent magnets with recycled content, for instance an 
economical bonus for manufacturer or customer. Such incentive could also rely on product 
carbon footprint (based on LCA) but in that case, a clear framework should be established, 
with a strong attention to avoid dumping product using “Energy Attribute Certificate”. 
- Setting a stronger framework for end -of-life waste  (Article 25) to avoid that permanent 
magnets are going back outside Europe a fter being utilized. This is the case in some offshore 
wind contracts today for instance, and there is a strong risk to not reach European targets on 
resilience. 
- Increasing of 5 years the timeframe of this regulation (Article 28.11) for motor vehicles, 
resonance imaging devices and light means of transport is unjustified. Permanent magnets are 
used in these applications for a long time and in the case of motor vehi cles, should not need 
new vehicle development or to wait for a new approval type. 
- The establishment of rules for the calculation and verification of recycled content should not 
include the scraps produced during magnet processing  operation ( from rare -earth 
concentrate to magnet manufacturing), as state-of-the-art is already to recycle them.  
Finally, increasing European research on low (or no) rare -earths magnets and high -performance 
electrical motors is also needed, as well as increasing skills and training in mag netic materials and 
systems.

Conclusion for the CRM-Act consultation 
MagREEsource supports the proposed with a focus on 4 points of importance: 
1. For short term, recycling and circular economy are the only way to build a sovereign supply 
chain in Europe - If resources are still there! We must do whatever is possible to keep our 
magnet scraps available within European Union boundaries. It must be forbidden to sale and 
ship scraps and any RE materials outside Europe. 
2. Revising unfair trade policy is crucial. China is today taking advantage of a VAT (13 to 17%) on 
rare earth semi products to provide unfair advantage of its magnet manufacturing industry. 
A carbon tax of the same level would balance this unfair trade position. 
3. Strengthening EU critical raw materials value chain and revealing a sustainable level playing 
field across the Single Market means a real financing engagement at very high levels (IPCEI is 
needed and any kind of investments procedures in between member states and Europe). 
Private financing only will not help. Against IRA financing scheme that subsidies their made-
in-America supply chain up to 50% of the total costs, Europe should develop a made-in-
Europe regulations and finance it. 
4. Standards and certification for the sustainable and ethical production of raw materials must 
also be a tool to protect fair business and our European market.

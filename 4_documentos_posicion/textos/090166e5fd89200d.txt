2 June 2023 
ePURE feedback to the Commission consultation on 
Net-Zero Industry Act (NZIA) 
ePURE, representing the EU producers of sustainable ethanol from crops, waste, and residues  and accounting for 
85% of the EU renewable ethanol production would like input into the Commission consultation on the Net-Zero 
Industry Act (NZIA) proposal, specifically highlighting several points: 
• The lack of ambition level compared to the newly enacted Inflation Reduction Act (IRA). The USA is a major 
industrial competitor. While the IRA establishes many incentives (including tax credit programs for hydrogen, 
biofuels, and aviation fuel ) to accelerate the production and use of clean fuels and further defossilize the 
transport sector  without discriminating against any type of biomass used , EU renewable fuels which  
outperform its competitors in terms of GHG emissions savings, are not even in the scope of the NZIA proposal 
and not considered as strategic sectors . To achieve our climate goals without penalizing European industries 
all sustainable renewable energy sources should be considered as contributing to energy independence and 
net-zero emissions ambitions. 
• An unfair d iscrimination against EU renewable crop -based and advance d biofuels. The proposal fails to 
acknowledge the significant scientifically proven contribution of  crop-based and advanced biofuels to the 
swift decarbonization of the transport sector when it should instead be promoting their use. Sustainable crop-
based biofuels, such as renewable ethanol, are today the largest and cheapest decarbonization tool in the EU 
to immediately reduce the EU’s reliance on fossil fuels. EU renewable ethanol produced by ePURE members 
achieved 77% GHG emissions savings on average compared to fossil  petrol. Crop-based biofuels delivering 
such a GHG emission saving must be included in the definition of net -zero technologies. Advanced biofuels, 
which are encompassed in the NZIA definition should benefit from the recognition and advantages of strategic 
net-zero technologies. Indeed, it is unacceptable that certain technologies such as sustainable 
biogas/biomethane are recognized wh ile sustainable biofuels subject to strict sustainability criteria and 
proven GHG emission savings performance are not.  
• A lack of clear criteria to justify this discrimination . The European Commission’s methodology for selecting 
sectors and technologies t hat should be recognized as net -zero or strategic net -zero lacks clarity and 
transparency and should be based on track record of GHG emissions savings  and carbon capture and 
replacement, regardless of the sector and be examined as part of an in-depth impact assessment.  
• A lack of long-term and consistent vision. Combined with a blatant lack of transparent and consistent rules, 
the lack of a coherent long-term visibility generates mistrust in EU policy and discourages needed investment 
in the EU. 
ePURE’s recommendations 
• Any proven sustainable alternative to fossil CO 2, such as sustainable renewable crop-based biofuels, must be 
included in the list of net -zero technologies. EU ethanol already reduces GHG emissions by 77% on average, 
and with a consistent long -term and dogma -free policy framework fostering investments, this p erformance 
can grow further.  
• To include advanced biofuels (Annex IX-A of the RED) in the Annex of the NZIA proposal.

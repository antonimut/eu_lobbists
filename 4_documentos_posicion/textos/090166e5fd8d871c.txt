Input to the public consultation on the European Commission proposal for the Critical 
Raw Materials act. 
We refer to the European Commission’s consultation on the Critical Raw Materials Act 
(CRMA) and appreciate the opportunity to give our comments.  
About Naturvernforbundet: Our NGO was founded in 1914 and is Norway's oldest 
environmental and nature conservation organization. We are democratic and membership 
based and most of our work are done by volunteers, organized in over 100 groups all around 
the country. With 39,000 members, Naturvernforbundet plays a significant role as civil 
society both locally and nationally in Norway, and we are internationally part of Friends of 
the Earth.  
General comments:  
Naturvernforbundet recognizes the need for raw materials to support the green and digital 
transition of Europe. We welcome a stronger prioritization of societally beneficial mineral 
projects and circular economy activities to increase the supply of CRMs. This prioritization 
would be more effective with a stronger emphasis on sufficiency and reducing demand as 
well as clear mechanisms for discarding projects not deemed critical or necessary at an early 
stage. It is imperative for the legitimacy of the green transition that democratic principles, 
civil society involvement and public participation as well as strong environmental and social 
safeguards, including the rights of indigenous people, are upheld, and strengthened in the 
regulation.  
Summary of Naturvernforbundets main comments: 
• There is a need for a stronger emphasis on sufficiency and demand reduction.

• We propose separate tailored targets for the collection and recycling of each critical 
mineral. 
• We ask the European commission to secure and strengthen environmental and social 
safeguards and local communities and indigenous peoples right to give or withhold 
their Free, Prior and Informed Consent. 
• We would like to emphasize that prioritizing strategic and critical projects to supply 
the green transition should also lead to closing the door for non-strategic and non-
critical projects with little benefit to society and low relevance for the green transition. 
Reducing demand: 
Reducing demand for and consumption of raw materials in the EU is missing from the 
regulation. Connected to the target for sourcing of recycled material, there should be a 
specific target promoting sufficiency and limiting the demand of raw materials overall. In the 
same way that member states are required to draw up national programs for exploration and 
increased recycling, member states should also be required to draw up national programs for 
decreasing demand and consumption of raw materials. Such programs could involve national 
plans for energy sufficiency, plans for prioritizing public transport over private cars and 
reducing energy consumption in both private, state, and commercial sectors.  
Recycling targets and circular economy: 
Naturvernforbundet are pleased that there are targets for increasing the collection and 
recycling of critical raw materials in the regulation. The market situations and value chains 
are different for each mineral. In order to not water down ambitions and to drive progress 
towards a circular economy, the regulation should include separate targets for the collection 
and recycling of each mineral. 
Fast-tracking permits and environmental and social safeguards:  
Naturvernforbundet are concerned that the proposed speeding up of the permit granting 
process will come at the cost of social and environmental safeguards and democratic 
principles.  
Faster granting processes will demand more in terms of administration and enforcement costs 
from local and national authorities. A demand for faster processes at an EU-level should also

A precondition for fast-track programs are high quality environmental, impact assessments at 
the earliest possible stage of the permit granting process.  This can exclude unsustainable 
projects and avoid lock-in effects from projects based on extraction and waste management 
methods with unacceptable costs to the environment. Mechanisms for refusing 
environmentally unsustainable projects at an early stage would also lessen administrative 
burdens for local and national authorities and negative social impacts for affected 
communities. Social and human rights impact assessments should be mandatory for all 
projects.  
Early involvement and effective mechanisms for meaningful participation from civil society 
and affected communities are a precondition for democratic and good processes. All projects, 
strategic or not, must respect indigenous communities and their right to give or withhold their 
Free, Prior, and Informed consent. Shortcuts often comes at the cost of trust, experiences from 
mining projects in Norway show that shortcuts and insufficient impact assessments lead to 
time-consuming and difficult processes having the opposite effect than intended. Clear 
legislation with strong environmental and social safeguards creates predictability for all actors 
and is a necessity for faster granting processes. We would propose a duty for permit 
applicants to consult with local and regional authorities, civil society and to involve local 
communities, as well as producing independent impact assessments before starting the 
permitting process. This would save applicants and authorities time and resources and could 
also reduce the risk of unnecessary conflicts in local communities.  
Advisory Board: 
Naturvernforbundet are concerned about the lack of involvement of civil society in the 
proposed advisory board for critical raw materials. To ensure democratic and transparent 
processes representatives from civil society should be consulted and involved in decision 
making. The same strict demands for stakeholder involvement and public participation as in 
the The Water Framework Directive (2000/60/EC), should also be applied for this act.

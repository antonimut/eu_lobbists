 
 
Naftogaz of Ukraine position 
on the EC proposal of the Regulation establishing a framework of measures for strengthening Europe’s 
net-zero technology products manufacturing ecosystem (Net Zero Industry Act). 
 
Naftogaz of Ukraine welcomes the EC Net Zero Industry Act proposal and would like to contribute to the 
consultation process. 
 
Naftogaz’ interest in contributing to the consultation 
Naftogaz Group is the largest Ukrainian national energy company, with current assets mainly in gas and oil 
production, refining, and gas storage. As such, Naftogaz Group enterprises could make a material 
contribution to the goals of the NZIA, provided a conducive regulatory environment is created. 
As a country with almost a century-long fossil energy production history, Ukraine has many depleted fields 
potentially suitable for new applications, including geological CO2 storage. About 300 mln cm of potential 
storage capacities have already been preliminary researched by Naftogaz Group enterprises for permanent 
and safe CO2 storage, 90% whereof located close to the Western border. While a precise assessment of 
the CO2 amount to be potentially stored in the mapped fields requires further research, a pre -feasibility 
study based on the geological structure of the formation gives an estimation of up to 180 Mt of CO2 potential 
in total. Further research for more sites would require significant regulatory and financial incentives.  
The Group is also active in the research of the  captured carbon utilization in the industrial processes to 
replace Ukraine’s dependence on feedstock and fuels previously imported from Russia and Belarus.   
  
Reasons to include Ukraine into the NZIA framework 
CO2 storage and transportation do not have a strong business case in Europe yet. It is a nascent market. 
The EU should then stimulate both the demand and supply side to launch this new market. 
Allowing the obligated parties under the NZIA to use CO2 storage capacities located not only on the territory 
of the EU member states, but also in the EEA and EU candidate countries will help create the business 
cases for CO2 transportation and storage supply and demand. It is reasonable to include the capacities in 
such countries (Ukraine included) in th e mapping already today, due to geographical proximity, geology, 
existing in some countries specific expertise, but also given the availability of existing  gas transport 
infrastructure potentially suitable for CO2 transportation, which otherwise would be another additional cost 
for the future market players to consider.  
Furthermore, Naftogaz believes that rebuilding post-war Ukraine and transforming European industry into a 
net-zero one is a part of the same process towards economically strong Europe, wi th new possible value 
and supply chains, located strategically on the European continent. 
Ukraine is a future EU member state. Full integration into the EU single market will be part of the upcoming 
accession process. The country is already part of the EU internal gas market. Making Ukraine part of the

nascent CO2 storage market will create incentives for the European industrial sectors to consider projects 
in Ukraine under the umbrella of the NZIA. It will give an impetus to regulatory alignment in Ukraine to allow 
for the repurposing of sites and provide the Ukrainian oil&gas and extractive industries with an incentive to 
stir relevant feasibility studies for new CO2 storage sites. Given that it takes at least 5 years to repurpose 
the sites, only by including Ukraine into NZIA today can we potentially be ready for 2030 with the first sites. 
The potential of CO2 storage in Ukraine will not be met by the corresponding demand from the Ukrainian 
domestic companies any time soon, also considering the war destruction of heavy industry in Ukraine and 
current low carbon price. Before 2030, development of such services in Ukraine will likely be conditional to 
the demand from the EU countries, where the CO2 price is substantially higher. European companies 
already expressed interest in CO2 storage in Ukraine and approached Naftogaz on the issue before the full-
scale invasion. Allowing the Ukrainian CO2 storages to account for the EU annual 50 Mt goal by 2030 will 
drive their supply from Ukraine and create first cases to scale-up afterwards, during the reconstruction 
period.  
Additionally, including Ukraine in the NZIA framework would also allow for new synergies with the EU -
Ukraine Strategic Partnership on Critical Raw Materials (2021) and the EU Critical Raw Materials Act 
proposal (2023). 
Naftogaz, therefore, proposes as follows: 
 CO2 storage sites 
● Enlarging the geographical scope under the Article 16 of the NZIA to the EEA and the EU candidate 
countries (including Ukraine), to allow the EU member states and entities subject to individual 
contribution to the CO2 storage target to develop CO2 storage sites in Ukraine as eligible to account 
for their target/contribution (without increasing the current target of overall 50 Mt per year annually); 
● Allow the EU entities who would develop CO2 sites in Ukraine, to be supported by the EU and/or EU 
member states funds under the umbrella of NZIA; 
● Given the current costs of research and repurposing of sites into CO2 storages, Naftogaz believes 
that further action is needed on the demand side for the CO2 storage ( for example, a fair burden -
sharing mechanism with hard-to-decarbonize sectors). 
 
      Other strategic technologies  
● Naftogaz believes that using the captured CO2 , provided it is permanently bound in the material, 
should be seen as an alternative to its storage. For instance, this could be utilization of captured CO2 
for producing chemicals and building materials. 
Hence, the list of strategic technologies referred to in the Annex to NZIA needs to be extended also carbon 
capture and utilization technologies to drive research for new applications and abatement of costs.

Position Paper Brussels, 08.06.2023 High Efficiency Cogeneration: A key European Net-zero Technology COGEN Europe recommendations for the Net Zero Industry Act COGEN Europe welcomes the a Regulation on establishing a -zero technology products manufacturing ecosystem (Net Zero Industry Act or NZIA). In this context, COGEN Europe calls for the Net Zero Industry Act to take a comprehensive and whole value chain approach to safeguard and grow European industrial leadership in net-zero technologies, supporting growth opportunities for all relevant energy efficient and renewable energy solutions across integrated energy systems. A range of EU-made cogeneration technologies already deliver higher energy efficiency, while enabling a more resilient, affordable and locally integrated and renewables-driven energy system for European consumers, industry, and cities. To safeguard and scale up manufacturing capabilities and industrial value chains for cogeneration in Europe, it is critical that the Net Zero Industry Act explicitly recognises cogeneration as a strategic net-zero technology.   1. Recognise energy efficiency solutions with an already strong European manufacturing base, such as cogeneration and district heating, to ensure future growth opportunities and retain the sectors in Europe.   Cogeneration and district heating have a strong manufacturing base in Europe, with more than 50% of supply being covered by EU-made technology.   Innovative cogeneration technologies are being developed in Europe, including cogeneration that is increasingly flexible, H2-ready, based on fuel cells and/or running on waste heatus climate and energy policies.   Cogeneration and efficient district heating are increasingly used as part of integrated energy systems in combination with heat pumps, PV and wind, either at district level, in industry or buildings. The deployment of these solutions together ensures higher energy efficiency, increased uptake of renewable energy across multiple energy vectors and lower energy cost for consumers.    2. Acknowledge the role of all renewable and clean energy sources contributing to net-zero emissions by 2050, including biomass, waste heat and sustainable municipal waste  The EU must rapidly scale up clean energy uptake, while ensuring consumers have access to secure and affordable energy. To achieve this, a diversified mix of renewable heat and electricity sources is needed to meet the diverse needs of industry, buildings and cities.

Promote net-zero technologies across entire net-zero value chains, from manufacturing to distribution and their end-use  Industries, SMEs, districts and domestic consumers adopting one or more net-zero technologies can significantly drive an increase in manufacturing capacity in the EU.  Stimulating demand via one stop shops, simplified permitting or higher priority and visibility for large scale projects, is key to ensure EU industrial competitiveness in the field of net-zero solutions.   4. Support flagship projects that showcase synergies of net-zero technologies as part of integrated energy systems at local level   The future of clean energy lies in combining net-zero technologies at local level or on-site to deliver higher energy efficiency, increased resiliency and lower cost for consumers.  About cogeneration  A diverse range of cogeneration technologies is produced in Europe, from the kW to the MW, for industrial, district heating and residential applications.   More than 50% of cogeneration deployed in Europe is produced by EU manufacturers and installed by highly specialized installers across well-established distribution channels.  The cogeneration sector creates more than 100.000 jobs across Europe.  Cogeneration produces today 12% of the total electricity and 16% of the heat in the EU. More than 60% of the heat supplied through district heating comes from local cogeneration plants. This ensures significant energy savings (>30 bcm) and CO2 emission reductions (>150 million tons of CO2 reduced yearly).   Renewable cogeneration is scaling up, with renewable shares in the CHP mix increasing 3 times between 2009-2019.  Manufacturing capacities for innovative cogeneration systems are increasing across Europe. Fuel cell micro-CHP production capacity increased by 500% between 2016 and 2023. The industry is significantly investing in hydrogen-readiness, as well as improving efficiency and flexibility capabilities of CHP systems.

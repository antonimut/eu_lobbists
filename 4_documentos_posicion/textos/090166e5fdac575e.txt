 
 
 
Net Zero Industry Act (NZIA) Public Consultation – Urenco Submission  
ABOUT URENCO 
1. Urenco is an international supplier of enrichment services and fuel cycle products with 
sustainability at the core of its business. Operating in a pivotal area of the nuclear fuel 
supply chain for 50 years, Urenco facilitates zero carbon electricity generation for 
consumers around the world. Urenco is a global asset for the EU.  
 
2. Urenco’s global presence ensures diversity and security of supply for customers through 
enrichment facilities in Germany, the Netherlands, the UK and the USA. Urenco is 
committed to continued investment in the responsible management of nuclear materials; 
innovation activities with clear sustainability benefits, such as nuclear medicine, industrial 
efficiency and research; and nurturing the next generation of scientists and engineers. 
SUBMISSION 
Recognising nuclear as a strategic technology  
3. Nuclear is a key affordable, stable, and flexible energy source. Europe cannot afford to 
exclude any proven energy source for European energy security, strategic autonomy, and 
climate transition goals. By failing to fully embrace nuclear energy, the transition away from 
fossil fuels will take longer, will cost more, and will negatively impact EU competitiveness. 
More and more countries in the EU, and globally, are recognising the positive role nuclear 
energy can have  alongside other emission -free energy sou rces. It is a proven strategic 
technology and reduces EU energy dependence on other countries. Nuclear accounts for 
the greatest share of the EU’s electricity mix (currently around 2 6%1 of all electricity 
produced and 50% of low carbon electricity 2), and the European nuclear sector is 
composed of a highly skilled manufacturing capacity based in the EU.  
 
4. It is concerning that despite nuclear being included in the Complementary Delegated Act 
for the EU Taxonomy, the Net Zero Industry Act (NZIA) is ambiguous in its support for 
nuclear. The formal definition of Net Zero technologies in the legislative text includes "… 
advanced technologies to produce energy from nuclear processes with minimal waste 
from the fuel cycle, small modular reactors, and related best-in-class fuels…", yet nuclear 
energy is not included in the list of “strategic net-zero technologies” — detailed in the annex 
to the legal text — that can benefit from faster permitting and easier access to funding.  
 
5. Urenco recognises the value of  including small modular reactors (SMRs) and advanced 
nuclear technologies that will make a significant contribution to the decarbonisation of the 
European industrial landscape. However, we are concerned that the whole nuclear sector 
has not been included as a strategic Net Zero technology. Most of the first generation of 
SMR’s will operate on existing technology. By excluding existing technology, innovation 
will be harder. If innovation is stifled, the next generation of SMR’s will not come from the 
EU and it will fall behind on other countries where this innovation is clearly stimulated.

Urenco calls on the EU Commission to recognise the whole nuclear sector as key 
strategic technology that accelerates EU decarbonisation, enhances industrial 
competitiveness and supports security of energy 
 
6. The NZIA will have a critical role in establishing the framework for the EU’s future industrial 
strategy and the degree of EU strategic autonomy in terms of key technologies needed for 
the energy transition. As such, given nuclear’s role in enhancing EU and global energy 
security and strategic autonomy , and the complementary role that large -scale nuclear 
plays alongside small and advanced nuclear and other low-carbon sources, it is essential 
that the NZIA clearly recognises the  whole nuclear energy sector as a key strategic 
technology that contributes to enabling European decarbonisation, industrial 
competitiveness, and security of energy supply. 
 
7. In doing so, this will ensure that the European nuclear energy sector is on a level playing 
field with other regions such as the United States which has incorporated its entire nuclear 
energy industry under the Inflation Reduction Act  (IRA).  
The IRA contains large tax credits, funds for Advanced Fuels and supports research and 
innovation.  
 
8. The NZIA as currently drafted is a significant missed opportunity for the EU to establish 
itself as a key market for nuclear technology development.  By supporting the European 
nuclear sector through the NZIA, the EU will remain it’s key player s in the global 
competition for clean technologies.

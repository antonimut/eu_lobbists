Renault Group position 
• We welcome the CRMA as a first step to secure a sustainable EU value chain by 2030.  
• We call for a clear ambition and adequate EU Industrial Plan  to be among the world 
leaders on green technologies. 
• We ask for a level playing field among competitors by refusing unfair trading  practices, 
as OEM are investing billions of euros exploring new areas of know-how.  
 
I. European policies coherence and competitiveness 
→ Objective: decrease complexity and delays.  
 
1. Ensure coherence between legislations and ambitions . There is a multiplicity of 
correlated regulations that creates uncertainty and investment delays  whereas risks 
identification remains unshared by non-EU countries. E.g., Battery Regulation, End-Life-
Vehicle Directive, REACH revision, Waste Shipments Regulation.  
2. Define a European raw materials funding strategy in complement to the Temporary  
Crisis and Transition Framework . We regret the apparent absence of dedicated EU -
level financial mechanism  for supporting projects in the Commission proposal. [Art. 15]. 
a. Set clear guidelines to orientate key stakeholders towards earmarked funds.  
b. Couple EU public funding and investment support as well as de-risking financing 
tools for strategic projects with the other measures.    
c. Tackle the issue of energy costs, key pilar of competitiveness.  
 
II. Supply risk coverage 
→  Objective: reduce risk management of CRM contracts for the downstream level  
 
 
3. Support strategic supply investments and demand stimulus . As Intelligence gathering 
and sharing is key, we support the role of agencies such as OFREMI  (French 
observatory of mineral resources for industrial sectors ) and we call for rationality and 
consistency between the new requirements.  
a. Assess the relevancy of EU  strategic stockpiling mechanisms  (e.g., silicon, chips) 
as an option to address short -term supply disruptions. We remain cautious 
about modalities of direct private financial participation. It should be managed 
at EU level and avoid artificial scarcity that would impact operational costs. [Art. 
21-24] 
b. Test joint purchasing for some raw materials , as a relevant option in crisis 
situations on areas of limited competitiveness for the whole industry. 
c. Create a dedicated public EU -level financial to de -risk long -term commitment 
of Companies  engaged through volumes’ contract s (5-10 years) to comply with 
the EU decarbonization objectives.

d. Create a de-risk procedure to offset the financial exposure of off -balance sheet 
exposure for battery capacity and CRM.  
 
III. – Circular economy and EU projects support 
→ Objective: control raw materials flow as a competitive asset  
4. Boost recycling capacities and keep raw materials’ waste under control .  
a. Increase waste collection  of European materials through cross -sectorial 
measures (Electric Vehicles batteries, laptops, energy storage systems, etc.). 
b. Ease shipment of end -of-life batteries/black mass intra -EU: specifying 
harmonized waste codes for spent L i-ion batteries and intermediate waste 
streams (like black mass) and designing a fast -track validation procedure for 
intra-EU shipment. 
c. Control strategic exports to non -EU countries  through the action on wastes [Art. 
25].  
5. Define a realistic approach and pragmatic targets. Focusing solely on closing material 
loops cannot be the solution because recycled materials will not be able to meet the 
demand for raw materials during the market ramp-up of electromobility. 
a. Avoid unneces sary restriction of recycled materials’ sources  by limiting them to 
post-consumer waste.  
b. Rethink mandatory recycled content targets  as they may impact repair and 
second-life applications, affecting the potential of a circular economy model. 
c. Establish a fair and transparent calculation methodology  of recycled content 
and monitor progress towards reaching the benchmarks. 
6. Clarify the specific regulatory framework needed for permanent magnets. For 
purposes , we ask for a specific delegated act within the future End-of- simplification Life 
Vehicle regulation  which should be reviewed by the end of this year  (approval 
specification to be included in the EU WVTA  - Whole Vehicle Type approval ). The 
objective would be  to specify requirements for permanent magnets [Art. 27/28  - 
recycled content]: 
a. Types considered (our recommendation: all types 5 years after new types) 
b. Usage of a digital passport imposing information sharing from the suppliers 
 
IV. Environmental, Social & Governance rules to be clarified 
→ Objective: develop reliable & available data system throughout the value chain.  
 
 
7. Ensure relevancy of methodology for the environmental footprint calculation of 
Critical Raw Materials ( CRMs). [Art. 30]  
8. Ensure robust certification for responsible sources’ CRMs. [Art. 29] 
a. Implement a transversal ‘digital passport’ to ‘Track / trace’ and check relevancy.  
b. Accept the existing industry standards for responsible sourcing (e.g. , IRMA 
industry mining standard)  
9. Check the feasibility of taxonomy criteria for mining & refining activities . We alert on 
the complexity of the taxonomy regulations that may have a counterproductive effect.

 Facts & definitions 
• Raw Materials prices needed for the transition have an impact from Internal 
Combustion Engine (ICE) to Battery Electric Vehicles (BEV) in 2020 = +1.789 kg and 
+1.870 €.   
• Black-mass: Black-mass is the intermediate waste stream, containing valuable 
metals such as lithium, cobalt and nickel. 
• EU WVTA: Technical harmonisation in the EU is based on the Whole Vehicle Type-
Approval System (WVTA). Under the WVTA, a manufacturer can obtain certification 
for a vehicle type in one EU country and market it EU-wide without further tests. The 
certification is issued by a type-approval authority and the tests are carried out by the 
designated technical services.

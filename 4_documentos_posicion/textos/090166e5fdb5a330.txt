 
Commission proposal establishing a framework of measures for 
strengthening Europe’s net-zero technology products manufacturing 
ecosystem  
AEGIS Europe position  
 
Introduction  
This document sets out AEGIS Europe’s views on the Commission’s Proposal for a Regulation 
of the European Parliament and of the Council on establishing a framework of measures for 
strengthening Europe’s net -zero technology products manufacturing ecosystem  (NZIA) 
published on 16 March 2023. The EU will need to strengthen its net zero industry technologies 
in order to achieve the  twin transition goals while preserving Europe’s strategic autonomy . 
The European industry cannot be bluntly divided into two categories: (i) net-zero industrial 
sectors and (ii) the rest since the entire industrial ecosystem is closely interlinked and the 
various sectors depend on each other.  
The EU industrial sectors grouped within AEGIS Europe represent the entire value chain 
ranging from commodities  such as ferro -alloys, non -ferrous metals or ceramics  to end 
products like railway equipment, bicycles or solar panels. Our members account for an annual 
turnover of €530 – €550 billion and 1.65 – 1.7 million of jobs across the EU. 
Bearing in mind the initiatives taken by the US (i.e., Inflation Reduction Act), Japan (i.e., Green 
Transformation Programme) and China (i.e., latest Year Plan), the AEGIS Europe membership 
acknowledges the importance of the Critical Raw Materials Act and the Net-Zero Industry Act. 
Before delving into the technicalities of the NZIA, AEGIS Europe would like to point out that 
the NZIA together with the Critical Raw Materials Act are essential for the EU industry to 
achieve the objectives of the European Green Deal and REPowerEU. However, the EU 
proposals need further improvement and must be fully complementary with one another in 
order to be able to really support the European industry in their pledge to decarbonise and 
do away with third country dependencies. Moreover, the goal of an EU industrial policy must 
be to make Europe more internationally competitive for companies by lowering operating 
expenditures and securing resilient supply chains for strategic applications in Eur ope (e.g. 
automotive, aerospace, hygiene, medical, military, construction, agriculture etc ). Thus, the 
Net Zero Industry Act and a Critical Raw Materials Act alone are not enough. 
Nevertheless, echoing commissioner Thierry Breton’s statement, these two acts could 
represent the bedrock of the re -industrialisation of Europe but in order to achieve such 
ambitious goals much more is needed.

For the successful implementation of the NZI legislation, we have outlined four key 
recommendations: 
1. Enhanced resources and support for energy intensive industries 
The Energy-Intensive Industries ecosystem covers a broad range of sectors such as chemicals, 
steel, paper, plastics, mining, extraction and quarrying, refineries, cement, wood, rubber, 
nonferrous metals, man-made fibres, glass, ceramics and fertilizers. The sectors included in 
the ecosystem are characterised by a high energy and carbon intensity and by being at the 
starting point of many value chains, providing raw, processed and intermediate materials, and 
ensuring EU strategic  autonomy. The industries of the EII ecosystem employ millions of  
people in Europe and provide significant added value.  
The EU economic resilience and transition toward climate neutrality rely on Europe’s 
industrial base and its competitiveness . The adoption of measures aimed at ensuring better 
framework conditions to strengthen the competitiveness of the EU industry  are therefore 
paramount. The European producers are suffering an unprecedented pressure deriving from 
the current geopolitical tensions and the increase in energy costs, while still recovering from 
the impact of COVID -19 pandemic. The energy crisis further opens the door to competitors 
from regions with lower energy costs and non-equivalent environmental/climate regulations, 
resulting in a surge of imports into Europe and a loss of global market share  for a number of 
our industries. As a result, European companies have been forced to scale back operations or 
shut down production completely, part icularly SMEs, leading to significant job losses and 
possibly, to irreversible consequences. Deindustrialisation in Europe is not just an economic 
issue, it is a social and environmental one too. 
These factors all together risk to severely undermine the tr ansition to a carbon neutral 
economy set out in the European Green Deal and demonstrate the urgency for a strong EU 
industrial policy. Therefore, in order to make its industries more competitive globally, and 
increase their autonomy and resilience, the EU must strengthen its industrial base through 
the development and expansion of clean technologies production at each stage of their value-
chains and boost investments for the transition to an economic and production model that 
respects our planet’s climate change limits.  
AEGIS Europe welcomes the publication of the Green Deal Industrial Plan for the Net -Zero 
Age meant to complement the European Green Deal and REPowerEU. However, we consider 
that it is a long way to go until the EU regulatory framework ensures the European industry’s 
global competitiveness and a level playing field. The Industrial Plan should better recognise 
that European industry is central to Europe’s future progress and pr osperity; consistency 
among the various instruments, and easy access to financing  for both CAPEX and OPEX  is 
needed to preserve the existing EU industrial base and facilitate its expansion in light of 
strategic autonomy. 
The ambitious decarbonisation path towards carbon neutrality will involve a radical 
transformation of the European industry, requiring a combination of increasing production 
capacities and technological development, by setting out the key framework conditions to 
boost investments and accelerate the creation of markets for green products.  
Resource and energy -intensive industries (REIIs) are an important supplier of materials and 
products to support the green transition in other sectors of the economy. Furthermore, 
downstream products that are strategic and key for the decarbonization of the EU economy 
should also be given special attention. 
EU climate and industrial policy should be aligned to promote the commitment of the EU 
industry to reduce CO2 emissions at both European and global  levels. Therefore, AEGIS 
Europe calls for a mandatory commitment to introduce a target of 20% of GDP from industry, 
with an ambitious and realistic timeline. Hereby, the EU must continue providing carbon 
leakage protection to REEIs. In that context, the E uropean Commission must amend the 
Guidelines on certain State aid measures in the context of the system for greenhouse gas 
emission allowance trading post-2021 to restore the status quo before 2020 and to reinstate 
the eligibility of all REEIs for indirect cost compensation least the period of the transformation 
phase. 
2. Expanding the scope to other products critical for decarbonization 
AEGIS Europe considers that clean hydrogen, as well as industrial downstream products key 
and strategic for the decarbonisat ion of the EU economy – such as, for instance, renewable 
fuels of non -biological origi n production , construction products, refractory ceramic, bikes, 
fibres, railway products (e.g. rolling stock, infrastructure and signaling), recycling technologies 
and related activities etc. – which will all enable the decarbonisation of industry, transport 
and energy – should also fall under the scope of this proposal, and the annex should be revised 
accordingly. 
Railway systems should be deemed a key component of the ne t-zero industry ecosystem 
since, beyond the coverage of batteries and fuel cells in Annex 1, they strongly contribute as 
a whole to the achievement of EU’s climate objectives and through zero-emission mobility.  
3. Eligibility of net-zero strategic projects and consistency with the “Temporary Crisis and 
Transition Framework” (TCTF) 
AEGIS Europe considers that the selection criteria for net-zero strategic projects (article 10 of 
the NZIA) should be consistent with the rules in place for the selection of “projects of strategic 
importance for the transition towards a net -zero economy” under the Temporary Crisis and 
Transition Framework. It should be clarified in the NZIA that the production of any component 
and sub-components as well as related equipment used as an input for the production of the 
strategic net-zero technologies is sufficient to qualify as net-zero strategic project.  
4. Ensuring fair international competition and a level playing field on the EU market  
As the voice of the manufacturing industries in Europe, AEGIS Europe has always called for a 
level playing field in line with international trade and competition rules.  
The EU needs to address all unfair practices that our industries face from certain state-run or 
other non -market economics . For this , the Commission should tackle  export restrictive 
actions taken by trading partners in a resolute manner. In addition, the Commission should 
continue protecting the EU market by making a faster and stronger use of its trade defence 
instruments where the facts  justify it, in full respect of its  international and internal legal 
obligations. During the energy crisis, i mports of manufactured goods have surged by 30.3% 
The EU has become a net importer of goods according to data comparing figures from August 
2021 to figures from August 2022. The situation is going to worsen if no  emergency brake is 
envisaged. [Add smth on overcapacities]  
5. The Composition of the Net Zero Europe Platform  
AEGIS Europe welcomes the establishment of the Net Zero Europe Platform that shall be 
composed of Member States representatives and chaired by the Commission ; however, we 
consider that representatives of the industry should also be actively involved in the activities 
of this platform as they would be able to provide input as suggested in article 29 (11) of the 
Commission proposal.   
6. Public Procurement provisions 
The Commission ’s proposal has specific sustainability and resilience criteria that public 
authorities must take into account in public procurement procedures, in addition to price or 
cost. Public authorities shall give the tender’s sustainability and resilience contribution a 
weight ranging between 15% and 30% of the award criteria. This goes beyond mere (low) 
price criteria and is aligned with the Most-Economic Advantageous Tender (MEAT) principle, 
which is of great importance for the rail sector  in particular . This approach shall be 
continuously promoted beyond the NZIA in order to have a more binding approach on 
sustainability/MEAT. In addition, the EU should explore the imposition of local content 
requirements for public procurements that cover the value chains  of finished goods  to be 
purchased. In this respect, it is key that any upcoming revision of the public proc urement 
directives from 2014 makes such targets binding across all utility sectors.  
Conclusion 
The EU should put forward initiatives that promote European content in products across the 
value chain. Public procurement should be a role model for the end -consumer and prioritize 
goods whose components originate in the European Union, especially in case that such 
products are of strategic importance or destined for essential uses. 
The Green Deal Industrial Plan must be a comprehensive roadmap that supports and  boosts 
our industries, creating jobs, and generating economic growth, while ensuring a cleaner and 
greener Europe for generations to come. This historic opportunity must be seized to build a 
more resilient and sustainable Europe that works for all.  Furthermore, the envisaged 
framework should foster reciprocity in international trade exchanges and provide leverage to 
push third countries to open their markets in the same manner that the EU has opened its 
market in order to ensure a level playing field that the global trade system needs.

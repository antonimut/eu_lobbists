 The Green Deal Industrial Plan: 
Net Zero Industry Act 
Position of the European Man-made Fibres Industry 
 
FACTS & FIGURES 
The European man -made fibres (MMF) industry has  an annual output of 4.4 million 
tonnes and is valued at over €1 2 billion, making the EU the third largest producing 
region in the world. It is a supplier of essential raw materials to the textile and clothing 
industry and many other downstream sectors, e.g. wind energy, e -mobility 
battery/storage and grid technologies. Closely linked to the MMF industry are products 
which have undergone further processing such as certain types of fabric, and some  
finished products for industrial uses. The MMF industry is an indispensable actor when 
it comes to recycling textiles in Europe and has been actively involved in circularity for 
many years. 
MMF is an energy -intensive industry  that has  achieved significant improvements in 
terms of energy efficiency , but, despite those efforts,  it is currently exposed to an 
existential threat . Not only h igh gas and electricity costs but also raw 
material/intermediate costs need to be urgently addressed in the light of rising 
subsidies in competing countries. Moreover, the MMF industry has been damaged by 
unfair competition for many years. Both the above phenomena have recently led to 
massive company closures  in the EU . Indeed, within the last 2 years about 11,4% of 
MMF capacity have been lost in the EU  while world capacity increased by 4,4%. MMF 
imports into the EU reached this year an all-time record at € 3.382 million – 1.582.452 
tonnes whereas EU MMF exports have dropped in 2022 to 471.107 tonnes – a historic 
low point.

Yet, it is very unlikely that the new industrial plan will  become a game changer for 
the EU industry’s competitiveness . The ongoing crisis require s emergency policy

1. A European industrial gas and electricity price during the transformation phase

2. An emergency brake in form of additional tariffs or quotas to cap the import

labour related standards in Free Trade Agreements.

transformation phase at least. Revenues harnessed from the EU Emission

climate protection and transition projects. 
 
4. Funding and support schemes for both CAPEX and OPEX to boost the energy 
transformation of energy intensive industries such as MMF, in particular state 
aid, tax incentives and carbon contracts for difference.

NET ZERO INDUSTRY ACT: GENERAL REMARKS 
The European MMF industry, represented by CIRFS, has consistently fully supported 
the sustainability of MMF production processes and products  from cradle to cradle, 
including use and end-of-life in order for the impact on the environment to be reduced 
to a minimum. As a matter of fact, the European MMF industry  has been a pioneer in 
recycling and circularity for many years.  MMF are therefore an essential component 
of the European Green Deal and can play a key role in achieving the EU’s sustainability 
and carbon neutrality objectives by 2050.  Besides that, our products directly 
contribute to the deployment of net zero technologies, for instance: 
✓ Mooring ropes made of h igh tenacity yarns  of polyester or para-aramid yarns 
are used to anchor floating offshore energy platforms (e.g. wind) in deep water. 
✓ Man-made cellulose and acrylic fibres are the basis for carbon fibres for 
different applications  in automotives, wind turbines, and high temperature 
insulation (energy savings). 
✓ Para-aramid yarns are further used in deep water submarine power cables. 
✓ Aramid fibres are a key component of pipes for hydrogen transportation and 
optical fibre cables or anti-ballistic applications.  
✓ Cellulosic fibres are the basis for electrical separators in applications such as 
Lithium-ion batteries, supercapacitors and electrolytic condensers, particularly 
in the new generation of electric vehicles and other energy storage devices. 
✓ Battery solutions in the e-mobility require separators made of polypropylene 
fibres (PP). 
✓ High tenacity yarns made of cellulosic, polyester and para-aramid filaments are 
used in a variety of applications in electric vehicles, for instance tyres and hoses. 
✓ Heat- and flame -resistant fibres are incorporated in garments used by 
professionals operating in high-risk environments.  
✓ Cellulose fibres are reinforcement materials for composites used for 
construction materials and can be used to produce biodegradable materials for 
packaging.

NET ZERO INDUSTRY ACT: RECOMMENDATIONS 
▪ CIRFS believes that the broader the scope of the proposal, the more likely industry 
will be able to benefit from it.  Thus, we recommend clarifying in Article 3 that any 
component, sub-component and equipment related to a technology listed in the 
Annex is covered by this regulation. 
 
▪ CIRFS recommends including one of the key technologies of decarbonization in 
the Annex, namely recycling and related activities (such as sorting, collecting etc.) 
linked to fibre-to–fibre recycling together with other solutions in the direction of 
industrial symbiosis, in order to enhance overall recycling efficiency.  
 
▪ CIRFS welcomes the proposal to introduce sustainability and resilience criteria 
for public procurement procedures. We call on the EU institutions to expand this 
mandatory requirement for public authorities to all products and technologies. 
 
▪ CIRFS calls on the EU institutions to introduce  mandatory local content 
requirements along the value chains of net zero technologies to incentivize the EU 
production and reduce the dependency on third countries.  
 
 
 
 
CIRFS is the association for Europe’s € 12 billion man-made fibres industry, representing the industry 
to the European authorities and providing the industry with a wide range of services. Its members 
cover about 75% of European man-made fibres output. It provides for around 20,000 jobs in ca. 250 
plants. The European man -made fibres industry, with a total production in 2022 of ca. 4.4 million 
tonnes, is the world’s second largest in terms of output and one of the global leaders in terms of

CMA CGM welcomes the proposal 
for a Net Zero Industry Act as it marks 
the EU’s recognition of the need to 
act in response to anti -competitive 
measures that are increasingly 
threatening its industry.  While CMA 
CGM supports the overarching 
objectives of the upcoming proposal, 
the following remarks are intended to 
suggest improvements on the  
discussions and strengthening of the 
regulation. 
 
 
1. Favor a goal-based approach 
 
Technology neutrality should be prioritized in EU regulations as it ensures a level-playing field among the EU 
industries which contributes to the EU sustainable transition. Companies will have to diversify technologies 
and solutions as much as possible to ensure the largest sustainable panel of solutions to achieve the EU 
objectives. A limited list of strategic net-zero technologies will have serious consequences on the ability of 
companies to comply with all the EU regulations and will lead to uncompetitive technology market in Europe. 
The scope of the Act should be broadened accordingly: a goal-based approach seems to be more appropriate 
than an approach which selects economical winners.  Removing the strategic/non -strategic distinction w ill 
therefore avoid the Act not achieving its purpose by omitting some technologies that are key for achieving the 
Union’s climate neutrality objectives.

2. Ensure an evolving framework 
 
The above being said, if the decision is to maintain a list of strategic net-zero technologies that could, among 
others, deliver GHG benefit s, the list should allow for the possibility of extending it in the future to any 
technology capable of addressing the challenge of decarbonizing the Union´s industry. This, of course, should 
rely on some very clear criteria and thresholds to be defined subsequently. Due to fast technological innovation 
and developments, it will be essential to periodically review and adjust the scope of technologies falling under 
the scope of the Act.  
 
 
3. Extend the list of strategic net-zero technologies 
 
Should the technology neutrality approach not to be retained, the selection of activities included in the Annex 
and Recital 10 is questionable. Some of them, although included in the net-zero technology definition in the 
text, are not listed as “strategic” technologies that will benefit from accelerated licensing procedures.   
 
3.1 Strategic net-zero technologies to be added to the list 
 
In any case, some technologies should be added in the list of strategic net zero technologies contributing to 
the European Net Zero goals.

4. Provide a better response to the U.S. Inflation Reduction Act (IRA) to access to cost -
competitive and renewable energy in Europe  
 
While the draft proposal constitutes a key piece of legislation to fight for the competitiveness of EU industry in 
a globalized economy, the Act should provide for more financial clarity as no specific budget is allocated. The 
proposal only mentions several existing European funds and mechanisms that could be partly redirected to 
this initiative (Innovation Fund, Horizon Europe, R ePowerEU, Modernisation Fund, InvestEU, etc.) which is a 
good signal for the industry to start investing in these new technologies. However, the tax credit offered by the 
U.S. Inflation Reduction Act  provides for more visibility on technologies’ investment  as it clearly defines the 
condition to access and the amount to expect. On the contrary, grants and subsidies  offered through the Net 
Zero Industry Act  bring less visibility as  the amount may differ depending on the project negotiation and 
competitive process. Companies’ investment in EU strategic technologies will be more progressive in order to 
limit risk exposure while waiting for the subsidies. As a consequence, the introduction of the Net Zero Industry 
Act mechanism might not have the intended effect on strategic industries’ development in Europe. Additional 
mechanisms such as Contract for Differen ce or Fixed premium for technologies applications, as a 
complementary option to grant s in investment, could be a game changer for investment in net-zero 
technologies.

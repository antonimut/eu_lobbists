Response by the Fraunhofer-Gesellschaft to the 
proposal for a regulation  
 
 
Brussels, June 25, 2023 
 
Ref. Ares(2023)4454939 - 27/06/2023
 
Fraunhofer Response to Net Zero Industry Act 2 
 
 
 
 
Fraunhofer expresses its explicit support for the NZIA and its overarching goal to 
enhance the manufacturing capacities of net-zero technologies for the EU’s resilience, 
competitiveness, and climate neutrality. At the same time, Fraunhofer recognizes the 
missed opportunity of not having a direct link to R&I in the proposed regulation 
to ensure the efficient and effective implementation of the NZIA.  
 
Ensure R&I capacity by increasing net-zero technology production  
 
Fraunhofer has been a longstanding advocate for preserving or resettling the 
net-zero industry in Europe, but the full potential of the technologies remains 
untapped due to the lack of production in Europe. This does not only inflict the EU’s 
resilience and security of supply, but also endangers its R&I capacity and thus its 
competitive know how. Fraunhofer leads European research and development 
efforts for these technologies and is ready to deliver its fair share in increasing the EU’s 
manufacturing capacities with innovative solutions (see for example the proposal for 
Sustainable PV Manufacturing in Europe, but also in the area of hydrogen, geothermal 
energy or batteries). There is a clear need for strategic support by member states 
and the EU to reestablish and maintain Europe’s leadership and strategic autonomy in 
the net-zero industry. Apart from the need for European manufacturing capacities, the 
potential for saving greenhouse gas emissions by producing in Europe should be 
appreciated.  
It is crucial to shorten the time until implementation to arrest the ongoing 
outflow of industry to other geographic areas and thus avoid a brain drain of 
European expert knowledge.  
In particular, the simplification and acceleration of permitting procedures will be key to 
keep the industry in Europe and to enhance the manufacturing capacities.  
The EU’s aim to task standardization organizations with creating clear and transparent 
standards for net-zero technologies will further contribute to a simplified regulatory 
framework. RTOs have long standing experience in standardization efforts, 
especially since most new EU standards to deliver on the green and digital transitions 
originate from R&I projects.  
 
Involve research community to empower national competent authorities and connect 
with industry 
 
The designation of national competent authorities as one-stop-shops for project 
promoters is appreciated. Simultaneously, the capacities and competencies of the 
authorities need to be monitored and assessed to make sure that they have the 
required resources. To enable the authorities to deliver on the tasks at hand, the 
research community needs to be involved in the implementation process. This will 
allow for the development and deployment of cutting-edge technologies and 
innovative solutions in the daily operations of the responsible authorities.   
 
Fraunhofer welcomes the close interlinkage with industry and particularly the focus on 
SMEs. As an organization for applied research, Fraunhofer’s activities contribute

with SMEs being a founding principle. The involvement of relevant European industrial

The alignment with the Critical Raw Materials Act will be beneficial for R&I to continue

Additionally, in terms of skills, RTOs should be integrated in the to be established 
academies. RTOs provide a substantial contribution to market uptake and have a work

with industry and on the job training at the forefront of industry’s needs. 
 
Include negative emission technologies to facilitate resilience 
 
The resilience of future energy systems is likely to include renewables as well as 
negative emission technologies, which system analyses have proven to be beneficial, 
e.g., to reduce residual emissions. Clean technologies, which increase efficiency, 
include for example digitalization, smart home approaches, sector coupling, domestic 
heat management, circularity approaches and advanced materials. Technologies for 
carbon cascade use and carbon cycle management are also needed. The focus 
on DACCS and BECCS for negative emission technologies is too exclusive. CO2 storage 
sites, if understood as sequestration, should include carbon management, such as 
the use of wood. Even though Europe might not be at the forefront of negative 
emission technologies, regarding DACCS and BECCS, the EU pioneers in PyCCS.  
 
Integrate R&I as a key to the NZIA’s implementation  
 
Whereas the connection to the SET Plan to set research priorities and the 
opportunities of European manufacturing capacities for R&I is welcome, Fraunhofer 
sees a clear need to integrate R&I stronger into the debate about the NZIA and 
its ensuing implementation. Fraunhofer supports the list of strategic net-zero 
technologies and has contributed to their development and deployment for decades. 
However, it is key for Europe’s climate leadership to work today on the solutions of 
tomorrow. The regulatory sandboxes are a good step forward. Yet, since they seem to 
be specifically directed towards industry, a pathway for the R&I community to 
contribute with novel technologies and solutions should be established. By 
intertwining regulatory sandboxes with research and technology infrastructures, e.g. , 
test beds or living labs, experimentation and disruptive innovation will be facilitated 
and lead to the upscaling of prototypes to market readiness.  
It is vital to not divert dedicated research budget from funding programs , which 
has become a customary method of the Commission to finance new initiatives. A 
stable, long-term investment in R&I coupled with a balanced approach towards 
TRLs is a key prerequisite for an impactful and timely fulfillment of the NZIA. 
 
Fraunhofer proposes to the Commission to establish a direct link to R&I efforts and 
ensure greater participation of stakeholders from R&I throughout the NZIA’s 
implementation and the preparation of subsequent initiatives. This will allow the 
Commission to draw significant added value from RTOs’ in-depth expertise in 
the field of materials, production and energy research and technologies and 
ensure the NZIA delivers on its ambitious goals.

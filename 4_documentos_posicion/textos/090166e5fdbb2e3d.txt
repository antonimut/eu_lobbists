CONSULTATION  ON THE NET -
ZERO INDUSTRY ACT (NZIA)  
 
 
WirtschaftsVereinigung Metalle (WVMetalle) represents the German Non-Ferrous (NF) metals indus-
try in the public eye and is a valuable and trusted partner in the German & European political and 
economic landscape. We aim to represent our members' interest in climate, energy, environment, tax, 
trade and transport policies and ensure a constructive dialogue with legislators, policymakers and the 
general public. 
WVMetalle represents about 625 companies involved in all stages of the Non -Ferrous metals (NFM) 
value chain. It includes producers and proces sors of most base and special metals and compounds. 
Furthermore, WVMetalle is an active member of the German Industry Association (BDI) and the Euro-
pean Non-Ferrous Metals Association (Eurometaux). 
General remarks  
WVMetalle welcomes the possibility of giving feedback to the Net-Zero Industry Act (NZIA) published 
in March 2023 by the EU Commission. In conjunction with the Critical Raw Material Act (CRMA), this 
proposal could strengthen EU industrial policies to secure access to critical and strategic raw materials 
to reach the twin transition and the Union's strategic autonomy.  
Nevertheless, the proposal must acknowledge the importance of the non-ferrous metals industry and 
its strategic value chains as key players in developing the clean technologies described in the proposal. 
With our consultation contribution, we would like to provide constructive suggestions to improve and 
establish a practicable NZIA. 
Furthermore, the approach of the NZIA needs to be more defensive to reach the Union's goals of be-
coming climate neutral and more resilient. The key measure of the proposal is to accelerate approval 
procedures. Certainly, this is an important step, but a more progressive approach is needed compared 
to, e.g. the US Inflation Reduction Act (IRA). The EU should consider a general change in its policy 
making. We urge to reduce regulatory burden, administrative costs and bureaucracy. A bove all, a re-
form of the co mpetition law is needed to  allow easier public support for the industrial transition to-
wards climate neutrality. That is an indispensable requirement to reach strategic autonomy.    
Please find below our detailed analysis of the major points of the NZIA. 
 
Scope and Definitions 
While the NZIA proposal focuses primarily on "net-zero technologies manufacturing" (Art. 1 and 2), it 
fails, in our view, to fully recognize the role of the non-ferrous metal industry as a producer of materials 
necessary for the manufacturing of the strategic net-zero technologies. We want to raise the following 
points: 
• The production of key components  that serve a key role in the manufacturing of net-zero 
technologies needs to be covered by the NZIA as well. It should recognize the upstream value 
chain of non-ferrous metals as strategic building blocks for the  transition to a n et-zero and 
circular economy.

• Speaking of circular economy, we would like to see the recognition of recycling processes as 
net-zero technologies. Recycling of aluminium, copper, zinc, galvanized steel and other NFM 
plays a huge role in reducing material usage and energy consumption, contributing to the 
overall goal of the NZIA. Furthermore, given aluminium's importance in various applications 
in net-zero technologies (solar panels, wind turbines, grid infrastructure etc.), we want to 
stress once again that aluminium must be added to the strategic raw materials list of the 
CRMA. Detailed use of each metal in net-zero technologies can be found in the KU Leuven 
"Metals for Clean Energy" study1 (see Policy Maker Summary p. 6-7). 
 
• Coherency between legislation such as the Critical Raw Materials Act (CRMA), a yet-to-be-
defined "Semi-Finished Products Act", and the European Chips Act (ECA) are key. Due to the 
strong interdependencies between the different approaches, an alignment between these 
proposals is crucial. That should also be extended to other areas, such as the chemical legis-
lation (REACH and CLP), where materials such as lead and lithium are under scrutiny. Due to 
their inherent qualities, they play a fundamental role in the green and digital transition. 2 
 
Enabling Conditions and Financing of Net-Zero technologies 
We generally welcome the NZIA objective of reducing administrative burden and streamlining net-zero 
technologies' approval process. However, these measures are ineffective if they are not supported by 
a regulatory framework that allows suitable national and European financing instruments to boost net-
zero technologies' uptake significantly. 
• Simplifying administrative and approval procedures alone won't ensure the fast implemen-
tation of NZIA projects. These need to go hand in hand with reducing the administrative bur-
den on the side of the companies. That is especially the case for the obligation in the NZIA for 
applicants to improve their documentation within 14 days of being requested to do so. Espe-
cially in the case of large projects, it can be challenging to ensure compliance with such tough 
deadlines. 
 
• Administrative burden generally depends on outdated legislation, which, although ineffec-
tive, still need to be applied. We urge the EU Commission to consistently apply the "One In, 
One Out" principle, which it pledged and presented in December 2019. That could help 
to avoid increasingly far-reaching and sometimes contradictory regulations. 
 
• One Stop Shops, as envisioned by the NZIA, could be a relevant tool to reduce the adminis-
trative burden of companies if applied correctly. First, it should be clear and well-defined 
which national authority or domestic ministry is responsible for ensuring a central point of 
contact and avoiding ambiguities and misinformation among companies. That main point of 
contact should also guarantee easy and uncomplicated access to all relevant information. 
Finally, given the goal of streamlining processes, this should be reflected on the company's 
side by reducing costs for companies (e.g., for environmental impact assessments). 
 
• As mentioned, we welcome the idea of speeding up application and approval processes for 
Net-zero technologies. However, The European Commission should be aware that applica-
tion and approval processes outside the Net-Zero ecosystem need also be granted new ex-
pedited procedures to avoid bottlenecks. That is especially the case in upstream value

• The NZIA and the CRMA, which are part of the Green Deal Industrial plan, don't really con-
sider the financing behind those projects. While faster permitting and reduced administrative 
burden are pleasant, these projects are capital-intensive operations that require a stable 
and predictable regulatory framework and a lot of public and private investment. As of to-
day, it is unclear and uncertain how the NZIA and the CRMA address that point. Furthermore, 
to activate the few financing measures or subsidies described in the NZIA or the CRMA, high 
thresholds and conditions prevent companies from doing so. That is in strong contrast to the 
Inflation Reduction Act (IRA), which is easy to understand and focuses only on simplicity by 
focusing only on tax credits for operating and capital expenditures. Therefore, the EU should 
also facilitate national measures that enable transition and support the EU in achieving stra-
tegic autonomy. 
 
Innovation 
The non-ferrous metal industry has always strived to improve our processes for energy-related or en-
vironmental reasons. That has significantly helped to reduce the industry's impact on the environment, 
but it was only possible due to continuous investment in research in development (R&D). It is essential 
that technologies not (yet) ready for the market be made eligible for funding under the NZIA. Research 
and innovation should be given at least as much priority in the NZIA as market-ready technologies. 
 
Conclusion 
As mentioned before, we welcome the proposal of the NZIA; however, the suggestions above are nec-
essary conditions for the sake of the goal it set out to achieve. We want to reiterate the main points 
that are essential to make the NZIA work in practice for all sizes of companies: 
• Scope – Acknowledge and include the non-ferrous metals industry and its strategic value chains 
in the NZIA. 
• Recycling – Recognize recycling technologies as net-zero technologies to facilitate transition to 
net zero.  
• Legislative coherence – align legislation (CRMA, REACH, CLP etc.) to avoid contradictions, dys-
functional goals, and additional burden for companies. 
• Financing – accelerate public and private investments.

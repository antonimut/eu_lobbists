JSW SA position paper on the European Commission’s proposal on Regulation on establishing a 
framework for ensuring a secure and sustainable supply of critical raw materials 
 
Jastrzębska Spółka Węglowa SA welcomes the initiative of the European Commission (EC) to create a 
legal framework that guarantees favorable conditions for the development of the critical raw materials 
(CRMs) value chain in the European Union. Ensuring safe  and sustainable access to raw materials of 
our domestic  production is crucial for the success of EU’s green and digital transition  and the 
achievement of climate goals. As a producer of coking coal, a critical raw material, we see some areas 
of the presen ted regulation th at could be improved to better  address challenges currently faced by 
the mining industry and to stronger facilitate its development in the future. 
 
I. Chapter 2: Critical and Strategic Raw Materials 
The EC propos al, in addition to the existing since 2010 l ist of critical raw materials, introduces a 
new list of so -called strategic raw materials . Both lists are based on completely different data and 
different methodologies. The list of critical raw materials is created basing on a scientifically developed 
methodology that defines clear guidelines for conducting the criticality assessment process, which is 
supported by sc ience and industry experts. On the contrary , raw materials considered by the EC 
strategic, were identified  unilaterally, basing on  calculations w ith unknown and unpublished data 
source, while the methodology of the calculation itself  described in the A nnex I to the regulation is  
very imprecise.  
Considering the fact that most of the solutions proposed in the regulation are de facto focused on 
strategic raw materials only, in our opinion process of their identification should be based on specific 
data of a known source, and, as it is in the case of criticality assessment, the principles of transparency 
should be ensured. The entire process should be carried out with the participation of scientific and 
industry experts with appropriate knowledge and competence in the field of raw materials.  
In addition, in our opinion, strategic raw materials that are important for the transformation of the 
European economy and do not meet the requirements set out for critical raw materials should not 
automatically gain the status of critical raw materials, as this list is created on the basis of clearly 
defined thresholds set by the methodology. The division into these two lists should therefore be clear 
and transparent - there are no reasons for strategic raw materials to be automatically included in the 
list of critical raw materials. Raw materials for which a significant increase in demand is forecasted 
despite the lack of supply risk should not be considered critical by the EU, as ensuring access to them 
does not pose such a challenge as it is the case with  critical raw materials identified in the criticality 
assessment process. 
 
 
II. Chapter 3: Strengthening the EU raw material value chain 
 
Article 5 
The criteria for recognizing projects as strategic should be extended to include all critical raw materials. 
The Commission should take into account the fact that CRMs are particularly at risk of supply, therefore 
projects aimed at increasing their local production and thus reducing the EU's dependence on imports 
should be prioritized and benefit from all facilitations provided for Strategic Projects.

Articles 8, 10 
The draft regulation pro poses establishing so-called one -stop-shops, that shall be responsible for 
facilitating and coordinating the process of issuing permits for critical raw materials  projects, which 
will certainly translate into facilitations for producers. However, it would be adv isable, as in the case 
of Strategic Projects, to set a maximum permitting time for both - new projects concerning CRMs and 
also for development of already existing mining facilities. Withou t specifying the time frame,  
permitting process may even be prolonge d for CRMs projects , due to the number of administrative 
units involved in the concession procedure, and the mere establishment of one-stop will not translate 
into elimination of obstacles and facilitation of the entire process from the administrative side . 
Therefore, if according to the proposal , 2 years period is enough to issue a permit, it should apply to 
all critical raw materials, as they are all of strategic importance for European industry. 
 
Art. 15 
The key element s missing in the European Commission's draft proposal are funding, financing and 
incentives to investors. Mining projects are always long-term and very capital -intensive business 
initiatives with a high investment risk burdening investors . With the current regulatory risks and 
uncertainty combined with  lack of social acceptance for new mining projects, it will stay extremely 
difficult for the European Union to attract funding for new projects without the right incentives.  
Additionally, dedicated instruments for the banking sector should be considered, such as lower capital 
ratios and risk weights, which shall ensure a stable financial environment. 
 
III. Chapter 4: Monitoring and Mitigating Risk 
Art. 23 
The methodology for selecting a strategic raw material should take into  account not only the global 
demand for a given strategic technology, but also the supply risk factor, i.e. ensuring stable supplies of 
raw materials from sources within the EU in relation to stable and fair supplies of raw materials from 
global markets. C oking coal falls fully within the scope of  the value chain of technologies listed as 
strategic, e.g. batteries, production, storage and use of hydrogen, renewable energy technologies or 
defense industry. 
 
IV.  Chapter 5: Sustainability - Section 2: Certification and Environmental Footprint 
The regulatory framework obliges European producers to comply with environmental standards, while  
reporting regulations impose obligations in the area of environmental footprint reporting. Given the 
high dependence of European industry on supplies of raw materials from third countries, it would be 
reasonable to extend the proposed provisions also on commodities imported from outside the EU –
often from countries whose legal regulations do not  necessarily guarantee compliance for 
environmental or social standards. Therefore, the European Commission should take a more holistic 
approach to both environmental and social costs and thus consider extending the environmental 
footprint declaration obligation to raw materials imported from third countries, in particular those that 
have not implemented appropriate environmental, social and governance (ESG) standards, or where 
ESG compliance is difficult to verify. 
 
V. Chapter 7: Management 
The European Critical Raw Materials  Board in one of the permane nt subgroups should also include 
representatives of the sector - both industry, importers and industry associations.

VI. Annex I, section 1: List of strategic raw materials 
According to th e RePower EU  assumptions, the capacity of European wind energy is  foreseen to 
increase from the current 190 GW to 480 GW in the perspective of 2030, where each MW of wind 
power requires between 120 and 180 to ns of steel. To launch the planned, new 290 GW of capacity, 
over 43.5 million tons of steel will be neede d, for the prod uction of which, almost 31 million tons of 
coking coal will be needed. Coking coal is, and will continue to be, a key ingredient in steel production 
in the upcoming years. The development of zero-emission steel technologies is currently at the stage 
of pilot projects, and due to the complexity of the sector's transformation, it will not be possible to 
completely switch to clean technologies. A ccording to various scenarios, the decarbonisation of the 
steel sector will be achieved with further steel production through the traditional blast furnace method 
using coke, combined with carbon capture and use technology  (CCUS). Given that steel is used in all 
key sectors of the economy and is the basis for transformation and achieving Green Deal goals, its 
demand is expected to increase on a global scale.  
In this context, it is reasonable that locally mined coking coal, which accounts for 25% of EU demand, 
should be considered a strategic raw material. Due to the significant dependence on imports from 
third countries as distant as Australia, the USA or Canada, it is necessary to ensure access to this raw 
material and support its further production in the EU. The status of a strategic raw material will enable 
easier development of the resource base, maintaini ng a uninterrupted level of extraction and 
guarantee stable supplies for the steel industry. 
 
 
Summary 
The regulation, which, according to its title, is supposed to be the main regulatory instrument to ensure 
improvement of the critical raw materials value chain and guarantee their safe and sustainable supply, 
does not really meet these goals. Critical raw materials, which have been regularly assessed for over 
10 years on the basis of an extensive scientific methodology, are marginalized in the regulati on, in 
favor of those  “strategic” defined in a completely non -transparent process  through European 
Commission's subjective key, based on the imprecise methodology. With this in mind, in our opinion, 
the publication of the first list of strategic raw materi als should be preceded by an in -depth analysis 
and eva luation process carried out by  experts and academia . Having several raw material to hold 
double -critical and strategic- status, while some just being critical will introduce additional ambiguity 
within the industry and will make it difficult to apply the provisions of the regulation in practice. Critical 
raw materials should also be enabled to apply for Strategic Projects, as they are subject to a particular 
supply risk, and they should be at the center of the European Commission's proposal.

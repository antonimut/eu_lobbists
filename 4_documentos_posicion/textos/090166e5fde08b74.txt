Talga’s position on the European Commission’s proposal for a Critical Raw Materials Act 1. Introduction Talga welcomes the Commission’s proposal for a Critical Raw Materials Act, along with the other pillars of the Green Deal Industrial Plan. This legislation has the potential to form a competitive critical raw materials supply chain which will make a substantial contribution to resilient and green supply chains across Europe. Talga presents its recommendations on how to improve and strengthen this proposal so it can more effectively reach its objectives.  2. Retain natural graphite as a strategic raw material  It is positive that battery grade natural graphite is considered a strategic raw material with targets for domestic extraction and processing. Talga supports these targets, although care should be taken to ensure that they are a floor and not a ceiling and that, in the long term, there should be an ambition to increase targets where feasible.  Furthermore, Talga supports the target of no more than 65% supply of each strategic raw material coming from a single third country.  Explanation Natural graphite is a vital component of electric vehicle battery anodes, which are themselves an important technology for the European green transition. According to Benchmark Mineral Intelligence’s Graphite Forecast, three-fifths of the world’s natural graphite feedback is mined in China, with just over 3% of the global supply of flake graphite expected to be sourced from Europe by 20301.  Without adequately supporting new or existing projects with potential to expand, Europe will struggle to increase domestic supply. This will exacerbate Europe’s supply chain vulnerability and may lead to worse climate outcomes, jeopardising the intent of the Green Deal Industrial Plan. 3. Creation of Net-Zero Integrated Projects to facilitate strategic raw materials projects Talga supports the identification and support of sustainable strategic raw materials projects essential to Europe’s green transition. However, to set Europe on the best path towards net zero, consideration should be given to not only strategic raw materials, but also their downstream uses in net-zero technologies.  Talga proposes the inclusion of Net-Zero Integrated Projects, defined as a project that qualifies as strategic under both the Critical Raw Materials Act and Net-Zero Industry Act. Explanation  The natural graphite extracted at Talga’s mine outside Vittangi, northern Sweden, will be processed into green anode material at its downstream facility in Luleå, northern Sweden. This anode manufacturing facility, under the proposed guidelines, would likely be granted Strategic Net-Zero Project status under the Net-Zero Industry Act. Recognition of projects whose entire supply chain supports the principles of the Green Industrial Plan will enable the EU to better support its own green transition. 4. Recycling targets should be aggregate The establishment of a circular economy is fundamental to ensure resource security, and Talga fully supports the EU’s determination for ambitious recycling targets. However, different critical raw materials face different recycling challenges. These challenges may be economic, geological, technological, or environmental. For Europe to build a truly circular economy, it must consider all these challenges.  Talga proposes that the 15% recycling target for all strategic raw materials should remain as an aggregate rather than be applied to each strategic raw material. The recycling targets of each strategic raw material should be subject to assessments which investigate the economic, technical and sustainability challenges that material faces. Explanation Talga has invested and continues to invest in recycling and reuse programs at its R&D facility. These programs include recycling spent graphite battery anode for production of other battery materials and reprocessing unused scrap anode from battery production waste streams.  However, natural graphite is not a metal and, owing to how the material changes both structurally and chemically over the life of a battery, spent graphite battery anode cannot yet be recycled back into graphite battery anode in a commercially feasible manner. Therefore, an individual recycling target for graphite should only be set with these challenges in mind.  5. Streamlined permitting process  Talga supports the streamlining of permitting processes while noting the importance of maintaining stringent environmental standards. Ambitious and responsible permitting standards will enable Europe to decarbonise faster and with greater strategic autonomy, while still maintaining a responsible approach to growth. However, to ensure permitting reforms achieve their intended effects, further clarity must be provided in the Act. Talga proposes that the two-year permitting time limit for extraction projects should have a clearly defined start date – from the moment that the application is received.  Furthermore, Talga recommends that the Act encourages Member States to develop comprehensive guidelines and scope for permit applications to reduce uncertainty for project promoters. This would include certainty for all parties on the timing of the process and the process itself, reducing the potential for scope change or interpretation.

Explanation Europe’s permitting challenge is not a result of its high environmental standards; rather, it results from inefficient bureaucracy and a lack of predefined scope and clarity required for permit applications. This lack of scope means that many Member States struggle to grant permits to worthy projects in a timely manner.  By establishing clear processes with fixed timelines, Europe will greatly streamline its permitting procedures without compromising on high environmental standards. This would also provide certainty for developers, consequently increasing the attractiveness of European projects. 6. Increased funding and investment mechanisms  In light of intense international competition for investment in the green transition, Talga strongly supports the establishment of robust funding and investment mechanisms such as a European Sovereignty Fund and/or other mechanisms to help ensure the EU is in a strong position to deliver on its green transition objectives.  Explanation The United States’ Inflation Reduction Act has caused a seismic shift in global climate action. While the Act is to be applauded as a transformative call to action, it has given substantial advantage to non-European actors. Europe must ensure that its industry is not left behind in the growing green economy. For example, in May 2022 it was reported that the US Department of Energy made available USD 3.16 billion to develop the country’s battery supply chain2, including up to USD 370 million of grants earmarked to two graphite anode producers with US-based operations3,4.  A similar European policy would boost investor confidence in European projects and slow the movement of capital to the US. In March 2023, Volkswagen announced an investment of €15 million in North America for battery production to take advantage of the generous grants provided by the IRA5. Therefore, Europe should take bold action to provide financial and investment support for extraction of raw materials crucial for the green transition. 7. Enhancing worker skills Talga is in favour of funding to support upskilling of Europe’s workforce across traditional population centres and remote areas to help achieve the ambitions of this regulation. In addition, Talga recommends the European Commission work in partnership with national authorities to develop a skilled regulatory workforce supporting streamlined permit application processes. This will ensure a better understanding of the underlying subject matter and allow national authorities to make better, faster decisions. Reporting obligations  Talga notes the importance of regularly monitoring and reporting on environmental outcomes to the Commission or relevant authority. However, care should be taken so that reporting obligations are not too onerous and do not offset efficiencies made in other areas. Explanation Existing European legislation is setting increasingly high sustainability requirements for businesses and projects operating within the EU.  As President von der Leyen noted at the European Parliament Plenary on 15 March 2023, reporting obligations should not unreasonably burden EU businesses. Talga supports the reduction of unnecessary, burdensome or duplicative reporting, in line with the aim of the industrial plan to reduce reporting by 25%7. 9. Conclusion Talga Group supports the European Commission’s proposal for a Critical Raw Materials Act and believes that with further support to the project promoters, including assistance with permitting, funding and skills, the EU can become the world leader in sustainable extraction and processing of critical raw materials.

Critical Raw Materials Act – Feedback on Commission Proposal 
The Critical Raw Materials Act (CRMA) proposal is a vital first step towards boosting the EU’s critical raw 
materials capacities, increasing the resilience of raw materials supply chains, and promoting supply 
chain sustainability and circularity. It sets high ambitions and a conducive framework for a secure and 
sustainable supply of critical raw materials for the European economy. 
The Parliament and Member States must build on this momentum to deliver a Critical Raw Materials Act 
that can catalyse new investments and boost European competitiveness, including through domestic 
lithium battery recycling. As battery manufacturing in the EU continues to grow exponentially to as 
much as 1.5 TWh by 2030, as projected by the Fraunhofer Institute, Europe must rapidly scale recycling 
projects to secure supplies of critical battery materials, including lithium, cobalt, nickel, and copper. 
Summary of recommendations: 
1. Clarify and strengthen 2030 benchmarks, and reference these benchmarks across future EU 
legislations of relevance (Article 1) 
2. Streamline permitting to support the scale-up of the European battery recycling industry 
(Articles 8 – 13) 
3. Provide targeted incentives to support the commercialisation and scale-up of battery recycling 
projects in Europe (Article 15) 
4. Ensure recycling-related Strategic Projects meet the highest ESG standards (Article 5, 29 – 30, 
Annex III) 
5. Updated list of critical and strategic raw materials (Annex I & II); removing battery grade 
requirement for certain metals 
 
1. Clarify and strengthen 2030 benchmarks, and reference these benchmarks across future EU 
legislations of relevance (Article 1): 
The current proposal stipulates that 15% of the European demand for critical raw materials should be 
sourced from domestic recycling projects. However, these targets' non-binding and aggregate nature 
may not deliver the desired outcomes. The Parliament and Member States should: 
a. Set relevant commodity-level benchmarks: Benchmarks should be transparently set based 
on projected volumes of the feedstock of each commodity. For example, the 2030 recycling 
benchmark for lithium should be largely linked to the estimated volume of battery 
manufacturing scrap and end-of-life batteries available for recycling in 2030. 
b. Monitor project pipeline, assess bottlenecks, and propose action plans: The Commission 
should be empowered to prepare a periodic report that analyses progress and project 
pipeline towards meeting defined benchmarks for each commodity. The report should also 
assess investment bottlenecks and propose action plans for member states to meet these 
targets.

c. Strive for policy coherence:  The 2030 benchmarks that enshrine supply chain de-risking, 
should be north stars that should be referenced in all EU policy priorities. This should help 
drive regulatory coherence and alignment, not only through the Ecodesign Regulation as the 
proposal currently specifies in Article 1.4, but this could be expanded to cover REACH, CLP 
Regulations, and the Industrial Emissions and Water Directives. 
 
2. Streamline permitting to support the scale-up of the European battery recycling industry 
(Articles 8 – 13): 
Securing relevant environmental, fire safety, emergency response, and construction permits can be the 
longest and the most critical step towards commercialising a new battery recycling facility. Delays and 
uncertainty can compound investment risks, and affect the viability of new projects. 
Li-Cycle supports the EU Commission’s intentions to identify and accelerate permitting for Strategic 
Projects through a single-window clearance or one-stop-shop proposal to ensure the European battery 
recycling industry can compete internationally.  
a. Ensure that permitting process for new recycling facilities is time-bound and harmonised 
across EU member states 
b. Maintain single-window clearance or one-stop-shop permitting processes with suitably 
resourced authorities and departments. 
 
3. Provide targeted incentives to support the commercialisation and scale-up of battery recycling 
projects in Europe (Article 15): 
Incentives for recyclers that help reduce upfront costs of capital-intensive recycling facilities and 
accelerate the scale-up of the recycling industry will be critical towards meeting the growing demand for 
battery materials in Europe. As the industry works to scale its operations to meet future demand rapidly, 
such targeted support best reflects the current state of maturity of the industry in Europe and globally. 
As Li-Cycle plans for future Spokes and Hubs, we are drawn to jurisdictions with existing clusters of 
battery manufacturers and OEMs, which offer attractive government incentives and funding 
opportunities to mutualise the risk of Li-Cycle’s investments and expansion efforts. 
a. Set up new EU-level financing mechanisms: The CRMA should unlock new EU-level funding 
mechanisms, along the lines of the proposed Sovereignty or STEP Fund to support the scale-
up and commercialisation of recycling facilities. Such mechanisms should include all 
instruments, including but not limited to subsidised loans, tax breaks, and grants.  
b. Reform the process to access financing: Accessing most existing EU-level funding options 
require extensive lead times and resource commitments. Additionally, such options have 
low chances of success.  This is prohibitive for emerging enterprises with innovative 
technologies. A stage-gate process with regular feedback and checks could help recycling 
companies such as Li-Cycle to contribute toward building a resilient raw materials supply 
chain for Europe.

c. Adapt financing mechanisms to progress against achieving CRMA benchmarks: The 
quantum and process of financial mechanisms should also be adapted to the EU’s progress 
toward meeting the benchmarks in the CRMA. 
 
4. Ensure recycling-related Strategic Projects meet the highest ESG standards (Article 5, 29 – 30, 
Annex III):  
Li-Cycle supports the EU’s ambitions to accelerate the energy transition to meet its 2050 carbon 
neutrality goals and build a secure and resilient supply chain for critical materials.  
Sustainability is at the core of all Li-Cycle operations and decision-making.  As such, Li-Cycle encourages 
the continued regulatory push to propel carbon reduction initiatives in the battery supply chain. 
However, sustainability, by definition, is holistic, and we urge the Parliament and the Member States to 
also include other metrics, such as water usage and waste management, in the environmental footprint 
requirements for critical raw materials – virgin and recycled. 
a. Set high ESG standards for Strategic Projects for battery recycling: Ensuring that sourcing of 
critical materials – including through recycling – meets ambitious ESG standards will not just 
be an enabler towards the EU’s decarbonization goals, but will also drive innovation and be 
a source of competitive advantage for the European industry in a rapidly changing market 
environment.  
b. Ensure verifiable disclosure and progressively higher standards for the environmental 
footprint and sourcing of critical battery materials. Beyond carbon emissions, the 
environmental footprint methodology should incorporate other metrics, such as water 
usage and waste management. 
 
5. Updated list of critical and strategic raw materials (Annex I & II); ); removing battery grade 
requirement for certain metals. 
We recommend removing the “battery grade” classification from the critical and strategic raw materials 
list. “Battery grade” classification depends on multiple criteria, such as the existence of specific 
impurities or the physical characteristics of the material in question. Additional processing and refining 
can often enable such materials to be reintegrated into the battery supply chain.  
The domestic EU lithium battery recycling industry already has ample value-based incentives to ensure 
materials are readily used in the battery supply chain. Hence, additional purity criteria are redundant 
and potentially administratively burdensome. Regulators should first observe the effect of existing 
mandates under the battery regulation and monitor the impacts of existing tools under the Battery 
Regulation before adding purity restrictions. Additional purity criteria would be redundant anyway if no 
significant down-cycling is observed.  
For the principal battery metals, a more pertinent definition could be: 1) Nickel metal, intermediates, 
and salts, 2) Manganese metal, intermediates, and salts, and 3) Lithium compounds, intermediates, and 
salts

About Li-Cycle: 
Li-Cycle (NYSE: LICY) is an industry leader in lithium-ion battery recycling and resource recovery. We 
continue growing our innovative and environmentally friendly Spoke & Hub Technologies™ to provide 
an end-of-life solution for all lithium-ion batteries. We produce recycled battery-grade materials to help 
create local, closed-loop supply chains. 
We have four operational Spoke recycling facilities in North America that can process 51,000 tonnes of 
lithium-ion battery materials per year, with flexibility for expansion. Additionally, Li-Cycle’s first 
hydrometallurgical refinery ‘Hub’ facility, is undergoing construction in Rochester, NY, and is on track to 
start commissioning in 2023. The Rochester Hub will process up to 35,000 tons per year of black mass 
and will produce battery-grade nickel sulphate, lithium carbonate, and cobalt sulphate. Once completed, 
the Rochester Hub will be one of the world's largest facilities and sources of recycled lithium. 
We are rapidly expanding our footprint in Europe,  with a Spoke in Germany expected to be operational 
in the second half of 2023. We are also developing additional Spokes in France and Norway. Recently, Li-
Cycle, in partnership with Glencore, announced plans to jointly study the feasibility of and later, develop 
a Hub facility in Portovesme, Italy (the “Portovesme Hub”).  The Portovesme Hub would produce critical 
battery materials, including nickel, cobalt and lithium, from recycled battery content. The Portovesme 
Hub will leverage Li-Cycle’s state-of-the-art hydrometallurgical technology and is expected to be the 
largest producer of sustainable battery grade products in Europe.

UNESID Comments to the European Critical Raw Materials ACT 
REGULATION OF THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND OF THE COUNCIL 
establishing a framework for ensuring a secure and sustainable supply of 
critical raw materials and amending Regulations (EU) 168/2013, (EU) 
2018/858, 2018/1724 and (EU) 2019/1020 
UNESID is the trade association of the Spanish steel Industry and the first tier of downstream 
activities. It gathers 46 companies that employ 50,000 people, in addition to more than 20 ,000 
related to scrap recovery. Every tonne of steel produced in Spain carries out 7 € in environmental 
investments. The Spanish steel industry is the recycling champion, as every year 11 m tonnes of 
ferrous scrap become new steel. The steel industry makes  efficient use of resources to take 
advantage of almost 80% of the waste and by -products generated in the process; the water 
recirculation ratio is over 90%. The steel industry is the paradigm of the circular economy. 
UNESID welcomes this new initiative ai med to stablish a framework that ensures the Union's 
access to a secure and sustainable supply of critical raw materials : indeed, they are strategical 
for the ecological and digital transition wherein the steelmaking industry is a key sector. The 
Spanish steel industry also welcomes the efforts made by the proposed regulation to reduce 
dependence on unreliable or highly concentrated sources of metals, which reinforces both 
European strategic autonomy and the circular economy production model. 
We consider that, in general, the fields addressed by the act are appropriated, however, in some 
cases the scope should be improved to achieve the desired objectives. In particular  we note and 
recommend the following general proposals as well as some specific amendments. 
Objectives 
In order to boost the efficiency and promote circular economy, the objectives of the proposed 
act should be defined by raw material basis, as far as possible.  
On the other hand, the objective to produce by recycling at least the 15% of the Union's annual 
consumption of strategic raw materials does not appear to be enough ambituous and therefore 
is not aligned with the European Green Deal neither the last Circular Economy Action Plan 
principles. To effectively foster circularity, regulation sh ould include the possibility to recycle 
another secondary materials or wastes elements to substitute raw materials. The regulation 
should take a further step in recycling, specially when materials are produced in countries with 
lower environmental, social and governance standards (ESG). 
Critical and Strategic Raw Materials Lists 
Formulas used for calculate strategic and critical raw materials should take into account the real 
potential of substitution of critical raw materials through recycling of wastes elements or 
secondary materials. Indeed, recycling could be considered in the calculations, so increasing 
rates of recycling will reduce the dependence of critical raw materials. By doing so, the industry’s 
efforts for recycling will be reflected in the upgrades made to the critical and strategic raw 
material lists. In any case it is essential to ensure those lists revisions and updates are carried out 
with sufficient agility and flexibility.

Permit Granting Process 
The current permitting processes are often a problematic issue for the ecological transition, as 
evidenced by the simplification proposal outlined in the Critical Raw Materials act and UNESID 
acknowledges the efforts made by  the act. However, new permitting mechanisms should be 
considered and developed in general procedures. In particular, measures addressed to assistance 
and to increase the capacities of the Member States should be clearly adopted, including 
instruments such  as monitoring reports on the efficiency of the granting process among 
Members States. This type of measures will contribute to harmonize procedures and concession 
periods in the internal market.  
Risk mitigation 
To ensure a level playing field, both strategic stocks and joint purchasing should be promoted 
and managed at European . This approach prevents market distortions and among member 
states. Any individual intervention by the member states should not be considered. 
Measures on circularity 
In addition to the measures outlined in article 25, the programmes designed by Member States 
must include specific measures to enhance the collection of waste containing critical and 
strategic raw materials that possess high substitution potential, and ensure their introduction 
into an appropriate recycling system. Moreover, conducting a triennial review seems to be a 
suitable approach to assess the effectiveness of these measures over time. 
Penalties 
European Commission should establish a penalty system at European level to prevent deviations 
from members states and therefore, to encourage actions to ensure that the regulation 
objectives are meet.

Strategic raw materials  
SECTION 1  
LIST OF STRATEGIC RAW MATERIALS 
The following raw materials shall be 
considered strategic: 
(k) Nickel 
Justification 
The scope of nickel should be increase to all grades, not only to battery grade, especially when 
alike varieties can be substitute in different industries (for example class 1 and 2 of nickel). To 
avoid potential bottlenecks among sectors, all relevant materials containing nickel should be 
considered in the critical and strategic list, and therefore, the list should include all grades of  
nickel.

The International Zinc Association (IZA) is pleased to add our voice to the 2 amendments related to 
Zinc: 
1. the recognition of the importance as a base metals/host of minor CRM co-metals, and 
2. the Strategic importance of Zinc itself in the Infrastructure requirements of the Green Deal 
Ambitions. 
We request 1 further consideration, which is a concern for several strategic metal groups. It 
addresses the confusion arising from the substitution text for SRMs. We would like to offer this 
simple but important solution – to make a differentiation based on supply risk. 
3. It is important to adapt the supporting text regarding supply risk and substitution to ensure 
that this substitution text only applies to CRMs (and not SRMs), with a supply risk score of 
> 1.  
Justification: The proposed Critical Raw Materials Act is an important starting point for improving 
the supply chains of metals in the EU, from mining to processing, from use to recycling, but: 
• SRMs have limited supply risks, but are necessary for Europe's strategic independence.  
• On the contrary, increasing the local production capacity of these SRMs should be supported. 
However, the designation of strategic minerals and metals for substitution could lead to 
divestment and further negatively affect already limited local output.

Specific Recommendations: 
 
Strategic Raw Materials   
  
Justification: Zinc is a strategic raw material used across almost all clean energy technologies, and is 
also the primary commodity needed for accessing other critical/strategic raw materials like 
germanium and gallium. Europe’s production base is under threat today due to its high 
electricityintensity, with 50% of EU capacity offline today. As well as the importance of maintaining 
this capacity, there are several potential zinc (and byproduct) mining projects in Europe that would 
benefit from the Critical Raw Materials Act, as well as a need to further develop zinc recycling.

Justification: Several strategic raw materials are mined or recycled mainly as byproducts. This 
means their production is only viable from bigger operations focused on the primary commodity, 
such as zinc for germanium. The Critical Raw Materials Act should reflect this necessary 
integration.

Executive summary  
 
SUEZ welcomes the European Commission’s proposal on the Critical Raw Material Act. Indeed, 
elements in this proposition would contribute to the strategic independency of the European 
Union while maintaining with regards to the circular economy, a particular reduced environmental 
footprint through increased recycling.  
 
Our key messages for improving the European Commission’s proposal are the following involves 
clearly defining strategic technologies, limiting the possibility of removing critical materials from 
the critical raw material list, introducing recycled content obligations for magnets, ensuring 
permitting granting does not exceed 12 months and ensuring adequate representation and 
circular economy focus within the proposed Critical Raw Materials Board.  
  
 
Introduction  
SUEZ welcomes the European Commission ’s proposal and supports the initiative to create a more 
independent European Union regarding critical  raw material  supply. SUEZ contributes through 
recycling and wastewater treatment which enables the recuperation of secondary critical raw 
materials that can then be re-employed.  
Consequently, SUEZ recommends the European Commission’s proposal takes into consideration the 
following:  
 
1. Objectives and definition of strategic technologies  
The first article lays down different objectives due to the extraction, processing and recycling of raw 
materials in the European Union:  
i) reach an extraction capacity covering at least 10% of the EU’s annual consumption of raw materials,  
ii) reach a processing capacity covering at least 40% of the EU’s annual consumption of raw materials, 
iii) reach a recycling capacity covering at least 15% of the EU’s annual consumption of raw materials.

However, it is not clear how this overall target will be applied for each critical raw material. Without 
clear targets per material, it will not further the recycling of such materials.  
Article 2 provides several definitions. The definition of strategic technologies (point 31) is unclear. The 
use of the  word “green” is broad and a clear definition and clarification is needed . SUEZ suggest 
adding a definition to specify the inclusion of water and circular economy.  
 
2. Distinction between raw materials and modification of the list  
The European Commission proposal distinguishes between strategic and critical raw materials  which 
are listed in annex I and II. The consequences of this distinction are not very clear.    
SUEZ can recover several raw materials during the recycling activities, such as lithium, magnesium, 
bauxite, and cobalt. Furthermore,  SUEZ has the knowledge to recover  materials from sludge or 
biowaste such as phosphor, potassium, organic matter, and nitrogen. Phosphor being a critical raw 
material can then be recovered and used as a fertilizer.  SUEZ welcomes that phosphor is part of the 
list of critical raw materials and recommends recognizing the value of sludge as a fertilizer.  
Also, the legislative proposal envisions modifying the list every four years. For this, the European 
Commission's ability to modify the list should be restrained to adding new elements. It should not 
be able to suppress materials as markets are set up to recycle them and to ensure availability in line 
with strategic independence of the EU.   
 
3. Circularity  
The proposed regulation dedicates a chapter on the circularity of raw materials. SUEZ thinks that it 
does not meet the initial expectations.  
The chapter encourages the collection of waste and to reuse products and components with a critical 
raw material recovery potential, yet it misses essential aspects of a circular concept.  
First, an obligation for a minimum recycled content  will encourage the recycling of the critical 
materials. With this obligation, the positive effects are that a secondary market of raw materials will 
be created . Minimum recycled content exists in other Directives such as the  Single Use Plastics 
Directive and the upcoming Battery Directive.  Therefore, the obligation for a minimum recycled 
content target within the Batteries Directive should be referenced in the Critical Raw Material Act. In 
case where minimum recycled content targets do not exist, they should be added where possible.    
Second, there is a lack of obligation on the part of member states to maintain strategic reserves. SUEZ 
propose that these strategic reserves need to be replenished with recycled materials  in order to 
develop this sector.  
 
4. Recycled content of magnets  
SUEZ welcomes the initiative of the European Commission to have a recycled content of permanent 
magnets (article 28). Having those obligations is important to promote recycling. Magnets are used in 
several electronic devices (wind energy generators, electric vehicles, heat pumps etc.).

For instance, hybrid or electric vehicles or renewable energies (wind turbines) will be major consumers 
of rare earths via permanent magnets. In permanent magnets are two (Neodymium and Dysprosium) 
of the five most rare earths. With further electrification t heir demand is set to significantly increase 
over the next few years. 
Therefore, the c ollection of these materials  is the main problem, because today's magnets are very 
small (in IT-products), but tomorrow with wind turbines their size will change. Our recommendation is 
for rare metals (Cobalt, Nickel, Lithium), to put in place a mandatory rate of incorporation of recycled 
earth in the production of permanent magnets.  This would help to create a secondary market for 
permanent magnets. In addition, ensuring the recycling of magnets is developed in Europe, it will 
continue to reduce emissions and lower the risk of exporting magnets  when they could be used in 
Europe.  
 
5. Strategic projects 
In chapter 3 of the proposal, the objective is to strengthen the European value chain by identifying 
strategic projects in the European Union and in third countr ies that want to become active in the 
extraction, processing or recycling of raw materials. The EU will support them through financing.  
For projects that involve processing or recycling , the limitation of the permit granting process shall 
not exceed 12 months and for those that already entered the permitting granting process before being 
granted the status of Strategic Project the limitation of it shall not exceed 9 months.  
During the summit “Choose France” which to ok place the 12 May 2023 in Dunkerque, President 
Emmanuel Macron indicated that permit granting processes are too long and this it discourages every 
participant of the project and increases costs. The president announced that France will pass a law to 
go much faster and to guarantee a project by giving1 authorizations in nine months. 
SUEZ underlines the importance that the permit granting process should not be longer than 12 
months. Often , administrative processes are too long and take too much time to finally start the 
project. Therefore, an overall permit granting process of no more than one year is essential and takes 
into consideration from the identification of the project till the permitting finalization.  
 
6. Governance of the Critical Raw Material Board 
The European Commission plans to establish a European Critical Material Board that advises the 
Commission and will facilitate the coordination and implementation of actions, as to monitor strategic 
projects. The Board will be composed of representatives from the Commission, who will ch air this 
board as well, from Member States and observers from the European Parliament. For now, it is only 
intended to keep regular contact with stakeholders.

Professionals of the sector should be included as full members of the Board. Even if it is expected to 
consult them on a regular basis, having them as a full member on board would ensure a more accurate 
representation as well as an ongoing representation of the recycling sector.  
Moreover, SUEZ recommends creating a sub-group dedicated to circular economy. Becoming more 
resilient on raw materials, used for example in electronics, means also strengthening the circularity of 
those elements. The European Commission’s proposal indicates one chapter to it, and this is the reason 
why SUEZ see s the importance of aligning  these two aspects also in the work of the Critical Raw 
Materials Board.   
 
Conclusion  
SUEZ welcomes the initiative of the European Commission to enhance the recycling of critical raw 
materials in the European Union. To truly achieve a circular economy in Europe, some aspects should 
be taken into consideration: 
- Strategic technologies (point 31 of Article 2) need to be better defined. It should be clear that 
water and circular economy are included.  
- Regarding the list of critical raw materials, the European Commission's ability to modify the 
list should be restrained to adding new elements. It should not be able to suppress materials as 
markets are set up to recycle th em and to ensure availability in line with strategic 
independence of the EU.   
- SUEZ welcomes the obligation of minimum recycled content of critical raw materials  and 
insists on maintaining this ambition.  
- Recycled minimum content should be also the case for magnets to ensure the increasing 
demands for electrical products.  
- The permit granting process shall not exceed 12 months for strategic projects.  
- The Critical Raw Materials Board should include waste professionals and a sub -group about 
circular economy.

1.2. Extend the list of (strategic) net-zero technologies 
 
The selection of technologies included in the definition of Article 3 and in the Annex is questionable. As 
an example, Carbon Capture Utilization (CCU) is not mention ed when we are currently investigating 
inside our coalition for a CCU process linked to aviation electro fuel and or carbon fiber development.  
The example of alternative fuels should also be given: European transport and industry  cannot be 
electrified. For aviation for example, sustainable alternative fuels are expected to contribute most to the 
decarbonisation of the sector. Furthermore, considering that Fit for 55 legislation such as FuelEU 
Maritime and ReFuelEU Aviation include specific RFNBO and sustainable fuels quotas, it seems 
imperative that these technologies have additional support to enable their fast ramp-up and compliance 
with EU legislation. 
 
Therefore, some technologies should already be added in the list of (strategic) net zero technologies 
contributing to the European Net Zero goals.

Justification 
Instead of establishing additional training structures in the form of academies, it would be much more 
useful to support existing structures in the member states. As the proposed Act addresses skills related 
issues in a way, which can set a precedent for future EU initiatives, it is important to secure that this 
new form of EU intervention appropriately respects national competences for vocational training as 
defined in the EU Treaty article 166. Moreover, it is important not to start these net zero academi es 
from scratch, whereas the relevant EU sectoral skills blueprints can offer a useful steppingstone on 
which to build. Should academies be established in which learning programs, contents and materials 
for the production of net -zero technologies are developed, it must be ensured that this is done by 
practitioners from companies and not politically steered away from the professional practice.

 
Microsoft Position Paper 
EU Cyber Solidarity Act Proposal 
 
 
 July 2023 - Response to Public Consultation 
 
  
 
Microsoft welcomes the opportunity to share our views on the recently proposed Cyber Solidarity Act, 
the aims of which - improving preparedness, detection, and response to cybersecurity incidents across 
the EU – we fully support . We believe it is our  responsibility to help protect the digital systems that 
underpin our society, including through close partnerships with others in private industry, government, 
and civil society. 
On February 23, 2022, the cybersecurity world entered a new age of hybrid war. On that day, hours 
before missiles were l aunched and tanks rolled across borders, Russian actors launched a destructive 
cyberattack against Ukrainian government, technology, and financial sector targets. Key among st the 
lessons learned was that cloud computing could significantly enhance physical and logical security and 
resiliency as well as  enable advances in threat intelligence and endpoint protection that have proven 
valuable against ongoing cyberattacks in Ukraine.  
Cybercriminals and nation state threat  actors continue to  invest in cyber  capabilities and t he 
sophistication, persistence, and impact of their attacks against global targets likewise continues to grow 
apace. Threat actors are increasingly targeting Internet of Things (IoT) devices or Operational 
Technology (OT) control devices as entry points to networks and critical infrastructure . The trillions of 
signals we analyze from Microsoft’s worldwide ecosystem of products and services reveal the ferocity, 
scope, and scale of digital threats. We are taking action to defend o ur customers and the broader 
ecosystem; staying one step ahead requires speed, agility, and resources. 
Addressing global cyber threats requires a skilled cybersecurity workforce  yet we are facing a  severe 
skills shortage. In 2022, the shortage of cybersecurity professionals  in the EU ranged between 260,000 
and 500,000, while the estimated cybersecurity workforce needs are estimated at 883,000 professionals. 
Demand for cybersecurity skills has grown by an average of 35% over the past year. New cybersecurity 
legal a nd regulatory requirements will also require increased familiarity with cyber challenges and 
corresponding compliance obligations.  
Against this background, we address three clear suggestions which we believe would improve the Cyber 
Solidarity Act proposal, namely: 
a) Ensure cooperation with the  essential infrastructure entities (NIS2) and the European Cyber 
Shield and Security Operations Center (SOC) of SOCs, depending on relevance such as expertise 
and knowledge. 
b) Reconsider references to ‘technological sovereignty’ without a clear definition and ensure that 
‘Trusted Providers’ include the EU’s security partners active in European Cybersecurity and 
Defense, such as NATO members and partners , enabling providers headquartered in NATO 
Member Countries to  participate and compete for tenders within the Digital Europe 
Programme, Pillar 3.

c) Ensure that state of the art Cloud and AI technologies will be at the center of the SOCs to enable

Include essential entities (NIS2)  
The CSA aims to improve the cooperation between the public SOC s, however it appears to ignore 
including the SOCs or cyber expertise of entities in scope of the NIS2 Directive, particularly the essential 
entities. These entities are responsible for the EU’s critical infrastructure and as such  are regulated by 
national and EU legislation like the NIS2 Directive. The essential entities are currently on a security 
journey and actively (must) invest in further developing their cyber capabilities and coordination among 
the SOCs of entities in scope, but also with the National Competent Authorities (often the NCSC’s or 
SOCs in scope of CSA). Microsoft recommends that the CSA formally acknowledge that the SOCs 
impacted by the CSA must intend to cooperate with the entities in scope of the NIS2 Directive.  
 
Technological sovereignty  
Microsoft strongly believes that cybersecurity is global, and that its solutions are global as well. Our 
annual Microsoft Digital Defense Reports reinforces that cyber threat actors are not bound by physical 
borders between countries or regions . This strengthens Microsoft’s firm belief in the multistakeholder 
approach to cybersecurity:  
- Inclusivity and transparency are key to successful policy development and adoption of 
cybersecurity requirements, in particular related to cooperation mechanisms.  
- In order to stimulate high-end quality and to ensure strong cyber resilience in the EU, state-
of-the-art technical expertise is required with an emphasis on critical and public 
infrastructures. By referring to political ly sensitive definitions such as technological 
sovereignty, the Cyber Solidarity Act risks focusing on  political interests instead of 
addressing risk-based security interests.  
  
The suggested requirements for participation in Digital Europe Program (Pillar 3) projects will practically 
prevent cybersecurity service providers with headquarters in the US, but compliant with EU cybersecurity 
legislation, from participating and being a ‘Trusted Prov ider’ as defined in Article 16 of the CSA. This 
could negatively impact both non -EU and EU companies , as exclusion could hamper trade and 
innovation in cybersecurity , particularly amidst  geopolitical tensions  which have caused multiple 
cyberattacks on critical infrastructure and continue to destabilize the rules based economy. EU Member 
States should be free to choose which ‘Trusted Providers’  will aid their cybersecurity in a digital and 
global economy. We recommend considering a different approach that allows companies operating in 
the EU and compliant with EU and national legislation to be able to become a Trusted Provider and 
equal partner in the projects set up as extension of the CSA.   
  
Ensure State-of-the-Art technology and skills deployed in SOCs 
Today the odds remain stacked against cybersecurity professionals. Too often they fight an asymmetric 
battle against prolific, relentless and sophisticated attackers. To protect their organizations defenders 
must respond to threats that are often hidden amongst ‘noise’. Compounding this challenge is a global 
shortage of skilled security professionals leading to an estimated  3.4 million openings in the field. The 
volume and velocity of attacks requires us to continually create new technologies that can tip the scales 
in favor of defenders. Security professionals are scarce and must be empowered to disrupt attackers’ 
traditional advantages and drive organizational innovation.  In the last few months the world has 
witnessed a wave of innovation as organizations apply advanced AI to new technologies and use cases. 
We are ready for a paradigm shift and a massive leap forward by combining Microsoft’s leading security 
technologies with the latest advancements in AI. 
 
Human creativity and knowledge will always be imperative for defense. AI enabled cyber operations can 
augment security professionals with machine speed and scale so human ingenuity is deployed where it 
matters most. To deliver this experience, Microsoft suggests three guiding principles: 
 
• Simplify the complex.  - In security, minutes count. With State-of-the-Art technology , 
defenders can respond to security incidents within minutes instead of hours or days.  
• Catch what others miss.  - Attackers hide behind noise and weak signals. Defenders can 
discover malicious behavior and threat signals using AI -enabled technologies that could 
otherwise go undetected.  
• Address the talent gap. - A security team’s capacity is limited by the team’s size and the natural 
limits of human attention. AI boosts your defenders’ skills with its ability to answer security -
related questions – from the basic to the complex.

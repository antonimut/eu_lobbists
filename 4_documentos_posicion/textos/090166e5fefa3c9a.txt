EE-ISAC Position Paper | Feedback Submission to the European 
Commission Proposal: Cyber Solidarity Act  
1. Statement of Interest 
The European Energy - Information Sharing and Analysis Centre (EE -ISAC) welcomes the 
opportunity to submit its feedback to  the European Commission legislative proposal “Cyber 
Solidarity Act” (CSA) aimed at enhancing cybersecurity and improving cyber resiliency in the 
Union. 
The EE -ISAC is an industry -driven, information sharing network of trust which encourages the 
exchange of trust -based information between the private and public sectors to improve the 
resilience and security of the European energy infrastructure, by enabling a joint effort for the 
analysis of threats, vulnerabilities, inc idents, solutions and opportunities. The Malware 
Information Sharing Platform (MISP) platform is a key instrument for the Members the 
Association represents , being used for detection analysis and subsequent phases of incident 
handling, to share information about ongoing threats  artifacts, i n quasi -real time,  to assess 
whether these are recognized malicious activities or not, and thus providing useful inputs for the 
decision process. With such instances, EE -ISAC tries to leverage the partner’s eco system to 
cooperate with national and supranational entities, inviting them to contribute to the platform. 
The Association aims to enrich the threat intelligence community by giving interesting outlooks 
on the trends of the threats and attack schemes affec ting the energy sector. Furthermore, the 
EE-ISAC employs the Nextcloud  IT platform , hosted by ENISA,  to build on threat intelligence 
updates operators and services providers share in order to  enhance situational awareness  by 
exchanging data and information in a safe and secure environment. 
Established in 2015, the EE -ISAC continues on consolidating its presence as a reference in the 
European energy infrastructure protection scenario and assures to its members first -hand 
information on the latest changes and trends of the sectorial regulations and market. 
2. Main Observations

proposed CSA Regulation and the NIS 2 Directive (EU) 2022/2555 in order to avoid duplications 
or overlaps.

foreseeable future, so when a physical incident occurs it will also likely impact and affect the IT 
system of the energy operator. This will require consistent and clear definitions, coherent across 
the entire regulatory landscape. The global pandemic COVID -19 posed new challenges to

computer security incident response team (CSIRTs), the NIS Cooperation Group , ENISA and the

responding to incidents and encourages  the exchange of relevant information about cyber 
threats at the international level with EU like-minded countries and partners. It is of fundamental

incident response and reporting. In this regard, the EE-ISAC has established a close collaboration

based in the United States and the JE-ISAC based in Japan in order to share cyber threats trends 
and incidents response approaches that interest the global commu nity. Therefore, the EU shall 
pursue in establishing dialogue and partnerships at an international level with the aim of fighting 
common global treats.

3. Policy Development Suggestions (specific articles) 
The EE-ISAC would like to express its main observations related to the following articles: 
CHAPTER I – GENERAL OBJECTIVES, SUBJECT MATTER, AND DEFINITIONS  
Article 1.2 (a) Subject matters and objectives 
CHAPTER III – CYBERSECURITY INCIDENT REVIEW MECHANISM 
Article 16 – Trusted Providers  
In the above-mentioned articles, the objective of  strengthening the Union detection and 
situational awareness of cyber threats and incidents  by reinforcing the competitive position of 
industry and services sectors in the Union to contribute to the Union’s technological sovereignty, 
along with the requirements set by the Digital Europe Program (DEP) to become trusted providers, 
seem to underline that in the procurement procedure only service providers established in the 
EU are allowed to participate. 
As the cyber threat is global, and therefore the fight against cyber threats should be carried out 
at a global level, established partnerships with trusted providers should not prevent from 
involving global expertise in the incident response and threat intelligence activities. As innovation 
is key for cybersecurity technologies, innovative and competitive companies compliant with EU 
legislation,  based in  like-minded EU countries  should be able to participate in the procurement 
procedure as any other EU service provider. Understanding the concerns about different security 
standards being implemented by services providers, the assessment can be carried out based  on 
the validity of their technologies, rather than on their geographical area of establishment.  
 
Chapter II - THE EUROPEAN CYBER SHIELD 
Article 4.1 – National Security Operations Centres 
With regards to the designation of a National Security Operations Centre (SOC) by each Member 
State which shall act as a reference point and gateway to other public and private organisations  
for collecting and analysing information , the efforts of collaboration and coordination with the 
CSIRTs (specified under the NIS2 Directive (EU) 2022/2555 ) should be emphasized. As essential 
and important entities already have reporting obligation s to notify incidents to competent 
authorities and the CSIRTs, information can also be circulated by the latter to the relevant national 
SOCs in order to reduce additional administrative burdens on the entities. 
Close collaboration between the entities under the NIS2 Directive, the European Cyber Shield and 
Security Operations Center (SOC) should be encouraged.

Article 7.1 – Cooperation and information sharing with Union entities 
As stated in the article, once the Cross-border SOCs obtain information relating to a potential or 
ongoing largescale cybersecurity incident, they shall provide relevant information to EU -
CyCLONe, the CSIRTs network and the Commission, in view of their respective crisis management 
roles in accordance with Directive (EU) 2022/2555, without undue delay.  
However, as currently, the cross-country exchange of information, from operators to competent 
authorities back to other competent authorities is not running smoothly , a two-way process is 
advisable, in this case by requesting the national SOCs to also report to the entities and specific 
ISACs (depending on the sector of interest) on incidents, rather than only vice versa. The ISACs 
are in a position to enrich the information, making it sector specific, to provide more actionable 
information to their members.

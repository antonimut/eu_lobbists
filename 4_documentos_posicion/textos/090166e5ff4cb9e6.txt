CECA COMMENTS ON THE COMMISSION PROPOSAL FOR A REGULATION OF THE EUROPEAN 
PARLIAMENT AND OF THE COUNCIL AMENDING REGULATION (EU) NO 806/2014 AS REGARDS 
EARLY INTERVENTION MEASURES, CONDITIONS FOR RESOLUTION AND FUNDING OF 
RESOLUTION ACTION 
 
 
 
The Spanish Association of Savings and Retail Banks (CECA) welcomes the opportunity to 
comment on the Commission proposal for a Regulation of the European Parliament and of the 
Council amending Regulation (EU) No 806/2014 as regards early intervention measures,  
conditions for resolution and funding of resolution action (hereinafter called, proposal for a 
Regulation).  
 
In general terms, we assess positively the proposal for a Regulation since it will adapt the 
Regulation (EU) No 806/2014 of the European Parliament and of the Council , of 15 July 2014 , 
establishing uniform rules and a uniform procedure for the resolution of credit institutions and 
certain investment firms in the framework of a Single Resolution Mechanism and a Singl e 
Resolution Fund and amending Regulation (EU) No 1093/2010  (SRMR), to the new resolution 
framework and to the finalisation of the building period for the Single Resolution Fund (SRF). 
 
However, we believe that some adjustments should be made in order to improve the regulatory 
framework and promote the level playing field. 
 
 
I. GENERAL REMARKS 
 
Liquidity in resolution 
 
We welcome the recent SRB initiative to assess banks’ capacity to measure and report liquidity 
in resolution1including the availability of collaterals eligible for monetary policy and ELA. This 
allows to take into account the potential mobilization of available contingent liquidity. 
However, the current safety net might not be enough to provide liquidity in resolution if several 
banks faced liquidity constraints at the same time or if a global systemically important bank (G-
SIB) confronted a liquidity crisis. In this sense, it was very welcome the agreement reached by 
the European Parliament and the Council to imp lement the ESM backstop. However, we think 
that this backstop should be complemented with a liquidity in resolution instrument to be used 
in the aforementioned circumstances.  
 
Furthermore, the European Union is the only jurisdiction in which a centralised  lender of last 
resort (LLR) does not exist (the Emergency Liquidity Assistance -ELA- is too discretional and not 
sufficiently operational in practice to address liquidity crises as the ones observed in the United 
States, for example). Consequently, we bel ieve that a liquidity -in-resolution tool could be 
directly implemented by the European Central Bank, whose experience in providing 
collateralized funding makes it the perfect candidate to set up a credible mechanism with 
sufficient firepower to address a liquidity crisis.

In addition, procedural rules should be well defined and known ex -ante by banks. This new 
mechanism could be inspired by those used by the US Federal Reserve (Fed) to solve recent 
banking crises, such as: (a) loans by the Fed to bridge b anks established by the Federal Deposit 
Insurance Corporation-FDIC- (for instance, those established for SVB and Signature Bank) where 
the Federal Reserve loans to these banks were secured by collateral and the FDIC provided 
repayment guarantees 2; and (b)  the new Fed’s Bank Term Funding Program 3 (BTFP) where 
eligible borrowers are financially sound banks, loans can be repaid without penalty and the Fed 
will lend up to par value of the securities used as collateral (no haircut).  
 
In this sense, we support  the statement4 recently made by Mr. Domin ique Laboureix, Chair of 
the Single Resolution Board, on the policymakers’ need to come back to the drawing board on 
the issue of funding in resolution stressing that “ clear inter -institutional support” from EU 
Member States, the ECB and the resolution authorities is key. Mr. Laboureix’s proposal would 
consist of the ECB agreeing to fund banks in resolution, potentially backed by an EU public 
guarantee. He added that the new lines would not necessarily be drawn, but that “It’s a way of 
showing that if there is a need of liquidity support, we are here ”. From our point of view, 
funding provided under this new tool should not be considered as State aid. 
 
Therefore, we propose to amend the explanatory memorandum and the recital 3 of the proposal 
for a Regulation and to add a new article 4bis in the SRMR: 
 
- Explanatory memorandum – Summary of the crisis management and deposit insurance 
(CMDI) reform elements: 
 
“The amendments included in the CMDI package cover a range of policy aspects 
and constitute a coherent response to the identified problems: 
 
(…) 
 
strengthening the funding in resolution by complementing the internal loss - 
absorbing capacity of institutions, which remains the first line of defence, with the 
use of DGS funds in resolution to help access resolution funds without imposing 
losses on depo sitors where appropriate, subject to conditions and safeguards.  
Moreover, the safety net should be complemented with a liquidity in resolution 
instrument that could be used when several banks faced liquidity constraints at 
the same time, or if a systemic entity faced a liquidity crisis. This liquidity -in-
resolution tool could be directly implemented by the European Central Bank (ECB) 
whose experience in providing collateralized funding makes it the perfect 
candidate to set up a credible mechanism with suff icient resources to address a 
liquidity crisis. Procedural rules should be well defined and known ex -ante by 
banks. In this sense, the ECB could fund banks in resolution, potentially backed by

II. SPECIFIC REMARKS 
 
Article 69. Target level 
 
According to the modification proposed to Article 69, the Board may defer the collection of ex-
ante contributions when the amount to be collected is not proportionate to the costs of the 
collection process. We welcome this possibility, and we believe that the legislative text should 
foresee that this decision will be communicated in good time to credit institutions in order to  
facilitate their planning tasks accordingly. 
 
 
Article 24. Sale of business tool 
 
During the functioning of the resolution framework, the only resolution tool which has been used so 
far is the sale of business tool. Regarding this tool, we are of the view that there are some elements 
which could be improved and the review of SRMR is an opportunity to leverage.  
 
Under the current framework, the acquirer of a failing or likely to fail (FOLTF) bank has to address 
the uncertainties derived from facts or events that were underestimated or unrecorded prior to the 
resolution because it holds the subsidiary liability. In this sense, we propose to enhance the provided 
protection of the acquirer’s responsibility regarding contingent and hidden liabilities. Making the 
acquirer directly or indirectly responsible for the conduct of management in resolution is unjustified 
and cannot be easily prevented through due diligence, particularly bearing in mind the fast

acquirer has to face, the most interesting the purchase will be for potential investors.
